What ELVIA adds to post-publication review

ELVIA is built around a bounded question about a scientific claim. A case connects an exact source location to the assumptions examined, the argument or check, counterevidence and the limits of the conclusion. Readers can download the original article and analysis report, cite a specific revision and inspect later corrections.

The aim is to help researchers understand, apply and improve results. A case may clarify a condition, support the original argument, identify a possible issue or leave a question open. Browse the 300-paper NeurIPS 2025 collection to see the current format, and read the methodology and decision process.

How should I evaluate a paper analysis?

Check whether you can identify the source version and reproduce the steps that matter to the claim. Look for the exact scope, assumptions, evidence against the analysis and a clear revision history. Ask who contributed the analysis and whether independent review has actually taken place. A severity label by itself cannot answer those questions.

A computational reproduction and a mathematical verification also answer different questions. Reproducing a reported number does not alone establish the assumptions of a theorem; finding a local derivation issue does not alone measure its effect on an experiment.

Where can PhD students and researchers contribute?

Choose a task close to your expertise. For a focused claim analysis or review on ELVIA, follow the contributor guide. For discussion, venue review or reproducibility documentation, consult the relevant project's participation rules. You can often make a useful contribution by examining one clear question.
