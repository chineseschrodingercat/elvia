01 · Statistics · An explicit counterexample

Does pooling calibration scores preserve coverage for every output?

Paper: Accelerating Feature Conformal Prediction via Taylor Approximation. Source: NeurIPS 2025 proceedings PDF, checked in the record on 21 October 2026. Read the original paper ↗

Locate the claim

Problem 1 points to Remark 1 and Equations (9)–(11), pages 5–6; vector coverage Equation (13), page 8; and the segmentation description, page 9. Its target is the claimed passage from pooled coordinate calibration to fixed-coordinate or whole-vector coverage.

Follow the decisive example

The report uses 99 independent, identically distributed constant vectors with 11 coordinates: ten zeros followed by one. Predictions are zero and each relevant Jacobian row has norm one. The pooled calibration scores contain 990 zeros and 99 ones. The 90% finite-rank index is 981, so the threshold is zero.

The final coordinate is then missed every time, and so is the whole vector. Average-coordinate coverage is still 10/11, above 90%. The distinction between those events is the point of the construction.

Keep the counterevidence and limits

The scalar fixed-score rank argument and the weaker average-coordinate interpretation survive this example. It is an analytic construction, not a measurement of the paper's datasets or benchmark coverage. The report proposes separate coordinate calibration or a per-vector maximum score, with independent verification and adoption still unestablished.

A useful next contribution

Check the exact coverage event in the cited passages, verify the order-statistic construction, and test whether an explicit assumption already narrows the disputed statement. Explain whether that reading resolves or changes Problem 1.

02 · GPU inference · A source obligation and an open runtime question

Does a decoder's boundary read change the decoded weights?

Paper: 70% Size, 100% Accuracy: Lossless LLM Compression for Efficient GPU Inference via Dynamic-Length Float (DFloat11). Source: arXiv:2504.11651v3, the author version identified in this record. Read the inspected paper version ↗

Locate the claim

Problem 1 connects Section 2.3.2 to decode.cu, lines 85–87, and the encode packing function, lines 164–170, in the inspected release. Each of 512B launched threads stores a 5-bit gap; the decoder reads two adjacent bytes without a sentinel or conditional.

Follow the boundary calculation

The logical array contains 320B bytes. The final thread accesses byte indices 320B − 1 and 320B, so its second read is outside that array. The same alignment shifts the second byte away: its value cannot change the extracted gap.

This separates a logical storage-contract issue from a demonstrated incorrect result. Allocator padding may also make the address physically readable. The report therefore does not establish a GPU crash or wrong decoded weights.

Keep the unresolved question separate

Problem 2 examines inactive tail threads whose registers are not initialized by a conditional load before the counting loop. Its runtime and output consequences remain unresolved; assumed register values are not observed GPU behavior.

A useful next contribution

Verify the pinned source and bit extraction first. If investigating the runtime candidate, distinguish an actually observed native-GPU symptom from the source obligation, and document the device, build, inputs and controls. The existing report does not claim such a run occurred.

03 · Computer vision · A local operation and its controls

Does resizing a depth image compute a complete-patch average?

Paper: 4D-VLA: Spatiotemporal Vision-Language-Action Pretraining with Cross-Scene Calibration. Source: the retained NeurIPS 2025 official proceedings PDF. Read the original paper ↗

Locate the claim

Problem 1 compares Section 4.2, PDF page 6, with unproject.py, function interpolate_depth, lines 54–67, in the inspected release. The paper describes a patch mean and a rule to skip a feature when more than 90% of the patch is masked.

Follow the local examples

For a 14 × 14 patch reduced to one output with the inspected bilinear rule, only four central pixels contribute. One valid central pixel gives interpolated mask 1/4, even though 195 of 196 pixels are missing; the feature is retained. Conversely, four invalid central pixels can cause rejection while the other 192 pixels are valid.

A separate all-valid example isolates averaging: four central depths equal 2 and all other depths equal 4. The complete-patch mean is 776/196, approximately 3.959, while the bilinear result is 2.

Keep the controls and limits

Constant and completely invalid patches agree under the compared rules. The examples establish a local discrepancy under the stated alignment; they do not establish its prevalence in the dataset or an effect on robot performance. The report's proposed patch-sum rule has not been independently verified or adopted by the authors.

A useful next contribution

Check the source's interpolation and mask conventions against the cited paper passage. Explain whether another stated processing step resolves the discrepancy before proposing any evaluation of downstream performance.

04 · Generative models · Supporting the inspected reasoning

Can a focused check support a paper without validating every result?

Paper: Policy Optimized Text-to-Image Pipeline Design. Source: the retained NeurIPS 2025 official proceedings PDF. Read the original paper ↗

Locate the inspected reasoning

The source checks identify Sections 3.1–3.4, especially Equation (5), PDF pages 4–6, and the reported metric tables. The report considers the training-stage interpretation, normalized logit guidance and aggregate numerical consistency.

Follow the supporting checks

Interpolating the two logit vectors gives a distribution proportional to pGRPO(w)λ pSFT(w)1−λ, with the softmax supplying the normalizer. At λ = 0 and λ = 1, it recovers the respective endpoint policies.

The reported R² = 0.643 and correlation r = 0.816 also satisfy the recorded consistency check R² ≤ r² = 0.665856. These local checks did not establish a substantive error in their inspected scope.

Keep the unverified work visible

The note does not independently verify reward calibration, generated images or human-study outcomes. A full implementation, original reward predictions, image samples and human-response records were not available for the report's independent empirical reproduction. No author code was executed in this check.

A useful next contribution

Verify the algebra and the metric definitions in the exact source, or narrow a new question to material you can actually inspect. Supporting this conclusion is a useful result; there is no need to invent a problem to contribute.
