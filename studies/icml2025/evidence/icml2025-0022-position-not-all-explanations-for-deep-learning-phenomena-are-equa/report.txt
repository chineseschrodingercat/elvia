# 3. Problem 1 - The bias-variance decomposition does not imply convex risk

Source claim and location. Section 3.2, PDF p. 6, right column, attributes a convex relationship between model complexity and predictive error to the bias-variance tradeoff and infers that, after increasing complexity begins to worsen performance, further increases should not improve it. The suspect step is the claimed implication from a decomposition of risk into components to a restriction on the shape of their sum.

The source gives no formal theorem or assumptions guaranteeing that implication. The following counterexample uses ordinary nested, full-rank least-squares regression with finite independent Gaussian noise. It addresses the unconditional mathematical wording; it is not a counterexample to an unstated, more restricted theorem about a specific model family.

Parameters and design. Let the four training-design rows be (1,1,1), (1,-1,-1), (-1,1,-1) and (-1,-1,1). Write this 4-by-3 matrix as X. Set beta = (sqrt(2), sqrt(1/10), sqrt(2))^T. Training outcomes are Y = X beta + epsilon, with independent epsilon_i distributed as N(0,4). The independent test covariate is uniform over the same four rows; its response is x^T beta plus independent N(0,4) noise. For k = 0,1,2,3, fit least squares using the first k columns, with the zero predictor for k = 0.

\[X^{\mathsf T}X=4I_3,\qquad \mathbb E_x[xx^{\mathsf T}]=I_3.\] Assumption verification. Every fitted design has full column rank; the feature sets are nested and contain at most three fitted coefficients for four observations. Orthogonal columns make omitted signal components orthogonal to every included column. Training and test noises are mutually independent with mean zero and variance four. All second moments exist. Thus no interpolation singularity, exotic loss, distribution shift, or nonidentifiable fit is needed.

\[\widehat\beta_j=\beta_j+\frac14\sum_{i=1}^{4}X_{ij}\varepsilon_i,\qquad\mathbb E[\widehat\beta_j]=\beta_j,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\beta_j)=1\quad (j\le k).\] Complete calculation. Averaging over training noise, the independent test covariate and test noise gives the exact squared-error risk below. The integrated squared bias is the sum of the omitted coefficient squares; the integrated estimator variance is k because the test second-moment matrix is the identity. The irreducible test-noise contribution is four.

\[R_k=\mathbb E\!\left[(Y_{\rm test}-\widehat f_k(x_{\rm test}))^2\right]=4+\sum_{j>k}\beta_j^2+k.\]

k | Squared bias | Variance | Noise | Test MSE

0 | 4.1 | 0 | 4 | 8.1

1 | 2.1 | 1 | 4 | 7.1

2 | 2.0 | 2 | 4 | 8.0

3 | 0.0 | 3 | 4 | 7.0

The squared bias decreases and the variance increases. Nevertheless, test MSE falls from 8.1 to 7.1, rises to 8.0, and falls again to 7.0. The claimed convexity would require the middle value at k = 2 to be at most the average at k = 1 and k = 3. Instead:

\[R_2=8>\frac{R_1+R_3}{2}=7.05,\qquad R_2-\frac{R_1+R_3}{2}=\frac{19}{20}=0.95.\] Alternative reading and impact. The intended reference may be the familiar U-shaped heuristic often used to introduce model selection. That heuristic can be useful under additional assumptions. It is not a consequence of the decomposition alone. The defect is a bounded mathematical overstatement in a motivating example, not evidence that the paper’s main research-policy argument or its empirical figure is false. Severity: Minor.

Precisely supported repair. Replace the alleged implication by a statement that conventional heuristics often depict a U-shaped risk curve, while the exact bias-variance decomposition itself neither forces convexity nor forbids later improvement. The derivation and exact arithmetic verify that narrower statement. No proposed change claims to prove a universal shape theorem or to repair an empirical experiment.

# 4. Static code observation - multiplication in a division-labelled upstream task

Appendix A, p. 14, cites teddykoker/grokking as a base implementation. In the inspected train.py snapshot, division_mod_p_data describes modular division but assigns result = x * y % p and passes y itself as the second input token. For the default prime p = 97, x = 2 and y = 3 satisfy 0 \(\leq\) x < p and 0 < y < p. The implemented expression returns 6. Since the inverse of 3 modulo 97 is 65, modular division returns 2\(\times\)65 mod 97 = 33. The prime, range and invertibility conditions were checked using independent integer arithmetic. [S3, function division_mod_p_data]

This proves a discrepancy between that upstream file’s description and its arithmetic. It does not prove that Jeffares and van der Schaar used that exact snapshot or unchanged dataset generator. Their prime-count implementation and ten-seed run configuration were not found in the linked base file. Also, multiplication and division on the nonzero second operand are related by the bijective input-token relabelling y \(\mapsto\) y^(-1); this substantially limits the implication for the qualitative grokking illustration. No published-result failure is inferred.

The scalar operation x times the modular inverse of y would implement the advertised division, but no tensor-level patch was applied or executed. Publication linkage remains Needs Evidence: the actual experimental snapshot and configuration would be needed. The observation is attributed to the third-party repository, with its exact byte hash, and is not claimed to be a novel discovery or an established defect in the paper’s experiments.

# 5. Other checked claims and dependencies

Regularization (§2.1, p. 3). Nakkiran et al. prove monotonicity under specific linear-model/data assumptions and optimally tuned regularization; their neural-network evidence is empirical and they also present a non-Gaussian counterexample to sample monotonicity. The target paper’s wording expresses an expectation under appropriate regularization, so it should not be read as a guarantee for every practical optimizer and setting. This is a scope clarification, not an additional demonstrated error. [S4, §§1 and 4.1]

Lottery tickets (§2.3, p. 4). Malach et al.’s theorem concerns high-probability pruning approximation of a bounded target ReLU network by a sufficiently large random network under stated depth/width conditions. It is not a universal guarantee about training every dense architecture or identifying useful tickets cheaply. The target paper already distinguishes existence from discovery and hardware utility; no separate central defect is established. [S5, Theorem 2.1]

Prime-count illustration (Figure 4, p. 6; Appendix A, p. 14). The appendix explicitly describes parameter magnitude and parameter count as confounders. It does not claim that primality is a causal explanation. The illustration is consistent with the methodological point that an observed correlate need not be useful or generalizable. The plotted means, standard deviations and prime-count construction remain unreproduced.
