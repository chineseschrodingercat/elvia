Abstract: Two Major defects are established in the stated core guarantees: the simultaneous coverage theorem omits the finite-grid correction present in its proof, and the claimed equivalence of calibration and inference uses the opposite score-bound sign. Explicit legal-input examples quantify both failures. Four bounded Minor findings concern the poisoning extension and released implementations. The valid Lipschitz score bounds, corrected concentration guarantee and reported performance remain distinct; no author experiment was executed.

# The simultaneous coverage theorem is false as stated

Problem 1 (Missing grid correction in the central auditing guarantee: Major).  Theorem 3.3, p. 5, sets \(\delta'=\delta/(2m-2)\) and claims, almost surely in the calibration set, \[\Pr_{D_{\rm eval}}\!\left\{\forall\epsilon>0:
 \underline\gamma_m^-(\epsilon,\delta')
 \le\gamma_h(\epsilon)
 \le\overline\gamma_m^+(\epsilon,\delta')\right\}\ge1-\delta.\] However, Theorems B.2–B.3, pp. 15–16, include respectively \(+1/m\) and \(-1/m\). Their grid-bridging proof does not establish the uncorrected statement.

The following counterexample proves failure, rather than merely identifying a missing proof step. Set \[\mathcal X=\mathbb R,\quad X\sim{\rm Uniform}[0,1],\quad Y=1,\quad
s(x,1)=x,\quad n=1,\quad\alpha=\tfrac12,\quad m=2,\quad\delta=\tfrac18.\] All draws are independent. The domain is closed and convex, the score is continuous and 1-Lipschitz, and the conformal rank is valid. Then \(q_\alpha=X_{\rm cal}\). Condition on \[q_\alpha\in(0.499,0.501),\] an event of calibration probability \(0.002>0\). A positive-probability set of failing calibrations suffices to refute the theorem’s almost-sure qualifier.

Define \(h(x,\epsilon)=x+g(\epsilon)\), where \(g\) is the continuous piecewise-linear interpolant of \[(0,0),\quad(0.26,0.26),\quad(0.27,-0.27),\quad(0.48,0.48),\] and \(g(\epsilon)=0.48\) for \(\epsilon\ge0.48\). The cone \(|g|\le\epsilon\) is convex, so all segments obey the attack budget. The attack is fixed independently of every sample and of \(q_\alpha\).

Here \(\delta'=1/16\). The binomial-inversion formulas in Eqs. (14)–(15) give a lower endpoint \(L_2=1/4\) when both restrictive indicators succeed, an upper endpoint \(U_0=3/4\) when both conservative indicators fail, and a lower endpoint \[L_1=1-\sqrt{15}/4=0.03175416\ldots\] when exactly one restrictive indicator succeeds. For the two evaluation inputs, consider these three disjoint events:

1. Both are below \(0.239\). At \(\epsilon=0.26\) both restrictive indicators succeed, but \(\gamma_h=q_\alpha-0.26\in(0.239,0.241)<L_2\).

2. Both are above \(0.771\). At \(\epsilon=0.27\) both conservative indicators fail, but \(\gamma_h=q_\alpha+0.27\in(0.769,0.771)>U_0\).

3. One is below \(0.019\) and the other above \(0.241\). At \(\epsilon=0.48\) exactly one restrictive indicator succeeds, but \(\gamma_h=q_\alpha-0.48\in(0.019,0.021)<L_1\).

The first and third events are disjoint because the latter has an input above \(0.241\); the second is disjoint from both because each has a small input. Their total probability is \[0.239^2+0.229^2+2(0.019)(0.759)
=\frac{34601}{250000}=0.138404>\delta=0.125.\] Thus, for every calibration in the stated interval, the claimed simultaneous event fails with probability exceeding its permitted risk. The parent independently evaluated the exact fractions and all strict endpoint inequalities in `check.py`; no sampling approximation is involved.

## Repair, dependency and counterevidence

The appendix supports the precise corrected guarantee \[\Pr\!\left\{\forall\epsilon>0:
 \max\{0,\underline\gamma_m^-(\epsilon,\delta')-1/m\}
 \le\gamma_h(\epsilon)
 \le\min\{1,\overline\gamma_m^+(\epsilon,\delta')+1/m\}\right\}
 \ge1-\delta.\] The original continuity and grid arguments were checked with their actual assumptions. The failure concerns the first advertised contribution and the central simultaneous audit guarantee, so Major is supported even when \(1/m\) is small in large experiments.

A material counterpoint is that released `coverage.py`, lines 23–31, already adds \(1/n\) in its uniform upper-bound helper; subtracting that helper from one supplies the lower correction. The source therefore contradicts any assertion that the experiments necessarily implemented the uncorrected theorem. This is a defect in the reviewed statement and its claimed derivation, not evidence that the reported curves omitted the correction.

# The calibration–inference equivalence has the wrong sign

Problem 2 (Lower-score calibration reverses the robust threshold shift: Major).  Section 4.2, p. 7, claims that calibration on the underlined score \(\underline s_{\rm lip\text{-}rcp}\) of Eq. (19) yields the same result as robust inference by quantile translation equivariance. Actual visual inspection confirms the underline. Equation (19), p. 6, defines this as \(s-K\epsilon\), where \(K=L_nL_s\).

Translation equivariance instead gives \[q_{\rm lower}=q_\alpha-K\epsilon.\] Ordinary inference with that threshold tests \(s\le q_\alpha-K\epsilon\). Robust inference tests \[s-K\epsilon\le q_\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s\le q_\alpha+K\epsilon.\] These are opposite shifts. The equivalent calibration uses the upper score \(\overline s=s+K\epsilon\).

For a complete probabilistic witness, retain \(\mathcal X=\mathbb R\), \(X\sim{\rm Uniform}[0,1]\), \(Y=1\), \(s=x\) and independent draws, now with \(n=9\), \(\alpha=0.1\), \(K=1\) and \(\epsilon=0.1\). Then \(q_\alpha=M=\max_iX_i\). Under the legal attack \(h(x,\epsilon)=x+\epsilon\), lower-score calibration followed by ordinary inference includes the label iff \(X\le M-0.2\). Its marginal coverage is \[\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(M-0.2)_+]
&=\frac9{10}-0.2+\frac{0.2^{10}}{10}\\
&=0.70000001024<0.9.\end{aligned}\] This integrates the density \(9u^8\) of \(M\); it is also verified with exact fractions. Robust inference, or calibration on the upper score, includes iff \(X\le M\) and has coverage \(0.9\).

The correction is a single bound-symbol change, but the printed derivation directly supports the central efficient calibration/inference construction and, followed literally, invalidates its robust coverage. Repair difficulty does not reduce this to a typographical-only finding. Conversely, Eqs. (18)–(19) themselves are valid, and the released inference code enlarges conformity scores in the correct direction. No historical undercoverage is inferred from this printed error.

# Poisoning-extension quantile indexing

Problem 3 (The displayed poisoning routine uses the wrong order statistic: Minor).  Appendix H, p. 22, explicitly assumes the standard conformal rank. Figure 6, p. 23, and the matching released `poisoning.py`, lines 21–23, use the zero-based index \(\lfloor\alpha(n+1)\rfloor\) for an ascending conformity-score array.

The released caller supplies sigmoid conformity scores \(A=1-R\). The charitable conformity-score reading requires instead \[i_{\rm correct}
=n-\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil
=\lfloor\alpha(n+1)\rfloor-1.\] If the displayed array were interpreted as nonconformity, its low-\(\alpha\) index would likewise not implement the high-\(1-\alpha\) rank stated in Appendix H.

Take \(n=9\), \(\alpha=0.1\), \(\epsilon=0\) and \(k=0\). For independent continuous conformity scores, the displayed function returns the second-smallest score; zero perturbation leaves sorting unchanged. A new score exceeds that value with probability \(8/10\), whereas the correct first order statistic gives \(9/10\). Such scores can be realized within the Lipschitz setting by \(X=\operatorname{logit}(U)\), \(U\sim{\rm Uniform}(0,1)\), a fixed linear logit and its sigmoid. All calibration/test pairs remain independent.

Fixing the index repairs this zero-attack boundary case; a full verification of the nonzero-budget greedy poisoning procedure is not claimed. Its loop also applies a decrement when \(k=0\) if \(\epsilon>0\), which requires separate specification. Minor is assigned because the demonstrated dependency is to the additional poisoning certificate, not to the main score bounds or simultaneous-audit proof. Appendix placement is not the reason. Table 9’s historical outputs remain unverified.

# Released auditing implementation

Problem 4 (The optional softmax restrictive set selects favorable corners: Minor).  In `deel/lip_rcp/vanilla_bounds/coverage.py`, lines 35–43, the softmax branch constructs one logit corner per class and takes `torch.any` over corners. Lines 113–125 request the restrictive set by passing a negative radius, but retain that favorable-corner union.

Let \(f(x)=(x,0)\), a globally 1-Lipschitz network, with temperature one, and let all calibration/evaluation pairs be the point mass \((X,Y)=(0,1)\). The conformity threshold is \(1/2\). For radius \(0.1\), the negative-radius construction has corner probabilities \[(0.450166,0.549834),\qquad(0.549834,0.450166).\] The union includes both labels. Yet the legal attack \(x\mapsto x-0.1\) gives true-class probability \(0.475021<1/2\), so vanilla coverage is zero. The purported restrictive indicator is one for every evaluation point. For example, the released binomial formula with \(m=100\) and \(\delta=0.05\) then returns a positive lower certificate about \(0.91708\).

A restrictive per-class test must use that class’s worst-case corner, rather than whether any class-specific corner admits it. The paper’s mathematical monotonicity bounds in Appendix E.2 remain valid. The default benchmark uses `lac_sigmoid`, which follows a separate branch. Hence this is a demonstrated optional implementation defect; impact on the paper’s reported results is Needs Evidence.

Problem 5 (The confidence routine changes the risk budget: Minor).  The same file, lines 23–31, inverts \(F_{n,p}(k)=\delta/(n-1)+10^{-5}\), while lines 138–145 pass the full \(\delta\) to both tails of an advertised joint interval.

The additive \(10^{-5}\) is a probability-risk change, not merely a root-finding tolerance. For \(n=5000\), requested \(\delta=10^{-8}\) and zero successes, its reported upper bound, including \(1/n\), is \[u=1-\left(\frac{10^{-8}}{4999}+10^{-5}\right)^{1/5000}
 +\frac1{5000}=0.00249993614\ldots.\] Under a true Bernoulli probability \(p=0.0026>u\), the zero-success event has probability \[(1-0.0026)^{5000}=2.2223856\cdot10^{-6}>10^{-8}.\] This already defeats the helper’s unrestricted confidence interpretation at one threshold. The gap \(p-u>10^{-4}\) is far greater than its \(10^{-8}\) bisection tolerance. It is not a numerical refutation of the particular risk level used in Figure 4.

For a joint two-sided certificate, the appendix’s union bound also requires allocating \(\delta/2\) to each tail before dividing by \(n-1\). A supported repair must retain \(\pm1/n\), use the allocated binomial risk without the added probability slack, conservatively round numerical endpoints and clip to \([0,1]\). No patched author routine was executed. Minor reflects the released-helper scope and unestablished historical mapping; the core theoretical defect is separately Problem 1.

# Training state and reproduction boundaries

Problem 6 (Released training evaluation updates state using its test loader: Minor).  `scripts/train.py` calls `model.train()` at line 92, invokes `test` at line 154 without switching the model to evaluation mode, and passes `test_loader` as that routine’s validation argument at line 268.

The selected CIFAR model uses `BatchCentering`. Its implementation in , lines 58–77, updates the stored running mean when `self.training` is true: \[\mu_{\rm new}=(1-\rho)\mu_{\rm old}+\rho\mu_{\rm batch}.\] Thus test inputs affect state that is subsequently saved and used at inference. Disabling gradients does not disable this update. With \(\rho=0.05\), old mean 2 and test-batch mean 10, the new mean is 2.4. This is a direct static dependency, not a measured accuracy change or proof of the historical training protocol.

Evaluation should use the intended validation split and frozen model state, with training mode restored where needed. Main robust-calibration/inference routines do call evaluation mode; that counterevidence prevents conflating this training-path issue with every inference invocation. A paper-level test-leakage or coverage claim would require actual split identities, checkpoints and run provenance, which were unavailable.

Additional source facts limit numerical reproduction. The calibration/evaluation loops in `robust_ps.py` check \(i+1>\texttt{num\_batches}\) only after processing: default \(-1\) consumes one batch, and a positive limit \(N\) can consume \(N+1\) if available. Explicit benchmark arguments and shorter loaders can mask that difference. `scripts/vcp_coverage.py`, lines 113–114, averages the final scalar bounds rather than the accumulated bound lists. These are preserved as local bookkeeping observations in the source audit; no changed historical mean or sample size is asserted.

The released ImageNet factory explicitly describes a smaller model than the paper, and the external `orthogonium` dependency is unpinned. The acquired commit therefore cannot by itself identify the exact paper-era model or numerical Lipschitz certificates.

# What survives the checks

The composition inequality \(|s(x,y)-s(x',y)|\le L_nL_s\|x-x'\|\) follows directly from the two Lipschitz assumptions. The sigmoid derivative is bounded by \(1/(4T)\) for \(T>0\). Bounding each output coordinate suffices for the associated class score; one should not incorrectly demand that a multi-output classifier with individually bounded rows have vector Lipschitz constant one.

Appendix E.2’s softmax corner estimates follow from increasing the target logit and decreasing the others, or vice versa. They are valid box bounds, though all coordinates need not reach their extrema simultaneously under one input perturbation. The corrected concentration result retains its required independent evaluation set. Continuity of extrema over a closed convex domain and a bounded perturbation ball was checked; convex interpolation supplies feasible approximating points.

The empirical comparisons disclose use of different architectures, while Appendix I adds a same-model comparison: at the displayed setting CAS reports coverage \(94.60\%\), set size 2.302 and runtime 2615 seconds, versus lip-rcp’s \(92.83\%\), 1.889 and 10 seconds. These are source-reported means, not independently reproduced measurements. The paper acknowledges the absence of a smoothing-parameter search for that comparison. On ImageNet, unequal evaluation counts are also addressed by a smaller matched-count run; they are not silently treated as identical.

The source calibration’s interpolation can select an extra conservative conformity order statistic. That direction enlarges the set and does not by itself establish undercoverage. This differs from the upward-index poisoning error. Original prior-method papers criticized in Appendix A were not independently audited, and no later-version or errata survey was performed.
