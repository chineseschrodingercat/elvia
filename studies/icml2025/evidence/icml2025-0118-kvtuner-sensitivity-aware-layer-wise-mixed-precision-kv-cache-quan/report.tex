Abstract: The theoretical explanation connecting concentrated attention to reliable low-bit quantization contains a Major unsupported implication: concentration before quantization does not control the projected quantization error. A finite counterexample using the stated two-bit quantizer nearly reverses an initially concentrated attention distribution. The same difficulty persists under the appendix’s Gaussian model. Two Minor findings concern overlapping released layer groups and a zero-remainder prefilling boundary. Empirical improvements and the practical value of offline mixed-precision search are not disproved. Evidence limits, alternative readings, and the precise scope of verified corrections are stated.\par
Date: Initial review, 24 October 2026\par
Title: Scientific review of KVTuner\par
\# Identity, coverage, and definitions\par
Paper 0118, I25-43487: X. Li et al., KVTuner: Sensitivity-Aware Layer-Wise Mixed-Precision KV Cache Quantization for Efficient and Nearly Lossless LLM Inference, ICML 2025, PMLR 267, pp. 36451–36485 [Retained latex: \textbackslash{}cite\{paper\}]. The supplied original contains 35 pages; page references below use its printed pages 1–35. The pinned repository is commit `96dd05eb2fe350c72c1a3dfdca04e878506f7c17` [Retained latex: \textbackslash{}cite\{code\}].\par
The main argument, Appendix A, calibration/search settings, layer-group tables, benchmark tables, and appendix figure captions were read. Original p. 16 was rendered and visually inspected to verify the equations. Plot curves were not digitized. Source inspection was static; no author code was executed or imported, no model inference was run, and no installation was attempted. Independent checks use elementary arithmetic and parsing of literal configuration data.\par
For one attention head, absorb the usual \(1/\sqrt D\) into the effective query \(u\), and write \[z_j=uK_j,\quad \delta_j=u(\widehat K_j-K_j),\quad
 a_j=\frac{e^{z_j}}{\sum_k e^{z_k}},\quad
 \widehat a_j=\frac{a_je^{\delta_j}}{\sum_k a_ke^{\delta_k}}.
 \tag{1.1}\] Section 3.2, Eq. (2), quantizes each selected group by \[\widehat x=z+s\,\operatorname{round}((x-z)/s),\qquad
 z=\min x,\quad s=(\max x-\min x)/(2^B-1).
 \tag{1.2}\] The method searches layer precision pairs using calibration accuracy and average nominal bit width, then reuses the chosen configuration online. The arithmetic mean bit objective is appropriate as a nominal payload measure when the layers have equal KV tensor sizes; it is not itself an accounting of metadata, residual tokens, packing, or peak allocations.\par
\# Supported Problems\par
Problem 1 (Major: the attention-concentration proof drops an uncontrolled factor).  Claim and dependency. Lemma 1 in Section 4.4, p. 5, asserts an exclusivity relation between sparse, concentrated attention and consistent low-precision robustness. Appendix A, p. 16, Eqs. (5)–(6), is its proof. Case 2 drops every non-dominant term because \(e^{z_j-z_i}\) is small, even though that term is multiplied by \(e^{\delta_j-\delta_i}\). This theoretical explanation is advertised in the abstract and introduction and used to interpret sensitive layers in Sections 4.4–4.5 and Appendix E. No bound on \(u\), on the projected errors, or on their size relative to logit margins is supplied.\par
A finite counterexample to the actual quantization argument. Use two keys in dimension three, per-token asymmetric quantization with \(B=2\), and \[K_1=(0.49,0,1),\quad K_2=(0.48,0,1.2),\quad u=(1000,0,0).\] These groups have positive ranges and no rounding ties. The original query in Eq. (1) can be \(q=\sqrt3\,u\), so the scale convention causes no discrepancy. The two group scales are \(1/3\) and \(0.4\). Thus \[\widehat K_1=(1/3,0,1),\qquad \widehat K_2=(0.4,0,1.2).\] The original logit margin is \(490-480=10\), whereas the quantized margin is \(1000(1/3-0.4)=-200/3\). Consequently \[a_1=0.9999546021313,\qquad
 \widehat a_1=1.11438315784\times10^{-29},
 \quad \operatorname{TV}(a,\widehat a)=0.9999546021313.\] The initial attention is extremely concentrated, but the quantized distribution is almost reversed. All coordinates and errors are finite. Replacing 1000 by \(c\to\infty\) makes the original attention arbitrarily concentrated while the quantized attention converges to the other token. This is not a rounding-level discrepancy. Values such as \(V_1=(0,0,1)\), \(V_2=(1,0,0)\) are exactly representable by the same per-token rule and transmit the attention reversal to the output. This construction is an attention-level counterexample, not a claim that these exact activations occurred in a tested LLM.\par
The Gaussian assumption does not rescue the derivation. Appendix A also models coordinate errors as Gaussian with \(\sigma=(\max K-\min K)/(2^B-1)\). This is an additional stochastic model, not the distributional law of deterministic rounding. Even granting independent Gaussian coordinates, take \(K_1=(0.001,0)\), \(K_2=(0,1)\), \(u=(10000,0)\), \(B=2\), and \(\sigma=1/3\). Again \(a_1=0.9999546\). Now the noisy margin is \[10+Z,\qquad Z\sim N(0,2(10000/3)^2).\] Its probability of reversing sign is \[\Phi\!\left(-\frac{10}{\sqrt2(10000/3)}\right)=0.4991537163.\] Thus even a natural interpretation of the appendix’s own noise assumption gives almost one-half probability of overturning the dominant token. The two examples test separate interpretations and are not conflated.\par
Necessity, alternatives, and scope. For finite logits, equality of the entire two softmax distributions holds exactly if and only if every \(\delta_j\) is the same: compare their pairwise probability ratios. The paper acknowledges this as Case 1 but dismisses it as atypical without an assumption that excludes it. For instance, \(K_j=(x_j,0,1)\), \(0<x_j<1\), and \(u=(0,0,1)\) give a uniform, non-concentrated distribution that is preserved exactly for every such \(x_j\) and every positive integer bit width, although the first coordinates may have nonzero rounding errors. This is a whole family with a nonzero query, not only \(q=0\).\par
A fixed, bounded perturbation becomes harmless when a margin tends to infinity. That restricted reading is correct; it is precisely the missing condition. Empirical correlations in Figures 4 and 11–19 may still hold for the measured models. They do not prove the exclusive or general robustness statement. The finding is Major because the paper’s central theoretical explanation is invalid as derived, without requiring a failure of its entire search framework or a lower benchmark score.\par
Verified correction bound. If \(z_i-z_j\ge m\) and \(\delta_j-\delta_i\le\rho\) for every \(j\ne i\), then (1.1) gives \[\widehat a_i\ge \frac{1}{1+(S-1)e^{-m+\rho}}. \tag{2.1}\] Each denominator ratio is at most \(e^{-m+\rho}\), proving the bound directly. The sufficient condition must therefore compare the margin with projected error and sequence length. With \(|\delta_j|\le\eta\), one may use \(\rho=2\eta\). This repairs a conditional single-head statement only; it proves neither prompt-independent layer sensitivity nor full-model accuracy preservation. Targeted inspection of arXiv v5, dated 20 November 2025, found the same Lemma 1 and Case 2 approximation still present [Retained latex: \textbackslash{}cite\{later\}]; no full-version diff or author correction is asserted.\par
Problem 2 (Minor: released layer groups are not partitions for two model settings).  Location and assumptions. Section 5.3 and Appendix D.1.2 group layers to define one precision pair per layer. In the supplied `search\_optuna\_adaptive.py`, lines 37–43, the Qwen2.5-14B per-channel configuration repeats layer 25, and the Qwen2.5-32B per-channel configuration repeats layers 22, 24, and 25. The independent static audit checks all twelve model/mode combinations; these are the two failures.\par
For the 14B setting, 49 memberships are used for 48 distinct layers. Lines 218–228 construct a dictionary that overwrites a repeated layer, but accumulate bit cost over every membership and divide by \(2L\). If every group uses KV4, the actual dictionary assigns four nominal bits per element while the returned objective is \[\frac{49(4+4)}{2\cdot48}=4.083333\ldots.\] Thus even the uniform configuration’s reported objective and constraint value are wrong.\par
For the 32B setting, the group at zero-based index 2 contains 22, 24, and 25 outside its designated special candidate set. Assuming required dependencies are available, the explicit consistency condition at lines 176–185 raises `ValueError` before the search starts. This is a static control-flow deduction, not an executed author program.\par
Impact and correction bounds. These defects obstruct or mismeasure the released extensions to these settings. They do not establish that the main 3B/7B/8B results used these groups, and they do not invalidate the separately reported uniform-precision 14B/32B measurements. That limited impact supports Minor. For 32B, removing 22, 24, and 25 from group index 2 yields a complete disjoint 64-layer partition compatible with the supplied special sets; this exact data-level repair was checked. For any setting, computing the nominal bit sum from the final distinct-layer mapping avoids double counting, but the grouping still must be made unambiguous. No corrected search performance has been verified.\par
Problem 3 (Minor: the forced-prefilling path mishandles zero remainder).  Claim and source. Section 5.3, p. 7, and Appendix E.1, p. 23, emphasize applying quantized KV during prefilling. In `flexible\_quant/flexible\_quant/flexible\_quantized\_cache.py`, lines 173–190, the `force\_quant=True` path with nonzero residual length \(R\) computes \(r=S\bmod R\), passes the slice `:-r` for quantization, and retains `-r:` at full precision.\par
For \(S=64,R=32\), \(r=0\). Python’s `:-0` is empty and `-0:` is the entire sequence. Hence this path sends zero tokens to quantization and retains all 64, rather than quantizing the complete prefix and retaining an empty suffix. If a backend rejects the empty input, it can fail earlier; that runtime outcome was not tested. The source-level slice defect is independent of such backend behavior.\par
Alternatives, impact, and bounded repair. The defect is confined to this flag and boundary condition, and does not occur for the same reason when \(r>0\) or \(R=0\). The released search file itself sets `force\_quant=False`; its historical relation to the prefilling-enabled paper runs is unresolved. Therefore no numerical table damage is asserted, and the supported severity is Minor. Computing \(c=S-r\), using prefix `:c` and suffix `c:`, restores the intended partition at this boundary. Only the slicing identity is verified; backend empty-input handling and complete inference behavior require separate evidence.\par
\# Other checks and unresolved evidence\par
Search is a heuristic, and its empirical scope matters. The enormous search-space counts \(9^{32}\) and \(5^6=15625\) are consistent with the stated configurations. Local Pareto pruning and shared group precision do not by themselves certify the global accuracy frontier under nonlinear layer interactions. The paper evaluates final model accuracy and reports a finite-search ablation; those are useful empirical checks. No global-optimality or universal prompt-independence theorem is established. The twenty-prompt observations in two datasets support limited transfer evidence, not independence for arbitrary prompts.\par
Held-out validation and historical configuration remain Needs Evidence. Appendix C/D uses the first 200 GSM8K samples for search. The released script calls the external `gsm8k` task with a sample limit; the pinned adapter/task submodule is not present in the supplied tree. Exact calibration/evaluation sample identifiers and a disjoint held-out account were not available, so test overlap is not asserted as a demonstrated finding. The paper specifies MOEA/D, while the inspected script selects `NSGAIISampler`; it also has defaults of 20 samples and 100 trials rather than the paper’s 200/200, which could be overridden. Historical commands and search databases are needed to resolve these differences. Appendix D explicitly discloses 500 iterations for one Qwen7B KIVI run; that disclosure is retained.\par
Arithmetic and performance interpretation. From the displayed integer throughput counts in Table 8, the C3.25 gains over KV8 are approximately \(21.2722\%\), \(17.6044\%\), and \(16.7577\%\). These are close to the reported \(21.25\%\), \(17.55\%\), and \(16.79\%\), within plausible rounding of the displayed rates; no substantive arithmetic finding is supported. The six Llama3.1 C3.25 accuracies in Table 5 average \(0.7925\), versus approximately \(0.8038\) for BF16. The interpretation of nearly lossless needs an explicit tolerance and paired uncertainty; the numerical loss is already visible in the table. No significance claim or wholesale empirical failure is inferred.\par
Thirty-two supplied YAML presets were checked arithmetically for nominal average bits. The vanilla quantizer stores integers in `int8`, so that simulation path alone does not demonstrate packed two-bit memory savings. Section 6.4 explicitly uses a KIVI GPU-kernel implementation for throughput; those run records and a complete corresponding kernel package were not available here. The analysis preserves that distinction. LongBench aggregate scoring, run-specific model revisions, raw predictions, and the exact treatment of residual/metadata overhead remain unverified. Separate OpenReview supplements could not be determined because the acquisition metadata reports HTTP 403.\par
\# Conclusion\par
Item | Judgment | Established result | Remaining scope\par
P1 | Major | Concentration alone fails; conditional bound proved | Model-level and empirical guarantees\par
P2 | Minor | Duplicate groups; wrong cost or rejected setup | Corrected searches and historical use\par
P3 | Minor | Zero-remainder slice defect | Backend and historical-run effects\par
Other | Needs Evidence | Arithmetic checks retained | Raw runs, held-out split, scoring\par
The overall supported severity is Major. Scientific decisions remain pending and human reviews are empty. This is an initial scientific review, not acceptance. The theoretical finding is material to the paper’s claimed explanation; it does not establish that offline mixed-precision search cannot be useful.\par
