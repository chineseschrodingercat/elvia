\# 2. Summary and governing assumptions\par
The central demonstrated defect is in Section 3.2’s item-selection information formula. The paper introduces a three-parameter logistic (3PL) response model, but its main-text information expression is the two-parameter special case. An admissible two-item example reverses the selected item. Because this directly affects the proposed adaptive-selection mechanism supporting efficient evaluation, Major is supported under the task’s core-derivation standard.\par
The correct general formula already appears in Appendix B, Eq. (5). This preserves a sound route to the intended mechanism and is explicit counterevidence against alleging that the published experiments necessarily used the wrong expression. Three additional concerns are bounded: a continuous/ordinal response-domain mismatch, an inherited erroneous GSM8K exemplar, and an incorrect MedQA mapping in the released data.\par
For the analytic check, ability theta and difficulty beta are finite real numbers, discrimination alpha is positive, guessing c lies in [0,1), and the response is Bernoulli with success probability p. Item parameters are held fixed when calculating Fisher information for theta. No empirical calibration or hidden training assumption is required for this local identity.\par
\# 3. Problem 1 - The main 3PL selection rule uses the wrong information (Major)\par
Source claim and dependency. Page 3, Section 2.1 and page 18, Eq. (1) define the 3PL model. Page 7, Section 3.2 then assigns each candidate item information alpha\textasciicircum{}2 p(1-p) and selects its maximizer. That is an operational selection prescription, not merely a background mention: it is the mechanism used to explain why adaptive evaluation can use fewer informative items. Section 3.2 does not restrict this prescription to c=0. Appendix Eq. (4), p. 19, explicitly uses the probabilities defined in Eq. (1).\par
\[\begin{aligned}s&=\sigma(\alpha(\theta-\beta)), & p&=c+(1-c)s,\\p'&=\frac{\alpha(p-c)(1-p)}{1-c},\\ I(\theta)&=\frac{(p')^2}{p(1-p)}=\frac{\alpha^2(p-c)^2(1-p)}{(1-c)^2p}.\end{aligned}\] Complete derivation. For y in \{0,1\}, the derivative of y log p + (1-y) log(1-p) is (y-p)p’/[p(1-p)]. Its squared expectation equals (p’)\textasciicircum{}2/[p(1-p)] because the Bernoulli variance is p(1-p). Differentiating the stated 3PL probability yields the displayed expression. The main-text simplification follows for c=0; it does not hold for general c.\par
Counterexample. Take two distinct candidate items with theta=0, alpha\_A=alpha\_B=1, beta\_A=beta\_B=log(2), c\_A=1/4, and c\_B=1/10. Thus s\_A=s\_B=1/3. Both discriminations are strictly positive, both guessing probabilities are strictly between zero and one, all parameters are finite, and the paper permits item-specific guessing parameters. All operative conditions are satisfied.\par
Quantity | Item A | Item B\par
Success probability p | 1/2 | 2/5\par
Derivative p’ | 1/6 | 1/5\par
Main-text information | 1/4 = 0.25 | 6/25 = 0.24\par
Correct Fisher information | 1/9 = 0.111111... | 1/6 = 0.166666...\par
The main prescription selects A; the correct maximum-information rule selects B. A supplies only two-thirds of B’s Fisher information in this example. This is an analytic identity failure with an exact finite witness, independently replayed using rational arithmetic. It does not purport to prove a particular change in aggregate benchmark rankings.\par
Alternative reading and repair. Interpreting Section 3.2 as an unstated 2PL illustration makes its formula correct, but that restriction is absent and excludes the nonzero guessing factors emphasized elsewhere. Appendix B, p. 20, Eq. (5) already states the correct general expression. Replacing the main expression with Eq. (5), or explicitly restricting that passage to c=0, repairs exactly this selection identity. The corrected identity has been analytically checked; experiment-level implementation and results have not been repaired or reproduced. Ease of this correction does not remove the demonstrated damage to the core prescription. Evidence: E1, E2, E3.\par
\# 4. Problem 2 - Raw continuous scores are assigned an ordinal likelihood (Minor)\par
Source claim. Page 3 describes the graded response model (GRM) as accommodating continuous BLEU scores. Page 18, Appendix B, calls y continuous on [0,M], but sets P(Y=m) equal to P(Y>=m)-P(Y>=m+1). For an arbitrary response variable the difference is P(m<=Y<m+1), not its point probability. In a nondegenerate continuous model, a point has zero probability while such an interval can have positive probability.\par
Counterevidence and scope. The term polytomous and the finite ordered threshold vector strongly indicate that an ordinal model was intended. For already-discrete categories the stated identity is correct. No discretization step is stated in the inspected text. The supported defect is therefore the claimed direct use on raw continuous scores; no effect on the binary examples or Problem 1 is established.\par
Scoped correction. Map the raw score explicitly into ordered categories 0,...,M. Define S\_0(theta)=1, S\_(M+1)(theta)=0, and p\_m(theta)=S\_m(theta)-S\_(m+1)(theta), with positive discrimination and ordered thresholds. The category masses are nonnegative and telescope to one. This repairs the categorical model, without claiming to preserve all information in the original continuous score. Evidence: E1, E2.\par
\# 5. Problem 3 - A known erroneous GSM8K item is used as a clean exemplar (Minor)\par
Source claim. Appendix C, p. 24, interprets GSM8K ID7876 as a high-difficulty/low-guessing item whose properties arise from complex multistep calculation. The item specifies a 900-watt air conditioner and a reduction in runtime BY five hours per day for 30 days, but prints 81 as the answer.\par
\[E_{\rm saved}=0.9\,{\rm kW}\times5\,{\rm h/day}\times30\,{\rm day}=135\,{\rm kWh}.\qquad81=0.9(8-5)30.\] The printed value is the remaining energy use under the stated wording; it would be energy saved if runtime were reduced TO five hours. The source also confuses power and energy units. Hence this item is not clean evidence that low guessing/high estimated difficulty is caused by calculation complexity. The fitted parameter values under a corrected scoring key are unknown.\par
Historical attribution. The same text and wrong key occur in upstream GSM8K test item 404 (zero-based index 403). Kermittt publicly reported the issue in openai/grade-school-math issue \#20 on 4 December 2023; the fetched source still retains it. This is a known-issue reproduction and a current local interpretation defect, not a newly discovered original error by these authors.\par
Correction of the arithmetic to 135 kWh is verified. Interpreting corrected item parameters would require rescoring and refitting. Alternatively changing BY to TO repairs the answer only by changing the task. Neither empirical repair was performed. Evidence: E1, E2, E4.\par
\# 6. Problem 4 - Released MedQA item mapping contains GSM8K questions (Minor)\par
Source claim and complete static check. Appendix C and the repository README offer intermediate item features. At the pinned commit, both GSM8K\_irt\_feature.json and MedQA\_irt\_feature.json contain 1,000 records. An independent comparison found all 1,000 question texts, IDs, and answers identical across the corresponding records; all 1,000 triples of alpha/beta/c differ. The MedQA file therefore pairs medical-labeled features with GSM8K question/answer metadata. For example, id8131 asks about cooking times for rice, beans, and pork in both files.\par
This invalidates the released mapping as a means to verify the MedQA item-specific analysis. Different numerical parameter triples remain consistent with an accidental metadata-copying error after otherwise valid fitting. The release alone does not establish which data generated the published figures. No demonstrated link supports upgrading this release defect to core empirical damage.\par
No mapping repair is claimed. A defensible repair requires original MedQA identifiers/text, a checked match to parameter rows, and provenance for the response matrix used in fitting. Evidence: E5, including the complete pinned tree, comparison script, and results.\par
