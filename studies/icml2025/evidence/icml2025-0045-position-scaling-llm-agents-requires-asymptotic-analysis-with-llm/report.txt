# 3. F1 (Major): component speedup is substituted for total debugging speedup

Claim and dependency. Table 1 (p. 4) summarizes a total improvement of Theta(b k^2); Section 3.2.3 and Eq. (11) (p. 6) use the same order to motivate scaling to large codebases. This is one of the paper’s three concrete demonstrations of its central analytical approach.

Grant the paper’s assumptions: k independent functions, l tokens per function, n=k l, b bugs with at most one per function, perfect specifications, reliable QA and debugging agents, retained conversation history in the naive system, and model sizes m_q and m_d. Let b>=1. Equations (7) and (10) imply:

\[\begin{aligned}
 C_{\mathrm{naive}}&=\Theta(m_q b^2kl+m_d b^2k^2l^2),\\
 C_{\mathrm{focused}}&=\Theta(m_qkl+m_dbl^2),\\
 R_{\mathrm{total}}&=\Theta\!\left(\frac{b^2k(m_q+m_dkl)}{m_qk+m_dbl}\right).
\end{aligned}\] The component ratios are correctly Theta(b^2) for QA and Theta(b k^2) for the debugger. A component’s ratio does not automatically equal the ratio of the sums. In particular, the focused algorithm still scans every function.

Decisive admissible family. Fix b=l=m_q=m_d=1 (normalized constant model sizes) and let k grow. Then n=k>=b and the debugging model is no smaller than QA. The naive cost is Theta(k^2); the focused QA scan costs Theta(k), while its single repair costs Theta(1). Thus the total improvement is Theta(k), not the Theta(k^2) instance of the displayed claim. This is an asymptotic factor-k discrepancy, not a hidden-constant difference. With l=log_2 k and the other quantities fixed, the discrepancy persists: the total improvement is Theta(k log k)=o(k^2).

Counterevidence and limits. The prose says debugger dominance is likely, and the per-debugger improvement is valid. Both are retained. However, n>=b and the intuitive difficulty of debugging do not entail debugger dominance in the focused system. No observed industrial-code experiment is refuted; this is an internal cost-aggregation failure in the motivating example.

Repair. State the general ratio above, or explicitly restrict the regime. A sufficient condition is m_d b l=Omega(m_q k), which makes the focused debugger at least a constant share of total cost; together with b<=k and l>=1 it recovers Theta(b k^2). Explain the intended joint growth of bug count, function count, function length and model sizes. This narrows the quantified core result without removing the potential advantage of decomposition.

Evidence: independent_formal_checks.py and independent_formal_results.json give representative exact operation counts and the sparse-bug families; primitive_framework_memo.json records an independent assumption challenge. These are reviewer-created mathematical checks, not author code or measured LLM runtimes.

# 4. F2 (Major): the displayed local evolutionary loop need not terminate

Claim and dependency. Section 3.3.1 (p. 6) permits an arbitrary initial solution. Section 3.3.2 gives expected local cost Theta(m_u k l^2), and Eq. (14) and Table 1 give improvement Theta(2^k k). The local pseudocode initializes i=0 and increments i only after a strict fitness improvement. Direct visual inspection of p. 4 confirms that indentation.

The following exact counterexample grants independent blocks and calls, a reliable mutation role, free decomposition and evaluation, and the stated primitive cost. It even ensures that every selected block actually changes.

Let k=2 and l=1. Tokens A and B are correct; a and b are incorrect. Fitness is the number of correct blocks. Mutate a token uniformly to one of the two outcomes below:

Input token | Outcome 1 (probability 1/2) | Outcome 2 (probability 1/2)

A | B | a

B | A | b

a | A | b

b | B | a

Every mutation alters the selected token, and each output is correct with probability exactly 1/2. Take the permitted nonoptimal initial state x=(A,a), with fitness 1<2 and index i=0.

Local proposal | Fitness | Printed-loop action

\((B,a)\) | 1 | Reject: no strict improvement; x and i unchanged.

\((a,a)\) | 0 | Reject: fitness decreases; x and i unchanged.

These are all possible local transitions. The state (x=(A,a),i=0) is therefore closed and nonterminal. The algorithm never reaches the incorrect second block, terminates with probability zero, and has infinite expected local cost. This is an exhaustive state argument, not a simulation timeout.

This failure extends to every k>=2: initialize x=(A,a,...,a), so the first block is correct and at least one later block is incorrect. Every local proposal at index zero is neutral or worse, and the state remains fixed. Thus the counterexample is an asymptotic family, not a finite-size exception. Under the independent kernel, global success has conditional probability 2^(-k) at every attempt, giving expected 2^k calls from a nonoptimum.

Under the same kernel, global mutation produces the optimal pair (B,A) with probability 1/4. Every unsuccessful proposal has fitness at most 1 and is rejected, so each attempt restarts from the same state. Its expected hitting time is exactly four calls. The advertised local advantage therefore fails on an allowed instance.

Why Major. The finite-cost guarantee and dramatic separation are central analytical evidence for the paper’s position. The defect directly invalidates the printed algorithm’s guarantee under its declared initialization scope. Its small repair size does not make that dependency peripheral. The finding does not say local search or decomposition is generally ineffective.

Repair and scope. Add an explicit test that skips already-correct blocks, stating how block correctness is obtained and whether its cost is counted. The paper’s boundary-decomposition oracle alone does not supply this test. For r initially incorrect blocks, a repaired procedure with such a test needs 2r expected mutation calls and has mutation cost Theta(m_u r l^2); Theta(m_u k l^2) is then a worst-case order when r=Theta(k), and an upper bound for all r<=k. Alternatively, impose and justify all-incorrect initialization, including its construction cost and capabilities.

The global 2^k calculation also presumes a nonoptimal initial solution and suitable independent fair correctness across blocks and attempts; marginal 1/2 probabilities alone are insufficient. The counterexample already grants the stronger intended independence. The phrase super-exponential on p. 7 should be changed: k 2^k is exponential times a polynomial in k. This terminology point is recorded with F2, not counted as a second finding.

Evidence: independent_source_p4.png, independent_formal_checks.py, independent_formal_results.json, independent_formal_memo.json, and primitive_repair_limits.json. The proof script enumerates the complete transition kernel and uses exact rational success probabilities.

# 5. Qualifications that should not be inflated into further Major findings

Routing regime. Retain Eq. (2)’s delegator term. With the allowed family m_s=1 and m_g=m_d=k, the ratio is Theta(k n/(k+n)); when k=n^2 it grows as Theta(n), not Theta(k). A sufficient general condition for the stated linear-in-k improvement is m_d=O(m_s n), given m_g=Theta(k m_s). The phrase as n grows supports a charitable large-input or sequential-limit reading. Clarify that regime rather than treating the conditional example as wholly disproved.

Task-count citation. Kaplan et al. (2020), especially pp. 1-4 and Section 1.2, study cross-entropy loss versus model parameters, data and compute. Those results do not establish minimum model size proportional to the number of distinct tasks. Present m_g=Theta(k m_s) as an additional hypothesis for a task family. The reviewed paper already explicitly assumes this relation and questions its usefulness in Section 5.1.

Routing analogy. The Mixtral primary paper (Jiang et al., 2024, Table 1 and Section 2.1) specifies width 4096, eight experts and 32 layers. The calculation 4096*8=32,768 gate weights is per layer; all 32 gates total 1,048,576. Its experts are feedforward sub-blocks and its routing occurs for each token at each layer. Footnote 1 should distinguish these units from a complete task-level LLM delegator. Corrected accounting still supports a qualitatively inexpensive router.

Resource interpretation. Under Definition 2.2, generating t tokens from an n-token prompt has assigned cost m[nt+t(t-1)/2]. A standard billed-input/output expression has form alpha*n+beta*t for fixed model and price coefficients. The current official Anthropic documentation distinguishes those billing categories; it is not a historical reconstruction of 2025 prices. A deployment-specific mapping is required before treating the primitive sum as a monetary bill. This does not contradict derivations that explicitly adopt the paper’s mn resource definition.

Declared idealizations. Sections 5.1-5.6 explicitly identify linear-cost and task-scaling assumptions, reliable role execution, independent components, oracle decomposition, omitted parallelism and deferred learning costs as limitations or future research. The two Major findings preserve those assumptions. No additional finding is based on ordinary LLM mistakes, actual quadratic attention, absence of a new benchmark, or unimplemented future research. Claims about future sentience and suffering remain proposals, not established empirical results of this paper.
