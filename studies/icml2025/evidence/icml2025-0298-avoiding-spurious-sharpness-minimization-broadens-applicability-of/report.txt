Abstract: The assessment is Major for an invalid quantitative derivation directly used to motivate the preconditioned method: Appendix B mixes trace normalizations and gives an incorrect matrix-product ratio. A corrected, non-strict comparison is proved here and preserves the intended trend under the toy model’s assumptions. The core first-order Functional-SAM decomposition remains valid. Separate Minor findings concern reversed gradient/auxiliary unpacking in the illustrative implementation and the radius needed for Angle-SAM to match SAM. The reported language-model improvements are not refuted or reproduced; causal transfer from the matrix model, full implementation provenance, and some experimental details remain unresolved.

# Definitions and checked central derivation

Write \(g=\nabla_\theta L\), \(u=g/\|g\|\), and let \(J=\partial F/\partial\theta\) have outputs as rows. For a smooth loss composed with \(F\), \[H_L=H_G+H_F,\qquad
 H_G=J^\top H_\ell J,\qquad
 H_F=\sum_a(\partial_{F_a}L)\nabla_\theta^2F_a,\] including the paper’s sample averaging. At nonzero \(g\) and under the smoothness needed for Taylor expansion, \[\nabla L(\theta+\rho u)
 =g+\rho(H_G+H_F)u+O(\rho^2).\] Holding the output-loss cotangent \(v=\nabla_F L(\theta)\) fixed while perturbing only the Jacobian gives \[J(\theta+\rho u)^\top v
 =g+\rho H_Fu+O(\rho^2),\] which verifies the first-order interpretation of Equation (11). These are positive gradient conventions; an optimizer applies descent.

A direct smooth binary cross-entropy check with logits \((\theta^2,0)\), first-class target, and \(\theta=1\) gives \(g=-0.53788284\), \(H_G=0.78644773\), and \(H_F=-0.53788284\). For this example the displayed Functional-SAM expansion is exact because the Jacobian is affine in \(\theta\). This confirms the intended isolation of the functional term locally, without claiming that it always decreases total sharpness or improves generalization.

Equations (4) and (7) omit a common \(\rho\) relative to \(\mathrm{SP}=\rho\|g\|\); the correct derivative is \(\nabla\mathrm{SP}=\rho H_Lu\). This is treated as a scaling clarification: it cancels from Equation (9)’s normalized composition if used consistently and does not invalidate the explicit-\(\rho\) update above. Also, the squared-norm terms in Equation (9) sum to one only together with the cross term; individual terms need not be probabilities or fractions bounded by one.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The preconditioning ratio uses inconsistent trace normalization).  Assessment: Major; a core motivating derivation is invalid.

## Original claim and dependency

Section 4.2, p. 5, cites Appendix B.1 to argue that inverse-GGN preconditioning amplifies the functional path relative to the logit path, motivating preconditioned SAM and its combination with Functional-SAM. Appendix B.1, p. 16, assumes symmetric invertible, freely independent matrices \(A,B\) and a random unit vector \(v\). It defines \(w=A^{-1}v\) and compares \[r_1=\frac{\mathbb E\|Bv\|^2}{\mathbb E\|Av\|^2},
 \qquad
 r_2=\frac{\mathbb E\|Bw\|^2}{\mathbb E\|Aw\|^2}.\] Equation (14)’s factor \(1/N\) uses ordinary trace. But Equation (13) factors ordinary traces without \(1/N\), Equation (16) sets \(\mathbb E\|Aw\|^2=1/N\), and Equation (17) gives an incompatible product of trace moments, followed by a strict \(r_2>r_1\) conclusion.

## All-assumptions certificate

Take \(N=2\), \(A=B=I_2\), and \(v\) uniformly distributed on the unit circle. These matrices are symmetric and invertible, are valid degenerate random matrices, and their scalar algebras satisfy freeness: every centered scalar polynomial is zero. The same construction works as a sequence for every \(N\), so it is not a finite-size approximation issue.

Here \(w=v\) exactly and \[\mathbb E\|Av\|^2=\mathbb E\|Bv\|^2
 =\mathbb E\|Aw\|^2=\mathbb E\|Bw\|^2=1.\] Thus \(r_1=r_2=1\). Equation (13), at powers one, instead gives \(2=4\); Equation (16) gives \(1=1/2\); and Equation (17)’s middle trace equals two while its final factorized expression equals four. Most directly, \(Aw=v\) proves that the printed \(1/N\) norm is wrong for every invertible \(A\) and unit \(v\).

This is not only an equality-edge case for the numerical ratio. With \(A=Q\operatorname{diag}(1,2)Q^\top\), \(Q\) an independent uniform random rotation, and \(B=I_2\), scalar \(B\) is still free from \(A\). For independent uniform \(v\), \[r_1=\frac1{(1+4)/2}=\frac25,\qquad
 r_2=\frac{1+1/4}{2}=\frac58.\] The middle and final expressions printed in Equation (17) give \(5/4\) and \(5/2\), respectively, instead of \(5/8\). The calculations are recorded in `check.json` and follow exactly from isotropy.

## Verified corrected statement

Assume \(v\) is independent and isotropic on the unit sphere, the necessary moments are finite, and the stated free mixed-moment factorization holds with normalized trace \(\tau(C)=\operatorname{tr}(C)/N\). Set \[\alpha=\mathbb E\tau(A^2),\quad
 \beta=\mathbb E\tau(B^2),\quad
 \gamma=\mathbb E\tau(A^{-2}).\] Then \[\mathbb E\|Aw\|^2=1,\qquad
 r_1=\frac{\beta}{\alpha},\qquad
 r_2=\beta\gamma,\qquad
 \frac{r_2}{r_1}=\alpha\gamma\ge1.\] Indeed, cyclicity and normalized factorization give \(\mathbb E\tau(A^{-1}B^2A^{-1})=\beta\gamma\). Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the absolute eigenvalue and its reciprocal, with an eigenvalue drawn uniformly and averaged over matrix randomness, gives \(\alpha\gamma\ge1\). Equality is possible, including \(A=aI\); strict inequality needs nonconstant squared spectral scale.

With ordinary trace, the corrected ratio is \[r_2=\frac{\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(B^2)\,
                \mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(A^{-2})}{N^2}.\] Interpreting every printed trace as normalized does not reconcile all equations, because Equation (14) and the unit-vector norm in Equation (16) then remain wrong.

## Impact and counterevidence

The wrong quantitative derivation is directly invoked for a core proposed method, supporting Major under the assigned calibration. The correction preserves nondecrease in the idealized model; it does not establish failure of preconditioning in Transformers. The paper explicitly acknowledges that actual gradients and Hessian components do not satisfy its toy calculation exactly. Adam’s diagonal second-moment preconditioner, normalized perturbations, and coupled training dynamics require additional justification or measurements. This report neither transfers the corrected theorem to those settings automatically nor claims that the empirical improvements disappear.

Problem 2 (The illustrative implementation reverses gradient and auxiliary outputs).  Assessment: Minor; implementation illustration only.

Listing 1, p. 17, calls `grad(loss_fn,hax_aux=True)` and binds the result as \[\texttt{dL\_dlogits,\ dL\_dtheta = grad(...)(params)}.\] The consulted official JAX API uses `has_aux` and returns the parameter gradient before auxiliary data [Retained latex: \cite{jax}]. Thus correcting the keyword alone still reverses the two spaces: the logit cotangent is used to perturb parameters, and the parameter gradient is supplied to the output VJP.

A finite calculation shows that this is substantive even when shapes happen to agree. Take scalar \(F(\theta)=\theta^2\), loss \(\ell(z)=z^2/2\), and \(\theta=2\), with the auxiliary value defined as \(\partial\ell/\partial z\). The correct pair is \((16,4)\). Both entries normalize to positive one, so the perturbed parameter is \(2+\rho\) even after the mistaken swap. The intended VJP is \[4\cdot2(2+\rho)=16+8\rho,\] whereas the swapped cotangent yields \(16\cdot2(2+\rho)=64+32\rho\). At \(\rho=0.1\) these are 16.8 and 67.2. This uses a smooth generic loss accepted by the illustration and tests the return-order issue without author-code execution. With unequal logit and parameter structures the mismatch can instead prevent evaluation.

The local correction is \[\texttt{dL\_dtheta,\ dL\_dlogits =
 grad(loss\_fn,has\_aux=True)(params)},\] provided the auxiliary value is explicitly the unperturbed logit-loss cotangent. The remaining VJP then matches Equation (11). This API/formula correspondence is verified statically; a complete runnable implementation is not certified. JAX was absent from both checked runtimes, and the helper and loss wrapper were missing. The illustration is not proven to be the code that produced historical runs, and the mathematical update itself survives.

Problem 3 (Angle-SAM requires a radius rescaling to recover SAM).  Assessment: Minor; optional extension.

Appendix D, p. 18, states that \(\phi=\pi/4\) recovers SAM to first order in \(\rho\). Equation (20) instead gives \[G_{\rm angle}=g+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt2}(H_G+H_F)u+O(\rho^2),\] whereas standard SAM has coefficient \(\rho\). Their difference is generally order \(\rho\), not order \(\rho^2\).

For an admissible binary cross-entropy example with logits \((\theta,0)\), first-class target, and \(\theta=0\), one has \(g=-1/2\), \(u=-1\), \(H_G=1/4\), \(H_F=0\). Writing \(\sigma\) for the logistic function, \[G_{\rm SAM}=-\sigma(\rho),\qquad
 G_{\rm angle}=-\frac12+
       \frac{-\sigma(\rho)+1/2}{\sqrt2}.\] At \(\rho=0.1\) these equal \(-0.5249791875\) and \(-0.5176629529\). Moreover, \[\lim_{\rho\to0}\frac{G_{\rm angle}-G_{\rm SAM}}{\rho}
 =\frac{1-1/\sqrt2}{4}\ne0.\] The corrected statement is that Angle-SAM at radius \(\rho\) matches SAM at radius \(\rho/\sqrt2\) to first order; equivalently use Angle-SAM radius \(\sqrt2\rho\) to match SAM radius \(\rho\). This does not assert exact finite-radius equality. The \(\phi=0\) Functional-SAM endpoint is unaffected. The paper presents this interpolation as future work rather than the method used in its main experiments, so the supported impact is local.

# Empirical scope and unresolved interpretation

The first-order decomposition does not alone identify the cause of a cross-domain performance difference. The paper measures Hessian-vector composition during AdamW training and uses its imbalance as a hypothesis motivating interventions. Those interventions and the reported loss improvements are useful evidence, but normalized composition does not establish an optimizer-independent causal mechanism. The paper itself allows that Functional-SAM may be inappropriate in other settings.

Tables 2–3 report improvements of preconditioned Functional-SAM over AdamW across the listed scales. The five-seed standard deviations and the single-seed exception for the largest Chinchilla-style run are disclosed. The 10,000-step token count is consistent with \(256\cdot512\cdot10{,}000=1{,}310{,}720{,}000\) tokens. The principal comparisons use equal steps; Section 8 explicitly acknowledges roughly twice AdamW’s gradient computation. This is not an undisclosed equal-compute claim.

Table 4 also shows that ordinary SAM can have the smallest maximum Hessian eigenvalue while worse validation loss, so lower scalar sharpness alone is not sufficient to rank generalization. No direct measured loss or Hessian value was reproduced here. Smaller untested SAM radii, the exact norm/second-moment convention, warm-up handling, and seed-paired uncertainty remain outside the available record.

A concrete reproducibility ambiguity remains: Section 5.3 says the common learning rate for Tables 6–7 is \(10^{-4}\), whereas both appendix captions state \(10^{-3}\). Without configurations, neither value can be selected as the true historical one. The preconditioned variant used in the principal tables is not fully implemented in the embedded listing. These are Needs Evidence items and do not independently establish a false experimental ranking. No later correction was established from the available frozen sources.

# Conclusion and state

The overall assessment is Major, confined to the invalid core preconditioning derivation, with its corrected toy-model result explicitly preserved. The two Minor findings affect an implementation illustration and an optional interpolation claim. Functional-SAM’s checked first-order decomposition remains intact; empirical performance and broader causal explanations are not certified by this review.
