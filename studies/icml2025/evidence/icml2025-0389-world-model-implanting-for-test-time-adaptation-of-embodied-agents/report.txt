Abstract: The theoretical bound used to justify prototype retrieval confuses geometric covering with transport of probability mass. An exact k-center example with distinct points violates the displayed Wasserstein bound by a wide margin. A weighted prototype measure restores the bound, but changes the retrieval rule. Selective static inspection also finds an unmasked cross-sequence attention path in the released training implementation. Local numerical summaries need correction. The original adaptation results and retrieval ablations remain empirical evidence; their historical execution has not been reproduced.

# Problem 1: covering radius does not control equal-mass transport

Equation (4) (p. 4), proved in Appendix A.1 (p. 11), claims \[W(p_i,p_j)\le W(E_i,E_j)+2\rho,\] where \(p_i,p_j\) are optimal \(k\)-center subsets and \(\rho\) bounds the distance of every embedding to a nearest center. The proof uses \(W(E_i,p_i)\le\rho\). That inference needs the prototype mass at each center to equal the mass assigned to it. It does not hold for uniform empirical measures on the center sets.

A finite counterexample avoids repeated-point conventions. Put \[S=\{0,.0001,.0002,\ldots,.0100\},\quad
 E_1=S\cup\{1\},\quad E_2=S\cup\{2\},\quad k=2.\] Each set contains 102 distinct real points. The unique optimal center sets are \(p_1=\{.005,1\}\) and \(p_2=\{.005,2\}\), with \(\rho=.005\). To verify optimality, an isolated outlier must have its own center for any radius below \(.005\); the remaining center must cover both endpoints of \(S\), requiring radius at least \(.005\), attained only at \(.005\). Independent enumeration of all center pairs confirms this argument.

For uniform empirical measures, ordered one-dimensional transport gives \[W_1(E_1,E_2)=1/102,\qquad W_1(p_1,p_2)=1/2.\] Thus the asserted inequality would require \[.5\le 1/102+.01=.0198039,\] which is false. This uses exact centers, so an approximation algorithm cannot explain it away. The example also extends to the reported \(k=15\): append the common isolated points \(4,5,\ldots,16\) to both full sets and center sets. Then there are 115 data points and 15 centers, still with radius \(.005\), but the two sides are \(1/15=.0666667\) and \(1/115+.01=.0186957\).

The frozen `TensorSet.dist` in `wormi/modules/utils.py` calls `scipy.stats.wasserstein_distance_nd` without mass weights. This supports the equal-mass reading rather than supplying the missing cluster masses. `ModelStore` in `wormi/model_store.py` uses KMeans centers rather than the paper’s subset-constrained \(k\)-center optimization. This is an additional source-to-description discrepancy, but it is not needed for the counterexample. The inspected scripts do not establish that this release generated the original retrieval experiments.

## A precise weighted repair

Let \(\mu_i\) be the empirical data measure, let \(Q_i\) assign each point to a center within \(\rho_i\), and set \(\nu_i=(Q_i)_\#\mu_i\). The coupling \((X,Q_i(X))\) proves \(W_1(\mu_i,\nu_i)\le\rho_i\). The triangle inequality then yields the stronger two-sided statement \[\big|W_1(\nu_i,\nu_j)-W_1(\mu_i,\mu_j)\big|
 \le\rho_i+\rho_j.\] In the two-center example the masses are \((101/102,1/102)\), and the repaired prototype distance is exactly \(1/102\). The same argument works for finite-order Wasserstein distances under the corresponding moment assumptions because every transported point moves by at most its radius.

This repair requires retaining cluster masses. It does not validate uniform-center distances, and it changes the intentional suppression of frequent objects described on p. 4. If equal weighting is a desired modeling choice, its relationship to full empirical transport needs a different statement. Even the repaired bound alone does not ensure identical top-\(K\) retrieval without separation between candidate distances.

This is Major: the false bound is the theoretical justification for substituting prototypes in a central retrieval component. Its failure does not imply that the prototypes select poor models on VirtualHome or invalidate the attention mechanism. Table A.11’s near-equal success rates for full and prototype retrieval are useful empirical counterevidence, but cannot prove the universal inequality.

# Problem 2: the released attention path is not causal over training tokens

In `wormi/modules/hooks.py`, lines 150–187, the base hook calls its reasoning-level cross-attention with query, key and value but no `attn_mask`, `key_padding_mask`, or causal flag. The stored `world_mask` is converted to floating point but not passed into the call. In the world-wise subclass, lines 423–459, world representations are combined separately at each sequence position, then returned as a full sequence for this outer attention.

The model’s forward path supplies the same token sequence to the world models and the main model. The training wrapper uses completion-only causal-LM collation, and the VirtualHome chat conversion includes the assistant action in that sequence. Consequently a hidden state at an earlier prediction position can attend through the implanted path to a later world-model state that already includes a target action token. A causal mask inside each frozen language model does not prevent this new cross-position path.

The mechanism can be checked without a language model: with two scalar values \((0,z)\) and equal unmasked attention logits at the first query, the first output is \(z/2\). Changing only the future value from zero to two changes that output from zero to one; a causal first-position output remains zero. This illustrates the dependence permitted by the code, not the numerical behavior of a trained WorMI checkpoint.

The defect is assessed as Minor at the released-source level because the historical training implementation and effect size are unknown. It warrants a causal mask aligned to token positions, together with padding treatment and a future-token invariance check on the actual training path. Such a check was not run here, and no repaired training system is certified. Prefix-only autoregressive inference does not supply unavailable future tokens; the issue is the mismatch with full-sequence teacher forcing, not a claim that evaluation directly reads unseen answers.

# Problem 3: local performance summaries do not match their tables

Several discrepancies can be resolved using the printed numbers alone. In Table 1, the VirtualHome unseen-task/unseen-scene success-rate difference is \(66.12-45.71=20.41\) percentage points. The introduction calls this an average zero-shot improvement, whereas the equal-weight mean over all three reported VirtualHome conditions is \(17.2767\) points. The \(20.41\) value should be tied to the stated hard condition rather than an unspecified average.

The ALFWorld pending-step reduction in Section 4.1 is \(23.86-20.18=3.68\), or \(15.42\%\) relative to the baseline, rather than the stated \(18.23\%\) (which uses the new method as denominator). In the compound-attention ablation, Table 3(b) gives a reduction versus WorMI-ADD of \(17.43-15.17=2.26\) steps, not \(1.79\). The long-horizon goal-conditioned success difference in Table 6 is \(49.35-39.22=10.13\) points, not \(13.13\).

These are Minor reporting errors: all corrected comparisons retain the direction of advantage. The few-shot mean gain is \(26.5867\) points from the rounded per-shot values, consistent with approximately \(26.58\) using reported aggregate rounding; that comparison is not challenged. The intervals in Table 2 cannot be independently validated from aggregate means alone.

# Additional checks and limits on interpretation

The zero-shot protocol explicitly excludes target-domain demonstrations during training, while few-shot adaptation supplies 1, 5 or 10 such demonstrations. World models and the composition module undergo substantial source-domain training. Thus zero-shot refers to target-domain data, not to an entirely untrained policy. No split leakage was established by the materials inspected.

The two-level attention shapes are coherent when world models use aligned token positions: world-wise weighting yields one value vector per position, and the summed keys have the same sequence length. The temporal masking issue is separate from this dimensional compatibility. The apparent training callback that sets exposed parameters trainable is not evidence of unfreezing the entire LLM: `WorMI.named_parameters` filters internally stored model parameters, which were separately frozen.

Figures 4 and A.6 show changing world-attention weights. They support examples of selective attention, not causal identification of every model’s knowledge. Figure 5 intentionally adds pertinent models; it is not evidence that arbitrary additions always help. Table 5 and the adverse-model results expressly show degradation in other regimes. Removing a module demonstrates behavioral dependence; it does not certify erasure of all domain information from the learned composer or base model. The paper’s deployment and unlearning language is kept within those experimental limits here.
