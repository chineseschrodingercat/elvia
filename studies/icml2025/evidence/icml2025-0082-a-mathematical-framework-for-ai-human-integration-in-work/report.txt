# 2. Definitions and dependencies

Subskill abilities X lie in [0,1], errors are zeta=1-X, and the job error Err is a monotone composition of skill, task and job aggregators. Job success is P=Pr(Err<=tau). The independent-noise results assume independent subskill draws and an L-Lipschitz Err in the l1 metric. The mean job error is Err_avg. MinDer is intended to lower-bound the magnitude of its decreasing derivative in the varied ability parameter, and MaxDisp bounds the sum of squared centered subgaussian MGF proxies.

Parameter extrema must range over the admissible ability-profile domain; for a constant mean/probability this is [0,1]. The printed notation inf/sup over all mu>=0 is broader than those families permit. Any useful threshold statement also needs positive MinDer, an admissible critical point, and shifted parameter values inside the domain. We state this domain reading explicitly when illustrating Theorem C.1; the direct Theorem C.2 counterexample does not depend on it.

The logical chain is: input concentration in C.2 -> Eq. (2) on p. 18 -> threshold separation in C.1 -> general-noise merging and productivity statements C.5/C.6. The polynomial calculation supplies the sensitivity denominator for an advertised alternative-profile extension. Appendix placement does not make these steps peripheral.

# 3. Problem 1 (Major): the concentration inequality is false

Source claim: Theorem C.2, p. 17, defines ||X||_psi2 as the smallest a satisfying E exp(beta X)<=exp(beta^2 a^2/2), then claims each one-sided tail of a 1-Lipschitz function G of independent inputs is at most exp(-2t^2/S), where S=sum_j||X_j-E X_j||_psi2^2. The proof of C.1 explicitly applies this formula in Eq. (2), p. 18.

Complete counterexample: choose T=1, G(x)=x, X~Bernoulli(1/2), and t=1/2. G is globally 1-Lipschitz on R, independence is automatic, X is even bounded in [0,1], and E X=1/2. The centered MGF is cosh(beta/2)<=exp(beta^2/8), so the proxy is at most 1/2. Comparing second derivatives at beta=0 forces its square to be at least Var(X)=1/4; hence the minimum proxy is exactly 1/2.

\[\Pr\{X\ge1\}=\frac12,\qquad \exp\!\left[-\frac{2(1/2)^2}{(1/2)^2}\right]=e^{-2}=0.1353352832<\frac12.\] The lower tail at X<=0 gives the same contradiction. This is an analytic finite-distribution refutation, not a simulation. Replacing >= by > would not save the statement: take t just below 1/2. The issue is not an alternative convention for a norm because the paper supplies its exact MGF definition.

The cited primary reference, Kontorovich (2014), uses the subgaussian diameter of the symmetrized distance epsilon|X-X’| and a different exponential constant. Its Theorem 1, physical p. 4, does not state C.2’s substitution of the centered-input proxy. The relevant source PDF and hash are retained.

The advertised C.1 width also fails on the intended admissible-domain reading. Take n=m=1; one task containing the only skill; h=max and g=f=identity; decision ability X1~Bernoulli(mu),0<=mu<=1; action ability X2=1 deterministically. The noise family can be written X1=mu+sigma(B_mu-mu) with fixed sigma=1, B_mu~Bernoulli(mu); sigma2=0. All values lie in [0,1], the abilities are constant across difficulty, the decision family is stochastically increasing, and the two subskill draws are independent. Then Err=1-X1, L=1, Err_avg=1-mu, MinDer=1, and MaxDisp=1/4 over this family. Hoeffding’s centered MGF bound gives proxy squared at most 1/4 for every Bernoulli(mu), and equality holds at mu=1/2; hence the supremum is exactly 1/4.

\[\tau=\frac9{10},\quad\theta=\frac25,\quad\mu_c=\frac1{10},\quad\gamma=\frac12\sqrt{\log(5/2)}=0.4786153810.\] At mu=7/12=0.5833333, mu>=mu_c+gamma=0.5786153810, but actual P=Pr(X1=1)=7/12<3/5=1-theta. Thus the upper guarantee fails. A second lower-branch example is recorded in the calculation JSON. If one insists on an unrestricted nonnegative parameter domain, the source must first define valid profiles there; that domain ambiguity does not rescue C.2 and is not concealed in this C.1 example.

Impact: this invalidates a substantial derivation of the central general-noise phase-transition guarantee and undermines downstream claims that use its unmodified constants. It does not by itself refute every bounded-uniform special case, the existence of threshold phenomena, or the observed simulations. In particular, in the positive-width, nondegenerate case, the main bounded-uniform statement can use ordinary bounded differences: each coordinate range is at most sigma_l, giving a valid tail bound with denominator L^2 n(sigma1^2+sigma2^2); its stated width then has sufficient slack. The general-noise extension needs a distinct repair.

# 4. Problem 2 (Major): polynomial-profile sensitivity uses the wrong derivative

Source claim: Appendix C.2, pp. 20-21, considers the mean-zero scaled-uniform polynomial ability profile 1-s^beta and mean error aggregation. The printed formula has absolute-value bars: it claims |d Err_avg/d beta1|=(beta1/(2n))sum_j s_j1^(beta1-1). The objection is to this magnitude, not to a lost sign in text extraction. The paper then uses it to claim a zero derivative at beta1=0 and a lower bound (1/(4n))sum_j s_j1 on beta1 in [1/2,2].

Because the noise has mean zero for every parameter value, the correct expectation and derivative, for interior difficulties 0<s_jl<1, are:

\[\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac1{2n}\sum_j(s_{j1}^{\beta_1}+s_{j2}^{\beta_2}),\qquad\left|\frac{\partial\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{avg}}}{\partial\beta_1}\right|=\frac1{2n}\sum_j s_{j1}^{\beta_1}\log(1/s_{j1}).\] The source expression is the derivative of s^beta with respect to s, rather than beta. A complete numerical check uses n=1,s11=0.9,beta1=1, any fixed interior s12 and fixed beta2, and any permitted scaled-uniform noise level, for example sigma=0.1. These profiles remain in [0,1]; all aggregators are averages and independent noises can be used. The actual derivative magnitude is 0.9 log(1/0.9)/2=0.0474122320. The printed magnitude is 1/2, and the claimed interval lower bound is 0.9/4=0.225, which is greater than the actual magnitude.

On the full local interval beta1 in [1/2,2], the true minimum in this example is 0.9^2 log(1/0.9)/2=. The magnitude of the right derivative at beta1=0 is log(1/0.9)/2=0.0526802578, not zero. For an unrestricted beta1>=0 the infimum is nevertheless zero as beta1 tends to infinity, so the global infinite-width conclusion can survive for a different reason.

Impact: the false local sensitivity bound is used in the displayed transition-width derivation at the top of p. 21. This is a substantive invalid derivation in an advertised theoretical extension, even though linear profiles and qualitative phase transitions can survive. We have not inferred that every final plotted transition or every possible loose upper bound is false.

Scoped correction: on a prespecified compact interval J=[b_min,b_max], with 0<s_j1<1, the exact minimum derivative magnitude is D_J=(1/(2n))sum_j s_j1^b_max log(1/s_j1). It can replace the erroneous denominator only after checking D_J>0 and that both threshold-offset points remain in J. The repaired concentration bound below must also be used when general noise is intended. This correction verifies the derivative and its interval minimum; it does not adopt or validate the original width.

Evidence: primary pp. 20-21; `source_p20.png`; `agent_claim_p20.png`; symbolic and numerical checks in `agent_claim_check.py` and `agent_claim_calculation.json`.

# 5. Problem 3 (Major for the stated implication): complementary margins alone do not ensure a gain

Source claim: p. 6, ’Illustration with linear ability profiles’, states that opposite ability gaps of order sigma sqrt(log(1/theta)/n) imply P12-P_l>=1-2theta for both original workers. The full Theorem 3.3 on p. 5 also requires the success threshold to lie between shifted expected errors. The simplified implication does not retain that essential condition.

Use exactly the illustration’s scaled-uniform linear profiles, common independent noise, and average aggregation. Let n=m=20 and each task contain every skill, so Err=(1/40)sum_jl zeta_jl. Draw difficulties independently from Uniform[0,1]. Set sigma=0.001, theta=0.1, tau=0.25, W1=(a1,a2)=(0.9,0.8), W2=(0.8,0.9), and W12=(0.9,0.9). Thus each worker is stronger in one component by 0.1, with all ability parameters in [0,1].

For every difficulty s in [0,1],1-a<=0.2 and the noise half-width is sigma min((1-a)s,1-(1-a)s). Hence every error of either original worker is bounded above by (1+sigma)(1-a)s<=0.2002<0.25. The merged worker has errors at most 0.1001. Therefore all jobs succeed with probability 1 for all three workers, and both actual gains equal 0, whereas the displayed conclusion requires at least 0.8.

\[P_1=P_2=P_{12}=1,\qquad P_{12}-P_1=P_{12}-P_2=0<1-2\theta=0.8.\] The margins can be checked without guessing the hidden asymptotic constant. On the positive-probability difficulty event [1/4,3/4]^40 (probability 2^-40), MinDer for each component is at least 1/8 and L=1/40. Each exact main-theorem gamma is at most 0.0019194104, so the sum is at most 0.0038388207, far below 0.1. More generally, keeping the abilities and threshold fixed and taking sigma to zero defeats any fixed finite hidden O-constant, while both original workers remain certain to succeed.

Alternative reading and precise consequence: if the illustration is intended to inherit the full threshold sandwich silently, it becomes a conditional claim and this example is excluded. That is exactly the missing qualification to restore. The counterexample refutes the explicit margin-only sufficient-condition reading; it does not refute Theorem 3.3 with its original threshold assumptions. The distinction matters because the paper uses the illustration to interpret when complementary human/AI abilities guarantee an improvement. Restoring the threshold qualification repairs that inference; it does not repair Problem 1’s general-noise constants.

Evidence: primary pp. 5-6; visually checked `agent_empirical_p06.png`; `agent_empirical_merging_audit.json`; exact support bounds and margin arithmetic in `agent_empirical_margin_check.py` and `agent_empirical_margin_check_result.json`.

# 6. Scoped repair of the general independent-noise concentration step

A defensible nonoptimal replacement is available under explicit assumptions. Let independent real inputs have centered MGF proxies K_j, let Err be L-Lipschitz in l1, and let S=sum_j K_j^2. For an independent copy X_j’, the symmetrized-distance variable epsilon|X_j-X_j’| has the same law as X_j-X_j’. Independence and the two centered MGF bounds give E exp(beta(X_j-X_j’))<=exp(beta^2 K_j^2), so its subgaussian diameter squared is at most 2K_j^2.

Kontorovich’s one-sided martingale argument then gives the following safe, possibly loose bound and threshold width:

\[\Pr\{\mathrm{Err}-\mathbb E\mathrm{Err}\ge t\}\le\exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{4L^2S}\right),\qquad\gamma_{\rm safe}=\frac{2L\sqrt{S_{\max}\log(1/\theta)}}{D}.\] The same bound applies to the lower tail. Here D>0 must bound the magnitude of the decreasing, differentiable mean error over the entire relevant interval, Smax>0 must bound S there, and the critical and shifted points must be admissible. Integrating the derivative gives a mean separation of at least D gamma_safe, and the tail bound yields the desired theta and 1-theta limits. This verifies a widened, conditional independent-noise theorem. It is not a proof of the original width, the dependent-noise extension, or all empirical claims. Degenerate zero-noise cases and equality at the critical point should be stated separately.

# 7. Counterevidence, surviving claims and unresolved matters

The intended scaled-uniform stochastic dominance survives. Couple abilities as X(mu,U)=mu+min(mu,1-mu)U with the same U in [-sigma,sigma],sigma<=1. Its piecewise slopes in mu are 1+U and 1-U, both nonnegative. Thus incorrect CDF notation in Appendix B is not grounds to deny the underlying order. Weighted-average Lipschitz calculations also have the expected maximum-coordinate-weight form.

Theorem 3.3/C.5’s algebraic transfer from P12>=1-theta and P2<=theta to their difference is valid once a valid concentration result and the full threshold assumptions are supplied. Under the best-combination interpretation and common human decision profile, the productivity-compression argument can be written PC=min(P2,Q_AI)-P1; this preserves the conditional proof logic. Its general-noise numerical guarantees still inherit Problem 1.

A potential empirical calibration accusation was tested and substantially resolved by counterevidence. Using the printed p. 28 difficulty vector directly with Table 3 produces the wrong slope direction. Replacing that printed vector by 1-s yields fixed-intercept slopes -0.776287 and -0.916636, rounding to the reported -0.78 and -0.92, with mean squared residuals 0.013013 and 0.029024. These recover the reported 0.013/0.029 values and agree with Figure 11’s descending visual pattern. This strongly supports a complemented-vector reporting correction; it is not evidence that the authors’ entire calibration was fabricated or numerically unsupported. Author adoption and the exact original script remain unverified.

The truncated-normal subskill reconstruction is explicitly approximate, and p. 32 already discloses a change in a job-success value from 0.84 to 0.55 after decomposition. Independent moment checks quantify boundary discrepancies, but no universal approximation tolerance is supplied; these calculations are retained as scope evidence, not a newly proved core falsification. Likewise, the claimed variance matching in Figures 16-17 requires the actual noise parameterization to distinguish scaled-uniform, unscaled-uniform and whole-skill versus subskill interpretations.

C.7’s proof uses a weighted mixture of an entirely independent and an entirely shared-noise regime, while Section 4.2 describes selection for each subskill. A global switch is a possible intended interpretation; the implementation is unavailable. Endpoint/root-log definitions also need clarification. No additional complete counterexample to its final guarantee was established here, so this remains Needs Evidence rather than a separate confirmed Major.

The printed max-aggregation illustration on p. 19 also fails at its stated numerical threshold; an ability-versus-error threshold transcription is a plausible local explanation. It is retained in the evidence files and excluded from additional substantive findings under the task’s treatment of isolated presentation mistakes. No extra Major is inferred from it.
