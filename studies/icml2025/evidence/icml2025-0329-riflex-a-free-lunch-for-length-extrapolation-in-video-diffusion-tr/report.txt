RIFLEx measures a repeated-frame lag, selects the nearest RoPE component period, and reduces that frequency to \(2\pi/(sL)\). Other components are retained. # Frequency analysis and limits of the inference

Problem 1 (A component period is not a content non-repetition guarantee).  Sections 3.2–3.4, pp. 5–6, infer repetition of generated content from individual RoPE periods and call (8) a non-repetition condition. That inference is invalid as a general implication. Severity: Minor as a scope correction to this empirical contribution, not a refuted formal theorem.

For a rotary pair \(R(p\theta)x\), the relative attention term is \(q_p^TR((q-p)\theta)k_q\). Periodicity of \(R\) does not make the content-dependent vectors \(q_p,k_q\) or the value vectors equal. With multiple components, a period of one component is not even a period of the full positional representation. In the Hunyuan example documented in `identify_k`, \(b=256,d'=16\), hence \[(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_8)=(1,1/2,\ldots,1/128).\] At \(N_4=2\pi/(1/8)=16\pi\), component four returns to phase one, but component five has phase \(-1\), with encoding distance \(2\) from its initial value. Thus the full embedding has not repeated. In discrete time, \(2\pi/\theta_j\) is generally noninteger; a continuous period also does not imply an exact collision at an integer frame. A model ignoring the edited component can still produce repetitive values, even while its remaining position codes are distinct. These observations invalidate the implication, not the possibility that a learned component strongly influences repetition.

Equation (8) correctly ensures at most one continuous cycle for the chosen component over the target interval. With positions \(0,\ldots,L'-1\) and \(\theta'=2\pi/L'\), its first and last phases are nevertheless very close: \[\|e^{i(L'-1)\theta'}-1\|=2\sin(\pi/L').\] For \(L'=66\), this distance is about \(0.0952\). Absence of an exact phase collision therefore does not yield robust separation, much less non-repetition of semantic content. A defensible replacement is a stated empirical hypothesis, with controlled frequency interventions, unchanged seeds and model weights, complete lag statistics, and held-out prompts.

Equation (6) uses a signed cosine difference, which is neither positive nor pointwise monotone in frequency. For the complete sine/cosine pair, the norm of the one-step change is instead \(2|\sin(\theta/2)|\), independent of position and increasing on the frequencies \(0\leq\theta\leq1\) used here. That supports an encoding-scale intuition, but no formula given connects it quantitatively to generated optical motion. Moreover, isolating one frequency and fine-tuning changes model weights; it cannot alone identify causal dominance inside the original multi-frequency network.

As a positive consistency check, the Hunyuan NTK formula gives intrinsic period \(16\pi s^{3/7}\). It exceeds the approximate target latent length \(33s\) for \(s=2\) and falls below it for \(s=2.3\), matching the reported change of behavior. This is support for the heuristic on those settings, not independent verification of the generated videos.

# Implementation and evaluation findings

Problem 2 (The released frequency differs from Algorithm 1).  Algorithm 1 uses \(2\pi/L'\), whereas `riflex_utils.py`, lines 43–48, uses \(0.9(2\pi/L')\). Severity: Minor; the historical experimental setting needs evidence before attributing published scores to either variant.

The source and README explicitly say the extra factor addresses occasional tail-frame repetition. It changes the period to \(L'/0.9\), an increase of \(11.11\%\), and changes the first/last phase separation for \(L'=66\) from about \(0.0952\) to about \(0.699\). This is a substantive specification difference for a method whose defining operation is one frequency edit. It remains within the inequality (8), so it is not a violation of that inequality; it is different from the claimed minimal equality choice. Report which setting produced each result and ablate the safety factor.

Both entry points correctly convert output frames to latent frames through \((F-1)/4+1\) and apply the edit only to the temporal axis in their default branches. Hunyuan uses \(k=4\), CogVideoX uses \(k=2\), and normal-length training-free generation is left unchanged when its latent length does not exceed the supplied period. The fine-tuned branches deliberately use a checkpoint frequency length passed as `N_k`, rather than the current output length; callers must match that value to the checkpoint. The CogVideoX 1.5 branch at lines 175–183 omits \(k\) and \(L\_test\), so its helper receives the defaults and applies no edit. This is a local release limitation, not evidence against the paper’s default CogVideoX-5B 1.0 results. The Hunyuan script uses 8-bit quantization; the README already warns that it may alter quality and directs demo reproduction to the unacquired multi-GPU branch.

Problem 3 (Metric specification and broad quality claims need qualification).  Table 1, Section 4.2 and Appendix D do not establish universal non-repetition or preservation of all quality measures. Severity: Minor, with raw-data-dependent conclusions explicitly unresolved.

The NoRepeat Score searches near a frequency-derived lag, selects the closest first-frame match, and thresholds an average \(L_2\) distance at \(100\). The search window, pixel scaling, resizing, aggregation and whether the lag is from the original or modified frequency are not specified. A restricted-lag score can miss a different period: an independent sequence cycling through four orthogonal vectors of norm \(200\) repeats exactly at lag \(4\), yet all paired distances at lags \(9,10,11\) equal \(200\sqrt2>100\). This is a sensitivity example for the stated kind of metric, not a rerun or claimed bug in the unavailable evaluator. An all-lag check and blinded human validation would separate method-specific search assumptions from actual repeat detection. Threshold units and all settings must be released.

On Hunyuan twofold training-free extrapolation, RIFLEx has NoRepeat \(72\) versus NTK \(81\), motion rank \(1.6\) versus \(1.5\) (lower is better), and imaging quality \(65.2\) versus \(65.3\). Its overall rank improves from \(1.6\) to \(1.4\) and dynamics from \(55\) to \(57\). This is a trade-off, not dominance on every measure. On CogVideoX twofold training-free generation, relative to normal length, dynamics fall \(67.5\to59.4\), imaging \(64.4\to56.9\), and overall consistency \(25.8\to23.5\). The \(70.2\%\) normal-length preference further precludes interpreting “free lunch” as no quality cost. It can reasonably mean no added training requirement for that setting, while longer inference still costs resources.

Conversely, the table does support RIFLEx’s best overall average user rank in each listed extrapolation group, a \(34\)-point NoRepeat improvement over NTK at Hunyuan \(2.3\times\), and a \(48.1\)-point improvement over fine-tuned PE at CogVideoX \(2\times\). Those positive comparisons should be retained. Ten participants and aggregate ranks do not supply paired uncertainty or the effective number of independent judgments. Prompt counts, repeated seeds, ranking/tie rules, randomization and respondent-level results are needed before interpreting small differences as statistically established. Appendix D does specify that ties are allowed for training-free comparisons and disallowed after fine-tuning; the remaining gap concerns aggregation and respondent-level uncertainty. The stated \(1/50{,}000\) training-compute ratio also lacks a reproducible denominator and FLOP/time accounting in the available setup table.

# Assessment and verification boundary

The static source confirms an inexpensive frequency modification, and the published aggregates show useful regimes. The mathematical limitation is that a phase-period constraint is not a sufficient condition on generated content. The authors’ empirical framing, explicit theoretical limitation, and reported less-than-perfect NoRepeat scores argue for correcting scope and reproducibility rather than declaring the method disproved. The overall assessment is Minor. Scientific judgments remain pending, adoption is unknown and human reviews are empty. Focused independent acceptance is recorded in the existing verification files.
