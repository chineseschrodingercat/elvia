Abstract: Two Major findings concern MargE: the length-normalized training loss does not inherit the proved reference-policy interpolation limit, and the supplied reproduction configuration makes its direct reward gradient identically zero through singleton self-normalization. A separate released REINFORCE branch omits the stated importance weights (Minor, with historical impact unresolved). The synthetic benchmark construction and reported empirical observations are not thereby refuted. Coverage, alternatives, and exact limits of the findings are stated below.

# Definitions and dependencies

Equation reference: eq:pure. For one prompt, write \(p=\pi_\theta\), \(q=\pi_{\rm ref}\), \(p^*\propto\exp(\beta r)\), and \(n_y=|y|\). Sections 4.2–4.3 and Appendix A.3 consider \[F_\lambda(p)=D_{\rm KL}(p\|p^*)+\lambda D_{\rm KL}(q\|p).
 \] On a finite response set with positive \(q,p^*\) and a sufficiently flexible policy family, its endpoint limits are valid. Indeed, comparing its minimum against \(p^*\) bounds \(D_{\rm KL}(p_\lambda\|p^*)\) by \(\lambda D_{\rm KL}(q\|p^*)\), and comparing against \(q\) bounds \(D_{\rm KL}(q\|p_\lambda)\) by \(D_{\rm KL}(q\|p^*)/\lambda\). Thus the respective KL divergences tend to zero. This scoped result is not challenged.

Equation reference: eq:implemented. The implemented form in Eq. (2), p. 5, instead contains \[\mathbb E_{y\sim q}
 \left[\frac{p_y}{q_y}\left(\frac{\log p_y}{n_y}-\beta r_y\right)
       -\lambda\frac{\log p_y}{n_y}\right].
 \] Appendix A.4, Eq. (17), p. 30, identifies the length-weighted cross-entropy with the reverse KL after dropping constants. Appendix A.7, Eqs. (30)–(33), p. 36, adds within-batch self-normalization. These modifications are precisely where transfer of the theoretical guarantee and the code behavior need checking.

# Supported problems

Problem 1 (Major: the modified loss does not have the stated interpolation endpoint).  Claim and location. Section 4.2, p. 5, Appendix A.3, pp. 28–29, and Section 6.2, p. 9, attribute Strong Interpolation Criteria (SIC) to MargE, including return to the reference distribution as \(\lambda\to\infty\). The dependence is Eq. (1) to the computational Eq. (2), via Appendix A.4, especially Eq. (17).

Complete finite counterexample. Take two responses, lengths \(n_1=1,n_2=2\), a full-support reference \(q=(1/2,1/2)\), and zero rewards. Zero margin rewards are consistent with equal objective values. Let the policy family contain every Bernoulli distribution \(p=(t,1-t)\), \(0<t<1\). There is no infinite response-space or partition-function issue. The Boltzmann target is \(p^*=q\), so [eq:pure] (#eq:pure) has the unique minimum \(q\) for every \(\lambda\geq0\).

The term dominating [eq:implemented] (#eq:implemented) as \(\lambda\to\infty\) is instead \[C(t)=-\tfrac12\log t-\tfrac14\log(1-t).\] It is strictly convex, with \(C'(t)=-1/(2t)+1/[4(1-t)]\), hence its unique minimizer is \(t=2/3\). Numerically \(C(1/2)=0.5198603854\) whereas \(C(2/3)=0.4773856262\). The remaining finite-action entropy/reward term is bounded, so minimizing the full objective converges to this unique minimum of \(C\). Thus the limit is \((2/3,1/3)\), not \((1/2,1/2)\). Generally the reference limit becomes \[p_y=\frac{q_y/n_y}{\sum_z q_z/n_z}.\] Self-normalizing the first term does not repair a different reference-dominant term.

Alternative readings. Appendix A.4 explicitly calls part of the first-KL manipulation an approximation. That acknowledgement is relevant, but no approximation error bound preserves SIC, and Eq. (17)’s claimed equivalence is itself false for unequal lengths. If all output token lengths are exactly equal, the reference endpoint objection disappears; this restricted case does not justify the stated general LLM loss. At the other endpoint a common length \(L\) also changes the reward temperature unless \(\beta\) is rescaled. The counterexample tests the general policy objective, not a claim that the particular Ehrlich runs emitted these two responses.

A genuinely normalized distribution proportional to \(p_y^{1/n_y}\) has an additional parameter-dependent normalizer. It cannot be replaced by a log-likelihood divided by length while dropping that normalizer as a constant, nor sampled as though it were \(p\) without correction.

Impact and correction. Major: the claimed theoretical property is part of the motivation and causal interpretation of MargE, including the SIC ablation. The valid proof for [eq:pure] (#eq:pure) is not a proof for the modified objective. Removing the length factors and using the exact population objective restores the finite, full-support endpoint argument given above. This is a verified mathematical repair for that objective only; it does not certify a practical estimator, length control, or improved performance. Alternatively, the present loss may be presented as a heuristic without the inherited SIC claim. Original p. 30 was visually checked; calculations are in `independent_checks.py/json`.

Problem 2 (Major: the advertised reproduction configuration removes the direct reward gradient).  Claim and exact source. The paper presents MargE as using reward magnitudes to improve policy training (Sections 4.4 and 6.2). The README’s reproduction command selects `config/pipeline_marge_f2.yaml`. In that file, lines 151–167 set code coefficients `alpha=1.0`, `beta=10.0`, `self_normalize_weights=True`, and `per_device_train_batch_size=1`; gradient accumulation is 32 and training uses two processes. Here the code’s `beta` is the reference-term coefficient, not the paper’s Boltzmann temperature.

The settings are passed through `run_pipeline.py`, lines 347–366, and instantiated in `marge.py`. The decoder-only path of `marge_trainer.py`, lines 994–1047, preserves the single target example. Lines 1049–1094 normalize the ratio by its local tensor sum; lines 1198–1234 compute ratios, call the loss, and return its mean. There is no cross-process or cross-accumulation normalization before this loss.

Decisive calculation. For a singleton microbatch and a finite positive ratio \(w=p_y/q_y\), \[\widetilde w=w/w=1,\qquad \nabla_\theta\widetilde w=0.\] Writing \(\ell=\log p_y\) and \(n=|y|\), the code’s loss is exactly \[\alpha(\ell/n-r)+\beta_{\rm code}(-\ell/n)
 =-9\ell/n-r.\] Thus its direct reward term has zero parameter gradient. For a binary logit \(z\) with \(p_y=1/4\) and \(n=32\), the gradient is \(-9(1-p_y)/32=-0.2109375\) for each of \(r=0,0.5,1\). Independent central differences reproduce this value. Accumulating 32 such gradients and averaging across two devices cannot restore the missing dependence: normalization has already occurred separately.

Alternatives and impact. Rewards can still affect which pairs enter the dataset and checkpoint selection; the entire pipeline is not reward-blind. The demonstrated problem is loss of reward-magnitude influence on each parameter update, leaving a scaled token cross-entropy gradient. The effective optimization batch size is not the batch over which the ratios are normalized. Larger actual local batches, a different normalization implementation, or historical overrides could avoid this particular collapse; none is established by the inspected reproduction configuration.

This is Major for the supplied core-method reproduction: the designated MargE training path does not implement the claimed direct reward-driven learning mechanism. It does not prove that the paper’s Figure 3 or Figure 5 runs used this exact path. Historical configuration-to-figure provenance remains Needs Evidence.

Correction bounds. Normalization would have to use multiple examples jointly, with a correctly defined sampling law and objective scaling, or be replaced by an otherwise justified estimator. Merely increasing gradient accumulation does not do so. No complete distributed-training repair or performance recovery has been verified; the report does not recommend executing the author pipeline as a check.

Problem 3 (Minor: the released REINFORCE control drops its stated importance weights).  Location and check. Section 6.2, p. 9, and Appendix A.7, Eq. (31), p. 36, describe an importance-weighted REINFORCE control. In `marge_trainer.py`, lines 1080–1090, the `reinforce_style` branch returns \(-\log p_y\,r_y+\beta_{\rm code}(-\log p_y/n_y)\) and does not use the ratio argument at all. The calculated running reward baseline is logged but also absent from this loss.

With fixed off-policy data \(q=(0.9,0.1)\) and positive rewards \((0.5,1)\), the unregularized returned objective is weighted maximum likelihood: \[-0.45\log p_1-0.1\log p_2.\] Its minimizer is \((9/11,2/11)\), whereas maximizing the policy’s expected reward puts all mass on response 2. This is an exact two-action comparison under a full-support proposal, not an author-code run.

Alternatives, scope, and correction. An on-policy resampling scheme could justify an unweighted score-function estimator, but this branch is used in a fixed-data training procedure, and the paper expressly specifies importance weights. The supported judgment is Minor for the released ablation path; whether it materially changes the published SIC ablation cannot be established without its actual run configuration and data. Restoring a valid off-policy estimator also requires specifying the proposal law and differentiating the intended score-function surrogate correctly. A full repaired ablation is not certified, and the reported empirical curve is not declared false.

# Other checked claims and unresolved evidence

The feasible-set construction and the self-transition filler argument for jointly satisfiable motifs (Appendix A.2.5, p. 26) were checked. A small independent motif construction reaches value 1 and satisfies every selected transition. The quantization condition \(q\mid k\) is explicitly present on p. 23; no false counterexample using incompatible \(q\) is asserted. The real-benchmark rank-correlation comparison on p. 27 uses 64 variants of one genetic algorithm and is evidence about those variants, not a universal guarantee of biological validity. No wet-lab extrapolation is certified.

The sign of `get_ehrlich_rewards` was checked against `negate: true` in the supplied test-function configuration. Its apparent reversed subtraction is consistent with minimizing the negated objective and is not reported as a bug. Lemma A.1’s cancellation of a prompt-only reward offset is also valid on a finite domain with a common response set and finite partition function.

Budget and sampling provenance remain Needs Evidence. Algorithm 4 (p. 33) uses oracle feasibility labels while filtering before the nominal \(j\) evaluations. The released generation code scores each parsed candidate; some such evaluations may be diagnostics only. The inspected formatter also samples candidates before later pair-level infeasibility filtering, differing from that pseudocode order. A trace distinguishing optimization-used oracle calls from retrospective diagnostic scoring is required to audit the claimed evaluation budget. No numerical budget inflation factor is established here.

Similarly, PropEn matching, likelihood filtering, greedy generation and temperature mixtures change the empirical proposal law; the derivation’s \(y\sim q\) assumption is not established merely by evaluating \(q(y|x)\) on those pairs. This limits estimator interpretation without proving the empirical optimizer cannot work. External `pytorch-holo==0.0.1` and LaMBO-2 implementations were not fully audited. Reproducing all comparative curves, confidence intervals and historical configurations remains outside the available evidence.
