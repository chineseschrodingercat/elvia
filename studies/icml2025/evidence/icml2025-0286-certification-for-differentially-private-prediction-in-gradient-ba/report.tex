Abstract: The paper’s pointwise sensitivity bound survives inspection, but its substitution into a dataset-dependent Cauchy mechanism is invalid. A one-step logistic example with sound AGT intervals and a sparse certification grid violates the advertised pure differential privacy inequality after binary thresholding. Separately, the released minibatch schedule can produce a false stability certificate after one record deletion. Both failures concern central guarantees. Per-batch clipped-gradient bounds and conditional stability lemmas remain valid within stated scopes. The overall assessment is Major; scientific adjudication is pending.\par

\# Problems\par
Problem 1 (Major: a pointwise upper bound is not a smooth noise scale).  Theorem 5.1 and Eq. 9, p. 6, establish a pointwise sensitivity upper bound. Corollary 5.2, p. 7, substitutes it into Cauchy noise and claims pure \((\epsilon,0)\)-DP. Appendix D.6, p. 17, justifies this by saying that any upper bound can replace smooth sensitivity because increasing noise cannot reduce privacy. That assertion is false for dataset-dependent increases.\par
A suitable scale must control variation between neighboring datasets. Pointwise domination \(B(D)\ge SS_\beta(f_x,D)\) does not imply \[B(D)\le e^\beta B(D')\quad\text{whenever }d(D,D')=1.\] Appendix C, pp. 14–15, explicitly selects the largest certified value from an affordable grid. A sound but incomplete or grid-rounded certificate can jump between neighbors.\par
\#\# A counterexample using sound AGT bounds\par
Use a scalar logistic model with logit \(\theta x\), label \(1\{\theta x>0\}\), binary cross-entropy loss, public initialization \(\tau=1/10000\), learning rate \(1\), one epoch, coordinate clipping \(\gamma=1/2\), and a public maximum batch size of 121. Let the public dataset domain contain labeled multisets with sizes between 61 and 121. Thus every permissible dataset has exactly one batch and all selected radii below are smaller than the batch size. This restriction makes the example independent of the batch-count defect in Problem 2.\par
Let \(D_{121}\) contain 121 individuals with \((x,y)=(1,0)\), and obtain adjacent \(D_{120}\) by deleting one. Query \(x=1\). The original gradients at initialization are \(\sigma(\tau)>1/2\), hence clip exactly to \(1/2\). Both nominal trained parameters are \[\theta_{\rm nom}=\tau-\tfrac12=-0.4999,\qquad f_x(D_{121})=f_x(D_{120})=0.\] Theorem 4.6 and Algorithm 1 give, for \(n=120,121\), \[\Delta\Theta_k=[\tfrac12-k/n,\tfrac12],
 \qquad
 \Theta_k(D_n)=[\tau-\tfrac12,\;\tau-\tfrac12+k/n].\] These are sound bounds: arbitrary added gradients are clipped to \([-1/2,1/2]\), and the formula even covers independently up to \(k\) removals and \(k\) additions, a larger perturbation set than a total edit budget \(k\).\par
Take the permissible grid \(\mathcal K=\{0,50,60\}\). Its 50-to-60 gap also occurs in both released OCT and IMDB grids; the example is not a reproduction of those datasets or hyperparameters.\par

 p_{50}=0.00091814122188,\qquad

\#\#\#\# Possible repairs and their limits.\par
The exact maximal true stable radius is 1-Lipschitz across neighboring datasets: for equal predicted labels, distances to the opposite-label set differ by at most one; opposite-label neighbors both have radius zero. A certified lower bound need not inherit that property. One mathematical repair is a global smooth majorant \[\overline B(D)=\sup_Z e^{-\beta d(D,Z)}B(Z).\] It dominates \(B\), and the triangle inequality proves its neighbor ratio is at most \(e^\beta\). But this may reintroduce the intractable dataset-space optimization the paper avoids. A practical replacement must prove smoothness or supply a different valid mechanism, then revisit utility at the claimed privacy levels. No efficient repair or revised benchmark curve is verified here.\par
Problem 2 (Major: changed update counts can invalidate parameter enclosures).  Theorem 4.5, p. 5, and Appendix D.3, pp. 15–16, claim valid parameter bounds for gradient training with fixed hyperparameters. The induction encloses a common sequence of updates but does not ensure that neighboring datasets have the same number or correspondence of updates. Fixed data ordering does not establish that property. The released incomplete-batch policy gives an explicit false prediction-stability certificate.\par
Use a scalar bias logit \(\theta\), binary prediction \(1\{\theta>0\}\), BCE loss, 20 identical zero-input/zero-label records, and \[\theta_0=3/20,\quad \gamma=1/10,\quad
 \alpha=1,\quad b=10,\quad E=1,\quad k=1.\] All original gradients over the relevant pre-update parameter intervals exceed \(1/10\), so clipping gives exactly \(1/10\). Each nominal full batch has certified gradient bounds \[g_L=\frac{9(1/10)-1/10}{10}=\frac{2}{25},
 \qquad g_U=\frac{9(1/10)+1/10}{10}=\frac1{10}.\] Interval SGD therefore gives \[[0.15,0.15]\longrightarrow[0.05,0.07]
                 \longrightarrow[-0.05,-0.01].\] The nominal parameter is \(-0.05\), and the entire final interval predicts zero. The method therefore certifies stability at distance one.\par
Delete one individual. The neighbor has 19 records. The released wrapper processes ten, then skips the final batch of nine, leaving the actual parameter \[\theta(D')=0.15-0.10=0.05>0.\] This lies outside the certified enclosure and predicts one. Identical records remove any dependence on shuffle order, and the sign margins avoid numerical boundary cases.\par

\#\#\#\# Impact, counterevidence, and correction.\par
The counterexample invalidates Definition 4.3’s enclosure and the prediction certificate inferred from it. It is independent of Problem 1: there, the enclosure is sound and the noise scale is wrong; here, the enclosure itself fails for the released training schedule. The main experiments use maximum/full batches, so this example does not prove their reported certificates encountered this particular multi-batch boundary.\par
A repair must specify and enforce a neighboring-dataset-consistent update schedule and batch coupling, or enclose changes in both membership and update count. Removing the skip alone is insufficient: including a new last partial batch can instead introduce an extra update. The per-batch theorem can support a correctly coupled training definition; the unqualified minibatch claim and accessible implementation need correction. No patched training implementation was run.\par
\# Surviving mathematics and evidence limits\par
\#\#\#\# Bounded No Issue Found.\par
Lemma 4.2 follows from the dataset-distance triangle inequality; Lemma 4.4 is valid conditional on a genuine parameter enclosure. Theorem 5.1’s pointwise bound survives. For \(0\le k<b\) and nonempty batches, Theorem 4.6’s final clipped-gradient bound is independently recoverable: maximizing a scalar mean permits adding \(k\) copies of \(+\gamma\) and removing the \(k\) smallest originals, producing the top-\((b-k)\) sum plus \(k\gamma\), divided by \(b\). Each operation increases the mean. Coordinatewise interval extension is sound. The parent checked 20,250 finite exact-rational cases as a supplementary diagnostic; that finite check is not the proof.\par
Appendix D.4’s intermediate all-\(b\) summation indices do not match the final trimmed sum, and Appendix B repeats a lower-bound label where the upper-bound expression is intended. These local notation defects do not supply another false final theorem. The inspected IBP/BCE/clipping formulas pass bounded algebraic checks.\par
For positive vote margin and a public partition in which one record edit affects one teacher, the main ensemble radius \(\sum_{i\in S_n}k_i+n-1\) follows because flipping teacher \(i\) costs at least \(k_i+1\) edits. Appendix D.7 omits that additive term in its proof. Exact vote ties yield the undefined displayed \(K=-1\); they require a nonnegative fallback or corrected flip count. No additional implemented ensemble counterexample is asserted. A valid ensemble stability bound would still need a repaired privacy mechanism.\par
\#\#\#\# Experimental scope and Needs Evidence.\par
The OCT claim concerns private dense-layer fine-tuning with assumed-public pretraining. IMDB uses fixed assumed-public GPT-2 embeddings and a trained small classifier, not a certificate for updating all GPT-2 parameters. The paper candidly discusses loose bounds for deeper models, many iterations, small batches and multiclass losses. These limits are not treated as scientific errors.\par
Accuracy plots and Table 1 were read, but raw experiment outputs and historical model/configuration bindings were unavailable. Surprising student values in Table 1 do not alone prove a numerical defect. The empirical noise/accuracy observations can remain real, while their interpretation at the claimed DP level is unsupported by the invalid mechanism. No dataset, checkpoint, author training run, GPU benchmark, external proof or later correction was independently reproduced.\par
