Abstract: The published benchmark and its pinned evaluation code were inspected. A supported interpretive defect concerns comparing segmentation and detection performance on different measurement scales. The released thresholded box-IoU calculation is a conditional overlap average and can increase without any improvement in predictions. This warrants a bounded Minor finding. The mapping of the published average-precision columns to raw metric keys remains Needs Evidence; no corrected numerical benchmark is claimed.

# Task definitions and checked construction

The labels are generated from recorded well coordinates, using standardized 90-metre sites: disks for binary segmentation and boxes for detection (Section 3.3, p. 5; Appendix B.7, p. 19). Consequently, segmentation measures agreement with a constructed well-proximity mask, not independently traced physical infrastructure boundaries. The paper describes this construction; it is not itself an undisclosed annotation error.

Algorithm 1 (p. 4) assigns spatial clusters, rather than individual random patches, to splits. Selecting small clusters within every larger region creates a within-Alberta spatial holdout, not an evaluation on entirely unseen provinces. The paper does not supply an independently verified minimum train–test distance. The review found no demonstrated duplicate-patch leakage from the supplied evidence, but did not verify the actual spatial memberships.

The split counts \(167436+9463+11789=188688\) and the well counts \(194231+8243+10973=213447\) reconcile across Tables 2 and 9. The individual-well columns of Tables 11–13 also match those split totals. The inclusion of inactive wells and the NIR ablations are identifiable experimental contrasts; the reported directions of the NIR differences are not contradicted by simple arithmetic. Confidence-interval construction and historical execution provenance remain unverified.

# Supported finding

Problem 1 (Incommensurate and conditional metrics do not establish a better task framing — Minor).

## Source claim and dependency

Section 5.2 (p. 7) interprets lower object-detection performance than segmentation performance as evidence that segmentation may be a better real-world framing. Section 5.1 also discusses the segmentation recall as capturing actual well instances. Tables 4 and 5 provide different endpoints: mask IoU/precision/recall/F1 versus box overlap and confidence-ranked average precision.

The first conclusion is a proposed comparative interpretation, not a theorem, but its evidentiary step is invalid: these endpoint values are not on a common operational scale. A segmentation pixel recall is not a fraction of individually localized well sites, and a box-overlap average does not measure either that quantity or detection AP.

## Static implementation and exact check

The pinned detection trainer instantiates `IntersectionOverUnion` with thresholds 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 (lines 55–58), and feeds predictions and targets to these metrics during testing. The requirements pin TorchMetrics 1.4.0. That version’s source was newly acquired into the evidence directory and inspected without execution [tm]. Its functional update replaces pairwise IoUs below the threshold with an invalid sentinel; its class-level `compute` removes sentinel entries before averaging (lines 181–194 and 210–213). With matching class labels, the statistic is therefore \[J_\tau=
 \frac{\sum_{(i,j):\,\operatorname{IoU}(P_i,G_j)\ge\tau}
       \operatorname{IoU}(P_i,G_j)}
      {\#\{(i,j):\,\operatorname{IoU}(P_i,G_j)\ge\tau\}},\] when the denominator is nonzero. It is not an instance recall and does not perform a one-to-one matching in this calculation.

For a concrete permitted input, take one unit-square target \(G=[0,1]\times[0,1]\) and two same-class predicted unit squares shifted horizontally by \(d_1=1/9\) and \(d_2=2/3\). For \(0\le d<1\), \[\operatorname{IoU}(G,G+(d,0))=\frac{1-d}{1+d}.\] Their IoUs are exactly \(4/5\) and \(1/5\). The same unchanged predictions give \[J_{0.1}=\frac12,\qquad J_{0.5}=\frac45.\] Arbitrarily many additional disjoint same-class false-positive boxes leave both conditional means unchanged. Thus increasing scores across thresholds cannot establish improved detection accuracy or missed-well performance. These are exact geometric calculations, independently checked with rational arithmetic, not a run of the authors’ metric code.

The segmentation trainer computes per-batch metrics and feeds them to `AverageValueMeter`. The supplied ResNet50 inference configuration sets `bs_evalt`=1, so the corresponding evaluation path averages image-level scores. Its recall concerns foreground pixels and the treatment of empty masks; it cannot be reinterpreted as a count of detected individual wells without an instance-extraction rule and matching criterion.

## Counterevidence, impact and correction bounds

Conditional IoU is useful for describing localization among qualifying overlaps, and the authors separately report AP, which is intended to penalize detection errors. No defect in the library is alleged. Nor do these calculations establish that all published numbers came from the currently pinned tree. The paper-level comparison is nevertheless unsupported even if each task-specific number was computed correctly.

The correction is to label the endpoints and aggregation explicitly, restrict comparisons to the same endpoint, and evaluate both task framings on a common well-localization outcome before ranking their real-world utility. For segmentation this requires a specified conversion from masks to site candidates, followed by the same matching and false-positive criterion as detection. That new comparison was not performed here. The demonstrated damage is to a local comparative interpretation; the dataset’s existence, coverage and value are not refuted. Minor is therefore the supported severity.

# Unresolved numerical and provenance questions

## Average-precision columns: Needs Evidence

Tables 5 and 6 label the smaller column \(\mathrm{mAP}_{50}\) and the larger one \(\mathrm{mAP}_{50:95}\); for DETR the values are 15.22 and 38.45 (p. 7, visually checked). The prose on p. 8 explicitly interprets the larger value as improvement under broader IoU thresholds. The detection trainer logs distinct `map` and `map_50` keys, in that order (lines 50–54 and 208–210). A transposition of table labels is a plausible explanation, but raw outputs and the table-construction step are unavailable. This review does not infer a corrected column mapping from convention alone, or use an unproved universal AP monotonicity assertion as a substitute for tracing the actual evaluator. Numerical conclusions tied to those labels remain unresolved.

## Historical methods and denominator: Needs Evidence

Section 4.2 (p. 6) describes AdamW for all detection models, whereas the pinned FRCNN trainer initializes SGD with momentum and Nesterov acceleration (lines 39–43). This is a documented paper–current-source discrepancy, not proof of which optimizer produced the published experiments.

The positive test-patch counts in Table 10 and the detailed Table 11 sum to 5984, while Table 2 gives 5894. The well total remains 10973. Checking the actual release split would resolve the patch denominator; a speculative choice between the two numbers is not offered as a correction. No standalone typographical finding is assigned.

Without image-level outputs, this review cannot validate benchmark variances, inactive-well subset performance, unlabeled-well error rates, or independent real-world site recall. These pending questions do not raise the supported severity.
