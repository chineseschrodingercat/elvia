# 3. Problem 1: Theorem 3 doubles the stated gradient-flow speed

## Source claim and suspect step

Theorem 3, p.30, Eqs.(72)-(74), claims an exact solution of the loss in Eq.(71) and the gradient-flow vector field in Eq.(77). Eq.(74), repeated as Eq.(96) on p.32, contains exp(-4Dt/Z). The conversion to the Riccati equation in Eq.(89), p.31, swaps two coefficients in its first bracket and doubles the coefficient of its final quadratic. This reaches the stated theorem, so the issue affects an exact mathematical result rather than only typography. The actual PDF equations were visually checked [S1]; arXiv v2 retains them [S2].

\[\mathcal L=\tfrac12(\theta-S)^2,\quad
\dot a_i=\frac{S-\theta}{Z}b_i,\quad
\dot b_i=\frac{S-\theta}{Z}a_i.\]

## Complete analytic check

The paper’s definition gamma=exp[(2/Z) integral_0^t (S-theta(s)) ds] gives its parameter representation correctly. Set u_i=(a_i(0)+b_i(0))/2 and v_i=(a_i(0)-b_i(0))/2. Then a_i=u_i sqrt(gamma)+v_i/sqrt(gamma) and b_i=u_i sqrt(gamma)-v_i/sqrt(gamma). Multiplication and summation produce the following identity and differential equation:

\[\theta=\frac{A\gamma-B\gamma^{-1}}{2},\qquad
\dot\gamma=-\frac{1}{Z}\left(A\gamma^2-2S\gamma-B\right).\] Equivalently, the first bracket of corrected Eq.(89) is S0(gamma+1/gamma)/2 + Sigma0(gamma-1/gamma)/2 - S, multiplied by -2gamma/Z. For A>0, let r_minus=(S-D)/A and r_plus=(S+D)/A. Separating the Riccati equation yields:

\[\frac{\gamma(t)-r_+}{\gamma(t)-r_-}
 =\frac{1-r_+}{1-r_-}e^{-2Dt/Z},
\qquad
\gamma(t)=\frac{r_+-q(t)r_-}{1-q(t)},\quad
q(t)=\frac{1-r_+}{1-r_-}e^{-2Dt/Z}.\] This expression is algebraically the published rational formula with each 4 in its exponential rate replaced by 2. It starts at gamma=1. Its derivative is exactly the Riccati equation above, and substitution in the parameter representation recovers both original gradient equations. Since A>0, B>=0 and D>=S>0, gamma remains positive and converges monotonically to r_plus. The finite-equilibrium formula survives unchanged on this domain.

## Fully specified counterexample

Take p=1, Z=1, S=1, a(0)=1 and b(0)=0, with standard normal input. All setup assumptions hold, S0=0 and Sigma0=1/2>|S0|, so this is not a singular boundary case. The original gradient field gives a’(0)=0 and b’(0)=1. Differentiating the published formula gives a’(0)=0 and b’(0)=2. Independent SymPy 1.13.3 calculation confirms those derivatives and a nonzero original-ODE residual; after the correction the residual simplifies identically to zero [S4].

## Alternative reading, impact and verified repair domain

Judgment: Major (calibrated from Minor). Section 1.1, p.2, explicitly lists solving the Section 7 toy model as a contribution. Section 7.1, pp.7-8, advertises exact dynamics for general initialization and invokes Theorem 3. The dependency is the toy-model contribution, its exact general-initialization trajectory, and then its limiting amplification factor gamma_plus and weight-to-target interpretation. The existing counterexample invalidates the exact-time trajectory node, so the derivation is closely tied to a core contribution.

A doubled learning rate or loss without the factor 1/2 would give a coherent different clock, but that convention is not consistently used in this paper: Eqs.(2), (6), (9), (16)-(17), and (71)/(77) fix the original normalization. On the verified A>0, S>0, Z>0 domain, w_published(t)=w_correct(2t). Thus values at a stated time are values at twice that time, not generally twice the correct values; derivatives are twice the correct derivatives evaluated at 2t, and attained threshold times are halved. The parameter-space path, endpoint, and ratios of threshold times subject to this same rescaling survive. Stationary trajectories are unaffected.

The strongest counterevidence is the actual downstream use on p.8: the general-initialization rich/lazy argument uses gamma_plus and limiting products, not the erroneous exponential. Those endpoint expressions, the weight-to-target criterion and Remark 5 survive on A>0, as do the separately correct special-case equations on p.7. No evidence ties the erroneous formula to the empirical Figure 8 curves. These limits confine the demonstrated damage to exact-time predictions; they do not make the advertised exact-dynamics derivation peripheral. The Major judgment does not depend on invalidating the broader position or observed scores.

Scope clarification for ’any initialization’: the finite equilibrium expression divides by A. For A=0, all initial pairs satisfy a_i(0)=-b_i(0). The correct separate branch is gamma(t)=(1+Sigma0/S)exp(2St/Z)-Sigma0/S. All parameters then approach zero, theta approaches zero and the loss approaches S^2/2. For p=S=Z=1 and initial pair (1,-1), gamma=2exp(2t)-1, a=1/sqrt(gamma), b=-a. Both gradient residuals simplify to zero [S4]. Thus the verified finite-equilibrium repair explicitly assumes A>0, with this boundary treated separately; it does not assert convergence to the target for every initialization.

# 4. Problem 2: released Figure 6(b) axis disagrees with its parameter grid

## Source claim and complete static check

Figure 6(b), p.6, labels its vertical axis by sigma1-sigma2 and shows +5 at the top and -5 at the bottom; the PDF rendering was inspected. Appendix F.4, p.27, explains initialization using these standard deviations. At pinned code commit 95c2c4a4609b386c3f8c1af5504d3ac549b82620, fig6b_run.py line 18 instead sweeps lambda from -0.5 to +0.5. Line 40 calls get_lambda_unbalanced with scale=0.1; utils.py lines 327-332 set sigma1=0.1+max(lambda,0) and sigma2=0.1+max(-lambda,0). Hence their difference equals lambda exactly [S3].

The run script sorts rows in increasing lambda order (lines 62-64), fills that order (lines 98-100), and saves it without reversal (lines 144-145). The separate plot script uses labels [5,0,-5] at rows [0,rows//2,rows-1] (lines 21-22) and explicitly names sigma1-sigma2 (line 42). There is no intervening sign reversal or factor ten scaling.

Saved row | Actual initialization SDs | Actual difference | Displayed label

First: lambda=-0.5 | sigma1=0.1; sigma2=0.6 | -0.5 | +5

Last: lambda=+0.5 | sigma1=0.6; sigma2=0.1 | +0.5 | -5

The symmetrized initialization itself is correct: W2 W1 = sigma2[B,-B] sigma1[A;A] = 0. The defect is the quantitative axis mapping, which reverses the identity of the heavier layer and exaggerates the magnitude tenfold.

## Counterevidence, impact and local repair

The current repository contains no original heatmap arrays or run logs binding this checkout to the published figure. A different original pipeline remains possible. Therefore the released-code discrepancy is demonstrated; the exact provenance and quantitative correction of the published image remain Needs Evidence. This distinction prevents a code observation from becoming an unsupported claim about which experiments were run.

For the saved row order, derive all tick labels from the actual lambda grid: the endpoints must be -0.5 and +0.5. Reversing the matrix would instead permit +0.5 at the top and -0.5 at the bottom. With 40 grid points there is no exact zero row, so even the center label should be generated from its coordinate or placed by interpolation. This analytically repairs the axis mapping; it has not been validated on original experimental arrays. The code finding is Minor and does not disprove the qualitative existence of rich and lazy regions.
