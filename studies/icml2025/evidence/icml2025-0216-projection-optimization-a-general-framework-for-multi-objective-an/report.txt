Abstract: The frozen publisher version contains three demonstrated defects in core theoretical arguments: a false target-set sensitivity bound for negative aggregation powers, a missing first-order coefficient in the malfare proof, and a weight estimator that maximizes probabilities although its consistency proof requires log probabilities. Complete admissible examples isolate each failure. An additional exact reduction to ordinary linear RLHF fails when the positive-orthant constraint is active; a restricted interpretation is discussed explicitly. These findings support Major impact on the stated arguments, without establishing failure of every final policy regret rate or the practical experiments. Local reward-free formula inconsistencies are already resolved elsewhere in the supplied paper. Adjudication remains pending.

# Model and claims being tested

For each objective \(i\), the paper uses bounded linear rewards \(r_i=\theta_i^\top\phi_i\in[0,B]\), with \(\|\phi_i\|\le1\) and \(\|\theta_i\|\le B\). A known baseline has \(\mathbb E_{\pi_{\rm base}}r_i=C\) for all \(i\). With KL coefficient \(\beta\), the regularized reward vector is \[S_i(\pi)=\mathbb E_\pi r_i-\beta D_{\rm KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\rm ref}).\] Its coordinates need not be nonnegative. For simplex weights \(\alpha\) and aggregation power \(p\le1\), the target is \[W_{p,c}^{\alpha}
 =\left\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m:
       \left(\sum_i\alpha_i z_i^p\right)^{1/p}\ge c\right\}.\] The new objective is Euclidean distance from \(S(\pi)\) to this set, or to a group intersection. The alternative malfare objective aggregates squared group distances using power \(q\ge1\). The linear subproblem maximizes \(\sum_i d_i S_i(\pi)\) for a nonnegative, \(\ell_1\)-normalized direction \(d\).

Unattainable target sets are allowed: the guarantees compare against the best achievable distance. Consequently the examples below do not rely on imposing a target-feasibility condition absent from the paper. Where the pseudocode and prose differ in the running-average notation, the examples grant the intended arithmetic average stated in the prose.

# Core findings

Problem 1 (Negative-power target sensitivity is not uniformly Lipschitz).  Lemma C.2, p. 37, asserts \[d_B(W^\alpha_{p,c},W^{\alpha'}_{p,c})
 \le \frac{m^{3/2}B}{|p|}\|\alpha-\alpha'\|_\infty .\] For the paper’s truncated symmetric set distance, \(d_B\) includes the distance to the other full set of every point in either set with \(\ell_\infty\) norm at most \(B\). The assertion fails for allowed negative \(p\).

## Complete counterexample and exact check

Set \(m=2\), \(p=-1\), \(c=1\), \(B=2\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\). Define \[\alpha=(1-\varepsilon^4,\varepsilon^4),\quad
 \alpha'=(1-\varepsilon,\varepsilon),\quad
 x=(1+\varepsilon^2,\varepsilon^2).\] Both weight vectors are strictly inside the simplex and both coordinates of \(x\) are positive. Moreover \(\|x\|_\infty<2\), and \[\frac{1-\varepsilon^4}{1+\varepsilon^2}
 +\frac{\varepsilon^4}{\varepsilon^2}=1,\] so \(x\in W^\alpha_{-1,1}\). No convention about negative powers at zero is needed.

If \(z\) is within Euclidean distance \(r\) of \(x\), its coordinates are at most \(1+h,h\), where \(h=\varepsilon^2+r\). For positive coordinates, \[\frac{1-\varepsilon}{z_1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{z_2}
 \ge \frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+h}+\frac{\varepsilon}{h}
 =1+\frac{\varepsilon-h^2}{h(1+h)}.\] For \(r<\sqrt\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2\) this is greater than one, so \(z\notin W^{\alpha'}_{-1,1}\). A zero coordinate is also infeasible because its weight is positive. Therefore \[d_B(W^\alpha_{-1,1},W^{\alpha'}_{-1,1})
 \ge\operatorname{dist}(x,W^{\alpha'}_{-1,1})
 \ge\sqrt\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2 .\] The feasible point \((1+\sqrt\varepsilon,\sqrt\varepsilon)\) gives an upper bound \(\sqrt2(\sqrt\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2)\) on this particular point-to-set distance. Thus this family has square-root, not linear, sensitivity as \(\varepsilon\to0\).

At \(\varepsilon=1/10000\), the claimed upper bound is \(4\sqrt2(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^4)=0.000565685425\), whereas the proved lower bound is \(0.00999999\). Independently, every point in the radius-\(1/200\) ball is excluded by the exact rational check \[\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon^2+1/200}
 +\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2+1/200}
 =\frac{51000100000000}{50250101000001}>1.\]

## Dependency, counterevidence, and impact

The proof on p. 37 uses \(\sum_i x_i^p\le mB^p\) from \(x_i\le B\). For \(p<0\) the coordinate inequality reverses. There is no supplied positive lower-coordinate or uniform lower-weight assumption that rescues this step; the example has strictly positive coordinates and weights.

This lemma controls learned target-set changes in the online theory. In the consensus proof it feeds Eq. (31), p. 23, then terms in Eqs. (32)–(35), pp. 24–25, and Eq. (36) supporting Theorem 5.4. In the malfare proof it feeds Eq. (50), p. 28, and Eqs. (51)–(54), pp. 29–31, supporting Theorem 5.5. These are direct core dependencies, so the finding is Major. It is a false intermediate guarantee and a gap in the stated online proofs, not an independently established regret lower bound for every policy sequence.

Restricting coordinates or weights away from zero might yield a different constant or modulus, but no complete repaired theorem is certified here. The square-root behavior of this family alone is not a global replacement continuity theorem.

Problem 2 (The malfare expansion drops a first-order factor \(q\)).  The proof of Theorem 5.2 in Appendix B.3, p. 18, after Eq. (15), uses an invalid expansion for general \(q>1\).

Equation reference: eq:badexp. Writing the displayed vectors as \[x=(T-1)(\bar V^{T-1}-\operatorname{Proj}_{W}\bar V^{T-1}),
 \qquad y=V^T-\operatorname{Proj}_{W}\bar V^{T-1},\] the asserted bound has the form \[\|x+y\|^{2q}\le\|x\|^{2q}
 +2\langle x,y\rangle\|x\|^{2q-2}
 +3^q T^{2q-2}(2B)^{2q}m^q. \] The derivative of \(\|x\|^{2q}\) has coefficient \(2q\), not \(2\).

Take one group, \(m=1\), \(q=2\), \(B=1\), \(W=[1,\infty)\) and \(V^t=0\) at every round. Then \(x=-(T-1)\) and \(y=-1\), satisfying the norm bounds used in the proof. At \(T=100\), the left side of [eq:badexp] (#eq:badexp) is \(100000000\) and the right side is \[99^4+2(99)^3+9(100)^2(2)^4=99440199.\] The inequality fails by \(559801\). The omitted term is of order \(T^3\) and cannot be absorbed into the stated order-\(T^2\) remainder.

This is compatible with an actual offline instance, not merely arbitrary vectors: use one prompt, responses \(0,1\), feature \(\phi(y)=\mathbf1\{y=1\}\), parameter space \([0,1]\), true parameter zero, \(B=\beta=1\), uniform reference, and baseline concentrated on response zero with \(C=0\). A balanced preference dataset comparing the distinct responses has feature-difference covariance one and is consistent with true comparison probability \(1/2\). The pessimistic optimization in Eq. (6) selects parameter zero: its maximum regularized reward is nondecreasing in the nonnegative parameter, and the balanced negative log likelihood is minimized at zero. The optimal policy is then the reference and every estimated regularized reward is zero. Choose \(p=1,c=1\) for the target. The true comparator gap is zero in this example; only the proof inequality, not the final regret conclusion on this instance, is contradicted.

A correct local Taylor inequality, for \(q\ge1\), is \[\begin{aligned}
 \|x+y\|^{2q}\le{}&\|x\|^{2q}
 +2q\|x\|^{2q-2}\langle x,y\rangle\\
 &+q(2q-1)(\|x\|+\|y\|)^{2q-2}\|y\|^2 .\end{aligned}\] Indeed the Hessian operator norm of \(\|z\|^{2q}\) is at most \(2q(2q-1)\|z\|^{2q-2}\), and integration along the segment gives the result, with the usual continuous interpretation at zero. This repairs this inequality only. The downstream induction, constants and final rate have not been re-proved.

The invalid step is used in Eqs. (16)–(20), the main offline malfare guarantee, and reused in the online malfare analysis. It is therefore Major under a core-derivation standard. The correctness of the \(q=1\) first-order coefficient and the existence of a local repair are counterevidence against claiming that the entire approach is impossible.

Problem 3 (The printed weight estimator is inconsistent).  Equation (11), p. 8, maximizes a sum of selection probabilities. Theorem B.4, Eq. (57), p. 32, claims vanishing weight-estimation error, but the invoked Lemma D.1, p. 38, assumes maximization of a sum of log probabilities.

## An identifiable model with no reward-estimation error

Use one prompt, two responses \(0,1\), and two identical objectives \(r_1(y)=r_2(y)=\mathbf1\{y=1\}\). Each has scalar feature \(\phi_i(y)=\mathbf1\{y=1\}\), true parameter one and \(B=1\). Take uniform reference and baseline, \(C=1/2\), and \(\beta=1\). The baseline identification condition fixes each scalar parameter exactly at one, so there is no reward-model estimation error. Choose one group, \(p=1,c=2\) and true weights \(\alpha_*=(3/4,1/4)\). An unattainable threshold is permitted.

Every normalized nonnegative direction produces the same linear reward. Its optimal policy has \(\pi_*(1)=\sigma(1)\). The paper’s Assumption 5.3 holds with \(\gamma=1/2\): \[\mathbb E_{y_1\sim\pi_*,\,y_2\sim\pi_{\rm base}}
 |r_i(y_1)-r_i(y_2)|=\tfrac12.\] The baseline/reference density ratio is one. Unequal response pairs are sampled with strictly positive probability \(2\sigma(1)(1-\sigma(1))\). Their absolute reward gaps are \((1,1)\), while equal pairs provide no weight information.

For \(\alpha=(a,1-a)\), the selection probability of objective one on an unequal pair is \[s(a)=\frac{e^a}{e^a+e^{1-a}}=\sigma(2a-1).\] Thus the true probability is \(s(3/4)=\sigma(1/2)=0.6224593312\). If \(N_1,N_2\) count the two selected objectives on informative pairs, the printed Eq. (11) criterion is \[N_1s(a)+N_2(1-s(a))
 =N_2+(N_1-N_2)s(a).\] Eventually \(N_1>N_2\) almost surely. The unique maximizer is then \(a=1\), not \(3/4\). Both the raw estimates and their running averages converge to \((1,0)\), with \(\ell_\infty\) error \(1/4\). This contradicts the vanishing error in Eq. (57) for the routine as printed. At the population level the probability-sum score is \(0.5299925756\) at the true parameter and \(0.5565905580\) at the boundary.

The example respects positive true weights and an identifiable selection model; it does not exploit unobserved rewards, zero signal or an absent covariance condition. Because the objectives are identical, the policy can nevertheless remain optimal. A failure of the final policy regret rate on this example is not claimed.

## Existing intended interpretation and exact local correction

The surrounding terminology and Lemma D.1 strongly indicate intended log-likelihood maximization. Replacing the summand by \(\log P(I\mid\alpha,\ldots)\) gives, on informative pairs, \[\widehat a=
 \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
 \left(\frac{1+\log(N_1/N_2)}2\right).\] The law of large numbers yields \(\widehat a\to3/4\); for counts \(622,378\) the value is \(0.7490229486\). This verifies the correction for the constructed instance, not every finite-sample theorem.

Algorithm 4 explicitly calls Eq. (11). Its weight-estimation guarantee is a core component of online adaptation, so the printed estimator/proof mismatch is Major, despite a clear intended local fix. Neither the original formula nor the corrected one resolves Problem 1’s target sensitivity gap. No released implementation was available to determine which estimator was actually used.

Problem 4 (The claimed exact linear special case drops an active orthant constraint).  Example 3.1, p. 5, and the direction claim on p. 7 equate the \(p=1\) target-distance objective to ordinary linear MORLHF. The equality requires more than the displayed lower bound on \(c\).

Let there be one prompt and two responses. Use \[r_1=(0,1),\quad r_2=(0,0.01),\quad
 \alpha=(0.9,0.1),\quad \beta=B=1,\quad c=0.901.\] Both scalar features are \(\mathbf1\{y=1\}\); parameters are \(1,0.01\) and meet the stated bounds. The reference is uniform; the baseline is response zero with common \(C=0\). Both objective weights are positive. Moreover \(c=\max_\pi\sum_i\alpha_i\mathbb E_\pi r_i\), satisfying Example 3.1.

For \(q=\pi(1)\), write \(K(q)=q\log(2q)+(1-q)\log(2(1-q))\). Then \(S(q)=(q-K(q),0.01q-K(q))\), while the ordinary linear objective is \(F(q)=0.901q-K(q)\). Its unique maximizer is \[q_*=\sigma(0.901)=0.7111549596,\quad
 S(q_*)=(0.6191220874,-0.0849213226).\] The projection onto the unrestricted halfspace has a negative second coordinate and is not in \(W^\alpha_{1,c}\). The actual projection is the corner \(z=(901/900,0)\). The boundary KKT condition is strict: \[-S_2-\frac{z_1-S_1}{9}=0.0424780977>0.\] Consequently the true distance is \(0.3913147649\), whereas the paper’s hyperplane formula gives \(0.3890306687\).

This changes the optimizer, not just the distance value. In a neighborhood where the same corner remains active, \[\left.\frac{d}{dq}D(q)^2\right|_{q_*}
 =2(S_1-z_1)(1-0.901)+2S_2(0.01-0.901)
 =0.0756959701>0.\] At \(q=q_*-0.01\), the KKT residual remains positive, the true distance decreases to \(0.3907335660\), and the linear objective decreases from its unique maximum \(0.5487177464\) to \(0.5484759669\).

The strongest counterreading is important. The paragraph immediately preceding Example 3.1 defines a restricted class \(\Pi\) with every \(S_i(\pi)\ge0\) when relating a specially chosen attainable threshold to nonlinear welfare maximization. If this restriction is imposed globally, the present witness is outside that class. However, Eq. (4), Example 3.1 and the stated closed-form linear oracle do not impose it; the oracle in this admissible reward model returns \(q_*\), violating it. The unrestricted classical-RLHF reduction therefore fails as stated. This is Major for that exact core reduction; under an explicitly restricted policy reading it becomes a scope/enforcement issue, not a refutation inside \(\Pi\).

For \(p=1\) alone, removing the orthant constraint makes the halfspace distance formula correct and restores the unrestricted reduction. Alternatively, enforcing \(S_i\ge0\) makes the formula valid on that restricted policy domain, but yields a constrained policy problem. Neither modification is claimed to repair all nonlinear branches or to have been adopted. The target-distance objective itself remains a meaningful separate design.

# Local inconsistencies and inspected empirical scope

Problem 5 (Reward-free formulas have internally supplied corrections).  Two local displayed formulas conflict with derivations already provided in the frozen paper.

On p. 6, the combined policy is written proportional to \(\pi_{\rm ref}\prod_i\pi_{\theta_i}^{d_i}\). For normalized directions and individual policies \(\pi_{\theta_i}\propto\pi_{\rm ref}\exp(r_i/\beta)\), the correct combination is \(\prod_i\pi_{\theta_i}^{d_i}\) without the additional reference factor. The p. 4 description and Appendix E.2, p. 41, already give that correct product.

For example, with one objective, \(d_1=1\), constant reward \(1/2\), \(B=\beta=1\), and reference \((3/4,1/4)\), the exact optimum is the reference. The extra-factor formula gives \((9/10,1/10)\) and strictly loses regularized objective by its positive KL divergence. A constant unit feature and parameter \(1/2\), with the reference also used as baseline, satisfy the reward and common-baseline assumptions.

Likewise the opening formula in Appendix E.3, p. 42, has a minus sign on its last term, while its own proof and Algorithms 3–4 use the correct plus sign: \[V_i=C-\beta\mathbb E_{\pi_{\rm base}}
       \log\frac{\pi_{\theta_i}}{\pi_{\rm ref}}
       +\beta\mathbb E_\pi\log\frac{\pi_{\theta_i}}{\pi}.\] These are Minor local inconsistencies, credited as already corrected internally. They are not evidence that the incorrect branches generated the reported experiments.

The practical Algorithm 5 is explicitly distinguished from theoretical Algorithms 3–4 in Table 1. It averages directions and uses estimated expected reward vectors from 100 training prompts; the paper does not assert that it is literally the same guaranteed algorithm. The described experiments use Llama 2 7B, Anthropic HH, and Humor/Helpful/Harmless objectives. Inspection of the reported tables supports the wording that the method is generally better, not universally best: Table 3 has the \(0.3/0.7\) row with distance \(0.578\) versus MOD’s \(0.572\). The max-min comparisons in Table 4 also show mixed outcomes. No uniform-dominance failure is alleged.

No author code, per-seed outputs or historical run manifests were available for this review. The table entries were checked as reported, not independently reproduced; neither statistical significance nor the exact numerical reward computations are recertified.

# Overall assessment and remaining verification

The demonstrated failures affect specific core arguments, with distinct consequences. Problem 1 invalidates the stated uniform target sensitivity used by both online guarantees. Problem 2 invalidates the malfare expansion underlying an offline theorem and later analysis. Problem 3 disproves consistency of the printed weight-learning routine, while identifying the intended proper-likelihood alternative. Problem 4 refutes the unrestricted exact linear-special-case reduction and explicitly preserves a constrained interpretation. None establishes collapse of the whole framework or a loss in the practical benchmark scores.
