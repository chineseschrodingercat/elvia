## Checked reasoning and operative definitions

The contribution is a functional taxonomy: metacognitive knowledge, planning and evaluation regulate an underlying learning process. Section 3.1 supplies a prose definition and diagram, not a mathematical theorem or optimization guarantee. Section 3.2’s footnote defines intrinsic versus extrinsic control by who makes learning-time decisions, rather than whether humans originally programmed the system. Applying that definition avoids the invalid objection that any human-designed agent is automatically extrinsic.

Sections 3.2-3.3 describe domain shift and capability-mechanism mismatch and reinterpret STaR, Voyager and Generative Agents as case studies. The last paragraph of Section 3.3, p.5, explicitly identifies sustained-progress advantages as a hypothesis needing systematic study. Section 6.3, p.9, calls outcome-based inference indirect. Consequently, the title’s necessity position was not misrepresented as a proved universally quantified result.

Sections 4-6 were checked for the distinction between task-level reflection and regulation of learning, the available components, proposed evaluation strategies and shared human-agent control. The paper recognizes hallucinated self-assessment, feedback limitations, nonstationarity, forgetting and oversight risks. It does not claim that intrinsic control alone guarantees monotone progress, safety or optimal allocation.

## A candidate concern resolved by counterevidence

Section 2.2, p.3, broadly describes approaches as eliminating ground-truth labels; Section 3.3, p.4, correctly describes canonical STaR as using answer labels and hints. Primary STaR Section 3 / Algorithm 1 confirms answer-based filtering. However, the NeurIPS publisher version, Section 5 Temperature, p.9, and Appendix H.1 item 4, printed p.28 (supplement PDF p.15), report question-only training with majority-vote pseudo-labels. This variant supports the broader label-free reading and the self-consistency example on p.7.

Distinguish canonical answer-supervised STaR, generated rationales and the pseudo-label variant. The publisher supplement reports 45.5% CQA with majority-vote filtering versus 42.1% without filtering, both below gold-answer fine-tuning. The earlier arXiv v2 merely discusses the possibility. No allegation that STaR lacks such a variant is retained; this is a scope clarification, not a Minor error. No performance experiment was run.
