Abstract: The inspected contribution decomposes changes induced by safety fine-tuning and interprets orthogonal components through prediction and intervention. Four core concerns are established: the printed operator decomposition need not describe output shifts or their variance; a signed projection statistic does not establish the claimed likelihood increase; the stated 80-shot split includes the underlying test goals in training; and the reported non-dominant-only contrast includes early dominant components. A local error in a table denominator is also verified. Released code uses a different, potentially appropriate sample-shift decomposition, and several numerical tables match retained artifacts. These counterweights are preserved. The highest supported impact is Major, within the specified claims; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and dependencies

Equation reference: eq:shift. In the manuscript’s column-vector convention, a representation \(x\in\mathbb R^d\) changes to \(T(x)\) after training. Definition 3.1 fits an affine map \(S(x)=Wx+b\) by squared error. Put \(A=W-I\). For an exact fit, the output shift is \[
\Delta(x)=T(x)-x=Ax+b.\] The text first describes the residual space as the linear span of these shifts, then prescribes right singular vectors of \(A\) as its components. On p. 4, squared singular values of \(A\) define explained shift variance and effective rank. The first component at each layer, LN-C1, is called dominant; all others are non-dominant.

These are mathematical identifications underlying the empirical interpretation, not consequences of SVD orthogonality alone. Feature semantics, causal specificity, and predictive calibration require additional evidence. In particular, orthogonal coordinates at one layer can interact through subsequent nonlinear layers; that possibility is valid and is not an error identified here.

# Substantive problems

Problem 1 (The specified operator does not generally recover the stated shift space or its variance; Major).  Claim and dependency. Definition 3.1 and Extracting Principal Components, p. 3, connect the right singular vectors of \(W-I\) to representation shifts and learned safety features. The effective-rank and dominant-component definitions on p. 4 interpret their squared singular values as shift variance. This construction supports the paper’s principal directions, rank analysis, and subsequent semantic interpretation.

Complete exact-fit checks. First, assign equal probability to \[X=\{(-1,1),(1,1),(-1,3),(1,3)\},\qquad
W=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad b=0.\] Take \(T(x)=Wx\). The augmented affine design has rank three, so the minimizing affine map is unique and has zero fitting error. Thus nonlinearity, an inaccurate fit, and parameter non-identifiability cannot explain the discrepancy. Every shift is \((x_2,0)\), and its span is \(\operatorname{span}(e_1)\). However, \[A^\top A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\] so the unique leading right singular vector is \(e_2\), outside that span. For the fixed downstream refusal probability \(p(y)=1/(1+\exp(-y_1))\), all four training shifts increase refusal because \(x_2>0\), whereas moving the aligned representation along \(e_2\) leaves this probability unchanged. This demonstrates the distinction between an input gain axis and a direction of the output change; it does not assume that all learned features must be human-interpretable.

Second, the variance problem persists for a symmetric operator and centered data. Let \(X=\{(\pm1,\pm10)\}\) with independent signs and equal weights, \(A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\), \(b=0\), and \(T(x)=(I+A)x\). Again the affine fit is unique and exact. The operator selects \(e_1\) with energy fraction \(4/5\), but \[\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta)=A\operatorname{Cov}(X)A^\top
=\operatorname{diag}(4,100).\] Consequently, \(e_1\) explains only \(1/26\) of actual shift variance; \(e_2\) explains \(25/26\). At threshold \(0.9\), the printed operator prescription requires rank two, while the data-shift covariance requires rank one.

Third, an allowed pure translation \(T(x)=x+e_1\) has exact affine fit \(W=I\), \(b=e_1\). The shifts span \(e_1\), but \(W-I=0\) contains no nonzero direction. These are analytic counterexamples, independently checked by a small NumPy calculation and a separate exact-rational check [checks].

Counterevidence and scope. The p. 3 phrase referring to the input space supports an input-sensitivity interpretation of right singular vectors. Such an interpretation is mathematically legitimate, but does not establish the asserted output-shift span or data-variance fractions. The paper supplies no isotropic-input condition that repairs the second example.

Crucially, the released implementation is different. In `src/utils/extract.py`, lines 169–173 fit row samples \(XW\) without an intercept and return \(XW-X\); lines 187–195 apply uncentered SVD to the supplied sample matrix. The rank script, lines 60–72, instead decomposes direct differences \(Y-X\). Intervention and prediction scripts decompose fitted sample shifts. They do not decompose \(W-I\). Sample-shift SVD recovers the correct output direction in the first two examples, so a right-versus-left error is not attributed to that implementation. For centered inputs and a pure translation, its no-intercept fitted-shift branch can nevertheless return zero while direct differences are nonzero.

Verified geometric repair and limits. For a matrix \(D\) whose rows are actual shifts, its right singular vectors with nonzero singular values span the observed output-shift directions; the full right singular basis diagonalizes \(D^\top D\). Center the rows when the quantity of interest is variance; without centering, the eigenvalues describe second-moment energy. For an affine model, the centered population covariance is \(A\Sigma_X A^\top\); a constant \(b\) must also be retained when studying the full shift span or uncentered energy. These identities repair the geometric statement. They do not establish safety semantics or authenticate which pipeline generated the published figures. Reconciliation of the central printed method and empirical pipeline is necessary even if the broad multidimensional observation survives.

Problem 2 (The reported likelihood statistic does not support uncertainty reduction; Major).  Claim and dependency. Section 5.2, p. 7, and Figure 5 infer a late phase of increasing certainty because prediction accuracy plateaus while the reported log-likelihood rises. This is a central layer-wise mechanism claimed by the paper.

Equation reference: eq:margin. The retained prediction script, lines 280–285, instead computes \[
M(z,y)=\frac1n\sum_i(z_i-\bar z)(2y_i-1),\] and lines 325–328 save it as `all_comp_loglikelihoods.json`. There is no normalized probability or logarithm. The artifact is \(33\times100\), ranges from \(-11.05609\) to \(11.44332\), and its first-component entries at zero-based layers 15, 25, and 31 are \(1.33193\), \(6.69105\), and \(8.19584\). The original Figure 5 also displays a positive \(0\)–\(9\) scale. Ordinary mean log-probability for discrete labels cannot be positive.

A decisive finite check takes balanced labels \(y=(1,1,0,0)\) and \[z=(3,3,-3,-3),\qquad z'=(10,0.1,-10,-0.1).\] Both vectors are centered. The script’s prevalence-based median threshold is zero, and accuracy is \(100\%\) in both cases. Nevertheless, \[M(z,y)=3,\qquad M(z',y)=5.05,\] while the actual Bernoulli log-likelihood under the explicit logistic link, \[L(z,y)=-\frac14\sum_i\log\!\left(1+\exp(-(2y_i-1)z_i)\right),\] falls from \(-0.04858735\) to \(-0.32222103\). Mean Bernoulli entropy increases from \(0.19086497\) to \(0.34619906\) nats. The independent check independently computed both quantities [checks]. Thus even unchanged perfect classification plus an increasing reported statistic does not entail lower uncertainty under this specified probability model. More generally, \(M(cz,y)=cM(z,y)\) for \(c>0\), without supplying probability calibration.

Counterevidence, impact, and correction scope. The statistic can measure label-aligned separation, and the example does not prove that the actual model becomes less certain. An exact historical code-to-run mapping is also unavailable. However, both the retained calculation and the positive plotted scale fail to establish the stated likelihood interpretation. Relabeling the statistic as a signed projection margin is valid; inferring uncertainty reduction requires an explicitly defined, calibrated probability or uncertainty measure on an appropriate evaluation set. No corrected historical likelihood curve has been produced.

Problem 3 (The stated 80-shot partition does not hold out underlying harmful goals; Major).  Claim and dependency. Appendix C.1, p. 16, says the fixed 60 STRONG REJECT test samples are completely disjoint from training and frames the experiment as recognizing unseen harmful queries. Figure 7 describes the \(N=80\) training mixture. This split underlies the generalization interpretation of exposure-dependent attack results in Section 6.1.

At \(N=80\), Figure 7 explicitly includes all 313 raw STRONG REJECT samples: \[8\cdot80+313+347=1300\] harmful training rows, plus 1300 benign rows. The same 313-goal corpus supplies the 60 test goals before wrapping them with jailbreak methods. If \(G\) denotes the full 313-goal set and \(H\subset G\) the 60 test goals, including all raw \(G\) in training entails \(H\cap G=H\), of size 60. Splitting the wrapped variants does not remove their raw underlying goals.

Static source inspection independently confirms this mechanism in the released SSFT constructor [code]. The five-method, 1600-row default differs from the manuscript’s eight-method, 2600-row mixture, but at \(N=80\) it needs \(800-5\cdot80=400\) supplemental harmful rows. Lines 95–100 therefore select all 313 raw goals; lines 123–125 take that prefix without excluding test goal identifiers. Lines 63–72 and 107–112 split only the wrapped dataframes.

Counterevidence and bounded consequence. Distinct attack strings can remain held out, and learning to handle a new formulation of an already-seen goal is still meaningful. The result does not establish exact string overlap, invalidate every method comparison, or extend automatically to every \(N\) or the uninspected DPO payload order. It does invalidate the stronger goal-disjoint, unseen-query interpretation at the stated \(N=80\) configuration. This materially changes the core generalization question, without requiring a measured score decrease.

A goal-level partition must exclude held-out identifiers from both raw and transformed training sources. This is a necessary repair specification, not a verified rerun. Exact row-level enumeration and corrected scores remain unavailable because the dataset payloads were not supplied.

Problem 4 (The claimed non-dominant-only intervention also suppresses early dominant directions; Major).  Claim and dependency. Section 6, p. 7, describes suppressing non-dominant components while leaving dominant components untouched, to test blocking only indirect features. Section 6.1, p. 8, uses that contrast to claim that non-dominant directions causally affect the dominant component and safety behavior.

The original notation on p. 3 identifies every layer’s C1 as dominant. Appendix D, p. 19, explicitly states that all 4096 directions before layer 10 are suppressed. The set of all directions contains C1. Thus this reported intervention does not preserve all dominant components and does not isolate the stipulated secondary-component manipulation. Later changes in C1 or refusal are consistent with contributions from direct early-C1 perturbations as well as secondary directions. The logical inclusion was checked independently; the original pages were visually inspected.

Counterevidence and limit. The selection rule excludes directions with harmfulness correlation above \(0.7\). The authors may therefore intend to preserve semantically predictive late directions, while treating low-correlation early C1 directions as indirect. That is a different contrast from their explicit dominant/non-dominant definition. The finding does not prove that early C1 removal caused the reported effect, nor that secondary directions lack causal influence. The separate L14-C6 experiment provides additional evidence and is not erased by this criticism.

A contrast that preserves every C1 while varying secondary directions, accompanied by a matched control for early C1 perturbation, is needed for the specific attribution. No such rerun was performed. The released files do not expose a complete correlation-selected multi-component intervention pipeline that resolves the manuscript’s discrepancy.

Problem 5 (Table 4 uses a different denominator from its label; Minor).  Appendix A, p. 13, labels its last column as \(\mathrm{MSE}/\|X_u\|^2\). All six displayed values instead match MSE divided by the mean squared shift norm. At layer 31, \[\frac{0.02151}{484.2}=4.442379\times10^{-5}
\quad\text{(reported }4.443\times10^{-5}\text{)},\qquad
\frac{0.02151}{3526}=6.100397\times10^{-6}.\] At layer 1 the corresponding ratios are \(7.342428\times10^{-8}\) and \(2.226178\times10^{-13}\). The six-row independent check agrees with the shift denominator within rounding [checks].

The supported correction is to relabel the column or recompute it using the printed denominator. The corrected base-norm ratios are smaller, so this defect alone does not refute the small-error conclusion. The source does not expose the table-producing MSE reduction: whether MSE averages coordinates while the norms sum them remains Needs Evidence. No factor-of-4096 actual-error claim is made. The local numerical-label discrepancy is Minor.

# Surviving checks and unresolved evidence

Scope-bounded No Issue Found. SVD components are orthonormal. The optional projection branch in `extract.py`, lines 225–239, includes the correct \(\|v\|^2\) denominator and, without coefficient clipping, removes the projection at unit scale. The default constant subtraction agrees with the paper’s Eq. (2); it is not misreported here as a hidden projection bug. Cross-layer interactions between orthogonal coordinates are possible.

The independent check checked 24 Table 3 cells against one minus the retained SSFT classification accuracy, and all 54 Table 6 cells against the retained DPO CSV. They agree to displayed precision [checks]. This supports those particular values, not every interpretation attached to them. The appendices disclose SSFT safe/unsafe classification targets; DPO evaluations use generated responses. It would be incorrect to label all reported results as first-token classification.

Prediction protocol: Needs Evidence for the published comparison. The released prediction script uses the 700 training samples for shift fitting, component thresholds, and component scoring; its probe has a 50% held-out split. It computes fine-tuned labels but scores components against `base_output_label`, using base activations (lines 53–82, 158–176, and 268–315). Thus that routine does not implement a held-out comparison targeting the fine-tuned model’s outputs. The paper explicitly discloses test-set best-of-\(N\) selection on p. 4, which limits the criticism, but does not explain the additional fit/evaluation reuse or different targets. Exact historical masks, model identifiers, and run provenance are needed before assigning these released discrepancies to every published accuracy curve. README Guard-model commands and the manuscript’s Instruct-model setup also require reconciliation.

Attribution and capability limits. The p. 5 squared-projection target is invariant under \(v^\top x\mapsto-v^\top x\), whereas a signed refusal predictor need not be. Interpreting relevance as promoting or suppressing refusal requires sign/calibration evidence. The released intervention helper applies the selected vector at every block; the source-layer index does not itself restrict the hook to that layer. This limits a layer-local interpretation, although Figure 4 does not explicitly promise a single-layer hook.

The appendix reports Alpaca output perplexity \(6.59\) for SSFT, versus \(7.04\), \(7.32\), and \(7.23\) after the listed interventions: increases of \(6.83\%\), \(11.08\%\), and \(9.71\%\). It acknowledges modest degradation. This one proxy cannot establish universal preservation of general abilities. Exact multi-component reproduction, dependency semantics for PLRP, loss-variance raw series, and stronger matched controls remain open. Missing evidence is not counted as an additional demonstrated Major.

# Overall impact and state

The residual-space recipe, late-layer uncertainty inference, unseen-goal interpretation, and asserted isolated causal contrast each bear directly on a core contribution. The four Major assessments do not require collapse of the whole paper: orthogonal empirical directions may still influence refusal, many reported values are internally traceable, and a sample-shift analysis is mathematically sensible. No supplied erratum or later revision resolves these specific issues; no exhaustive external revision search was performed.
