\section*{Checked reasoning}
Fix a target series, forecast time, conditioning history, and fitted model. Write \(P\) for the true conditional target law and \(Q\) for the law whose quantiles define the predicted interval \(C\). By the definition of total variation, \[P(C)\geq Q(C)-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q).\] For quantile endpoints of \(Q\) at levels \(a\) and \(a+1-\alpha\), with both levels in \([0,1]\), the closed interval satisfies \(Q(C)\geq1-\alpha\). Consequently, \[P(C)\geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(P,Q),\] which is Proposition 3.1. For continuous distributions the probability identity used in Appendix A, Eq. (22), holds in the usual regular case; with atoms it can be an inequality in the favorable direction. That distinction does not invalidate the final lower bound. A width-minimizing choice among valid shifted quantile pairs in Eqs. (15)–(16) preserves this argument for each fixed conditioning history.\par
The premise is a coherent learned distribution and its approximation error, not exchangeability of arbitrary time series. The paper explicitly states approximate coverage on p. 5, acknowledges arbitrarily large coverage gaps under shifts on p. 6, and describes local-embedding adaptation as an empirical mitigation. Thus this theorem is not an unconditional finite-sample coverage guarantee. No failure of such a stronger claim is attributed to the authors.\par
For the independent Gaussian innovations in the GPVAR example (Section 5.2, p. 8), \(\sigma=0.4\) and \(\alpha=0.1\) give the central noise-only width \[2\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)=1.31588290156.\] This independently calculated value is consistent with Table 2’s widths near 1.32 when the point predictor captures the conditional mean. It does not reproduce the fitted model or prove that any particular predictor attains the theoretical conditional distribution.\par
\section*{Implementation correspondence and limits}
The inspected code uses 39 quantile levels from 0.025 to 0.975, pinball loss, and the stated lower/upper interval endpoints. Selected parsing routines remove overlapping calibration/test indices, and the inspected metric definitions correspond to coverage difference, interval width, and Winkler score. No leakage or metric-sign defect was demonstrated in these inspected paths.\par
The decoder in `lib/nn/decoder/multiquantile\_readout.py`, lines 44–61, has unconstrained linear quantile outputs and no monotonicity enforcement in that module. This source was inspected directly. The conditional theorem presumes a distribution associated with the estimated quantile function. Arbitrary crossed outputs would not satisfy that premise. No fitted outputs were available to establish that crossing occurred in the reported experiments, so this remains an applicability question, not a demonstrated error in the total-variation inequality. Likewise, the adaptive experiment’s actual chronological execution was not reproduced. No claim is made about complete repository correctness, all hyperparameter choices, or later revisions.\par
