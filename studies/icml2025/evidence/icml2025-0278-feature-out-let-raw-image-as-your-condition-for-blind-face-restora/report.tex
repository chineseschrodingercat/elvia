Abstract: Two demonstrated defects affect the central theoretical justification of P-I2SB. Stochastic one-to-many degradation does not preclude a unique optimal Schrödinger bridge, and a reversible residual hash does not ensure that supplied training pairs yield the marginal Schrödinger optimum. Smooth Gaussian examples establish both points, including an ideal-predictor check. Graphical counterevidence resolves the intended residual inverse despite conflicting equations. Reported controlled perceptual gains remain relevant. A repeated real-world comparison column requires source evidence. Adjudication remains pending.\par

\# Major: stochastic branching is not SB infeasibility\par
Problem 1 (Major; application of Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2).  The paper argues that a single HQ image with multiple LQ outcomes prevents a unified Schrödinger bridge (SB), invoking uniqueness of an SDE solution from an initial value. This argument motivates PHM as a correction guaranteeing feasible, optimal, reversible solutions (pages 1–2, 5, 9, and 16). The inference confuses a unique stochastic law with deterministic endpoint transport.\par
Consider the allowed reference process \[dX_t=dW_t,\qquad X_0\sim N(0,1),
 \quad X_0\ \hbox{independent of }W.\] Its marginals are \(p_t=N(0,1+t)\). Prescribe \(\nu_0=N(0,1)\) and \(\nu_1=N(0,2)\) in Equation (3). The reference law \(R\) is feasible and \[H(R\mid R)=0.\] Nonnegativity and the zero condition for relative entropy imply that \(P^\star=R\) is the unique optimum. Nevertheless, \[X_1\mid X_0=x\sim N(x,1).\] Every fixed starting value has a nondegenerate distribution of degraded endpoints. Thus a unique optimal SB coexists with exactly the one-to-many stochastic relation declared problematic.\par
All densities are smooth and positive, with finite second moments. The forward drift energy is zero. Under the backward-time convention, the reverse drift is \(x/(1+t)\), whose expected energy is \[\int_0^1\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{X_t^2}{(1+t)^2}\right]dt
 =\log2<\infty.\] The parent symbolically verified \(\partial_t p_t=\tfrac12\partial_{xx}p_t\), the endpoint covariance, and this energy. This example avoids singular Dirac endpoint or infinite-energy objections.\par
\#\# The narrow statement that does survive\par
If a law is already pinned to \(X_0=x,X_1=y\) almost surely, then \(P^{xy}=P\). Theorem 4.1 can be read as this valid conditional statement. Likewise, one already-pinned law cannot simultaneously have two different terminal Dirac marginals. Appendix C’s choice of a Dirac endpoint measure is consistent with that narrow reading.\par
It does not establish the application’s general infeasibility claim. In the example above, conditioning additionally on \(X_1=y\) produces a Brownian bridge with drift \((y-X_t)/(1-t)\). Different conditional drifts can belong to one valid unconditional Markov law. Pathwise uniqueness fixes the driving Wiener path as well as the initial state; it does not remove random terminal outcomes.\par
The defect directly concerns why the paper claims PHM is necessary and theoretically justified, supporting Major. It does not show that empirical PHM improvements are absent.\par
\# Major: a reversible hash need not yield the marginal optimum\par
Problem 2 (Major; PHM optimality claim and Equations (3), (13), (15)–(17)).  Even granting the intended, exactly reversible residual convention, the transformed supervised coupling need not minimize the SB objective with only endpoint marginals constrained.\par
Use a legitimate additive-noise degradation, \[X\sim N(0,4),\quad E\sim N(0,1),\quad X\perp E,\qquad
 Y=X+E,\qquad U=Y-X=E.\] Here \(U\) is the pseudo-image and the inverse \(X=Y-U\) is exact. The transformed endpoint marginals are \(N(0,1)\) and \(N(0,5)\), while their data coupling has covariance one.\par
Choose unit Brownian reference diffusion starting from \(N(0,1)\). This is Equation (15)’s schedule \(\sigma_t^2=t,\bar\sigma_t^2=1-t\). The reference endpoint covariance and alternative coupling covariances are \[\Sigma_R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_c=\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&5\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad |c|<\sqrt5.\] Every \(\pi_c=N(0,\Sigma_c)\) has the same prescribed marginals.\par
For the path law \(P_c\) obtained by mixing reference bridges with endpoint coupling \(\pi_c\), disintegration gives \[H(P_c\mid R)=H(\pi_c\mid R_{01})
 =\frac12\{5-2c-\log(5-c^2)\}.\] The parent independently derived this expression from the Gaussian trace and determinant formula and checked: \[\begin{array}{c|c}
 c& H(P_c\mid R)\\ \hline
 1\quad\hbox{(hashed data)}&0.8068528194\\
 3/2&0.4941995442\\
 c_\star=(\sqrt{21}-1)/2&0.4172447380
 \end{array}\] The supplied paired coupling is therefore strictly suboptimal. At \(c_\star\), the off-diagonal precision entry equals the reference’s value \(-1\); the density ratio factorizes as \(f(U)g(Y)\). The resulting path law is Markov. For any competing law with the same marginals, the expectation of \(\log f(U)+\log g(Y)\) is constant, so relative-entropy decomposition proves this law is the marginal SB optimum. The comparison is not an artifact of allowing a non-Markov optimum.\par
\#\# Ideal-score learning does not remove the discrepancy\par
An unconditioned network need not reproduce the entire supplied bridge mixture. The corresponding ideal-predictor interpretation also fails to attain the marginal optimum in this example.\par
Equation (15) samples \[Z_t=(1-t)U+tY+\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,\xi,\qquad
 S(t):=\operatorname{Var}(Z_t)=1+t+3t^2,\] with independent standard Gaussian \(\xi\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(U,Z_t)=1\). Thus the ideal endpoint predictor is \(\widehat U(z,t)=z/S(t)\). The one-dimensional Gaussian conditional mean and median coincide, so this result does not require inserting a square into the printed loss norm.\par
The continuum reverse Brownian-posterior update has drift \[b_{\rm rev}(t,z)=\frac{z-\widehat U(z,t)}t
 =\frac{1+3t}{S(t)}z.\] Starting at terminal variance five, its variance remains \(S(t)\): \(S'=2(1+3t)-1=1+6t\). Equivalently, its forward drift is \(3tz/S(t)\). The parent verified both variance equations symbolically. At \(t=1/2\), this construction has variance \[\frac94=2.25,
 \qquad\hbox{whereas the SB optimum has}\qquad
 \frac{6+\sqrt{21}}4\simeq2.645644.\] Hence even exact prediction in the continuous-time limit gives a different interior marginal. If instead the model conditions on the LQ endpoint and preserves the supervised pairing, the preceding endpoint-KL comparison applies directly.\par
\#\# Impact and counterevidence\par
A paired restoration objective may intentionally preserve the data coupling rather than optimize over all marginal couplings. That is a meaningful alternative objective. The result here concerns the claimed identification with Equation (3), not usefulness of supervised restoration. Reversible preprocessing alone does not supply the missing optimal-coupling condition. Indeed, \(Y\mid U=u\sim N(u,4)\), so this residual hash does not even eliminate all one-source/multiple-target relations.\par
The conditional Gaussian formula in Equation (15) itself checks: for independent increment variances \(a,b>0\), the bridge mean is \((bU+aY)/(a+b)\) and variance is \(ab/(a+b)\). The invalid step is promoting correct conditional bridges and reversible hashing to the asserted global optimality guarantee. This is another Major issue tied directly to the core theoretical contribution. No complete repaired optimality theorem or experimental score failure is claimed.\par
\# Minor: conflicting residual equations have a graphical repair\par
Problem 3 (Minor; Equations (11), (13), and (18)).  Equation (11) defines the residual as \(x-y\); Equation (13) defines it as \(y-x\); Equation (18) decodes it as \(\hat x-y\). The last expression inverts neither preceding definition.\par
For \(x=4/5,y=1/5\), the two printed forward targets are \(3/5\) and \(-3/5\); the printed decoder returns \(2/5\) and \(-4/5\), respectively.\par
Crucially, actual inspection of the Figure 2 inverse-PHM box on page 4 finds \(\bar x=y-\hat x\). Figure 3’s vector labels agree with \(\hat x=y-x\). Together with Equation (13), these provide the coherent inverse \[J(H(x,y),y)=y-(y-x)=x.\] That counterevidence limits the finding to a local equation inconsistency. It does not establish that the historical implementation used an incorrect inverse. The corrected composition was independently verified.\par
Noise-I2SB has an analogous specification ambiguity: Equation (11) and the prose describe HQ plus noise, but the expression introducing Equation (14) writes LQ plus noise. Its exact implementation is unavailable. Moreover, statistical reversal must be distinguished from samplewise inversion. For independent \(X,N,\epsilon\sim N(0,1)\), \(Y=X+N\), and \(Z=X+\sigma\epsilon\), \[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y,Z)=\frac{\sigma^2}{1+2\sigma^2}>0.\] At \(\sigma=1/10\), this is \(1/102\), independently checked. A denoiser cannot recover the original sample exactly from these inputs, although a reverse stochastic kernel remains legitimate. The intended meaning and implementation of the unqualified reversibility claim require clarification.\par
\# Empirical checks, local reporting error, and unresolved evidence\par
Problem 4 (Minor; Table 3, page 9).  The first two ablation columns are headed NIQE and FID, but their values appear in the opposite order. The baseline entries \(25.6026,6.5709\) are explicitly FID and NIQE in Table 1. The Cat entries \(13.9109,5.3300\) corroborate the same swap.\par
The parent visually checked both tables. Relabeling the columns preserves the reported improvement. With both LQ and degradation-aware guidance held present, row (c) versus Cat row (e) yields \[\begin{array}{c|rrr}
 &\mathrm{FID}&\mathrm{NIQE}&\mathrm{LPIPS}\\ \hline
 \text{Guided, no PHM}&25.4932&6.3898&0.2945\\
 \text{Guided, Cat PHM}&13.9109&5.3300&0.2395\\
 \text{Reduction}&45.43\%&16.59\%&18.68\%
 \end{array}\] Noise and residual hashing also improve all three listed metrics relative to row (c). These controlled reported results support a PHM contribution. There is no PHM-only row without degradation-aware guidance, so the feature-free configuration’s performance remains Needs Evidence. Guidance alone is not uniformly better: row (c)’s LPIPS \(0.2945\) exceeds baseline \(0.2851\).\par
Table 1 reports the best FID \(13.910\), NIQE \(5.3300\), and LPIPS \(0.2395\), with margins \(0.338,0.0140,0.0018\) over the best other listed values. PSNR and SSIM are lower than Vanilla-I2SB: \(25.405\) versus \(26.174\), and \(0.6581\) versus \(0.7047\). This is a reported perceptual/fidelity tradeoff, not universal dominance. No repeated-run significance or raw-score reproduction was established.\par

Other real-world metrics are mixed: P-I2SB does not lead either LFW metric, and PMRF has better WebPhoto FID and CelebChild NIQE.\par
Comparative timing evidence is also missing, despite speed claims. Figure 4’s caption names Res-I2SB while adjacent text names Cat-I2SB; the plotted variant is unresolved. Selected qualitative and failure panels were actually viewed, but visual plausibility does not establish identity preservation or SB feasibility. Appendix F’s loss curves cannot prove nonexistence of an SB.\par
