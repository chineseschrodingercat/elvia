# Definitions and operative assumptions

The paper writes the total deformation Jacobian as \(J_t=J_t^P+J_t^R\), where \(J_t^P\) is the spatial component and \(J_t^R\) is the temporal correction. Equations (12)–(13), p. 5, use a learned ODE and prescribe \(J_0^R=0\). The auxiliary loss in Eq. (18), p. 6, compares scores associated with the total and spatial-only sequences and adds a squared correction penalty. Equation (19) combines it with video SDS.

For a Gaussian perturbation \(Y=X+n\), \(n\sim N(0,c)\), a denoiser \(D(y)\) is a pointwise map, its mean squared error is an expectation, and a score is the derivative of a log density with respect to its argument. These objects must not be interchanged. The diffusion check below uses fixed kernel mean, differentiable positive covariance for \(t>0\), zero drift, and spatially homogeneous noise, exactly the specialization invoked in Appendix C.3.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Denoising risk is not the score used to justify the central loss).  The passage following Eq. (16), p. 6, identifies its normalized expected denoising error with a score function, then uses this identification to motivate Eq. (17). This derivation is invalid. Assessment: Major for the mathematical rationale of the central flow loss.

## Claim and complete check

In scalar notation the quantity in Eq. (16) is \[R(c)=\frac{1}{c}\mathbb E\left[(D(X+n;c)-X)^2\right].\] The following paragraph treats it as the score \(\nabla_X\log p(X)\); the next paragraph introduces a difference of scores to preserve dimension and invokes detailed-balance inspiration.

Take independent \(X\sim N(0,1)\) and \(n\sim N(0,c)\), with \(c>0\), and the optimal denoiser \(D(y;c)=y/(1+c)\). This is a smooth data density, positive scalar Gaussian covariance, and a valid denoised estimate satisfying the definitions in the passage. Since \[D(X+n;c)-X=\frac{n-cX}{1+c},\] independence gives \[R(c)=\frac{c+c^2}{c(1+c)^2}=\frac1{1+c}.\] However, the noisy-data density is \(N(0,1+c)\), whose score is \(s_c(y)=-y/(1+c)\). At \(c=1\), \(R(c)=1/2\), while \(s_c(0)=0\) and \(s_c(1)=-1/2\). The clean-data score, if intended instead, is \(-y\) and also differs. More generally a scalar expected risk cannot equal a pointwise vector field.

Equation reference: eq:denoiser. The correct Gaussian Bayes-denoiser relation is \[
 D(y;c)-y=c\,s_c(y).\] It can be verified directly here; generally it follows by differentiating the Gaussian convolution under suitable integrability. It relates the residual, not its expected squared magnitude, to the score. The independent arithmetic in `independent_check_results.json` reproduces the values above; the algebra, not finite testing, is decisive.

## Counterevidence, dependency, and boundaries

The loss is described as inspired by GFlowNet rather than a formal implementation of its transition probabilities. A score-comparison regularizer can therefore be used as a heuristic even though the stated derivation fails. Nevertheless the explicit transition from Eq. (16) to Eq. (17) is the paper’s justification of a named core component, not peripheral notation. This supports Major without requiring a decline in benchmark performance.

There is a related exact limitation: setting the permitted neural vector field \(f_R\) to zero, with the prescribed \(J_0^R=0\), gives \(J_t=J_t^P\) and makes both terms of Eq. (18) zero for every spatial trajectory. Thus zero auxiliary loss alone does not certify removal of temporal artifacts. Joint optimization with SDS could still be useful; failure of the full optimizer is not established. Equation [eq:denoiser] (#eq:denoiser) repairs the mistaken identity only. It does not by itself prove dimension preservation, GFlowNet detailed balance, or the proposed neural ODE’s claimed denoising behavior. Those stronger conclusions need additional evidence.

Problem 2 (The diffusion derivation deletes a nonzero density derivative).  Appendix C.2.3, p. 14, moves both spatial derivatives onto \(GG^\top\) alone. The result is false even under the homogeneous-diffusion specialization of C.3. Assessment: Major for the derivation explicitly invoked on p. 4 to support the probability-flow component.

## Claim, assumptions, and counterexample

In dimension one the printed step equates \[\frac12\partial_{xx}(G^2p)
 \quad\hbox{with}\quad
 \frac12(p_0*\kappa)\,\partial_{xx}(G^2).\] Choose \(p_0=N(0,1)\), \(\mu=0\), \(C(t)=t\) for \(t>0\), \(f=0\), and \(G=1\). The covariance is positive and differentiable, the drift vanishes, the diffusion is spatially homogeneous, and \(G^2=1=\dot C(t)\) satisfies even the final condition on p. 15. The convolution is \[p(x,t)=\frac{\exp[-x^2/(2(1+t))]}{\sqrt{2\pi(1+t)}}.\] At \(x=0,t=1\) the true values are \[p=\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi}},\qquad
 \partial_t p=\frac12\partial_{xx}p=-\frac{p}{4}
 =-0.07052369794346953.\] The printed right-hand side is zero. All hypotheses remain satisfied, so this is a counterexample to the displayed derivation, not a boundary-case objection. The original formula was visually confirmed on p. 14.

## Surviving result and supported correction

For fixed mean and spatially homogeneous covariance the correct calculation retains the kernel derivatives: \[\partial_t p=p_0*\partial_t\kappa
 =\frac12\sum_{ij}\dot C_{ij}\,\partial_{ij}p.\] For its interpretation as an Itô diffusion one additionally needs \(\dot C\succeq0\). This restores the standard Gaussian-convolution identity and shows that the final condition \(GG^\top=\dot C\) is itself correct. Consequently this finding does not refute the standard probability-flow ODE.

C.2.2 also prints a minus sign where the product rule requires \(\nabla\cdot(f\kappa)=(\nabla\cdot f)\kappa+f\cdot\nabla\kappa\). Its interchange of spatially varying drift multiplication and convolution also needs restrictions. The drift errors disappear in the later \(f=0\) specialization and are not used to exaggerate the counterexample. Severity concerns the invalid supporting derivation and its explicit dependency on the core pfODE construction; it does not depend on appendix placement or repair difficulty.

## Gradient path and numerical meaning

At `animate_clipart.py:211–218`, the code obtains `logp` and `logp_p`, detaches the latter, and adds their mean squared difference to SDS and an L1 Jacobian penalty. In `losses.py:136–198`, both log-probability tensors are constructed and returned inside `torch.no_grad()`. The ordinary backward graph therefore does not contain this score-related term [Retained latex: \cite{torch}]. The SDS implementation separately constructs a gradient-bearing surrogate at lines 214–215; there is no analogous construction for `logp`. The score-related MSE contributes zero parameter gradient, although the SDS and Jacobian penalties can train the model. This removes the training contribution of the named central score-comparison component in Eqs. (17)–(18), whose gradient is explicitly required on p. 6. That direct dependency supports Major within the pinned implementation; it does not require a demonstrated decrease in benchmark scores or proof that this release generated the historical experiments.

Independently, the quantity returned at `losses.py:177–193` has the form \[y=m+sz,\qquad
 \ell=-\frac{(y-m)^2}{2s^2}-\log s-\frac12\log(2\pi)
      =-\frac{z^2}{2}-\log s-\frac12\log(2\pi),\quad s>0.\] The predicted mean cancels. This is a Gaussian transition log density evaluated at its own sampled point, not the data score in Eq. (17). With \(s=0.5,z=0.7\), changing \(m\) from 0 to 2 leaves the computed result at approximately \(-0.4707913526447274\). Removing a detach or no-gradient context alone would not establish the intended objective.

## Initial correction

In `painter_clipart.py:917–928`, the first correction is initialized with \(F\) identity blocks and then normalized and added to the spatial Jacobian. At the initial integration time the correction per face is \(I_2/\sqrt{2F}\ne0\), contrary to \(J_0^R=0\). The layered branch repeats this at lines 1033–1046. This is a statement about the Jacobian passed to reconstruction; it does not quantify visible distortion after constraints and reconstruction.

## Provenance and counterevidence

The inspected release has not been established as the code that produced Tables 1–4. This uncertainty limits claims about those experiments; it does not change the demonstrated inactivity of a core objective in the inspected release. The correction penalty still operates, so a flow-loss ablation could change results even when the score surrogate is inactive. No author code was run, and no empirical performance correction is proposed. A defensible implementation repair requires a specified differentiable score objective, compatible initialization, and separate validation.

# Other checks and overall impact

Rescaling \(\widetilde x=A(t)x\) yields Eq. (10) when \(A\) is differentiable and invertible and the underlying score and covariance have their proper meanings. Assigning learned attention tensors the names \(C\) and \(A\) does not establish these conditions; this mapping remains unverified.
