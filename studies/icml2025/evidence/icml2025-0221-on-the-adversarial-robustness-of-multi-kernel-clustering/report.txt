Abstract: We check the theoretical argument, attack specification, selected experimental metrics, and a pinned public implementation of AdvMKC. A gradient inconsistent with the defined objective can increase that objective. The stability statement leaves iteration dependence unspecified, the printed reward has the opposite ordering to the released implementation, and the released attack does not enforce its noise cap. Two reported accuracy–purity pairs are also impossible under the stated definitions. The highest supported impact is Major. Historical experimental consequences of code discrepancies remain unresolved; scientific adjudication is pending.

# Definitions and necessary assumptions

Write \(m=N_k\) for the number of kernels and \(k=K\) for the embedding dimension. Equation (2), p. 2, defines \[K_\gamma=\sum_{p=1}^m\gamma_pK_p,\qquad
 \gamma\in\Delta_m=\{\gamma\geq0:\mathbf1^\top\gamma=1\}.\] Equation (17) normalizes by \(N\) and defines \(f(\gamma)=\min_{H^\top H=I_k}\operatorname{tr}
[\bar K_\gamma(I-HH^\top)]\). Thus \(f\) is the sum of the bottom \(N-k\) eigenvalues. Differentiation through the optimal spectral projector requires an appropriate gap. The examples below provide that gap on the feasible simplex. Stability of a spectral projector is distinct from stability of a potentially nonunique optimizer over \(\gamma\).

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The stated gradient can be an ascent direction).  Proposition 6.1 (p. 5), repeated in Appendix A.1 (p. 13), gives \(\partial_pf=2\gamma_p\operatorname{tr}[\bar K_p(I-\widehat H\widehat H^\top)]\). This is not the derivative of the linear mixture in Equation (2). It directly affects the MKKM-RG update in Equations (18)–(19).

## Complete check

Take \(N=2\), \(k=1\), \(m=2\), and \[K_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.8\\0.8&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.2\\0.2&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Both matrices are positive definite, have unit diagonal, and are Gram matrices of unit vectors. After division by \(N=2\), their eigenvalues in the common eigenbasis are \((0.9,0.1)\) and \((0.6,0.4)\). For every feasible \(\gamma\), the leading eigengap is \(0.8\gamma_1+0.2\gamma_2\geq0.2\). The minimizing projector is unambiguous and \[f(\gamma)=0.1\gamma_1+0.4\gamma_2,\qquad \nabla f=(0.1,0.4).\] At \(\gamma=(0.9,0.1)\), the printed gradient is instead \((0.18,0.08)\). With \(u=2\), Equation (19) gives \(d=(-0.1,0.1)\). For \(\eta=0.1\), both old and new weights are interior to the simplex, but \[f(0.9,0.1)=0.130,\qquad
 f((0.9,0.1)+0.1d)=f(0.89,0.11)=0.133.\] Indeed \(\nabla f^\top d=0.03>0\); every sufficiently small positive step increases the stated objective. Independent eigenvalue evaluation agrees (`check.py`, `calc.json`). This refutes the proposition for its printed objective without relying on the later globally worded Assumption 6.2.

## Alternative reading, impact, and local correction

Appendix Equation (25) uses \(\alpha_p^2-\beta_p^2\), indicating an intended squared-weight mixture. Under that changed definition and a spectral gap, the factor \(2\gamma_p\) is appropriate. This is substantive counterevidence about intent, but it changes the objective. Appendix A.1 also writes \(f=f_1+f_2\) for \(f_2=\max_H\operatorname{tr}(\bar K_\gamma HH^\top)\); the actual decomposition is \(f=f_1-f_2\). For a linear mixture with a unique optimal projector the local correction is \(\partial_pf=\operatorname{tr}[\bar K_p(I-\widehat H\widehat H^\top)]\). No complete repaired stability theorem is supplied. This is Major because the invalid derivative defines the central theoretical optimization procedure, irrespective of whether empirical attacks survive.

Problem 2 (The stability proof removes iteration dependence).  Theorem 6.3 (p. 6) bounds the difference between weights obtained from original and perturbed kernels by a constant times the largest kernel-noise norm. Appendix A.2, p. 17, Equation (34) and its final chain do not establish an iteration-independent bound.

## Recurrence and a conditional diagnostic

Writing \(e_t=\|\alpha^{(t)}-\beta^{(t)}\|_\infty\) and \(\varepsilon=\max_p\|N_p\|_{\rm op}\), a recurrence \(e_{t+1}\leq C e_t+C\varepsilon\), \(e_0=0\), gives \[e_T\leq C\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}C^j.\] Even \(C=1\) gives \(T\varepsilon\). No contraction argument removes this dependence.

Two interpretation limits are essential. Assumption 6.2 says every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R^m\); at \(\gamma=0\) a homogeneous kernel mixture is zero and its eigengap is zero. That literal premise is infeasible. The following diagnostic deliberately adopts the meaningful simplex domain and the squared-weight mixture suggested by Appendix (25). It is not claimed to satisfy the impossible all-real-weights premise.

Let \(0<\varepsilon\leq1/2\), \(N=2,k=1,m=2\), and \[K_1=K_2=J_2,\qquad
 \widetilde K_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1-\varepsilon\\1-\varepsilon&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \widetilde K_2=J_2.\] Every kernel is positive semidefinite with unit diagonal, and \(\|\widetilde K_1-K_1\|_{\rm op}=\varepsilon\). The matrices share the leading eigenvector. In the squared mixture normalized by two, the perturbed simplex eigengap is \((1-\varepsilon)\gamma_1^2+\gamma_2^2\geq(1-\varepsilon)/2\geq1/4\). The original objective is identically zero; the perturbed objective is \(\widetilde f(\gamma)=\varepsilon\gamma_1^2/2\). Starting both sequences at \((1/2,1/2)\), choose \(u=2\) and \(\eta=0.1\leq1/4\). Equation (19) leaves the original sequence fixed and gives \[\widetilde\gamma_1^{(t)}=\tfrac12(1-\eta\varepsilon)^t,\qquad
 e_t=\tfrac12[1-(1-\eta\varepsilon)^t].\] Every finite iterate is feasible, the perturbed objective decreases, and \(e_t\to1/2\). With \(T=10/\varepsilon\), independently calculated errors are \(0.316984,0.316152,0.316069\) for \(\varepsilon=0.1,0.01,0.001\).

## Bounded conclusion and counterevidence

A bound \(O_T(\varepsilon)\) at a fixed prescribed finite \(T\) can absorb iteration dependence and is not refuted. The theorem does not specify that restriction, and its proof does not establish stability of optimized weights uniformly over run length. Correcting the mixture and restricting the gap premise to the simplex alone do not recover such a bound. The theorem-level assessment is Needs Evidence / scope clarification: the paper does not explicitly require a constant uniform in the iteration count. A fixed-horizon interpretation remains admissible, so the conditional counterexample does not establish a Major defect in the original theorem. The remaining question is the permitted constant dependence and stopping rule. An upper bound on weight change also does not imply monotone deterioration in external clustering accuracy. The example’s leading eigenspace in fact remains unchanged.

Problem 3 (The printed reward orders the attack objective backwards).  Equation (9), p. 4, is a positive within-original-cluster cosine similarity. Equations (11)–(12), p. 5, maximize its expected return; Equation (16) minimizes the negative policy objective. The release instead uses one minus that similarity.

For original clusters \(\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\), compare \[H_0=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&0\\0&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 H_1=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Both have orthonormal columns and are leading embeddings of the positive-semidefinite, unit-diagonal kernels \(2H_iH_i^\top\). For the same original co-occurrence matrix, Equation (9) gives \(r(H_0)=8/16=1/2\) and \(r(H_1)=4/16=1/4\). Maximization favors the preserved grouping over the crossed grouping. This is an objective-ordering calculation, not a full data-perturbation construction at a specified small budget.

Counterevidence about the released intent is clear: `ClusterEnv.py:152--153` and `agents/agent.py:51--53` use \(1-r\). The printed sign must not be imputed to historical runs. Replacing the reward by \(1-r\) repairs its local ordering only. The printed core attack specification has a Major directional inconsistency; its historical empirical consequence needs evidence.

Problem 4 (The released RL path fails its central noise constraint).  The threat model requires \(\|\mathcal N\|\leq\epsilon\) (p. 3, Eq. 3); the generator is normalized in Eq. 10 (p. 4). The released RL path does not enforce that bound.

The entry point passes a Gaussian-sampled action directly to `ClusterEnv.step`. The method `perturbed_data`, lines 205–212, accumulates this action and rescales only when the noise norm is smaller than \(b=\texttt{thresh}\sqrt d\). From reset, take one environment, \(d=4\), \(\texttt{thresh}=10\), and cumulative action \((30,0,0,0)\). Its norm is 30, the bound is 20, and the condition \(30<20\) is false. The output remains norm 30. Any action in an open neighborhood of \(2b e_1\) similarly violates the cap; these neighborhoods have positive probability under the released Gaussian policy.

The alternate optimizer calls `normalized_noise`; the RL path does not. The soft penalty is on the actor mean, not sampled cumulative noise, so cannot provide the hard guarantee. The three released configurations also set `thresh: 10`, whereas p. 7 states \(\epsilon=0.1\sqrt d\), a 100-fold nominal RMS difference.

The projection \(v\mapsto v\min\{1,b/\|v\|\}\), with zero handled separately, enforces a local cumulative-vector cap. This algebraic observation is not a tested patch or repaired experiment. The failure is Major for the released bounded-attack procedure. Historical table-generating code is unknown; no published score loss is inferred. Evidence: `code.json`, `calc.json`.

Problem 5 (Two metric pairs violate accuracy–purity ordering).  Table 3 (p. 8), BBCSport, surrogate JMKSC and victim MKSSC-ERC, reports ACC \(71.73\%\), PR \(45.40\%\). Table 5 (p. 19), BBCSport, MVC-LFA with no attack, reports ACC \(81.88\%\), PR \(81.07\%\). Both pairs were visually checked in the original PDF.

For a contingency table \(n_{ij}\), the stated Hungarian-assignment accuracy and per-cluster majority purity satisfy \[\mathrm{ACC}=\frac1N\max_\pi\sum_i n_{i,\pi(i)}
 \leq\frac1N\sum_i\max_j n_{ij}=\mathrm{PR}.\] This holds for each run and paired-run averages. The released implementation and output indices agree with these definitions. Violations of 26.33 and 0.81 percentage points exceed rounding error. At least one value or association in each pair is incorrect. Unmatched aggregations would require an undisclosed comparison protocol. This is a supported Minor reporting defect. Raw predictions are needed to identify the bad cells; no substitute value is guessed.

# Dependence and unresolved provenance

Problems 1–2 concern the theoretical contribution, regardless of appendix placement. Problem 3 concerns the printed attack objective and Problem 4 a central constraint of the release. MKC vulnerability may survive these defects; that does not validate the failed derivations or budget compliance.

The release also selects a final candidate using ground-truth external metrics (`main.py:190–211`), an oracle-selection evaluation protocol. Training reward uses initial predicted clusters, so supervised training-label leakage is not established. Historical use and equal baseline treatment need provenance. The publisher and repository landing pages returned no correction notice in the inspected content; this was not an exhaustive erratum or priority search.
