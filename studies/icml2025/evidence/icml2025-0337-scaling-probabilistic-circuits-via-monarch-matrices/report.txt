Abstract: Independent checks support the local Kronecker contraction, untied Monarch map, normalization conditions and selected operation counts. Two narrower discrepancies remain: the public HMM initialization normalizes emissions locally without preserving the full product-distribution weights, and the printed color-transform specification has sign/range errors corrected in the inspected implementation. These findings do not establish failure of trained likelihood results or of the structured-matrix scalability claim. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Checks supporting the construction

For row-major vectorization of \(x\in\mathbb R^{p\times q}\), direct index contraction verifies equation (1): \[_{kl}
 =\sum_{ij}A_{ki}B_{lj}x_{ij}=[(B(Ax)^\top)^\top]_{kl}.\] An independent seeded NumPy example with rectangular matrices gives maximum difference \(2.22\cdot10^{-16}\). For the untied tensors in Definition 3.1, building the dense matrix \(M_{kl,ij}=A_{kij}B_{ljk}\) and comparing it with the two contractions gives error \(1.11\cdot10^{-16}\).

If all entries are nonnegative, \(\sum_iA_{kij}=1\) for each \((k,j)\), and \(\sum_jB_{ljk}=1\) for each \((l,k)\), then \[\sum_{ij}M_{kl,ij}=\sum_jB_{ljk}\sum_iA_{kij}=1.\] Thus a normalized sum map can be implemented with normalized factors. The independently constructed example has row-sum error \(2.22\cdot10^{-16}\). Sum/permutation stages on a common scope preserve smoothness, and do not alter the disjoint-scope requirement at product nodes. General arbitrary signed matrices would not be probability weights; nonnegativity and normalization remain necessary.

For equal dimensions \(h=c^d\), the factor representation uses \(dhc\) entries/elementary weighted edges. This yields \(2h^{3/2}\) for two equal factors and \(h\sum_t c_t\) for unequal factors with \(\prod_tc_t=h\). Selected Table 6 entries agree: \(h=4096,c_t=(64,64)\) gives 524,288; \(h=8192,c_t=(64,128)\) gives 1,572,864; and \(h=4096,c_t=(16,16,16)\) gives 196,608. These use the paper’s operation counting convention, not an independent hardware profiler. The memory argument correctly retains activation storage proportional to \(dnhB\); fewer parameters need not imply less total training memory.

Appendix A’s bit-factor sparsity discussion was read, but the exact input/output permutation and factor-order conventions in the fully indexed butterfly equivalence were not exhaustively certified. The numerical tests above establish the stated local two-factor identities, not every index formula in that appendix.

# Problem 1: local emission normalization changes the product model

Severity: Minor; initialization semantics in the public release. Sections 3.1–3.2 motivate initialization by a circuit satisfying \(p_C(x)\propto p_A(x)p_B(x)\). The release initializer adds log-emission tables and applies a per-state `log_softmax`; it forms initial state weights from the product of the original weights without including the state-dependent emission normalizers. These operations generally produce a valid normalized model that is different from the globally normalized product.

Consider a single-observation HMM, equivalently a two-component categorical mixture, with uniform initial weights for each source model: \[E_A=\begin{pmatrix}9/10&1/10\\1/5&4/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 E_B=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&1/5\\2/5&3/5\end{pmatrix}.\] The source observed distributions are \((11/20,9/20)\) and \((3/5,2/5)\). Their normalized product is \[p_C=(11/17,6/17).\] The four componentwise emission products have sums \(Z=(37/50,21/50,8/25,14/25)\). Normalizing each product separately, then averaging them with unchanged pair weights \(1/4\), instead gives \[\widetilde p_C=(183/296,113/296)\ne p_C.\] These are exact rational values from independent enumeration of the documented operations. The test does not load or execute the initializer. In the one-observation example, multiplying each pair’s prior weight by its \(Z_{ij}\) before global normalization restores \((11/17,6/17)\) exactly. For longer sequences, corresponding partition contributions must propagate through the circuit; local leaf normalization alone is insufficient.

This does not refute the linear Kronecker identity or existence of a properly normalized product PC. Nor does it show that the implemented initialization is poor: it may be an effective heuristic, and subsequent EM can change the distribution. The needed repair is to describe the release as a locally normalized product-parameter initializer, or implement and verify the partition-weight corrections. Historical initialization ablation results were not regenerated, and no claim is made that their reported improvement disappears.

# Problem 2: the printed color specification differs from the code

Severity: Minor; reproducibility-level formula errors with direct counterevidence against assuming the same bug affected experiments. Appendix B.3, page 15, defines normalized RGB values in \([0,1]\) and then prints \(y=2\,tmp+cg+1\), claiming \(y\in[-1,1]\). Since \(tmp=(r+b)/2\) and \(cg=g-tmp\), the printed expression is \(y=(r+b)/2+g+1\in[1,3]\). Black gives 1 and white gives 3. With the stated clipping quantizer for \([-1,1]\), this would collapse the luminance channel to the highest bin. The inspected source instead uses \[Y_0=tmp+cg/2,\qquad y=2Y_0-1=2\,tmp+cg-1,\] which sends black and white to \(-1\) and \(1\). Thus a sign correction in the paper reproduces the source, and no luminance-collapse defect in the actual released transform is established.

The lossless transform itself has the valid inverse \[tmp=Y-\lfloor Cg/2\rfloor,\quad
 G=Cg+tmp,\quad B=tmp-\lfloor Co/2\rfloor,\quad R=B+Co.\] But the printed range \(Cg\in[0,255]\) is false before an offset: RGB \((255,0,255)\) gives \((Y,Co,Cg)=(127,0,-255)\). Both chroma channels can range from \(-255\) to \(255\). The source correctly offsets both by 256, using a larger categorical support. Independent integer arithmetic checks black, white, two saturated colors and a small nontrivial RGB triple, recovering the inputs exactly. This supports losslessness on those cases and the displayed algebra, not a complete audit of the image dataset pipeline.

# Evaluation scope and disposition

A shared normalized patch model \(q\) on disjoint 8-by-8 patches defines a normalized full-image model \(Q(I)=\prod_bq(I_b)\). Its full-image BPD equals the average patch BPD when each image contributes the same number of equal-sized patches. Thus patchwise scoring need not invalidate likelihood comparison; it explicitly assumes independence across patches. This derivation does not certify that every reported loader and aggregate uses identical weighting or color conventions. Lossy YCoCg scores remain incomparable with original-RGB likelihoods; the paper appropriately separates lossy and lossless columns.

Figure 4’s x-axis says inference FLOPs/character while its caption says training FLOPs. Under identical token/pass counts these are proportional, but total training comparisons also require backward/EM and initialization cost accounting. The study’s plotted quality-versus-budget trend was viewed, not independently benchmarked. The approximately 200-fold sampling-time comparison agrees arithmetically with \(3.60/0.017\), but is specific to the listed diffusion baseline and timing setup.

The two confirmed discrepancies admit local repairs and do not establish a Major failure of the core structured sum-block method. Initial checks of the local mathematics are complete; historical outcomes, end-to-end repairs, author adoption and independent acceptance are unverified. Scientific adjudication remains pending with no human review recorded.
