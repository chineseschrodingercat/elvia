# 2. Problem 1 (Major): bounded fan-in does not imply the asserted approximation theorem

Claim and location. On p.3, Theorem 3.3(2), labeled an informal statement of Poggio et al. (2017), assigns a graph-mimicking deep network complexity O(d epsilon^-2) for a compositionally sparse function. Definition 3.1 requires only polynomially many constituents with constant fan-in. The paragraph following Corollary 3.4 expressly says that approximation imposes no further restriction on the node-function class. Corollary 3.4 is then derived by combining Theorems 3.2 and 3.3.

Source check. The cited 2017 paper specifies uniform approximation and derivative-bounded function classes. Its Theorem 2, printed p.5, assumes a binary-tree class with constituents in W_m^2 and an infinitely differentiable, nonpolynomial activation; its rate is O((n-1) epsilon^(-2/m)). Its proof uses Lipschitz control. The arbitrary-DAG Theorem 3, p.6, still requires regularity classes for individual nodes and gives a sum of rates depending on their dimensions and smoothness. Its ReLU discussion also retains regularity assumptions. None of these statements establishes a fan-in-only theorem for arbitrary real node functions.

Exact scope of the refutation. The target omits a norm and activation class. The following counterexample uses the uniform norm and continuous activations of the cited approximation setting; it also applies to ReLU networks. Under this natural source-based interpretation the unrestricted statement is false. Choosing a different norm or a discontinuous activation class requires a newly specified theorem and proof.

\[X=[-1,1],\quad d=2^k\geq128,\qquad
f_d(x)=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_i\geq0\right\}.\] Build a complete binary tree with constituent A(u,v)=(u+v)/2, then append h(t)=1{t>=0}. The averaging tree computes the mean using d-1 binary nodes; the final threshold is unary. This construction depends on every coordinate and avoids any need to interpret unused input slots.

Original hypothesis | Counterexample check

Function X^d -> X | The output is 0 or 1, both in X.

At most O(poly d) constituents | There are exactly d nodes: d-1 averages and one threshold.

Small constant fan-in c | Every node has fan-in at most 2, independently of d.

Node outputs remain in X | Averages of members of [-1,1] remain in [-1,1]; threshold outputs also belong to X.

No extra node-class restriction | Definition 3.1 and the explicit p.3 explanation admit this threshold constituent.

Network interpretation used | Every finite feedforward network with continuous activations computes a continuous function q.

Proof. Let a=q(0). For x^(j)=(-1/j,0,...,0), the target is zero and x^(j) tends to zero. Continuity gives q(x^(j))->a, whereas f_d(0)=1. Therefore the supremum error obeys:

\[\|q-f_d\|_{\infty}\geq
\max\{|a|,|1-a|\}\geq\frac12,
\qquad 1\leq |a|+|1-a|.\] The constant function q=1/2 attains the lower bound, so the obstruction is exact. Taking epsilon=1/4 contradicts the claimed availability of a finite O(16d)-parameter approximation: no finite continuous-activation network achieves that uniform error, irrespective of size. The proof is analytic. Reviewer-written rational spot checks are supporting arithmetic checks, not substitutes for the limiting argument.

Dependency and severity. This is a substantial invalid derivation in the paper’s central link from compositional structure to approximation complexity, expressly used to obtain Corollary 3.4. That direct dependency warrants Major. The result does not require showing that the entire research agenda fails or that every restricted theorem is false. The position-paper label and informal presentation do not remove the explicit scope mismatch.

Strongest alternative readings. Restricting the claim to the original regular node classes restores a meaningful restricted theorem, but changes the target’s definition-to-theorem implication and its express fan-in-only assertion. A distribution-dependent Lp approximation can behave differently for this discontinuity and is not refuted by this example; it is also not the uniform theorem cited. Threshold activations can represent the example exactly, but require a different network class and an independently justified general rate.

Limits. The threshold function is not claimed to be polynomial-time computable under a Type-2 real-number representation. Thus this example directly refutes the unrestricted Theorem 3.3 reading, not every possible formalization of the real-valued Corollary 3.4. The invalid displayed derivation and a separately false conclusion are distinct claims.

# 3. A bounded repair and the Boolean result that survives

A defensible repair of Theorem 3.3 would restore a precise domain, norm, activation class and constituent smoothness/norm assumptions. Node count, arity and error propagation through the graph must be reflected in the bound. The source’s linear node count for a binary tree cannot simply stand for every polynomial-size DAG. This report verifies the restricted source comparison; it does not certify a replacement theorem for all real-valued FP functions.

Theorem 3.2 has a sound Boolean reading: for a fixed polynomial-time machine and each bit-input length n, a polynomial-size bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit computes its output. Use a standard finite basis such as AND/OR/NOT or NAND, and interpret multiple output bits componentwise or with a multi-output DAG. A polynomial-overhead Turing-machine-to-circuit simulation suffices; the exact sharper simulation rate is unnecessary here. The primary circuit construction in Barak’s text corroborates this implication.

Boolean Corollary 3.4 also has an independent exact construction. For a,b in {0,1}, with R(t)=max(0,t), use:

\[\operatorname{AND}(a,b)=R(a+b-1),\quad
\operatorname{OR}(a,b)=a+b-R(a+b-1),\quad
\operatorname{NOT}(a)=R(1-a).\] Each gate costs constant network size on the Boolean domain; replacing polynomially many gates yields a polynomial-size neural DAG with exact outputs. Layered implementations may need identity relays and still have polynomial size. The full four-row Boolean truth table was checked exactly. This preserves Boolean representability without invoking the defective unrestricted approximation theorem, and says nothing by itself about learning the circuit from input-output examples.

Appendix A’s broad suggestion that any k-input gate can be replaced by k-1 binary gates needs a basis qualification. It is valid for k-input AND/OR gates, not arbitrary truth-table gates. Starting with a standard bounded-fan-in simulation already supplies the needed result. This is a proof-wording clarification, not a separate rejection of Theorem 3.2.

Real-valued scope remains unproved as written. Appendix A controls complexity in bitlength n; the corollary uses coordinate dimension d and error epsilon. A real-input representation, input/output precision, runtime dependence, and a justified connection from encoded-bit networks to raw real inputs in the chosen norm are needed. Those missing links are recorded as a scope/proof gap supporting Problem 1, not counted as an additional independently refuted theorem.

# 4. Problem 2 (Minor): convolution preserves hidden-channel permutation symmetries

Claim and location. Section 4.2, p.5, attributes avoidance of weight-space permutation symmetries to CNN translational equivariance and links that mechanism to a simpler optimization landscape. Standard convolutional networks retain permutations of hidden channels.

Counterexample with actual parameters. At every spatial site z, let a hidden 1x1 convolution have two kernels K=(1,2), zero biases and ReLU activations. Follow it with a linear 1x1 convolution with channel weights L=(3,5). Simultaneously swap the hidden channels and their corresponding output weights, giving K’=(2,1), L’=(5,3). Then:

\[F(z)=3R(z)+5R(2z)=13R(z)
=5R(2z)+3R(z)=F'(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}.\] The parameter vectors are distinct, all operations remain convolutional, and applying the same rule at each location preserves translation equivariance on a periodic or unrestricted spatial domain. The equality is an algebraic identity, not merely an equality on test examples. Independent reviewer checks used a second choice of kernels and biases and confirmed the same phenomenon.

The weaker interpretation that weight sharing restricts arbitrary permutations of individual spatial units is reasonable. It does not eliminate hidden-channel permutations. A correction should identify which symmetries are restricted, retain the channel symmetry, and qualify any optimization advantage rather than infer it from complete symmetry removal.

Severity is Minor because this is a bounded architectural/optimization explanation, with no demonstrated failure of the Boolean circuit result or additional failure of the main approximation bridge. Ease of correction and the paper’s genre are not the grading basis.

# 5. Counterevidence, version checks and unperformed work

The manuscript explicitly separates efficient representation from efficient learnability in Section 4 and acknowledges cryptographic worst-case barriers. Corollary 4.3 assumes training sets for the constituents. These qualifications prevent treating the paper as a claim that arbitrary FP functions are efficiently learnable from ordinary end-to-end examples. Chain-of-thought and optimization discussions include conjectural or agenda-level claims; no new numerical benchmark is presented here.

A later related primary record, Tomaso Poggio’s arXiv:2510.11942v1, was submitted on 13 October 2025. Its description explicitly treats input quantization, output precision and neural emulators of encoded computations. It is not treated as an erratum, an audited repair, or a substitute for the ICML PDF. The chronology and relationship to the target’s 2025 memo citation were not fully reconstructed. The later abstract and HTML have differing title/author displays; bibliographic attribution here follows the official arXiv abstract.

Not performed: a full proof audit of every cited learning/generalization theorem, verification of all Rademacher-complexity constants, a formal real-computability model or certified general repair, training experiments, author-code execution, and full comparisons with OpenReview/arXiv versions. No current-paper implementation or experimental reproduction package was identified in the inspected primary materials. Absence of a new experiment in this position paper is not itself a finding.

Evidence status: the unrestricted uniform approximation counterexample and CNN algebraic counterexample are established within their stated assumptions. Missing real-precision details remain a proof/scope question. Human scientific review and final scientific decisions remain pending; no correction has been adopted by authors.
