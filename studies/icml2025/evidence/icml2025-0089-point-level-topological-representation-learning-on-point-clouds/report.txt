# 2. Problem 1 — the generalized support claim and local construction

Source claim: Theorem C.1(v), p. 18 extends the sphere support result to any contractible union M of (n+1)-simplices whose boundary n-simplices satisfy the empty-circumsphere condition. Its proof, p. 19 infers that a contractible M has boundary homotopy equivalent to an n-sphere, then transfers the support argument. Contractibility alone does not supply either that boundary topology or the common filling scale used for spheres.

Take n=1. In local coordinates define five points and let M be the union of the two filled triangles OAB and OCD. Translation of this entire configuration will be used on the next page to satisfy even the theorem’s opening sphere premises.

M is star-shaped about O and hence contractible. Its boundary is two triangular circles meeting at O; all five points are boundary points. Both filled triangles have strict empty circumdisks and are Delaunay cells. Every one of their six boundary edges is strictly Gabriel relative to the whole local point cloud: no other point is in or on its diameter disk. Exact squared-distance tests are saved; the smallest Gabriel margin is 1/2.

Any interpolation gamma in (0,1), including the default 0.3, selects a scale within this same interval. These are actual alpha-filtration events, not an arbitrary abstract complex substituted for the paper’s geometry.

# 3. Problem 1 — preserving the opening random-sphere assumptions

An interpretation concern must be addressed: C.1 begins with random samples S on a unit sphere and allows arbitrary extra points at distance at least 2 from S. The five-point configuration alone is not such a sphere sample. To avoid relying on whether ’more generally’ cancels the opening premise, retain that premise explicitly.

Let S consist of three independent uniform points on the unit circle centered at the origin. Translate the five local points and M by T=(100,0), and define X=S union (T+{O,A,B,C,D}). The external points are fixed independently of the random sample. Every translated point has norm at least 97, so its distance from every point of S is at least 96, far exceeding the required 2.

All relevant local Delaunay circumdisks and Gabriel disks lie strictly to the right of x=95; thus no point of S enters them. Every simplex joining the two components has circumradius at least 48, since it contains an edge of length at least 96. Consequently the local alpha complex and all previous strict inequalities remain unchanged through radius sqrt(3). Almost surely, the three noncollinear sphere samples form a filled triangle at radius 1; this separate component contributes no first homology during the large class’s lifetime.

Even if one additionally imports the Gabriel condition for S itself, there is a positive-probability event on which it holds. Require one sample in each arc of angular radius pi/24 centered at 0, 2pi/3 and 4pi/3. This has probability 6(1/24)^3=1/2304. Consecutive angular gaps are between 105 and 135 degrees, making the triangle acute, hence its edges strictly Gabriel. The failure below persists throughout that event, so it cannot be dismissed as a measure-zero special configuration.

M is a contractible subset of the Delaunay triangles of this full X and meets every explicitly imposed boundary condition. Thus the embedded example also covers inheritance of the initial sampling/separation premises. A further restriction that M must use only vertices of S would exclude it, but that restriction is not stated in the universal ’any contractible subset M’ clause and would narrow the claimed extension.

Evidence: math_audit_embedded_replay.py/results.json proves the uniform bounds and checks an illustrative exact matrix realization. The probability argument uses the full arc event, not a claim that one fixed equilateral sample has positive probability. The earlier local example and its narrower-scope notes remain preserved.

# 4. Problem 1 — exact harmonic output, impact and repair limits

Use edge order OA, OB, AB, OC, OD, CD. Let cs=(1,-1,1,0,0,0) and cl=(0,0,0,1,-1,1). The oriented boundary of M is g=cs+cl. Both are real cycles, so no finite-field lifting failure is involved. During the entire interval above, the local curl space is spanned by cs, the boundary of the filled small triangle.

\[P_{\rm curl}g=c_s,\qquad P_{\rm harm}g=c_l,\qquad F(O,A,B,C,D)=(1/2,0,0,1,1).\]

The first homology is one-dimensional at this scale; every nonzero representative of the surviving class has the same normalized harmonic support. There is no basis-selection ambiguity. Under the printed coface weights, the three small edges have W=1/4 and the large edges W=1. The transformed input is 2cs+cl and the weighted curl direction 2cs; projection again gives cl. The released inverse-first-power weights also remove the small component.

Thus the stated support is all five points of M, whereas the calculated support is only {O,C,D}; all sphere-component values are zero. A and B are boundary vertices, so replacing ’points contained in M’ by ’boundary points’ does not fix this example. The standard algorithm appropriately discards an already-filled component, contradicting the overbroad guarantee rather than the Hodge machinery.

Dependency: Section 3, p. 6 and the conclusion, p. 9 invoke C.1 as theoretical support for recovering point-level topological structure. Part (v) is the step extending that guarantee beyond the common-scale sphere setting. Its invalid boundary inference and missing filtration-scale control yield a demonstrably false support conclusion. Major is supported for this core theoretical extension, independently of whether the empirical clustering scores survive.

Counterevidence and limited repair: the inspected C.1(i)-(iv) sphere proof retains a common filling radius 1, boundary births below 1 and separation from mixed simplices. Those conditions avoid this failure. Withdrawing the arbitrary-contractible extension preserves that supported scope. A new extension requires explicitly justified geometric and filtration assumptions; merely adding contractibility, or changing boundary wording, is insufficient. No complete replacement theorem, author adoption or changed experiment is claimed.

# 5. Problem 2 — fixed dimension does not imply linear complex size

Source claim: Appendix E.2, p. 23, theoretical runtime item 1(a), states that for fixed ambient dimension the alpha complex has a number of simplices linear in the number of points. This is distinct from the preceding empirical observation that Figure 11 appears linear on two particular data families. The unconditional combinatorial statement is false already in dimension three.

\[P_i=(i,0,0),\quad Q_j=(0,j,1),\quad 0\le i,j<m,\qquad N=2m.\]

For every adjacent pair on each line, the four points Pi, P(i+1), Qj, Q(j+1) form a tetrahedron of nonzero determinant -1. Set x=i+1/2, y=j+1/2 and z=(x^2-y^2+1)/2. Their circumsphere has center (x,y,z) and squared radius 1/4+y^2+z^2.

\[\|P_k-(x,y,z)\|^2-r^2=(k-i)(k-i-1)>0,\quad k\notin\{i,i+1\}.\]

Hence all (m-1)^2 such tetrahedra are strict Delaunay cells, and the full alpha filtration eventually includes them. The size is at least quadratic in N, despite dimension three being fixed. Strict inequalities allow small generic perturbations; the argument does not require an ambiguous cospherical triangulation. The reviewer replay checks exact instances m=3,5,10, while the formulas establish the entire family.

Judgment: Minor for a bounded worst-case complexity assertion. It removes a general linear-size justification for scalability but does not refute measured runtimes or the use of alpha complexes on suitable data. The CGAL 6.2.1 primary manual independently documents quadratic worst-case three-dimensional triangulations and near-linear behavior under appropriate distributions.

Precise correction supported here: distinguish empirical or distribution-dependent near-linear behavior from unrestricted worst-case size, which can be quadratic already in three dimensions. This is a combinatorial correction, not a complete runtime theorem for persistent homology or iterative least squares. Evidence: complexity_and_table_replay.py/results.json and the archived CGAL manual with retrieval receipt.

Source claim: Appendix H, p. 29 defines coface weights wDelta=(1+c)^(-2), with c the number of upper cofaces; p. 31 states that these weights are used in all experiments. In pinned topf commit 3c34aa4627e212f150dbf1008b1ba2bba05f7c5b, topfmain.py line 34 defaults weight_exponent to -1. Lines 1916-1922 compute weights=(1+c)^exponent, and the square root of those weights enters the projection.

The example notebooks call TOPF without overriding this argument. This establishes a released-default/formula discrepancy, not which setting produced the paper’s figures and tables. Both weight choices remain positive and define mathematically valid weighted Hodge projections. Supported judgment: Minor for the reproducible specification mismatch; empirical linkage and historical configurations are Needs Evidence.

Passing exponent -2 would realize the printed formula algebraically. No author code was modified or executed, and no empirical repair or accuracy change was verified. The difference does not rescue the counterexample in Problem 1: either positive coface weighting projects away the already-filled triangle.

Important negative checks: the current code correctly maps a cycle e to W^(-1/2)e, then removes the weighted gradient and curl projections. The subspaces are orthogonal because successive boundary maps compose to zero. There is no demonstrated missing-weight-transform defect. The default absolute-value normalization, clipping and incident-simplex mean implement the stated construction.

The apparently suspicious zero-dimensional bypass is consistent with pinned Ripserer representatives, which contain all leaves of the merging component, rather than a singleton vertex. Ripserer 0.16.13 explicitly uses circumdiameters, explaining TOPF’s factor 1/2 and the later squared-radius conversion to GUDHI. These checked contracts reject potential singleton-support and factor-of-two allegations.

Evidence: topf_code_audit.md, topf_static_line_evidence.txt, source-cell notebook extracts, and dependency files pinned to Ripserer commit 8b296a0dff95bc301ed360b479a18392918a2b6f. Iterative-solver tolerances, degeneracy handling and every exception path were not stress-tested.

# 7. Known correction history and empirical limits

Known-issue reproduction, not a current undisclosed defect: Section 3 and Appendix B.3, p. 17 acknowledge that balanced {-1,0,1} lifts of F3 cycles need not be real cycles. Release v1.0.2, 18 August 2024, introduced detection and integer-program repair, with inspiration attributed to DreiMac. Current code checks real closure and, on solver success, seeks integer y satisfying By=Be/3 and returns e-3y.

An independent theta-graph calculation reproduces the acknowledged problem: three oriented two-edge paths from vertex 0 to vertex 4 with all coefficients 1 give boundary (-3,0,0,0,3). Taking y as one path changes that path’s coefficients to -2; the other two stay 1. The corrected boundary is exactly zero and coefficients are unchanged modulo 3. The algebraic successful repair is verified; numerical MILP behavior and infeasibility handling were not executed.

Release v1.0.3, 22 August 2024, corrected platform-specific simplex-key construction; its explicit integer conversion remains in the inspected source. Release v1.0.4, 28 January 2025, addressed performance and large clouds. These author corrections are preserved with their historical attribution and are not relabeled as newly found defects.

Empirical scope: inspected the seven-dataset benchmark, 20-run ARI reporting, harmonic-projection ablation, noise/downsampling/heterogeneity discussions, qualitative protein examples and high-dimensional demonstrations. The paper discloses failures on 4Circles+Grid and difficulties selecting features without domain knowledge. DGCNN baselines were individually tuned, and neural-feature comparisons are acknowledged as transfer settings. No missing universal selection or purely qualitative ground-truth requirement is invented.

Local timing observations: Table 1’s TOPF and TPCC means recompute to 15.057 and 298.929 seconds, consistent with printed 15.1 and 298.9. The listed SC times imply a mean between 2.743 and 2.757 seconds, versus printed 0.4; SphereinCircle lists TOPF 14.5 versus TPCC 8.0 seconds, limiting the prose claim of consistently lower runtime. These are recorded for reconciliation without a separate core-impact finding.

Raw benchmark runs, full baseline reproduction drivers and exact historical configurations were unavailable in the inspected release. No ARI, GPU/CPU runtime, protein interpretation or statistical significance was reproduced. These limitations do not nullify the analytic counterexamples.
