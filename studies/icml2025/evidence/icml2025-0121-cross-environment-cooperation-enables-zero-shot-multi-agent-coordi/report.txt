Abstract: The published procedural generator admits a Forced Coordination layout in which all required facilities are individually accessible, yet onions cannot reach any pot. This invalidates the claimed solvability guarantee, a core infrastructure contribution. A separate calculation identifies an attracting non-CEC equilibrium in the published cross-play matrix. Static inspection also leaves the historical held-out protocol unresolved. These results do not establish the frequency of impossible training tasks or overturn the reported human-study measurements. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Necessary definitions

A generated task retains a base layout’s impassable wall cells, places plate piles, onion piles, pots and delivery goals on those cells, and puts one agent in each side of the split Forced Coordination layout. Agents move on free cells and interact with adjacent facilities. Under the standard empty initialization, a completed soup requires transporting onions to a pot before collecting and delivering it. A plate pile or goal cannot serve as an empty counter on which an onion can be deposited. This distinction is explicit in the inspected interaction code.

 \dot x_i=x_i\bigl((Ax)_i-x^\top Ax\bigr),\qquad x\geq0,\quad {\bf1}^\top x=1.\] This is a deterministic analysis of the published mean matrix, not a confidence statement about the underlying empirical population.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Individual facility reachability does not guarantee solvability: Major).

## Source claim and suspect step

The abstract and contribution 2 (pp. 1–2) advertise solvable coordination challenges. Appendix A.1 (p. 14) claims that making at least one facility of each required type reachable by at least one agent guarantees a solvable task. Algorithm 1 (p. 15) implements that criterion. The missing step is reachability of the material flow from onions to pots: separate access to each facility does not ensure that ingredients can move between the agents’ disconnected walking regions.

## Admissible input

Use the \(5\times5\) Forced Coordination base with zero-based row-major cell index \(5r+c\). Its free cells are \(\{6,11,16\}\) and \(\{8,13,18\}\). Put agents at 6 and 8. Choose facilities as follows:

Facility | Mandatory cell | Supplemental cell

Plate pile | 7 | 12

Onion pile | 5 | 10

Pot | 3 | 9

Delivery goal | 17 | 19

These are eight distinct wall cells. The mandatory sequence \((7,5,3,17)\) is an admissible ordered selection from the source’s `need_one` set \[\{1,5,10,15,21,17,12,7,3,9,14,19,23\}.\] The supplementary sequence \((12,10,9,19)\) uses distinct remaining wall cells. Agent 6 is an allowed left-side start and agent 8 an allowed right-side start. These facts follow directly from `layouts.py`, lines 815–883; the independent script additionally checks them as finite set statements.

Every facility is reachable by at least one agent. In particular, both onion piles touch the left walking component, both pots touch the right one, the two plate piles touch both components, and the goal at 17 touches both. Take the permitted no-rotation/no-flip choice and pad with walls. Neither operation creates a passage. Empty inventories and empty pots are the ordinary reset setting, explicitly supported by `overcooked.py`, lines 451–455 and 500–508. The arrangement differs from the five canonical held-out tasks, so Algorithm 1 with those tasks as its held-out set accepts it. This is an admissible input to the stated generation procedure; the argument does not assert that this exact task appears in the authors’ additional 100-task sample.

## Decisive argument

Proof. Let \(L=\{6,11,16\}\) and \(R=\{8,13,18\}\). Walls prevent an agent from moving from \(L\) to \(R\). The only wall cells adjacent to both components are \(\{7,12,17\}\). They contain, respectively, a plate pile, a plate pile, and a goal. None is an empty counter, onion dispenser, or pot.

Initially there is no onion in \(R\), in either pot, or in the right agent’s inventory. An onion can originate only at cells 5 or 10, adjacent solely to \(L\). Movement preserves the component containing its carrier. Dropping an onion across the cut is impossible because all shared cells are permanent piles/goals, and those facilities are not empty counters. No direct transfer between agents exists across a wall. Therefore the stated absence of onions on the right is an invariant of every action sequence. Both pots remain empty, and no completed soup or delivery is reachable, even with an unlimited horizon. ◻

The exact interaction conditions are `table_is_empty` at line 825 and `successful_drop` at line 862 of `overcooked.py`; pile pickup preserves the pile at lines 866–872. The proof is analytic. The reviewer-written script validates the coordinates, permitted selections, walking components, and shared boundary; it does not run or emulate the author’s training code. Its output is `independent_results.json`.

## Counterevidence, dependency, and correction bounds

A shared pot or shared onion dispenser can make some split layouts solvable without an empty counter. Neither occurs here. Random initial food could also defeat this invariant, but the counterexample uses the supported empty initialization. The rule’s two copies of each facility are present; the example does not exploit missing objects, duplicate placement, or an inaccessible mandatory item.

This is Major because the constructive solvability guarantee is a named core contribution and differentiator from generators permitting impossible tasks. It does not require proving that the average reward changes. The empirical benefit of environmental diversity may survive. The proportion of generated unsolvable tasks and their effects on the published comparisons require new enumeration or experiment records. Individual reachability must be replaced or supplemented by a condition certifying feasible ingredient transfer and delivery; no complete corrected generator is claimed or verified here.

Problem 2 (The published meta-game has an attracting non-CEC mixture: Minor).

## Source claim and calculation

Section 6, Q1 (p. 7), interprets Fig. 8 as having vector directions toward CEC or CEC-Finetune in both task settings and uses this as evidence of robust cross-algorithm generalization. Appendix A.8 (p. 19) identifies Fig. 21 as the original-task payoff matrix. With order IPPO, E3T, FCP, CEC, CEC-FT, that matrix is \[A=\begin{pmatrix}
 .51&.29&.77&.47&.49\\
 .29&.43&.41&.38&.33\\
 .77&.41&.55&.58&.71\\
 .47&.38&.58&.61&.67\\
 .49&.33&.71&.67&.78
 \end{pmatrix}.\] On the IPPO–FCP edge, putting IPPO mass \(p\) gives payoffs \(.77-.26p\) and \(.55+.22p\). They agree at \(p=11/24\), so \[x^*=(11/24,0,13/24,0,0),\qquad x^{*\top}Ax^*=781/1200.\] The E3T, CEC, and CEC-FT payoffs are \(71/200\), \(1271/2400\), and \(731/1200\), respectively. Their invasion growth rates in [eq:rep] (#eq:rep) are \[-71/240,\qquad -97/800,\qquad -1/24,\] all strictly negative. The tangent eigenvalue on the IPPO–FCP edge is \(-143/1200<0\). Thus \(x^*\) is locally asymptotically stable, including against small positive invasions by every excluded strategy. It is not merely a zero-mass absorbing boundary point.

The conclusion is robust to independent symmetric rounding errors of at most \(.005\) per displayed payoff. The perturbed edge equilibrium has \(p\in[.42,.50]\). Its resident payoff is at least \(.635\), while CEC-FT’s payoff there is at most \(.6226\), and CEC’s is at most \(.5388\). The negative invasion margins persist. This rounding bound does not account for empirical sampling uncertainty.

## Alternative reading and bounded impact

A narrower reading that CEC-FT is one attracting equilibrium is correct: at its pure vertex, \(.78\) exceeds every alternative payoff against CEC-FT. Figure 8’s top-left panel also visibly contains directions toward the lower edge. Therefore the discrepancy is an overbroad dynamical interpretation, not evidence that the displayed matrix was fabricated. The original-task meta-game has multiple basins; the argument does not challenge the distinct procedural-task matrix in Fig. 22 or the human-study results. This local interpretation defect is Minor. The defensible correction is to state coexistence of CEC-FT attraction and a stable IPPO–FCP mixture rather than a general directional preference.

Problem 3 (Released held-out filtering does not implement repeated rejection: Needs Evidence for historical impact).

Appendix A.1 and Algorithm 1 (pp. 14–15) resample until the generated task is outside the held-out set. In the pinned `overcooked.py`, lines 243–257 test the initial draw and conditionally take one replacement, returning that replacement without another membership check. With independently drawn membership probability \(q>0\), one-shot replacement leaves probability \(q^2\) of returning a held-out task, whereas repeated rejection with \(q<1\) returns none. The formula is an analytic control-flow illustration, not a measured probability for JAX’s deterministic key schedule.

The training initialization does build a held-out collection (`ippo_general.py`, lines 38–80), which is counterevidence against claiming that no exclusion was attempted. The example configuration also sets `check_held_out: False`, but it defaults to a different single-task setup; that default is not proof of the setting used in CEC experiments. The inspected release therefore cannot certify the paper’s strict train/test disjointness. Actual recorded configurations, keys, and generated tasks are required to determine historical overlap and its materiality. Neither contamination nor a benchmark reduction is established. A repeated rejection loop would restore the stated exclusion property under positive probability of an admissible task; its integration and runtime behavior were not tested.

# Overall impact and remaining evidence

Problem 1 establishes failure of a core construction guarantee and determines the overall assessment, Major. Problem 2 is a bounded correction to the original-task dynamical interpretation. Problem 3 remains unresolved for the published experiments. The finite oracle arithmetic on p. 5 is consistent: \([-3+97(3-1)]/200=.955\), and the difference from \(.931\) is approximately \(2.51\%\) of the oracle value.

The paper explicitly distinguishes higher human preference ratings from lower reward than E3T; that distinction was preserved. The within-participant protocol and seven-item ratings were checked descriptively, but the unit used for statistical tests cannot be reconstructed from the supplied aggregated plots. No significance finding is manufactured from that gap. No correction is documented in the inspected paper or README; a comprehensive search of later versions/errata was not performed. The pinned source’s correspondence to historical experiment code is unverified.
