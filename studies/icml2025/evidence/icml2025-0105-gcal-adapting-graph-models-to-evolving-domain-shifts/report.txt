Abstract: The information-bottleneck lower bound in Theorem 3.1 is false on its continuous-feature domain. A finite Gaussian construction has true objective zero and claimed lower bound approximately 2.493. The proof drops a conditional differential entropy that can be negative. This directly affects the stated theoretical foundation of the memory-generation objective and supports Major. The released edge sampler also has a Major discrepancy in the core graph-generation distribution; the Gaussian sampling formula has a separate Minor error. The empirical tables are not independently invalidated. Adjudication remains pending; this is an initial scientific review.

Write \(G\) for an original graph, \(Z\) for its latent representation, \(M=\widehat G\) for a generated memory graph, and \(Y\) for its discrete training signal. By the mutual-information chain rule, the objective inside Eq. (4) is \[J=I(M;Y)-\beta I(M;G,Z)+\beta I(M;Z\mid G)

# Problem 1: Theorem 3.1 drops a potentially negative conditional entropy

Assessment: Major; false core theoretical bound.

#### Claim and exact failed step.

Theorem 3.1, p. 4, Eq. (5), repeated as Theorem A.1, p. 13, claims the lower bound \[B=\mathbb E\log P_f(Y\mid M)
-\beta\mathbb E\operatorname{KL}\bigl(P_g(M\mid G,Z)\Vert Q(M)\bigr)
+\beta\mathbb E\log P_g(M\mid G,Z).\] Appendix A, Eq. (21), replaces \[I(M;Z\mid G)=\mathbb E\log P_g(M\mid G,Z)+h(M\mid G)\] by the first term alone. The first sentence on p. 14 explicitly justifies this by nonnegativity of the conditional entropy. That justification holds for discrete Shannon entropy. Conditional differential entropy of Gaussian node features may be negative.

#### Counterexample satisfying the stated probabilistic assumptions.

Let \(G\) be a fixed graph with one hundred vertices and fixed features; let the memory graph have one vertex, fixed empty edge structure, and a real feature \(M\). This also permits a smaller memory than the original. Set \(\beta=1\). Let \(Z,V\) be independent standard normals and define \[s=0.01,\qquad M=\frac{s}{\sqrt2}(Z+V).\] The memory is genuinely conditioned on the latent variable: \(M\mid G,Z\sim N(sZ/\sqrt2,s^2/2)\). Its marginal is \(N(0,s^2)\), and choose \(Q\) equal to this exact marginal, a valid variational approximation under the theorem. Let \(Y=0\) deterministically and use a binary classifier with \(P_f(0\mid M)=1/2\). The theorem does not require a nondegenerate label distribution or an optimal classifier.

All densities are nonsingular, and every entropy, mutual information, and KL term used below is finite. The source and label are constant, so \(J=0\). Moreover, \[\begin{aligned}
I(M;Z)&=\tfrac12\log2,\\
\mathbb E\operatorname{KL}(P_g(M\mid G,Z)\Vert Q)
 &=\tfrac12\log2,\\
\mathbb E\log P_g(M\mid G,Z)
 &=-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,s^2/2).\end{aligned}\] Consequently the stated lower bound evaluates to \[B=-\log2-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,s^2)
=2.493084472\ldots>0=J.\] This is a false final inequality, not merely a missing justification. Equation (4) also prints an outer maximization: if read literally as an optimum rather than a pointwise objective, the same example still refutes it, since fixed \(G\) and fixed \(Y\) make \(J=0\) for every generator. Its maximum remains zero.

#### The fixed standard-normal prior does not rescue the pointwise bound.

The implementation uses a standard-normal feature prior rather than the narrow prior above. For a second finite example, let \(X,U,V\) be independent standard normals, \(a=\sqrt{1-s^2}\), and \[G\text{ contains feature }X,\qquad Z=aX+sU,\qquad M=aZ+sV.\] Let \(Y\) be an independent fair binary label, \(P_f(Y\mid M)=1/2\), and \(Q=N(0,1)\). Then \(M\) is standard normal, \(G\to Z\to M\) is a Gaussian Markov chain, and \[\begin{aligned}
J&=\tfrac12\log(2s^2-s^4)=-4.258621596\ldots,\\
B&=-\log2-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e)=-2.112085714\ldots.\end{aligned}\] Again \(B>J\). Here the omitted conditional entropy is \(h(M\mid G)=-2.839683063\ldots\). The first construction handles the theorem’s arbitrary \(Q\) and outer-max notation; this second one addresses the proposed prior for the pointwise surrogate. Neither construction purports to reproduce historical learned embeddings. Their role is to test the universal probabilistic claim.

#### Dependencies, counterevidence, and severity.

The mutual-information chain rule and the KL upper bound in Eq. (20) are valid. A fully discrete model with finite entropies would also justify the disputed entropy drop, but the paper specifies continuous Gaussian features and does not discretize them or assume nonnegative conditional differential entropy. Quantization would define a different bound with its reference measure and resolution; it is not an implicit cure.

The abstract, contribution statement on p. 2, and Sections 3.2.3–3.2.5 explicitly use this lower bound to motivate three generator losses. In particular, the positive conditional log-density term is the proposed basis for the generation loss. The false bound removes that theoretical justification. This dependency warrants Major even if the practical heuristic, ablations, and empirical improvements remain useful. No reduction in reported AP or retention is established by these constructions.

#### What a valid repair would and would not restore.

For discrete \(Y\), \(\beta\ge0\), appropriate absolute continuity, and finite quantities, a valid looser bound is \[J\ge
\mathbb E\log P_f(Y\mid M)
-\beta\mathbb E\operatorname{KL}(P_g(M\mid G,Z)\Vert Q).\] Indeed \(I(M;Y)\ge\mathbb E\log P_f(Y\mid M)\); \(I(M;G)\le I(M;G,Z)\); and the expected KL is \(I(M;G,Z)+\operatorname{KL}(P_M\Vert Q)\). Alternatively retain the missing \(\beta h(M\mid G)\) in the original derivation. Both options change the theoretical objective or require an additional entropy calculation. They do not establish the paper’s original three-term bound or justify substituting the embedding discrepancy of Eq. (14) for a likelihood without further assumptions. No repaired algorithm or performance guarantee is certified.

# Problem 2: Eq. (7) samples the wrong Gaussian variance

Assessment: Minor; local formula error, with corrective source evidence.

The text on p. 4 defines \(\log\sigma\) as log standard deviation. Eq. (7) states a sample from \(N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) but writes \(\mu+\sigma^2\odot\varepsilon\), with \(\varepsilon\sim N(0,I)\). For one dimension, \(\mu=0,\sigma=2\), the stated law has variance four, whereas the displayed expression has variance sixteen. Every assumption is satisfied. The correct expression is \(\mu+\sigma\varepsilon\), or \(\mu+\exp(v/2)\varepsilon\) for log variance \(v\).

The released `VGAE.reparameter` function actually computes `exp(0.5*logvar)` and multiplies Gaussian noise by it, consistent with the corrected law. Renaming \(\sigma^2\) as the standard deviation would conflict with the printed distribution and explicit definition. This establishes a local mathematical specification error, not a demonstrated error in sampled training features. The variance identity and exact values are in the independent check output.

# Problem 3: the released edge sampler is categorical rather than independent Bernoulli

Assessment: Major; core graph-generation distribution mismatch in the pinned release.

Section 3.2.2, p. 4, describes an independent Bernoulli edge for each pair, with a per-edge continuous relaxation. In the pinned `GCAL.py`, `PGE.forward`, lines 30–68, instead applies `gumbel_softmax` to the \(K\times K\) matrix and retains entries exceeding \(0.8\). The PyTorch v2.2.1 source, matching the supplied dependency version, confirms the default last-axis normalization and one-hot hard branch [Retained latex: \cite{torch}]. The operation therefore normalizes across each row. Thus if \(p_j\) are a row’s relaxed entries, \[p_j\ge0,\quad\sum_jp_j=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\#\{j:p_j>0.8\}\le1.\] The hard branch likewise chooses one category per row. In contrast, two independent Bernoulli edges of probability \(1/2\) are simultaneously present with probability \(1/4\). The distributions are therefore different, even before comparing training results. No author function was run; the conclusion follows from row normalization and the inspected threshold.

The file contains a later two-category-per-edge sampling block, but it follows an unconditional return and is unreachable in this function. That block is counterevidence about possible intended design, not proof that the earlier executed branch implements it. A repair would apply a binary relaxation independently to each edge with the appropriate probability/logit convention and validate the resulting graph distribution. This was not implemented or tested.

The affected sampler is on the active memory-generation path: `VGAE.decoder` calls it at lines 148–152, and memory replay calls the same generator at lines 165–167. Its synthetic adjacency enters the generation losses at lines 253–269, while the edge regularizer uses a Bernoulli model at lines 157–158. The support restriction therefore changes the graph distributions available to a central component, rather than an unused helper. This demonstrated core method mismatch supports Major for the pinned implementation. Without historical code identity and logs, no assertion is made that the reported tables used this branch or would lose accuracy.

# Additional scope and empirical boundaries

The AP and AF definitions on p. 6 were checked; AF uses final-minus-initial performance and is therefore oriented upward as shown. Table 2’s reported GCAL means exceed the listed baselines on the four datasets, and Table 3 supports improvements in the reported ablations. These are checks of the displayed results, not independent reproductions.

Several source-correspondence questions require evidence. The release passes `argmax` labels into Twitch’s `roc_auc_score`, so that path computes AUC from binary decisions rather than continuous prediction scores. A simple ranking example gives score AUC one but hard-decision AUC one-half; this does not identify which path produced the paper’s table. The release also includes the source-domain test column in its aggregate performance matrix, while the formal setup describes a target-domain sequence. The exact domain list and aggregation used historically should be supplied.

Furthermore, Eq. (14) uses a norm of sums of embeddings, whereas the released `MMD_loss` compares means by mean squared error. The paper says that a new generator is used at each step and memory graphs are retained (p. 6); the inspected class instead retains latent parameter pairs and reuses its generator to reconstruct replay edges. These are version-bound observations, not assertions that historical results used the current implementation. Resolving their empirical importance requires the experiment commit, configuration, per-domain predictions, and saved memories.

# Disposition

The overall assessment is Major. Problem 1 refutes the core theoretical lower bound, and Problem 3 demonstrates a different graph distribution on the pinned release’s central memory-generation and replay path. Problem 2 remains Minor because the released Gaussian sampler implements the corrected law. Historical experimental effects remain unresolved.
