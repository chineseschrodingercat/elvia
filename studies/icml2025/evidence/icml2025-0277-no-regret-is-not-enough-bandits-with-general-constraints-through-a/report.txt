Abstract: The contextual extension’s realized-policy regret guarantee is false under its printed definitions. An admissible strictly feasible instance with exact mean regressors gives linear regret and violates the claimed adversarial-context reward guarantee. A separate small-scale instance contradicts the literal quadratic scale factor in the same core primal bound. The noncontextual competitive coefficient and dual-energy mechanism have independent support; local proof defects and unresolved stochastic assumptions are distinguished from those demonstrated failures. # Major: realized hindsight policies can memorize reward noise

Problem 1 (Major; Lemma 8.4 and Corollary 8.5).  Section 3.1 defines the adversarial comparator using realized rewards, \[\operatorname{Opt}_{\rm Adv}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}
 \sum_{t=1}^T f_t(x).\] Section 8.2 sets \(\mathcal X=\Pi=\{\pi:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal A\}\), the set of all deterministic policies, and explicitly retains realized utilities. Lemma 8.4 then claims sublinear interval regret against this entire class from a small error in predicting conditional means. That implication fails.

## A complete admissible instance

Let \(K=3\), \(m=1\), \(\mathcal Z=\mathbb N\), and take the oblivious context sequence \(z_t=t\). Draw \(B_t\) independently and uniformly from \(\{1,2,3\}\), and define the random functions \[f_t(z,a)=\mathbf1\{a=B_t\},\qquad g_t(z,a)=-1
 \quad\hbox{for every }z,a.\] Rewards and costs satisfy the required bounds. Their conditional means are \(\bar f=1/3\) and \(\bar g=-1\); a class containing these constant functions is realizable. Exact mean regressors have zero squared prediction error. Apply the claimed theorem with the valid positive upper bound \(\mathrm{Err}=1\), so the argument does not depend on a missing additive term at zero error.

Every action is strictly feasible, with \(\rho_{\rm Adv}=1\). Zero-initialized projected OGD remains at \(\lambda_t=0\), since \([0-\eta]_+=0\). Hence the utility scale is \(L=1\). Algorithm 3 assigns equal predicted scores, so \(\mu_t=3\) and it samples uniformly. In fact, every nonanticipating learner has conditional success probability \(1/3\), regardless of its selected action.

For each realized sequence, there exists a single fixed policy with \(\pi(t)=B_t\). The policy is selected by the hindsight supremum; it is not an online learner. Distinct contexts permit these assignments without conflict. Consequently \[\operatorname{Opt}_{\rm Adv}=T,\qquad
 \operatorname{Rew}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(T,1/3),\qquad
 R^P_{[T]}(\Pi)=T-\operatorname{Rew}.\] The full-horizon regret is therefore \(2T/3+O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt T)\), whereas Lemma 8.4 asserts \(\widetilde O(\sqrt T)\).

The parent independently enumerated all \(3^8=6561\) joint action and reward-label sequences at \(T=4\). The reward probabilities for \(0,1,2,3,4\) successes are \[(16,32,24,8,1)/81.\] Every hindsight optimum equals four, expected reward is \(4/3\), and expected regret is \(8/3\). This finite check complements the general analytical contradiction; it is not a simulation of author code.

## Direct damage to the stated core conclusion

Corollary 8.5, page 9, would require \[\operatorname{Rew}\ge \frac{T}{2}-r(T),
 \qquad r(T)=\widetilde O(\sqrt T).\] For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(r(T)\le T/12\). Hoeffding’s inequality then gives \[\Pr\{\operatorname{Rew}\ge T/2-r(T)\}
 \le \Pr\{\operatorname{Rew}\ge5T/12\}
 \le e^{-T/72}.\] Thus the claimed high-probability reward event instead has probability approaching zero. All violations equal \(-T\); feasibility is not the source of failure. At \(T=10^{12}\), \(\delta=.05\), the explicit page-22 bound \(504\log(T^2/\delta)\sqrt{3T}\) is approximately \(5.086\times10^{10}<T/2\), while regret exceeds \(T/2\) except with probability at most \(e^{-T/18}\).

The invalid dependency is visible on page 22: an analysis based on conditional-mean regression is applied directly to the supremum of realized utility differences over all policies. A union bound over time intervals does not control this additional hindsight selection. The demonstrated failure reaches both a core lemma and the resulting contextual guarantee, supporting Major.

## Counterevidence and correction scope

With a comparator defined using conditional mean rewards, every policy in this example earns mean \(T/3\), and this contradiction disappears. Restricting the policy class and paying an appropriate complexity term is another possible change. These are substantive qualifications to the printed comparison. No repaired general contextual theorem is certified here. The witness does not refute the finite-action noncontextual framework, its competitive coefficient, or Lemma 8.3.

# Major: the literal quadratic scale bound also fails

Problem 2 (Major, scoped to the core primal instantiation).  Definition 6.1 and Lemma 8.4 define \(L\) as the actual supremum of absolute utilities, without a floor at one. Even with a single repeated context and deterministic rewards, Algorithm 3 does not satisfy the stated \(L^2\) bound for arbitrarily small \(L\).

Take two actions, one constraint \(g=-1\), and deterministic rewards \((\varepsilon,0)\), where \(\varepsilon=T^{-1/2}\). Again \(\rho=1,\lambda_t=0\), exact regressors are available, and \(\mathrm{Err}=1\) is a valid bound. With the prescribed \(\eta_P=\sqrt{2T}\), Algorithm 3 has constant suboptimal-action probability \[\mu=\frac{2-\sqrt2+\sqrt6}{2},\qquad
 p_{\rm bad}=\frac1{\mu+\sqrt2}\simeq0.341081.\] Indeed \(1/\mu+1/(\mu+\sqrt2)=1\). Its regret is \(\varepsilon N_{\rm bad}\), with \(N_{\rm bad}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(T,p_{\rm bad})\). Expected regret is \(p_{\rm bad}\sqrt T\), and with probability at least \(1-e^{-p_{\rm bad}T/8}\) it is at least \(p_{\rm bad}\sqrt T/2\).

The claimed scale term is instead \[504\,L^2\log(T^2/\delta)\sqrt{2T}
 =504\sqrt2\,\log(T^2/\delta)/\sqrt T.\] At \(T=10^{12},\delta=.05\), the lower regret threshold is \(170540.689\), whereas the printed bound is \(0.041524\). These values and the normalization equation were independently checked.

Appendix D retains additive exploration terms before its last line; those terms cannot be absorbed uniformly into \(L^2\) as \(L\to0\). Also, its error bound in Equation (12) uses \(1+\sup_t\|\lambda_t\|_1^2\); the inequality \(L\le1+\sup_t\|\lambda_t\|_1\) has the wrong direction to justify the needed replacement by \(O(L^2)\).

This invalidates another essential property of the stated contextual primal algorithm. The severity concerns that core derivation, not an assertion that the self-bounding phenomenon is false. An explicit envelope at least \(1+\sup_t\|\lambda_t\|_1\), with appropriate localization, could address this scale issue. The first Problem uses \(L=1\) and survives such an adjustment. An envelope-based proof has not been completed here.

# Minor: the no-regret counterexample has inconsistent costs

Problem 3 (Minor; Example 5.2 and Proposition 5.3).  Example 5.2, page 5, sets the final-third costs of \(a_2,a_3\) to one. Figure 1 and the proof on page 13 instead use \(\rho\). The displayed proof also concludes \((2M\rho-2)T/3\le0\) from \(M\ge1/\rho\), which only gives the opposite sign, except at equality.

Using the example’s printed cost one, the proposed sequence \(a_3,a_2,a_2\) across the three phases, and multipliers \(0,0,M\), the exact primal regret for \(M\ge1/\rho\) is \[R_T^P=\frac T3\bigl[M(1+\rho)-2\bigr].\] At \(\rho=1/2,M=2\), this equals \(T/3>0\), so that sequence does not satisfy the claimed nonpositive primal-regret premise. Its dual regret is zero and violation is \(T/3\), not \(\rho T/3\).

The parent recomputed all three comparator utilities. Crucially, \(\rho=1,M=1\) gives both regrets zero and violation \(T/3\). Thus the existential conclusion that ordinary no-regret guarantees alone are insufficient survives in a valid special case. This is a local worked-example correction, assessed Minor.

# Checks that support narrower conclusions

## Competitive coefficient and noncontextual reward argument

Theorem 4.2’s two-instance construction obeys the bounded-cost and strict-feasibility requirements. Its limit establishes the asymptotic competitive constant. Independently, the constant instance \[f(a_1)=1,\ f(a_2)=0,\qquad
 g(a_1)=1,\ g(a_2)=-\rho\] gives \(V=(1+\rho)N-\rho T\), \(\operatorname{Rew}=N\), and \(\operatorname{Opt}_{\rm Adv}=T\). Sublinear expected violation forces asymptotic reward fraction at most \(\rho/(1+\rho)\).

For Theorem 7.2, mixing an optimal action with a fixed safe strategy using weight \(\rho/(1+\rho)\) yields nonpositive mean costs and at least that fraction of optimal reward. Combining a valid primal regret bound with dual regret against zero gives \[\operatorname{Rew}\ge
 \frac{\rho}{1+\rho}\operatorname{Opt}_{\rm Adv}
 -L^2R^P_{T,\delta}-\frac{\eta mT}{2}.\] This conditional argument checks out. The stray page-19 assertion that the played actions themselves have nonpositive weighted costs does not follow from comparator feasibility, but is not needed for this derivation.

## Self-bounding mechanism and remaining proof conditions

Projected OGD implies the energy inequality \[\sum_{t=a}^{b-1}\langle\lambda_t,g_t\rangle
 \ge\frac{\|\lambda_b\|_2^2-\|\lambda_a\|_2^2}{2\eta}
       -\frac{\eta mT}{2}.\] At the paper’s threshold crossings, conservative bounds \(\|\lambda_a\|_1\le3/\rho\), \(\|\lambda_b\|_1\ge13m/\rho\), and \(\eta^2mT\le1\) give the lower bound \((169m-10)/(2\eta\rho^2)\). This independently supports the dual-energy mechanism and supplies counterevidence against treating appendix arithmetic slips as a disproof of Lemma 6.2.

The full stochastic proof still needs explicit stopping or clipping to replace a whole-horizon random payoff/multiplier maximum by a deterministic pre-crossing envelope. Lemma B.3’s displayed invocation of Azuma with a future-dependent random maximum is not sufficient by itself. Confidence-event allocation and endpoint indexing also need consistent treatment. No counterexample to the properly localized self-bounding statement is established here.

The stochastic model should specify conditional means given history, or fresh independent draws. Mere common marginal means would not suffice. Theorem 7.3 also mixes per-round and cumulative \(\operatorname{Opt}_{\rm Stoc}\); writing \(B_T=T\sup_{\xi:\mathbb E_\xi\bar g\le0}\mathbb E_\xi\bar f\) resolves that notation. Under the usual stochastic interpretation and a valid self-bound, its intended reward argument is recoverable. These are Needs Evidence/proof-scope qualifications, not additional demonstrated Major conclusions.
