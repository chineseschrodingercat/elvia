# Problem 1 | A scalar plateau does not prove a martingale

## Source claim, location and suspect inference

Section 5.1, p. 7, first states a distribution-level rollout identity, then says the reported standard deviations stabilize, ’fulfilling the Martingale property’. Figure 2, p. 6, supplies the scalar trajectory; Appendix A.1 and Appendix B are on p. 15. The problem is the inference from a plateau in one statistic to equality of probability distributions.

## Definitions and complete check

Let Q_D be the predictive distribution at a fixed query x given the observed dataset D. Let Z be an additional observation drawn through the stated sequential predictive sampling procedure. The one-step distribution-valued property requires equality for every measurable set A; longer-horizon identities follow by iterating an appropriate one-step identity.

\[Q_D(A)=\mathbb E\!\left[Q_{D\cup\{Z\}}(A)\mid D\right]\quad\text{for every measurable }A.\] Countermodel to the diagnostic’s sufficiency: define Q_D = Normal(n, 1), where n is the number of observations. This is a proper finite-moment predictive distribution for each finite dataset. Inputs may follow the paper’s independent uniform input distribution; the rule ignores their values. Draw Z from Q_D and append it. The resulting predictive distribution is Normal(n+1, 1) for every sampled Z, so its mixture over Z is also Normal(n+1, 1).

\[\operatorname{SD}(Q_D)=\operatorname{SD}\!\left(\mathbb E_Z Q_{D\cup\{Z\}}\right)=1,\qquad Q_D\ne\mathbb E_Z Q_{D\cup\{Z\}}.\] For A = (-infinity, n+1/2], the two probabilities are Phi(1/2) and Phi(-1/2), which are unequal because the normal CDF is strictly increasing. Repeating this construction gives stable standard deviation at every rollout depth while the distribution keeps changing. Thus even an exactly flat standard-deviation curve cannot imply the paper’s displayed property.

Assumption boundary: this construction refutes the sufficiency of the statistic. It is not asserted to be the trained PFN, its neural implementation, or a counterexample satisfying the specific GP training experiment. The conclusion does not require that assertion. No failure rate or empirical degradation of the actual PFN is inferred.

## Impact and precise correction

The supported correction is to describe stabilization of the reported scalar statistic and leave the full martingale property unresolved. Establishing that property would require a correctly specified distribution-level comparison, or sufficiently justified separating statistics with an explicit tolerance and sampling uncertainty. This report verifies the logical scope of that correction; it does not supply new empirical verification.

# Counterevidence | What Figure 2 may measure

## Conditional uncertainty versus mixture uncertainty

If the plotted quantity is average within-trajectory predictive standard deviation, an exact Bayesian predictor can decrease it. Under the paper’s GP prior (RBF length scale 0.1, output scale 1, observation-noise variance 10^-8, uniform inputs), standard Gaussian conditioning gives the following latent-variance update. C_D is the posterior latent covariance after the initial dataset, and v_D(x) = C_D(x,x).

\[v_{D\cup\{(X,Y)\}}(x)=v_D(x)-\frac{C_D(x,X)^2}{v_D(X)+10^{-8}}.\] The denominator is positive, and the reduction is strict wherever the posterior cross-covariance is nonzero. It is independent of Y. Adding the same observation-noise variance to both sides preserves the predictive-variance reduction. Nevertheless, integrating the entire conditional predictive distribution over the newly sampled Y restores the original predictive distribution by the tower property. Thus shrinking conditional SD is not by itself a martingale violation.

Conversely, the variance of the mixture over trajectories contains both the average conditional variance and the variance of the conditional means. These two terms can compensate. If Figure 2 uses mixture uncertainty, the preceding GP argument does not refute its Bayesian null. If its horizontal axis instead denotes the number of independent Monte Carlo rollouts used to estimate a fixed mixture, estimator convergence must also be separated from predictive inconsistency.

# Other checked reasoning and conclusion

## Cross-entropy objective (Sections 2.1-2.2)

\[\mathbb E[-\log q(Y\mid X,D)]=\mathbb E[H(p(\cdot\mid X,D))]+\mathbb E[\operatorname{KL}(p(\cdot\mid X,D)\|q(\cdot\mid X,D))].\] This identity follows by adding and subtracting log p, under common measure/support and integrability conditions. The entropy term does not depend on the learned parameters. It justifies the ideal population target; it does not guarantee finite-capacity optimization or out-of-prior accuracy. The paper itself acknowledges these approximation and support limits. No issue was found in this inspected derivation.

## Coin-counting limitation (Section 6.5; Appendix A.1)

For the exact discrete prior p_j = j/100, j = 1,...,99, and n observed heads, Bayes’ rule gives m_n = sum_j p_j^(n+1) / sum_j p_j^n. The next increment equals Var_n(p)/m_n > 0. Independent exact-rational calculation gives m_1 = 199/300 = 0.6633333333, m_10 = 0.9119988890 and m_100 = 0.9844204810.

With identical context embeddings, no positional/count features and the Figure 1 attention mask, normalized attention returns the same value vector independently of the number of duplicate tokens. Tokenwise residual, feed-forward and normalization operations preserve this invariance. A test token attending only to identical training tokens cannot distinguish n from n+1. These conditions explain the stated original-architecture limitation. A special token, positional/count encoding or injected nonidentical features changes the assumptions; the paper explicitly discusses workarounds. No issue was found in the qualitative counting argument; trained numerical outputs were not reproduced.

## Additional scope clarifications

Synthetic pretraining eliminates direct reuse of real evaluation rows in those pretraining examples, provided the generator is independent of the held-out test data. It does not itself certify freedom from evaluation-driven prior design or downstream preprocessing leakage. The paper’s wording was interpreted within this pretraining context; no concrete leakage finding is asserted. Claims about future dominance, latent prediction and new architectures remain proposals.
