Abstract: Daily Oracle supplies a useful, densely sampled retrospective benchmark, and the reported figures support date-related differences in accuracy. The central inference that residual decline identifies outdated internal representations, and consequently the necessity of continuous pre-training, is not established by the design. Gold-context truncation in the pinned release further weakens the claim that all relevant information was supplied. Expanding regression slopes do not identify accelerating local decline; chronology validation and several aggregation conventions also require repair. The overall assessment is Major because a central explanatory conclusion is unsupported, while the descriptive benchmark contribution remains intact.

# Identity, contribution and inspected evidence

Hui Dai, Ryan Teehan and Mengye Ren, ICML 2025, I25-43699 (0330), Are LLMs Prescient? A Continuous Evaluation using Daily News as the Oracle. The frozen original has 35 pages. All main scientific text (pp. 1–10) and Appendices A–D (pp. 13–35), including all prompt text, were read. All Figures 1–22 were visually inspected, together with selected prompt figures. Tables 2–4 were checked against the rendered pages. Bibliography entries were not independently audited.

The acquired Daily Oracle repository is commit `9166ceb7b4badc3ac198077b0f030e9bd400ee5a`, dated 27 July 2025. Complete static reads covered seven Python files, README and requirements. No author program, API evaluation, training, dataset-generation pipeline or model was executed. The full news/QA datasets, response records, scoring/plotting implementation and separate prompt files were not in the acquired source-only mirror. The paper supplies prompt text, but byte identity with deployed prompts is unverified. Separate OpenReview material was unavailable behind an API 403/browser challenge. Code-level observations below concern this pinned release, not a proven reconstruction of the historical experiments.

The pipeline selects six articles per day, generates two true/false (TF) and two multiple-choice (MC) questions per article, then applies seven quality criteria with a threshold of 13 out of 14. The evaluated collection contains 16,783 TF and 14,727 MC questions from 2020–2024. Eight models are compared in closed-book, temporally restricted retrieval, and gold-article settings. There is no formal forecasting/generalization theorem. The reported beginning/end TF drop, \(64.68\%\to50.74\%\), is \(13.94\) percentage points or \(21.55\%\) relatively. For MC, \(58.30\%\to51.69\%\) gives \(6.61\) points, about \(11.34\%\) relatively; the stated \(11.33\%\) can reflect underlying unrounded values. These are descriptive summaries, not independently recomputed model results.

# The central explanatory inference

Problem 1 (A date trend does not identify representation obsolescence).  The abstract, Section 4.2 (p. 7) and Section 4.3 (p. 8) attribute residual gold-article decline to outdated representations and infer that continuous pre-training is still needed. Severity: Major. The experimental controls do not identify this mechanism or necessity, materially weakening a central scientific conclusion without invalidating the benchmark or its reported accuracy differences.

For a fixed model \(g\), let \(D_t\) be the distribution of generated questions, choices and supplied contexts at date \(t\). Observed accuracy is \[A_t=\mathbb{E}_{D_t}[\mathbf1\{g(X)=Y\}]
     =\sum_z\pi_t(z)a_t(z).\] A change in \(A_t\) does not distinguish changed mixture weights \(\pi_t\) from changed conditional accuracies \(a_t\). For example, keep accuracy on remembered outcomes at \(0.9\) and on unseen forecast questions at \(0.5\). Reducing the remembered fraction from \(0.8\) to \(0.2\) changes accuracy from \(0.82\) to \(0.58\) with unchanged forecasting ability. The paper itself explicitly distinguishes pre-cutoff historical questions from post-cutoff future questions (Section 4.1); combining them does not measure a single invariant ability. This example does not assert the actual memorization fractions or erase the practical cost of stale knowledge.

Even complete gold articles do not remove question difficulty, changing entities, distracting content, answer extraction or generator effects. Hold gold-context accuracy fixed at \(0.95\) for easy questions and \(0.70\) for hard questions. Reducing the easy fraction from \(0.8\) to \(0.2\) changes aggregate accuracy from \(0.90\) to \(0.75\) without any change in either conditional ability. This is an explicit nonidentification example, not an estimated decomposition of Figure 5. The generator/filter models also change from GPT-4/GPT-3.5 to GPT-4o/GPT-4o-mini around October 2024 (p. 4, footnote 6). That creates a design change near the end of the time axis; it cannot explain the entire earlier decline, but it must be controlled when interpreting the final months. A roughly stable category histogram in Figure 8 does not establish stable within-category difficulty.

There is also a concrete implementation obstacle to the claimed full-context control. In `evaluation/eval.py`, lines 127–128, gold articles pass through `get_first_k_words`, whose default at lines 183–187 is 512 words. The generation code in `qa-generation/utils.py` passes the whole article to prompts after accepting texts between 800 and 10,000 characters. A 5,644-character synthetic article with its answer at word 513 is therefore admitted by that length rule but loses the answer under the released gold preprocessing. This independent construction proves that the implementation does not guarantee answer-containing gold context. It does not prove any particular dataset item was affected, nor that this code version generated Figure 5. Historical configuration and per-question answer-span coverage are necessary to settle those questions.

The authors provide relevant counterevidence: Figures 9–11 show that declines remain after excluding refusals, and gold accuracy is often near \(0.9\). Thus refusal alone is not a sufficient alternative explanation, and many questions are answerable. The limitation section acknowledges generator and event-selection bias and the short post-cutoff horizon. Those concessions do not turn the gold experiment into a causal isolation of internal representations. Calling gold retrieval an “upper bound” also requires an operational assumption: additional or differently arranged context can change an LLM’s answers, so correctness is not monotone in context inclusion.

Repair requires freezing the generator and evaluator, auditing answer coverage, comparing full versus truncated gold text, controlling item difficulty/topic/source and separating historical recall from genuine post-cutoff forecasts. To establish a benefit of continued pre-training, compare matched models with and without that intervention on the same held-out future items, alongside retrieval/adaptation alternatives. Without these controls, the defensible conclusion is descriptive decline on this retrospectively constructed distribution; the proposed mechanism remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated necessity.

# Temporal statistics and chronology

Problem 2 (The expanding slope is not a local rate of deterioration).  Section 4.3 and Figure 6 use increasingly negative expanding-window coefficients as evidence of faster decline near and after the cutoff. Severity: Minor for the statistic itself; it reinforces the identification problem above. An expanding-window slope does not estimate the derivative at its last month.

For the specified monthly decay \(\lambda=0.995\), an observation 59 months old retains relative weight \(0.995^{59}=0.744\), and the weight half-life is \(\log(1/2)/\log(0.995)=138.28\) months. The fit therefore remains strongly influenced by earlier levels. As an independent check, let raw accuracy be \(0.8\) for months 1–36 and \(0.6\) for months 37–60. After a trailing five-month mean, expanding weighted slopes are approximately \(-0.000225\), \(-0.003078\), \(-0.004954\) and \(-0.005197\) at months 37, 42, 48 and 60. They become more negative although the post-change raw series is exactly constant. These are accuracy fractions per month; they are illustrative values, not digitizations of Figure 6. The paper’s moving-average alignment is not specified, so the calculation declares its own trailing alignment.

An expanding fit is a valid descriptive summary of accumulated data, but its coefficient cannot by itself establish ongoing local acceleration or a cutoff-induced change. Report unsmoothed monthly denominators and uncertainty, specify smoothing/alignment, and compare segmented or local models with separate level and slope changes. Multiple QA pairs share an article and event, so treating every question as independent understates uncertainty; article/event or date blocks are appropriate for sensitivity analysis. No significance calculation was reproduced without the raw data.

Table 3 also needs an explicit definition of the YoY observations, weights and cutoff masks. If Pre and Post exhaustively partition the same nonnegatively weighted observations, their combined mean must satisfy \[\min(\bar y_{\rm pre},\bar y_{\rm post})\leq\bar y_{\rm all}
 \leq\max(\bar y_{\rm pre},\bar y_{\rm post}).\] TF GPT-3.5 reports \((-4.33,-3.43,-2.84)\), and MC Claude reports \((-6.26,-11.78,-5.03)\), in Pre/Post/Avg order; both violate that interpretation. Excluding cutoff-straddling intervals or using different masks can explain the numbers. Consequently this is an unresolved definition/reproducibility problem, not a proved arithmetic error. Annual means are insufficient to reconstruct monthly YoY changes.

Problem 3 (Publication time does not certify earliest answerability).  Section 3.1 equates article publication with resolution time; Appendix A.3 requires that the answer was unavailable earlier. The prompts and quality audit do not establish that requirement. Severity: Minor with potentially material unknown prevalence; a dataset-wide invalid-question rate has not been measured in this review.

Figure 23 requires an event newly occurring on publication day, yet its second example accepts a May 28 temperature record for a May 29 article. That demonstrates a mismatch between the instruction and its own example; it does not establish the first public disclosure time of that record. Table 2 asks how many US states the April 8, 2024 eclipse path would cross, as reported by February 2024. NASA’s resource dated April 12, 2023 already describes the mapped April 2024 path [nasa]. This raises a specific prior-answerability problem for the example, rather than a speculative claim about all news questions. The exact 15-state answer in an archived 2023 source and the original news item’s first publication were not independently recovered, so no stronger historical count claim is made. The audit must establish earliest answer availability, not merely the date of the chosen reporting article. Retrospective question wording and distractors can also condition on outcomes already observed; the authors explicitly acknowledge that events never occurring may be absent.

The human sample is informative but limited. Table 4 reports Fleiss’ \(\kappa=0.02\) for non-answerability before publication and mean \(\kappa=0.26\). These values flag disputed labeling; they are not direct error rates. Sampling 60 questions from the already accepted dataset and obtaining \(85\%\) agreement on acceptance corresponds to 51/60 on the described frame. A binomial Wilson interval is approximately \([0.739,0.919]\), conditional on independent items and fixed consensus labels; shared events and label uncertainty can add uncertainty. This sample cannot measure rejection sensitivity or specificity because rejected candidates were not sampled. The reported \(89.52\%\) mean criterion agreement is also not a demonstrated forecast-validity rate. Audit both retained and rejected items with dated primary evidence, stratify by date and generator, and distinguish calculable/scheduled facts, retrospective recall and genuinely unresolved outcomes.

# Released protocol and assessment

Problem 4 (Date boundaries and released configuration need reconciliation).  Section 4.1 says retrieval reaches the day before resolution, while its half-open interval and the release exclude that day. Severity: Minor; the direction is less recent context, not demonstrated future leakage.

In `evaluation/rag.py`, lines 34–36, the bound is `article_date < yesterday`. For resolution on 10 January, an article dated 8 January passes and one dated 9 January does not. The fixed-cutoff branch similarly uses a strict inequality. This agrees with the printed half-open formula but differs from an inclusive reading of the accompanying prose. State an unambiguous timestamp/timezone rule and recompute boundary-sensitive comparisons if the intended interval differs. The retrieval script obtains ten candidates, but `eval.py` selects the first five; that is consistent with the paper’s top-five evaluation and is not a ten-versus-five defect.

Table 5 specifies `gpt-3.5-turbo-0125`, whereas the released model mapping uses the unversioned `gpt-3.5-turbo` alias. Exact endpoint versions, run dates, response parsing/refusal rules, random seeds and historical prompts are required to reproduce the aggregates. The release’s random distractor-ordering and article-selection paths are not all seeded. No observed score change is attributed to those differences without runs.

The original figures support real benchmark differences, heterogeneous benefits from newer retrieval, and remaining errors with gold context. The forecasting-market comparison also supports the practical advantage of denser, more uniform sampling; smoother plots do not prove a more valid forecasting construct. The authors appropriately avoid claiming that all LLM-generated questions are inherently higher quality. Overall assessment: Major, driven by the unsupported central explanatory inference in Problem 1. The other issues warrant specification and validation repairs rather than a claim that the reported accuracies are fabricated or the entire dataset is unusable. Scientific adjudication remains pending, human reviews are empty and adoption is unknown. Focused independent acceptance is recorded in the existing verification files.

It does not import author code or use the unavailable evaluation dataset. This is initial self-checking only, not a separate acceptance review.
