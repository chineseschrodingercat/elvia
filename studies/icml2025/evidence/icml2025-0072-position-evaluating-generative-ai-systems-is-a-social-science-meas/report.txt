# 2. Claims and reasoning actually checked

Framework attribution and scope (Sections 1-3, pp.1-4; Appendix A, pp.14-15). The paper distinguishes the background concept, systematized concept, measurement instruments and measurements, linked by systematization, operationalization, application and interrogation. Adcock and Collier’s original Figure 1, printed p.531, has the corresponding four levels and iterative revisions. Wallach et al. expressly identify their presentation as a variant, explain their terminology, and acknowledge that their treatment of conceptual validity differs from the original. The change is not falsely presented as a verbatim reproduction. [R1-R2]

Transfer claim (p.2 and Section 6, p.9). The paper explicitly rejects the proposition that human psychometric instruments can simply be transferred to GenAI or that systems should be anthropomorphized. It recommends adopting a measurement process. Accordingly, a counterexample showing that a particular human test is invalid for an LLM would not refute the paper’s actual claim.

Operational aggregation (Sections 4.1.1 and 4.2.1, pp.5-7; Figure 2, p.6). Four binary indicators describe a stipulated definition of stereotyping: any one pattern is sufficient. For x in {0,1}^4, max(x1,x2,x3,x4) equals the indicator that at least one pattern is present. Therefore averaging per-output maxima yields the proportion of outputs satisfying that definition. Independent enumeration checked all 16 input vectors, with no discrepancy. Summing indicator values would instead count simultaneous patterns, which the paper itself warns against. The sample-level version is called an estimate; this identity does not independently validate a sampling design or the definition’s substantive adequacy.

Human versus LLM annotations (Section 4.2.2, pp.7-8). The paper distinguishes agreement from validity and warns that human labels are not automatically ground truth. Its hypothetical disagreement example is evaluated relative to its stated systematized concept. A different defensible concept can yield a different judgment without an internal contradiction. No actual accuracy experiment is reported in this running example.

Validity lenses (Section 3.2 and Appendices B,D, pp.4,15-18). The source separates face, content, convergent, discriminant, predictive, hypothesis and consequential evidence. Jacobs and Wallach’s Section 3.2 contains the corresponding seven lenses and explicitly cautions against treating convergence with an unvalidated measure as sufficient reassurance. Wallach et al. also state that no universal threshold for sufficient validity exists and require contextual re-interrogation. This is a conditional methodological recommendation, not a guarantee of correctness from a checklist. [R3]

Applicability and adoption (Sections 5-7, pp.8-10; Appendix C, pp.17-18). The four cases are illustrations, not empirical demonstrations that the framework improves every evaluation. The paper discusses partial adoption, resource constraints and organizational support. The impact statement expressly rejects an inevitable transition from better measurement to better policy. No experimental efficacy requirement was added to this position paper.

# 3. Memorization case study and scope clarification

Appendix E, pp.18-20, distinguishes memorization, regurgitation and intentional extraction, and explains why context, token length, sampling choices and happenstance matter. It does not equate every matching short output with memorization. It also describes unseen-data controls and the possibility that an extraction procedure misses memorized material.

The final paragraph on p.20 uses the expected relationship between suffix perplexity and probabilistic extraction as an example of convergent validity, citing Hayes et al. Appendix G/Figure 13. This comparison has limited independent evidentiary force. For an exact k-token suffix with positive probability under a fixed sampling distribution, let P be its sequence probability and PPL its conditional token-normalized perplexity. Then P = PPL^(-k); for n independent attempts, the probability of at least one exact match is Q_n = 1 - (1 - PPL^(-k))^n. The identity requires the same sampling distribution, fixed suffix length, exact matching and independent attempts; changing these conditions can break this particular relation. [R4]

This is a scope clarification, not a demonstrated error in the paper. Expected correlation can be a consistency check, but by itself is not independent evidence that training caused the sequence to be retained. The paper does not assert such a standalone proof and separately discusses discriminant controls and accidental generation. Its broader framework and these qualifications therefore supply a defensible reading. No repaired empirical result or additional severity is claimed.
