Abstract: Two counterexamples affect the paper’s central spectral variance guarantees. A positive, diagonalizable transition matrix with distinct eigenvalues violates the explicit C-SoftTreeMax bound in Theorem 4.4 by a factor exceeding 100. A reversible three-state example violates the eigenvalue rate asserted for E-SoftTreeMax in Theorem 4.7: its variance has order \((4/5)^{2d}\), whereas the claimed rate is \((1/2)^{2d}\). These are Major findings about the stated guarantees. They do not refute the reported Atari scores or establish that exponential decay is impossible. Exact calculations are accompanied by independently written finite checks. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and common assumptions

The paper considers a finite discounted MDP with rewards in \([0,1]\), discount \(0<\gamma<1\) and a fixed behavior policy \(\pi_b\). Write \(P=P^{\pi_b}\) for its state transition matrix and \(P_s\) for the action-by-next-state matrix at a root state \(s\). The parameter \(\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^S\) assigns a scalar to each leaf state. The trajectory logit is \[\ell_{s,a}(d;\Theta)=
 \gamma^{-d}\sum_{t=0}^{d-1}\gamma^t r(s_t,a_t)+\Theta(s_d).\] C-SoftTreeMax normalizes \(\exp(\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_b}\ell_{s,a})\) over actions; E-SoftTreeMax normalizes \(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_b}\exp(\beta\ell_{s,a})\). Here \(\beta>0\). The paper assumes \(P\) irreducible and aperiodic in Section 4. E-SoftTreeMax’s analysis additionally assumes state-only rewards.

Equation reference: eq:variance. The variance under review is exactly the trace covariance in the paper’s (1): \[
 \operatorname{Var}(G)=\mathbb{E}\|G\|_2^2-\|\mathbb{E}G\|_2^2,\qquad
 G=\nabla_\Theta\log\pi_{d,\Theta}(a\mid s)\,Q^\pi(s,a),\] where \(s\) has the normalized discounted visitation distribution and \(a\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s)\). Both examples use an actual MDP \(Q^\pi\), not an arbitrarily rescaled function. Both set \(\gamma=1/2\), \(\beta=1\), \(\Theta=0\), two actions \(a\in\{+,-\}\) and an initially uniform state distribution.

Equation reference: eq:Cgradient. For C-SoftTreeMax, differentiating the finite softmax gives the paper’s Lemma 4.3: \[
 \nabla_\Theta\log\pi^C_{d,\Theta}(\cdot\mid s)
 =\beta(I_A-\mathbf{1}_A\pi_s^\top)P_sP^{d-1}.\] This identity is valid and is used directly below. For E-SoftTreeMax, differentiation of the positive finite trajectory sum likewise gives Lemma 4.6. The counterexamples concern the subsequent spectral bounds, rather than these derivative identities.

# Problem 1: the C-SoftTreeMax eigenvalue bound fails

Problem 1 (Theorem 4.4 omits the effect of nonorthogonal spectral projections).  Under its stated irreducibility and aperiodicity assumptions, Theorem 4.4 does not provide a valid finite-depth variance upper bound, even when all eigenvalues are distinct.

## Claim and suspect step

Equation reference: eq:claimedC. Theorem 4.4, p. 4, states \[
 \operatorname{Var}(G_C)\le
 \frac{2A^2S^2\beta^2}{(1-\gamma)^2}
 |\lambda_2(P)|^{2(d-1)}.\] Here eigenvalues are ordered by modulus. The proof outline on pp. 4–5 first assumes simple eigenvalues and says repeated eigenvalues can be handled by Jordan decomposition. Appendix A.4, pp. 14–15, expands \(P\) in spectral rank-one matrices and replaces the sum of their Frobenius norms by \(S-1\). Biorthogonally normalized left and right eigenvectors of a general stochastic matrix do not make every spectral projection have Frobenius norm one. The following example lies within the simple-eigenvalue case itself.

## Complete counterexample

Equation reference: eq:PC, eq:actionsC. Let \[u=(1,-1,0)^\top,\quad v=(1,1,-2)^\top,\quad
 J=\frac{\mathbf{1}_3\mathbf{1}_3^\top}{3},\quad
 \delta=\frac1{10000},\quad \eta=\frac1{24},\] and define \[
 P=J+\frac{uv^\top}{12}
       +\delta\frac{uu^\top}{2}
       +2\delta\frac{vv^\top}{6}.\] At each state \(s\), define the two action transition rows by \[
 P(s'\mid s,\pm)=P_{s,s'}\pm\eta u_{s'}.\] Use uniform \(\pi_b(\pm\mid s)=1/2\), and \(r(s,a)=1\) for every state and action.

Every operative hypothesis can be checked explicitly. The vectors \(u,v\) are orthogonal to \(\mathbf{1}_3\) and to each other. Thus \(P\) is doubly stochastic and the two rows in [eq:actionsC] (#eq:actionsC) each sum to one. The smallest probability among all action transitions is \(833/5000>0\). Consequently all action kernels, and in particular \(P^{\pi_b}=P\), are irreducible and aperiodic. In the basis \((\mathbf{1}_3,u,v)\), the action of \(P\) is triangular with diagonal entries \(1,\delta,2\delta\). These three eigenvalues are distinct, so \(P\) is diagonalizable and \[|\lambda_2(P)|=2\delta=\frac1{5000}.\] The rewards meet the \([0,1]\) requirement. At \(\Theta=0\), all trajectory rewards and hence both C-SoftTreeMax action logits coincide. Thus \(\pi^C(\pm\mid s)=1/2\), \(Q^\pi(s,a)=2\), the induced kernel is \(P\) and the discounted state distribution remains uniform.

Equation reference: eq:Cactual. Set \(d=2\). From [eq:Cgradient] (#eq:Cgradient), the two log-policy gradient rows at every state are \[g_\pm=\pm\eta u^\top P
       =\pm\eta\left(\delta u^\top+\frac16v^\top\right).\] Their action average is zero. Therefore the mean in [eq:variance] (#eq:variance) is zero and the exact variance is \[\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(G_C)
 &=4\eta^2\left(2\delta^2+\frac16\right) \notag\\
 &=\frac1{864}+\frac{\delta^2}{72}
   =\frac{25000003}{21600000000}
   \simeq 0.0011574075463. \end{aligned}\] By contrast, the right side of [eq:claimedC] (#eq:claimedC) is \[\frac{2\cdot2^2\cdot3^2}{(1-1/2)^2}
 \left(\frac1{5000}\right)^2
 =\frac9{781250}=0.00001152.\] The actual variance is approximately \(100.4694\) times the asserted upper bound. The independent exact-arithmetic check in confirms the eigenvalues, stochasticity, positive probabilities, action average, variance and strict inequality; results are stored in .

## Counterevidence and bounded correction

The discrepancy is not caused by a repeated eigenvalue, an unbounded \(Q\), a nonergodic kernel, a neural parameterization or a sampled trajectory approximation. The special rank-one case discussed on p. 5 and in Appendix C.1 is valid for \(d\ge2\): then \(P^{d-1}=\mathbf{1}\mu^\top\), and the left projection in [eq:Cgradient] (#eq:Cgradient) cancels it. That special case does not justify the stated bound for nonnormal \(P\).

Equation reference: eq:repairC. A valid replacement retaining the relevant matrix dependence follows directly from [eq:Cgradient] (#eq:Cgradient). If \(\mu\) is the stationary distribution and \(|Q|\le Q_{\max}\), then \[
 \operatorname{Var}(G_C)\le
 2A^2\beta^2 Q_{\max}^2
 \left\|P^{d-1}-\mathbf{1}_S\mu^\top\right\|_2^2.\] Indeed, the stationary rank-one term is annihilated, \(\|I_A-\mathbf{1}_A\pi_s^\top\|_F\le\sqrt{2A}\) and \(\|P_s\|_2\le\|P_s\|_F\le\sqrt A\). Applying these bounds to Lemma 4.1 proves [eq:repairC] (#eq:repairC). This repairs this variance inequality only. Obtaining a pure eigenvalue rate requires appropriate matrix-dependent constants or additional structural assumptions; Jordan blocks can also require polynomial factors. No corrected theorem for the full algorithm is claimed.

## Impact

Theorem 4.4 is identified as the first main result in Section 4 and supports the claimed quantitative dependence on the expansion policy’s second eigenvalue. The finite-depth guarantee is false as written; the repair changes the quantity governing that guarantee. This is a Major finding. Long-run mixing and useful tree expansion may still occur, and the counterexample does not measure Atari performance.

# Problem 2: reversibility does not give the claimed E-SoftTreeMax rate

Problem 2 (The reversible clause of Theorem 4.7 is false).  A symmetric irreducible aperiodic transition matrix satisfies all stated assumptions, but its E-SoftTreeMax gradient variance decays with a strictly slower exponential rate than the rate asserted in Theorem 4.7.

## Claim and proof dependence

Theorem 4.7, p. 5, asserts the existence of \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) such that \[\operatorname{Var}(G_E)\in O(\beta^2\alpha^{2d}),\] and states that if \(P^{\pi_b}\) is reversible or rewards are constant then \(\alpha=|\lambda_2(P^{\pi_b})|\). Appendix A.8, pp. 17–20, works with normalized products associated with reward-weighted transitions. Its displayed lower bound (31) depends on \(\gamma^{-d}\); it is not a positive constant uniform in depth. The subsequent convergence invocation therefore does not establish the claimed depth-uniform result. Moreover, the appendix’s own theorem restatement retains the constant-reward case but omits the reversible clause. The main-text reversible claim can be refuted directly without relying on either observation.

## Parameters and every assumption

Equation reference: eq:PE. Take \[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}
  9/20&1/20&1/2\\
  1/20&9/20&1/2\\
  1/2&1/2&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 r=(1,1,0)^\top,\qquad \eta=\frac1{100}.\] Use the two action kernels \[P(s'\mid s,\pm)=P_{s,s'}\pm\eta u_{s'},\qquad
 u=(1,-1,0)^\top,\] uniform behavior, \(\gamma=1/2\), \(\beta=1\), \(\Theta=0\) and uniform initial distribution. Every action probability is nonnegative and every row sums to one; the smallest modified positive entry is \(1/20-1/100=1/25\). The only zero is the original \((3,3)\) entry. The behavior average is exactly \(P\). It is irreducible and aperiodic because every state communicates with the other states and states 1 and 2 have positive self-loops. It is symmetric, hence reversible with uniform stationary distribution. Its eigenvalues are \[1,\quad -\frac12,\quad \frac25;
 \qquad |\lambda_2(P)|=\frac12.\] The state-only rewards are in \([0,1]\). Thus the additional E-SoftTreeMax and reversibility hypotheses are satisfied.

## Exact weighted-product calculation

Let \(n=d-1\), and define \[D_k=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,e^{-2^k}),\qquad
 M_n=D_nP\,D_{n-1}P\cdots D_1P,\qquad M_0=I.\] The positive scalar factors removed from the original reward matrices are common to both actions and cancel in the policy and its derivative. This is exactly the product in Lemma 4.5 after that cancellation.

Equation reference: eq:ab, eq:antisym. By symmetry write \(M_n\mathbf{1}=(a_n,a_n,b_n)^\top\). Starting at \(a_0=b_0=1\), multiplication gives \[
 a_n=\frac{a_{n-1}+b_{n-1}}2,\qquad
 b_n=e^{-2^n}a_{n-1}.\] Since \(u^\top D_k=u^\top\) and \(u^\top P=(2/5)u^\top\), \[
 u^\top M_n=(2/5)^n u^\top.\] The two action sums are equal because \(u^\top M_n\mathbf{1}=0\). Therefore \(\pi^E(\pm\mid s)=1/2\) for every \(s,d\). The true kernel is consequently \(P\) and the discounted visitation is uniform. Solving the Bellman equations gives \[V_1=V_2=\frac85,\qquad V_3=\frac45.\] The action perturbation only shifts mass between states 1 and 2, whose values are equal, so \(Q^\pi(s,+)=Q^\pi(s,-)=V_s\) exactly.

Equation reference: eq:Eactual. Put \[Z_H=\frac{a_n+b_n}{2},\qquad Z_L=a_n.\] Differentiating the normalized positive sums at \(\Theta=0\) and using [eq:antisym] (#eq:antisym) gives \[\nabla_\Theta\log\pi^E(\pm\mid s)
 =\pm\frac{\eta(2/5)^n u^\top}{Z_s},
 \qquad Z_1=Z_2=Z_H,\quad Z_3=Z_L.\] The mean gradient again vanishes conditionally on each state. The exact variance is \[
 \operatorname{Var}(G_E)=
 \frac{2\eta^2}{3}(2/5)^{2n}
 \left\{\frac{2(8/5)^2}{Z_H^2}
                  +\frac{(4/5)^2}{Z_L^2}\right\}.\]

## An asymptotic contradiction, not just finite numerics

Define \(A_n=2^na_n\). Then \(A_0=1\), \(A_1=2\), and for \(n\ge2\), [eq:ab] (#eq:ab) yields \[A_n=A_{n-1}+2e^{-2^{n-1}}A_{n-2}.\] This is an increasing positive sequence, and \[A_n\le A_{n-1}(1+2e^{-2^{n-1}}).\] Because \(\sum_{n\ge2}e^{-2^{n-1}}<\infty\), the associated infinite product is finite. Thus \(A_n\) tends to a finite positive \(A_\infty\). Also \[0\le b_n/a_n\le2e^{-2^n}\longrightarrow0.\] Consequently \(Z_H\sim A_\infty 2^{-n-1}\), \(Z_L\sim A_\infty2^{-n}\) and \[\operatorname{Var}(G_E)=\Theta\bigl((4/5)^{2n}\bigr)
          =\Theta\bigl((4/5)^{2d}\bigr).\] Its ratio to \((1/2)^{2d}\) diverges exponentially. No finite depth-independent big-\(O\) constant repairs the asserted \(\alpha=1/2\) rate.

The independent check evaluates [eq:ab] (#eq:ab) and [eq:Eactual] (#eq:Eactual), verifies the rational spectrum and Bellman values, and separately compares the scaled matrix-product derivative with finite differences. For illustration, \(\operatorname{Var}(G_E)/(1/4)^d\) is approximately \(4.8347\) at \(d=10\), \(5.8448\times10^4\) at \(d=20\) and \(8.5421\times10^{12}\) at \(d=40\). These diagnostics support the analytic argument; the proof of divergence is the recurrence above, not a finite fit.

## Counterevidence, impact and correction status

This example has nonconstant rewards, so it does not challenge the separate constant-reward special case. It is reversible and uses exact trajectory sums; neither nonnormality nor sampling error explains it. It continues to exhibit exponential variance decay with \(\alpha=4/5\). Therefore the finding refutes the claimed reversible eigenvalue rate, not the existence of some exponential rate in every setting.

The reversible case is part of a central theoretical guarantee and underlies the interpretation that the ordinary transition spectrum determines the relevant decay rate. Reward weighting changes that rate even for a symmetric kernel. This is a second Major finding. Removing the reversible clause would remove the directly contradicted assertion, but this report does not certify a repaired general E-SoftTreeMax theorem. A complete proof must control the reward-weighted products and all depth-dependent constants.

# Overall dependence and unresolved scope

The two Problems concern separate variants and different failures: nonorthogonal spectral projections invalidate the stated C-SoftTreeMax constant, while reward weighting invalidates the E-SoftTreeMax reversible rate itself. They directly affect contribution (ii) on p. 2 and the expansion-policy guidance in Sections 3–4. The gradient identities and the general idea of differentiable planning survive these checks. The paper’s approximate-model result is a distinct claim and is not resolved by either counterexample.

The static source implements neural logits with a factor \(\gamma^d\) and caches leaves, and implements bounded-width sampling/pruning. These facts require care when relating the exact tabular theory to the release. The release’s full relationship to the conference experiments, including effective temperature and cached observations, was not established; it is recorded as Needs Evidence rather than used to infer an experimental failure. The Atari comparisons and gradient-variance measurements require original run data or fresh authorized experiments for verification. No such experiments were run.

No later paper revision or author erratum was independently retrieved in this review. The staged release and manifest were consulted; this is not an assertion that no correction exists, and no priority claim is made.
