\section*{2. Model, quantifiers and evidence boundaries}
The covariate space is [0,1]\textasciicircum{}d and the price space [0,1]. Target contexts have marginal QX and source contexts PX. Source triplets are independent across time. The mean revenue function f(x,p), bounded in [0,1], is shared between domains and is Lipschitz in the infinity norm. Target small-ball masses are between cQ r\textasciicircum{}d and CQ r\textasciicircum{}d. Regret sums expected differences from the best price at each observed context.\par
Definition 2.3 defines gamma as the infimum of all nonnegative exponents for which some positive constant gives PX(B) >= c r\textasciicircum{}gamma QX(B) for every target-support-centered ball. Definition 2.4 defines kappa through the infimum of source covariate-price mass ratios over interior price intervals. Both the exponent quantifier and the range of interval centers matter to the arguments below.\par
Theorem 4.1 claims a constant C depending only on its displayed fixed problem/tuning constants, not on nQ or nP. Its effective sample size is N=nQ+(kappa nP)\textasciicircum{}((d+3)/(d+3+gamma)), with radius rtilde=Cr(log N/N)\textasciicircum{}(1/(d+3)) and upper regret C nQ(log N/N)\textasciicircum{}(1/(d+3)). Theorem 4.4 supplies the claimed matching minimax lower bound over a model class indexed by gamma,c\_gamma,kappa,CLip,cQ.\par
Analytic counterexamples to an algorithmic guarantee are distinguished from failures of a supplied proof. A release configuration discrepancy does not identify the implementation or settings that generated a published figure. A model-based auto-loan evaluation does not constitute an actual pricing intervention.\par
\section*{3. Problem 1 — The first target price contradicts the source-rich upper guarantee}
Source claim and location. Theorem 4.1, p.5, gives the source-dependent upper rate described above for Algorithm 1, p.4. Initialization sets A1=\{Z\}. The unique root ball is selected and p1 is sampled uniformly on its full price domain [0,1]. The source-driven refinement loop comes after this draw. It changes the selected ball and counters but never assigns a new price. [R1, R3]\par
Complete instance. Set d=1, let target contexts be i.i.d. Uniform[0,1], let the source covariate density be gP(x)=1/2+x, and draw source prices independently and uniformly on [0,1]. Source triplets are i.i.d. with deterministic revenue YP=pP. Target revenue is also deterministically Yt=pt. Thus f(x,p)=p in both domains and the optimal price is one.\par
Every operative condition is satisfied. The source density integrates to one, lies between 1/2 and 3/2, and differs from QX, giving genuine covariate shift. PX(B) >= (1/2)QX(B) on every ball, so the exact nonnegative transfer exponent is gamma=0 with c\_gamma=1/2. Independent uniform source prices give kappa=1 exactly. Uniform target mass obeys r <= QX(BX(x,r)) <= 2r, so cQ=1,CQ=2. The revenue is bounded and Lipschitz with CLip=1. Set CI=1 and Cr=2; then Cr\textasciicircum{}4 c\_gamma cQ=8, exactly the tuning requirement.\par
Correct result. The first price remains Uniform[0,1] for every possible source dataset and every nP. Its expected regret is integral from zero to one of (1-p) dp = 1/2. All later regret terms are nonnegative. Therefore total expected regret is at least 1/2, independently of the source sample size.\par
Contradiction with the stated bound. Take integer m>=10, nQ=m and nP=m\textasciicircum{}8. All constants above stay fixed and the prescribed radius is positive and less than 1/2. The claimed bound is C m[log(m+m\textasciicircum{}8)/(m+m\textasciicircum{}8)]\textasciicircum{}(1/4), asymptotic to C(8 log m)\textasciicircum{}(1/4)/m, which tends to zero. No allowed fixed C can dominate the first-round loss. This growing-target sequence avoids relying on a fixed-horizon asymptotic reading.\par
Proof dependency. Appendix B.1, Eq.(16), pp.13-14, uses the optimistic index-maximizing property of the initially selected ball. Algorithm 1 then replaces that ball by a child centered on the already selected context/price. Eqs.(20)-(21) and the source-rich counting step charge regret to the small final-child radius as though it retained the same argmax property. If the symbol means the original root, it may have no parent and cannot receive the child’s radius bound; if it means the final child, it was not chosen by that argmax. The distinction is essential to the claimed transfer benefit.\par
Static code corroboration. All five released TLDP routines preserve the same timing. In dynamic pricing functions for Scenario\_1.1.R, the price assignment is line 95 and refinement begins at line 111; in the auto-loan routine the corresponding lines are 96 and 118. The shared utility returns the full initial interval and samples it uniformly. These line numbers refer to the pinned commit and are recorded in tldp\_first\_price\_static\_check.json. This corroborates the written algorithm; it does not establish which commit produced the paper’s scores.\par
Alternative readings and impact. No source-only pre-partitioning or re-selection/repricing step appears in the reviewed algorithm. Treating the draw as provisional adds an operation absent from the specification. An additive startup term would cover this particular first-loss obstruction, but the rest of the bound and later rounds have not been re-proved. No complete repair is certified. The counterexample defeats a core quantitative guarantee used to establish minimax-optimal transfer, so Major is supported. It does not establish linear regret in nQ, refute target-only performance, or falsify the experiments.\par
The earlier arXiv v1 Algorithm 1 p.6 and Theorem 1 p.7 have the same order and rate. This is historical persistence, not a later corrected issue. Independent exact expectation/asymptotic calculations and a small numerical scaling check are saved in first\_price\_check.py and first\_price\_check\_results.json.\par
\[\mathbb E[1-p_1]=\int_0^1(1-p)\,dp=\tfrac12,\qquad R_{\rm TLDP}\ge\tfrac12.\] \[C\,m\left[\frac{\log(m+m^8)}{m+m^8}\right]^{1/\allowbreak{}4}\sim C\,\frac{(8\log m)^{1/\allowbreak{}4}}{m}\longrightarrow0.\]\par
\section*{4. Problem 2 — The hard family has exact transfer exponent zero}
Source claim. Appendix C Step 2, p.25, claims that the distributions constructed in Eqs.(68)-(69), p.23, have transfer exponent equal to the prescribed gamma and hence belong to the class used in Theorem 4.4. Definition 2.3, p.3, defines the exponent by an infimum over all admissible positive constants. [R1, R4]\par
Complete check of that inference. Write a=c\_gamma rtilde\textasciicircum{}gamma, which is strictly positive for any fixed constructed instance with finite gamma and positive rtilde. On each inner covariate box the source density is a times the target density; outside the outer boxes the two densities agree; in the remaining regions the target density is zero. Therefore pX(x) >= a qX(x) pointwise everywhere, and PX(B) >= a QX(B) for every measurable ball B. Exponent zero is admissible with constant a. Nonnegative exponents then imply that the infimum in Definition 2.3 is exactly zero.\par
For example, with prescribed gamma=1, c\_gamma=1 and rtilde=1/32, the constructed density ratio on the target’s inner support is 1/32, and outside the boxes it is one. The correct exact exponent is zero, while the proof asserts one. This is a countercheck of the construction’s membership claim, not an admissible instance used to falsify the final lower rate.\par
Alternative interpretation. In transfer-learning bounds it is often useful to index a class by any exponent satisfying a fixed-constant mass inequality, rather than by the smallest exponent. On that larger admissible-exponent interpretation, the construction may satisfy the intended inequality with the advertised fixed c\_gamma. But that is different from the literal infimum definition, and the proof explicitly claims equality. The fact that a depends on the horizon does not prevent exponent zero for each fixed distribution; it does explain why a uniform fixed-constant class is a different object.\par
Dependency and judgment. Under the paper’s literal exact-exponent class, the proposed hard instances with gamma>0 do not establish the claimed minimax lower bound for that class. This is a substantial invalid membership step in the core optimality derivation and supports Major with that precise interpretation. Recasting the model as an admissible-exponent class removes this specific objection, but the separate information-bound failure below remains. No complete corrected lower theorem or replacement exact-exponent construction is verified.\par
\[p_X(x)\ge a\,q_X(x)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_X(B)\ge a\,Q_X(B),\qquad a=c_\gamma\widetilde r^\gamma>0,\qquad \gamma_{\inf}=0.\]\par
\section*{5. Problem 3 — The low-visit bin is selected under a distribution it then changes}
Source claim. Appendix C Step 3.2, p.27, Eq.(79), uses a counting argument to choose a price-bin index u visited at most 2nQ/L times in expectation for many alternatives, where L=floor(1/rtilde). It then sets the common bump center ptilde to that bin and defines the source price density in Eq.(70). The resulting visit bound is used in the target KL calculation, Eq.(82), and ultimately in the matching lower-bound rate. [R1, R4]\par
Invalid inference. A pigeonhole argument is valid under a fixed collection of probability laws. Here the laws of the adaptive action counts already depend on the bump center through both the reward function and the source-price distribution. After selecting u, redefining the center changes those laws. The preceding average over bins does not supply a self-consistent low-visit center.\par
A concrete fixed-policy witness makes the failure explicit. Set rtilde=1/32, L=32, kappa=0 and nP=1000. In the candidate family whose center is bin u, Eq.(70) gives zero source-price probability to that bin and equal probability 1/31 to each of the other 31 bins. Use one policy for every possible u and every sign vector: count all source prices in the 32 bins; choose the center of the uniquely empty bin, breaking multiple-empty ties by a fixed rule; then use that price on every target round. The policy uses only observed source data and is admissible.\par
With probability at least 1-31(30/31)\textasciicircum{}1000, every nonempty bin has appeared in the source data. On this event the policy identifies the true empty bin u. Thus, for every candidate u and every sign vector, its expected count in the subsequently declared bump bin is at least [1-31(30/31)\textasciicircum{}1000]nQ. This exceeds 0.9999999999998 nQ, whereas Eq.(79) requires at most nQ/16. There is no candidate center satisfying the needed inference for this fixed policy.\par
Assumption and scope checks. Kappa=0 is explicitly allowed. Source prices are independent of source contexts and signs, and source triplets remain independent across time. The witness works with the paper’s covariate construction at gamma=0,c\_gamma=1/2: the inner density ratio is 1/2, the outside ratio is one, and positive shell mass gives genuine covariate shift. Its exact exponent is zero, so the witness does not rely on Problem 2. Rewards can be the paper’s bounded Lipschitz Bernoulli bump family. It refutes the selection/count inference at this construction step; it is not asserted to refute the minimax lower theorem itself.\par
Impact and repair. The extra small-bin visitation factor is used to obtain the target information budget and hence the claimed continuous-price minimax exponent. Replacing the argument requires a valid fixed-reference change-of-measure or another hard family; neither is supplied by the displayed pigeonhole step. This substantial invalid derivation supports Major. The abstract possibility of the same minimax rate, and the target-only lower-rate claim with a different proof, remain open within this inspection. No full repair is certified.\par
\[\mathbb E_{\omega,u}O_u\ge \left[1-31(30/\allowbreak{}31)^{1000}\right]n_Q\ >\ 0.9999999999998\,n_Q\ >\frac{2n_Q}{32}.\]\par
\section*{6. Core canonicalization defect and verified local repair}
Price canonicalization in Appendix C Step 3.1, p.26, uses the unknown sign omega\_i to choose a replacement core price. That displayed oracle-dependent argument does not establish one admissible policy for all alternatives. This is a Major defect in the printed core derivation: the reduction restricts all admissible policies to plateau prices, identifies regret with the semi-metric, and enables Proposition C.1(i) through Eq.(77) in the proof of Theorem 4.4. Its severity is assessed separately from the scoped correction. A precise local replacement is available for a fixed hard-family center: on an inner target box, write the mean at a slope price as 1/2+omega\_i a s, with a=Cphi rtilde and s in [0,1]. Select the bump’s core price with probability s and a different baseline core price with probability 1-s.\par
The mixture reward is Bernoulli(1/2+omega\_i a s) for both signs, exactly the original reward law. Feed the original requested price and resulting reward into a virtual history and let the original policy continue on that history, ignoring the rounding coin and actual replacement price. Outside the target boxes every price has baseline mean. By induction the virtual observations have the original law and expected regret is equal for every sign vector. This proves a universal randomized canonicalization for a fixed family with at least two price bins.\par
Repair status is deliberately narrow: this replaces the sign-dependent local reduction; it does not fix the later changing-center argument, exact-exponent membership or TLDP upper guarantee. The reviewer supplied the replacement; author adoption was not requested.\par
Other scoped points are retained without increasing severity. In Eq.(82), factoring an unconditional price-bin count from a context event needs care because the policy observes the current context; a direct joint-count bound may retain the rate with different constants, but the full lower proof was not repaired. In Lemma B.4, the interior-price mass bound is applied to arbitrary adaptive centers; a uniform example near the price boundary invalidates the displayed factor-two lower-mass step. A boundary-adjusted full probability proof was not established.\par
Source-dependent ball selection also calls for a uniform or predictable concentration argument beyond a fixed selected ball; no additional probability-level counterexample was completed. The main prose counts every historical point geometrically inside a ball whereas the pseudocode counts selected-ball updates. Problem 1 is independent of either convention. Conditional on a valid per-play gap and count estimate, the packing and target-dominated radius balances have the expected d+1-dimensional form. The Holder extension in Remark 4.2 is explicitly conjectural and was not misrepresented as a proven theorem.\par
\section*{7. Empirical and implementation evidence}
The numerical study compares TLDP with target-only TLDP, ABE and ExUCB. The manuscript explicitly studies posterior drift in Scenario 3 as a robustness test outside the covariate-shift theorem; it is not a proof of that broader setting. Scenario 2 uses Gaussian reward noise, so those simulations do not literally satisfy bounded revenues, although they can still illustrate behavior. No empirical score deterioration was demonstrated or required to establish the theoretical findings.\par
Table 1’s reported means decrease with added source data: target-only TLDP is 71.95; full Mountain transfer is 50.23 and full Pacific transfer 48.72. Target-only TLDP is worse than the reported ABE and ExUCB means, which the text acknowledges. Static CSV counting confirms the overall 208085 applications and the five division counts used in the manuscript. The plotted/table comparisons were checked as reported summaries, not reproduced training runs.\par
The auto-loan evaluation fits a random-forest surrogate on target observations and numerically optimizes that model to define comparator prices. Thus its regret is model-based. The use of target data for this disclosed simulator is not by itself evidence of fraudulent leakage into a real deployment. Fresh policy evaluation or a validated counterfactual model would be needed to establish actual deployment revenue.\par
Release-only discrepancies remain bounded. In Scenario\_2.1.R, released defaults use nQ=100, nP=200 and noise sigma=0.05 instead of the paper’s principal nQ=10000, nP=20000 and sigma=0.01 setting; one specified bump center also differs. This establishes configuration mismatch, not that those defaults generated the figures. The simulation-generation settings and outputs needed to resolve that provenance were not available in the inspected release.\par
The auto-loan release clips the random-forest-derived f/p to a probability in [0,1] when generating an acceptance outcome but computes regret against the unclipped surrogate. If the fitted surrogate exceeds p, those means differ. Whether that condition occurs materially in the actual reported runs was not established without fitting/executing the model. It remains Needs Evidence, with the static paths retained in empirical\_code\_audit.json. It is not used to elevate the theoretical severity.\par
Additional bounded release checks: the Scenario 2 price sampler uses a central interval of width 0.1 instead of the paper’s 0.25 and fixed mixture counts rather than i.i.d. component draws. At kappa=0.6 the central density is 4.6 instead of 2.2; at kappa=1 this distribution discrepancy disappears. Scenario\_2.2’s sixth positional argument binds sigma=0.05 to ABE’s M parameter instead of the stated M=0.1, multiplying its threshold factor by four. These are release-only Minor discrepancies without demonstrated publication-score effects. Missing result CSVs and an unidentified posterior-drift run script leave experiment provenance unresolved.\par
The random-forest price oracle uses numerical optimization without a global certificate for a potentially non-unimodal piecewise-constant predictor. No actual missed optimum was demonstrated, so this remains Needs Evidence. The source density normalization and the stated concave-quadratic and separated-bump simulation oracles were independently checked and found consistent within their specified assumptions.\par
\section*{8. Overall dependence and conclusion}
The upper counterexample directly defeats Algorithm 1’s stated source-rich guarantee. Problems 2 and 3 invalidate distinct steps of the supplied matching lower proof, while leaving the truth of a properly specified minimax rate unresolved. The printed canonicalization reduction is also a Major core proof defect; the fixed-family randomized replacement in Section 6 repairs that specific step. The covariate-shift formulation, practical value of source information, reported simulation comparisons and potential usefulness of contextual zooming survive these particular checks.\par
No later repair was found in the inspected materials. The earlier preprint and current pinned release preserve the first-price ordering. A repaired algorithm would need an exact specification and a new proof; a local additive startup allowance or source initialization change has not been verified to restore the full theorem. Empirical reproduction would be relevant to configuration and surrogate concerns, but cannot repair a uniform mathematical guarantee.\par
