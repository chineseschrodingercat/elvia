Abstract: The additive sufficient-statistic construction, quadratic residual identity, and fixed-dimension complexity accounting are verified. A supported source-domain defect occurs in the released high-dimensional breakpoint optimizer: a permitted scan can remove the full-rank condition before explicit matrix inversion. The core three-phase algorithm’s higher-dimensional refinement is not well-defined throughout its advertised scan, supporting Major. A separate Minor wrapper cleanup defect is also established. The central merging identities remain valid; historical execution and empirical impact are unresolved. Scientific adjudication is pending.\par

\# Verified central calculations and assumptions\par
Equation reference: eq:rss. Let \(X_S\) have predictor rows \(x_i^\top\) for a segment \(S\), and let \(z_i=(x_i^\top,y_i)^\top\). Then \[C_S=\sum_{i\in S}z_i z_i^\top
 =\begin{pmatrix}X_S^\top X_S&X_S^\top y_S\\
 y_S^\top X_S&y_S^\top y_S\end{pmatrix}.\] For \(q=(\beta^\top,-1)^\top\), \[
 q^\top C_Sq
 =\sum_{i\in S}(x_i^\top\beta-y_i)^2
 =\sum_{\ell,m}(qq^\top\odot C_S)_{\ell m}.\] For disjoint adjacent segments \(S,T\), \(C_{S\cup T}=C_S+C_T\). This proves the sufficient-statistic addition and residual formula in Sections 4.2–4.3 and Appendix A. The block orientation in a displayed formula is resolved by the explicit Gram construction and source; no substantive defect is assigned to that notation.\par
Separate fitted models can always reproduce the restriction of a common model, so \[\min_\beta(\operatorname{RSS}_S(\beta)+\operatorname{RSS}_T(\beta))
 \geq \min_{\beta_S}\operatorname{RSS}_S(\beta_S)
     +\min_{\beta_T}\operatorname{RSS}_T(\beta_T).\] Thus the exact-arithmetic merge cost is nonnegative. Numerical cancellation is a separate issue, not a contradiction of this variational argument.\par
The full-rank condition is needed for an inverse-based unique fit. For polynomial features \(x_i=(1,p_i,\ldots,p_i^{d-1})^\top\), at least \(d\) distinct abscissae ensure it. Arbitrary feature vectors require rank \(d\), not merely distinct rows. The paper explicitly fixes \(d\) for its constant-time terminology: statistic merging costs \(O(d^2)\), a general model solve \(O(d^3)\), and there are \(O(n/d)\) merges with logarithmic heap work. The stated upper bound \(O(n\log n+nd^2)\) is consistent on valid inverse-update paths. No uniform approximation ratio is claimed; the reported near-optimal errors are empirical, not a theorem that greedy segmentation always reaches the global optimum.\par
\# A source-bound finding\par
Problem 1 (Breakpoint scanning can invalidate the inverse precondition).  Section 4.1, page 4, allows moving a breakpoint by a quarter of an adjacent segment’s size. Section 5.2, page 6, and Appendix B.1, page 13, discuss inverse rank-one updates. In the pinned source, `polyreg.cpp`, lines 220–223, the optimizer first shortens the left segment by \(\lfloor L/4\rfloor\); lines 234–236 explicitly invert the resulting Gram matrices when \(d>\texttt{PWREG\_SM\_THRESHOLD}\). The default threshold is 4 (lines 9–12). There is no minimum-\(d\) or rank guard in this scan.\par
\#\# Admissible input and reachable mathematical state\par
Use degree-four polynomial features, \(d=5\), \(k=2\), \(n=1000\), and strictly ordered abscissae \(p_i=i\), \(i=0,\ldots,999\). Set \(y_i=0\) for \(i<5\), and \(y_i=1\) otherwise. This is an allowed two-piece function, with no required positive noise variance. Every initial five-point segment has full Vandermonde rank, and \(n\) is large relative to \(d\) and \(dk\).\par
Every merge entirely within the zero or one region has zero residual. Every merge across the jump has positive residual: a polynomial of degree at most four with five distinct zeroes is identically zero, so it cannot fit the five or more observations equal to one. The exact greedy reduction therefore leaves segments of lengths 5 and 995. This reachability argument concerns the defined exact-arithmetic algorithm, not an observed floating-point execution.\par
\#\# Undefined operation\par
For their single breakpoint \(b=5\), the source sets \(b_{\min}=5-\lfloor5/4\rfloor=4\). It transfers the point at \(p=4\) to the right segment and then attempts to invert the left \(5\times5\) Gram matrix. The remaining four predictor rows cannot have rank five. Explicitly, \[u=(0,-6,11,-6,1)^\top,\qquad
 p(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)=p^4-6p^3+11p^2-6p\] shows \(X_4u=0\) and hence \(X_4^\top X_4u=0\), with \(u\ne0\). The independently constructed exact matrices give \[\operatorname{rank}(X_5^\top X_5)=5,\quad
 \det(X_5^\top X_5)=82944,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(X_4^\top X_4)=4,\quad
 \det(X_4^\top X_4)=0 .\] Thus a valid original full-rank segment is converted to a singular candidate before the explicit inverse. The least-squares residual still exists, but the requested inverse does not. A rank-one downdate that removes the fifth independent observation has the same obstruction: the matrix determinant lemma forces its denominator to be zero.\par
\#\# Counterevidence, impact, and bounded remedy\par
For \(d\leq4\) the default source uses another fitting branch. A nondefault compile-time threshold may also alter the path. The central statistic addition and residual identity do not require inversion and remain valid. The paper’s empirical accuracy and runtime claims have not been reexecuted, and this snapshot is not proof of historical experiment provenance.\par
The supported classification is Major for the general-dimension refinement construction. Placement optimization is the third phase of the proposed algorithm, and its inverse-update argument presupposes matrices that the prescribed scan itself makes singular on valid original inputs. This is a demonstrated invalid operation in that core algorithmic path; it does not depend on observing a benchmark loss or on the difficulty of repair. The additive merging identity and the unaffected \(d=2\) branch survive. Historical binary behavior remains unresolved.\par
A precise polynomial-domain guard is to restrict candidate breakpoint \(b'\), between segment endpoints \(a<c\), to \[\max\{b-\lfloor L/4\rfloor,a+d\}
 \leq b'\leq
 \min\{b+\lfloor R/4\rfloor,c-d\}.\] With distinct abscissae this keeps both candidate designs full rank. For arbitrary predictors, a rank-aware method is additionally required. The rank implication is proved here; no modified implementation, floating-point stability guarantee, or empirical repair is claimed.\par
Problem 2 (The released Python destructor allocates instead of deleting).  In `polyreg.py`, lines 264–265, `PwReg.\_\_del\_\_` calls `pwrf64\_copy` and discards the return value. The corresponding `pwreg\_copy` in `polyreg.cpp`, lines 353–358, returns a newly allocated `Regression`; the distinct `pwreg\_delete` at lines 360–366 is the routine that deletes the original allocation.\par
\#\# Static check, counterevidence, and impact\par
Under a normal destructor invocation for an initialized wrapper, the original C++ pointer is never passed to the delete routine; a further copy is allocated and its pointer is discarded. This is a static resource-lifetime defect, supported by the actual function bodies. The benchmark creates such wrappers, but no measured leak magnitude, memory exhaustion, or corruption of a published score is asserted. Its classification is Minor, release-only. Correct cleanup would require invoking the matching deleter exactly once with valid ownership; no executable repair or full ownership audit was performed. This finding does not change the mathematical regression calculations.\par
