# 2. Problem 1 - false variational upper bound (Major)

Claim and location. Section 4.2, p. 4, Eq. (17), and Appendix A.5, p. 14, Eqs. (25)-(29), state that I(Z_alpha;X_alpha) is upper-bounded by the average KL divergence between the paired modality-conditional representation laws. This bound is then converted into the squared-distance regularizer in Eq. (18), and used in the claimed information-bottleneck objective and loss, Eqs. (19)-(20).

\[I(Z_\alpha;X_\alpha)\ \leq\ {\mathbb E}_{X_\alpha,X_\beta}D_{\rm KL}\!\left(p(z\mid X_\alpha)\,\|\,q(z\mid X_\beta)\right)\quad\hbox{(claimed).}\]

Complete counterexample. Let S,A,B be independent fair bits. Take X_alpha=(S,A), X_beta=(S,B), essence Y=S, and nuisances N_alpha=A, N_beta=B. Each encoder outputs the shared bit through the same binary symmetric channel: P(Z=S|S)=9/10 and P(Z!=S|S)=1/10. The two encoder draws can be conditionally independent.

All operative assumptions hold. The inputs are conditionally independent given S; Y is determined by either input, so both essence Markov chains hold. Each nuisance is independent of Y and determined by its own input. In bits, H(X_alpha)=H(X_beta)=2 and H(Y)=H(N_alpha)=H(N_beta)=1, verifying Definition 2’s entropy identity. The encoders are stochastic functions of their own inputs and all their output probabilities are positive.

\[\mathbb E D_{\rm KL}(p\|q)=0,\qquad I(Z_\alpha;X_\alpha)=\log 2+\tfrac9{10}\log\tfrac9{10}+\tfrac1{10}\log\tfrac1{10}=0.3680642072>0.\]

The conditional laws coincide on every positive input pair, making KL zero, while Z still encodes S. The claimed inequality is therefore false, independently of any numerical approximation.

Gaussian restriction does not rescue it. Let S instead be a fair sign, retain independent nuisance bits, and use N(S,1) as both encoders’ conditional law. Their means agree for every positive pair, so both KL and the proposed squared-distance term are zero. Yet I(Z;S)>0 because N(-1,1) and N(1,1) are distinct. Reviewer-owned quadrature gives approximately 0.3368308203 nats; strict positivity follows analytically from non-independence.

# 3. Problem 1 - exact repair scope and contribution dependency

Invalid step. Appendix A.5 treats the remainder after inserting q(z|X_beta) as a nonnegative KL term in Eq. (28). The averaging measure still depends on the same z being integrated inside the alleged KL. The stated expression is not the nonnegative KL decomposition needed for Eq. (29). Marginal input information cannot be discarded merely because paired conditional encoders agree.

Correct identity. Under the local-encoder Markov relation Z_alpha - X_alpha - X_beta, let p(z|X_beta) be the conditional distribution induced by the alpha encoder. Directly add and subtract its log density:

\[\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E D_{\rm KL}(p(z\mid X_\alpha)\|q(z\mid X_\beta))
&=I(Z_\alpha;X_\alpha\mid X_\beta)\\
&\quad+\mathbb E_{X_\beta}D_{\rm KL}(p(z\mid X_\beta)\|q(z\mid X_\beta)).
\end{aligned}\]

Thus the computable cross-modal KL upper-bounds conditional input information, not total input information. Equivalently, total information is bounded by that KL plus I(Z_alpha;X_beta). In the counterexample, conditional information is zero while the shared-information term is positive, exactly accounting for the discrepancy.

\[I(Z_\alpha;X_\alpha)\leq \mathbb E D_{\rm KL}(p\|q)+I(Z_\alpha;X_\beta).\]

Impact. Equation (17) is the stated theoretical bridge from input compression to the proposed distance penalty. Its failure invalidates that bridge and the claimed exact substitution into the ordinary IB objective. This is central derivation damage even if the same agreement regularizer improves practical alignment or captioning.

What a correction does and does not establish. Conditional-information regularization can give a defensible alternative motivation. With exact information quantities, I(Z;X_beta)-beta I(Z;X_alpha|X_beta) equals (1+beta)I(Z;X_beta)-beta I(Z;X_alpha). It changes the coefficient relationship and does not restore the printed Eq. (17). Replacing information terms with InfoNCE lower bounds is also a surrogate optimization, not exact equality of the objectives or their minimizers.

Appendix A.6 survives: equal spherical covariance yields KL = ||mu_alpha-mu_beta||^2/(2 sigma^2). The error occurs before that valid Gaussian algebra. The analytic conditional identity is verified; no corrected training run, performance claim or author-adopted repair is asserted.

# 4. Problem 2 - perfect kernel alignment does not force minimality

Claim and scope. Theorem 2, p. 4, and Appendix A.3, p. 13, assert that alignment in the sense of Eq. (2) implies I(Z_alpha;N_alpha)=I(Z_beta;N_beta)=0. Equation (2), p. 2, permits general kernel functions. The theorem is a necessary-condition claim; this review does not reverse it into a sufficiency claim.

Use independent fair signs S,A,B, inputs (S,A) and (S,B), common essence S, and independent nuisances A,B. Set Z_alpha=(S,A)/sqrt(2), Z_beta=(S,B)/sqrt(2). Both representations are deterministic functions of their own inputs, unit norm, sufficient for S, and preserve one bit of their respective nuisance. The essence and nuisance assumptions verified in Problem 1 also hold in this sign-valued construction.

\[k(z,z')=l(z,z')=z_1z'_1,\qquad K=L=\tfrac12 ss^\top,\qquad {\rm CKA}(K,L)=1.\]

This is a valid positive-semidefinite kernel with nonzero centered Gram norm whenever both signs occur. Nevertheless, I(Z_alpha;N_alpha)=I(Z_beta;N_beta)=1 bit. The Gram equality holds for arbitrary repetitions of the full support and in the population limit. It is not the finite-sample exception already acknowledged by the appendix.

Proof failure. Appendix A.3 infers a deterministic surjection from Z_alpha to Z_beta when nuisance information is present. Here H(Z_beta|Z_alpha)=H(B)=1 bit, so no such function exists. It also assumes that the kernel distinguishes the relevant unequal representations, which a permitted projection kernel need not do.

Counterevidence and exact impact. The actual release uses full-feature linear CKA. In this same example its CKA equals 1/2, so the counterexample does not refute those measured values or the narrower empirical association. The supported Major judgment concerns the central stated theoretical guarantee and its proof under the kernel generality allowed by Eq. (2). Perfect similarity under a kernel cannot certify removal of information that kernel ignores.

Repair remains scoped. A valid guarantee needs explicit restrictions on the kernel and representation coupling, followed by a proof appropriate to them. No blanket repaired theorem for all kernels is claimed. The software’s full-feature linear choice and the empirical trends survive this particular counterexample.

# 5. Problem 3 - essence uniqueness uses an unstated premise (Major)

Source: Definition 1 and Lemma 1, p. 3; Appendix A.1, p. 13. The definition supplies two Markov chains, while the proof immediately treats the essence as a minimal sufficient statistic and infers a bijection. Those Markov chains alone do not supply that premise.

Counterexample. Let independent fair signs S,A,B define X_alpha=(S,A), X_beta=(S,B). Both Y=S and Y’=X_beta satisfy X_beta - Y - X_alpha and Y - X_beta - X_alpha. For Y, this follows from independent nuisances and the fact S is known from X_beta. For Y’=X_beta, both conditional-independence statements are immediate. Yet Y has two states and Y’ four, with H(Y’|Y)=1 bit; no bijection on their supports exists.

Even the displayed nuisance constraints can be met: for Y=S choose A,B; for Y’=X_beta, both input entropies and H(Y’) are two bits, so constant nuisances meet Definition 2’s displayed identities. The latter interpretation shows why the formal definition does not capture the intended common-information meaning.

Alternative reading and calibrated impact. The surrounding prose calls essence the common part, and Appendix A.1 clearly intends a minimal sufficient statistic. That stronger intended definition is not supplied by the two displayed Markov chains. The supported judgment is Major: Section 3.1 explicitly identifies the unique essence-induced input partition as the learning target, treats the essence as unique thereafter, and Section 3.2 uses Lemma 1 to claim that all ideal representations create the same partition. The verified nonbijective essences invalidate this formal uniqueness guarantee within the paper’s central information-theoretic formulation. The finite-discrete repair below and the survival of Theorem 1 and Appendix A.4 do not establish the original lemma under Definition 1.

Precise finite-discrete repair. Add H(Y|X_alpha)=0 for each candidate essence. Equation (5) then forces H(Y|X_beta)=0. Given Eq. (4), any other deterministic variable shared by both conditionally independent inputs must be constant conditional on Y; hence it is a function of Y. Apply the same reasoning in reverse to obtain a bijection on the supports. This repairs the lemma in the stated finite-discrete setting; no unrestricted continuous-variable repair is asserted.

Checked statements that survive. Theorem 1 follows directly by selecting the identity task T=Y in its premise about all deterministic tasks. Its proof’s unnecessary Markov-chain sentence can be replaced by T - Y - Z_alpha, without changing the conclusion. Appendix A.4’s equality I(Z_alpha;Y)=I(Z_alpha;X_beta) follows from the two stated Markov chains and local encoder conditional independence. Neither result is refuted by the uniqueness example.

# 6. Problem 4 - incorrect adaptive-temperature derivation (Minor)

Location: Appendix B, p. 15, Eqs. (34)-(40). The appendix differentiates a variant whose positive-pair numerator uses tau’ and whose denominator uses tau, then claims a sample-dependent effective temperature gap. The printed positive-log signs already disagree with minimization and the displayed gradients. Even after correcting those signs to the intended negative log loss, the positive-pair derivative in Eq. (38) is wrong.

\[D=\sum_k e^{s_{ik}/\tau},\qquad L'_i=-s_{ii}/\tau'+\log D,\qquad
\frac{\partial L'_i}{\partial s_{ii}}=-\frac1{\tau'}+\frac{e^{s_{ii}/\tau}}{\tau D}.\]

The denominator’s positive-pair term differentiates at tau, not tau’. The printed Eq. (38) instead places tau’ in both the prefactor and the numerator exponential of this correction. At s_ii=0.2, one negative similarity s_ij=0.1, tau=0.5 and tau’=0.25, the correct derivative is -2.9003320054; Eq. (38) gives -0.7189762528. These cosine similarities are feasible for unit vectors, for example an anchor e1 and two vectors having first coordinates 0.2 and 0.1.

An exact calculation is simpler than Eq. (40). For unit-norm vectors, the squared-distance penalty is proportional to 2 beta(1-s_ii). Therefore:

\[\begin{aligned}
L_i&=-s_{ii}/\tau+\log D+2\beta(1-s_{ii}),\\
\frac1{\tau'}&=\frac1{\tau}+2\beta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_i=L'_i+2\beta .
\end{aligned}\]

The equivalence uses a constant coefficient mapping for fixed beta and tau; it does not yield the appendix’s claimed sample-dependent adaptive gap. Symbolic differentiation and simplification in regularizer_replay.py verify the derivative and the zero residual of this identity.

Impact and limits. This invalidates the appendix’s particular adaptive-temperature derivation, a bounded secondary explanation. It does not refute the observed learned-temperature trajectories in Section 5.3 or the regularizer’s implementation. The repaired algebra concerns this numerator/denominator variant only; it is not an empirical explanation or a verified dynamical model of information homeostasis.
