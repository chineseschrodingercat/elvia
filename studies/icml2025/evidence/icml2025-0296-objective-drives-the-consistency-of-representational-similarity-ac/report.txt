# Checked definitions and surviving reasoning

For model sets \(\Theta,\Phi\), the paper forms a vector \(s_{A,\Theta,\Phi}\) of CKA values on dataset \(A\), using distinct model pairs and excluding a model’s comparison with itself. It defines consistency as \[r_{AB}=\operatorname{corr}(s_{A,\Theta,\Phi},s_{B,\Theta,\Phi}),\] and summarizes these values over distinct dataset pairs. There are \(\binom{23}{2}=253\) unordered pairs per model-set comparison.

The released aggregation uses an upper triangle with offset one for within-group comparisons and unordered combinations for dataset pairs. Anchor aggregation removes the anchor from its peer set. The similarity computation fills a symmetric matrix with unit diagonal; its subsequent exclusion is material and correctly present. No diagonal-inflation defect was established. CKA computation is delegated to `thingsvision==2.6.10`; that dependency was not executed or downloaded.

Pearson correlation measures linear agreement, not equality of absolute scales: \(\operatorname{corr}(s,a+bs)=1\) for \(b>0\). This supports the paper’s distinction between direct transfer and relative consistency. A high coefficient does not guarantee every pairwise ordering, so the ranking language on p. 4 is an aggregate indication, not an exact ranking theorem.

The overall mean 0.756 and standard deviation 0.124, subgroup orderings, and accuracy-gap correlations are reported descriptive results. Their arithmetic was not independently reproduced without underlying matrices. Static inspection verifies aggregation structure, not those historical values.

# Substantive release finding

Problem 1 (A plotted significance threshold ignores the computed probability).  Assessment: Minor; outcome: issue found in the inspected release.

## Claim, location, and suspect step

Figure 15, p. 22, prints \(p<0.001\) for comparisons of consistency values obtained with different metrics. In , code cell 7 (zero-based), the released plotting path computes \[(r,p)=\texttt{stats.pearsonr}(\texttt{r coeff sm1},\texttt{r coeff sm2}),\] but its annotation interpolates only \(r\) and always appends the literal `p-value < 0.001`. It neither uses \(p\) nor checks the threshold. The inspected cell produces two panels; the proceedings figure has four. Its exact historical relationship to that figure is not presumed.

## Complete finite check

Consider 23-by-23 symmetric correlation matrices \(A,B\) with unit diagonals, all off-diagonal entries zero except these three pairs: \[(A_{12},A_{13},A_{23})=(-1/2,0,1/2),\qquad
 (B_{12},B_{13},B_{23})=(1/4,-1/2,1/4).\] Their smallest eigenvalues are respectively \(0.2928932188\ldots\) and \(0.3169872981\ldots\), so both are positive definite correlation matrices. The 253-element upper-triangle vectors have zero means, nonzero norms, and zero dot product. Their Pearson coefficient is exactly zero. The ordinary two-sided Pearson test then has probability one, whereas the annotation would still state \(p<0.001\).

These are reviewer-generated valid coefficient inputs to the plotting logic, not claimed outputs of the paper’s features. No author code was run. Reviewer-written `check.py`/`check.json` confirms eigenvalues, coefficient, and sample count; the contradiction follows algebraically from the literal string.

## Counterevidence, impact, and correction

The actual probabilities might all be below 0.001. Then the displayed threshold would happen to be correct despite the defective path. The release cannot alone establish a false published threshold or changed objective ordering. The supported defect is local reporting, without established dependence of the central descriptive conclusion on it; its severity is Minor.

Rendering the computed \(p\), or conditioning a threshold label on its value, restores correspondence with the numerical result. This local correction does not validate the probability’s statistical model. Historical data, plotting provenance, and a justified analysis of dependence remain necessary to verify published significance.

# Statistical and methodological boundaries

## Overlapping pairs and inferential calibration

Figure 15 pools coefficients across dataset pairs and model-set pairs. Many observations reuse datasets and models. The descriptive coefficient is well-defined, but the ordinary independent-observation null calibration does not follow merely from counting plotted points.

For an explicit diagnostic, let \(Z_i\) be \(K\) independent dataset effects with variance \(\sigma^2\), and set \(T_{ij}=Z_i+Z_j\), \(i<j\). Then \[\overline T=\frac2K\sum_i Z_i,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\overline T)=\frac{4\sigma^2}{K}.\] Treating all \(\binom K2\) pair values as independent instead gives \(4\sigma^2/[K(K-1)]\). At \(K=23\), the variance ratio is 22. Choosing \(Z_i\) uniform on \([-1/2,1/2]\) keeps pair statistics in \([-1,1]\). This demonstrates the need to address shared endpoints, not Figure 15’s actual distortion or a replacement probability.

The anchor notebook contains rank-sum calculations on pair vectors and Bonferroni correction. Its final plotting call, however, sets `add_significances=False`; the original Figure 18 on p. 24 has no significance labels. Those unused diagnostics are not evidence of an invalid published anchor test. Dataset-level resampling or an explicit probabilistic model could address uncertainty, but no complete correction is certified without actual data and the target sampling population.

## Objective attribution and controls

The model pool is observational: objective, corpus, architecture, size, and extraction layer are correlated. The paper acknowledges these dependencies in Sections 4.1 and 4.3 and extraction-layer alternatives in Section 5. Appendix G fixes one anchor while comparing it to heterogeneous peer groups; it does not independently change objective while holding all other choices fixed at both endpoints. Appendix I changes training domain within a small supervised CNN subset and supplies useful domain-dependence evidence.

The evidence supports associations in the selected pool. It does not identify an objective-specific causal effect or guarantee that changing objective alone yields the reported ordering. That stronger reading of the title remains Needs Evidence, rather than a demonstrated false causal conclusion, particularly given the paper’s qualifications.

## Scale, sampling, and downstream claims

Appendix E applies \(\arccos\) and \(\tan\) and observes a similar mean–standard-deviation shape. Under its stated \([0,1]\) domain, both transformed ranges remain bounded: \([0,\pi/2]\) and \([0,\tan(1)]\). For any empirical distribution on \([a,b]\), \[\operatorname{Var}(X)\le (b-\mathbb EX)(\mathbb EX-a),\] as \(\mathbb E[(X-a)(b-X)]\ge0\). Persistence after these transformations therefore does not isolate a mechanism independent of range constraints. It remains a descriptive robustness check. The release requests unbiased CKA; its finite-sample range and delegated implementation were not fully verified, so no separate range defect is asserted.

Appendix C’s ImageNet sample-size and bootstrap studies support empirical stabilization for that dataset and selected anchors, rather than a uniform accuracy theorem. Appendix B specifies validation-based hyperparameter selection and full-training-set refitting before test evaluation. No test-selection leakage was established in the inspected path. Accuracy-gap pairs also share models and require care for uncertainty, while their plotted correlations remain valid summaries. Raw outputs, seed-level results, and a frozen full dependency environment are unavailable for numerical reproduction.
