\# Supported findings\par
Equation reference: eq:max. Problem 1 (A correct answer is accompanied by an invalid extremum argument).  Figure 9 (p. 12), the main-text interpretation on p. 8, and the full response on pp. 13–18 present a box-and-sphere example as successful verification. For positive \(a,b,c\) with \(ab+bc+ca=27\) and \(abc=23\), a sphere that can contain each eligible box requires \[
 r^2=\frac14\max_{a,b,c>0\,:\,ab+bc+ca=27,\ abc=23}(a^2+b^2+c^2).\] The displayed response repeatedly minimizes this quantity instead, eventually asserting that \((2,2,23/4)\) yields the minimum and that other eligible dimensions yield a larger radius.\par
Let \(u=2\sqrt6-1\) and \(v=23/u^2\). The equation \[(u-2)(u^2+2u-23)=u^3-27u+46=0\] implies \(u^2+2uv=27\) and \(u^2v=23\). Thus \((u,u,v)\) is another positive eligible box. Its squared circumradius is approximately \(8.1733\), strictly less than \(657/64=10.265625\) for the displayed box. This is a counterexample to the asserted minimum, not to the final answer 721. At an extremum, the Lagrange equations force two sides to coincide: if all three differed, their pairwise differences would force the multipliers to give the impossible equation \(a+b+c=0\). The two positive repeated-side candidates are \(2\) and \(u\). Comparing them gives the maximum in ([eq:max] (\#eq:max)) at \(2\), so the final answer is indeed 721.\par
The alternative interpretation that the example merely transcribes model behavior is partly correct: the model may emit wrong intermediate reasoning and still answer correctly. However, p. 8 says its additional verification ensures that no case is overlooked, and Figure 9 presents the strategies as ensuring correctness. The supplied transcript does not support that mathematical-verification claim. The issue is Minor: it invalidates this illustrative explanation, not aggregate final-answer accuracy. Repair the annotation by acknowledging the erroneous reasoning, or supply an independently checked maximum argument. Relabeling the original generated trace must not masquerade as a new model output.\par
Problem 2 (The repeated AIME evaluation is not determined by the stated decoding protocol).  Appendix A.1 (p. 13) specifies 30 AIME problems, 32 evaluations per model to obtain stable averages, and greedy sampling for all datasets. With a fixed model, prompts and deterministic greedy decoder, every repetition has the same predictions. Its percentage accuracy is \(100k/30\) for an integer \(k\). Table 1’s T1-32B result, 50.6\%, is not a rounded member of this grid: the adjacent values are 50.0\% and 53.3\%.\par
This is a reproducibility ambiguity, not proof that a reported number is false. Hardware nondeterminism, varied prompts, stochastic decoding or another evaluation detail could explain differing repetitions; the paper does not identify which. Borrowed baseline scores can also follow other protocols, but that does not explain the paper’s own T1 entry under the stated fixed deterministic interpretation. Provide actual decoding parameters, prompts, seeds or run conditions and per-question outputs; distinguish within-question response variability from uncertainty over the 30 questions. Repeating a deterministic inference does not add 32 independent benchmark samples. The local reporting repair is Minor; the historical score impact remains Needs Evidence.\par
\# Other substantive checks and limits\par
For an iid on-policy batch, leave-one-out KL subtraction in Eq. (4) is a score-function baseline. With \(d(Y)=\log(\pi_\theta(Y)/\pi_{\rm ref}(Y))\) and \(s(Y)=\nabla\log\pi_\theta(Y)\), treating the sampled reward as fixed in the policy-gradient estimator and the reference as fixed during the update gives \[\mathbb E\!\left[\left(d(Y_i)-\frac1{K-1}\sum_{j\ne i}d(Y_j)\right)s(Y_i)\right]
 =\mathbb E[d(Y)s(Y)],\] because independence and \(\mathbb E[s(Y_i)]=0\) eliminate the baseline term. A two-outcome, \(K=2\) enumeration with policy probability \(0.7\) and reference probability \(0.4\) verifies equality numerically. Consequently centering alone does not lower the expected KL-gradient coefficient under these assumptions. It can change variance and practical optimization; EMA changes the reference. High-temperature sampling, clipping or other unreported implementation choices can change the analysis. The paper’s combined empirical exploration claim is therefore not disproved, but the phrase “ease KL regularization” should not be interpreted as a demonstrated expected-gradient reduction from centering alone. The entropy term in Eq. (3) has the appropriate sign for encouraging entropy when minimizing loss.\par
Figures 5–6 explicitly use a separate summarization model on prefixes of completed responses; the horizontal axis counts thinking tokens, not total end-to-end computation. Fractional full-response truncation is a post-hoc analysis. Figure 7 additionally uses fixed maximum generation lengths, addressing the concern that only retrospective fractions were tested. These plots support a trend for this stated two-stage evaluation; they do not establish universal strict monotonicity, a universal compute-optimal stopping policy, or a matched-FLOP advantage over repeated sampling. Figure 6 itself contains small decreases, e.g. Omni-MATH 49.8 to 49.0 before returning to 49.8. No raw output or uncertainty data were available to assess significance.\par
The larger-\(K\) training comparison in Figure 3 fixes the prompt count, not total sampled tokens; the paper says so. Therefore faster improvement per update is not automatically a demonstrated compute-efficiency improvement. Table 1’s T1-32B minus SFT differences are 9.0, 25.7, 15.0 and 6.6 percentage points across its four columns. The distinction between relative percentages and percentage points resolves the MATH500 “over 10\%” wording. The second worked example’s elimination polynomial factors as \[y^4-88y^2-25y+1736=(y+7)(y-8)(y^2+y-31).\] Each of its four real roots yields a valid solution by \(x=(44-y^2)/5\); independent substitution confirms the reported product 1736. Its verbose uncompleted checks do not make that answer false.\par
