Abstract: Two local equivalences fail under the paper’s own Fourier definitions: global average pooling is not the DC amplitude, and a complex Fourier loss is not an amplitude-plus-phase loss. Exact calculations establish these specification defects without attributing them to unavailable historical code or claiming that benchmark results fail.

# Identity and inspected scope

Dong Li, Yidi Liu, Xueyang Fu, Jie Huang, Senyan Xu, Qi Zhu and Zheng-Jun Zha, FourierMamba: Fourier Learning Integration with State Space Models for Image Deraining, ICML 2025, PMLR 267:35342–35365, OpenReview . Two local mathematical equivalences are false as written: the stated global-pooling/amplitude equality omits scale and sign, and the frequency-loss equality changes the loss. Both are assessed Minor: their demonstrated consequences concern the interpretation and reproducibility of particular components; no invalidation of the central architecture comparison is established. Literal phase extraction and exact scan indexing require implementation evidence.

# Definitions and surviving observations

Equation (1) uses a unitary spatial DFT, with scale \(1/\sqrt{HW}\); Equation (2) defines amplitude as the complex modulus. The channel transform in Equation (3) uses \(1/C\), a different but permissible normalization. The Fourier spatial module processes amplitude and phase separately and reconstructs with an inverse transform. The channel module first pools the input feature \(F_r\) and then applies a channel DFT. There is no restriction that every feature entry or channel mean is nonnegative.

Table 1 supports best PSNR/SSIM on three of its four datasets, not on Test2800; the main text says “most”, so that is consistent. Tables 3–4 support the printed ablation rankings. Appendix A.5 supplies approximate computation-matched comparisons, so an unqualified allegation that all ablations merely add capacity would be unsupported. The five-task mean of the final row of Table 11 recomputes to \(39.046\) dB and \(0.96984\), agreeing with its rounded entries. These checks concern reported values, not their experimental provenance.

# Problem 1: pooling is not the stated DC amplitude

Problem 1 (Missing normalization and modulus in Appendix A.7).  Appendix A.7 (p. 15) claims that global average pooling equals \(\mathcal A(0,0)\) and treats Equation (14) as an average. Section 3.3.3 (p. 7) invokes this correspondence to interpret the pooled channel feature.

For any real feature \(x\in\mathbb R^{H\times W}\), put \(g=(HW)^{-1}\sum x(h,w)\). Direct substitution into the printed DFT gives \[\mathcal F(x)(0,0)=\sqrt{HW}\,g,
 \qquad \mathcal A(x)(0,0)=\sqrt{HW}\,|g|.\] These equalities follow for every real input, without an approximation. A constant \(2\times2\) feature of value \(1\) has GAP \(1\) and DC amplitude \(2\). A constant feature of value \(-1\), also allowed by the feature domain, has GAP \(-1\) and amplitude \(2\). The independent FFT diagnostic reproduces both cases. Thus the discrepancy persists for nonnegative inputs through scale alone and for signed inputs through the modulus as well.

An alternative forward normalization \(1/(HW)\) would give DC coefficient \(g\) but amplitude \(|g|\); it repairs the claimed equality only with the additional assumption \(g\geq0\). Neither change is stated in A.7. If “corresponds” on p. 7 means only that both encode a DC statistic, the weaker interpretation is valid. The exact assertion of equality in the appendix is not. The correction is to retain the displayed scaled signed-coefficient relation above. It does not require replacing GAP in the architecture, and no effect on the reported PSNR is inferred.

# Problem 2: the frequency loss changes under polar decomposition

Problem 2 (Equation (15) is not an identity for a complex \(L^1\) loss).  Equation (12), p. 7, penalizes \(\|\mathcal F(Y_{\rm out})-\mathcal F(Y_{\rm gt})\|_1\). Appendix A.16 (p. 22), Equation (15), equates this to the sum of the amplitude-difference and phase-difference \(L^1\) norms.

Consider allowed real nonnegative one-row images \[x=(3,1,1,1)/3,\qquad y=(3,2,1,0)/3.\] All pixels lie in \([0,1]\). Their unitary DFTs are \[\mathcal F x=(3,1,1,1)/3,\quad
 \mathcal F y=(3,1-i,1,1+i)/3.\] Every coefficient is nonzero with positive real part, so no zero-phase or quadrant convention causes the discrepancy. The usual complex-modulus loss, and even a componentwise real/imaginary \(L^1\) interpretation, give \(2/3\). The asserted polar expression gives \[\frac{2}{3}(\sqrt2-1)+\frac{\pi}{2}
 \simeq 1.84694\ne\frac23.\] The independent script computes the transforms and both sides. Scaling both images changes the amplitude and complex losses but leaves the nonzero phase difference unchanged, showing why no fixed equivalence holds. More generally, for \(z=a e^{i\phi}\) and \(w=b e^{i\psi}\), \[|z-w|=\sqrt{(a-b)^2+2ab[1-\cos(\phi-\psi)]},\] not \(|a-b|+|\phi-\psi|\).

Appendix A.16 may be read as defining a different polar-coordinate training objective and using the left side as shorthand. Under that reading, the right side is a valid chosen loss, but it must not be described as the same complex \(L^1\) norm. Correct reproduction requires naming which objective was actually used, its phase wrapping convention, and its reductions/normalization. Table 16 shows similar printed results for several other losses and explicitly places the focus on architecture; this is counterevidence to claiming a collapse of the main empirical contribution. Without code or run provenance, no historical training error or numerical score change is established.

# Open implementation questions and visual scope

Equation (2) and the corresponding channel formula use \(\arctan(I/R)\). Taken literally, \(z=-1+i\) is assigned \(-\pi/4\) instead of \(3\pi/4\), so \(|z|e^{i\phi}\) reconstructs \(-z\). A quadrant-aware argument resolves this local definition, but the notation may stand for an actual `atan2`/angle operation. Implementation impact therefore remains Needs Evidence.

The half-spectrum description is compatible with real-valued reconstruction only when Hermitian pairing, self-conjugate DC/Nyquist bins and phase modulo \(2\pi\) are handled correctly. The schematic scan in Figure 2 is insufficient to certify every discrete index and boundary case; no failure is inferred from it. Exact frequency-radius ordering also was not certified from the schematic. The source images on pp. 2–4, 7–9, 15, 17, 20, 22–24 were viewed. The presented restoration examples and error maps provide qualitative support, while raw pixels, selection procedures, confidence intervals and independent reruns are unavailable. Other image panels were read through captions but not individually visually certified.

# Overall impact and state

The two false equivalences require local mathematical and specification corrections. The proposed architecture, its measured benchmark rankings and the usefulness of Fourier-domain interactions are not refuted by these checks. No later author correction was reviewed.
