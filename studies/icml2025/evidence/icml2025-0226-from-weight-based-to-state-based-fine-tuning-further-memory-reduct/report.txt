Abstract: The nonlinear expressivity theorem evaluates the activation Jacobian at the wrong point. A ReLU example satisfies the printed singularity condition yet gives a surjective weight-perturbation differential, even using rank-one directions. This invalidates a central theoretical comparison and supports a Major assessment. Replacing the hypothesis by singularity at the actual preactivation repairs the local theorem. The deep-linear rank bound survives; the experiments and possible partial activation-memory savings are not refuted by this counterexample. Adjudication remains pending.

# A core nonlinear comparison fails under its stated hypothesis

Equation reference: eq:FG. Problem 1 (Major: Theorem 4.2 uses the activation Jacobian at \(x\) instead of \(Wx\)).  Equations (2)–(3), p. 3, define, for fixed state \(x\), \[
F_x(H)=x+f((W+H)x),\qquad G_x(H)=x+f(Wx)+Hx.\] Theorem 4.2, p. 6, asserts that singularity of \(J_f(x)\) makes the pushforward at \(H=0\) under \(F_x\) a proper subspace of \(\mathbb R^d\), whereas \(G_x\) reaches the whole space when \(x\ne0\). Appendix A.2, p. 12, Eq. (19), repeats \(J_f(x)Hx\) as the first-order term. The correct chain rule gives \(J_f(Wx)Hx\).

Here \(J_f\) denotes the Jacobian. The claim and displayed proof were checked directly in original PDF renders; the discrepancy is not an extraction artifact.

## Counterexample satisfying the printed assumptions

Let \(d=2\), \(x=(-1,-1)^\top\), \(W=-I_2\), and let \(f\) be coordinatewise ReLU, an activation expressly mentioned on p. 3. Then \(x\ne0\), \(J_f(x)=0\) is singular, but \(Wx=(1,1)^\top\) and \(J_f(Wx)=I_2\). Both points are strictly away from the ReLU kink. For sufficiently small \(H\), all coordinates of \((W+H)x\) stay positive, so \[F_x(H)=Hx,\qquad G_x(H)=Hx.\] For any \(v\in\mathbb R^2\), choose \[H_v=\frac{vx^\top}{\|x\|^2}=-\tfrac12v(1,1).\] This has rank at most one and satisfies \(H_vx=v\). The curve \(H=\varepsilon H_v\) remains in the permitted local region for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), and \(DF_x(0)[H_v]=v\). Thus the image is all of \(\mathbb R^2\), contradicting the asserted proper subspace.

Independent finite differences at \(\varepsilon=10^{-6}\) for \(v=e_1,e_2\) give the two coordinate directions with numerical error below \(3\times10^{-11}\); both perturbation matrices have rank one (`check.py`, `calc.json`). These numerical checks supplement the exact argument. If global smoothness is desired, a scalar counterexample is \(x=1\), \(W=2\), \(f(z)=(z-1)^2\). It has \(f'(x)=0\), but \(F_x(h)=2+2h+h^2\), hence \(DF_x(0)=2\), whose image is all of \(\mathbb R\).

## Corrected statement and proof

Equation reference: eq:repair. For \(x\ne0\), \(W\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}\), and \(f:\mathbb R^d\to
\mathbb R^d\) differentiable at \(Wx\), \[
\operatorname{im}DF_x(0)=\operatorname{im}J_f(Wx),\qquad
\operatorname{im}DG_x(0)=\mathbb R^d.\] Indeed \(H\mapsto Hx\) is surjective, as the explicit \(H_v\) above shows. Composition with \(J_f(Wx)\) gives the first identity; the second follows directly from [eq:FG] (#eq:FG). Thus singularity of \(J_f(Wx)\), rather than \(J_f(x)\), is the sufficient condition for a proper subspace. A differentiability assumption gives a first-order remainder \(o(\|H\|)\); an \(O(\|H\|^2)\) remainder additionally requires suitable local smoothness. ReLU satisfies the stronger local expansion at the counterexample’s interior points.

The same reachable-direction conclusion holds on the rank-at-most-\(r\) cone for \(r\ge1\), because each \(H_v\) has rank one. The cone is not a manifold tangent space at zero. Nor does this statement automatically describe derivatives with respect to a particular initialized pair of LoRA factors \(A,B\); the theorem explicitly parametrizes by \(H=\Delta W\). These distinctions do not rescue the incorrect evaluation point.

## Dependency and counterevidence

Section 4.4 introduces these theorems to justify preserving expressive capacity while reducing memory and parameters. The paragraph following Theorem 4.2 explicitly uses its singularity condition to assert greater nonlinear adaptability. The counterexample defeats that theoretical comparison under the stated condition, so its impact is central even though the hypothesis can be repaired briefly. One could reinterpret \(f\) as a precomposed function or assume \(Wx=x\), but neither is the function definition or an assumption in Equations (2)–(3); such a reading changes the theorem.

# Other checked claims and unresolved empirical attribution

## The deep-linear bound is valid

For \(T\) layers, consistent indexing gives \[M=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\bigl[(W_{T-1}+R_{T-1})\cdots(W_{t+1}+R_{t+1})\bigr]
R_t\bigl[W_{t-1}\cdots W_0\bigr].\] Empty products are identities. This telescopes to the difference of adapted and original products, and every term has rank at most \(\operatorname{rank}R_t\). Consequently \(\operatorname{rank}M\le\sum_t r_t\), establishing Theorem 4.1’s all-input inclusion after repairing the appendix’s indexing slips. A three-layer, four-dimensional check with rank-one updates has residual \(8.2\times10^{-15}\) and difference rank 3. The proof, not that finite check, establishes the general result. The phrase about equal expressive power should be read as no loss: exact equality of the two model classes does not follow and need not hold with bottlenecks.

## What the memory explanation does and does not establish

For frozen linear layers, input adjoints need only the weights, so some saved wide states can indeed be eliminated when their LoRA branches are removed. This is valid counterevidence to a blanket objection to Figure 2, p. 5. For a nonlinear frozen block \(f(x)=W_2\sigma(W_1x)\), however, \[D f(x)^\top g=W_1^\top
\operatorname{diag}(\sigma'(W_1x))W_2^\top g.\] Earlier trainable controls still require this input adjoint. The activation-derivative information must be retained or recomputed; frozen weights alone do not remove it. For example, the scalar residual \(a+\mathrm{GELU}(a)+\mathrm{GELU}(-a)\) has derivative \(2.1666309412\) at \(a=1\), rather than 1 if the frozen nonlinear branch is incorrectly omitted.

The inspected ViT implementation keeps the nonlinear branch on the graph: , lines 86–94 and 150–159, evaluates GELU, the added identity-initialized linear layers, and the residual plus control. No detached-branch error is alleged. Removing a postactivation needed only by LoRA can still save memory while a preactivation remains. Therefore the general interpretation that every wide nonlinear activation disappears is not established by the schematic. Per-operation allocation and recomputation traces are needed to attribute measured savings. This limitation does not falsify the reported memory reductions.

# Local release reproducibility defects

Problem 2 (Minor: the GLUE driver does not implement the reported regression and dual-MNLI protocol).  The preserved , lines 186–187, unconditionally reads categorical label names; line 287 sets `is_regression=False`; lines 384–386 use class argmax.

The accompanying download script requests standard `nyu-mll/glue`. Its STS-B labels are scalar floating-point similarity scores, not a `ClassLabel` with a `names` attribute [Retained latex: \cite{glue,features}]. Thus the ordinary STS-B input fails the categorical feature assumption. Even bypassing that access would leave classification decoding where the paper reports Pearson correlation of similarity predictions. For MNLI, lines 356–358 select only `validation_matched`; the inspected remaining evaluation path does not evaluate `validation_mismatched`, whereas Table 2 specifies both. A regression-specific model/decoding branch and explicit evaluation of both MNLI splits are needed. These are static defects in the preserved reproduction driver; no dataset was downloaded or author program run. Another historical implementation could have produced the reported numbers, and no preserved GLUE run logs settle that correspondence.

Problem 3 (Minor: the supplied nonquantized LLaMA path does not activate its requested checkpointing).  , lines 139–145, loads with `load_in_8bit=False`, then passes its checkpointing flag to at line 205.

The bundled helper , lines 45–68, calls only if both `loaded_in_8bit` and the flag are true. The first condition is false on the supplied path, so requesting the flag cannot activate that branch.
