Abstract: Both quantitative conclusions of Theorem 4.4 are false under its stated assumptions. Exact counterexamples use a genuine four-bit dynamic quantizer satisfying a global relative squared-error bound with \(c=0.01\), a norm-one linear projection and finite deterministic input sequences. The theorem supports the central error-compensation contribution, so the finding is Major. Correctly oriented residual recurrences still support geometric forgetting of older errors; benchmark failure is not established. A separate released activation-axis discrepancy is Minor, with historical experiment effects unresolved.

# Method and dependency

MoDiff caches layer states and computes quantized changes across diffusion steps, using error compensation to reduce accumulated approximation error. For a fixed linear operator \(A\), standard modulation uses \[\widetilde o_t=AQ(a_t-a_{t+1})+\widetilde o_{t+1},
 \quad \widetilde o_T=Aa_T.\] The compensated version maintains \[\widehat a_t=Q(a_t-\widehat a_{t+1})+\widehat a_{t+1},
 \quad
 \widehat o_t=AQ(a_t-\widehat a_{t+1})+\widehat o_{t+1},\] initialized by \(\widehat a_T=a_T\) and \(\widehat o_T=Aa_T\). Theorem 4.4’s bounds are the paper’s principal quantitative justification for error accumulation versus suppression. Its scope is a fixed linear operator, not an end-to-end nonlinear diffusion distribution theorem.

# Problem 1: both core error bounds are false

Rating: Major. Locations: p. 6, Theorem 4.4, Eqs. (24)–(31); p. 13, Theorem A.2, Eqs. (43)–(50) and recurrence (59); p. 14, Eqs. (65)–(68).

## Exact claim and assumptions

Write \(\Delta_k=a_k-a_{k+1}\) and \(o_t=Aa_t\). Under \(\|u-Q(u)\|^2\le c\|u\|^2\), the claimed conclusions are \[\|o_t-\widetilde o_t\|^2
 \le c\|A\|^2\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}2^{T-k-1}\|\Delta_k\|^2, \tag{R1}\] \[\|o_t-\widehat o_t\|^2
 \le\|A\|^2\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}(2c)^{T-k-1}\|\Delta_k\|^2. \tag{R2}\] Main Eq. (29) permits \(0<c<1\); the appendix and accompanying prose use the stronger \(c<1/2\). The following examples satisfy both. The exponent directions were verified from the original page images.

## A globally admissible quantizer

Use the two-dimensional, four-bit max–min floor/clamp quantizer of Eqs. (19)–(22). For a nonconstant vector \(u\), set \(m=\min u\), \(M=\max u\), \(s=(M-m)/15\), \(z=\lfloor-m/s\rfloor\), and \[Q(u)_i=s\{\operatorname{clamp}(\lfloor u_i/s\rfloor+z,0,15)-z\}.\] Define \(Q(u)=u\) for constant vectors, including zero. This supplies the zero-range case left undefined by the scale formula and satisfies the abstract theorem’s requirement \(Q(0)=0\).

Here is a global check of the hypothesis. Write \(m/s=j+\theta\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\), \(0\le\theta<1\). For \(\theta=0\) both extrema are exact. For \(0<\theta<1\), the two coordinate errors, up to permutation, are \(-s(1-\theta)\) and \(s\theta\). Thus \[\|u-Q(u)\|^2
 =s^2[(1-\theta)^2+\theta^2]\le s^2
 \le\frac2{225}\|u\|^2<\frac1{100}\|u\|^2.\] Constant vectors are exact, so \(c=1/100\) is valid uniformly. This is an analytic bound for all vectors, not a hypothesis inferred from finite testing.

Take \(A=[1,0]:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), whose spectral and Frobenius norms both equal one, and \[x=(-6.999,8.001),\quad Q(x)=(-6,8),\quad
 \|x\|^2=113.002002,\quad
 |A(x-Q(x))|^2=0.998001.\] The scale is one and zero point six. All decimal inputs here are exact terminating rationals.

## Standard and compensated counterexamples

For (R1), take \(T=3\), \(a_3=0\), \(a_2=x\), \(a_1=2x\), and evaluate \(t=1\). Both increments are \(x\), giving \(\widetilde o_1=-12\) and \(o_1=-13.998\). Therefore \[\|o_1-\widetilde o_1\|^2=3.992004
 >3c\|x\|^2=3.39006006.\] This strictly violates Eq. (30)/(49).

For (R2), take \(T=4\), \(a_4=a_3=a_2=0\) and \(a_1=x\). All earlier updates are zero; the final compensated output is \(AQ(x)=-6\). Therefore \[\|o_1-\widehat o_1\|^2=0.998001
 >(2c)^2\|x\|^2=0.0452008008.\] This strictly violates Eq. (31)/(50).

The auxiliary exact-Fraction computation and the parent’s separate implementation agree on all values and assertions. These sequences can be independent degenerate random variables, so the pre-theorem independence sentence does not exclude them. No unbiased quantization, random error cancellation, nonzero initialization or diffusion-specific input restriction appears among the theorem’s hypotheses. The norm-one projection removes matrix-norm ambiguity. The bandwidth corollary gives a sufficient generic bound, not an additional restriction excluding a quantizer whose stronger global bound has been proved directly.

## Proof diagnosis and verified correction

The displayed standard recurrence is \[E_t^2\le2c\|A\|^2\|\Delta_t\|^2+2E_{t+1}^2.\] It does not yield (R1). Direct unrolling gives the looser but correctly indexed weights \(c\,2^{k-t+1}\). A sharper useful standard bound follows from telescoping: \[o_t-\widetilde o_t=A\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}(\Delta_k-Q(\Delta_k)),\] \[\|o_t-\widetilde o_t\|^2
 \le c\|A\|^2\left(\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\|\Delta_k\|\right)^2
 \le c(T-t)\|A\|^2\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\|\Delta_k\|^2. \tag{R3}\]

For compensation, linearity and initialization imply \(\widehat o_t=A\widehat a_t\). Define \(r_t=a_t-\widehat a_t\), \(r_T=0\). Then \[r_t=(I-Q)(\Delta_t+r_{t+1}),\qquad
 \|r_t\|\le\sqrt c(\|\Delta_t\|+\|r_{t+1}\|).\] Consequently valid bounds include \[\|o_t-\widehat o_t\|^2
 \le c\|A\|^2
 \left(\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}c^{(k-t)/2}\|\Delta_k\|\right)^2, \tag{R4}\] \[\|o_t-\widehat o_t\|^2
 \le\|A\|^2\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}(2c)^{k-t+1}\|\Delta_k\|^2. \tag{R5}\] Equation (R5) follows from \(\|r_t\|^2\le2c\|\Delta_t\|^2+2c\|r_{t+1}\|^2\). Older increments can be forgotten geometrically, but the newest increment has coefficient \(2c\) independently of the total timeline. If \(\|\Delta_k\|\le B\), (R5) gives \(2c\|A\|^2B^2/(1-2c)\) for \(c<1/2\), and (R4) gives \(c\|A\|^2B^2/(1-\sqrt c)^2\) for \(c<1\).

The proof’s Eq. (67) also prints a plus sign before a quantized term where the residual requires a minus sign. Correcting that sign alone does not repair the reversed geometric weights. These replacement bounds are reviewer derivations, not claimed author corrections.

## Bounded impact and counterevidence

Both core quantitative inequalities and their derivation are invalid. This merits Major without requiring a benchmark score reduction or difficulty of repair. The corrected recurrences preserve a substantial part of the method’s qualitative rationale. No conclusion is drawn that all generated images fail, that reported quality scores are false, or that compensation cannot be useful. The paper itself notes that nonlinear feedback in a full diffusion network goes beyond this fixed-operator model.

# Problem 2: released activation scaling does not implement the described channel axis

Rating: Minor, release-scoped. Locations: Section 5.1, p. 7; Appendix D.2, p. 17; README lines 61–85; ; ; .

The paper distinguishes channel-wise activation LCQ from tensor-wise LTQ and says the main activation scale uses each channel’s minimum and maximum. The pinned entry points pass `args.act_tensor` directly to `channel_wise`; the flag is false by default and described as enabling tensor-wise activation quantization. The documented CIFAR and LSUN dynamic commands omit it.

For an activation of shape \(N\times C\times H\times W\), the quantizer’s true branch reduces all three trailing dimensions and reshapes the remaining scales to \((N,1,1,1)\). The false branch takes extrema over the entire input and produces one scalar. The wrapper sends conventional convolution activations, possibly split along channels, without moving the channel axis to dimension zero. Thus neither route creates the advertised \(C\)-axis scales: for \((N,C,H,W)=(2,3,1,1)\) the scale shape is \((2,1,1,1)\) or scalar, not \((1,3,1,1)\).

Preserving axis zero is appropriate for output-channel weight tensors, and per-example quantization can reasonably be called tensor-wise. The defect concerns the described activation channel axis and default reproduction commands, not terminology alone. Stable Diffusion’s deliberately tensor-wise setting is also not refuted.

This static discrepancy prevents equating the pinned documented route with the stated LCQ configuration. Historical experimental impact remains Needs Evidence because exact experiment commits/configurations and logs were not supplied. A repair should make the activation channel axis explicit and align defaults and flag meanings. No author-code or patched-code run was performed.

# Other inspected claims and coverage

The fixed-linear modulation identity and compensated state relation were checked without finding an issue in their stated scope. For affine layers the inspected release includes the bias at initialization and omits it on subsequent residual calls before adding the cached output; this also preserves a fixed affine map. No repeated-bias defect is alleged.

Theorem 4.3’s no-clipping intermediate claim is too strong: for nonintegral \(m/s\), \(\lfloor m/s\rfloor+\lfloor-m/s\rfloor=-1\), so the lower endpoint clips. The clipped endpoint still has error at most \(s\), preserving the theorem’s final \(\|x-Q(x)\|^2\le ds^2\) bound for nonconstant vectors. This local correction was considered when checking Problem 1’s quantizer and is not represented as a second false error theorem. The theory’s flooring and the release’s rounding conventions were kept distinct.

Experimental tables, setup and stated limitations were read, but FID, sFID, IS, BOP counts and image quality were not recomputed. The source explicitly says wall-clock runtime was not evaluated; its BOP proxy is not misrepresented here as a runtime measurement. Apparent table-label ambiguities remain unresolved rather than claims of fabricated scores.
