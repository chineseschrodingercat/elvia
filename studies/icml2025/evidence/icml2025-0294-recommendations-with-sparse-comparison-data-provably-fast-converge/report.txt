Abstract: A Bernoulli concentration inequality used in the proof of the principal convergence theorem is false, including at the parameter specialization actually used. An exact binomial atom already exceeds the claimed entire-tail bound. This is a Major defect in a core proof dependency. A replacement argument is provided and locally verified: it restores the downstream matrix-norm bound without changing its stated sample threshold. The final convergence theorem is therefore not refuted by this finding. Two Minor findings concern the missing singular-value factor in a stated curvature lemma and an impossible reported incoherence value. No empirical simulations or author code were executed; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Assumptions and valid scope

There are \(n_1\) users, \(n_2\) items, \(n=n_1+n_2\), and a rank-\(r\) utility matrix \(X^*=U^*\Sigma^*V^{*T}\). The balanced factor is \[Z^*=\begin{bmatrix}U^*\\V^*\end{bmatrix}\Sigma^{*1/2},
 \qquad
 \mu=\frac{n\|Z^*\|_{2,\infty}^2}{\|Z^*\|_F^2}.\] The centered item factors satisfy \(1^TV^*=0\). Comparisons sample a user and an ordered pair of distinct items uniformly and independently across observations. The theorem uses the exact probabilities \(g(x^*_{u,i}-x^*_{u,j})\), not Bernoulli outcomes, and assumes a warm start. These restrictions are explicit in the paper and are not treated as defects.

The objective is the average cross-entropy plus \((\lambda/4)\|Z^TDZ\|_F^2\), with \(D=\operatorname{diag}(I_{n_1},-I_{n_2})\). Algorithm 1 applies row clipping and then item-centering. Its error is distance to the rotational orbit \(\Phi\) of \(Z^*\), written \(\Delta=Z-\Phi(Z)\). The theorem requires positive link curvature \(\xi\), with \(\tau=\xi/\Xi\) and \(\alpha=\xi\gamma\sigma_r^*\), where \(\gamma=2/[n_1(n_2-1)]\).

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The Bernoulli tail bound supporting smoothness is false).  Assessment: Major; outcome: issue found.

## Claim and precise dependency

Equation reference: eq:false. Lemma D.6, p. 28, claims for independent Bernoulli\((p)\) variables and every \(\epsilon>0\) that \[
 \Pr\left\{\frac1m\sum_{k=1}^m X_k\ge p+\epsilon\right\}
 \le \exp\left(-\frac{m\epsilon^2}{2p(1-p)}\right).\] It is immediately specialized to \(p=\epsilon=1/n_1\) to control row counts. Equations (57)–(59) then support Lemma D.7; D.7 supports Lemma 5.7, which supports the smoothness inequality in Lemma 5.2 and the step-size/contraction argument of Theorem 4.1. Thus the false inequality is an operative core proof dependency, not an unrelated appendix statement.

## Counterexample, including the used specialization

Equation reference: eq:atom. Take \(m=10000\) and \(p=\epsilon=1/100\). These satisfy all stated conditions and exactly match the specialization with \(n_1=100\). For \(S=\sum_kX_k\), the event is \(S\ge200\). Its single atom at 200 has probability \[
 \Pr(S=200)=\binom{10000}{200}\frac{99^{9800}}{100^{10000}}
 =2.8705633596420493\ldots\times10^{-19}.\] The right side of [eq:false] (#eq:false) is \(e^{-10000/198}=1.1639524998859821\ldots\times10^{-22}\), more than 2400 times smaller than this one atom. Even the subsequently weakened \(e^{-m/(2n_1)}=e^{-50}\) is smaller than the atom. Multiplying that weakened bound by \(n_1=100\) in (57) still gives only \(1.9287498\ldots\times10^{-20}\), below the probability of the single specified row-count event contained in the maximum event.

This is supported by exact rational certificates, not only floating-point tails: the rational number in [eq:atom] (#eq:atom) exceeds \(10^{-19}\), while \(\sum_{j=0}^{100}50^j/j!>10^{21}\) implies \(e^{-50}<10^{-21}\). The reviewer-written `check2.py` verifies both comparisons and also evaluates the displayed values to 60-digit precision. No author code is involved. The original p. 28 was visually inspected (`s28.png`); the denominator is indeed \(2p(1-p)\).

## Verified local repair and bounded impact

The stated D.7 conclusion can nevertheless be proved with its existing sample threshold. Let \(s=\min(n_1,n_2)\) and write \(B_D=B_D^1+B_D^2\) as on p. 28. Across the two component matrices there are \(2n\) row and column counts. Each marginal count \(C\) is binomial with parameter \(p\le1/s\). Counts across matrices need not be independent. Markov’s inequality applied to \(2^C\) gives \[\Pr\{C\ge2m/s\}
 \le2^{-2m/s}(1+p)^m
 \le\exp[-(2\log2-1)m/s].\] If \(m\ge2n\log(4n/\delta)\), then \(n\ge2s\) implies \(m/s\ge4\log(4n/\delta)\). Since \(4(2\log2-1)>1\), each tail is at most \(\delta/(4n)\). A union bound over the \(2n\) counts gives failure probability at most \(\delta/2\). Outside this event, each component has all row and column sums below \(2/s\), so \[\max\{\|B_D\|_1,\|B_D^T\|_1\}\le4/s.\] This restores D.7 without changing its assumptions, threshold, or norm constant. It does not restore the false general Lemma D.6 or Equations (57)–(58) as written. The repair was independently challenged and verified locally. The entire revised theorem is not certified. Major reflects the demonstrated invalid core derivation; it does not assert a false final convergence theorem or failed experiments.

Problem 2 (The stated curvature lemma omits the singular-value scale).  Assessment: Minor; outcome: issue found, with downstream counterevidence.

Lemma 5.1 on p. 7 and its restatement on p. 31 use \((\xi\gamma/4)\|\Delta\|_F^2\) as the first lower-bound term. The derivation on p. 32 and the final theorem proof on p. 34 instead use the necessary factor \(\sigma_r^*\).

Here is an admissible quantitative counterexample to the statement. Set \(n_1=1\), \(n_2=2\), \(r=1\), \(s_0=0.01\), \[X^*=(s_0/\sqrt2,-s_0/\sqrt2),\quad
 Z^*=\sqrt{s_0}(1,1/\sqrt2,-1/\sqrt2)^T,\quad
 Z=1.01Z^*.\] Use the sigmoid link. Then \(\sigma_r^*=s_0\), \(\mu=1.5\), \(\kappa=1\), \(\gamma=2\), \(\xi=g'(24s_0)=0.24643428\ldots\), and \(\tau=0.98573712\ldots\). The item factors are centered; the factors are balanced, so the regularizer gradient and \(\Delta^TD\Phi\) vanish. Also \(\|\Delta\|_F^2=2\times10^{-6}<\tau s_0/50\) and the largest row norm \(0.101\) is below the allowed \(\overline C\) radius \(0.34641\ldots\). Thus the proximity, centering, and incoherence hypotheses all hold.

For either orientation of the only item pair, putting \(t=0.01\) and \(x=\sqrt2s_0\) gives \[\langle\nabla f,\Delta\rangle
 =2t(1+t)x\{g((1+t)^2x)-g(x)\}
 =2.0299964\ldots\times10^{-8}.\] The printed lower bound is \(2.4643428\ldots\times10^{-7}\), which is larger. Both sample orientations give the same expression, so the violation holds for every dataset, including any sample size above the lemma’s finite threshold. The correctly scaled bound is \(2.4643428\ldots\times10^{-9}\) and holds. These checks are in `check.py`/`check.json`; pp. 7 and 32 were visually inspected.

The correction is to insert \(\sigma_r^*\) in the first term. The paper’s own derivation and final proof already retain it. Hence this is a false quantitative lemma statement with a locally verified correction, but no additional failure of the theorem’s final contraction calculation is established. That counterevidence bounds its assessment to Minor.

Problem 3 (The reported incoherence is impossible for the stated balanced factors).  Assessment: Minor; outcome: issue found.

Section 6, p. 8, reports \(\mu\approx1.01\) for dimensions \((200,300)\) and \((2000,3000)\). Equation (2) gives equal squared Frobenius energy \(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma^*\) in the user and item blocks. In the smaller block, at least one row therefore has squared norm at least \(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma^*/\min(n_1,n_2)\). Substitution into Equation (10) yields the deterministic lower bound \[\mu\ge\frac{n_1+n_2}{2\min(n_1,n_2)}=1.25\] in both reported settings. Rank is positive, so the energy denominator is nonzero. This argument holds regardless of the random distribution used to generate the matrix.

The released Figure 1 notebook assigns `mu=1.01`; it does not recompute Equation (10) after balancing its matrix. A user-chosen tuning constant with that name is possible, but it is not the defined ground-truth incoherence. The stated value and its connection to sample-size and projection formulas require correction. This does not establish that the plotted errors or qualitative convergence observations are false. The actual incoherence of historical matrices was not recovered.

# Additional checks, implementation scope, and limits

The identity in Lemma C.1 follows from \(\mathbb E(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T=2J/(n_2-1)\) and user sampling; an independent exact enumeration for a \(2\times3\) matrix gives \(14/3\) on both sides. The vectorized quadratic-form statement of C.4 and the uniform operator-norm argument of C.6 were checked. The two projection steps are nonexpansive relative to a ground truth lying in both constraint sets, and centering expands the row bound by at most two, as used in A.3. The final step-size algebra with \(a=\xi\gamma\sigma_r^*/4\) agrees with E.3.

A link-domain qualification is needed. Smooth strict monotonicity and symmetry alone do not imply \(\xi>0\): \(g(x)=1/(1+e^{-x^3})\) has all those properties but \(g'(0)=0\), making \(\xi=0\) on the interval used by the paper. The theorem’s finite sample threshold and positive rate require \(\xi>0\). This is an effective-domain clarification; no finite-threshold counterexample to Theorem 4.1 is claimed from a case where its constants are undefined.

Static source inspection identifies further differences requiring historical run evidence. The Figure 1 notebook generates normalized feature rows and balances their product rather than truncating an iid Gaussian utility matrix as described. It clips rows without the subsequent centering projection of Algorithm 1, uses a different clipping radius, and supplies different regularization and step-size formulas. A later singular-value rescaling is not applied to the already built \(Z^*\). Its actual optimization gradient uses noiseless probabilities, despite an unused Bernoulli data helper. Figure 2 openly uses Adam without projection, as Appendix F permits; its implemented error centers the residual. Neither an unused helper nor a disclosed algorithm variant is promoted into a defect. Full release traces are retained in `a1.json`. Their effects on the historical plotted curves remain Needs Evidence.

No author notebook, training code, tests, or original simulation was executed. No real recommendation dataset was required by the paper’s claimed simulation scope. Separate external supplementary discovery was unavailable; the complete embedded appendices were available.
