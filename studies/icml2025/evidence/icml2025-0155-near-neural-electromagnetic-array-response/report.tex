Abstract: A finite, physically admissible array response disproves the unique-coefficient guarantee in Theorem 4.5. Two distinct target directions can have identical projected frequencies along one array axis, making the first two Hankel columns identical and yielding multiple exact recurrence coefficients. This is a Major defect in a theoretical guarantee used to justify the central regularizer. The existence of a zero-residual relation and the reported empirical benefits are not disproved. A separate Minor discrepancy concerns the released entrywise \(L_1\) regularizer versus the published \(L_2\) expression. The review combines original-source inspection, static release inspection, exact analysis, and independent numerical corroboration; historical experiments were not rerun. Scientific adjudication remains pending.\par

\# Substantive findings\par
Problem 1 (Theorem 4.5 does not ensure unique recurrence coefficients).  Assessment: Major. Theorem 4.5, p. 6, asserts that for the response in Eq. (8), if \(K\leq\min\{\lceil M_1/2\rceil,\lceil M_2/2\rceil\}\), the last column of \(\mathcal H_{M_1,M_2-K}(Y)\) is uniquely representable by its first \(K\) columns. The analogous assertion is made for the other axis. This is a paraphrase of the printed claim, visually verified in the original PDF.\par
\#\# An admissible counterexample\par
Equation reference: eq:parameters. Take \[
\begin{aligned}
&K=2,\quad M_1=M_2=4,\quad \lambda=1,\quad d_1=d_2=\tfrac12,\\
&x_1=x_2=1,\quad \theta_1=\theta_2=0,\quad
\phi_1=\tfrac{\pi}{6},\quad \phi_2=\tfrac{\pi}{4}.
\end{aligned}\] The size bound holds with equality. Both amplitudes are nonzero, the physical directions are different, and the spacings meet the stated half-wavelength requirement. The target ranges can be chosen arbitrarily large compared with the finite aperture, satisfying the far-field assumption. Neither the setup nor Theorem 4.5 requires distinct projected frequencies on each axis.\par
Substitution into [eq:response] (\#eq:response) gives \[Y_{m,n}=c_m
 =e^{i\pi(m-1)/2}+e^{i\pi(m-1)/\sqrt2},
\qquad 1\leq m,n\leq4.\] The response is independent of \(n\), and \(c_1=2\). Therefore \[H_2(y_m)=
\begin{pmatrix}c_m&c_m&c_m\\c_m&c_m&c_m\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal H_{4,2}(Y)=[\,h\ h\ h\,],
\quad h=(c_1,c_1,c_2,c_2,c_3,c_3,c_4,c_4)^\top\ne0.\] Consequently both \(m_1=(1,0)^\top\) and \(m_1=(0,1)^\top\) satisfy \(\mathcal H_{4,2}(Y)Sm_1=\mathcal H_{4,2}(Y)b\). Every vector \((a,1-a)^\top\), \(a\in\mathbb C\), does so. This is an exact failure of uniqueness, independent of numerical tolerances.\par

\#\# Where the proof fails and why the dependency matters\par
Appendix B, pp. 20–22, establishes Lemmas B.1–B.3 and then invokes them to prove Theorem 4.5. Lemma B.2 treats a Vandermonde factorization as sufficient for rank \(K\) and first-\(K\)-column independence. Lemma B.3 then inverts the transpose of the \(K\)-row Vandermonde matrix. However, the sampled nodes \[z_{2k}=\exp(2\pi i d_2\sin\phi_k\sin\theta_k/\lambda)\] all equal one in [eq:parameters] (\#eq:parameters). The required inverse does not exist.\par
This is not solely a consequence of the whole block Hankel matrix losing rank. With the same response and \(N_1=N_2=2\), all size conditions of Lemma B.1 hold: \(K=2\leq N_i=2\leq M_i-K+1=3\). The \(4\times9\) matrix \(\mathcal H_{2,2}(Y)\) has rank two but its first two columns coincide and have rank one. Thus the claimed basis property fails even while the claimed total rank is retained.\par
Theorem 4.5 explicitly motivates the learned coefficients and least-squares regularization in §4.4, Eqs. (9)–(11). It is a central theoretical guarantee, not an unrelated appendix observation. A demonstrated false guarantee with this dependency supports Major under the stipulated calibration.\par
\#\# Counterevidence and bounded impact\par
The exact recurrence still exists in this example: its coefficient set is nonempty and nonunique. Generic random directions can avoid repeated projected nodes. Noise can perturb numerical ranks. None of these facts proves the deterministic uniqueness statement as written, which contains no almost-sure qualifier or coordinatewise distinctness premise.\par
The example does not establish failure of the trained response predictor, loss of a reported benchmark gain, or failure of every regularizer optimum. In particular, an optimization penalty can be useful without a uniquely identifiable auxiliary coefficient vector. These surviving possibilities are distinct from the false theorem.\par
The proof also uses \(N_2=M_2-K\) in a lemma requiring \(N_2\geq K\), whereas the published ceiling bound allows \(M_2=2K-1\). This is an additional boundary in the proof’s applicability; no second Major claim is needed for it.\par
\#\# A sufficient restricted repair\par
One sufficient revised statement adds all of the following assumptions: \(M_1,M_2\geq2K\), every \(x_k\ne0\), and the sampled nodes \[z_{1k}=e^{2\pi i d_1\sin\phi_k\cos\theta_k/\lambda},
\qquad
z_{2k}=e^{2\pi i d_2\sin\phi_k\sin\theta_k/\lambda}\] are pairwise distinct on each respective axis. Under these strengthened conditions, the two recurrence coefficient vectors are unique.\par
Indeed, write \(A_q(z)_{jk}=z_k^{j-1}\). The first \(K\) columns of each row Hankel block factor as \[A_{M_2-K}(z_2)
\operatorname{diag}(x_k z_{1k}^{m-1}) A_K(z_2)^\top.\] The first factor has full column rank and the remaining two factors are invertible. The coefficient \[m_1=(A_K(z_2)^\top)^{-1}(z_{21}^{K},\ldots,z_{2K}^{K})^\top\] is unique and independent of \(m\). Stacking the blocks preserves this conclusion. Swapping axes proves the other direction. This verifies only the stated restricted algebraic repair; it certifies neither training convergence nor experimental performance. No author adoption is known.\par
Problem 2 (The inspected release changes the residual norm).  Assessment: Minor. Eq. (10), p. 7, defines the regularizer as the sum of two Euclidean norms of stacked recurrence residuals. The original \(2\) subscripts were visually confirmed. In the pinned release, , lines 88–99, the row and column loops instead accumulate `torch.abs(pred - H @ coeff).sum()`; the loss is called in the training loop near line 203 [Retained latex: \textbackslash{}cite\{release\}].\par
For nonzero residuals these are different objectives. The real residual vectors \(a=(3,0)\) and \(b=(2,2)\) satisfy \[\|a\|_2=3>\sqrt8=\|b\|_2,
\qquad
\|a\|_1=3<4=\|b\|_1.\] Thus even candidate preference can reverse. The exact-zero sets agree, so this discrepancy does not refute recurrence feasibility. The inspected release is not proven to be the exact code used for the historical tables. Its quantitative experimental effect remains Needs Evidence. A documented choice of norm and an aligned equation or implementation would resolve the specification discrepancy; a replacement training outcome has not been verified.\par
\# Additional inspected claims and limitations\par
Theorem 4.1 and Appendix A were examined for the harmonic-support induction. Multiplying trigonometric polynomials gives the stated type of upper support bound. This one-way support result should not automatically be read as arbitrary coefficient and phase attainability for a fixed input mapping. For example, a mapping consisting only of \(\sin(\pi r)\) identifies \(r=1/6\) and \(r=5/6\), whereas \(\cos(\pi r)\) distinguishes them. The implementation includes sine and cosine features, and the proof also uses a one-way representability formulation. This observation is retained as a scope clarification, not a further false-theorem verdict.\par
Main experimental descriptions, Tables 1–6, figure captions, and Appendix C were read for consistency and for the scope of the claimed response-recovery, resolution, and denoising benefits. No independent radar measurements, raw-data audit, training run, confidence-interval reconstruction, or benchmark reproduction was performed. The evidence here does not refute those numerical results. External cited theorems and later errata were not comprehensively audited.\par
\# Overall impact and conclusion\par
The highest supported impact is Major, because the stated uniqueness guarantee underpinning the central recurrence regularizer is false under the published assumptions. Recurrence existence and empirical utility may survive. The \(L_1/L_2\) discrepancy is a separate Minor release issue whose historical effect is unresolved.\par
