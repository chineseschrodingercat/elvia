# Contribution and checked core reasoning

The method combines an audio-visual model with reasoning-focused supervised training, then forms preference pairs between alternative reasoning steps sharing a prefix. Rollout answer correctness estimates a prefix’s utility; an input-perturbation score selects steps for more expensive comparisons. Full-path preference examples complement process-level pairs. The empirical claims depend on both the optimizer and the quality of rollout judging, annotations and evaluation.

## Process-DPO: No Issue Found within the checked algebra

For fixed context \(c=(s_{<k},H^{AV})\), Eq. (4), p. 4, writes \[r(s)=\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(s\mid c)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(s\mid c)}
       +\beta\log Z(c).\] For two alternatives with the same \(c\), the partition term cancels: \[r(s)-r(s')=\beta\left[
 \log\frac{\pi_\theta(s\mid c)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(s\mid c)}
-\log\frac{\pi_\theta(s'\mid c)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(s'\mid c)}
 \right].\] The Bradley–Terry sigmoid and Eq. (6)’s negative binary cross-entropy then have the correct preference direction. With soft label \(\alpha\), the derivative with respect to preference logit \(z\) is \(\sigma(z)-\alpha\), minimized at \(\log(\alpha/(1-\alpha))\) for \(0<\alpha<1\).

The assumptions are positive \(\beta\), finite normalization, positive reference support, identical prefix and multimodal conditioning for both alternatives, and consistent likelihood evaluation. A reward defined up to a context offset must not be confused with an absolutely calibrated probability of correct reasoning. Eq. (2)’s rollout average estimates continuation-policy-specific answer success given an accurate evaluator; it does not prove every intermediate statement is logically correct.

An independent numerical check with \(\beta=0.7\), reference probabilities \((0.4,0.6)\) and rewards \((1.2,-0.3)\) yields \(Z=2.6119465848\), policy \((0.8503555024,0.1496444976)\), reconstructed reward difference 1.5 and preference probability \(0.8175744762\). This verifies algebra, not training.

## Contrastive selection and counterevidence

Section 4.3, p. 5, averages the token-level divergence \[D_{\rm KL}(P_{\rm original}\|P_{\rm perturbed})\] over a step’s token positions. The inspected `contrast_probs` function in `videosalmonno1/llava/train/llava_trainer.py:1540--1556` uses predictive logits aligned to current tokens, sums \(\exp(\log p)(\log p-\log q)\) over vocabulary, then averages token positions. This agrees with the stated direction and normalization. Image perturbation is active while audio zeroing is commented; image-only sensitivity is not by itself a contradiction of the stated video-focused heuristic.

The source presents high sensitivity as a useful selection heuristic, not a theorem identifying all erroneous steps. Six-rollout noise, judge error and soft-label construction remain empirical questions. The main pDPO loss visibly includes the minus sign absent from the separate PRM expression below.

# Problem 1: SynthDec metrics need a consistent definition or correction

Rating: Minor, reporting scope. Location: Table 2 and its caption, p. 7.

The caption describes SynthDec as F1-score (Precision/Recall). Its own-model rows report \[\text{SFT: }5.8\%\ (97\%/5\%),\qquad
 \text{pDPO: }17.8\%\ (87\%/13\%).\] For a common positive class, predictions and aggregation, ordinary \(F_1=2PR/(P+R)\) gives \(9.5098039\%\) and \(22.62\%\). Rounding cannot explain the differences. Allowing precision and recall their full nearest-integer-percent intervals yields \[F_{1,\rm SFT}\in[8.59901,10.41262]\%,\qquad
 F_{1,\rm pDPO}\in[21.84343,23.39109]\%.\] These are disjoint from the respective nearest-tenth intervals \([5.75,5.85]\%\) and \([17.75,17.85]\%\). The harmonic mean is increasing in both positive inputs, so the endpoint check covers the full rounding rectangles.

Separate macro-averages, per-run averages or different class conventions can make averaged F1 differ from the harmonic mean of averaged precision and recall. No such alternative was specified in the inspected caption/discussion. The established problem is incomplete or inconsistent quantitative reporting under the ordinary interpretation, not an unconditional recovery of the true scores. The conditional values above are not rerun evaluation results.

The pDPO-over-SFT ordering survives both the displayed F1s and those conditional harmonic means. This finding does not invalidate the other accuracy columns or the central loss. Exact scoring code, confusion matrices and predictions are needed to resolve the magnitude of the synthetic-detection result. Four nonzero commercial-model entries were compatible with ordinary F1 after rounding, supporting but not proving a common convention. The bounded repair is to provide a precise averaging definition or correct the affected entries.

# Problem 2: the printed PRM loss has the wrong minimization sign

Rating: Minor. Location: p. 4, Eq. (3), labelled as the PRM training loss.

For target success probability \(p\) and prediction \(0<r<1\), the printed summand is \[L(r)=p\log r+(1-p)\log(1-r).\] Its derivative is \((p-r)/(r(1-r))\) and \[L''(r)=-p/r^2-(1-p)/(1-r)^2<0.\] Thus \(r=p\) maximizes this expression; minimizing it drives predictions toward a boundary, with arbitrarily negative value when \(0<p<1\). For \(p=1\), minimizing \(\log r\) drives \(r\) toward zero rather than one.

A leading minus sign yields minimized binary cross-entropy, or the expression may be explicitly called a maximized log likelihood. This is a local specification error under the stated loss interpretation. The main pDPO Eq. (6) is correctly signed, and no actual PRM optimization implementation or historical log was bound to the literal sign in Eq. (3). Consequently this does not establish that the reported PRM baseline was trained incorrectly. The distinct binary target described for the baseline in Section 7.3 also needs implementation clarification rather than an assumption that it must match the earlier soft target.

# Problem 3: a released mathematical demo makes a false inference

Rating: Minor for the public demo; scored-benchmark effect Needs Evidence. The pinned README, lines 66–84, presents a RivaBench mathematics example. Its selected answer and explanation infer from \(w_t-w_{xx}\le0\) that the function can only decrease or remain constant over time at interior points.

Take \[w(x,t)=x^2+t,\qquad (x,t)\in(-1,1)\times(0,1].\] Then \(w_t=1\), \(w_{xx}=2\), and \(w_t-w_{xx}=-1\le0\), while the function increases at every fixed \(x\). The asserted implication is false. A parabolic maximum principle concerns extrema relative to an appropriate parabolic boundary under its assumptions; it does not imply pointwise temporal monotonicity. Non-strict formulations also allow a constant solution to attain its maximum inside.

The demo was directly read from the source bytes. Its exact inclusion in the frozen scored Academic test set was not established; the original PDF’s illustrated Academic cases are different. The linked video and full annotation dataset were not downloaded. Therefore one faulty released explanation does not establish a dataset-wide label-error rate or invalidate the reported Academic accuracy. Repair the explanation and answer, and provide item-level benchmark provenance before drawing broader evaluation conclusions.
