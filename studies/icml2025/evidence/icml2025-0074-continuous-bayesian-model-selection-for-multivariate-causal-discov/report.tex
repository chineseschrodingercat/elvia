\section*{2. Summary and scientific dependencies}
Three findings are supported. P1 is a bounded inconsistency between coordinate-additive appendix equations and the joint-input kernel described in the main text and implemented in the release. P2 is a release-level pruning defect: an admissible three-node input can return a directed cycle. P3 is a Major inferential defect in the experiment’s attribution of error to posterior overlap alone. The experiment still uses approximate variational scores and nonconvex optimization after enumerating graphs; enumeration does not isolate the stated error source.\par
P1 and P2 remain Minor with distinct evidence boundaries. P1 has affirmative main-text and implementation evidence for the intended interaction-capable model. P2 is an actual release defect without an authenticated link to publication outputs. P3 is different: Section 3 explicitly relies on Section 6.1 as evidence for the low-error cost of flexible multivariate Bayesian discovery, while Section 6.1 claims an error-source isolation that its procedure does not provide. This core inference is materially unsupported irrespective of whether the observed scores or the broad flexibility thesis survive. Appendix placement, repair difficulty and an unproved score decrease are not severity criteria.\par
The conditional Bayesian distinguishability argument, finite-sample qualifications and continuous empirical-Bayes approximation were also examined. The conditional asymptotic results were not disproved. Low measured error remains useful evidence for the implemented approximate scorer on the tested datasets; neither the exact Bayes error nor the allocation of observed error among overlap, variational approximation and optimization was independently measured.\par
\section*{3. Problem 1 — incompatible additive and joint kernel specifications}
Source and suspect step. Section 4.2, p5 describes the input-dependent Gaussian-process construction; Appendix C.1, p16, Eqs18-23 supplies the explicit five-family kernel. Eq18 sums kernel families. In addition, Eqs20-22 sum separate univariate functions of each observed coordinate and the latent coordinate; Eq19 is also additive. Thus the literal appendix formula has the following decomposition, even after the families are added [S1]:\par
\[k((x,w),(x',w'))=k_X(x,x')+k_W(w,w').\]\par
Complete structural check. With the zero GP mean in Eq11, consider two input coordinates x and w and the mixed difference Delta=f(1,1)-f(1,0)-f(0,1)+f(0,0). Every coordinate-only covariance term cancels in its variance, so E[Delta]=0 and Var(Delta)=0. More generally all rectangular mixed differences vanish. For the continuous sample paths of these kernels, f(x,w)=g(x)+h(w) almost surely (and the argument extends to multiple observed coordinates). Sums of families or adjustment of their hyperparameters preserve this restriction.\par
In the structural mechanism of Appendix C.2, p17, Eq24, the latent W and additive Gaussian noise are independent of the parents. Conditional on one sampled mechanism f, the appendix’s literal additive model therefore obeys:\par
\[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X=x,f)=\operatorname{Var}(h(W)\mid f)+\phi^2,\quad\text{independent of }x.\]\par
An explicit interaction illustrates the consequence: take X, W and epsilon independent standard normal, and Y=XW+epsilon. This satisfies independent exogenous noise, positive Gaussian measurement-noise variance and an acyclic X-to-Y graph. Its joint density is positive; X and Y are dependent, so the two-node graph is faithful. An independent third normal variable can be added without changing the argument. The true conditional variance is 1+x\textasciicircum{}2. On the positive-probability set |x|<=0.1 it is at most 1.01; on 0.9<=|x|<=1.1 it is at least 1.81. No constant conditional variance matches both. This is an example of the restriction imposed by the literal formula, not a claim that this exact polynomial is itself a GP sample of positive point mass. Additive h(W) may still produce non-Gaussian noise; only parent-dependent structural variability is excluded here. Variation in posterior predictive uncertainty over f does not remove this fixed-mechanism restriction.\par
Decisive counterevidence. The released CausalRBF, CausalMatern12, CausalMatern32 and CausalRationalQuadratic classes inherit GPflow’s joint stationary kernels and use inverse graph parameters as ARD lengthscales. CausalGPCDEBase.py, lines502-539, adds these families over the full augmented input; GeneralisedGPLVM.py, lines151-155 and187-214, concatenates observed variables and the latent coordinate. No split into univariate active dimensions occurs there. The pinned GPflow 2.9.0 dependency forms a scaled Euclidean distance over the whole vector [S3,S4]. Accordingly, the actual inspected implementation can express interactions.\par
For a unit-variance joint squared-exponential kernel with unit precisions, the same rectangular difference has Var(Delta)=4(1-exp(-1/2))\textasciicircum{}2=0.6192724869847019, rather than zero. The independent symbolic calculation confirms both results [C1]. The main text’s product-style description is further evidence for the joint-kernel reading. Joint Matern and rational-quadratic kernels should be specified through a joint distance; they are not generally products of coordinate kernels.\par
Impact and precise repair scope. The supported finding is a consequential inconsistency between the printed equations and the intended/released specification. It does not demonstrate loss of flexibility in the code or invalidate the experiment tables. To document the inspected implementation, replace the nonlinear family equations with joint-distance formulas, using a separate distance and parameter set for each family:\par
\[\begin{gathered}r^2=\sum_{j\ne i}\theta_{ij}^{\,2}(x_j-x_j')^2+\theta_{ii}^{\,2}(w-w')^2,\\ k_{\rm SE}=v e^{-r^2/2},\qquad k_{\rm M12}=v e^{-r},\\ k_{\rm M32}=v(1+\sqrt{3}r)e^{-\sqrt{3}r},\qquad k_{\rm RQ}=v\left(1+\frac{r^2}{2a}\right)^{-a}.\end{gathered}\]\par
Here v is that family’s variance and a>0 its rational-quadratic shape. Retain the sum across families and the linear term. This repair matches the static GPflow construction and restores nonzero mixed contrasts analytically. It is not an author-adopted correction, a full implementation equivalence proof, or a new empirical validation.\par
\section*{4. Problem 2 — positive-tolerance pruning can return a cyclic graph}
Source and implementation dependency. Appendix C.3, p19 describes acyclic thresholding before cooldown, while pp18 and20 use a spectral-phase stopping tolerance of 0.005. In SpectralWithPenaltyTrainer.py, lines780-792, the release first zeros entries smaller than the minimum threshold (default 0.0001), then removes minimum-weight edges only while cyclicity(A)>1e-10, and finally binarizes every nonzero entry. most\_likely\_graph, lines794-801, calls this routine. metrics.py, lines82-104 defines cyclicity(A)=trace(exp(A))-D [S3].\par
Admissible counterexample. Let P have ones at (1,2), (2,3), (3,1) and zeros elsewhere, and set A=0.0005 P. It is a nonnegative, zero-diagonal three-node weighted adjacency. Every edge is above the minimum threshold. Its spectral radius is 0.0005, below 0.005, so the preceding spectral stopping condition does not exclude it. The abstract routine imposes no other input condition that removes this case. Since P\textasciicircum{}3=I and trace(P)=trace(P\textasciicircum{}2)=0, exact arithmetic gives:\par
\[\begin{aligned}c(A)&=3\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(0.0005)^{3k}}{(3k)!}\\&=\frac{(0.0005)^3}{2}+\frac{(0.0005)^6}{240}+\cdots\\&=6.2500000000065104\ldots\times10^{-11}<10^{-10}.\end{aligned}\]\par
The initial minimum-weight pruning removes no edge. The while condition is false, so the loop makes zero deletions, then binarization returns P, the directed three-cycle. An independently written exact-series calculation and a topological-sort check reproduce these steps [C1]. The gap from the tolerance is substantial; this is not a rounding artifact. Small positive background weights subsequently removed by the minimum threshold do not rescue the guarantee.\par
Counterevidence and limits. This input is a counterexample to the release routine’s universal DAG-return behavior, not evidence that a trained model actually produced it. The spectral constraint is also a tolerance, so it cannot exclude the construction. Static inspection of train\_continuous\_causal.py, lines62-89, shows graph extraction used for cooldown; results/get\_results.py, lines26-41, leaves an optional cycle-pruning loop commented out. No later exact support-acyclicity check was identified on these inspected paths. Nevertheless, no published predicted graph was replayed and no affected published SHD, SID or F1 value was demonstrated.\par
Impact and repair scope. The code can pass a cyclic support into downstream processing that expects a DAG. That is a real implementation defect, classified Minor because its link to publication-critical outputs remains unresolved. A precise property-level remedy is to test acyclicity of the binary support with a topological sort and remove an existing edge while that support remains cyclic. Each deletion reduces a finite edge set, so this procedure terminates with a DAG. Merely lowering a positive matrix-exponential tolerance is not a general exact-DAG guarantee without further weight bounds. No author code was patched or executed, and the behavior of a concrete repair has not been empirically retested.\par
\section*{5. Problem 3 - the experiment does not isolate posterior-overlap error}
Claim and core dependency. Section 3, p4 points to Section 6.1 as the estimate showing a low probability of error for the chosen multivariate model class. Section 6.1, p7 specifically says that discrete model comparison estimates the error only due to overlap in posteriors of different causal models. It also states an expectation of higher error due to continuous-relaxation optimization; this tentative comparison is not treated here as a failed universal claim. This experiment supplies a central empirical justification for the advertised exchange of restrictive identifiability assumptions for flexible Bayesian models with a small error probability [S1].\par
Decisive design evidence. Enumerating all 25 graphs removes a discrete graph-search approximation. It does not make the graph scores exact. Appendix D.1, p21 compares a collapsed variational lower bound using 200 inducing points and optimized latent-variable variational parameters. It explicitly describes a highly nonconvex objective, Adam followed by BFGS, and ten restarts selected by their best lower bound. Closed-form evaluation of the collapsed lower bound is not closed-form marginal likelihood. Figure3, p22 further reports different SHD, SID and F1 values as the number of restarts changes for the same datasets and model family. Its caption and visible curves therefore directly show that the enumerated procedure’s reported errors depend on optimization, not solely on unchanged posterior overlap. The figure was rechecked in the saved source-page image during acceptance; no new experiment was run.\par
The inferential gap can be expressed without inventing a numerical counterexample. For a fixed graph \(G\), model parameters \(\eta\), observed data \(\mathcal D\), and admissible variational distribution \(q\) over latent quantities \(Z\), a variational bound has the form \[\mathcal L_G(q,\eta)
 =\log p(\mathcal D\mid G,\eta)-\Delta_G(q,\eta),
 \qquad \Delta_G(q,\eta)\geq0.\] For the ordinary ELBO, \(\Delta_G\) is the corresponding posterior KL divergence; collapsing analytically tractable variables still leaves the stated variational approximation. Choosing among all graphs using these bounds does not cancel graph-dependent gaps or remove incomplete optimization. No equal-gap, zero-gap or ranking-preservation result is supplied for the evaluated procedure. In addition, the continuous and discrete implementations change the variational treatment, inducing-point count, kernels and restart schedule together, so their difference does not identify a contribution from continuous relaxation alone.\par
Alternative readings and surviving evidence. Definition3.1 allows a general score, so an empirical error estimate for the actual approximate scorer is meaningful. The paper also discloses its variational approximation and restart sensitivity. Those disclosures preserve a faithful procedure-performance reading, but do not justify the explicit error-source isolation. Under a matched generating distribution and classification loss, a decision rule’s population risk can upper-bound optimal Bayes risk; that is a different statement from attributing observed errors only to overlap, and finite SHD/SID/F1 measurements do not by themselves supply such a risk decomposition. No exact-score reversal on the published datasets, true overlap rate, corrected experimental score, or actual contribution of any one error source is asserted here.\par
Severity and correction scope. P3 is Major because the invalid isolation inference bears directly on the core empirical justification for flexible multivariate Bayesian discovery and on the interpretation of the proposed relaxation. The observed performance, conditional theory and interaction-capable intended model remain separately supported within their inspected scope. A precise textual correction is to describe the discrete experiment as measuring the complete approximate inference procedure and to describe the continuous-discrete comparison as a comparison of complete procedures. Retaining a quantitative error-source decomposition would require matched implementations, controlled approximation/optimization comparisons, or validated marginal-likelihood and ranking-error bounds. These missing quantities do not prevent confirming the present design’s failure to isolate its claimed source.\par
\section*{6. Checked reasoning and qualified non-findings}
Conditional distinguishability. The Bayesian model comparison argument in Appendix B was traced through Lemmas B.2-B.3 and Corollary B.4 (pp14-15). The faithfulness and Bayes-factor-consistency conditions are operative assumptions. In particular, consistency is not automatically obtained just by noticing that two permitted distribution classes do not overlap. No counterexample satisfying all stated conditions was found; this review does not replace a general proof audit for all priors and singular models.\par
Finite N versus population. Definition B.1 uses equality for every sample size. Failure of that equality implies a difference for some sample size, not necessarily for each fixed N. The population qualification made on p4 is essential when interpreting Proposition B.7. A small independent illustration uses X-to-Y binary mechanisms with mutually independent theta in \{2/5,3/5\}, q0 in \{1/10,1/5\}, q1 in \{4/5,9/10\}, each uniform; theta=P(X=1) and qx=P(Y=1|X=x). Reverse the roles of X and Y for the opposite prior. All eight sampled distributions are positive and faithful. The reverse marginal m=(1-theta)q0+theta q1 takes eight distinct values and determines a nonconstant reverse conditional theta q1/m, so the induced reverse mechanism prior is not separable. Nonetheless the one-sample predictives coincide at (17/40,3/40,3/40,17/40); their total variation is zero and equal-prior classification error is 1/2. Two-sample total variation is 19/2500 [C1]. This is a fixed-N scope illustration, not a refutation of the paper’s population statement. A tie convention is needed when reconciling a strict score comparison in Definition3.1 with Bayes-error expressions.\par
Continuous hyperparameter MAP versus a discrete graph posterior. Under the continuous Gamma(shape1, rate10) prior of Section4.3 and Appendix C.5, a parameter is exactly zero with probability zero. Under Eq10’s exact-nonzero support map, independent positive off-diagonal parameters yield a complete directed support almost surely for D>1, rather than a nondegenerate sparse-DAG prior. The density penalty can still favor small weights, and constrained optimization can reach boundaries. Maximizing a hyperparameter density is not integration over all parameters yielding a discrete graph. Because the paper explicitly uses a continuous relaxation, empirical-Bayes hyperparameter learning, an ELBO and thresholding, this distinction is retained as a scope clarification, not a refuted exact-equivalence theorem that the paper never states.\par
Counts, selection and metrics. Independent enumeration confirms 25 labelled three-variable DAGs in six permutation classes, with class sizes1,3,3,6,6,6. Five datasets per class means30 datasets. The 20/50-variable settings use four graph types with five instances each; Syntren has ten datasets. Figure3 permutes existing restart runs, not fifty new independent datasets. Different restart budgets and the separate 10-variable hyperparameter tuning set are disclosed. Appendix I acknowledges settings/metrics on which other methods win; no universal-dominance claim was imposed.\par
Static result checks rejected two tempting allegations. Restart selection uses minimum loss (negative ELBO), not minimum test SHD/SID. Although result CSVs contain both reversal-cost-one SHD and SHD\_dac, the plotting/table path selects SHD\_dac, consistent with the paper’s adjacency-matrix Hamming convention. Thus neither alleged selection leakage nor a reported SHD-convention discrepancy is supported on the inspected path.\par
Released CSVs provide limited descriptive corroboration. The 30-row discrete three-variable table has3 nonzero-SHD cases, mean SHD\_dac0.1667 and mean SID0.2667. Selecting the minimum loss within each graph/data pair from the corrected continuous table’s60 rows gives30 rows,19 nonzero-SHD cases, mean SHD\_dac1.6 and mean SID1.8 [C1]. These are recomputations of released tabular summaries, not reproduction of Figure1, proof of exact publication provenance, or an estimate with certified sampling uncertainty. The original CSV’s other SHD column has mean0.9667; it must not be substituted for SHD\_dac when matching the stated metric.\par
\section*{7. Conclusion, repair and remaining work}
The core flexibility rationale remains compatible with the joint-kernel implementation, and the conditional theoretical distinction between observational equivalence and Bayesian comparison was not disproved. The experiment’s stronger isolation of posterior-overlap error is unsupported by its approximate, optimization-dependent design. Correcting that inference is required for this report’s Major finding; calculating the exact source contributions is a separate unresolved task. Resolving P2’s publication impact requires the actual predicted graph supports and publication-run provenance.\par
