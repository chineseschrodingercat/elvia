Outcome: Scope Clarification, with substantive stronger interpretations remaining Needs Evidence. No Major or Minor scientific defect is established under the defensible reading of this position paper as a research proposal. \section*{What the evidence establishes}
Sections 3.1–3.3, pp.3–5, distinguish serving-time interventions from changes to data, training, and model parameters. Runtime output filtering does not retroactively change how a model was trained; retraining alone does not immediately incorporate arbitrary new external rights information. Those observations support coordination. They do not establish impossibility for every isolated method, task, environment, or information source. A model trained offline can still implement a context-conditioned response policy at inference; external updating and responding to an input are different capabilities.\par
The abstract and §5.5, pp.8–9, describe scalable effectiveness and improvement on successive iterations. The paper supplies a conceptual architecture, borrowed benchmark results, and an illustrative ChatGPT exchange, but no joint objective, update rule, convergence argument, implemented-system evaluation, or latency measurements. Its millisecond-response and per-iteration-improvement descriptions therefore remain design intentions. Appendix C acknowledges resource constraints, and §5.4 makes model updates conditional on need. These are counterevidence to an unconditional guarantee reading. No invented bad optimizer is treated as a counterexample to an algorithm the authors never specified.\par
\section*{Detailed mathematical scope check: n-gram length and precision}
Source claim. Section 5.2, p.7, Log suspicious activities, presents higher n as improving precision while recall drops. Its subsequent sentences acknowledge that flagged cases may be noninfringing and unflagged cases may remain risky. This supports a heuristic reading, but longer matches alone do not mathematically guarantee higher infringement-classification precision.\par
Definitions and assumptions. Hold fixed the candidate texts, the blocklist, and independent binary evaluation labels. Let the detector flag a candidate if it contains any exact n-gram from the blocklist. With no approximate-filter errors, every longer-match alarm is also a shorter-match alarm. Let T and F be the initial true and false positives; increasing n removes t true and f false positives, with 0<=t<=T, 0<=f<=F and at least one remaining alarm. Recall cannot increase under these assumptions. Precision has a different condition:\par
\[P_{\rm new}-P_{\rm old}=\frac{T-t}{T+F-t-f}-\frac{T}{T+F}=\frac{Tf-Ft}{(T+F)(T+F-t-f)}.\] The denominator is positive, so precision increases exactly when Tf>Ft. Alarm-set nesting does not imply this condition.\par
Complete finite witness. Use one reference containing 60 distinct artificial tokens x0,\(\ldots\),x59. Seven positive candidates contain x0,\(\ldots\),x29 followed by distinct unmatched suffixes. An eighth positive contains the full reference plus an unmatched suffix. Two negative candidates also contain the full reference with distinct suffixes. Labels are stipulated independently: the eight positives represent prohibited uses in this hypothetical benchmark and the two negatives represent authorized uses. The artificial tokens are not asserted to constitute a copyrighted work, and no legal decision about actual excerpts is made. This is a classifier countermodel with fixed labels, not a clinical or legal dataset.\par
Exact detector | True / false alarms | Precision / recall\par
n=20: all 10 candidates match | TP=8, FP=2 | 8/10=0.80; 8/8=1\par
n=40: only three full-reference candidates match | TP=1, FP=2 | 1/3\(\approx\)0.333; 1/8=0.125\par
All structural conditions hold: the same texts and blocklist are used; each 40-gram match contains a matching 20-gram; the seven short positive strings have fewer than 40 tokens; labels do not change. Here T=8, F=2, t=7 and f=0, giving Tf\(-\)Ft=\(-\)14. The independent program reproduces both alarm sets and exact rational scores. Therefore a general promise of increasing precision is false even in this simpler matching setting.\par
Counterevidence and precise supported statement. The cited MemFree source explains a practical tradeoff and selects n=10 qualitatively (§3.2 and Appendix B.2–B.3), not by proving legal-label precision monotonicity [E3]. SHIELD uses n together with other thresholds and copyright verification; its Appendix D fixes 10-grams [E4]. Changing an actual decoder may change its outputs, while Bloom filtering introduces other assumptions. Thus even recall nesting cannot be transferred automatically to those systems. A defensible clarification is: n changes the matching tradeoff; on fixed texts with nested exact alarms, recall is nonincreasing, while precision can move either way. The actual trend must be measured under specified labels and parameters. This narrows an informal expectation without refuting the recommendation to log and calibrate.\par
\section*{Benchmark and legal-scope checks}
Table 1, p.3, reproduces the copying columns from CopyBench Table 5 (printed p.15142). Negative parenthetical changes indicate reductions, despite the target caption’s reversed legend. This presentation issue is excluded from standalone scientific finding counts under the task contract. Rounded displayed rates do not permit an exact audit of relative changes: hypothetical underlying values 0.08\(\rightarrow\)0.04 display as 0.1\(\rightarrow\)0.0 with \(-\)50\%, and 2.4\(\rightarrow\)2.568 displays as 2.4\(\rightarrow\)2.6 with +7\%. These are compatibility witnesses, not recovered source data.\par
The §3.1 summary on p.4 must be read as inconsistent non-literal mitigation across tested settings. For Tulu, system prompts reduce event and character scores by reported 28\% and 29\%; other combinations improve little or worsen. CopyBench itself uses the same broad qualitative summary, so no altered-data allegation is supported. CopyBench §6 expressly says its behavioral tests do not adjudicate infringement. Therefore neither this table nor the single Figure 2 interaction establishes legal liability, universal failure of online methods, or the efficacy of the proposed joint framework [E2].\par
For the Figure 2 jurisdiction example, the Canadian guide confirms the former life-plus-50 term and nonrevival after the 2022 extension. Conditional on the paper’s 1961 death year and ordinary applicable term, expiry is at the end of 2011 and public-domain entry is 1 January 2012. The target’s 2011 entry date does not change its 2025 cross-jurisdiction contrast. The US 95-year rule with calendar-year expiry gives end-2047 for a qualifying 1952 copyright; original registration and renewal conditions were not independently established [E5, E6]. These boundary-date observations are not promoted to substantive findings. Current court outcomes or later law were not retroactively used to criticize the 2025 paper.\par
\section*{Coverage, versions, and remaining work}
The inspected appendices give database examples, roles/access and qualitative costs, implementation constraints, and lifecycle mappings. They provide no complete rights oracle or numerical system evaluation. Figure 3 and Figure 5 consistently map mitigation, examination, and calibration; their arrows do not prove convergence. Figure 2 lacks a reproducible model/version/date and sampling protocol for estimating failure rates. Internal-state copyright prediction in §5.2 is explicitly proposed research, not an established detector.\par
No target-specific public implementation, separate supplement, later edition, or erratum was found in the inspected PMLR/institutional records and targeted searches. Appendices A–D are included in the source PDF. OpenReview’s verification challenge blocked revision/attachment history; a secondary asset-inventory request returned HTTP 404. These are access limits, not proof of absence. The CopyBench dependency repository was located, but no author code was executed. A secondary review of the cited NAF theory found conditional reference-model guarantees, with no specific theorem misattribution established in the target [E7].\par
Remaining work is concrete: specify the objective and updater, rights-label assumptions and jurisdictions, update acceptance criteria, dataset/sampling design, and compute budget; compare the complete framework with coordinated and isolated baselines on held-out copying, over-filtering, utility, latency, and newly emerging risks. Such experiments would test the stronger efficacy and continuous-improvement interpretations. They were not performed in this audit.\par
