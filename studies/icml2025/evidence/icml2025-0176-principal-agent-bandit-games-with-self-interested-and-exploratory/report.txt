Abstract: This claim-focused review identifies a failure of the bounded-estimator argument used to establish deterministic target-arm selection in the linear reward analysis. An admissible noise event of probability one half violates Lemma G.6 while satisfying its stated confidence event. This is a Major defect in the derivation of the core linear guarantee, without establishing that the stated regret rate is false. Separately, an exact bounded-reward example exceeds Algorithm 3’s stated round limit; its logarithmic order survives, so this finding is Minor. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Model and necessary distinctions

In the linear model on page 4, the finite arm set satisfies \(\mathcal A\subseteq B(0,1)\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and spans \(\mathbb R^d\). The true agent and principal parameters \(s^\star,\nu^\star\) lie in \(B(0,1)\). The observations are \[R_{A_t}(t)=\langle s^\star,A_t\rangle+\eta^A(t),\qquad
 X_{A_t}(t)=\langle\nu^\star,A_t\rangle+\eta^P(t),\] where the noises are conditionally 1-subgaussian. This linear model does not impose the \([0,1]\) reward restriction that the adjacent paragraph imposes on the separate i.i.d. model. The agent uses ordinary least squares, initialized by an arbitrary \(\widehat s_1\in B(0,1)\), and selects an empirical maximizer of \(\langle\widehat s_t,a\rangle+\pi_a(t)\). No projection or clipping of later estimates is specified.

To avoid the source’s inclusive-\(t\) observation-index ambiguity, \(\widehat s\) below means the estimate formed from the observations already received before the next action. The counterexample therefore applies under the usual causal reading of the learning model.

Equation reference: eq:optimal. For a target arm \(a\), Equation (2) gives the minimum incentive \[
 \pi_a^\star=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}
          \langle\widehat s,b-a\rangle.\] Algorithm 7 caps its target incentives at \(2d+T^{-1}\). The validity of this cap is a separate question from whether an OLS estimate is statistically close to \(s^\star\).

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The target-selection lemma assumes an absent OLS norm bound).  The proof of Lemma G.6 (page 46) uses \(\pi_a^\star\leq\max_b\|\widehat s_t\|\|b-a\|\leq 2d\) to conclude that the capped offer strictly exceeds the minimum incentive. The corresponding norm claim also appears in the explanation of Algorithm 8 on page 9. The stated OLS model does not imply \(\|\widehat s_t\|\leq d\).

## Source claim and dependency

Lemma G.6 asserts, conditional on the event \(\mathcal E\) defined on page 42, that every target offer in every phase strictly exceeds the target’s minimum incentive. Its base case is phase 1, when the offer is \(2d+T^{-1}\) for all target arms. The page 42 notation then identifies intended target rounds \(\mathcal T^E_{m,a}\) with actual arm rounds \(\mathcal T_{m,a}\). Algorithm 7, Equation (45), and Lemmas G.7–G.10 use the resulting sample allocation and design matrices. The proof of Theorem 5.1 on pages 43–44 expressly invokes Lemma G.6 in its regret decomposition and sample counts.

## An exact-design counterexample

Equation reference: eq:linear-example. Set \[
 d=1,\quad \mathcal A=\{+1,-1\},\quad
 s^\star=-1,\quad \nu^\star=0,\quad
 \widehat s_1=0,\quad T=65536,\quad \delta=T^{-1}.\] Both arms and both parameters satisfy the unit-ball restrictions, and the arms span \(\mathbb R\). Let the first agent noise be a Rademacher random variable, taking \(+1\) and \(-1\) with probability \(1/2\) each, and let every later agent noise and every principal noise be zero. The first noise is conditionally 1-subgaussian because \(\mathbb E e^{\lambda\eta}=\cosh\lambda\leq e^{\lambda^2/2}\); the later zero noises also meet the condition. The linear model permits this time-dependent noise sequence. There is no exploratory behavior.

Choose the phase-1 design \(\rho_1(+1)=\rho_1(-1)=1/2\) and list the \(+1\) arm first in Algorithm 7’s target loop. Its design matrix is \(G=1\), and \(\max_a a^2/G=1\); thus it is even an exact optimal design and certainly satisfies the stated 2-approximation requirement. Its phase-1 block has at least two rounds. There is an initial-index ambiguity in Algorithm8: it defines \(z_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb N\), but its initial unit ball has width2 in the normalized direction. Thus line3 has no admissible nonnegative index. Under the natural intended dyadic extension to \(i=-1\), one has \(z_{-1}=1/4\) and, with \(\epsilon_0=1\), line4’s threshold is \(4/\|a_1-a_2\|=2\). The algorithm therefore breaks before any incentive or observation and returns \(c_1\in[-1,1]\). For the variant \(\{1,-1/4\}\) below the threshold is \(16/5\), so the same immediate return holds. The execution witness uses this explicit intended reading; the literal natural-number indexing instead leaves the initial search undefined. It does not silently assume that a nonempty search has already occurred.

For every such permitted output, the \(33\epsilon_0\) term with \(\epsilon_0=1\) makes Algorithm7’s first-phase offer equal to the cap. The subsequent false norm bound and target rejection are independent of which \(c_1\in[-1,1]\) is returned.

Initially, the incentive \(2+T^{-1}\) makes the agent choose \(+1\). On the event \(\{\eta^A(1)=-1\}\), of probability \(1/2\), its first reward is \(-2\). After that observation, \[U=1,\qquad \widehat s=1^{-1}(1)(-2)=-2.\] At the next intended \(+1\) round, Equation [eq:optimal] (#eq:optimal) yields \[\pi^\star_{+1}=(-2)(-1-1)=4.\] The actual offer is \(2+T^{-1}<4\). Equivalently, the two scores are \[\widehat s(+1)+2+T^{-1}=T^{-1},
 \qquad \widehat s(-1)=2.\] The agent strictly prefers \(-1\), so arbitrary tie-breaking is immaterial. The claimed inequality \(\pi_{+1}>\pi^\star_{+1}\) is false.

## The confidence event does not exclude this path

Equation reference: eq:ols-path. This is not merely an example outside the good event. After any \(n\geq1\) observations on the specified branch, all arms have squared value one and all later noises vanish. Therefore \[
 U_n=n,\qquad
 \widehat s_n=s^\star-\frac1n,\qquad
 |a(\widehat s_n-s^\star)|=\frac1n
 \quad(a\in\mathcal A).\] Writing \(L_0=\log(4KT/\delta)\) with \(K=2\), we have \[\frac1n \leq \sqrt{\frac{2L_0}{n}}\qquad(n\geq1).\] Thus the path satisfies even the conventional confidence radius using the squared Mahalanobis norm. The event displayed on page 42 uses a norm under the square root; its radius is no smaller on this example. The agent part of \(\mathcal E\) holds at every later time, including all times after phase 1 to which that event applies. All principal observations and the principal estimate are zero, so the principal part holds exactly. Consequently \(\mathcal E\) holds on the probability-\(1/2\) branch that violates Lemma G.6.

## A variant makes the design mismatch explicit

For the two symmetric arms, a wrong sign happens to preserve the scalar Gram increment. This coincidence does not rescue the target-selection lemma. Replace the arm set by \(\{1,-1/4\}\) and retain all other parameters, the half-half design, and the same first noise. Now \[G=\frac{17}{32},\qquad
 \max_a\frac{a^2}{G}=\frac{32}{17}<2,\] so this remains an admissible design. After the first reward, \(\widehat s=-2\) and the minimum incentive for target \(1\) is \(5/2\), again larger than \(2+T^{-1}\). The agent selects \(-1/4\); its actual Gram increment is \(1/16\), whereas the prescribed target allocation uses \(1\). For every continuation, \(U\geq1\) and \(\widehat s-s^\star=-1/U\), so \(|a|/U\leq |a|\sqrt{2L_0/U}\) proves that the confidence event remains satisfied. Principal observations remain zero. This variant directly invalidates identifying the designed and actual sample allocations.

## Alternative readings, impact, and repair status

The norm restriction in the model is on the true parameter and the initial estimate, not on every later OLS estimate. Interpreting the agent as using projected OLS would change the specified behavior. Conditioning on \(\mathcal E\), taking a large horizon, or allowing arbitrary tie-breaking does not remove the counterexample. The comparison concerns the linear model, so it does not impose an unbounded reward on the paper’s separate bounded i.i.d. model.

The supported assessment is Major: a false lemma and invalid bounded-estimator step sit directly in the proof of the paper’s core linear regret guarantee. The calculation does not prove that the rate in Theorem 5.1 is unattainable, nor that the i.i.d. guarantees fail. A bounded-estimator assumption would restore the particular cap argument, but it would alter the agent model and would require a new analysis; no complete repair of Theorem 5.1 is verified here.

Problem 2 (The stated search-round limit is exceeded).  Lemma 3.1 (page 6), repeated as Lemma D.6 (page 18), states that Algorithm 3 uses at most \(2\lceil\log_2 T\rceil\) rounds. The two counters in the page 14 pseudocode permit more rounds, even with constant bounded rewards and no exploration.

## Exact construction and complete count

Let \(T=2^L\) with \(L\geq2\), and define \[h=2^{-(L-1)},\qquad q=h-2^{-2L}.\] Give the target arm constant reward zero and its only competitor constant reward \(q\). Both are valid \([0,1]\)-bounded i.i.d. distributions. Consider a call after the competitor has been sampled once, with target empirical mean zero and competitor empirical mean \(q\). Such a state is reachable from zero initial estimates in Algorithm 1: list the competitor first, complete its first search and first-phase block, and then search for the target. For \(T=65536\) the relevant first-phase block is much shorter than the horizon. The principal rewards may be identically zero. All later empirical means stay constant.

Equation reference: eq:duration. The first \(L-1\) midpoint bids are \(1/2,1/4,\ldots,h\). Each is strictly above \(q\), so each selects the target. The midpoint counter is then \(C_1=L-1\) and the stored upper offer is \(h\). The next \(L\) midpoint bids are \[h(1-2^{-j}),\qquad j=1,\ldots,L.\] They are all strictly below \(q\), since even the largest equals \(h-2^{-(2L-1)}<h-2^{-2L}=q\). Each therefore fails and triggers an asymmetric check at bid \(h\), which strictly succeeds. The algorithm does not test its \(C_1\) stopping condition on those failed midpoint rounds. It returns at line 17 when the successful-check counter reaches \(C_2=L\). The total is exactly \[
 (L-1)+2L=3L-1>2L.\] No tie-breaking convention is needed. The independently written exact rational diagnostic, with \(L=16\), recorded 47 offer rounds against the stated limit of 32. Its entire bid/action transcript is retained in `verification/0176/check_results.json`.

## Counterevidence and bounded impact

This example does not establish an error in the returned incentive bound: the returned offer is \(h+2/T\), with overpayment \(2/T+2^{-2L}\). Its runtime also remains \(O(\log T)\). The issue is therefore Minor: the precise round bound and the duration argument in Lemma D.6 are false, but a change of the constant does not by itself refute the asymptotic regret orders. The source proof’s proposed worst case, in which every midpoint fails, misses the initial successful midpoint rounds accumulated before the repeated checks. No new universal sharp bound is asserted in this report.

# Overall dependence and conclusion

The linear analysis needs a valid guarantee linking its offered incentives, the agent’s actual choices, and its designed sample counts. The failure of Lemma G.6 is not a peripheral appendix observation: its conclusion is used by the principal theoretical result in Section 5. A corrected model or algorithm would require fresh proofs, including the associated concentration and elimination steps. Empirical reruns cannot establish that missing universal guarantee.

The search-duration error is logically separate and has narrower supported impact. This review does not infer that the other regret theorems are false, and it does not certify their complete proofs. No later version or erratum was supplied or independently compared; absence of a known correction here is not a claim that no correction exists.
