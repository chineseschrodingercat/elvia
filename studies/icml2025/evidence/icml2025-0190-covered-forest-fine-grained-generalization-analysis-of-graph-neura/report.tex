Abstract: Four demonstrated problems affect central claims: the released arithmetic reconstructing Table 1 divides a statistical term by the wrong quantity; the general robustness theorem allows sample-selected partitions that defeat its concentration argument; the stated network class has no common continuity modulus when internal gains are unrestricted; and the Forest-distance padding proof implicitly requires zero-preserving updates. Exact finite constructions and an independent reconstruction of all eight bound entries support Major assessments. These findings do not establish that observed trained-model losses are false, nor do they refute fixed-partition or suitably restricted network results.\par

\# Scientific contribution and dependencies\par
The paper uses graph pseudometrics and covering numbers to study generalization of message-passing neural networks (MPNNs). It seeks a finer account of graph similarity than a discrete distinguishability metric, including normalized-sum, sum and mean aggregation settings. A robustness framework connects small within-cell loss variation to generalization; continuity of the network class controls cell size; metric covering estimates control the number of cells. Table 1 then presents estimated bounds for real datasets as evidence that this analysis can be quantitatively tight.\par
The four findings address distinct links in this chain. They are not consequences inferred from poor empirical performance. Their impact is bounded separately so that a defect in the general framework is not automatically attributed to every special case.\par
\# Problem 1: Table 1 uses an incorrect statistical normalization\par
Rating: Major. Locations: Propositions 15–16, p. 8; Table 1 and Section 6 Q3, p. 9; Table 3, p. 51; released `utils/plot/q3\_print\_generalization\_bound.py`, lines 4–29 and 32–49.\par
\#\# Claim and exact discrepancy\par
Proposition 15’s statistical term is \[M\sqrt{\frac{4\mathcal N\log2+2\log(1/\delta)}{|S|}},\] and Proposition 16 substitutes \(m/(k+1)\) for the covering-number upper bound. The released script defines the sample-size array \(S=(150,3288,22216,32902)\) but never uses it. Its implemented term is \[\frac{M\sqrt{4m\log2/(k+1)+2\log(1/\delta)}}{m}.\] The required denominator outside the square root is \(\sqrt{|S|}\), not \(m\). The ratio of the correctly normalized statistical term to the released term is \(m/\sqrt{|S|}\), independently of \(k\).\par
Using the script’s coefficients and \(\delta=0.05\), let \(A_k=4m\log2/(k+1)+2\log20\) and \(\widetilde C=2C_{FD}/n\). The script minimizes \(4\widetilde Ck+M\sqrt{A_k}/m\) over integer \(1\le k\le100\). The auxiliary agent and parent independently reconstructed these scalar formulas without importing or executing the author script.\par

The all-eight numerical match is strong evidence connecting the released formula to the reported table, while not proving which checkout produced the historical outputs. The table explicitly labels the constants as estimates, and Table 3 says its equivalence counts are dataset-dependent rather than counts over the full graph class. Those honest qualifications do not change the missing square root of sample size.\par

This is Major because quantitative tightness in real-world settings is a core empirical conclusion. The corrected numbers above are diagnostics, not certified repaired generalization bounds. Repair requires correct normalization plus validation of global constants, cover scope, readout normalization and any data-dependent radius selection. No model or dataset was rerun.\par
\# Problem 2: sample-selected partitions defeat the robustness theorem\par
Rating: Major. Locations: robustness definition and Theorem 4, p. 5; Definition 34/Theorem 35, p. 32; Theorem 37 proof and Proposition 38, p. 33.\par
\#\# Claim and admissible construction\par
The definition permits, for every training sample \(S\), a partition into \(K(S)\) cells with within-cell loss oscillation less than \(\epsilon(S)\). It does not require a partition fixed before the sample is drawn. Theorem 4 claims, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), \[|\ell_{\exp}(h_S)-\ell_{\mathrm{emp}}(h_S)|
 \le\epsilon(S)+M\sqrt{\frac{2K(S)\log2+2\log(1/\delta)}{|S|}}. \tag{R1}\] Consider a domain of 1000 distinct graph isomorphism classes, the paths \(P_1,\ldots,P_{1000}\) distinguished by their orders. Draw 100 training examples independently and uniformly, all with label zero. Let the hypothesis class contain all functions from this finite graph domain to \(\{0,1\}\), and take loss \(\ell(x,0)=x\), bounded by \(M=1\).\par
Let \(h_S\) be zero on the graph orders seen in \(S\) and one on all unseen orders. Choose \(C_1(S)\) to be the observed orders and \(C_2(S)\) their complement. Both cells are nonempty, \(K(S)=2\), and within-cell loss is constant. The strict robustness condition is satisfied with \(\epsilon(S)=0.001\). To define the learner for arbitrary nonempty sample sizes, if every domain order has been observed, use the identically zero predictor and any fixed nonempty two-cell partition; otherwise retain the observed/unseen partition. Thus \(K=2\) remains well defined beyond the size-100 construction.\par
Empirical loss is zero. If \(u\le100\) distinct graph orders were observed, population loss is \(1-u/1000\ge0.9\). Yet at \(\delta=0.05\), (R1) equals \[0.001+\sqrt{(4\log2+2\log20)/100}
 =0.29704143746015965.\] The bound fails for every sample, not merely with a small exceptional probability. Under the same per-sample-partition interpretation used in the main definition, giving both cells the same positive tolerance also refutes the cell-specific version in Theorems 35/37. Appendix Definition 34 places the partition phrase before the words such that for all samples, permitting a fixed-partition reading. That narrower reading excludes this example but does not repair the broader main definition. Every hypothesis is uniformly continuous on the finite discrete metric domain; the general theorem is not restricted to MPNNs.\par
\#\# Failure mechanism, dependency, and possible repair\par
Theorem 37’s proof declares the counts in the cells to have a multinomial distribution with fixed probabilities \(\mu(C_i)\) and applies a fixed-partition concentration inequality. This step cannot justify the per-sample-partition interpretation; it is compatible with the narrower fixed-partition reading of Definition 34. Here \(K\) is even the constant two; variability of \(K\) is not the essential problem.\par
A fixed, sample-independent partition would exclude this counterexample. That is an additional hypothesis, and Proposition 5/38 expressly considers a scale depending on \(h_S\), so the paper’s broader sample-dependent formulation cannot be justified by silently adding it. One can instead require a fixed partition, or establish concentration uniformly over the allowable selected partitions with an appropriate complexity penalty.\par
The finding is Major for the stated foundational theorem and proof. It does not automatically refute later applications using a deterministic metric, radius and cover fixed independently of the sample. No complete replacement framework is certified here.\par
\# Problem 3: unrestricted internal gains prevent a common modulus\par
Rating: Major. Locations: class definitions and Eqs. (1)–(2), p. 3; Theorem 8 and equicontinuity interpretation, p. 6; Theorem 43 and its proof, pp. 35–36.\par
\#\# Quantifiers and witness\par
The stated normalized-sum layer is \[h_v^{(t)}=\phi_t\!\left(\frac1n\sum_{u\in N(v)}h_u^{(t-1)}\right),
 \qquad h_G=\psi\!\left(\frac1n\sum_v h_v^{(L)}\right).\] Each internal \(\phi_t\) is Lipschitz, without a common bound on its Lipschitz constant across the class. The readout/FNN has boundedness and Lipschitz constraints. Theorem 8 nevertheless claims a single \(\gamma(\epsilon)>0\) for all graph orders and all these architectures: \[\delta_\square^T(G,H)<n^2\gamma(\epsilon)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|h_G-h_H\|_2\le\epsilon. \tag{R2}\]\par
Fix \(L=1\), initial scalar features equal to one, and \(\epsilon=1/2\). Let \(H_n\) be empty and \(G_n\) have one edge and \(n-2\) isolated vertices. Use \(\phi_1(x)=n^2x\) and \(\psi(x)=\tanh x\). The internal map is Lipschitz and allowed; the readout is bounded by one and is 1-Lipschitz.\par
For every doubly stochastic \(S\), the nonnegative matrix \(A(G_n)S\) has total sum two, while \(SA(H_n)=0\). Its cut norm is exactly two, hence \(\delta_\square^T(G_n,H_n)=2\). Each edge endpoint’s first-layer feature is \(n\), and the normalized readout input is two. Therefore \[h_{G_n}=\tanh2,\quad h_{H_n}=0,\quad
 |h_{G_n}-h_{H_n}|\approx0.96402758>1/2.\] If the class notation is read as including an additional bounded FNN after this readout, another \(\tanh\) still gives approximately \(0.746068>1/2\).\par
For any purported positive \(\gamma(1/2)\), choose \(n>\sqrt{2/\gamma(1/2)}\). The antecedent of (R2) holds and its conclusion fails. This analytic family disproves the universal claim; finite numerical evaluations merely corroborate its values.\par
\#\# Scope and correction\par
An individual network can be uniformly continuous, and a fixed finite graph quotient can have a common small scale. Neither provides the claimed scale uniformly over all \(n\) and unbounded internal gains. Restricting internal Lipschitz constants and initial feature size, or allowing the modulus to depend on the complete architecture, removes this obstruction. A full repaired theorem would need to state these dependencies and propagate them into covering bounds.\par
Theorem 8 is explicitly used to construct a common modulus for subsequent unlabeled-graph results, including Proposition 9 and Theorem 10, making this a Major core issue. Graph pseudometric definitions and unrelated fixed-network claims are not refuted. The external theorem cited in the proof was not independently audited; this review attributes the verified error only to the statement and class in the reviewed paper.\par
\# Problem 4: padding proof assumes zero preservation\par
Rating: Major. Locations: sum-aggregation Eq. (3), p. 3; Lemma 14, p. 8; Lemma 57 and its \(L=2\) proof, pp. 43–45.\par
\#\# Claim and missing condition\par
The claimed Forest-distance bound has the form \[\|h_G-h_H\|_2\le\frac{C(L)}n L_\psi
 \prod_{i=1}^L L_{\phi_i}\,FD_L(G,H), \tag{R3}\] where \(C(L)\) depends on depth and layer Lipschitz constants. The printed \(L=2\) proof with identity self/neighbor weights gives \(C(2)=2\).\par
On p. 44 a missing neighbor is matched to a padded vertex whose first-layer value is set to zero. The next step bounds differences through \(L_{\phi_1}\) times differences of original features. Lipschitz continuity controls \(|\phi(x)-\phi(0)|\), not \(|\phi(x)-0|\) when \(\phi(0)\ne0\). Sum aggregation allows general Lipschitz updates. Positive homogeneity is imposed separately for mean aggregation and cannot be imported into the sum setting.\par
\#\# A fixed-network contradiction\par
Let \(n=2\), \(d=1\), \(L=2\), all self/neighbor scalar weights equal one, \(\phi_1(x)=x+1\), \(\phi_2(x)=x\) and \(\psi(x)=\tanh x\). All Lipschitz constants are one. Give every original vertex the positive feature \(a>0\). Let \(G\) be an edge and \(H\) be empty. The edge graph’s first- and second-layer vertex values are \(1+2a\) and \(2+4a\); the empty graph’s are \(1+a\) at both layers. Thus \[h_G=\tanh(2+4a),\qquad h_H=\tanh(1+a).\] The two depth-two trees of \(G\) have four non-root attributes equal to \(a\); their matched padded attributes in \(H\) are zero. Root attributes match. Every allowed level- and edge-preserving matching has the same total cost \(FD_2(G,H)=4a\).\par
As \(a\downarrow0\), all original features remain nonzero, \(FD_2\to0\), while \[|h_G-h_H|\longrightarrow\tanh2-\tanh1
 \approx0.2024334241>0.\] No finite Forest-distance Lipschitz constant works for this fixed network on the stated real-feature domain. For \(a=0.01\), the printed \(C(2)=2\) bound is \(0.04\), versus actual difference \(0.2009852695\).\par
\#\# Connection to one-hot labels\par
The obstruction is not confined to arbitrarily small real labels. Take \(n=100\), all original features equal to the one-dimensional one-hot vector 1, one edge versus no edges, and degree bound \(q=2\). Set \[\phi_1(x)=x+B,\quad \phi_2(x)=x-(B+1),\quad
 B=100,\quad\psi(x)=\tanh x.\] All layer Lipschitz constants and identity weights remain one. The empty graph’s final features are zero; the edge endpoints have final feature \(B+3\), all other features zero. The normalized readout input is \(2(B+3)/n=2.06\), giving output difference \(0.9680303033\). Here \(FD_2=4\), while the stated \(L=2\) proof gives \(2FD_2/n=0.08\). Letting \(B=n\) also defeats a constant depending only on depth and the fixed Lipschitz constants.\par
These constructions establish the missing offset dependence in the sum-aggregation theorem supporting Propositions 15–16. Mean aggregation’s explicit positive homogeneity and bias-free zero-preserving updates avoid this particular obstruction. Adding \(\phi_t(0)=0\) is a precise candidate restriction, but no complete corrected arbitrary-depth/arbitrary-weight theorem has been certified.\par
