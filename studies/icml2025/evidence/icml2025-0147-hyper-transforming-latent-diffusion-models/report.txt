# Definitions and dependencies

Equation reference: eq:operators. For a generated weight column, write \(u=w^o_{\lceil c/k\rceil}\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(b=\bar w^b_c\in\mathbb R^m\). The paper compares \[R_{\mathrm{norm}}(u,b)=\frac{u\odot b}{\|u\odot b\|_2},
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{scale}}(u,b)=(\mathbf 1+u)\odot b.

The latent code is sampled from an encoder posterior and mapped to INR parameters by the HD. A deterministic map of a random latent code can induce a nontrivial distribution of functions; no contradiction follows from implementing that map deterministically. # Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The stated normalized-weight mechanism is incorrect).  The discussion following equation (8), page 5, attributes instability near \(u=0\) to normalized weights and their gradients vanishing. As an explanation of the displayed operator, that behavior is incorrect in general.

## Original claim and complete check

The authors frame this as a hypothesis motivated by training behavior, not a proved theorem. The displayed normalization nevertheless admits an exact calculation. Set \(m=2\), \(b=(1,1)^\top\), and \(u=(r,0)^\top\) for any \(r>0\). A single column and one group satisfy the grouping condition. The base weight is nonzero, the denominator is \(r\), and the operator is defined. No stated restriction in the operator prevents these values. Rescaling the nonzero base-weight direction gives the same output-norm conclusion.

Equation reference: eq:jacobian. For \(v=u\odot b\ne0\), let \(\widehat v=v/\|v\|_2\). Differentiation gives \[D_u R_{\mathrm{norm}}(u,b)
 =\frac{I-\widehat v\widehat v^\top}{\|v\|_2}\operatorname{diag}(b).
 \] The admissible example yields \[R_{\mathrm{norm}}((r,0)^\top,(1,1)^\top)=(1,0)^\top,\qquad
 D_uR_{\mathrm{norm}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&r^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.\] The output norm is exactly one for every \(r>0\), while transverse sensitivity grows as \(r^{-1}\). For \(r=10^{-2},10^{-6},10^{-10}\), the independent diagnostic returns output norm \(1\) and derivative norm \(10^2,10^6,10^{10}\). The formula evaluation and output are preserved in `independent_check.py` and `independent_check.json`.

## Counterevidence and supported impact

There is a null derivative in the radial direction, and a downstream loss may annihilate other derivative directions. Neither fact implies vanishing normalized weights or a vanishing full Jacobian near zero. At \(v=0\), equation (8) is undefined. An implementation using \(\max(\|v\|,\varepsilon)\) can make the output approach zero within the floor region, but its local derivative is then \(\operatorname{diag}(b)/\varepsilon\), again generally nonzero. The inspected `normalize_weights` function calls `F.normalize`; it does not establish the claimed general mechanism.

A defensible replacement is that exact normalization removes radial information, is undefined at zero, and can have large transverse sensitivity near zero. In contrast, \[R_{\mathrm{scale}}(0,b)=b,\qquad
 D_uR_{\mathrm{scale}}=\operatorname{diag}(b).\] These identities support a local conditioning distinction. They do not prove globally stable optimization or measured improvements. Equation (9) and the reported empirical gains are not refuted. The assessment is Minor: this is a local mathematical explanation error, and no logical dependence of the core performance conclusion on the erroneous vanishing argument has been established.

Problem 2 (The released metric CSV repeats cumulative observations).  The arrays in `experiments/image_metrics.py` accumulate all observations seen so far, but the entire cumulative dataframe is appended after every batch. The saved rows therefore do not form a once-per-example evaluation dataset.

## Source location and complete check

Metric accumulation occurs at lines 85–92; prefix FID calculation and broadcasting at lines 94–102; dataframe construction and `to_csv(..., mode=’a’)` at lines 105–108 in the pinned snapshot [Retained latex: \cite{code}]. The defect matters when a run has at least two nonempty batches and its CSV is interpreted as an ordinary table of evaluation observations.

For \(B\) equal-size batches of \(b\) examples, the file receives \(b,2b,\ldots,Bb\) rows, totaling \[b\sum_{i=1}^B i=\frac{bB(B+1)}2\] instead of \(bB\). An observation from batch \(j\) appears \(B-j+1\) times. With two one-example batches whose PSNR values are \(10\) and \(30\) dB, the written rows are \(10,10,30\); their mean is \(50/3\) dB instead of \(20\) dB.

FID is recomputed on each cumulative prefix and broadcast over that prefix’s rows. For illustrative prefix FIDs \(5\) and \(3\), the file contains \(5,3,3\); its mean is \(11/3\), whereas the final full-set statistic is \(3\). Averaging prefix FIDs does not produce a full-set FID. These numbers are synthetic serialization inputs, not measured LDMI activations. The independent diagnostic records them without importing or executing repository code.

## Counterevidence, correction, and scope

The in-memory arrays accumulate each observation once. The final progress-bar PSNR and FID can therefore be correct despite duplicated serialized rows. The final appended block also contains every PSNR once together with the final FID, so a reader deliberately selecting that block can recover a valid summary. Neither the paper nor the inspected files establishes that Tables 1–4 were computed by averaging the entire CSV.

For a fresh evaluation file, a precise correction is to write the completed PSNR array once after the loop and store the final global FID separately as a summary scalar. Alternatively, per-prefix records must be explicitly labeled and cannot be interpreted as independent observations. This correction is justified for serialization only; no modified author program was executed. The released-code defect is Minor. Historical impact on published conclusions remains Needs Evidence and does not raise supported severity.

# Overall dependence and remaining evidence

The HD architecture, equation (9), and two training routes survive these checks. Scale modulation is selected in the inspected CelebA-HQ configuration; the transformer implements encoder self-attention and decoder self/cross-attention. The CSV defect occurs after inference and does not alter parameter updates.

Tables 1 and 4 report different CelebA-HQ HD PSNR values, \(24.80\) and \(27.72\) dB. The description does not establish that they refer to the same run; this review does not label their difference an error. Metric provenance would require the relevant configuration, checkpoint, evaluation population, output records, and aggregation rule. Quantifying a changed score would require a new evaluation. No dataset or checkpoint was acquired here.
