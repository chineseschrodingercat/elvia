Abstract: This claim-focused review finds four invalid derivations connected to the paper’s core upper and lower bounds: an insufficient known-bound cancellation constant, a false clipping-error lemma, a mismatch between sparse penalty support and its proof, and an inadmissible lower-bound construction. Each is Major at the level of the affected derivation. None of the counterexamples is presented as a disproof of the actual algorithm’s final regret rate. Local repairs are distinguished from complete revised guarantees; an alternative existential lower-bound construction is proved. A generic scalar-solver defect is Minor. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Setup and core dependencies

The decision domain is \(\mathbb R^d\). Convex losses have true gradients \(g_t\) with \(\|g_t\|\le G\); feedback \(\widetilde g_t\) may be corrupted. A supplied budget \(k\) satisfies the two conditions on pp. 2–3: \[|\{t:\|g_t-\widetilde g_t\|\ge G\}|\le k,\qquad
 \sum_t\min(\|g_t-\widetilde g_t\|,G)\le kG .\] The analysis clips feedback to the ball of radius \(h_t\). Write \(C_h\) for that projection. Its regret decomposition includes \[\mathrm{ERROR}=\sum_t\langle g_t-C_{h_t}\widetilde g_t,w_t\rangle,\qquad
 \mathrm{CORRECTION}=\sum_t r_t(w_t).\] The aim is to cancel their difference without bounding the decision domain. Theorem 5.1 treats known \(G\), with \(h_t=G\) and regularizer scale \(c=kG\); Theorem 6.1 treats unknown \(G\) through FILTER and TRACKER. Both regimes know a corruption bound \(k\). The lower bounds support the claimed optimal dependence on comparator size, horizon and corruption.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The known-\(G\) error bound misses a factor of two).  The estimate used for cancellation in §5.1, p. 4, and the proof of Theorem 5.1, pp. 14–15, is not valid under the stated corruption budget.

## Claim, admissible check, and consequence

The proof uses \(\mathrm{ERROR}\le kG\max_t\|w_t\|\) and cancels that coefficient with the regularizer at \(c=kG\). Take \(d=1\), \(T=4\), \(G=k=1\), and one round with \(g_t=1\), \(\widetilde g_t=-1\), \(w_t=M>0\). Put all other gradients and iterates at zero. These are gradients of convex linear losses. There is exactly one large corruption and its capped cost is \(\min(2,1)=1\), so both budget conditions hold. Clipping at one leaves the feedback unchanged. Thus \[\mathrm{ERROR}=2M,\qquad kG\max_t|w_t|=M.\] This is a counterexample to the uniform algebraic estimate, not a claim that this iterate history is generated by Algorithm 2. With the single nonzero iterate, the regularizer contribution is at most \(cM\); the displayed cancellation therefore cannot follow from these estimates alone.

## Verified local repair and counterevidence

Projection onto a ball containing \(g_t\) is nonexpansive and cannot increase its distance from \(g_t\). Both points after projection have norm at most \(G\), so \[\|g_t-C_G\widetilde g_t\|
 \le\min(\|g_t-\widetilde g_t\|,2G)
 \le2\min(\|g_t-\widetilde g_t\|,G).\] The correct general sum bound is \(2kG\), attained by the sign-flip example. Replacing \(c=kG\) by \(c=2kG\) gives, using the paper’s regularizer lower bound with \(\alpha=\epsilon/k\), \[\mathrm{ERROR}-\mathrm{CORRECTION}
 \le2kG\max_t\|w_t\|-2kG(\max_t\|w_t\|-\epsilon/k)
 =2\epsilon G.\] This verifies the local cancellation repair; the comparator bias remains of the same asymptotic order. It does not certify all base-learner and implementation dependencies. A sharper analysis of actual iterates could conceivably justify the original parameters, but none is supplied by this step. Because this is the mechanism of the central known-\(G\) guarantee, the invalid derivation is Major, despite the short repair.

Problem 2 (Lemma H.1 charges clipping-created errors to the corruption budget).  Lemma H.1, p. 23, is false when a clipping threshold is below the true gradient norm.

## Every hypothesis and the exact discrepancy

The lemma assumes the original corruption conditions and \(0<h_t\le\tau\), and asserts \[\sum_t\|g_t-C_{h_t}\widetilde g_t\|\le2k\max(\tau,G).\] Choose \(d=1\), \(T=4\), \(G=k=1\), \(h_t=\tau=1/4\), losses \(\ell_t(w)=w\), and \[(g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4)=(1,1,1,1),\qquad
 (\widetilde g_1,\widetilde g_2,\widetilde g_3,\widetilde g_4)=(1/2,1,1,1).\] The true gradients have norm one, the large-corruption count is zero, and the capped corruption budget is \(1/2\le1\). There is exactly one changed feedback. Every clipped value equals \(1/4\), whence \[\text{left side}=4(1-1/4)=3,\qquad
 \text{asserted upper bound}=2.\] The last line of the proof invokes the budget for \(\|g_t-C_{h_t}\widetilde g_t\|\) although the assumption bounds \(\|g_t-\widetilde g_t\|\). Uncorrupted rounds can acquire clipping error.

## Dependence, special case, and limited correction

Lemma H.2 uses H.1 in the unknown-\(G\) error/bias analysis, including a fixed-threshold comparison; that analysis feeds Theorem 6.1. The failure is consequently Major for the presented core derivation. If \(h_t\ge G\), this particular objection disappears, but that is not H.1’s hypothesis and is unavailable throughout unknown-\(G\) adaptation.

A valid general replacement, for the same \(h_t\le\tau\), is \[\sum_t\|g_t-C_{h_t}\widetilde g_t\|
 \le \sum_t(G-h_t)_+ +2k\max(G,\tau).\] Indeed, insert \(C_{h_t}g_t\) by the triangle inequality. The first distance is at most \((G-h_t)_+\); the second is at most \(\min(\|g_t-\widetilde g_t\|,2h_t)\) by nonexpansiveness and the diameter bound. For \(e\ge0\), \(\min(e,2h_t)\le2\max(1,\tau/G)\min(e,G)\). Summation proves the claim. The new truncation term may grow with \(T\); merely inserting this correction does not restore the advertised final rate.

Problem 3 (The sparse penalty is positive on fewer rounds than its proof assumes).  Equation (11), p. 7, and the OFFSET\(_1\) calculation in Lemma H.2, p. 24, use incompatible supports.

The definition is \(\alpha_t=\gamma_\alpha\mathbf1_{\{h_{t+1}\ne h_t\}}\). The proof instead substitutes \(\alpha_t=\gamma_\alpha\) on every clipped round and applies \[(G+h_t)X-\alpha_tX^2\le \frac{(G+h_t)^2}{4\alpha_t}.\] A clipped round need not increase the threshold. Set \(T=4\), \(k=2\), \(G=1\), \(\tau_G=1/4\), \(\epsilon=1\), and consider a first uncorrupted feedback \(g_1=\widetilde g_1=1\). Initialize the clipping counter to zero and grant the conventional unchanged-threshold fallback omitted from the pseudocode. After this round its counter is one, so neither a trigger at \(k\) nor at \(k+1\) doubles the threshold. Consequently \(\alpha_1=0\). All corruption conditions hold with budget at most two.

Lemma H.2 allows arbitrary iterates bounded by \(\epsilon 2^T/2=8\); for example \(w_1=1\) and the others zero satisfy that condition. Other parameters may be chosen \(c=\gamma_\alpha=\gamma_\beta=\tau_D=1\), \(p=\ln4>1\), and the prescribed \(\alpha=\epsilon\tau_G/c=1/4\). The displayed pointwise argument divides by zero in this admissible case. Its supremum over \(X\ge0\) is infinite when \(\alpha_t=0\). This refutes the proof step, without proving H.2’s final aggregate bound or Algorithm 2’s regret false.

There is also a concrete FILTER discrepancy. Algorithm 3, p. 21, doubles at counter \(n=k\), whereas the proof of Lemma F.2, p. 22, requires \(k+1\) exceedances. For \(G=k=1\), initial threshold \(10\), \(g_1=1\), \(\widetilde g_1=11\), the printed rule gives \(h_2=20\), exceeding the claimed \(\max(10,4G)=10\). The capped corruption cost and large-corruption count are both one. Using \(k+1\) removes this example but does not resolve the penalty-support mismatch.

The sparse correction mechanism is essential to Theorem 6.1, so the demonstrated invalid derivation is Major. Defining \(\alpha_t\) on every clipping event makes the displayed completion of the square valid locally; it also changes the algorithm and requires a fresh count and global regret analysis. No complete repair is certified here.

Problem 4 (The logarithmic lower-bound construction has coupled sign and domain errors).  The comparator in Lemma E.4, p. 19, and its use in Theorem E.5, p. 20, do not give the asserted admissible construction.

For the all-\(e_1\) feedback sequence, the proof sets \(f(x)=\epsilon\exp(x^2/T)\) and prints \(f'(-T)=+2\epsilon e^T\). Direct differentiation gives \(f'(-T)=-2\epsilon e^T\). The always-zero learner satisfies its origin-regret requirement \(R_T(0)=0\le\epsilon\), but for the printed positive comparator \(u=Ue_1\) its regret is \(-TU\), not the positive lower bound obtained in (19).

Correcting the sign alone is insufficient. The next proof defines on corrupted rounds \(g_t=\widetilde g_t-u/\|u\|\). With the corrected \(u=-Ue_1\) and \(\widetilde g_t=e_1\), this is \(2e_1\), violating the explicit unit-gradient condition. With the printed positive comparator the gradient is admissible, but the inherited lower bound is not. These are analytic, source-visible failures in the advertised matching-logarithm argument, hence Major under the core-derivation calibration. The existential theorem can nevertheless be recovered as follows.

## A verified replacement with its quantifiers

Suppose a deterministic learner has origin regret at most \(\epsilon>0\) for every received gradient sequence of norm at most one, as in the uniform premise of (17). Its iterates satisfy \(\|w_t\|\le\epsilon2^{t-1}\). To see this directly, at any prefix choose the next gradient aligned with \(w_t\) and the remaining gradients zero. The origin guarantee implies \(\|w_t\|\le\epsilon-\sum_{s<t}\langle\widetilde g_s,w_s\rangle
 \le\epsilon+\sum_{s<t}\|w_s\|\); induction gives the bound.

Let \(T\ge1\) and \(0\le k\le T\) be integers, \(U=2\epsilon e^T\), \(u=-Ue_1\), true gradients \(g_t=e_1\) always, and feedback zero on any exactly \(k\) rounds and \(e_1\) on the others. Both gradient sequences have norm at most one and there are exactly \(k\) corruptions. Then \[R_T(u)\ge UT-\epsilon(2^T-1)\ge UT/2.\] The second inequality follows from \(2^T-1\le Te^T\). Also, \[\log(1+U^2T/(2\epsilon^2))=\log(1+2Te^{2T})\le4T.\] For \(T\ge1\), \(1+2T\le e^{2T}\) gives the latter bound. Since \(\epsilon\le UT\) and \(k\le T\), \[R_T(u)\ge\frac18\left[
 \epsilon+U\sqrt{T\log(1+U^2T/(2\epsilon^2))}+Uk\right].\] This restores an existential logarithmic-form lower bound for this exponentially large comparator under the stated uniform origin guarantee. It does not prove matching logarithmic dependence at every prescribed comparator magnitude, nor a pathwise result from an expectation-only origin premise.

The coarser Theorem 5.2 also survives the checked construction with explicit random tie-breaking: if \(S\) is the sum of the last \(T-k\) Rademachers, use \(\sigma=\operatorname{sign}(S)\) for \(S\ne0\) and an independent fair sign for \(S=0\). The first \(k\) true gradients are \(\sigma e_1\) with zero feedback; choose \(u=-D\sigma e_1\). Expected regret equals \(D(k+\mathbb E|S|)\). For \(m=T-k>0\), the moments \(\mathbb ES^2=m\), \(\mathbb ES^4=3m^2-2m\) give \(\mathbb E|S|\ge\sqrt{m/3}\) by Hölder; \(m=0\) gives \(Dk\). This verifies the coarse order and handles the tie and endpoint cases.

Problem 5 (The generic scalar inverse and bisection bracket are incorrect).  The paragraph after Algorithm 2, p. 14, gives an algebraically incorrect inverse and an unsupported universal bracket.

For \(p>1\), \(c,S>0\) and \(R(x)=cp\,x^{p-1}/(S+x^p)^{1-1/p}\), set \(q=(y/(cp))^{p/(p-1)}\). Solving gives \[R^{-1}(y)=\left(\frac{Sq}{1-q}\right)^{1/p},
 \qquad 0\le y<cp .\] The paper prints \(1+q\) and claims all \(y\ge0\). For \(S=c=1\), \(p=2\), \(y=1\), the printed \(x=1/\sqrt5\) yields \(R(x)=\sqrt{2/3}\ne1\); the correct \(x=1/\sqrt3\) yields one. No finite inverse exists for \(y\ge cp\).

The asserted generic upper bisection endpoint \(S_t^{1/p}\) also fails for parameters allowed by the generic base result. Take \(\epsilon=1\), \(c=10^{-6}\), \(p=2\), \(\alpha=10^{-12}\), \(h_1=h_2=1\), \(w_1=0\), and first gradient \(-1\). The consistent first-order update has target \(\|\theta\|=1\), \(V_2=2\), \(B_2=32\), and \(a_2=1/(\sqrt{32}\log^2 32)\). On its square-root branch, \[L_2(x)=6\sqrt{2\log(1+x/a_2)}
       +\frac{2c x}{\sqrt{\alpha^2+x^2}}.\] At the advertised upper endpoint \(\alpha\), this is \(0.0000713580111<1\); independent bisection gives the root \(0.0002058350167\). These calculations concern the consistent first-order equation, not an exploitation of the pseudocode’s separate \(\theta\) index slip.

A valid local bracket follows from \(L=\Psi'+R\ge\Psi'\): use \([0,x_\Psi]\) where \[x_\Psi=
 \begin{cases}
 a\{\exp(\theta^2/(36V))-1\},&\theta\le6V/h,\\
 a\{\exp(\theta/(3h)-V/h^2)-1\},&\theta>6V/h .
 \end{cases}\] Then \(L(0)=0\) and \(L(x_\Psi)\ge\theta\); continuity and monotonicity permit bisection. This verifies the local solver repair. Failure under the main theorem’s coupled \(c=kG,\alpha=\epsilon/k\) was not established, so this generic implementation/domain defect is Minor, separately from the Major cancellations above.

# Applications, surviving scope, and conclusion

Corollary D.1, p. 15, invokes a pathwise gradient-bound theorem from only \(\mathbb E_t\|g_t\|\le G\). Those hypotheses differ. For \(G=1\), let \(g=3\) with probability \(1/5\) and \(g=-3/10\) with probability \(4/5\). Then \(\mathbb E|g|=21/25\), \(\mathbb Eg=9/25\), and \(\mathbb EC_1g=-1/25\): clipping can reverse the mean without corruption. This establishes that the cited premise is not automatic, but no complete actual-algorithm stochastic counterexample is claimed. Strengthening to an almost-sure bound repairs this invocation conditional on a repaired known-\(G\) theorem. The weighted-gradient invocation in Corollary D.2, p. 16, similarly needs a separate argument when importance weights exceed one; its final validity remains Needs Evidence.

The findings do not negate the goal of robust unconstrained optimization, and the supplied lower-bound replacement preserves an existential optimality statement. Unknown-\(G\) analysis requires more than a constant adjustment. Figure 1’s illustration was inspected as a disclosed protocol, without rerunning simulations; no empirical failure is inferred from proof defects.
