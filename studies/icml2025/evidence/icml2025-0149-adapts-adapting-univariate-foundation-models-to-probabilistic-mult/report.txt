Abstract: The stated optimal linear-adapter theorem is false under its own invertibility assumption: its proposed solution is singular for every multivariate instance. A feasible exact counterexample, a nonattainment example, and a curvature calculation establish the defect independently of experimental reproduction. This is a Major issue in a theoretical contribution explicitly used to motivate the method. A tabulated MAE is also incompatible with the corresponding MSE. Static checks identify two further issues in the acquired released software, concerning decoded predictive summaries and cross-validation training labels; their effect on the historical experiments is unresolved. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Problems supported by the inspected evidence

Problem 1 (Major: the optimal linear-adapter theorem returns an inadmissible matrix).  Proposition 3.3, Eq. (4), and its proof in Appendix A.1 claim an optimizer over invertible adapters but produce a singular matrix whenever \(D>1\). The ridge expression in Remark 3.4 does not remove this obstruction.

## Claim and complete assumptions

Equation reference: eq:loss, eq:claimed. Section 3 assumes \(W=W_\phi\in {\rm GL}_D(\mathbb R)\) and a shared univariate affine foundation model \[f_{\rm FM}(X)=W_{\rm FM}^{\mathsf T}X+b\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.\] No nonzero-rank assumption on \(W_{\rm FM}\), no restriction on \(Y\), and no nonorthogonality requirement involving \(b\) and \(Y-W_{\rm FM}^{\mathsf T}X\) are stated. Put \[A=Y-W_{\rm FM}^{\mathsf T}X,\qquad B=b\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.\] The loss reduces exactly to \[L(W)=\|A-BW^{-1}\|_F^2.
 \] The paper proposes \[W_*=(B^{\mathsf T}A)^+B^{\mathsf T}B.
 \] Appendix A.1 explicitly writes the optimization domain as \({\rm GL}_D(\mathbb R)\).

## Rank obstruction and explicit counterexample

Because \(B=b\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), its rank is at most one. Consequently \[\operatorname{rank}(W_*)\leq
 \operatorname{rank}(B^{\mathsf T}B)\leq1.\] For every \(D>1\), Eq. [eq:claimed] (#eq:claimed) is outside the optimization domain; the inverse required by Eq. [eq:loss] (#eq:loss) is undefined. This is an exact structural obstruction, not a numerical conditioning issue. Whenever the left inverse in the proposed ridge modification exists, \[W_\lambda=(B^{\mathsf T}A+\lambda I)^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}B\] also has rank at most one.

For a fully specified admissible instance, take \(L=H=1\), \(D=2\), \(X=(0,0)\), \(W_{\rm FM}=0\), \(b=1\), and \(Y=(1,2)\). These satisfy the paper’s model assumptions. Then \(A=(1,2)\), \(B=(1,1)\), and independent exact arithmetic gives \[W_*=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&1/5\\2/5&2/5\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 W_{\lambda=1}=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\1/4&1/4\end{pmatrix}.\] Both determinants are zero. In contrast, \[W_{\rm feasible}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1/2)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 BW_{\rm feasible}^{-1}=(1,2)=A,\qquad L(W_{\rm feasible})=0,\] while \(L(I)=1\). Thus an invertible optimum exists in this example, but the reported formula does not produce it.

## Existence and convexity also require correction

The theorem does not always have any attained minimum. With the same dimensions, \(A=0\), and \(B=(1,1)\), every invertible \(W\) gives \(BW^{-1}\ne0\), hence \(L(W)>0\). The sequence \(W=tI\), \(t\to\infty\), has \(L(W)=2/t^2\to0\). The infimum is zero and is not attained.

The final paragraph of Appendix A.1 asserts convexity in \(W\), citing the inverse operation. Even on a convex positive scalar domain this is false. For \(A=B=1\) and \(w>0\), \[f(w)=(1-w^{-1})^2,\qquad
 f''(w)=\frac{6-4w}{w^4},\qquad f''(2)=-\frac18.\] Equivalently, \(f(2)=1/4>53/225=[f(3/2)+f(5/2)]/2\). The loss is a quadratic in \(M=W^{-1}\), but this does not make it convex in \(W\), and invertibility still restricts the admissible \(M\).

## Diagnosis, correction, and counterevidence

The stationary equation \(B^{\mathsf T}AW=B^{\mathsf T}B\) does not justify discarding its null-space freedom and selecting a singular minimum-norm matrix. The omitted freedom is precisely what can make a solution invertible. Remark A.4 proposes channel-dependent biases to obtain full-rank \(B\). This changes the shared-univariate-model assumption and therefore does not establish Proposition 3.3 as stated. Full-rank \(B\) by itself also does not guarantee a full-rank \(B^{\mathsf T}A\).

A corrected characterization for the stated shared-bias case is simple. If \(b\ne0\), write \(q^{\mathsf T}=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}W^{-1}\), which must be nonzero. Every nonzero row \(q^{\mathsf T}\) can be realized by some invertible \(W\), by extending nonzero row vectors to bases. The unconstrained least-squares row is \[q_*^{\mathsf T}=\frac{b^{\mathsf T}A}{b^{\mathsf T}b}.\] If \(q_*\ne0\), any invertible completion satisfying \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}W^{-1}=q_*^{\mathsf T}\) attains the minimum. If \(q_*=0\), the infimum is not attained. If \(b=0\), the loss is constant on all invertible \(W\). This characterization is an analytic correction, not an author-adopted repair.

The paper explicitly identifies theoretical foundations as a contribution on page 2, calls Proposition 3.3 its foundational motivation on page 3, and claims to evaluate Eq. (4) in Figure 2 on page 4. The demonstrated invalid derivation therefore affects a core theoretical contribution and its synthetic optimality interpretation. This supports Major. It does not establish that the separately trained encoder–decoder method fails or that any real-data score is wrong. The useful existence of better adapters in some cases is compatible with this counterexample; the theorem and its advertised optimizer are nevertheless invalid. Historical Figure 2 code and outputs would be needed to determine how an inverse of the displayed singular expression was handled.

Problem 2 (Minor: incompatible MSE and MAE entries).  The Illness, \(H=24\), LinearAE entry is \(2.624\pm0.035\) in both Table 1 (MSE, page 6) and Table 6 (MAE, page 18). These cannot be the stated metrics of the same residual arrays with the same normalization and run averaging.

For each run \(r\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \[{\rm MAE}_r=\frac1n\sum_i|e_{ri}|
 \leq\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_i e_{ri}^2}
 =\sqrt{{\rm MSE}_r}.\] Averaging over the three runs and applying concavity of the square root yields \[\overline{\rm MAE}\leq\sqrt{\overline{\rm MSE}}
 =\sqrt{2.624}\simeq1.61988,\] far below the reported \(2.624\). Rounding cannot bridge the gap: even the upper rounded MSE \(2.6245\) permits only \(1.62004\), whereas the lower rounded MAE is \(2.6235\). Both entries were visually verified in the original PDF. The identical mean and standard error suggest a copied cell, but that cause is not established.

The released `compute_metrics` applies MSE and L1 loss to the same targets and predictions, consistent with the conventional interpretation, but its later provenance does not identify the historical table-generation process. Different runs or scales would require explicit relabeling and would defeat the current like-for-like presentation. The correct MAE cannot be reconstructed from MSE alone and must be recomputed from original predictions. This is a localized reporting issue; it does not by itself overturn the main MSE comparisons. Separately, the page-6 ETTh1 improvement from \(0.411\) to \(0.395\) is \(3.89\%\), not the stated \(8\%\); this arithmetic discrepancy likewise does not reverse the ranking.

Problem 3 (Minor, released implementation: predictive summaries are transformed incorrectly).  The acquired `ADAPTS.predict_multi_step` summarizes latent predictions before applying the decoder, although a nonlinear or sign-mixing decoder does not preserve those summaries. The effect on the proceedings experiments remains Needs Evidence.

In `src/adapts/adapts.py`, lines 217–254, the program obtains latent sample means and standard deviations, then applies `inverse_transform` to the mean and to componentwise mean-plus/minus-standard-deviation vectors. With decoder \(g\) and latent forecast \(U\), it therefore returns \(g(\widehat{\mathbb E}U)\), whereas the Monte Carlo predictive mean is \(\widehat{\mathbb E}[g(U)]\). These differ for nonlinear \(g\). For equal samples \(U=0,2\) and \(g(u)=\tanh u\), independent arithmetic gives \[g(\bar U)=0.761594,\qquad
 \frac{g(0)+g(2)}2=0.482014.\] The released VAE decoder contains nonlinear LeakyReLU and Tanh layers, so the required affine identity is not imposed.

Even an affine decoder can invalidate the returned bounds. For \(g(u)=-u\), latent mean zero and standard deviation one, the returned lower value is \(g(-1)=1\), and the upper value is \(g(1)=-1\). A negative decoder weight is permitted by the released linear decoder classes. More generally, a multivariate linear decoder requires propagation of the covariance, not simultaneous transformation of componentwise endpoints.

The proper computation is to decode each joint sample with consistent decoder and normalization state, then calculate the desired means, standard deviations, or quantiles in the original space. The supplied `compute_ks_metric` already decodes individual draws before its calibration calculation; this is material counterevidence against claiming that the identified summary-order defect automatically invalidates every ECE value. No historical plot or score was reproduced. The established result concerns the current returned summaries and their interpretation, not a measured loss of the paper’s empirical advantage.

Problem 4 (Minor, released implementation: outer validation labels enter Moment head fitting).  The current hyperparameter-search script constructs outer folds but fits the Moment forecasting head on the complete pooled data, including the current outer validation fold.

In `scripts/hyperopt.py`, lines 145–148 combine the original training and validation arrays. Lines 290–316 construct fold-specific train and validation subsets. However, lines 334–345 call `fine_tune_iclearner` with `X=X_train` and `y=y_train`, not the fold-training arrays. The subsequent adapter fit does use the fold subsets. The forecast model and learner are created before the fold loop; only the adapter is re-created inside it.

This is supervised label access, not merely fitting a feature scaler on contexts. The Moment wrapper creates a tensor dataset from every supplied pair and, without explicit validation arguments, randomly splits that entire dataset 90/10 before optimizing the unfrozen forecasting head. For a concrete 30-example, three-fold case, each outer validation set has ten elements and the inner head-training set has 27. Their intersection must contain at least \(27+10-30=7\) elements, regardless of the random split. Thus at least 70% of that outer validation set’s labels enter supervised head training. The independent check verifies this set bound; no training was executed.

This defeats the interpretation of the resulting fold metric as validation of the entire fitted forecasting pipeline. It does not prove that test labels were trained on: the separate test arrays remain outside this pool, and the inspected search objective selects by validation MSE, not the saved test score. The corrected procedure must reinitialize the trainable head and adapter for each outer fold and confine all supervised fitting to the outer training portion, with a suitable inner validation split. The released snapshot demonstrates the protocol defect; experiment-time configurations and logs are needed to determine whether it affected the paper’s selected hyperparameters or final scores.

# Other checks and unresolved claims

The adapter definition initially requires an inverse, while Section 2 explicitly relaxes that requirement to separately trained encoders and decoders. Consequently a noninvertible practical autoencoder alone is not an additional contradiction. It also does not repair the invertible linear theorem.

The Jensen steps in Proposition 4.1 and Appendix A.2 give a valid ELBO for the written latent-variable likelihood when the density/support assumptions hold. The paper separately identifies the tunable \(\beta\)-weighted objective; this was not confused with an unmodified ELBO. However, with the concrete decoder mean \(\operatorname{dec}_\theta(f_{\rm FM}(Z))\) and an unconditional prior \(p(Z)\), the displayed generative marginal has no explicit \(X\) dependence beyond any dependence separately retained in the likelihood. Predictions sampled using \(q_\phi(Z\mid X)\) need a careful distinction from that prior-based marginal. The paper’s appeal to universal conditional-density results likewise requires conditions on the fixed intervening foundation model. A constant fixed model can erase encoder stochasticity. No full audit of the cited external theorem or a quantified consequence for the trained models was performed; these interpretations remain Needs Evidence, not an additional established Major finding.

The main forecasting tables explicitly include settings in which adapters match or worsen the baseline. Table 2 labels results on additional foundation models as preliminary. Three runs and reported standard errors do not by themselves establish significance across tasks. The review checked the reported arithmetic and identified the impossible MAE cell; it did not independently reconstruct datasets, training, forecasts, or uncertainty calibration.

The current forecasting data reader fits its initial StandardScaler on the training partition. This is counterevidence against inferring target leakage solely from the prose order of preprocessing and splitting in Appendix C. The `ADAPTS.transform` path refits its scaler on supplied forecast contexts, which is a distinct transductive preprocessing choice requiring experimental protocol clarity. It is not by itself proof that future targets were supplied to that scaler.

# Initial assessment

The paper has a Major issue in Proposition 3.3 and its advertised theoretical optimality argument. The remaining supported problems have the narrower scopes stated above. No conclusion is drawn that the complete practical approach fails, that all published numerical results are invalid, or that the authors intended to mislead. Correction of the theoretical statement and clarification or repair of the affected reporting and released protocols are required.
