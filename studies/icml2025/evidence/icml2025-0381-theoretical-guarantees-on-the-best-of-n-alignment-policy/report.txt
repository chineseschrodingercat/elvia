# Definitions and surviving checks

Fix one prompt, finite support with distinct rewards, and ordered masses \(p_i>0\). Set \(F_i=\sum_{j\le i}p_j\). Lemma 2.3 gives \(q_i=F_i^n-F_{i-1}^n\), by the distribution of the maximum of independent uniform variables. Write \(S=\sum_i p_i^2=e^{-H_2(p)}\) and \(B_n=\log n-(n-1)/n\). Theorem 3.1 follows from the integral log-sum inequality on each interval \([F_{i-1},F_i]\) for density \(nv^{n-1}\), followed by integration over \([0,1]\). Its gap is a sum of nonnegative conditional KL divergences (Lemma A.1, p. 12). The two cases in Lemma A.3 yield the collision upper bound for \(n\ge2\); \(n=1\) is identically zero.

The win rate equals \(\sum_iq_i(F_i+F_{i-1})/2\); the interval inequality in Lemma A.4 gives \(n/(n+1)\) as its upper bound. For rewind-and-repeat with \(0<w\le1\), \(\mathbb E H_{M-1}=\sum_{k\ge1}(1-w)^k/k=-\log w\). These arguments do not use the defective steps below.

# Problems

Problem 1 (Theorem A.7 does not define the claimed universal upper bound).  Theorem A.7 (physical p. 19) claims an upper-bound function for any reference distribution, parameterized for \(\tau\ge1\) by \[W_U(\tau)=\frac{\tau}{\tau+1},\qquad
 D_U(\tau)=\log\tau-\frac{\tau-1}{\tau}-2\tau(\tau-1)S.\] Definition 7.1 (p. 8) requires a function of nonnegative KL that is evaluable at every actual \(D_n\).

Admissible example. Use one prompt, two outcomes with rewards \(0<1\) and \(p=(1/2,1/2)\). Finite support and unique rewards satisfy Assumptions 2.1–2.2, and \(S=1/2\). At \(n=2\), \(q=(1/4,3/4)\), hence \[D_2=\tfrac14\log(1/2)+\tfrac34\log(3/2)=0.1308120359>0,\qquad
 W_2=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac14+\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac34=\tfrac58.\] Yet \(D_U(1)=0\), and for every \(\tau>1\), \[D_U(\tau)\le(\tau-1)\left(1-\frac1\tau-\tau\right)<0,\] using \(\log\tau\le\tau-1\). No parameter supplies the positive abscissa \(D_2\). In particular, \(D_U(2)=-1.8068528194\). ◻

The suspect step is converting two sample-count bounds to a function of KL without establishing an invertible domain or completing the range. This is a failure of the specified universal function, not a counterexample to Theorems 3.4 or 5.3. More generally \(D_U'(1)=-2S<0\) and \(D_U(\tau)\to-\infty\) for any finite distribution.

An interpretation on a suitable increasing branch for sufficiently small \(S\) would change the statement; the binary example has no positive branch. Defining a trivial value \(1\) outside a justified domain also requires a new statement. Figures 8–9 use a conjectured bound, distinct from A.7. They neither validate A.7 nor become invalid from this example. This is Major for the general theoretical tradeoff guarantee advertised in Section 7; the foundational KL and win-rate inequalities survive. No global repair is asserted.

Problem 2 (The precision dependence of averaging is incorrect).  The paragraph after Lemma 4.5 (p. 5, left column) claims that \(M=O(\log n\,\log(1/\delta))\) independent estimator draws suffice to make the mean’s standard deviation smaller than \(\delta\).

Set \(n=2\), rewards \(0<1\), and \(p=(0.9,0.1)\), satisfying finite support and uniqueness. Selected masses are \(0.9,0.1\) with probabilities \(0.81,0.19\). Substitution in equation (24) gives \[d_2(0.9)=0.07326269888,\qquad d_2(0.1)=0.19305943694,\] and therefore \[\sigma^2=0.81(0.19)\{d_2(0.9)-d_2(0.1)\}^2
 =0.002208658675>0.\] The mean’s standard deviation is exactly \(\sigma/\sqrt M\). At fixed \(n\), no constant multiple of \(\log(1/\delta)\) suffices as \(\delta\downarrow0\). At \(\delta=0.001\) at least \(2209\) draws are needed.

A supported correction follows from \(0\le d_n\le B_n\): \(\operatorname{Var}(d_n)\le B_n^2/4\), so \(M\ge B_n^2/(4\delta^2)\) ensures standard deviation at most \(\delta\); strict inequality gives a strict bound. This is not a bias or tail-probability guarantee. Reading \(\delta\) as a failure probability does not rescue the printed standard-deviation claim. Uniform reference distributions have zero variance, but the explicit nonuniform example avoids that exception. The impact is Minor: an ancillary precision claim fails, while the estimator definition, boundedness and conjectured expectation inequality remain separate.
