Abstract: The unconstrained transport reweighting derivation is valid under its finite-support assumptions. Two defects are established: the practical algorithm’s multiplicative mask can repeatedly select an already capped item for admissible cosine costs, and one natural-number statement labeled correct in the manual dataset audit has an elementary counterexample. Neither finding establishes changed theorem-proving benchmark scores. The overall assessment is Major because the practical masking rule can defeat the central diversity-preserving reweighting mechanism on admissible inputs. The manual-audit error remains Minor, and historical run and proof-artifact verification remain unresolved. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Problem 1: a zero mask does not exclude a conjecture for signed cosine costs

Assessment: Major; failure of the core reweighting mechanism.

#### Claim, assumptions, and location.

Appendix A.5, pp. 16–18, defines matching cost as negative cosine similarity. The discussion after the unconstrained solution says that the practical method limits overly large conjecture weights to avoid unstable weighted training. Algorithm 4, p. 18, line 6 selects the smallest values of \(d(x,y)M(x)\); lines 9–10 set \(M(x)=0\) when \(nP(x)>3\). The source page was rendered and inspected. No restriction to nonnegative cosine similarities is stated.

#### Complete admissible construction.

Let \(n=m=8\), with matching weight one for every target statement. In \(\mathbb R^2\), give conjecture \(j\) the unit embedding \[x_j=\left(-\frac{j}{10},\sqrt{1-\frac{j^2}{100}}\right),\quad 1\le j\le8,\] and give each of the eight target statement identifiers the unit embedding \(y_i=(1,0)\). The statements may be distinct while their embeddings agree; injectivity is not an assumption of the algorithm. All norms are one and all costs are finite, with \[d(x_j,y_i)=-\langle x_j,y_i\rangle=j/10.\] The weight increment \(n/m\) is exactly one.

Claim 1.  Algorithm 4 returns weights \((8,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)\) on this input, including four assignments to the first conjecture after its mask has become zero.

Proof. Initially the unique minimum cost is \(1/10\), so the first four targets are assigned to \(x_1\). The fourth assignment makes \(nP(x_1)=4>3\) and sets \(M(x_1)=0\). Thereafter its masked cost is zero, whereas all other masked costs remain at least \(2/10\). It is therefore the unique minimum at every remaining step. Its final weight is eight. ◻

This is failure of the masking mechanism, rather than merely a one-increment overshoot caused by the strict threshold. For arbitrary larger \(n=m\), replacing \(j/10\) by \(j/(n+1)\) yields the analogous concentration on one item.

#### Source correspondence and alternatives.

At the pinned commit, `RL/utils/RL_utils.py`, lines 607–638, normalizes embeddings and selects the largest values of `similarity * mask`. It sets the mask to zero once a weight is at least three. The reviewer independently translated this scalar rule; it has the same output on the construction, setting the mask after the third assignment instead of the fourth. The code uses total matching mass rather than \(m\) when target weights differ; that difference disappears in the weight-one example. The source was read, not executed.

If every unmasked candidate has positive cosine similarity, a zero mask ranks below those candidates. This is a valid favorable regime, but it is not a general property of normalized or mean-pooled embeddings. No sign statistics or training artifacts were acquired to determine whether the counterexample’s regime occurred historically. Therefore no benchmark reduction or training instability is claimed.

#### Repair and boundaries.

Use explicit exclusion, such as an infinite minimization cost for ineligible items, rather than multiplying a signed score by zero. If the intended maximum is three, eligibility must also be checked before the increment and sufficient feasible capacity must be ensured. For this example such a rule gives \((3,3,2,0,0,0,0,0)\), which was independently checked. This local repair does not prove that an ordered greedy algorithm solves a globally capacity-constrained transport problem. The paper’s unconstrained transport derivation is a different, valid calculation. Main-text p. 5 identifies reweighting as the mechanism that preserves conjecture diversity and prevents concentration, and Algorithm 2 invokes this practical reweighting routine. The admissible construction defeats that mechanism and can leave all matching mass on one conjecture, continuing to select it after it is marked ineligible. This is a Major defect in a core algorithmic component. Whether historical embeddings entered the failing sign regime remains unresolved; no empirical score reduction is inferred.

# Problem 2: a statement labeled correct in the manual audit is false over \(\mathbb N\)

Assessment: Minor; exact counterexample to an audit label.

#### Claim and location.

Appendix B.3, PDF p. 21, row 10 labels the following Lean statement as correct: \[a,b\in\mathbb N,\quad a\ne b,\quad a+b\mid a^2+b^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad ab+4\le\gcd(a,b)^4.\] The displayed formal statement has no positivity assumptions. The original row and its Yes label were visually inspected.

#### Counterexample and all hypotheses.

Take \(a=0\), \(b=1\), both admitted by Lean’s natural-number type. Then \(a\ne b\), and \(a+b=1\) divides \(a^2+b^2=1\). However \[ab+4=4,\qquad \gcd(0,1)^4=1,\] so the conclusion is false. This exact integer computation is independent of training behavior or a formal prover’s timeout.

#### Counterevidence, impact, and possible repair.

A positive-integer convention in an informal source would exclude this example, but the actual formal statement uses \(\mathbb N\) without \(a>0,b>0\). Adding those assumptions removes this counterexample; the full amended theorem was not proved here and is not certified as a repair. The immediate correction is to change this row’s formal-statement correctness label to No.

Section 4.2, p. 6, reports seven provable statements among the twenty sampled unproved statements. One of those seven is therefore incorrectly included; the reported count requires revision and the other labels require checking before a corrected total is asserted. This strengthens, rather than undermines, the qualitative observation that some benchmark statements are unprovable. It does not change the recorded fraction of statements actually proved by STP.

# Checks that limit the findings

#### Theoretical transport solution.

For a finite nonempty candidate set \(X\), arbitrary finite costs \(d\), and fixed target probabilities \(Q(y)\), allowing the source marginal \(P\) to vary makes the minimization separable by target: \[\min_{\mu\ge0:\ \sum_x\mu(x,y)=Q(y)}
\sum_{x,y}\mu(x,y)d(x,y)
=\sum_y Q(y)\min_x d(x,y).\] The lower bound follows by replacing each \(d(x,y)\) with its minimum; assigning each column’s mass to any minimizer attains it. Thus Appendix A.5’s unconstrained solution and Algorithm 3 are valid, with a tie-breaking convention. Negative cosine need not be a mathematical metric for this finite transport minimization to be valid. Calling it a distance does not create the masking defect.

#### Metric and split distinctions.

The reported 28.5% on LeanWorkbook is explicitly a cumulative training pass rate, not held-out accuracy. The base model’s exposure to miniF2F-valid and ProofNet-valid is disclosed, including for the variant without those sets in subsequent STP training (p. 6, footnote 7). Table 1 explicitly warns that tree-search and whole-proof generation budgets are not fully comparable. These facts prevent treating the corresponding qualifications as hidden leakage or exact compute equivalence. The 2.9 and 2.1 percentage-point gains in Table 3 agree with the stated roughly 2–3 point ablation gain.

#### Verifier and sampling boundaries.

The inspected Lean wrapper distinguishes absence of errors from completion and additionally rejects reported sorries and relevant warnings. This is consistent with filtering completed proofs; it is not an independent kernel audit of historical outputs. The metric code aggregates unique successful statement identifiers across sampling rounds. Benchmark data, generated proofs, checkpoints, and run logs were excluded from or not supplied with the acquired source-only snapshot, so exact pass rates, their uncertainty estimates, and the historical verifier environment remain unverified. This is an evidence limitation, not a demonstrated invalid score.

# Disposition

The highest supported assessment is Major because the practical reweighting mechanism fails on admissible signed costs, directly compromising its stated role in preserving conjecture diversity. The separate manual-audit label remains Minor. Both should be corrected; historical training effects and the central held-out improvement were not reproduced. No prior correction or author adoption was verified.
