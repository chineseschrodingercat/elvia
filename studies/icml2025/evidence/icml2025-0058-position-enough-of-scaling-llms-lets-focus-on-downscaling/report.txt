## 2. Summary and scientific dependence

The paper advances a research position favoring smaller, specialized models. It also presents an explicit sufficient condition for an ensemble of pruned models to improve loss at unchanged modeled compute, and a quantitative relationship between downstream performance and carbon emissions. These technical claims supply substantive support for the position.

Two derivations fail even when their surrounding fitted-law assumptions are granted. First, Proposition 4.1 deletes a multiplicative pruning-loss factor that does not cancel. A complete finite countermodel satisfies the printed sufficient condition and the stated compute equality, yet has higher ensemble loss. Section 4.5 explicitly relies on this proposition to prescribe the proposed pipeline. Second, Eqs. (8)-(10) discard an affine intercept and reverse an exponent sign. The stated universal carbon cost of a 10% performance increase does not follow.

These are Major findings because they damage central mathematical guarantees or quantitative conclusions. Neither finding requires proving that every small-model ensemble performs poorly, that the environmental motivation is false, or that the whole downscaling agenda should be rejected. The conditional fixed-hardware carbon proportionality and the elementary ensemble reparameterization have useful valid content, discussed below.

## 3. Definitions and operative assumptions

Let N0 be the pre-pruning model size, D the post-training token count, rho the removed fraction, L0 the original loss, and n the number of equal-size pruned models. The derivation uses rho = (n-1)/n, so n must be at least 2 to avoid the singular no-pruning endpoint. Assume positive N0, D and b, and a common loss scale.

Define the factor retained in Eq. (11):

`Q = NC/N0^alpha + DC/D^beta + E.`

The two fitted relations used by Appendix E are:

- Pruned-model loss: `Lc = L0 + Q/(rho^gamma N0^delta).`

- Ensemble loss: `Le = Lc - b + b/n^a.`

The review grants these relations exactly for the algebraic countermodel. It does not claim that arbitrary coefficients describe a measured LLM. The paper additionally assumes Q is approximately constant at matched budget; the countermodel makes Q exactly constant. Equal modeled compute follows from `n K (1-rho) N0 D = K N0 D`, with fixed positive K. This does not by itself establish equal latency, energy, or orchestration overhead.

For the second finding, write carbon emissions as Ccarb to avoid confusing them with the paper’s computational budget C. The review grants its fixed-data, fixed-hardware simplification `Ccarb = Kc L^(-1/alpha)`, with Kc positive and alpha = 0.08, and its affine performance model `P = w1 + w2 L`.

## 4. Problem 1: the sufficient downscaling condition drops a decisive factor

Judgment: Major, affecting Proposition 4.1 and the pipeline’s claimed guarantee.

### Source claim and invalid step

Section 4.4, p. 7, presents Eq. (13) as a sufficient condition for strictly lower ensemble loss at the same compute. Appendix E, pp. 17-18, begins with the complete pruning law in Eq. (27), then replaces Q by 1 in Eq. (28). The explanation says Q is approximately constant and therefore cancels from both sides of the comparison. Section 4.5, p. 8, expressly directs pruning to obey this condition and uses it to assert better performance at equal cost [E1].

Substituting the two full fitted laws actually gives:

`Le - L0 = Q/(rho^gamma N0^delta) - b(1 - n^(-a)).`

Only L0 cancels. Q multiplies the pruning penalty and has no identical additive counterpart on the right-hand side. Its being constant does not make its magnitude irrelevant.

Let `g(n) = (1 - n^(-a)) ((n-1)/n)^gamma`. The printed condition is `g(n) >= 1/(b N0^delta)`. Under the assumed laws, the correct non-worse condition is:

`g(n) >= Q/(b N0^delta).`

Strict improvement requires a strict inequality. The printed non-strict antecedent cannot guarantee strict improvement even in the special case Q = 1.

### Complete countermodel

Choose the following exact values:

Quantity | Value

n, N0, D, L0 | 2, 8, 1, 2

a, b, gamma, delta, alpha, beta | all 1

NC, DC, E | 8, 2, 2

rho | 1/2

Q | 8/8 + 2/1 + 2 = 5

Every size and token-count variable is positive; n is a positive integer greater than 1; the pruning fraction lies strictly between 0 and 1; all fitting coefficients here are positive; and Q is exactly fixed. The example is an algebraic countermodel to the claimed implication of the assumed laws, not a purported empirical eight-parameter language model.

The printed condition passes strictly:

`g(2) = (1 - 1/2)(1/2) = 1/4 > 1/8.`

But the unabridged relations give:

`Lc = 2 + 5/[(1/2) 8] = 13/4,`

`Le = 13/4 - 1 + 1/2 = 11/4 > L0 = 2.`

The modeled compute is unchanged: the two retained-size-4 models each use D = 1, giving total cost 8K, equal to the base model’s 8K. Thus neither a violated budget nor a boundary equality explains the failure. The correct condition fails, as it should: 1/4 is less than 5/8. Exact rational evaluation is preserved with the independent mathematical audit [E5].

### Alternatives, counterevidence and repair

One could introduce an explicit normalization Q = 1, or redefine the ensemble coefficient after rescaling the loss. Neither follows from Q being approximately constant; either changes the hypotheses or coefficients. Similarly, allowing approximation error does not establish a robust sufficient condition without a bound on the omitted factor and on the two fitted laws.

The p. 7 LLaMA illustration also uses coefficients transferred across settings. Upstream Chen arXiv:2411.10272v2 prints gamma = +1.08 in the relevant fitted table, while v3 changes it to -1.08. The target’s positive sign can therefore be faithful to the earlier cited version and is not classified as an independent misquotation. The retained evidence preserves this history; the demonstrated factor omission is independent of it. No claim that the printed threshold is the smallest possible n is needed.

A precise conditional repair is to retain Q, use the corrected threshold above, and distinguish non-worse from strictly better loss. This repairs the algebra exactly. It does not validate transferring CNN ensemble coefficients to domain-specialized, pruned LLMs. Such transfer requires a consistent loss, units, pruning-law version, ensemble diversity assumptions and suitable measurements. If an 8B model is divided under this compute model, each member has size 8B/n; only n = 8 gives 1B members at the same total size.

## 5. Problem 2: the carbon-performance conversion is algebraically invalid

Judgment: Major, affecting the claimed quantitative environmental-performance relationship.

### Source claim and exact derivation

On p. 4, Eq. (7) relates carbon to loss with exponent -1/alpha. Eq. (8) states `P = w1 + w2 L`. Eq. (9) then uses carbon proportional to `P^(-1/alpha)`, and Eq. (10) changes to performance proportional to carbon raised to positive alpha. The text derives a fixed carbon multiplier for a 10% performance gain [E1].

Even granting Eq. (7), correct substitution gives:

`Ccarb = Kc ((P - w1)/w2)^(-1/alpha),`

on the domain `(P - w1)/w2 > 0`, or equivalently:

`P = w1 + w2 (Ccarb/Kc)^(-alpha).`

The additive intercept cannot be absorbed into a proportionality constant. Moreover, even if w1 were zero, inverting Eq. (9) gives a negative exponent, not the positive exponent in Eq. (10). Redefining performance as inverse loss would be a different premise, inconsistent with the displayed affine model.

The cited Chen et al. primary source fits an affine loss-to-performance relation to emerged, above-random observations. It neither sets the intercept to zero nor establishes a universal positive-exponent carbon law. Its current v2 also explicitly discusses limits of composing compute and performance fits across different training trajectories [E6, p. 3, Sections 2.2-2.3].

### Complete numerical check

Choose alpha = 0.08, w1 = 1 and w2 = -1/2. Let performance increase from 0.50 to 0.55, exactly 10% relative. The corresponding positive losses are L1 = 1 and L2 = 0.9. Both performance values lie in [0,1], and the sign expresses the intended improvement as loss decreases.

These values can be realized within the paper’s assumed scalar laws by taking a positive reference size Nr, setting `L = (N/Nr)^(-0.08)`, holding D and hardware fixed, and taking carbon proportional to N. Thus the witness satisfies the premises used in the carbon substitution.

Calculation | Carbon ratio C2/C1

Correct consequence of Eqs. (7)-(8): 0.9^(-12.5) | 3.732232

Printed Eq. (9): 1.1^(-12.5) | 0.303803

Printed Eq. (10): 1.1^(12.5) | 3.291611

The three outcomes disagree. This is a check of the claimed implication, not a new measured carbon estimate. More generally, the ratio depends on the intercept and starting performance; there is no task-independent 10% rule from the stated premises. The reviewer-authored scalar calculation and output are preserved [E7].

The displayed arithmetic also confuses percentage of baseline with percentage increase: 3.291611 times the baseline is 329.161% of the baseline, or 229.161% more. This local arithmetic issue is subordinate to the invalid model conversion. A fixed power law is also not an exponential law in performance.

### Impact and precise repair

This removes the stated quantitative carbon-versus-performance conclusion used in the paper’s environmental argument. It does not establish that scaling is environmentally benign. Retaining the affine offset and exponent gives the valid conditional relation above; estimating a practical multiplier additionally requires task-specific coefficients, an operating point, the scaling trajectory for N and D, and appropriate hardware assumptions. No corrected universal percentage is justified.

## 6. Other checked claims and evidence limits

Conditional carbon proportionality. Given the paper’s fixed hardware, power, efficiency, lifetime and data-center coefficients, substituting runtime proportional to ND into its emission formulas does yield carbon proportional to ND. This is a conditional algebraic result. Changes in those coefficients or in the scaling path require separate treatment. The proof does not make K1 + K2 universal across deployments.

What Figures 2-3 show. Static inspection of `analysis.ipynb` identifies a single GPT3 database row in zero-based cell 11. Cells 18-19 vary N or D while reusing its hardware settings, append predicted emissions, and cells 20-21 plot the resulting curves. `model.py`, lines 68-117, computes FLOPs from 6ND and carbon from power times modeled runtime. Cells 14-17 similarly evaluate analytic loss bounds. These are model-generated curves; the figure labels themselves say estimated. A cached single-row residual is not independent validation of the across-size scaling relationship. The p. 4 statement of empirical validation therefore remains unsupported by an independent scaling experiment in the inspected release [E3]. This qualification does not invalidate the conditional algebra.

Ensemble reparameterization. Appendix D correctly derives Eq. (12) from L = c + b/n^a by setting the single-member loss to c + b, provided the member family and fitted coefficients remain fixed. That valid rearrangement does not justify the subsequent deletion of Q or guarantee transfer to heterogeneous domain specialists.

Loss-floor interpretation. A finite-scale lower bound depending on N and D is not a positive bound uniform in both variables. In the displayed Kaplan formula with positive exponents, both terms tend to zero when N and D grow together. Chinchilla’s explicit positive E can supply a floor. The p. 4 statement should keep these cases distinct; the review does not infer that real-world cross-entropy can be driven to zero.

The recommendations for data curation, pruning and domain specialization were read as research proposals. Their merits are not rejected for lacking a new benchmark experiment. Conversely, the empirical applicability of the proposed composed law remains unverified. The review did not reproduce training, inference, carbon measurements, the complete cited literature or every historical source revision.
