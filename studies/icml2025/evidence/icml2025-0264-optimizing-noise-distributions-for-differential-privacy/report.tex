Abstract: The fixed geometric-tail family need not use the entire permitted cost budget at an optimum. Exact counterexamples show that enforcing cost equality excludes strictly better feasible distributions, invalidating the central optimization guarantee. The finite divergence formula also double-counts terms for an allowed large-shift regime. A pseudocode line-search defect and bounded-support release samplers are separate local findings. Convexity at fixed order, symmetrization, variance calculations and the grid-alignment argument survive the inspected checks.\par

For order \(\alpha>1\), the objective before the logarithm is \[g_\alpha(P,t)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}P(j+t)^\alpha P(j)^{1-\alpha},
\qquad g_\alpha(P)=\max_{|t|\le s}g_\alpha(P,t).\] The continuous version replaces the sum by an integral. Minimizing \(g_\alpha\) is equivalent to minimizing RDP at fixed order. The paper’s optimization (11), and finite version (22), impose \(\mathbb E[c(Z)]\le C\). Its symmetric discrete family has probabilities \(p_i\) for \(0\le i\le N\) and \(p_Nr^{|i|-N}\) in the tails, with \(0<r<1\). All counterexamples below have strictly positive mass, finite variance and finite RDP.\par
\# The budget constraint is not necessarily active\par
Problem 1 (Enforcing cost equality excludes better feasible noise: Major).  Page 7 asserts that the cost constraint is active and places both cost and normalization in \(Ap=b\). Algorithm 3, p. 6, projects every update into this equality space while claiming to solve the inequality-constrained convex problem (22).\par
Equation reference: eq:gsmall. Take the discrete family with \(N=1\), \(r=1/2\), \(s=1\), \(\alpha=2\) and quadratic budget \(C=\sigma^2=5\). Write \(a=p_0\), \(b=p_1\). Normalization and variance are \[a+4b=1,\qquad \mathbb E[Z^2]=24b.\] Direct summation over the center and two geometric tails yields \[
g_2(a,b)=\frac92b+\frac{a^2}{b}+\frac{b^2}{a}.\] The negative shift has the same value by symmetry, and shift zero gives one, so this is the worst-shift objective.\par
Cost equality forces \((a,b)=(1/6,5/24)\) and gives \(g_2=213/160=1.33125\). The legal alternative \((a,b)=(1/5,1/5)\) has cost \(24/5=4.8<5\) and \(g_2=13/10=1.3\). Both are normalized, positive on every integer, symmetric and in exactly the same fixed family. Their order-two RDP values are \(\log(213/160)=0.2861183505\) and \(\log(13/10)=0.2623642645\). The improvement does not use a different tail parameter, a truncated distribution or an infeasible utility budget.\par
This is also not an artifact of an unreachable variance target: Algorithm 2’s Gaussian-parameter bracket has limiting variance zero at its lower end and variance approximately \(5.24620237>5\) at its upper end \(\widehat C=10\). The decisive result concerns the constrained optimization, not a claimed executed return value. For \(N=1\), two equalities leave zero tangent dimension and the pseudocode also needs a zero-gradient stopping convention.\par
\#\# Changing the RDP order does not repair the witness\par
For any finite \(\alpha>1\), put \[A_\alpha=2^\alpha+2^{1-\alpha},\qquad
B_\alpha=(4/5)^\alpha+(4/5)^{1-\alpha}.\] Direct geometric summation gives the difference between the equality-cost distribution’s objective and the alternative’s: \[g_\alpha(1/6,5/24)-g_\alpha(1/5,1/5)
=\frac{A_\alpha}{120}+\frac5{24}B_\alpha-\frac25.\] It is zero at \(\alpha=1\) and strictly increasing for \(\alpha>1\), because both \(A_\alpha\) and \(B_\alpha\) are strictly increasing there. Thus the lower-cost alternative improves every finite-order RDP value, not just the selected order. This compares the RDP objectives; it does not assert a strict improvement of every exact composed DP curve.\par
\#\# A nonzero-dimensional counterexample\par
For \(N=2\), \(r=1/2\), \(s=1\), \(\alpha=2\), write \((a,b,c)=(p_0,p_1,p_2)\) and choose \(C=39/5\). Then \[a+2b+4c=1,\quad \mathbb E[Z^2]=2b+44c,\quad
g_2=\frac92c+\frac{a^2}{b}+\frac{b^2}{a}
                  +\frac{b^2}{c}+\frac{c^2}{b}.\] At cost equality, nonnegativity of \(a\) implies \(b\le4/25\) and hence \(c\ge17/100\). Since \(u^2/v+v^2/u\ge u+v\) for positive \(u,v\), \[g_2\ge a+2b+\frac{11}{2}c
=1+\frac32c\ge\frac{251}{200}.\] Zero masses give infinite divergence and cannot improve this bound. The strictly positive point \(a=b=c=1/7\) is normalized, has cost \(46/7<39/5\), and objective \(17/14<251/200\). Thus every equality-cost point is worse than a legal inequality-feasible point even when the equality manifold has a free dimension.\par
\#\# Dependency and repair boundary\par
This refutes the asserted activity of the constraint and the resulting general solution guarantee for the central finite optimization. Convexity of the original problem does not justify deleting its interior. Adding independent noise can preserve privacy in an unrestricted distribution class, but need not remain in this fixed geometric-tail family. Requiring exactly a given variance is a coherent different problem; Eqs. (11) and (22) explicitly use an upper bound.\par
The released primary notebook corroborates equality preservation: zero-based cell 1, source lines 38–73, builds cost/normalization rows; lines 186–196 project with \(A\operatorname{diag}(p)\). Parent and auxiliary static reads agree. Keeping the inequality in the optimizer, or proving activity under additional valid assumptions, is necessary. No full repaired optimizer was implemented or certified. The published numerical gains at other parameter settings are not refuted.\par
\# A domain failure of the finite divergence formula\par
Problem 2 (Overlapping summation ranges in Theorem 3.6: Major).  Theorem 3.6, p. 5, states Eq. (23) with no restriction relating \(s/\Delta\) to \(N\). Its derivation, p. 16, Eqs. (70)–(75), splits a sum into ranges that overlap when the shift exceeds \(2N+1\).\par
Use the discrete case \(\Delta=1\), \(N=1\), \(r=1/2\), \(p_0=1/2\), \(p_1=1/8\), \(s=t=4\), \(\alpha=2\), and budget \(C=3\). Normalization is \(p_0+4p_1=1\) and variance is \(24p_1=3\), so every stated condition holds. Extend \(p_i\) by the prescribed geometric tail. Exact summation gives \[g_2(P,4)=\frac{42131}{2048}=20.57177734375,\] whereas Eq. (23) gives \[\frac{42259}{2048}=20.63427734375.\] The excess is \(1/16\): the last two ranges both include \(j=-2\), whose term is \(p_2^2/p_2=p_2=1/16\). If the \(p_i\) symbols in (23) are interpreted only as the finite vector, the expression instead refers to undefined \(p_2\); extending them as the proof does still leaves the numerical error.\par
Equation reference: eq:correctsum. A valid finite expression, already available before the faulty split, is \[
g_\alpha(P,t)=\frac{p_Nr}{1-r}
 \left(r^{(1-\alpha)t}+r^{\alpha t}\right)
 +\sum_{j=-N-t}^{N}p_{|j+t|}^{\alpha}p_{|j|}^{1-\alpha},
\quad t\in\mathbb N,\] where all outside indices use the geometric extension. Alternatively, restricting every candidate shift to \(t\le2N+1\) prevents this overlap in the published split. The parent checked the exact rational discrepancy and the corrected partition.\par
This is an invalid derivation of the central objective under its stated parameter domain, supporting Major under the claim-level review standard. The error is bounded to the missing large-shift restriction; the illustrated settings with \(N\gg s/\Delta\) avoid it. The example overestimates divergence, so it does not demonstrate an unsafe understated privacy bound or wrong benchmark ranking.\par
\# Local pseudocode and release findings\par
Problem 3 (Line search keeps the last improvement, not the best: Minor).  Algorithm 3, lines 12–18, compares each candidate to a fixed \(g_{\min}\) initialized before the loop, but never updates \(g_{\min}\). Page 9 says it chooses the largest reduction.\par
An independent evaluation of the stated formulas at the legal iterate \(N=2\), \(r=1/2\), \(s=1\), \(\alpha=2\), \(p=(0.4,0.2,0.05)\), cost \(2.6\), gives initial objective \(1.9375\). Among the eleven listed candidate steps, index 5 gives \(1.9331317453\), while the last index 10 gives \(1.9372084608\). Both improve on the fixed initial value, so the printed loop overwrites the better point with the worse one. This is a conditional legal-iterate calculation, not an executed author-code trajectory. Updating \(g_{\min}\) on acceptance restores the stated selection rule. The released notebook already uses a minimum and argmin over candidate values (cell 1, lines 164–178), so the defect is not attributed to that implementation.\par
Problem 4 (Released example samplers omit the geometric tails: Minor).  The paper requires full-support geometric tails and explains on p. 8 why support mismatch makes finite-order RDP infinite. Released ML helpers sample only a finite array of bins and bounded uniform jitter.\par
In `jax\_optimization.py`, lines 297–299 and 319–335, the bin support is finite and jitter lies in \([-1/(2k),1/(2k)]\). In `rdp\_mnist.py`, lines 105–116 and 383–392, the same bounded construction is used while a saved finite RDP parameter is reported. No geometric-tail branch is present in these inspected paths.\par
A nondegenerate bounded-support additive noise law and any nonzero shift fail mutual absolute continuity: near an essential support endpoint, one shifted law assigns positive mass where the other assigns zero. Thus an order-\(\alpha>1\) divergence in the required neighboring direction is infinite. This exact support argument does not depend on how small the omitted tail mass is. Approximate DP might tolerate a quantified support-loss event, but that requires new accounting.\par
These are released MNIST/coordinate-optimization examples, not established as the programs behind the final paper’s Table 1. No pickle was loaded, no actual saved tail mass was measured and no author code ran. The finding is therefore bounded to the source-level sampling mismatch; published numerical privacy claims are not declared false on this evidence.\par
\# Surviving results and unresolved evidence\par
Theorem 3.1’s fixed-order convexity and reflection argument survive: the power perspective is jointly convex and averaging a distribution with its reflection preserves the symmetric cost bound. Proposition 3.5’s geometric second-moment sum was checked. The continuous objective is piecewise linear in the shift between aligned grid boundaries, so the reduction of the shift maximization to grid points is valid; Problem 2 concerns the subsequent range split.\par
Figures 5 and 7 explicitly plot a pointwise envelope of different optimized distributions. They are not misrepresented here as a single mechanism’s full privacy curve. Table 1 excludes the common privately released quantile-preprocessing cost by explicit design; total end-to-end privacy would need that cost, but the declared comparison is conditional on it. Subgroup sensitivity choices and raw accounting outputs are unavailable for independent numerical validation.\par
Fixed-order convexity does not itself prove global convergence of the joint \((p,\alpha)\) Newton procedure, nor equivalence of the moments-accountant upper-bound optimum to exact composed DP optimality. These broader assurances remain Needs Evidence. Theoretical counterexamples above do not estimate the empirical size of an effect.\par
