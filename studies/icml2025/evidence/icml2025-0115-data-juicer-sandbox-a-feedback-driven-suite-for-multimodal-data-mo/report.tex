Abstract: The small-pool transfer argument contains a demonstrated invalid inference: decreasing variance does not yield the asserted exponential accuracy improvement with sampling rate. This affects the theoretical justification of the central cost–feedback trade-off (Major), while leaving the reported trained models and the usefulness of the software unrefuted. A separate aggregation inconsistency is Minor. Total-cost comparisons and historical experiment provenance remain unresolved.\par
Date: Initial review, 24 October 2026\par
Title: Scientific review of Data-Juicer Sandbox\par

\# Definitions and dependence\par
Equation reference: eq:valid. Write \(X_r=\Delta_{\rm pool}\) for the gain of a probe trained using sampling fraction \(r\), and \(\mu=\mathbb E\Delta_{\rm full}\). Appendix C.1 (pp. 16–17) assumes bounded \(X_r\in[a_r,b_r]\) and, for positive operators, \(\mathbb E X_r\leq\mu\). The displayed one-sided inequality \[\Pr(X_r-\mu\geq\epsilon)
 \leq \Pr(X_r-\mathbb EX_r\geq\epsilon)
 \leq \exp\!\left[-\frac{2\epsilon^2}{(b_r-a_r)^2}\right]
 \] is valid under these assumptions. It need not be an average of independent training runs for this bounded-single-variable inequality to hold. The suspect step is what is inferred about \(r\) and about useful full-scale feedback, not the algebra of [eq:valid] (\#eq:valid).\par
Section 3.2.4 (p. 5) uses this argument to support a cost reduction from \(M T_{\rm full}\) to \((1+mr)T_{\rm full}\), assuming probe cost proportional to \(r\) and informative transfer. The arithmetic cost identity is conditional and unobjectionable; a quantitative accuracy–cost guarantee additionally needs a justified relation between \(r\) and the distribution of \(X_r\).\par
\# Supported problems\par
Problem 1 (Major: variance does not establish the claimed transfer rate).  Claim and location. Section 3.2.4, p. 5, and Appendix C.1, pp. 16–17, conclude that the probability of error exceeding \(\epsilon\) decreases exponentially as \(r\) increases. The final paragraph of C.1 argues that increasing \(r\) reduces variance, that variance is positively related to \((b-a)^2\), and hence that the Hoeffding right-hand side decreases.\par
Decisive check. Even granting decreasing variance, the range need not shrink. Here is an exact finite uniform-subsampling example satisfying all stated probabilistic assumptions. Take a population of \(20\) distinct items, full-data gain identically \(1/2\), and \(\epsilon=1/4\). For a uniformly sampled singleton, let the probe gain be \(2/5\) on ten items and \(3/5\) on ten items. For a uniformly sampled unordered pair, let the gain be \(9/10\) on exactly the pair \(\{0,1\}\) and \(9/20\) on each other pair. These gains specify one training-response function of the selected pool and its size; no stated stability or additivity hypothesis excludes it. All gains are positive and bounded. Direct enumeration gives \[\begin{aligned}
 r_1&=1/20,&\mathbb E X_{r_1}&=1/2,&
 \operatorname{Var}(X_{r_1})&=1/100,\\
 r_2&=2/20,&\mathbb E X_{r_2}&=1719/3800<1/2,&
 \operatorname{Var}(X_{r_2})&=15309/14440000<1/100.\end{aligned}\] Thus both the positive-gain mean ordering and the stated variance decrease hold; the absolute-mean ordering also holds because gains are positive. Nevertheless the support width increases from \(1/5\) to \(9/20\). The right side of [eq:valid] (\#eq:valid) increases from \(0.043937\) to \(0.539408\), and the actual tail increases from \(0\) to \(1/190\). This contradicts the asserted implication from the stated assumptions, including a monotone decrease, before any exponential rate can be justified.\par
Moreover, an overestimation tail does not establish useful ranking or two-sided transfer. Deterministic operator gains \[(X^A,\Delta^A_{\rm full})=(0.1,0.8),\qquad
 (X^B,\Delta^B_{\rm full})=(0.5,0.6)\] satisfy positive gains, the assumed mean ordering and zero probe variance. Both upper-tail errors are zero for every positive \(\epsilon\), yet the probe ranking is reversed. This is a logical limitation of the supplied criterion, not a claim that these numbers describe the paper’s models.\par
Alternatives and counterevidence. The authors may intend variance as an informal proxy for a concentration scale or may have an empirically stable training regime in mind. Neither interpretation supplies a relationship between the support width, bias, sample fraction, and full-model ranking. The reported successful transfers remain experimental observations. Appendix location and the absence of a named theorem do not remove the dependence: the main text repeats this quantitative rate in its central cost argument.\par
Impact and correction bounds. The exponential transfer justification and any resulting certified choice of \(r\) are unsupported. This is Major because cheap, informative feedback is a central claimed contribution and its quantitative justification uses the invalid step. It does not refute the implementation, cost identity, or measured benchmark scores. A defensible local correction is to retain [eq:valid] (\#eq:valid) only as a conditional one-sided bound and remove the claimed \(r\)-rate. A useful replacement guarantee would require explicit bias/stability and concentration assumptions, with their validity established; no complete replacement theorem is certified here. Evidence: `source\_p17.png` and `independent\_checks.py/json`.\par
Problem 2 (Minor: reported aggregate and defined aggregate disagree).  Claim and location. Appendix D.2, Eq. (1), p. 17, defines overall relative change as \[R=\frac{\sum_i(s_i-s'_i)}{\sum_i s'_i}.\] Table 10, p. 27, reports a \(+2.12\%\) average change for MGM-2B using the four displayed metrics. The corresponding claim also appears in Tables 2 and the scaling discussion.\par
Complete calculation. The displayed baseline vector is \((59.2,51.6,1334,302)\) and the Data-Juicer vector is \((62.08,52.32,1323.37,311.07)\). Their total difference is \(2.04\), so Eq. (1) yields \[100R=100(2.04/1746.8)=0.116785\%.\] Instead, the mean of the four separate relative improvements is \[\frac{100}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{s_i-s'_i}{s'_i}
 =2.116668\%,\] which rounds to the published \(2.12\%\). These functionals are generally different: the former weights relative changes by baseline score magnitude and changes under independent changes of metric units.\par
Alternatives and impact. If the authors normalized every input score by its own baseline before applying Eq. (1), the reported number is reconciled, but this preprocessing is not the stated definition of \(s_i\). The exact numerical agreement strongly favors a formula/documentation mismatch, rather than a demonstrated false score. This is Minor: the aggregate must be specified consistently, but the four component measurements and the direction of their combined gain are not refuted. A verified local repair for this table is to state the mean-relative formula above. Whether every other aggregate, operator ranking and plot used that same formula requires original calculation records and is not certified. Evidence: Eq. (1) visually inspected on original p. 17; Table 10 transcription and independent arithmetic in `independent\_checks.json`.\par
\# Unresolved evidence and overall impact\par
Cost accounting. Tables 3 and 13 (pp. 9 and 29) list \(7.68\alpha\) EFLOPs for each of two successive Data-Juicer stages. Table 12 explicitly says the 228k stage further self-distills the 147k-stage model. Comparing \(167.3\alpha\) for VEnhancer with one such stage gives \(21.784\), consistent with the approximately \(22\)-fold statement; adding both listed stages gives \(10.892\) under the same-factor assumption. However, these are estimates from inference FLOPs multiplied by an unspecified \(\alpha\), not a verified, common accounting of end-to-end training. Teacher/reward computation, the upstream T2V-Turbo checkpoint, probe runs, and architecture-specific training factors matter. A per-incremental-stage interpretation remains possible. This is Needs Evidence, not a demonstrated reversal of model efficiency. Supply the intended cost boundary and actual stage-specific accounting before treating the factor as a total workflow saving.\par
Empirical scope. Held-out benchmark gains, operator selection and diversity interpretations were checked against the disclosed protocols, not reproduced. Correlated proxy attributes and combined-operator effects are not a causal identification of diversity. Negative combination results are acknowledged in the paper. No universal improvement guarantee was inferred from them. Differences between probe models and full-scale models, reuse of validation feedback, uncertainty across many candidate recipes, and final independent evaluation need experiment-level records to quantify.\par
Source provenance and versions. The pinned tree contains the inspected filter implementation, but the linked `docs/Sandbox.md` is absent from that tree. The current DJ-SORA document is not a substitute for the historical Sandbox runner. No scientific defect is inferred merely from that absence. The supplied manifest records unresolved separate-supplement availability after an OpenReview 403 response. The original contains its appendices. Official arXiv metadata [Retained latex: \textbackslash{}cite\{arxiv\}] lists v3 dated 4 June 2025; only version metadata was checked, not a full version diff. No verified erratum or author adoption is asserted.\par
