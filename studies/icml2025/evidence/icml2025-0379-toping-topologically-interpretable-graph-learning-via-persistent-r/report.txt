Abstract: Two central theoretical claims require revision. The uniqueness proof of Theorem 3.4 uses incorrect relations between edge counts, connected components and bottleneck distance. Separately, the displayed Rational Hat sum is not Lipschitz on the stated bottleneck diagram space, so the claimed computable Wasserstein lower bound is not established by constant rescaling. Exact small graph and barcode calculations confirm both issues. An incorrect diagonal projection is also identified. Relevant static code and earlier supplements clarify implementation differences, but do not authenticate the final experiments. Empirical strengths, limitations and the MUTAG follow-up are retained without treating the theory defects as a benchmark refutation.

# P1: invalid steps in the uniqueness proof

Location and classification. Theorem 3.4 on p. 6 and Appendix C on p. 16. Major: the stated ground-truth uniqueness guarantee is not established by the supplied proof.

The theorem assumes that the oracle rationale is smaller than its complement and is minimal for the label: every proper subgraph of that oracle loses label information. Minimality within the oracle does not imply the opening proof assertion that every predictive-optimal candidate contains it; another informative subgraph or redundant copy is not excluded by this condition. A stronger global identifiability assumption would be needed for that reduction. The architecture also feeds complementary topological features to the classifier, so prediction sufficiency cannot simply be identified with the selected-edge set without accounting for that channel.

Even granting the proof’s containment reduction, its subsequent graph identities fail. For a finite graph, \[\beta_0=|V|-|E|+\beta_1,\] not the edge-count relation used in the proof. On the common vertex set \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), let the rationale edges be \(\{01\}\) and the noise edges be \(\{12,23\}\). Then \(|E_\epsilon|>|E_X|\), yet \(\beta_0^\epsilon-\beta_0^X=2-3=-1\), while \(|E_\epsilon|-|E_X|=1\). If isolated vertices are discarded, both subgraphs are connected and the component difference is instead zero, still not one. Thus neither conventional vertex treatment supports the printed equality. This counterexample already uses an acyclic connected full graph, so excluding cycles would not repair the displayed sign/count identity.

Nor does a larger edge count imply more finite zero-dimensional bars: use rationale edges \(\{03,13\}\) and noise edges \(\{01,12,02\}\) on four common vertices. The noise has three edges versus two, but both have exactly two component-merging events. The extra noise edge creates a cycle rather than another \(H_0\) death. The independent union-find calculation records these counts.

The proof additionally asserts a bottleneck distance proportional to a difference of Betti numbers. Bottleneck distance minimizes a maximum matching cost and does not add up multiplicity differences. Let \(P_n\) contain \(n\) copies of \((0,1)\) and let \(Q\) be empty. Every copy can match the diagonal at cost \(1/2\), hence \(d_B(P_n,Q)=1/2\) for all \(n\geq1\). The independently enumerated partial matchings give exactly 0.5 for \(n=1,2,4\). A positive constant times the number of unmatched bars cannot represent this distance. These finite examples avoid complications from essential bars; if essential bars are retained at infinity, the convention must also be stated before the proof’s finite bound is meaningful.

Finally, Definition 3.2 is Wasserstein distance between distributions of diagrams, whereas the proof concludes from per-graph counts and an asserted weighted sum of homology-degree distances. The later finite-function lower bound need not preserve the maximizers of the original distance. An arbitrarily large learned scale is not automatically compatible with the one-Lipschitz requirement. None of these bridges is supplied. Consequently repairing a sign or bounding the first Betti number alone is insufficient. A valid replacement theorem would need explicit identifiability, diagram conventions, a justified objective and a proof for its actual surrogate. Such a theorem has not been established in this review; the finding concerns the proof, not a claim that every empirical fit must fail.

# P2: the stated kernel does not supply the required Lipschitz bound

Location and classification. Proposition 3.3 and the Rational Hat construction in Section 3.1, p. 6. Major for the claimed tractable lower bound.

Proposition 3.3 itself is valid when its functions really are one-Lipschitz under \(d_B\): for any coupling, the difference of expectations is bounded by the expected distance, and taking the infimum yields the result. The problem is the proposed realization. Set the allowed learnable parameters \(c=(0,0)\), \(r=1\), take \(P_\varepsilon=\{(0,\varepsilon)\}\) and \(Q=\varnothing\). The printed kernel gives \[\varphi(P_\varepsilon;c,1)-\varphi(Q;c,1)
 =\frac1{1+\varepsilon}-\frac1{2-\varepsilon}
 \longrightarrow\frac12,
 \qquad d_B(P_\varepsilon,Q)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\longrightarrow0.\] Its ratio to \(d_B\) diverges. For \(\varepsilon=10^{-6}\) the ratio is approximately \(999997.5\). No finite constant \(C\) makes this displayed map \(C\)-Lipschitz over the stated diagram space; multiplying by \(1/C\) therefore does not fix it. The problem is sensitivity to creation/deletion of a near-diagonal bar, not differentiability of the pointwise kernel. A common persistent component can be added to both diagrams without changing this argument. The variable-cardinality diagram setting is relevant to the paper’s variable-size graph rationale setting.

A restricted diagram domain, a diagonal-vanishing construction with proven cardinality control, and explicit norm constraints could support a different guarantee. Those conditions are not stated here. The sum of two individual maximizers also need not remain a lower bound with constant one: even two identical valid witnesses can yield twice the distance. An average or a separately justified Lipschitz bound would be required; learned attention does not establish this automatically. These are limitations of the asserted theoretical approximation, not evidence against the possibility of using the feature map as an empirical regularizer.

# P3: incorrect projection onto the diagonal

Location and classification. Appendix B Definition B.1, p. 15. Minor local mathematical error.

For \(p=(b,d)\) the closest diagonal point under the infinity norm is \(((b+d)/2,(b+d)/2)\), with cost \(|d-b|/2\). The paper prints \((d-b,d-b)\). For \(p=(0.1,0.7)\), the printed point is \((0.6,0.6)\) with cost 0.5; the correct point is \((0.4,0.4)\) with cost 0.3. Both the final PDF and the older supplement show this text, so it is not an extraction artifact. Replace the projection and use the standard diagonal cost. This local repair was checked algebraically and numerically, but does not repair P1 or P2.

# Implementation and empirical qualifications

The complete `src/baselines/lri_bern.py`, the relevant readout, Gaussian mixture and attention classes in `src/utils/model_utils.py`, the Rational Hat class, and selected training/checkpoint/C++ sections were read. The native backend uses extended vertex-filtration persistence; its \(H_0\) routine performs union-find merges rather than counting all edges. This does not certify the full extension. The released readout uses cycle-length features, sixteen structure elements, graph-dependent attention, and a negative sum of squared paired differences. This differs from the paper’s maximum absolute difference of expectations with eight functions. As a simple diagnostic, paired values \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\) have identical empirical distributions and zero difference of means, but the sum of squared paired differences is two. The early code is therefore not, on its face, an implementation of the stated discrepancy; historical applicability remains Needs Evidence.

The source uses an \(\ell_1\) point norm in its hat class, omits an identified bottleneck-Lipschitz normalization, and applies min–max normalization to node/edge attention. The paper’s later visualization prose says such normalization is unnecessary. These are version-specific observations, not a claim of concealed processing in the final experiments. The checkpoint helper selects validation prediction AUC (with a loss tie-break), whereas Appendix E.2 describes accuracy; it does not select by test performance in the inspected branch. Exact final configurations and correspondence to the paper remain unknown.

The empirical evidence is substantial but scoped: five-seed means and standard deviations, multiple backbones, ablations, varying motif counts, and synthetic spurious-correlation tests. Mean-minus-one-standard-deviation shading is descriptive, not a calibrated significance test. Tables 1–2 show that TopInG is not uniformly best on MUTAG; Appendix E.4 and Table 6 explicitly acknowledge this and report the degree-zero variant’s improved AUC/accuracy. That response was considered. Table 2 gives GIN TopInG SPMotif0.5 accuracy \(52.22\pm2.07\), whereas Table 5’s nominal Topo./Gauss row gives \(50.22\pm2.07\); the CINpp SPMotif0.5 AUC deviation is 1.84 in Table 1 and 0.82 in Table 5. Different configurations or transcription can explain this, so run-level reconciliation is requested rather than presumed fabrication.

All original figure panels were visually inspected. The rationale diagrams provide qualitative localization evidence but cannot establish population uniqueness or causal validity. The single/average edge-weight histograms in the older supplement illustrate separated empirical modes without proving universal two-cluster behavior of a Gaussian-mixture penalty. Runtime statements are reported measurements on one RTX 4090 and were not reproduced. The other 221-file snapshot contents, notebooks’ full execution histories, baseline implementations, raw arrays and checkpoints were not exhaustively audited. OpenReview metadata was unavailable with a recorded 403, and no later correction was authenticated.

# Conclusion

The assessment is Major: central uniqueness and computable-bound justifications contain demonstrable mathematical gaps. The local diagonal formula has a verified correction. The practical method may remain useful, and its empirical results are not invalidated solely by these findings. A full replacement theorem, repaired implementation and benchmark reproduction remain unverified. Scientific adjudication is pending, human reviews are empty, and author adoption is unknown.
