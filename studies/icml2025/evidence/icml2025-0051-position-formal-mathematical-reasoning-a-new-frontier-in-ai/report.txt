# 1. Identity, scope and outcome

The main outcome is scope clarification: formal proof checking establishes a formal claim relative to its logic, axioms and trusted checker; it does not automatically establish that a translation expresses the intended informal claim. The paper itself recognizes this boundary. No empirical experiment requirement was imposed on the proposed research agenda.

# 2. Checked dependencies and counterevidence

Formal soundness and statement fidelity. Section 2.2 (p3) describes sound formal reasoning and says Lean checks a proof relative to its statement. Section 4.2 (p6) explicitly identifies evaluation of autoformalized statements as a bottleneck. Section 4.3 (p7) explicitly requires generated specifications to match developers’ intent. The extended version reinforces this on pp13 and 19 [E2]. Therefore a proof of an unrelated, weakened or mistranslated statement would expose a translation failure, not refute the paper’s conditional proof-checking property. The trusted checker and accepted axioms are operative assumptions; adding an inconsistent axiom would not be a valid counterexample.

Data and self-improvement. Section 3 (p5) reports diminishing gains after a few expert-iteration rounds. Section 4.1 (pp5-6) leaves generalization beyond domains with many human-written problems open. Logical availability of indefinitely many provable statements is distinct from useful diversity or guaranteed learning improvement. The actual text does not promise the latter. This dependency supports treating synthetic-data claims as proposed mechanisms with stated limitations.

Neural verification and indispensability. Section 3 (p5) and Section 5.3 (p8) acknowledge that neural verifiers can improve performance. Section 6 (p9) treats neural-only approaches as an alternative view and expresses uncertainty about rigorous theorem proving. Its prospective advocacy is not a proved impossibility theorem about all future neural systems. Conversely, the paper does not establish a universal necessity theorem for formal reasoning. The position remains a proposed direction supported by examples, not a quantified lower bound.

Roadmap and code verification. Section 5 (pp7-9) proposes capability levels and identifies missing benchmarks. The levels do not assert a mathematically nested hierarchy or a guaranteed development order. The Level 4 proposal in Section 5.4 does not provide an implemented scoring rule that uniquely recovers user intent from code. Such evaluation design remains future work; it is not an observed failing benchmark.

# 3. Independent checks of concrete evidence

Figure 1 (p2) asks for x+y over complex x,y satisfying x^2+y^2=7 and x^3+y^3=10. Write s=x+y and t=xy. The first constraint gives t=(s^2-7)/2; substituting into the second gives the cubic below. Its three roots are -5, 1 and 4. For each root, choose x,y as the roots of z^2-sz+t. Every such quadratic has complex roots, and the symmetric identities recover both original equations. Thus the negative discriminants for s=-5 and s=4 are allowed, not counterexamples. Exact rational calculations return sum-of-squares 7, sum-of-cubes 10 and zero cubic residual for all three cases; see calculation_results.json.

Figure 2 (p3) defines addition recursively in the second argument. For 0+n=n, the base case reduces to 0=0 and the induction step reduces add(0,succ(n)) to succ(add(0,n)), then uses the induction hypothesis. This is an analytic inspection of the displayed example, not execution or verification by Lean.

AlphaProof evidence. The 2024 official infographic visibly labels approximately 1M informal problems and 100M formal problems [E3]; this supports the scale reported in Section 4.1. It does not show that all 100M were successfully proved. The surrounding training description includes proof and disproof search. The later Nature account reports about 80M formal problems [E5, Training], a later account that must not be silently equated with the earlier graphic. No demonstrated core error is inferred from the differing counts or from the paper’s loose use of ’theorems’; a success-count interpretation is not established.

The official 2024 account describes manually translated contest statements and multi-day computation. The paper’s Section 5.3 explicitly distinguishes future evaluation without manual formalization. The silver-medal-level example is therefore not treated as proof of autonomous, human-time-budget performance.
