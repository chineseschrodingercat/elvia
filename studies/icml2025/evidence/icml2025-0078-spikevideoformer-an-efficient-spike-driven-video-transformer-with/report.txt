# 2. Checked reasoning and operative assumptions

Proposition 3.1 (p4; Appendix A, p12) concerns nonzero fixed real vectors, a shared independent Gaussian projection, and zero-threshold binary signs. Each projected sign disagreement is Bernoulli with probability equal to the angle divided by pi. The empirical mean therefore satisfies a two-sided Hoeffding tail bound; the paper’s weaker exponential bound is valid for a fixed pair. The condition on D involving the number of points is not a substitute for stating a simultaneous all-pairs failure probability. A uniform guarantee needs a union bound; preserving every ranking additionally needs a score margin. Neither stronger guarantee is assumed in this review.

Equation (8) correctly rewrites binary Hamming similarity as one half plus a scaled bipolar inner product. Equations (9)-(10) define centered Hamming attention and reassociate two matrix products before the spiking nonlinearity. For L=T*N tokens and h fixed heads of width d=D/h, the attention products require order L*D^2/h arithmetic operations and a width-by-width intermediate, rather than an L-by-L attention matrix. Linear projections add order L*D^2. Neuron-state updates are also linear in sequence length for fixed width. This is an operation-count statement; it does not guarantee identical wall time or power scaling on every device.

The checked pose-tracking release flattens temporal and spatial positions, computes (2K-1)^T V followed by (2Q-1) times that result, and scales by 2*self.dim (backbone.py:411-434). This agrees with the centered, reassociated design. Static correspondence and small independent list/matrix checks support the algebra; no author module was executed.

\[H=\tfrac12\mathbf1_L\mathbf1_L^\top+\tfrac{1}{2D}(2Q-\mathbf1)(2K-\mathbf1)^\top.\] \[(\widetilde Q\widetilde K^\top)V=\widetilde Q(\widetilde K^\top V),\qquad O(TND^2/h).\]

# 3. Problem 1: factorized-attention dimensions do not support 7D squared parameters

Source claim and location: Table 1 (p5) and Appendix C (p13) give factorized attention 7D^2 parameters, versus 4D^2 for joint attention. Yet the appendix explicitly splits the channel dimension into halves, describes five half-width linear maps with a shared query, and concatenates the two outputs before a final full-width linear map. Equation (26) likewise uses five (D/2)^2 projection terms.

Under that stated shared-query construction, ignoring biases and normalization parameters consistently with the joint 4D^2 comparison, the count is 5(D/2)^2+D^2=9D^2/4. For a concrete legal dimension D=4 and one head, five 2-by-2 matrices plus one 4-by-4 output matrix contain 36 weights, whereas 7D^2 gives 112. If Figure 5’s two drawn query maps are independent rather than tied, there are six half-width maps and the count becomes 5D^2/2=40 at D=4. Bias and normalization terms are lower order and do not explain the leading coefficient 7.

Equation (26) also omits the stated final output projection, unlike Eqs. (24)-(25). These observations show that the diagram, prose, FLOP expression, and parameter summary do not uniquely describe one architecture. A construction with full-width maps could produce a different count, but would not be the written half-width construction. The released repository contains no factorized or hierarchical implementation that authenticates the ablation architecture.

Judgment: Major for the quantitative derivation supporting a stated core design-analysis contribution. The paper’s abstract and contribution list (pp.1-2) advertise analysis and selection of an optimal space-time design, and Section 3.4 compares those prototypes using Table 1. Under either documented query-sharing interpretation, the factorized count is 9D^2/4 or 5D^2/2, below joint attention’s 4D^2 rather than the printed 7D^2 above it. This reverses the parameter comparison within that analysis. The conclusion concerns the described tensor dimensions; it does not assert wrong historical trained-model weights, a global optimality theorem, or failed accuracy results. Linear temporal complexity remains valid. The conditional counts are verified, while the intended architecture and actual ablation counts require the original implementation/configurations.

\[5(D/2)^2+D^2=\tfrac94D^2,\qquad 6(D/2)^2+D^2=\tfrac52D^2.\]

# 5. Theory and empirical scope that should remain explicit

The Gaussian shared-projection proposition supplies guidance for the similarity choice. The learned, separate query/key transformations and temporal LIF thresholds in the actual model do not automatically satisfy its assumptions. This distinction is a limit of the theorem’s applicability, not a counterexample under the theorem’s stated random-projection assumptions.

Removing the one-half offset preserves pairwise similarity rankings, but does not preserve the complete weighted-value aggregation: full Hamming attention adds the query-independent vector (1/2)*sum_j V_j. The paper explicitly defines the centered mechanism after dropping the offset. No assertion of identical full-Hamming outputs is needed to justify the matrix reassociation, and this review does not misread that design choice as an invalid equality.

Figure 3 is labeled average absolute error for different numbers of random pairs and motivates a dataset-size trend. Under the stated independent Gaussian-bit model, E|f_H-g(f_C)| is at most 1/(2*sqrt(D)); averaging more identically distributed pairs does not change that expectation. At D=64 the bound is 0.0625. A finite realized average is not deterministically bounded by its expectation, and dependence through a shared random projection matters for concentration. The plotted values and pair-count trend therefore require the generation script, random seeds, and exact average-versus-maximum statistic. They are recorded as Needs Evidence, not a proved falsification of Proposition 3.1.

The abstract’s ’over15%’ improvement is comparator- and dataset-dependent: the best listed pose SNN changes from45.7 to39.8, a12.91% error reduction; the cited CNN comparator48.2 gives17.43%. Cityscapes65.9 to73.1 gives10.93% relative gain, while VSPW32.3 to37.9 gives17.34%. The tables expose these distinctions. Similarly, the16-fold classification energy ratio is against ViViT, whereas the ratio against Swin-B is about3.15. The broad headline should not be read as a uniform guarantee against every baseline.

# 6. Energy and implementation evidence limits

Appendix E (p14) uses a generic spike-rate operation-cost model with 45nm MAC/AC reference costs. These are analytical energy estimates, not measured model-specific device power. Equation (28) prints E_MAC in the SNN expression although the surrounding text describes E_AC; without the accounting script, the isolated symbol does not establish which factor generated the table.

Bipolar Q and K have no zero entries. Efficient sparse implementations can still exploit binary structure through affine corrections, but skipping a zero in the original Q without accounting for its transformed -1 contribution is not generally valid. The public release contains no energy, FLOPs, spike-rate, or benchmark accounting routine establishing how these costs were handled. No numerical underestimate is asserted solely from this missing provenance.

The release adds a Hamming-only BatchNorm before attention LIF and uses a different LIF threshold from the dot-product branch. The historical ablation must therefore be identified before attributing all measured improvement to the score function alone. Neither the table-generating energy path nor the factorized/hierarchical variants, Kinetics-400 evaluation, or segmentation pipeline was present in the acquired tree.

The two timing points235ms and1087ms have a ratio4.63 when T grows from8 to32, consistent with but insufficient alone to establish asymptotic linearity. The algebra supplies the asymptotic argument. Main-text pose testing duration8s atT=32 versus Appendix H’s16s is an unresolved protocol discrepancy; original evaluation configurations are needed. No fresh training, inference, or hardware measurement was performed.
