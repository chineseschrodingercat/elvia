Abstract: MARINE’s inspected token-level logit combination is a valid normalized decoding rule. Its central derivation of the resulting sequence distribution, however, drops prefix-dependent normalizers. A strictly positive two-token counterexample disproves the stated sequence law, including at the default guidance strength. This is a supported Major derivation issue. A separate Minor concerns a universal aggregation comparison contradicted by the paper’s table. Neither finding proves that the reported empirical benefits disappear. Adjudication is pending.

# Major: a sequence law loses prefix-dependent factors

Problem 1 (Major; Section 4.2, page 4).  The displayed derivation identifies the product of normalized next-token guided distributions with a sequence distribution proportional to \(Q(y)^\gamma P(y)^{1-\gamma}\). That proportionality does not generally hold.

Equation reference: eq:local, eq:chain. Fix the image and instruction, suppressing them in the notation. Let \(P(a\mid h)\) denote the original next-token distribution and \(Q(a\mid h)\) its guidance-conditioned counterpart at prefix \(h\). For positive probabilities and \(0<\gamma<1\), define \[w(a\mid h)=P(a\mid h)^{1-\gamma}Q(a\mid h)^\gamma,\qquad
 Z(h)=\sum_a w(a\mid h).\] Softmax sampling from the mixed logits in Algorithm 1 gives \[
 R(a\mid h)=\frac{w(a\mid h)}{Z(h)}.\] The correct chain rule is therefore \[
 R(y)=\frac{P(y)^{1-\gamma}Q(y)^\gamma}
              {\prod_{t=1}^{m} Z(y_{<t})}.\] Although \(Z(h)\) is constant when comparing candidate tokens at a fixed prefix, it can change with the prefix. Its product cannot be absorbed into one sequence-independent proportionality constant. The corresponding displayed log-probability equality on page 4 also needs the term \(-\log Z(h)\); omitting it is harmless for within-prefix token ranking, but not for the claimed sequence law.

## A complete positive counterexample

Use the alphabet \(\{A,B\}\) and fixed length two. Both distributions assign first-token probabilities \((1/2,1/2)\). Their continuations are \[\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{prefix} & P(\,\cdot\mid h)&Q(\,\cdot\mid h)\\ \hline
 A &(1/2,1/2)&(1/2,1/2)\\
 B &(9/10,1/10)&(1/10,9/10).
\end{array}\] At \(\gamma=1/2\), \(Z(\varnothing)=Z(A)=1\), but \(Z(B)=3/5\). Equation [eq:local] (#eq:local) consequently generates \[(R(AA),R(AB),R(BA),R(BB))=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4).\] The asserted global geometric law instead has unnormalized weights \((1/4,1/4,3/20,3/20)\), hence normalized probabilities \[(5/16,5/16,3/16,3/16).\] The two distributions differ in total variation by \(1/8\). There are no zero probabilities, support ambiguities, variable-length effects, or forbidden guidance values.

This example also allows \(P\) and \(Q\) to arise from one consistent joint distribution. Let the guidance event have probability \(1/10\), and let its complementary conditional sequence law be \[S=(1/4,1/4,89/180,1/180).\] All components are positive and \(P=(1/10)Q+(9/10)S\). Thus the counterexample does not rely on incompatible marginals and conditionals.

At the paper’s default \(\gamma=0.7\), the same construction gives \(Z(B)\simeq0.658871877\). The local sequence law is \[(0.25,0.25,0.146703943,0.353296057),\] whereas the globally normalized geometric law is \[(0.301409655,0.301409655,0.116535947,0.280644743).\] Their total variation is approximately \(0.102819310\). An independent reviewer-authored enumeration verifies these values, the exact half-guidance case, the joint-distribution construction, and Equation [eq:chain] (#eq:chain).

## Core dependency, counterevidence, and correction

The failed step is the principal sequence-level justification in the method section, directly linking the proposed token rule to its claimed classifier-free guided sequence distribution. That demonstrated invalid derivation supports Major under the assigned calibration. No benchmark loss or whole-paper collapse is required.

The token rule remains mathematically well-defined. At \(\gamma=0\) and \(1\), the two constructions agree; they also agree whenever the normalizer product is constant across sequences. The paper imposes no such general restriction. Its principal evaluations use greedy decoding, which depends only on token rankings. Accordingly this counterexample does not demonstrate wrong reported captions, scores, latency, or hallucination reductions.

The supported correction is to retain Equation [eq:chain] (#eq:chain) and describe MARINE as local logit guidance. If the intended target is the global geometric sequence law, its conditionals require continuation partition factors. For a finite tree, let \(H(h)=\sum_a w(a\mid h)H(ha)\), with terminal value one. Then those conditionals are \(w(a\mid h)H(ha)/H(h)\), not generally Equation [eq:local] (#eq:local). This identifies the missing mathematical quantity, without claiming a practical new decoder or a verified software repair.

# Minor: the intersection comparison is not universal

Problem 2 (Minor; Section 5.3, page 7, and Table 7, page 8).  The text says intersection aggregation consistently outperforms union. For LLaVA-v1.5, Table 7 gives intersection \((\mathrm{CHAIR}_S,\mathrm{CHAIR}_I)=(6.2,3.0)\), but union \((5.4,2.7)\). Both metrics are lower-is-better.

The union thus improves the two reported scores by \(0.8\) and \(0.3\) points in that setting. The other two listed models favor intersection, so a correction should state model-dependent performance with benefits on two of the three tested models. The table does not establish that intersection is ineffective or that union generally dominates.

Likewise, aggregate success should not be read as universal per-model dominance: in Table 1, MiniGPT-v2’s MARINE CHAIR scores \(11.8,4.9\) exceed greedy’s \(8.2,4.2\), while recall rises from \(41.1\) to \(49.7\). This is an explicit trade-off, compatible with the lower average CHAIR scores. No new inferential significance claim is made.

# Implementation checks and unresolved empirical questions

The entire 44-line `marine/utils/utils_guidance.py` was read. It log-normalizes both branches, computes \(\gamma(\ell_Q-\ell_P)+\ell_P\), and log-normalizes again. This implements Equation [eq:local] (#eq:local); it supplies no continuation-partition factor. The parent also inspected the complete generation driver and dataset collation file. Prompt construction uses the same image with original and guidance queries; the processor continues the guided cache using the selected token. There is no candidate-vocabulary filter in this processor. Object consensus is an upstream operation, not such a token filter.

The cached branch omits an explicit padding mask after its first forward call. Its behavior depends on the backbone/library treatment of cached masks and positions, which was not resolved here; no score corruption is asserted. The staged example primarily covers LLaVA-v1.5, not a complete reimplementation of every evaluated model. Auxiliary source checks and this parent’s reads are static only.

The report treats CHAIR/POPE, GPT-assisted judging, latency, memory, temperature, and prompt ablations as reported observations. Appendix A.5 discloses a 200-sample Woodpecker subset compared with the larger main evaluation setting; matched-sample uncertainty and the underlying outputs were not reanalyzed. Table 19 (page 19) also gives the mPLUG-Owl2 original baseline as \(5.0/3.2/33.2\), whereas Table 1 gives \(6.2/3.4/38.8\); the former matches Table 1’s InstructBLIP row. Whether this is a labeling error or a different run requires evidence; no replacement score is inferred. Ground-truth guidance is properly identified as an oracle variant, not an available detector. A paid judge used in the authors’ evaluation does not contradict an API-free inference method.
