\section*{2. Independently checked central result and coverage scope}
For IID Bernoulli outcomes, the paper’s Wald standard error is sqrt[p\_hat(1-p\_hat)/N]. It vanishes at all-failure or all-success samples, so the stated boundary failure is real. Reviewer-authored exact binomial integration, without simulation or author-code execution, gives the following 95\% interval coverage averaged over a uniform true success probability:\par
N | Wald | Uniform-prior Bayes | Wilson\par
3 | 49.5743\% | 95.0000\% | 95.5958\%\par
10 | 76.9256\% | 95.0000\% | 95.4080\%\par
30 | 87.4913\% | 95.0000\% | 95.2403\%\par
100 | 92.2254\% | 95.0000\% | 95.1136\%\par
These calculations corroborate the scale of the main failure; they are not a reproduction of the authors’ sampled simulation or every plotted point. Bayesian average calibration follows from averaging posterior set probability over the joint model. It is different from coverage at each fixed parameter. At N=3 and true theta=0.95, the paper’s Bayesian and Wilson 95\% intervals contain theta only for three successes, giving coverage 0.95\textasciicircum{}3 = 85.7375\%; Wald gives 13.5375\%.\par
Accordingly, Table 1’s small-N coverage checkmarks (p. 9) need to be read as a summary of the examined regimes, not a universal pointwise guarantee. Appendix I.1 and Figure 20 explicitly include fixed-theta checks; their existence rules out a claim that the paper concealed or omitted this distinction entirely. This report treats the table wording as a scope clarification, not another independent numerical finding.\par
\section*{3. Problem 1: Extra normalization in the importance estimator}
Minor; high confidence. Appendix E, p. 21, Eq. (55) defines normalized weights w\_hat[k] = w[k]/sum\_i w[i], which sum to one. Equation (60) then places a further factor 1/K outside the weighted sum. The correct self-normalized estimate is:\par
> E\_hat[theta | Y] = sum\_k theta[k] w\_hat[k] (no additional 1/K).\par
Complete witness: with a uniform Bernoulli prior, one success and one failure, take proposal values 1/4 and 3/4. Both likelihoods equal 3/16, so normalized weights are 1/2 and 1/2. The corrected weighted estimate is 1/2, also the exact Beta(2,2) posterior mean. The printed Eq. (60) returns 1/4. For proposals in [0,1], the printed estimate is bounded by 1/K and therefore cannot consistently estimate a positive posterior mean as K grows.\par
Repair and impact: remove the extra 1/K. This repairs a quantitative derivation, not merely wording. However, Snippet 4 on p. 22 and the inspected clustered implementation normalize once and resample using those probabilities, then take quantiles. They do not use the erroneous expectation formula to construct their intervals. Thus no reported coverage failure is inferred from this local error.\par
\section*{4. Problem 2: Latent correlation is not binary correlation}
Minor; high confidence. The final sentence of Appendix C, p. 19, says the binary evals attain the specified correlation rho; p. 20 describes an example with marginal accuracies 0.6 and 0.9 and rho=0.7. Thresholding correlated Gaussians preserves the stated Bernoulli marginals, but generally changes Pearson correlation. Writing Phi2 for the bivariate standard-normal CDF, the induced correlation is:\par
> Corr(Y\_A,Y\_B) = [Phi2(Phi\textasciicircum{}-1(theta\_A),Phi\textasciicircum{}-1(theta\_B);rho) - theta\_A theta\_B] / sqrt[theta\_A(1-theta\_A) theta\_B(1-theta\_B)].\par
For theta\_A=theta\_B=1/2 and latent rho=1/2, the binary correlation is (2/pi)arcsin(1/2)=1/3. More decisively, the paper’s 0.6 and 0.9 Bernoulli marginals permit correlation at most [0.6-0.54]/sqrt(0.6*0.4*0.9*0.1)=0.408248, so binary correlation 0.7 is impossible. Independent quadrature gives approximately 0.339587 for that latent model.\par
Repair and impact: consistently call rho the latent Gaussian correlation and report induced binary correlation separately. The main p. 7 construction and Appendix E.2 likelihood can remain valid under this interpretation. This finding changes parameter interpretation; it does not prove that the paired-model simulations or their Bayesian likelihood were incorrectly implemented.\par
\section*{5. Problem 3: Wrong full-coverage range for a difference}
Minor; high confidence. Figure 7 and Appendix A.1 (pp. 13–14) concern delta=theta\_A-theta\_B. The continuation on p. 14 dismisses t intervals wider than one because [0,1] supposedly provides 100\% coverage. The actual parameter range is [-1,1]. For independent Bernoulli models theta\_A=0.2 and theta\_B=0.8, delta=-0.6, so the constant interval [0,1] has zero coverage for every N. Under the paper’s independent uniform priors, its average coverage is only 1/2.\par
Repair and impact: use [-1,1], with width two, as the trivial full-coverage reference and reassess the efficiency statement. Width greater than one alone does not establish domination by a valid full-range interval. This invalidates that specific dismissal; it does not show that the plotted t intervals have acceptable calibration or competitive efficiency.\par
\section*{6. Problem 4: The clustered-bootstrap comparator fixes clusters}
Minor; high confidence in code behavior, conditional attribution to the published run. Section 3.2 and Figure 3 (pp. 5–6) target the global theta in Eq. (8), with cluster probabilities drawn around theta. In no\_clt\_paper, eval\_lib/src/intervals\_clustered.py, bootstrap\_subtask\_confidence\_interval (lines 120–200, especially 159–176 and 191), the algorithm resamples observations within each existing cluster and averages the cluster means. It never resamples clusters. subtask\_experiments.py lines 65–68 selects this routine for the named comparator.\par
The source file is byte-identical at the inspected July 2025 snapshot and the initial 3 March 2025 commit, with SHA-256 `84b2dc23faf2a2a4a5e182d8e7f4a2ad86a5156c657a16dff22ea2d003f3ae3a`. Thus this is not merely a later regression. The exact commit used for the published figure remains unverified.\par
Analytical witness: take two observed clusters of size five, one all zero and one all one. Every within-cluster resample preserves both cluster means, producing a degenerate [0.5,0.5] interval. Resampling the two clusters instead produces overall means 0, 0.5 and 1 with probabilities 1/4, 1/2 and 1/4, giving a 95\% percentile interval [0,1]. This proves omission of between-cluster variation. It does not prove nominal coverage of a corrected bootstrap when only two clusters are available.\par
A fixed-cluster conditional target would be a possible alternative interpretation, but Eq. (8) explicitly targets a global mean over random clusters. Repair: identify the actual resampling unit, relabel the existing comparator as within-cluster resampling, and add a cluster-resampling comparison before extending its poor results to that alternative. The supported consequence is limited to one comparator and the breadth of the comparison, not the Wald failure or universal superiority of another method.\par
