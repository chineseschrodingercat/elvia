Abstract: Two exact counterexamples invalidate derivations central to the theoretical justification of Oracle-MoE. A Gaussian norm-comparison lemma used for the main locality theorem fails with probability one for admissible parameters, and the attention analysis uses a false bilinear inequality. Moreover, reduced embedding variation alone does not guarantee fewer discrete expert changes. These are Major mathematical findings; they do not establish that the reported latency measurements or the practical method fail. Scientific adjudication remains pending.\par

\# Claim and dependency\par
The paper decomposes a token embedding as \(t_i=s_i+u_i\), separates semantic and identity subspaces, groups tokens by thresholded attention, averages each growing group, and routes the mean to a nearest oracle-space cluster. Theorem 1 on p. 5 claims a high-probability reduction in consecutive embedding variation at sufficiently large identity dimension and then concludes \(\mathrm{CSD}_{\rm token}>\mathrm{CSD}_{\rm oracle}\) through its approximate CSD framework. For one active expert, actual CSD is a count of changes of expert index. Appendix A.4 explicitly invokes Lemma 5 with \(K=C(W_g,k)\) and its additive threshold identified with \(2r\). Appendix A.1 is separately cited on pp. 3–4 to justify attention as an indicator of high-level semantic similarity. Both derivations therefore support central contributions.\par
\# Substantive findings\par
Problem 1 (The norm-comparison lemma used by the main theorem is false).  Lemma 5, pp. 14–15, asserts that \(\|Y\|+m<K\|Z\|\) has probability tending to one as dimension increases for any fixed \(m>0,K>0\) and \(n<m\). Its stated Gaussian construction admits an exact zero-probability counterexample.\par
\#\#\#\# Complete check.\par
Let \(n=1\), \(m=2\), \(K=1/4\), and let \(x_1,x_2\) be independent \(N(0,I_d)\) vectors for any \(d\). Select the singleton subset \(S\) uniformly from \(\{1\}\) and \(\{2\}\), exactly as required. In the paper’s notation, \[Z=x_1-x_2,\qquad
 Y=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}-x_S=
 \begin{cases}-Z/2,&S=\{1\},\\ Z/2,&S=\{2\}.\end{cases}\] Hence \(\|Y\|=\|Z\|/2\) identically. The claimed event becomes \[\frac12\|Z\|+2<\frac14\|Z\|,\] which is impossible, including when \(Z=0\). Its probability is zero for every dimension, not asymptotically one. The example satisfies isotropic Gaussianity, independence of the \(x_i\), random subset selection, positive fixed constants and \(n<m\).\par
The proof’s comparison of \(\mathbb E\|Y\|^2\) with \(\mathbb E\|Z\|^2\) cannot justify an arbitrary factor \(K\). In addition, its variance calculation assumes independence of the two squared norms, although here one is exactly one quarter of the other. The claimed Chebyshev conclusion therefore cannot repair the counterexample.\par
For fixed \(n,m\) and nonzero Gaussian variance, separate norm concentration gives \[\frac{\|Y\|}{\|Z\|}
 \ \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\
 \sqrt{\frac{1/n-1/m}{2}}.\] A strict inequality \(K>\sqrt{(1/n-1/m)/2}\) is a sufficient corrected asymptotic condition for a fixed additive threshold. Below that threshold the asserted conclusion fails. In the exact example the threshold is \(1/2\). No appropriate lower bound on \(C(W_g,k)\) is supplied when the lemma is used on p. 16.\par
\#\#\#\# Why a norm repair alone is insufficient.\par
The passage from a smaller continuous displacement to fewer discrete assignments is an approximation on pp. 3, 5 and 14, not a proved error bound. The explicit routing model permits the following check. Let \(H=\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R^d\), let \(s_1,s_2\) be independent uniform variables on \([1,2]\), and let \(u_1,u_2\) be independent \(N(0,I_d)\), independent of the semantic variables. These satisfy Assumptions 1–4 with semantic center \(1.5\) and radius \(0.5\). Take \(W_Q=P\), \(W_K=2P\), where \(P\) projects onto the semantic coordinate. The two tokens belong to one thresholded group with \(\epsilon=0.01\): even including self-attention, the second token’s attention to the first is at least \(1/(1+e^4)>0.01\).\par
A raw top-one gate with rows \(P\) and zero assigns both tokens to expert 1. For oracle routing, the permitted nearest-center rule with centers \(c_\pm=(1.5,\pm a,0,\ldots,0)\), \(a>0\), assigns by the sign of the first identity coordinate. The growing means are \(z_1=t_1\) and \(z_2=(t_1+t_2)/2\). If \(X=u_{1,1}\) and \(Y=u_{2,1}\), the oracle changes expert precisely when \(X\) and \(X+Y\) have opposite signs. Rotational symmetry of \((X,Y)\) gives probability \(1/4\), independently of \(d\). Thus token CSD is zero for the pair, while oracle CSD is one with probability \(1/4\).\par
This does not set the raw router to a globally constant dense model: negative-semantic groups elsewhere use its other expert. Nor does it claim these centers were observed in a trained run. It shows that the explicit distribution and nearest-centroid assumptions do not supply the claimed routing guarantee; a further restriction connecting learned centers, semantic directions and boundary margins would be needed. The appendix’s informal cluster-density approximation does not provide such a bound. The independent 100,000-pair sign diagnostic gave \(0.24843\), consistent with the exact \(1/4\); the proof does not depend on this simulation.\par
\#\#\#\# Severity and counterevidence.\par
This is Major: a demonstrated false lemma is used directly in the core theorem, and its claimed discrete-routing consequence needs additional control. Averaging independent noise can still reduce variance, and actual learned centers may be favorable. Those facts neither establish the stated universal high-dimensional result nor refute the reported empirical improvements. The threshold condition above is a partial lemma repair, not a verified replacement for the full CSD theorem.\par
Problem 2 (The attention-semantic argument uses an invalid inner-product bound).  Appendix A.1, p. 13, claims for unit \(X,Y\) and full-rank maps on the shared semantic subspace that \[|\langle M_QX,M_KY\rangle|
 \le \|M_Q^\top M_K\|\,|\langle X,Y\rangle|.\] This is false under the stated assumptions.\par
Take the two-dimensional shared semantic subspace and \[M_Q=I_2,\quad
 M_K=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 X=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.\] Both transformations are full rank, are different as allowed, and operate on exactly the same subspace. Both input vectors are unit. The left side equals \(1\) and the right side equals \(0\), since \(\langle X,Y\rangle=0\). Equal subspaces and equal effective ranks therefore do not imply the proposed preservation of input inner products. The correct general inequality is \[|\langle M_QX,M_KY\rangle|
 \le\|M_Q^\top M_K\|\,\|X\|\,\|Y\|.\] It does not infer high original semantic alignment from a large transformed attention product. A stronger condition such as \(M_Q^\top M_K\) being a scalar multiple of the identity would be sufficient for the disputed form, but is not among the assumptions.\par
This finding is Major: pp. 3–4 explicitly rely on this analysis to connect attention-defined groups with high-level semantic similarity, which is the motivation for oracle-space routing. The counterexample invalidates that mathematical justification. It does not show that empirical attention groups are useless, and the later semantic-distribution assumptions can still be posited independently. The valid spectral bound is a verified algebraic replacement, but not a repair of the claimed semantic implication.\par
\# Empirical evidence and scope of the conclusion\par
The reported first-token latencies in Table 2 imply speed ratios of approximately \(4.56\), \(4.77\) and \(2.60\) relative to FIFO, LRU and SwapMoE, respectively. These are arithmetic checks of reported measurements, not reproduced timings. Table 1 supports similar or slightly higher aggregate task scores while several individual task scores decrease; no statistical performance-loss conclusion is possible without uncertainty and matched evaluation details. The appendix reports further examples, including fine-grained experts, which remain empirical counterevidence against assuming the method cannot work.\par
Some experimental identities require clarification: Table 2 names a 765M model while the main configuration is 729M, and the largest-model expert count differs between the main text and Table 3. No particular configuration is inferred to have run. Exact memory accounting, caching traces, timing methodology, model artifacts and source-to-result provenance are unavailable in the staged material. These are Needs Evidence items rather than additional established Major defects.\par
The overall initial assessment is Major for both demonstrated mathematical problems. Practical latency and accuracy results are not invalidated by assertion.
