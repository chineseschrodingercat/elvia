# Method and operative assumptions

PAS learns a small vector of correction coordinates shared across samples at selected diffusion sampling steps. It pins the first basis vector to the current update direction, obtains three principal components from history augmented with that direction, and orthogonalizes the four vectors. It fits coordinates to states on a more finely discretized teacher trajectory and retains corrections whose teacher-state loss improvement exceeds a threshold.

Equation reference: eq:basis. Write \(d\ne0\) for the current direction, \(X'\) for the augmented history, and \(v'_1,v'_2,v'_3\) for its leading right singular vectors. Equation (14), p. 4, specifies \[
 U=[u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4]
 =\operatorname{Schmidt}\left(
      \frac d{\|d\|},v'_1,v'_2,v'_3\right),
 \qquad U\in\mathbb R^{D\times4},\] describing its columns as four orthogonal unit vectors. Equations (15)–(18) and Algorithms 1–2 use their four coordinates for the actual correction.

The geometric explanation is expressly heuristic. This review does not invent a universal theorem that all diffusion trajectories have rank three or that lower teacher-state error must improve FID. It does check the unqualified mathematical construction on which the four-coordinate procedure itself depends.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The core four-vector basis construction is undefined on admissible dependent inputs).  Assessment: Major. The specified orthogonalization cannot produce four orthogonal unit vectors from the dependent inputs that arise in the exact low-rank setting.

## Exact counterexample and all relevant conditions

Take \(D=4\) and \[X'=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0&0\\
 0&2&0&0\\
 0&0&3&0\\
 1&0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad d=e_1.\] There are four history rows, the current direction is nonzero and is included as a row, and the positive singular values are distinct: \(3,2,\sqrt2\). The leading right singular vectors can be chosen as \(e_3,e_2,e_1\). Thus [eq:basis] (#eq:basis) receives \[e_1,\ e_3,\ e_2,\ e_1.\] After the pinned vector, the Gram–Schmidt residual norms are exactly \(1,1,0\). Normalizing the last residual is undefined. Sign choices for singular vectors do not remove the dependence.

More generally, if \(X'\) has rank three and contains \(d\) as a row, then \(d\) lies in the span of its leading three principal components. Appending the normalized \(d\) to those three components cannot increase their span to dimension four. The p. 4 observation that adding a third PC suffices to span the relevant low-dimensional trajectory does not establish the independence required for four unit vectors.

## An admissible smooth diffusion case

This is not only a matrix detached from diffusion. For centered Gaussian data of variance one under the variance-exploding/EDM convention, \[q_t=\mathcal N(0,(1+t^2)I),\qquad
 \nabla_x\log q_t(x)=-\frac{x}{1+t^2},\qquad
 d(x,t)=-t\nabla_x\log q_t(x)=\frac{t}{1+t^2}x.\] At finite positive times, each nonzero trajectory stays on a radial line. Its initial state and update directions are collinear. Accumulating enough history rows does not remove this rank-one structure. A leading PCA direction is parallel to the pinned direction and gives a zero orthogonalization residual in exact arithmetic.

The example uses a smooth positive density and well-defined finite nonzero times. It does not rely on a zero current direction, an undefined score or an insufficient number of rows. Centering the radial history likewise leaves at most a one-dimensional span.

## Implementation and core dependency

The preserved , lines 109–126, implements the four-vector branch with divisions by all three residual norms and no rank guard. Lines 69–70 also normalize the current direction. The initial exclusions in , lines 311–320, address too few rows, not dependence of later rows. The adaptive improvement test occurs after basis construction, so it does not make the invalid construction defined.

The paper states no restriction requiring the pinned direction to lie outside the leading three-PC span or every residual norm to be positive. This defect therefore concerns an actual mathematical step in the core correction method, not an unused local identity.

## Counterevidence, severity and possible repair

Empirical image histories may be only approximately low rank, leaving enough independent components for the four-vector calculation to run. No NaN, exception, unstable image or failed benchmark was observed in this review; author code was not executed. The exact construction’s invalidity nevertheless follows from the stated inputs and is closely tied to the central method, supporting Major without requiring benchmark score loss.

A rank-revealing basis or an explicit orthogonal-complement completion could supply a well-defined alternative. Its relation to fixed learned coordinate vectors and its inference convention would need specification. Neither alternative is the algorithm currently written, and no complete repair is verified.

Problem 2 (Inference retains extra uncorrected directions absent from the training history).  Assessment: Minor, preserved release. Historical experiment impact remains Needs Evidence.

## Static dataflow

In , `correction_d` passes the original list to `PCA_process` (lines 27–34), which appends the raw current direction at line 54. Training’s `pre_PCA_process` instead passes a copy at line 164; after an accepted correction, , lines 369–371, stores only the corrected direction.

Euler inference in , lines 80–85, passes its persistent list directly to `correction_d` and then appends the returned corrected direction. The iPNDM path repeats this at lines 169–174. Therefore, writing \(\Vert\) for list concatenation, \[H_{\rm train}^{+}=H\Vert[\widetilde d],
 \qquad H_{\rm infer}^{+}=H\Vert[d,\widetilde d].\] An earlier selected correction can add an extra raw row to a later inference PCA. Because coordinates are learned in a history-dependent basis, this is a substantive correspondence discrepancy.

This demonstrates that the extra row can change the correction. It is not a reproduction of PyTorch’s randomized PCA or a claim that this synthetic history occurred in the published image runs.

## Limits and correction

The first selected correction is unaffected by a previous selected-step mutation; a configuration with only one selected correction need not encounter this discrepancy at all. Some histories make the extra row immaterial. No historical commit binding, saved trajectories or independently recomputed FID results are available.

A local repair is to construct a local augmented history or pass a copy during inference, retaining each actual update direction once. No source was modified and no executed patch is certified. These limitations support a release-specific Minor finding.
