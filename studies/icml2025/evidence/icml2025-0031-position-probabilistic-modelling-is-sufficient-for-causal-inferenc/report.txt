# 2. Definitions and assumptions used in the checks

For the aspirin model, let U=log Z, V=log T and W=log Y. The original model has positive Z,T,Y, independent Gaussian log-noises, a,b>0, and parameters treated as fixed in the conditional-mean calculation. Write sZ=Var(U), sT=Var(log epsilon_T), sY=Var(log epsilon_Y). In the counterexamples all three variances are strictly positive. The model implies V=aU+E_T and W=(b-ac)U-cE_T+E_Y, with U N(muZ,sZ), E_T N(0,sT), E_Y N(0,sY).

An atomic intervention fixes a treatment value. A stochastic parentless intervention replaces the treatment kernel by a distribution g(t) independent of its former parents; it need not make treatment independent of its descendants. For Problem 2 the graph is an acyclic two-node DAG T -> Y, all probabilities are positive, and the outcome mechanism is invariant. This exactly satisfies the paper’s stated parentless-intervention conditions.

The checks distinguish representability from identification: defining a cross-setting joint distribution does not identify that distribution from observational data alone. The paper itself makes this distinction in Appendices F, H and J.1. A posterior can retain prior dependence in a nonidentified problem.

# 3. Problem 1: incorrect log-normal conditional mean

Source claim and location. Section 2.1, p. 2, gives a special-case expression for E[Y|T] when c=0. Appendix B.2, p. 14, Eq. (9), gives its general expression. The latter uses the coefficient [a(b-c)sZ-c sT]/[sZ+c^2 sT+sY] multiplying (T-a muZ). The preceding Gaussian conditioning identity on p. 14 and the covariance matrix in Eq. (8) on p. 14 instead imply a different coefficient multiplying log T. Source pages 2 and 14 were rendered and inspected; this is not a text-extraction artifact [S1, E1].

Suspect step. Substitution from Eq. (8) into the immediately preceding correct Gaussian conditioning formula changes both the covariance numerator and the conditioning variance, and drops the logarithm. Define the following quantities directly from the stated model:

\[D=a^2s_Z+s_T,\quad C=a(b-ac)s_Z-cs_T,\quad H=(b-ac)^2s_Z+c^2s_T+s_Y,\quad \beta=C/D.\] \[\mathbb E[Y\mid T=t]=\exp\!\left((b-ac)\mu_Z+\frac{C}{D}(\log t-a\mu_Z)+\frac12\left(H-\frac{C^2}{D}\right)\right),\quad t>0.\] Complete analytic check. Since (V,W) is jointly Gaussian, E[W|V=log t]=(b-ac)muZ+(C/D)(log t-a muZ) and Var(W|V)=H-C^2/D. The log-normal expectation is the exponential of this conditional mean plus half the conditional variance. This gives the displayed correction without an approximation. D>0 in the stated nondegenerate model.

Assumption-satisfying counterexample. Take a=b=2, c=1, muZ=0, sZ=sT=sY=1. All original positivity and independence conditions hold; doses 1 and 2 both lie in the strictly positive support. Then D=5, C=-1, H=2 and H-C^2/D=9/5. Thus E[Y|T=t]=exp(0.9)t^(-0.2). The correct means at t=1 and t=2 are 2.4596031112 and 2.1412088739, with ratio 0.8705505633. Eq. (9)’s printed coefficient is 1/3; its ratio is exp(1/3)=1.3956124251. Any additive exponent constant independent of T cancels, so no interpretation of ’const.’ repairs this reversal of the trend [E2].

The p. 2 special case is independently wrong even without a trend reversal. For a=b=1, c=0, muZ=0 and all variances 1, the correct mean ratio between doses 4 and 1 is 2; the printed expression gives exp(3/2)=4.4816890703. In general the c=0 coefficient should be ab sZ/(a^2 sZ+sT), multiplying log t, not t.

Alternative readings and counterevidence. Reading T as an already logged variable conflicts with Eq.(1) and the explicit log T in the preceding derivation, and does not repair the coefficient. Eq.(8) and the general Gaussian-conditioning identity remain correct. At c=0, the corrected mean still increases with dose for a,b>0, preserving the qualitative confounding example. At c>0, the paper claims the possibility of positive association, not that every allowed parameter setting has it; the checked reversal does not refute that existential statement. Eq.(4)’s fixed-dose intervention mean also remains valid.

Calibrated judgment: Major at the claim level. The abstract identifies concrete examples as the paper’s demonstration; Section2 introduces this as its running example, and Section2.1/p.3 explicitly invokes Eq.(9) to explain observational versus interventional response. The wrong quantitative function, including a reversed trend for allowed fixed parameters, is therefore a failed derivation closely tied to the core explanatory contribution. The reversal is established for the saved c=1 example, not the c=0 specialization or the paper’s plotted parameters. The existing analytic replacement remains verified. No figure or empirical effect is claimed; the previously checked later version retains the error [S2].

# 4. Problem 2: stochastic intervention marginal confused with a conditional

Source claim and location. Appendix C.1, p. 17, Eq. (35), and Appendix J, p. 25, Eq. (37), assert q(x_y)=q(x_y|x_t) when discussing interventions whose new parent set is empty. Both explicitly extend the result beyond atomic interventions to any such intervention. The parent-adjustment sum that follows is a conditional treatment response; it is not the unconditional outcome under a nondegenerate random treatment [S1, E1].

Suspect step. Removing incoming edges to T makes T independent of its former parents, but it does not remove the edge T -> Y. Therefore it does not imply q(Y)=q(Y|T=t). An atomic treatment is constant, which is why the equality is valid at the assigned value in that special case.

Complete finite check. Let the original graph be T -> Y. Use p(T=1)=1/2 and unchanged outcome probabilities p(Y=1|T=0)=1/5 and p(Y=1|T=1)=4/5. Intervene with g(T=1)=1/4 and no parents. All four original joint cells and all four intervened joint cells have strictly positive mass. There is no hidden confounder, no cycle, no support problem, and the sole original outcome mechanism is preserved.

T | Y | Intervened joint probability q(T,Y)

0 | 0 | 3/5

0 | 1 | 3/20

1 | 0 | 1/20

1 | 1 | 1/5

\[q(Y=1)=\frac34\frac15+\frac14\frac45=\frac7{20},\qquad q(Y=1\mid T=1)=\frac45\ne\frac7{20}.\] Repair. Under the stated valid parent-adjustment setting and positivity on the treatment values used, the fixed-treatment response is obtained first. A stochastic policy then requires averaging over its treatment distribution:

\[q(y)=\sum_t g(t)\sum_z p(y\mid t,z)p(z).\] Here z denotes the original parents of T; for the two-node counterexample this parent set is empty. Summing the four displayed cells verifies the repair exactly. Alternatively, the paper can restrict the equalities in Eqs. (35) and (37) to the atomic case at its assigned treatment. These are two precise repairs, with different scope.

Calibrated judgment: Major at the claim level. Section2.3, p.5, defines general kernel replacement in Eq.(5)/Figure5 and directs readers to Appendix J for adjustment shortcuts. Appendix J expressly asks for q(y) and extends the atomic result to every empty-parent intervention; Appendix C.1’s proof establishes the conditional response only. This failed generalization drops the policy weights and answers a different query from the advertised stochastic marginal. The two-node example also satisfies Y not being a parent of T, and uses positive joint cells and an unchanged outcome kernel. Reading the notation as a family of atomic outcomes would require withdrawing the explicit stochastic extension.

Counterevidence and scope. Eq.(5)’s general stochastic joint factorization remains valid. Appendix I Eq.(36) identifies the treatment-conditional response on the intervention support. Under an atomic intervention, the marginal equals that conditional at the assigned treatment value only; the main fixed-dose aspirin calculation survives. Restoring the outer policy average repairs the stochastic result. Neither this repair’s simplicity nor the survival of the broad expressivity thesis downgrades the failed core generalization. The previously checked arXiv text retains it [S2]; no empirical policy consequence is asserted.

# 5. Dependence, counterevidence and review limits

The two findings are independent: Problem 1 concerns a Gaussian-to-log-normal substitution; Problem 2 concerns marginalization over a stochastic intervention. Neither requires the other. Their correct replacements use the same probability framework advocated by the paper.

Scope clarification, not an additional error finding: Definition 3.2, pp. 7-8, uses full endogenous evidence X=x, whereas Appendix N.1 considers partial evidence (T,Y). Partial evidence can induce dependencies among originally independent noises, so their full posterior joint must be retained. Appendix N.1 does retain that joint and applies interventions to structural equations. A partial-evidence collider example is therefore not an assumption-satisfying refutation of the definition’s literal full-X formulation. The same-G Bayesian-network wording warrants care, but no failed counterfactual result was demonstrated.

The partial-noise-sharing query in Section 3 and the all-noise-sharing SCM query in Appendix N have different model assumptions. The paper identifies that difference. For full sharing, Y*=Y(T/T*)^c follows by eliminating Z^b epsilon_Y; an independent finite illustration agrees. This is an analytic identity plus a finite illustration, not an empirical validation.

No explicit erratum or dedicated author-code link was located in the examined publisher/arXiv records and scoped searches.
