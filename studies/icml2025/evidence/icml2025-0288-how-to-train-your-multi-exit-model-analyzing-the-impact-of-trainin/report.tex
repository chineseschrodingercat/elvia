Abstract: The supplied paper and public source are reviewed for metric validity, training-regime semantics, evaluation, and numerical conclusions. A dimensional error in the released mutual-information estimator invalidates a core component of the proposed explanatory framework: constant representations receive nonzero or negative scores, and adding an unused coordinate changes the score. This supports Major for that released metric, with historical Figure 5 provenance unresolved. A separate Minor finding corrects overbroad comparisons of alternative regimes. The displayed main accuracy results substantially support the paper’s qualified medium/high-budget recommendation. Scientific adjudication remains pending.\par

\section*{Background and evidentiary distinctions}
Disjoint training first trains the backbone and then freezes it while training internal classifiers (ICs). Joint training optimizes backbone and ICs together from initialization. Mixed training first trains the backbone and then jointly optimizes the complete multi-exit model. The paper proposes gradient alignment, mode connectivity, numerical rank, and mutual information as explanatory tools in addition to accuracy–cost comparisons.\par
A defect in the released metric is distinct from proof that its current bytes generated a historical figure. Likewise, a counterexample to a causal interpretation does not refute the measured accuracy. These distinctions matter here: the inspected basic regime and offline routing implementations are consistent with their declared roles, while the information calculation has an exact mathematical defect.\par
\section*{Substantive findings}
Problem 1 (The released mutual-information score mixes scalar and vector densities).  Assessment: Major for the released core framework metric; historical figure impact needs evidence.\par
\subsection*{Claim, location, and dependency}
Section 3.4, pp. 3–4, defines \(I(X;Z)\) and says it is estimated by Monte Carlo sampling. Figure 5 and pp. 5–6 use the layerwise values to explain information retention, compression, and which training regime is suitable for easy or difficult data. This is an explicit component of the paper’s proposed analytical contribution.\par
In `mutual\_information.py`, line 217 computes a one-dimensional Gaussian log density at zero; line 302 sums feature log densities to obtain a \(d\)-dimensional joint density; line 239 subtracts the latter from the former. Lines 255 and 273 repeat the mismatch for the conditional calculation. The logged unconditional score at line 187 averages values over 14 bandwidths. These are source statements at the pinned revision [code]; numbered excerpts and hashes are preserved in .\par
\subsection*{Complete counterexample and invariant failure}
Let every sampled representation be the same vector \(z_0\in\mathbb R^d\), with \(d=2\), and let the positive kernel standard deviation be \(\sigma=1\), one of the released bandwidths. Any number of source samples and either sampling seed are admissible: every reference and evaluation vector is \(z_0\). The true information is \[I(X;Z)=0,\qquad Z=z_0.\] It is also zero if independent Gaussian observation noise is added to this constant vector.\par
Equation reference: eq:mi. Write \(c_\sigma=\log\phi_\sigma(0)=-\log(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})\). In the released Monte Carlo branch, the mixture of identical \(d\)-dimensional kernels has log density \(dc_\sigma\) at every evaluation vector. The scalar conditional-density term is only \(c_\sigma\). Thus the returned score is exactly \[
\widehat I_{\rm release}=(1-d)c_\sigma
=(d-1)\log(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}).\] For \(d=2,\sigma=1\), this is \(0.9189385332\) nats instead of zero. At the also-supported \(\sigma=0.01\), it is \(-3.6862316528\) nats. The Jensen branch has the same values because all components coincide. Random sampling cannot explain the discrepancy: the result is identical for every draw.\par
The actual bandwidth list is \[.01,\ .1,\ .25,\ .5,\ .75,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 6,\ 9,\ 12,\ 15,\ 18,\ 21.\] Averaging [eq:mi] (\#eq:mi) over that list gives \(1.4642039381\) for a constant two-dimensional representation and \(2926.9436723553\) for a constant 2000-dimensional representation. These are independent scalar calculations in /, not author-code execution.\par
More generally, append an identically zero coordinate to any representation. The true information obeys \[I(X;(Z,0))=I(X;Z).\] The released joint log density acquires \(c_\sigma\), while its scalar numerator is unchanged, so the reported score increases by \(-c_\sigma\). This holds for arbitrary source vectors and either log-mixture branch. Averaged over the released bandwidths, each unused coordinate adds \(1.4642039381\) to the score. If the appended coordinate instead carries independent Gaussian noise in a noisy-channel interpretation, it likewise adds no true information. Thus an apparent layerwise change can be manufactured by adding or removing unused coordinates, without changing predictions or information content.\par
\subsection*{Counterevidence and historical boundary}
A kernel-based surrogate can be useful, and finite-sample estimates need not obey every population inequality exactly. Neither observation excuses this deterministic dimension mismatch. Calling the output a bound also requires a valid derivation: the displayed score is not justified as the paper’s \(I(X;Z)\), and the invariant failure is not sampling uncertainty.\par
However, the current standard early-exit entry point comments out `setup\_mi` at `methods/early\_exit.py`:654. Default MI flags and even the named MI example’s flags are false; enabling them without setup would access uninitialized analysis objects. No Figure 5 run file binds the figure to the current revision. The report therefore does not claim that the historical plotted values equal the defective current output. The plot’s exact provenance and estimator must be supplied.\par
\subsection*{Impact and precise repair scope}
The invalid computation directly affects a core analytical metric and its proposed evidentiary use for choosing training regimes. Major applies to that demonstrated released computation, not to an inferred change in the accuracy tables. Establishing that those tables lose their advantage is unnecessary to establish the metric defect.\par
Equation reference: eq:repair. Multiplying the scalar term by \(d\) removes this dimension mismatch but does not by itself make evaluation at noiseless feature centers an estimator of the specified mutual information. A complete repair must declare the random variable. For example, for an empirical uniform input distribution and the changed, noisy representation \(\widetilde Z=f(X)+\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_d)\), the valid target is \[
\mathbb E_{i,\varepsilon}\left[
\log\phi_{\sigma,d}(\varepsilon)-
\log\left\{\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n
\phi_{\sigma,d}(f(x_i)+\varepsilon-f(x_j))\right\}\right].\] This definition gives zero for a constant map, and an additional independent noise coordinate cancels in the likelihood ratio. It is not the unsmoothed deterministic estimand. Its statistical implementation, bandwidth choice, and any repaired Figure 5 have not been verified. The associated compression-based interpretation requires re-evaluation.\par
Problem 2 (Alternative-regime comparisons overstate the displayed results).  Assessment: Minor.\par
\subsection*{Original comparison and exact contrary cases}
Appendix A, p. 13, says alternating training consistently falls short of both mixed and joint training, then says mixed-gradual outperforms mixed. The paper’s own results contradict these unqualified comparisons.\par
Table 10, p. 14, reports ViT-T/CIFAR-100 accuracy at the 100\% budget of \(68.39\pm0.78\) for alternating and \(66.49\pm1.08\) for joint; mixed is \(70.25\pm0.56\). At 75\%, alternating is \(67.81\) versus joint’s \(66.22\). More directly, Table 14, p. 15, gives STS-B MSE at 100\% of \(0.50\) for alternating, \(0.52\) for joint, and \(0.51\) for mixed: lower is better. At 75\%, alternating is \(0.51\) versus \(0.53\) for each comparator. These are comparisons of reported means, not newly claimed significance tests.\par
Mixed-gradual also fails to dominate mixed: Table 13 gives 20-Newsgroups accuracy \(81.35\pm1.24\) versus \(85.25\pm0.64\) at each of 50\%, 75\%, and 100\%. Lower-budget ResNet-34 results provide further exceptions. Original page images verify the signs, labels, and metric directions.\par
\subsection*{Correction, counterevidence, and effect}
The defensible comparison is task- and budget-dependent. Alternating trails mixed in the cited CIFAR-100 cases but can exceed joint; it also has lower displayed STS-B MSE than both at high budgets. Mixed-gradual improves several high-budget vision/regression results but performs worse on the cited classification setting. The tables disclose these exceptions, and the main paper’s more qualified recommendation remains well supported. The flaw is a local comparative conclusion, with no demonstrated reversal of the central mixed-versus-joint empirical trend.\par
\section*{Positive checks and issues requiring evidence}
\subsection*{Regimes, routing, and the central empirical trend}
Static inspection verifies that the main regime configuration distinguishes backbone pretraining, joint optimization, and frozen-backbone IC training. The frozen-backbone path disables its gradients and places its core modules in evaluation mode. Early stopping uses the designated validation loader, with per-head patience tracking.\par
The offline evaluator obtains all head predictions, chooses the first head exceeding a confidence threshold, falls back to the final head, and averages cumulative head FLOPs using exit counts. The table generator selects the last threshold before averaged cost exceeds the specified budget; it does not maximize accuracy over all feasible thresholds. Consequently occasional lower accuracy at a larger budget is possible. Labels enter scoring, not the inspected confidence-routing or cost choice. No test-label threshold optimization was demonstrated.\par
The independent check independently recomputed Table 1: mixed exceeds joint in 19 of 24 displayed means, including 17 of 18 at 50–100\% budgets and only two of six at 25\%. This supports the paper’s qualified medium/high-budget recommendation. The one medium/high exception is MSDNet at 50\%, \(72.01\) versus \(72.02\). Standard deviations in the appendix and explicit exceptions for disjoint at high budgets provide important counterevidence to universal-superiority readings.\par
\subsection*{Gradient alignment and causal explanation}
The defined gradient-dominance score is a cosine, and the released implementation calculates that cosine. It need not rank influence by magnitude or projected contribution. For a concrete realizable diagnostic, use shared representation \(\theta=0\in\mathbb R^2\), three bias-free binary-logit heads \(w_i^\top\theta\), label zero, and cross-entropy loss. Set \(w_i=2g_i\) with \[g_1=(.001,0),\quad g_2=(1,100),\quad g_3=(1,-100).\] At \(\theta=0\), each backbone gradient is \(g_i\) and every head-weight gradient is zero. The total is \(G=(2.001,0)\). Cosines are \(1\) for \(g_1\) and approximately \(0.0099995\) for the others, yet \(g_2\cdot G=g_3\cdot G=2.001\), a thousand times \(g_1\cdot G=0.002001\). Common averaging of losses does not change this ordering. Identity intermediate layers can realize the early-exit depths without changing this check.\par
This establishes that alignment alone cannot identify the largest causal update contribution. Section 3.1 says potentially dominates, and scaling ablations provide additional qualitative evidence. No reversal of the actual Figure 2 influence ranking was established. The causal explanation therefore needs norm/contribution measurements or a more targeted intervention; it is not an additional demonstrated Major.\par
\subsection*{Other bounded reproducibility and interpretation limits}
The current scaling code deserves clarification. Its SDN coefficient is \[\min\left(c_i,\ .01+(t+1)\frac{c_i}{t+1}\right)=c_i\] at every step. The active GE branch gives internal-head coefficients \(1/(K+1)\) and final-head coefficient \(2/(K+1)\) on the shared backbone for \(K\) total heads, before common loss normalization. The independent check independently derived and checked these coefficients. The commented earlier branch differs. Whether these are the intended reference methods, and whether Table 5 used them, needs exact version/configuration evidence; the present ablation script names CIFAR-100 whereas Table 5 concerns TinyImageNet. No historical table corruption is inferred.\par
Equation (5)’s rank of an activation matrix differs from the released rank of its centered covariance with a relative eigenvalue threshold. A constant nonzero representation has activation rank one and covariance rank zero. This needs an explicit metric convention before interpreting capacity. Likewise, a high-loss straight interpolation does not rule out a low-loss curved connection: \((x^2+y^2-1)^2\) has a zero-loss semicircle between \((1,0)\) and \((-1,0)\) but a straight-line midpoint loss of one. This is a logical diagnostic, not a counterexample instantiated in the paper’s networks. Figure 3 establishes its inspected section of the landscape, not global disconnectedness.\par
Table 11 labels as 35\% the same ResNet-50 means labeled 25\% in Tables 1 and 19. The correct budget needs run binding. Table 6’s broad head-size caption also needs a budget qualification: at 25\%, mixed increases from 44.03 with one layer to 44.92 or 44.61 with larger heads, although the higher-budget degradation is displayed. The reported 1166 versus 1610 epochs is 27.58\% fewer epochs; total training compute and wall-clock savings require full phase accounting.\par
A one-survivor threshold-deployment mask is squeezed to a scalar in `architectures/early\_exits/base.py`:124, raising a concrete indexing concern downstream. This path is separate from the paper’s offline evaluator. An independent tensor-shape probe could not run because `torch` is absent in both available Python environments; no package was installed. The concern is retained as static, unverified runtime evidence. The table generator also leaves `result\_valid` uninitialized or stale when no threshold crosses a budget; no effect on the published finite-budget columns was established.\par
\section*{Overall impact and conclusion}
The released information estimator cannot support the information-theoretic interpretation as written. Its connection to historical figures, the intended estimand, and a corrected analysis need evidence. The alternative-regime discussion needs a local scope correction. The main accuracy comparisons, basic regime semantics, and offline threshold routing survive the bounded checks performed; no complete experimental replication is claimed. No later erratum or author adoption was supplied.\par
