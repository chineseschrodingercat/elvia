Abstract: Two bounded discrepancies are established. First, the stated negligible auxiliary-branch overhead is inconsistent with the paper’s own compressed-baseline accounting: adding the low-rank branch increases equivalent operations by about 134.6% and effective parameters by about 35.0%. The full-model compression ratios remain arithmetically consistent. Second, the text initializes the low-rank branch before the sparse branch, whereas Figure 3 and the pinned release use the reverse order. A finite matrix calculation demonstrates that the orders do not generally agree. Both findings are assessed Minor; no historical image-quality degradation or measured hardware slowdown is established. The rank/frequency explanation is treated with explicit empirical scope.

# Summary, scope, and notation

The method augments a binary matrix branch (BMB) with a full-precision low-rank branch (LRMB) and a sparse branch (SMB). The review inspected the main scientific text, Eqs. (1)–(12), Tables 1–3, figure descriptions, the complete released supplement, and relevant source. Dense qualitative-image claims were not independently scored, and training or restoration was not rerun.

For a weight matrix \(W\), write \(L_r(W)\) for its truncated rank-\(r\) SVD approximation and \(T_k(W)\) for the matrix retaining its \(k\) largest-magnitude entries. The paper uses rank \(r=8\) and \(k=2\max(C_{\rm in},C_{\rm out})\) in the described configuration. The branch costs are reported as effective parameter counts and equivalent operations, using the paper’s bit-precision accounting convention.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Auxiliary overhead is substantial relative to the binary baseline).  Assessment: Minor. The abstract and the end of §3.5, p. 5, describe the auxiliary storage/computation overhead as ignorable. Eq. (2), p. 4, compares full-precision low-rank storage \(Br(m+n)\) with binary storage \(mnB'\), where \(B'=1\) and \(B\) is 16 or 32.

The exact storage ratio in this simple matrix model is \[\frac{Br(m+n)}{mnB'}.\] For a square matrix and \(B'=1\), it is \(2Br/n\). Merely describing \(r\) as small relative to the matrix width does not give a numerically negligible ratio at finite dimensions; the precision factor remains relevant.

The decisive internal evidence is Table 3(a), p. 8, visually checked in the original:

Configuration | Equivalent operations (G) | Effective parameters (M)

BMB | 0.78 | 3.69

BMB + LRMB | 1.83 | 4.98

BMB + LRMB + SMB | 1.83 | 4.98

The low-rank branch’s increments are therefore \[\frac{1.83-0.78}{0.78}=1.3461538\ldots,\qquad
\frac{4.98-3.69}{3.69}=0.3495935\ldots.\] These are approximately 134.6% of the binary baseline’s operations and 35.0% of its effective parameters. LRMB accounts for about 57.4% of the resulting operation budget. These are calculations from rounded published totals, not newly measured costs. Parent and auxiliary output bindings are and .

## Counterevidence and correction

The paper openly reports these totals, and the rank ablation explicitly recognizes the effect of rank on complexity. SMB produces no visible increment at the table’s precision. Relative to the full-precision baseline, the LRMB increment can be small; that is a plausible intended interpretation of acceptable overhead.

The overall reductions in Table 2 remain arithmetically consistent: \[50.23/1.83=27.4480874\ldots,\qquad
118.59/4.98=23.8132530\ldots.\] Thus the finding does not invalidate the reported total compression advantage or show a restoration-quality failure. The appropriate correction is to state the baseline: auxiliary costs are small relative to the full-precision backbone but substantial relative to BMB. They must be included in compressed-model comparisons. No empirical repair is required for this arithmetic clarification, and no author adoption is known.

Problem 2 (The text and figure/release specify opposite initialization orders).  Assessment: Minor. Main §3.4, pp. 4–5, Eqs. (5)–(8), first extracts a rank-\(r\) approximation, then selects sparse entries from its residual. Figure 3, p. 4, instead extracts sparse entries before taking the low-rank approximation. Parent visual inspection confirms the diagram’s ordering.

Equation reference: eq:textorder, eq:releaseorder. The text specifies \[

R_{\rm text}=W-L_{\rm text}-S_{\rm text}.\] The release and diagram specify \[

## Independent finite calculation

The operations \(L_r\) and \(T_k\) do not commute. To check a nontrivial scale compatible with the stated rank and sparse-entry rule, construct a \(160\times160\) matrix \[W_{ij}=\sin((i+1)(j+2))+0.03(i+1)+0.007(j+1)
       +\operatorname{diag}(160,159,\ldots,1)_{ij},\] with zero-based \(i,j\), \(r=8\) and \(k=320\). Separate parent and auxiliary NumPy calculations implement the two mathematical decompositions without importing author code. The resulting Frobenius differences are \[\begin{array}{lr}
\|L_{\rm text}-L_{\rm rel}\|_F&434.6541495\\
\|S_{\rm text}-S_{\rm rel}\|_F&420.9855802\\
\|R_{\rm text}-R_{\rm rel}\|_F&135.1607553.
\end{array}\] Both unquantized sums reconstruct \(W\), to residual norms \(0\) and \(5.62\times10^{-14}\). Equality of the unquantized sum therefore does not imply equality of the branch parameters or residuals.

For an additional weight-level diagnostic, quantize the residual using per-row mean-absolute scaling, \[Q(R)_{ij}=\left(\frac1{160}\sum_{\ell}|R_{i\ell}|\right)
                    \operatorname{sign}(R_{ij}).\] Then \[\|L_{\rm text}+S_{\rm text}+Q(R_{\rm text})
 -L_{\rm rel}-S_{\rm rel}-Q(R_{\rm rel})\|_F
=106.0913795\ldots.\] The full matrix definition, settings and outputs are saved in and . This is a synthetic weight-decomposition calculation, not a full neural forward pass, trained-image test, or reconstruction of historical checkpoints. In particular, it does not bypass the separate activation quantization present in the real network.

## Counterevidence and correction

The release agrees with Figure 3. Both orders are coherent initialization algorithms; it would be inaccurate to say the code contradicts the whole paper. The ablations may reflect either particular historical implementation, and they are not invalidated by the finite example.

The demonstrated consequence is an underspecified initialization procedure whose two readings can yield different branch parameters and quantized weight approximations. To resolve it, specify one sequence consistently in text, equations, diagram and code, and identify the sequence used for the reported experiments. No retraining, historical checkpoint matching, or author-adopted correction was performed.

# Scope clarifications and excluded allegations

## Rank versus frequency

Low matrix rank alone does not imply low spatial frequency. For example, \(A_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\) on a \(16\times16\) grid is rank one, with all two-dimensional Fourier energy at the Nyquist pair. Two finite checks confirm both properties. A rank-one channel map can likewise transmit an oscillatory spatial signal.

This limits a universal interpretation of the rank/frequency explanation around Eqs. (5)–(7), but does not refute the measured tendency in trained branches. Main §4.4 and Figure 5 describe an empirical division of contributions and explicitly acknowledge high-frequency LRMB output in some layers. The original p. 8 image confirms this qualification. Historical branch-frequency distributions were not recomputed, so a stronger empirical conclusion remains Needs Evidence.

## Supporting checks

The paper’s operation convention divides binary operations by 64. The approximately \(27.4\times\) figure is consequently an equivalent-operation ratio, not an independently measured wall-clock acceleration. The released supplement, E.1–E.2 on supplement p. 3, explicitly states that practical hardware deployment has not been tested and lists it as future work [supplement]. The independent check read that passage. No hardware-speed failure is alleged.

Eq. (10)’s XNOR/popcount shorthand omits the explicit signed-dot conversion \(2\,\mathrm{popcount}-\mathrm{fan\_in}\). However, Figure 2 includes postprocessing and the inspected release uses signed, scaled floating convolution/linear operations. This is counterevidence against asserting an actual missing-popcount-correction implementation bug. The full hardware operation-count implementation was not audited.

## Empirical results

Tables 1–3, supplement training-time data and qualitative-comparison descriptions were examined for the stated comparisons and scope. No raw metric recomputation, data audit, confidence assessment, image restoration, or full model inference was performed. The random-binary-rank discussion in supplement B is a heuristic for trained weights; its independence assumptions were not established empirically. No additional core theorem failure is asserted from that heuristic alone.

# Overall impact and state

The highest supported assessment is Minor. The auxiliary-overhead comparison needs a clear denominator and the initialization specification needs reconciliation. The arithmetic total compression advantages survive; image-quality and practical timing consequences remain unresolved.
