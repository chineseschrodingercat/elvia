# 2. Problem 1: Prior paraphrasing experiment omitted

F28-01 — Minor; high confidence. Section 4, p. 7, under “Paraphrasing Prompt Sensitivity,” states that none of Mireshghallah et al. (2023), Ghalebikesabi et al. (2024), or Shao et al. (2024) accounted for prompt variation. The cited Ghalebikesabi paper provides a direct counterexample in its historical arXiv:2408.02373v1 version, dated 5 August 2024, before the reviewed paper.

In Operationalizing Contextual Integrity in Privacy-Conscious Assistants, Section 4.2 (PDF p. 6) varies form titles, descriptions and field wording. Section 5 (pp. 8–9) analyzes robustness across paraphrased forms; Figure 5 (p. 9) plots within-form-group variation in privacy leakage and utility. Appendix C.2 (pp. 33–34) gives paraphrasing instructions. These forms enter the assistant’s decision input. The page-9 figure and caption were visually inspected, and the exact historical PDF was archived with SHA-256 `6a6764795f9d19ac2cb7b87cc4655388c5d7550fc7eb4ee317a85bd009320c0c`.

The strongest alternative reading is that the reviewed authors meant variation only in a fixed instruction or assessment template, excluding the wording of form inputs. Section 4 does not state that restriction. The finding therefore concerns the unrestricted published assertion, not a claim that all possible prompt dimensions were previously tested. It is not a missing-citation complaint: the counterexample is already cited.

Supported impact and repair: replace the blanket statement with an explicit acknowledgement that Ghalebikesabi et al. tested form paraphrasing, then identify any distinct instruction-template variation left untested. The assertion of uniformly absent paraphrasing controls must be narrowed. This does not establish adequate coverage of position bias, validate every CI-tenet classification, reproduce the prior experiment, or overturn the social-theoretical position. No Major issue is supported.

# 3. Core argument and candidate objections

Claim / location | Check and result

Four CI tenets; Section 2, pp. 2–3 | Consistent with Nissenbaum’s 2019 primary account: appropriate flows, contextual norms, parameterized flows, and ethical assessment of departures. No Issue Found in this conceptual correspondence.

Legal rules as privacy norms; Section 3 and Table 2, p. 8 | The heading alone could appear absolute. Table 2 and Section 5 explicitly allow institutions that reflect contextual values. A claim that the paper prohibits all legal embodiments of norms is not supported.

Partial use of CI; Section 5, p. 8 | The paper acknowledges useful applications of parts of the framework with limited claims. This qualification avoids interpreting all partial implementations as intrinsically worthless.

ConfAIde flow representation; Section 3 / Table 1 | The targeted 2024 primary version acknowledges five CI parameters but uses a three-factor Tier-2 template. The reviewed critique has a concrete target; no additional verified error was established.

Experimental hygiene; Section 4, p. 7 | Separating repeated-prompt, paraphrasing and option-position variation is useful. F28-01 affects the literature summary for the second dimension; the other universal negatives were not exhaustively verified.

Nissenbaum, Contextual Integrity Up and Down the Data Food Chain (2019), pp. 224, 227 and 231–234, supports the distinction between descriptive departures from prevailing norms and ethically illegitimate flows. It also permits partial use of the framework. The reviewed paper’s qualified discussion is compatible with this account. This is a conceptual consistency check, not an empirical test of privacy protection or a legal compliance opinion.

# Scope and limitations

Coverage includes the complete reviewed PDF and targeted checks of two cited LLM studies plus the primary CI account. It does not independently audit all nine rows and all four tenet labels in Table 1. Claims about every prior study’s lack of position-bias controls therefore remain Needs Evidence for an exhaustive verification. No new empirical experiment, equation-based performance claim, or author implementation is supplied by this position paper; no executable author code was required to test F28-01, and none was run.

The evidence bundle preserves publisher and arXiv metadata, primary-source PDFs/HTML, acquisition hashes, extracted text and page images. is reviewer-authored and only extracts, checks and renders public documents. Its successful check establishes the presence and historical date of the counterexample, not the correctness of the reported experimental measurements. OpenReview discussion and a complete all-study robustness audit remain unavailable or unperformed.
