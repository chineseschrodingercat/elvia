\section*{2. Claims, definitions and operative scope}
DynaMind combines frame abstraction, temporal prediction of dynamic representations and action generation. Section 3 and Appendices C.1-C.3 were checked against each other; the evaluation protocols, Tables 1-9 and Figures 3-17 were read for quantitative consistency and claim scope. The coverage ledger distinguishes calculation, source inspection and unresolved implementation details. References were read as context; a full novelty audit was not performed.\par
Let e\_t denote an encoded image, q a frozen DistilBERT language embedding, F\_theta the two-layer sigmoid FrameScorer, and C the window length. For a defined nonempty window I\_n with positive total weight, the pooling rule is\par
\[w_t=F_\theta(e_t),\qquad h_n=\frac{\sum_{t\in I_n}w_t e_t}{\sum_{t\in I_n}w_t},\qquad \mathcal{L}_{fs}=\mathcal{L}_{consistency}+\mathcal{L}_{saliency}.\] Finite sigmoid logits give strictly positive weights in exact arithmetic. The two auxiliary losses use cosine similarity and a zero-margin relative-similarity hinge, with equal weights. The temporal transformer conditions on q and historical dynamics; the action transformer uses visual/action history and a goal representation. This report does not add a universal anti-collapse, causal-identification or closed-loop stability theorem to these empirical design claims.\par
The actual training graph, masking implementation, sequence-to-vector cosine reduction, final partial-window policy, checkpoints, per-seed outputs and raw trial logs were not available. No author code, model execution, paid API, installation or robot experiment was used. Reviewer arithmetic below is finite and independent.\par
\section*{3. Problem 1 - incompatible FrameScorer training prescriptions}
Faithful source claims and exact locations. Section 3.1, p.4, immediately after Eq.(5), says that downstream modules receive fixed dynamic inputs and do not influence optimization of F. Appendix C.1, p.13, in its second paragraph on behavioral grounding, explicitly says the FrameScorer importance scores are optimized through the downstream action loss, with loss weight 1.0. Both passages describe the same FrameScorer. Source pages 4 and 13 were visually checked [E1].\par
Suspect step and complete check. These descriptions prescribe different derivatives with respect to the scorer parameters theta. Detachment yields no action-loss gradient through the pooled dynamics. The appendix’s differentiable route includes a chain-rule term. For one window, holding the image embeddings fixed,\par
\[\left.\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{action}\right|_{\mathrm{detached}}=0,\qquad
\left.\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{action}\right|_{\mathrm{connected}}
=J_\theta(H)^\top \nabla_H \mathcal{L}_{action},\qquad
\frac{\partial h_n}{\partial w_j}=\frac{e_j-h_n}{\sum_{t\in I_n}w_t}.\] A small exact computational-graph illustration makes the distinction explicit. Take C=2, scalar nonzero embeddings e\_1=1 and e\_2=3, sigmoid logits z\_1=z\_2=0, hence w\_1=w\_2=1/2, and a differentiable downstream scalar loss L=h\textasciicircum{}2. Then h=2, dh/dw\_1=-1, dw\_1/dz\_1=1/4, and dL/dz\_1=-1 through the connected route, versus zero through detachment. The denominator is positive, both weights are admissible and every derivative is exact. This illustrates non-equivalent gradient prescriptions; it is not presented as a realization of a trained action transformer or as a counterexample to the reported task scores [E2].\par
Counterevidence and alternative readings. The end of Section 3.1 also describes broader end-to-end action supervision, which is compatible with the appendix’s route. Shared image encoders can receive action supervision while F is detached; that does not reconcile the appendix’s explicit claim about F itself. An unstated freeze/unfreeze schedule could reconcile the passages. The documented hybrid assignment changes the source of the goal token, and does not specify such a schedule. Incidental zero gradients on particular examples also do not make the two general training procedures identical.\par
Dependency and supported impact. FrameScorer parameters determine importance weights, which determine dynamic prediction targets and policy conditions. The conflict therefore matters to reproducing the scientific method and interpreting its behavioral grounding. It establishes a bounded method-specification inconsistency, classified Minor. It does not determine the implemented procedure or demonstrate invalid task scores. Repair requires the actual computational graph, detachment points and phase schedule, followed by consistent documentation. No training variant is adopted as a guessed correction.\par

\section*{5. Counterevidence and cleared interpretations}
Language conditioning. Equation (1) and Appendix C.1 define F(e\_t) as a frame-local function, without q as a current input. Language helps train the scorer and explicitly conditions temporal reasoning. Thus the score of the same encoded frame is not instruction-adaptive at inference under this specification, but the complete policy remains language-conditioned. No claim that it ignores instructions is supported.\par
Temporal abstraction and saliency. Pooling in Eq.(2) is invariant to a joint permutation of features and weights inside one window. The ordered window sequence and historical frame inputs still carry temporal information. The paper does not claim lossless or injective video representation. Likewise, Eq.(5)’s hinge can be zero at equal similarities; the surrounding p.4 discussion explicitly treats the losses as soft guidance. Degenerate admissible representations would not by themselves refute a stated universal collapse-prevention theorem, because none is given.\par
Future targets and inference. Figure 2, p.3, Sections 3.2-3.3, p.5, and Appendix C.2-C.3, p.14, separate future-frame supervision and early training goal assignment from inference using observed history and predicted goals. This does not demonstrate future-information leakage. Re-anchoring predictions to newly observed frames avoids recursively rolling out predicted latents; it does not prove that all policy/environment errors vanish. Exact masks and token-head alignment remain implementation questions.\par
