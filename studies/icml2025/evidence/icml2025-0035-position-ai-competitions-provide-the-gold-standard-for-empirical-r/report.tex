\section*{2. Definitions, assumptions, and review limits}
A submitted-prediction ensemble is any rule whose test-time information is the vector of submitted predictions. The permitted hypothesis class may contain methods absent from that vector. Population accuracy is the objective in the diagnostic example. Its probabilities are known exactly, so the calculation has no sampling error or numerical tolerance. The example tests an implication in the argument; it is not a reconstruction of an actual Kaggle competition.\par
Three distinctions guide the other checks: data becoming public is different from its demonstrated incorporation into a particular model; newly curated annotations are different from newly discovered facts; and a benchmark pipeline that can be refreshed is different from a dated record of actual refreshes. Novelty restrictions define a target evaluation distribution and need not invalidate every use of IID sampling. Normative recommendations are assessed as proposals, without converting them into universal statistical theorems.\par
No complete audit of entrants’ training data, private leaderboard feedback, original competition submissions, or benchmark releases was possible. The reported median of 1,400 forum messages and individual historical leakage incidents were not independently recomputed. Bibliography reading and selected source verification are not an exhaustive reference audit. These limits are itemized in coverage.json.\par
\section*{3. Problem 1 — ensemble saturation is not an optimality certificate}
Source and suspect step. Section 8, PDF p.9, left column, second paragraph, answers the objection that deadlines may exclude valuable work. It moves from the authors’ experience of little or no ensemble improvement to the conclusion that competitions extract nearly maximal signal within their constraints [S1]. The empirical observation may be accurate. The unsupported step is treating performance within the submitted prediction family as a bound on the larger permitted family.\par
Complete construction. Let X1 and X2 be independent Bernoulli(1/2) variables and let Y=X2. Both input coordinates are available to every competitor. Permit one-coordinate binary rules using either coordinate with the same simple model and input-access budget. The submitted rules are h1(X)=X1 and h2(X)=1-X1. The permitted but unsubmitted rule is g(X)=X2. There is no data leakage, access advantage, or more expensive model in g.\par
\[\begin{gathered}\Pr(X_1=a,X_2=b)=\tfrac14,\quad a,b\in\{0,1\},\qquad Y=X_2,\\ H_{\rm sub}(X)=(X_1,1-X_1),\qquad g(X)=X_2.\end{gathered}\]\par
Input (X1, X2) | Y | Submitted vector (h1,h2) | g\par
(0,0) | 0 | (0,1) | 0\par
(0,1) | 1 | (0,1) | 1\par
(1,0) | 0 | (1,0) | 0\par
(1,1) | 1 | (1,0) | 1\par
For either attainable prediction vector, the conditional label remains a fair bit. Thus every deterministic ensemble e(H\_sub), including a combination learned from separate labeled data, has population accuracy 1/2. This is true even for an oracle selecting the best such map. Randomized rules are mixtures of deterministic rules and retain accuracy 1/2. Adding any number of submitted models whose joint predictions depend only on X1 leaves the same information limitation.\par
\[\begin{gathered}\sup_e \Pr(e(H_{\rm sub}(X))=Y)=\tfrac12=\max_i\Pr(h_i(X)=Y),\\ \Delta_{\rm ensemble}=0,\qquad \Pr(g(X)=Y)=1.\end{gathered}\] Finite verification. independent\_check.py evaluates the four support points with exact rational arithmetic and enumerates all 16 binary lookup maps on the four possible binary prediction pairs. Only two pairs occur in this distribution, but checking all 16 maps covers every deterministic combination. Every map scores 1/2, both submitted models score 1/2, and g scores 1. All assertions passed under Python 3.13.5, with actual process exit code 0. The saved results are in verification/0035/independent\_check\_results.json.\par
Original-condition check. The paragraph supplies no formal model-class or search-coverage assumption. The example preserves its premises of available submissions, no improvement through aggregation, a common evaluation metric, and fixed competition constraints. It shows that those premises alone cannot imply nearly optimal attainable performance. It does not claim the actual competitions use this distribution, that entrants miss an obvious coordinate, or that their unsubmitted improvement is 50 percentage points.\par
Alternative readings and counterevidence. Diverse teams may provide useful practical evidence of broad search. That evidence still needs a connection to unexplored permitted methods. An ensemble that additionally accesses X2 and learns g has introduced a new information source and hypothesis; its success would not follow from earlier prediction-only saturation. The authors’ experiential observation remains unrefuted. Krogh and Vedelsby’s established ensemble analysis [S3] supplies related context about shared errors and prediction disagreement; its squared-loss identity is not used as a proof of this binary-accuracy example.\par
Calibrated dependency and judgment: Minor retained. Section8, p.9, uses the near-maximality inference to answer the objection that a deadline excludes valuable submissions. Because time bounds are part of the definition on p.1, this is a substantive failed rebuttal. However, Section1.1, p.2, locates the gold-standard rationale in anti-cheating practices and renewable tasks. Sections6.1-6.2, pp.7-8, describe contemporaneous comparisons and guarded/prospective test data; Section7, p.8, explicitly treats evaluations as point-in-time results. No method, metric, contamination-control mechanism or downstream recommendation is derived from exhaustive signal extraction. The demonstrated damage is consequently bounded to the unsupported reassurance about excluded approaches. This is a dependency-based Minor judgment, not one based on paper type, section placement, ease of repair or the general usefulness of competitions.\par
Alternative scope and supported repair. If competition constraints mean all permitted methods, the checked implication fails. If they mean only the submitted prediction family, saturation may describe that family but does not answer the deadline objection. Retain only the observed small gains among tested submissions and state that gains from unsubmitted permitted approaches are unbounded by that observation. A common cutoff supports contemporaneous comparison while exclusion of stronger approaches remains an unresolved tradeoff for renewed evaluations. This narrows the claim; it does not restore near-optimality or certify the categorical gold-standard recommendation. Actual missed gains and the authors’ historical ensemble record remain unmeasured here.\par
\section*{4. Other checked claims and evidence boundaries}
IID, novelty, and exposure (Sections 2-5). Finite IID samples need not preserve every realized correlation. Within-dialogue dependence does not prevent independently sampling entire reset interactions. A fixed exclusion region around training examples changes the evaluation target, while mere absence of exact duplicates need not. These are scope clarifications; the paper acknowledges that task similarity needs definition. Its immediate invalidation of exposed tests is explicitly a conservative evaluation policy. This review does not interpret it as proof that every exposed item has entered model parameters.\par
Dynamic benchmarks (Section 5.2, p.6). LiveBench v2, Sections 2.7, 6 and A.6 [S4], confirms that newly collected questions are withheld for one month, with a private subset. The source specifies monthly rather than weekly collection; cadence wording alone is not treated as a substantive issue. The cited SWE-bench v3 [S5] describes 2,294 instances and a continually updatable collection pipeline. Evidence of actual frequent test-set updates for that specific benchmark version remains missing. Capability to refresh cannot substitute for release history; no later SWE-bench variant is silently substituted.\par
Prospective ground truth (Section 6.2, p.7). CAFA’s annotation-after-submission design is supported. The stronger statement that answers were unavailable to any human or model needs a publication-date check, because annotation dates need not equal experimental discovery dates. A later organizer preprint [S6], Sections 2.4 and 3.3, explicitly distinguishes these events. It is a June 2026 version, not peer reviewed, and analyzes an expanded evaluation using later annotations. Its subset percentages cannot be assigned to the original December 2023 competition. No original-cohort item/publication crosswalk was established here; no entrant contamination is demonstrated. Status: Needs Evidence for the universal knowledge claim, with the paper’s own ’reasonably leak-proof’ qualification retained.\par
Other prospective mechanisms (Section 6.2, pp.7-8). The AIMO2 organizer notice [S7] confirms a new 110-problem set at approximately National Olympiad difficulty. Official indexed WSDM content [S8a] supports a submission freeze followed by forecasting on newly acquired data. Konwinski’s official description likewise supports post-freeze GitHub issue collection [S8b]. The exact three-month interval was not independently reconstructed from a dated rules snapshot. No individual problem originality audit or guarantee of total contamination absence is claimed.\par
OpenVaccine (Section 8, p.9). The original article [S9], pp.1177 and 1179, supports a 25\% private-test mean columnwise root mean squared error (MCRMSE) reduction against DegScore and blind validation on much longer 504-1,588-nucleotide molecules. Its three-week description is compatible with the position paper’s within-four-weeks wording. These concern RNA degradation prediction, not a measured vaccine shelf-life improvement or universal competition superiority. This positive example survives Problem 1.\par
Historical rankings and security. Primary abstracts [S10-S11] support the bounded observations of relative ranking stability and little detected leaderboard overfitting in the studied settings; absolute accuracy stability and universal immunity do not follow. Network isolation in Section 6.1 needs a full threat model covering returned scores and logs, but no actual competition exploit was established. The paper also acknowledges traditional leakage difficulty and requests ecological-validity studies. These are preserved qualifications, not additional proven defects.\par
