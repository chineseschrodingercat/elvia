Abstract: The printed grammar used for the central expressivity construction has no terminating production and generates the empty language, invalidating its key containment step. Adding an empty production repairs this specific proof dependency. Separately, the released symbolic-equivalence checker changes floor-division precedence and can accept a wrong answer to an example printed in the paper. These support Major findings for the formal construction and the released validation rule, respectively; neither establishes a change to published benchmark rankings. Local empirical wording inconsistencies are Minor. The finite-output circuit argument and the direction of the tabulated accuracy gains survive the checks. Adjudication remains pending.

# Necessary distinctions

Proposition 3.1 concerns a fixed finite output language. If its maximum word length is \(N\), generation takes at most \(N\) autoregressive steps. Under the cited constant-depth, log-precision forward-computation bound, composing a constant number of steps and finite-prefix masks preserves a constant-depth polynomial-size threshold circuit. The comparison to \(NL\) is conditional on the stated unproved complexity separation.

Proposition 3.3 is existential: for a machine \(M\), it selects a specially constructed simulator \(L_M\) and augmented grammar. It is not a guarantee for every pretrained LLM. Section 4 explicitly acknowledges this boundary; the p. 8 limitations explicitly acknowledge the finite-language restriction of Proposition 3.1. The practical CRANE algorithm is separately motivated and evaluated. Grammar membership alone does not verify the semantics of arbitrary reasoning traces.

# Demonstrated problems

Problem 1 (Major: the displayed augmented grammar generates no complete output).

Claim and exact location. Section 3.2, p. 5, constructs \[G_a\to R_M G,\qquad R_M\to S R_M,\qquad
 S\to\bar\gamma^{(1)}\mid\cdots\mid\bar\gamma^{(l)}.
 \tag{1}\] Appendix C, p. 14, proves Proposition 3.3 by claiming \(\bar\Gamma^*\subseteq L(R_M)\) and hence that a finite simulator trace followed by \(M(x)\) belongs to \(L(G_a)\).

Independent exact check. In standard context-free grammar semantics, every finite derivation starting from \(R_M\) retains one occurrence of \(R_M\): its only production replaces that occurrence with another one, and no other production removes it. Therefore \[L(R_M)=\varnothing,\qquad L(G_a)=\varnothing.
 \tag{2}\] Take any deterministic machine that halts after finitely many steps and outputs `0`, with output grammar \(G\to\texttt{0}\). This satisfies the construction’s requirement that \(G\) contain the machine’s valid outputs. Whatever its finite trace, (1) cannot derive that trace followed by `0`. The containment used in the proof is false, and no sound constrained decoder can produce a terminal word of this grammar.

Counterevidence and scope. The original p. 5 image confirms the absence of a terminating alternative; this is not an extraction omission. The p. 14 proof explicitly invokes \(\bar\Gamma^*\), strongly indicating the intended language. The issue may originate as an omitted symbol, but its consequence is an empty central constructed language, rather than a cosmetic change. Under the assigned standard it supports Major for this construction and its dependent proof step. The practical delimiter algorithm does not instantiate this \(R_M\) grammar, and the corrected existence claim is not refuted.

Complete local repair. Replace the middle rule by \[R_M\to S R_M\mid\varepsilon.
 \tag{3}\] Every derivation now produces only symbols of \(\bar\Gamma\) and can terminate, so \(L(R_M)\subseteq\bar\Gamma^*\). Conversely, for any word \(\bar\gamma_1\cdots\bar\gamma_k\), apply the recursive production \(k\) times, choose each corresponding terminal production for \(S\), and finish with \(\varepsilon\). Thus \(L(R_M)=\bar\Gamma^*\). If the cited simulator emits \(rM(x)\), then \(r\in L(R_M)\) and \(M(x)\in L(G)\), establishing membership in the repaired \(L(G_a)\). The paper’s stated sound-decoding property then preserves that output. This repairs the containment dependency conditional on the imported simulator result; it is not a new proof of that external construction.

Problem 2 (Major, released-checker scope: floor-division rewriting can certify a wrong answer).

Claim, source, and core dependency. Section 5, p. 7, describes verifying generated GSM-Symbolic expressions against gold expressions with Z3. Pinned `src/prompting/gsm_symbolic.py`, lines 74–80, rewrites the entire expression around `//` using a textual regular expression. Lines 170–173 apply the rewrite to both candidate and gold; lines 176–180 interpret the resulting helper; lines 193–205 accept an unsatisfiable inequality between the translated expressions. The checker is called by the answer-evaluation path at line 40.

Paper-bound witness and all assumptions. Listing 1 on p. 15 prints the gold expression `y//d*t`. The grammar on p. 19 makes multiplication and floor division left-associative at the same precedence. Consider the candidate `y//(d*t)`. Both use only allowed operations and problem variables and can be wrapped in the required delimiters.

The source pattern takes the shortest nonempty left part before `//` and the entire nonempty right remainder. It therefore makes the following translations: \[\begin{array}{rcl}
 \texttt{y//d*t}&\longmapsto&\texttt{z3\_floor\_div(y,d*t)},\\
 \texttt{y//(d*t)}&\longmapsto&\texttt{z3\_floor\_div(y,(d*t))}.
 \end{array}\] These are the same expression. Yet at positive integers \(y=12,d=3,t=2\), \[(\lfloor12/3\rfloor)\,2=8,\qquad
 \lfloor12/(3\cdot2)\rfloor=2.\] The witness satisfies the evaluator’s positive-integer domain constraints. Denominators are nonzero; both transformed expressions are valid helper calls. The symbolic inequality is identically false, so the code’s unsatisfiable branch accepts. This conclusion follows statically; neither the author evaluator nor Z3 was executed.

Impact, counterevidence, and limits. This is a demonstrated invalid correctness decision in the released rule that implements a core benchmark criterion, using an actual paper-listed gold expression. That supports Major for the released validator without requiring a measured score change. The supplied snapshot does not establish which historical outputs or tables used this exact rule. Its effect on published accuracy, method ordering, or the number of affected examples is Needs Evidence. A solver can correctly decide the translated formula while the translation itself is wrong. This is not evidence that Z3 is unsound, or that all symbolic checks fail.

Repair boundary. The expression must be parsed according to its operator grammar and translated recursively from the resulting syntax tree. For this witness the correct gold translation is \(\operatorname{floorDiv}(y,d)\,t\), not \(\operatorname{floorDiv}(y,dt)\). This restores the witness’s distinction. A complete evaluator repair additionally requires testing every supported operator, domain restriction, and fallback path; it was not implemented or certified here.

Problem 3 (Minor: model-specific syntax and strict-improvement wording disagree with the tables).

Source and check. The p. 7 discussion follows the Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct accuracy result with a statement that CRANE has no syntax errors and unconstrained CoT has 10%. Table 1, p. 9, instead gives that model parse rates of 94% and 82%, hence error rates of 6% and 18%. The 100%/90% pair belongs to Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Interpreted as an across-model assertion, the zero-error statement is also contradicted by the table.

The same page says CRANE has higher accuracy for all evaluated few-shot settings. Table 3, p. 21, has a tie at two shots for Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct: CRANE and unconstrained generation without CoT both score 37%.

Bounded correction and impact. Use the correct named-model parse rates and replace strict superiority in the tied comparison with at-least-as-high accuracy. The tables themselves disclose both qualifications. They do not overturn the main Table 1 accuracy advantage, so these are local reporting defects assessed Minor.

# Checks that support the reported direction

Table 1 supports gains over the strongest listed baseline of \(5,2,9,3,5,10,3,6,3\) percentage points across its nine models, an unweighted mean of \(5.111\) points. Thus the headline maximum of ten percentage points is supported by the reported numbers. Table 2’s FOLIO percentages are consistent with counts out of 203: best-baseline versus CRANE successes of \(57\to63\), \(76\to86\), and \(80\to94\).

The sampling ablation explicitly acknowledges that Llama unconstrained CoT pass@3 reaches 35%, exceeding CRANE’s 33%; its token ratio \(607.7/170.22=3.5701\) reasonably rounds to approximately four. This is material counterevidence to a claim that the paper hid this baseline win. These are table-consistency checks, not recomputation from raw model outputs.

FOLIO’s inspected `src/prompting/fol.py`, lines 49–76, compares the solver’s answer label with the reference label; it does not establish semantic equivalence of the generated FOL translation to every gold premise/conclusion. Auxiliary source inspection also found that random fallback answers are excluded from correctness. End-to-end label accuracy is therefore the supported interpretation of this metric.

# Remaining boundaries and conclusion

The delayed delimiter transition needs an explicit token-boundary condition for a universal syntax guarantee. A token that includes the opening delimiter and invalid body text may be emitted while unconstrained. Inspection of `src/crane/main.py`, lines 931–950 and 1012–1022, found that the release resets the parser at the start delimiter after preserving the full emitted token. Whether an affected token and path occurred in the tested tokenizers or benchmark traces is unresolved; no measured syntax failure is attributed to this mechanism here. The paper already reports nonzero parse/compile failures and finite generation budgets, so its practical results cannot be read as unconditional correctness.
