Abstract: The diversity objective, its low-class-count behavior, the Gaussian complexity calculation and selected empirical summaries are checked against the published proceedings paper and preserved public release. Three demonstrated problems concern the core argument: minimizing the printed determinant rewards collapse, normalized binary non-target predictions carry no diversity signal, and the Gaussian KL substitution underestimates its stated quantity. The public release uses a different negative log-determinant, which addresses the first sign direction but does not restore the binary signal. Historical experimental accuracy is not independently reproduced, and scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and contribution

Equation reference: eq:loss, eq:gram. The paper seeks particle diversity while retaining low loss and low sharpness in Bayesian parameter-efficient fine-tuning. On p. 4, its joint loss for \(K\) particles is \[
 \ell(\theta_{1:K};x,y)
 =\frac1K\sum_{i=1}^K l(\theta_i;x,y)
   +\alpha l_{\rm div}(\theta_{1:K};x,y),
 \qquad \alpha>0.\] Let \(f^i_{-y}\) contain the prediction probabilities after deleting the true-label coordinate, and let \[
 F=\left[\frac{f^1_{-y}}{\|f^1_{-y}\|},\ldots,
          \frac{f^K_{-y}}{\|f^K_{-y}\|}\right]
 \in\mathbb R^{(C-1)\times K}.\] Section 4.3, p. 5, defines \(l_{\rm div}=\det(F^\top F)\). Page 6 identifies this with squared spanned volume and states the intention to maximize that volume, while retaining the positive sign in the minimized objective.

The statistical development uses an independent joint distribution \(Q^K\), with \(Q\) subsequently represented as \(K^{-1}\sum_i\mathcal N(\mu_i,\sigma^2I)\) and prior \(P=\mathcal N(0,I)\). Corollary 4.3 supplies the Gaussian complexity term used to motivate the practical objective. These are central links between the proposed mechanism and its formal justification.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The printed minimization has the opposite diversity preference).  Assessment: Major. The positive determinant term in [eq:loss] (#eq:loss) rewards less diversity when minimized, contrary to its stated role in the central method.

## Decisive example and assumptions

Consider one input, three classes, true label \(0\), and two particles. All probabilities below are strictly positive and sum to one, so they are attainable softmax outputs. Compare \[\begin{aligned}
 f^1=f^2&=(0.8,0.1,0.1), &&\text{collapsed case},\\
 f^1&=(0.8,0.19,0.01),\quad
 f^2=(0.8,0.01,0.19), &&\text{diverse case}.\end{aligned}\] The mean cross-entropy is \(-\log0.8\) in both cases. In the collapsed case the normalized non-target columns coincide, giving determinant zero. In the diverse case their inner product is \(19/181\), hence \[\det(F^\top F)=1-\left(\frac{19}{181}\right)^2
 =\frac{32400}{32761}\simeq0.9889808.\] At the paper’s \(\alpha=0.02\), the printed losses are \[\ell_{\rm collapsed}=0.2231435513,\qquad
 \ell_{\rm diverse}=0.2429231673.\] Thus the printed minimization strictly prefers the collapsed pair while the supervised component is held fixed. The example directly tests the defined diversity loss; it does not assume a complete optimization trajectory or replace the DRO problem with an empirical claim.

## Counterevidence and correction

The preserved release differs: , lines 290–296, uses a negative log-determinant; , lines 33–54, forms the normalized Gram matrix, adds \(10^{-7}I\) and computes its log-determinant. Minimizing this negative quantity encourages a larger regularized determinant. This is substantive counterevidence against claiming that the inspected code literally minimizes the wrong-sign determinant.

It does not reconcile the published equations with their stated meaning or establish which implementation produced the reported tables. Reversing the determinant’s sign, or specifying the actual negative log-determinant and its regularization throughout, addresses the local direction. The latter also changes the objective and its range, so the theorem’s loss-bound assumptions need rechecking. No complete theoretical repair or historical-code binding is certified.

The finding is Major because the incorrect optimization preference is in the mechanism named as the central contribution, independently of any benchmark reversal or difficulty of repair.

Problem 2 (The binary-task diversity mechanism is constant).  Assessment: Major. For binary classification, the normalized non-target construction cannot supply the claimed particle-diversity pressure, including for the reported Camelyon task.

## Exact rank calculation

For \(C=2\), every non-target vector is a positive scalar \(a_i\). Its normalization is \(a_i/|a_i|=1\). Therefore \[F=[1,\ldots,1],\qquad F^\top F=\mathbf1_K\mathbf1_K^\top.\] For \(K>1\), the printed determinant is identically zero, independent of all particle predictions. More generally, the printed determinant vanishes whenever \(K>C-1\), because the Gram matrix lacks full rank.

The release’s jitter does not restore a binary prediction signal. For a hard-label binary example, \[\det(\mathbf1_K\mathbf1_K^\top+\varepsilon I)
 =\varepsilon^{K-1}(K+\varepsilon).\] This is positive but constant. At \(K=4\), \(\varepsilon=10^{-7}\), its negative logarithm is \(46.9679925668\), with zero prediction derivative in exact arithmetic. This conclusion concerns mathematical signal, not floating-point residuals.

## Connection to the reported task

Page 7 states that the Bayesian methods use four particles. The staged , lines 103–108, assigns two classes to `patch_camelyon`, and , line 27, sets four particles. Camelyon appears in the main comparisons and diversity ablations. The counterexample is therefore tied to an actual low-class-count setting of the core mechanism, rather than an unrelated unsupported application.

The source masks coordinates using the target tensor. The exact code corroboration above uses hard labels, matching the mathematical definition of removing the ground-truth label. Soft-label masking conventions require separate care and are not used to claim an executed training result.

## Counterevidence, impact and correction

Robustness regularization, supervised loss, ensembling and other training differences may still improve Camelyon accuracy. The calculation does not falsify the reported score. Nor does it prove that all multiclass settings lack a diversity signal: regularized Gram objectives can vary in higher-dimensional cases.

The supported Major finding is the absence of the claimed explicit interaction signal in a reported application of the principal mechanism. Fixing the sign in Problem 1 alone does not fix it. A rank-aware diversity construction, a different representation, or a stated restricted domain would be needed. No replacement method or its empirical behavior is verified here.

Problem 3 (The Gaussian KL substitution is incorrect).  Assessment: Major. Corollary 4.3 and its p. 18 proof use a false Gaussian KL expression in a central generalization-bound derivation.

## Original and correct quantities

Equation reference: eq:wrongkl, eq:rightkl. Convexity in the first argument gives the valid mixture inequality \[\mathrm{KL}\left(\frac1K\sum_i\mathcal N(\mu_i,\sigma^2I)
       \,\middle\Vert\,\mathcal N(0,I)\right)
 \le\frac1K\sum_i
   \mathrm{KL}(\mathcal N(\mu_i,\sigma^2I)\Vert\mathcal N(0,I)).\] The proof then replaces the right side by \[
 \frac1{2K}\sum_i(\|\mu_i\|^2+d\sigma-d\log\sigma).\] For standard deviation \(\sigma>0\), direct expectation of the Gaussian log-density ratio instead gives \[
 \frac1{2K}\sum_i
 \bigl(\|\mu_i\|^2+d(\sigma^2-1-2\log\sigma)\bigr).\] The paper’s covariance notation and reparameterization \(\theta_i=\mu_i+\sigma\epsilon_i\) both identify \(\sigma\) as a standard deviation; interpreting it as a variance would not reproduce [eq:wrongkl] (#eq:wrongkl) either.

## Admissible finite witness

Take \(d=1\), all \(\mu_i=0\), and the practical value \(\sigma=0.1\). Coincident mixture means are not excluded by the stated family, and the mixture is then exactly \(\mathcal N(0,0.01)\), so the mixture inequality is equality. Its actual KL is \[\tfrac12(0.01-1-2\log0.1)=1.8075850930.\] The printed substitution gives \(\tfrac12(0.1-\log0.1)=1.2012925465\), an underestimate of \(0.6062925465\). Thus the substituted expression is neither the asserted equality nor a valid upper bound in this case. This checks the Gaussian substitution itself; it does not purport to construct an entire statistical experiment violating every possible version of the final bound.

## Impact, counterevidence and bounded repair

Conditional on the preceding theorem and its hypotheses, replacing the Gaussian term would produce the complexity contribution \[L\sqrt{\frac{\sum_i\|\mu_i\|^2+
       Kd(\sigma^2-1-2\log\sigma)+2\log(1/\delta)}{4N}}.\] The full PAC-Bayesian/DRO argument is not certified by this local correction. In particular, uniformity requirements, the loss bound, and the relationship between iid mixture draws and one draw from each component deserve separate checking.

The practical fixed-\(\sigma\) setting makes the erroneous part a constant with respect to the means, so this error alone need not alter their gradients or measured accuracy. It nevertheless invalidates the displayed derivation of a central formal bound. That supports Major under the requested standard without asserting that the final statistical inequality is false in every setting.

# Conclusion and limitations

Three Major Problems are supported, with distinct dependencies and counterevidence: the sign of the central diversity objective, the absence of binary diversity signal, and the invalid Gaussian KL substitution. They are not a claim that all results fail. The code’s negative log-determinant, surviving higher-class applications, possible benefits from other method components and fixed-\(\sigma\) gradient invariance are explicitly retained.
