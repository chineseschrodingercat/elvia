Title: Technical review: video-enhanced offline reinforcement learning

# Scope and assessment

This report reviews I25-43738, Video-Enhanced Offline Reinforcement Learning: A Model-Based Approach, in the 17-page PMLR version and the acquired VeoRL release. Assessment: Minor, with substantive mechanism and reproduction questions marked Needs Evidence. The displayed aggregate gains are supported by internal arithmetic, and the code’s main loop trains from fixed datasets. One strongest-performance statement is contradicted by its table; the released reward objective and parameter sharing also differ from the manuscript. No false formal theorem or demonstrated failure of the historical experiments is established.

# What the learning objectives do and do not establish

The method combines a vector-quantized behavior abstraction network, a real-action trunk model, a temporally compressed latent-action plan model, and actor–critic learning guided by proximity to the plan’s predicted state. The two branches are reset from the same trunk state for each imagined step in (5), consistent with Figure 2 and the inspected imagination code. The plan branch therefore supplies a locally renewed longer-term target, rather than necessarily an autonomous open-loop plan trajectory throughout the entire actor rollout. The different temporal scales are a design choice that should not be mistaken for two predictions of the identical physical timestep.

Needs Evidence: negative distance is not a calibrated overestimation correction. The paper motivates the intrinsic reward as mitigating value overestimation (page 2). Interpreting (5)’s printed \(\|s_t,\bar s_t\|_2\) as the intended distance \(\|s_t-\bar s_t\|_2\) is supported by the code. It creates a nonpositive reward term, but its magnitude is not shown to bound reward-model error or transition-model bias. There is no theorem supplying such a link, and returns alone do not measure it.

For a one-step illustration, let actions \(A,B\) have true terminal rewards \((1,0)\), model rewards \((1,0.4)\) and plan distances \((20,0)\). At the manuscript’s Meta-World weight \(\omega=0.05\), shaped model rewards are \((0,0.4)\). The penalty changes the preferred action from the correct \(A\) to \(B\), while leaving \(B\)’s positive overestimate \(0.4\) untouched. This is not an experiment on VeoRL. It shows why negative shaping alone supplies neither general pessimism nor preservation of the original task optimum. Task-aligned plan predictions could make this regularizer beneficial, as the ablations suggest. To identify the stated mechanism, compare predicted values with realized returns, control for extra parameters and compute, and test erroneous or unrelated source behaviors.

Needs Evidence: mean matching and semantic alignment are different claims. The loss on page 4 is \[\ell_{\rm MMD}=\|\mathbb E e^{\rm src}-\mathbb E e^{\rm tar}\|_2^2.\] This is a legitimate linear-kernel MMD, and the release explicitly selects its linear implementation. It matches only first moments. Equal-probability features \(\{-1,1\}\) and \(\{-2,2\}\) give zero loss but variances 1 and 4 and disjoint supports (total variation 1). Even matching a full marginal feature distribution would not uniquely align behavior semantics under a permutation of latent action labels. The codebook, prediction losses and target training can provide additional structure; the example does not show that they failed in the reported experiments.

Figure 10 visibly shows greater overlap in a two-dimensional PCA projection, and Table 7 reports a \(0.45\to0.60\) success-rate improvement, correctly a \(33.3\%\) relative increase. These are useful empirical observations, not proof of full distributional or conditional semantic alignment. Likewise Figure 8 plots selected model-generated action sequences from a policy already conditioned on the latent code, restricted to sequences whose codes remain constant. Clustering is compatible with the intended mechanism but is not an independent ground-truth validation of source-to-target action semantics. An all-code held-out evaluation with annotated action correspondence or conditional probes would better test the interpretation.

# Empirical checks and a localized reporting error

Problem 1 (Minor: the blanket best-success-rate statement is too strong).  Section 4.1, page 6, says VeoRL consistently achieves the best success-rate performance in Appendix Table 3. In Handle Pull, VIP is \(0.36\pm0.15\) and VeoRL is \(0.30\pm0.10\); in Handle Press, DrQ+CQL and VeoRL tie at a displayed mean of \(0.60\).

The table itself correctly bolds VIP for Handle Pull. Narrow the statement to best average performance and best or tied means in five of the six listed tasks. This does not contradict the separate claim about episode-return means in Figure 3, since return and binary success are different metrics. Independent recomputation gives Table 3 average success rates \(0.1383\) (DrQ+CQL), \(0.3167\) (DreamerV2), \(0.0417\) (APV), \(0.2700\) (VIP), \(0\) (LOMPO), and \(0.5333\) (VeoRL), agreeing with displayed rounding. The MineDojo improvements \(0.10\to0.35\), \(0.36\to0.83\) and \(0.27\to0.55\) are respectively \(250\%\), \(130.6\%\) and \(103.7\%\), consistent with the stated approximations.

Figures 3–7 and 9 were inspected as images. They support the reported directions of average gains and losses in the tested configurations. They do not alone establish statistical significance: some error bars are broad and only three training seeds are used. Captions describe deviations of 50 episodes over three seeds without unambiguously distinguishing within-policy episode variation from variation of seed-level means. For example, a pooled Bernoulli outcome with success probability \(0.70\) has standard deviation about \(0.458\), whereas the reported \(0.07\) is compatible with an aggregate-rate deviation. State the aggregation, provide per-seed rates/returns, and avoid treating evaluation episodes as independent training replicates. This is a reporting ambiguity, not evidence of fabricated uncertainty.

The source-video quantity ablation (Table 4) changes quantity, not a controlled quality measure. The random-target-data ablation (Table 5) addresses a distinct robustness question and supports benefit in that tested setting. Figure 7’s no-video control is more directly relevant to the auxiliary-data contribution than the comparison with plain DreamerV2. The offline-to-online curves in Figure 6 explicitly include new environment interactions; they should remain separate from strictly offline results. Table 6’s frozen-codebook transfer covers two specified task pairs and does not establish arbitrary-domain transfer. APV’s omitted CARLA bar is explicitly disclosed as approximately \(-500\); its omission should not be mistaken for an unavailable baseline. The computational overhead of the two-branch method is acknowledged in the conclusion but not comprehensively quantified.

# Release-scoped implementation discrepancies

The acquired source is commit `44c16d931eaaa81a406e685690b213aea85694f0`, dated 9 September 2025. It is a released snapshot, not an attested record of the ICML experiments. No author program or model checkpoint was executed.

Problem 2 (Minor, release-scoped: intrinsic-reward weight differs from the specification).  Equation (6) adds \(\omega\bar r_t\), and Table 2 specifies \(\omega=0.05,0.1,10^{-5}\) for Meta-World, CARLA and MineDojo. The inspected training path adds the negative Euclidean distance with coefficient 1.

In models.py 465–467, \(\texttt{int\_reward}=-\texttt{pos\_dist}\) is added directly to the objective. The supplied objective in dreamer.py 170–172 is the reward head evaluated on the trunk feature, with no inverse scaling or intrinsic coefficient. The configuration’s reward-scale fields concern model-loss setup and do not multiply this distance. For unchanged latent distances, this is a factor of 20, 10 and 100,000 relative to the table’s prescribed intrinsic contribution. Latent feature scales and historical configurations could differ, so no resulting score change is inferred. Restore an explicit task-specific coefficient or document and validate the alternative normalization and configuration.

Problem 3 (Minor, release-scoped: decoder initialization is not decoder sharing).  Section 3.2, page 5, says the trunk and plan models share encoder and decoder parameters. The release constructs distinct ConvDecoder objects for image and image_latent at models.py 59–66 and trains them on different reconstruction losses at 195–211.

dreamer.py 157–159 copies weights at the BAN-to-world-model transition. This supplies common initialization; it does not tie subsequent parameters. The common encoder is used for both later branches, which is counterevidence against a claim that all representation sharing is absent. The decoder discrepancy matters because the intrinsic reward compares latent states from separately parameterized dynamics, and plan dynamics and the imitation network are applied to trunk states (models.py 510–523). Shared output reconstruction would constrain their coordinate systems more than independent decoders do, although neither alone proves full latent alignment. The current version needs a documented alignment mechanism or a tied decoder if it intends to implement the manuscript literally. No assertion is made that the original runs used these bytes.

Other inspected code matches important parts of the description. The vector quantizer computes nearest codewords and uses straight-through gradients (networks.py 525–546), although the manuscript’s norm notation omits the square used by its MSE-based implementation. Change-point masks construct compressed trajectories before plan training (models.py 108–169); the full edge-case behavior of this compaction routine was not validated. The actor and critic concatenate latent behavior with the trunk feature, and the lambda-return implementation uses \(\gamma=0.99\), \(\lambda=0.95\) in the default settings. The main loop loads the fixed target/source datasets separately, evaluates with \(\texttt{training=False}\), and trains on the target loader, rather than feeding the evaluation cache into policy training (dreamer.py 338–368). This static check supports the offline training path; it is not a complete audit of all launch configurations or baseline adaptations.
