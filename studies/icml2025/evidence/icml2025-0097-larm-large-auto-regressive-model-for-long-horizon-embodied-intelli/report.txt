# 1. Summary and coverage

The paper motivates Referee RL by a mathematical explanation that ordinary PPO feedback vanishes on long successful trajectories and that nonconstant referee rewards prevent this behavior. The failure is in deriving the TD-residual pattern from reward imbalance and in the proposed nonzero-residual guarantee. The geometric discounting identity itself is valid.

Read the complete ten-page publisher PDF, including the methods, Algorithm 1 and Tables 1-5. Equations on pp. 4-5 and all five tables on p. 7 were visually inspected. Reviewer-authored exact-arithmetic calculations were executed. Official project links and the website repository were inspected; no training/evaluation implementation was located within that search. No author code or model was run.

# 2. Definitions and operative assumptions

Sections 3.1-3.2 define a finite-state MDP, a policy, and a critic intended to estimate expected discounted return. Section 4.1 considers a successful trajectory with a small negative time penalty -epsilon before a final positive task reward R. It assumes gamma and lambda lie strictly between zero and one. It does not specify the duration or continuation value of failed trajectories, require a constant critic, or impose a critic approximation-error bound.

The source places its terminal reward at r_T, while GAE is summed through T-1. The construction below retains that reward convention and uses the terminal state’s continuation value. A consistent one-step reindexing gives the same conclusion; the finding does not depend on this indexing discrepancy.

\[\delta_k=r_k+\gamma V(s_{k+1})-V(s_k),\qquad A_t=\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}(\gamma\lambda)^{k-t}\delta_k.\]

# 3. Problem 1 (Major): reward imbalance does not determine actor feedback

Source claim and location. On PDF p. 4, between Eqs. (5) and (6), the paper infers from the predominance of -epsilon rewards that critic optimization yields approximately zero preterminal TD errors and one terminal residual R. It then concludes that the early GAE and useful actor feedback vanish. PDF p. 5 further argues that changing the rewards with a referee prevents TD errors and GAE from converging to zero.

Suspect step. The realized rewards of a successful rollout do not determine its state values. Those values depend on the current policy and other possible continuations. Likewise, reward variation is not a lower bound on a Bellman residual. These are substantive gaps in the mathematical rationale advertised in the abstract, introduction and method.

# 3.1 Complete counterexample for the sparse-reward inference

MDP and policy. There is one target and fully observed states s0,s1,...,sT, plus absorbing termination. At s0 the policy chooses continue with probability p=1/2 and abort with probability 1/2. Both choices earn -epsilon at s0. Abort then terminates with zero continuation. Continue advances through every required success-chain state in order, with a deterministic action thereafter. Each nonterminal success state earns -epsilon and the target state sT earns R-epsilon. The initial distribution is concentrated at s0. All transition probabilities and policy probabilities are valid.

Choose epsilon=0.01, R=1, gamma=0.9, lambda=0.8 and T=100; the same construction applies to arbitrary T. Every successful trajectory has exactly the rewards specified by Eq. (5). The abort behavior is not constrained by that successful-trajectory condition or by Sec. 3.1. The critic is the exact expected discounted return defined by the paper.

Calculate the two initial action values and the state value as follows. Downstream values satisfy the deterministic Bellman recurrence, with V(sT)=R-epsilon. Hence every later successful-path TD residual is zero, but the initial residual is an action advantage.

\[Q_g=-\varepsilon\frac{1-\gamma^{T+1}}{1-\gamma}+\gamma^T R,\qquad Q_b=-\varepsilon,\qquad V(s_0)=pQ_g+(1-p)Q_b.\] \[A_0=\delta_0=(1-p)(Q_g-Q_b)=(1-p)\left[-\frac{\varepsilon\gamma(1-\gamma^T)}{1-\gamma}+\gamma^T R\right]\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac{(1-p)\varepsilon\gamma}{1-\gamma}=-0.045.\]

# 3.2 Numerical check, consequence and alternative reading

The initial-state conditional PPO actor-logit derivative at the old policy is p(1-p)(Qg-Qb), approximately -0.022492762; clipping is inactive at probability ratio one. This is a conditional initial-state contribution, not a claim that the time-averaged whole-rollout gradient has a nonzero limit. The direct contradiction is the claimed vanishing initial GAE.

The negative sign is essential: with a distant discounted reward, the accumulated time penalties favor aborting. The construction disproves the stated implication to vanishing feedback. It does not show positive feedback for task completion, establish that ordinary PPO solves Minecraft, or disprove the practical utility of shaping.

If every unsuccessful trajectory were required to last exactly as long and incur exactly the same intermediate penalties, the abort construction would be excluded. Under that stronger model the two-action initial advantage can instead be (1-p) gamma^T R and vanish. Those extra assumptions are not in the paper. The exact-critic argument uses the source’s expected-return definition and conventional fixed-target TD regression, not an assertion about a global minimum of a fully differentiated stochastic residual objective.

# 3.3 Nonconstant referee rewards do not guarantee nonzero TD errors

For any deterministic finite chain, including an arbitrarily long one, add a nonconstant sequence of allowed referee bonuses and define the critic by backward recursion. Select category rewards ra=0.2, rb=0.1, rc=-0.1 and rd=-0.2, satisfying the stated ordering. Correct actions can alternate positive-outcome and no-immediate-progress states before eventual success. An illustrative four-step total reward sequence is [0.19,0.09,0.19,1.19]; the same alternating prefix can be extended to T=100.

With the exact shaped return, every TD residual is identically zero, GAE is zero, and Eq. (1)’s squared residual loss is zero. Thus the p. 5 categorical inference from changed/nonconstant rewards to nonzero TD/GAE is false, even at a global zero of the deterministic squared loss. This does not imply that shaped rewards are ineffective during learning; no such conclusion is drawn.

\[V'_k=r'_k+\gamma V'_{k+1},\quad V'_{T+1}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \delta'_k=r'_k+\gamma V'_{k+1}-V'_k=0.\]

# 4. Dependence, surviving evidence and scoped correction

The paper explicitly promotes the vanishment argument as a theoretical explanation for classic RL failure and uses its proposed removal to motivate Referee RL. Invalidating that derivation therefore directly affects a central contribution and supports Major. This judgment does not require a drop in empirical scores, failure of the architecture, or inability to repair the exposition.

What survives is precise. If all preterminal TD errors are zero and a bounded terminal residual is the only contribution, GAE equals a discounted terminal residual and decays when the remaining distance tends to infinity. This conditional identity is analytically valid. It is not a verified repair of the broader theorem-like rationale, because the paper has not established that residual pattern or a nonzero-residual guarantee for its referee.

Tables 1-5 remain empirical evidence. Table 1 has 13 strict point-estimate wins and one tie; Table 2 has three 100% ties, 28/30 diamond successes versus STEVE’s 3/3, and 16/30 enchanted successes versus each listed baseline’s 0/3. These disclosed unequal sample sizes do not establish universal rate dominance, but they do preserve the observed enchanted-sword capability. The reward ablation’s improvements do not independently validate the defective derivation.

The 0.58-second claim concerns high-level skill selection on an RTX4090, not low-level control or full task completion. No independent timing or benchmark replication was performed. The public code search found a website repository, not training/evaluation code. Missing logs, exact reward values, referee versions/prompts, seeds and deployment configuration remain explicit evidence gaps.
