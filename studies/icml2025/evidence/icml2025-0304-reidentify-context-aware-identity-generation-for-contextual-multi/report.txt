# Scope and method

CAID learns a contextual state representation and autoregressively assigns distinct identities to agents during centralized training. An identity-conditioned affine regulator acts on each agent’s action value before value decomposition. The intended deployment remains decentralized. This review reads the main method, the experimental discussion, Appendix A’s algorithm and hyperparameters, the environment descriptions in B, and the additional comparison/ablation captions in C. No author implementation was located in the frozen source search; this is an access limitation, not proof that none exists. Figures 3, 4, 6 and 10 on pages 5, 7, 9 and 17 were inspected as images.

# The uniqueness mechanism does not enforce uniqueness

Problem 1 (Multiplying logits by a binary mask leaves forbidden identities selectable).  Section 4.2, page 5, requires \(\mathrm{id}^i\ne\mathrm{id}^j\) for every distinct pair of agents. Equation (4), followed by sampling in Equation (5), does not satisfy this constraint.

The paper defines \(M[j]=0\) for an identity already assigned and \(M[j]=1\) otherwise, then gives \[P_j=\operatorname{Softmax}(h\odot M)_j,
\qquad h=f^{\rm Decoder}(s,z,\mathrm{id}^1,\ldots,\mathrm{id}^{a-1}).\] The natural elementwise interpretation is necessary for a vector probability distribution; interpreting the dot as a scalar inner product would not repair the algorithm. With finite logits, a forbidden index has numerator \(e^0=1\), rather than zero. Therefore \[P_j=\frac1{\sum_k e^{h_kM[k]}}>0\quad\text{for every forbidden }j.\] This is a direct support violation of the stated hard constraint. It is independent of the expressiveness of the encoder, the training loss, or the straight-through gradient estimator.

## Independent finite checks

Take two agents, and suppose identity 1 has been used. If the decoder logits are \((-5,-5)\), masking gives \((0,-5)\), so the repeated identity is drawn with probability \[\frac1{1+e^{-5}}=0.9933071491.\] This is an ordinary finite-logit output allowed by the specified decoder, not a numerical overflow case. A second example uses zero logits at every decoding step. Multiplication by any binary mask leaves them zero, so every step is uniformly distributed over all \(n\) labels, even conditional on earlier assignments. Exactly \(n!\) of the \(n^n\) complete sequences are permutations. Independent enumeration gives

Agents | All sequences | Unique sequences | Uniqueness probability

2 | 4 | 2 | 0.5

3 | 27 | 6 | 0.22222222

5 | 3125 | 120 | 0.0384

Thus at five agents the literal mechanism violates uniqueness with probability \(0.9616\). This is an exact illustrative distribution, not an estimate of collision frequency in the trained system.

## A locally verified correction

Equation reference: eq:repair. For a nonempty unused set \(U\), use \[P_j=\frac{M[j]e^{h_j}}{\sum_k M[k]e^{h_k}}.\] Equivalently, add \(-\infty\) to forbidden logits before softmax. Merely moving the multiplication after softmax also requires normalization before interpreting the vector as a probability distribution. Under [eq:repair] (#eq:repair), used labels have zero probability. Induction shows that before step \(a\leq n\), exactly \(n-a+1\) labels remain; choosing only among them produces a permutation after \(n\) steps. The independent enumeration of repaired branches confirms probability one of uniqueness for the checked sizes. This proves the local constraint repair; it does not validate training quality or straight-through gradients.

## Impact and alternative explanation

The supported assessment is Major for the written core algorithm: the advertised defining property of context-aware unique identities fails under its actual probability formula. Appendix A, Algorithm 1, directs identity generation through Equation (8), which in turn uses the same \(P(a)\); it supplies no corrective support mask or rejection rule. The error therefore propagates to the specified training procedure rather than being isolated from it.

The intended implementation may use a conventional additive mask, in which case this can be a single equation error with no effect on the reported runs. The paper’s repeated uniqueness statements and its visualization of permutations are evidence of that intent. They do not establish what the unavailable implementation executes. The finding does not assert that the authors ran the erroneous formula, that their observed learning improvements vanish, or that the proposed corrected method fails. Exact source code or logged assignments are needed to resolve that empirical consequence. No erratum or corrected code is present in the frozen input, and no exhaustive later-version search is claimed.

# Checks that survive and empirical limits

For the action regulator in Equation (6), a scalar \(w(\mathrm{id})>0\) and action-independent \(b(\mathrm{id})\) preserve every pairwise action-value ordering: \[\widehat Q(u)-\widehat Q(v)=w(\mathrm{id})[Q(u)-Q(v)].\] Consequently the greedy action is unchanged for fixed local history. This is consistent with Appendix A selecting actions from the agent network and applying the regulator only in the replay update. Context may still alter the learned policy through training gradients. It would be incorrect to claim an execution-time global-information leak merely because the training regulator uses global identities. The proof concerns positive scalar weights; absolute value alone is nonnegative and permits a zero-weight tie, so strict argmax-set equality needs positivity or a tie convention. This boundary observation is not used to assert failure of the whole value decomposition framework.

Figure 4 shows substantial gains in several Terran and Protoss tasks and mixed relative performance in Zerg tasks. These plots are genuine empirical counterevidence to a claim that CAID has no learning value. Their shaded bands and five-seed reporting do not allow this review to reconstruct significance tests without raw run data and a defined band statistic. Figure 10 provides useful fixed/random-identity comparisons, but those comparisons do not establish whether the masking code enforced a permutation. Figure 6’s two separately fitted t-SNE spaces are an illustrative projection; distances across them are not a quantitative proof of state-space invariance. No accusation of manipulated images is made.

The conclusion, page 9, explicitly reserves zero-shot generalization and dynamic agent populations for future work. Accordingly, the reported randomized-context experiments should not be silently upgraded into a verified held-out-context zero-shot guarantee. Table 1 also contains individual metrics in which an ablation has a lower travel time than full CAID; the broader performance narrative should be read as an aggregate observation, not strict dominance on every metric. These scope checks are not additional Major findings.

# Conclusion

The frozen written masking algorithm fails its uniqueness constraint. A finite probability calculation, exhaustive small enumeration and an inductive repaired-support argument establish the issue and a local correction. Actual implementation and experimental impact remain unresolved because code was not available. The positive-scaling order argument survives with its stated condition, and the empirical evidence remains informative within its tested scope. Focused independent AI acceptance has checked the stated Problem. Scientific adjudication remains pending; author adoption is unknown and no human review has occurred.
