# Definitions and checked reasoning

A spike is \(o(u)=u\,\mathbf 1_{\{u\geq V_{\rm th}\}}\); a binary synaptic weight \(b\) belongs to \(\{-1,1\}\). Consequently \(bo\) can be evaluated as a signed addition. This verifies the narrow synaptic arithmetic in Equations (6)–(9), not the absence of every multiplication in a complete implementation. For example, the leak and learnable spike-amplitude operations remain separate costs.

In Section 3.3 the trained weight is written as \(\alpha\operatorname{sign}(W)\), with a channel-wise learnable scale. For a scalar \(\alpha\), associativity gives \(\alpha Bx=B(\alpha x)\). For an input-channel diagonal scale \(D\), \(BDx=B(Dx)\) is likewise valid. The direction of channel scaling is essential to interpreting the displayed inference transformation.

For Table 5, the stated operation model gives \[\begin{aligned}
 E_{\rm vanilla}
 &=3.54\cdot12.5+71.20\cdot0.077
   =49.7324\ \mu{\rm J},\\
 E_{\rm ReverB}
 &=3.54\cdot12.5+74.50\cdot0.077
   =49.9865\ \mu{\rm J}.\end{aligned}\] The unrounded relative increase is approximately \(0.511\%\); the rounded table supports approximately \(0.52\%\). This is an arithmetic check of the supplied model, not a hardware measurement. The energy table concerns the vanilla ReverB configuration; it does not separately validate the learnable variant’s full cost.

# Findings

Problem 1 (The ImageNet improvement is comparator-specific).  The contribution statement on page 2 and the ImageNet discussion in Section 4.2 on page 8 present the learnable variant’s \(70.91\%\) accuracy as a \(3.22\)-point improvement over other state-of-the-art SNN models. Table 3 on page 7 contains a stronger matched comparator than the one that yields this difference.

## Complete check

Restrict the comparison to the published Table 3 entries with ResNet34 and four timesteps. The learnable variant reports \(70.91\%\), Ternary Spike \(70.12\%\), and Real Spike \(67.69\%\). All three satisfy these architecture and timestep restrictions. Direct subtraction gives \[70.91-67.69=3.22,\qquad 70.91-70.12=0.79.\] Ternary Spike is the highest non-proposed accuracy in that table. The claimed broad comparison therefore uses a weaker baseline: the best-listed-baseline margin is \(0.79\), not \(3.22\), percentage points. The exact decimal arithmetic is independently reproduced in `c.py` and `c.json` in the matching verification directory.

## Counterevidence, impact, and correction

The \(3.22\)-point subtraction is correct when explicitly attributed to Real Spike. The proposed method remains the best listed model, and the review does not dispute the reported \(70.91\%\) measurement. A valid local correction is to report \(3.22\) points over Real Spike and \(0.79\) points over the strongest listed matched baseline. This corrects the comparison without requiring new experiments. The supported impact is Minor: the comparative magnitude is overstated, while the ranking and core signed-addition construction survive. No statistical significance claim is inferred from the difference or the reported three-trial standard deviation.

Problem 2 (Channel-scale folding needs an explicit axis).  Section 3.3, page 4, Equation (12) calls the scale channel-wise. Equations (16)–(18) on page 5 move it from the weights to the previous layer’s output, and Algorithm 1 uses that transformation for inference. The PDF does not explicitly distinguish input-channel from output-channel scaling.

## Conditional diagnostic and its assumptions

Under an output-channel reading, the two expressions are \(ABx\) and \(BAx\), where \(A\) is diagonal. Let \[B=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\qquad
 x=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0.3\end{pmatrix}.\] Both weights are admissible binary values, both scale values are positive, and each input amplitude exceeds the dynamic-data threshold \(0.25\) specified on page 7. At the first timestep take the previous membrane state to be zero and \(\tau=0.25\), as in the experimental configuration. Then \[ABx=\begin{pmatrix}-0.7\\-1.4\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad BAx=\begin{pmatrix}-0.4\\-0.4\end{pmatrix}.\] Both computed states satisfy the displayed subthreshold restriction, yet the updates differ. This calculation refutes general commutation of an output-channel scale through a binary matrix.

## Counterevidence and bounded conclusion

The output-axis assumption is not established as the authors’ operative choice. A scalar or input-channel implementation validates the folding by associativity; the notation may intend exactly that. This is therefore Needs Evidence, not an unconditional counterexample to the implemented method. Resolving the issue requires the actual scale tensor shape and broadcast axis, together with the training-to-inference conversion. If output scaling were established, the failed equivalence would directly affect a core claimed inference construction and could justify Major. No such finding is asserted here. The conditional calculation supplies neither a measured accuracy effect nor a repair of an unknown implementation.

# Other checked boundaries and overall dependence

Equation (4) is ReLU when \(V_{\rm th}=0\), the static-data setting. For the positive threshold used for dynamic data it has a jump at the threshold. The page 4 differentiability description needs an almost-everywhere or surrogate interpretation. The available text does not establish an erroneous executed gradient or a training failure; this point is retained as a scope clarification, not a second supported defect. Similarly, an explicit \(\alpha\) factor need not appear separately in Equation (14) if it is included in \(\partial U/\partial W^b\).

Tables 1–4 were checked for internal comparison logic, not reproduced from images, data, or training logs.
