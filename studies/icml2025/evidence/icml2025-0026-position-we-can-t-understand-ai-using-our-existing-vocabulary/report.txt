# 2. Checked reasoning and decisive evidence

Unchanged behavior without a neologism. Figure 4 (p. 6), Section 5 step 3 and Section 5.2 (p. 7) promise unchanged model behavior when the new word is absent. A naive vocabulary expansion changes a softmax denominator, but Appendix B (p. 14) explicitly masks the new token during every generation step. That provision resolves the candidate concern.

> p_new(v | x) = exp(z_v(x)) / [sum_{u in V} exp(z_u(x)) + exp(-infinity)] = p_old(v | x), for v in V.

The equality assumes unchanged tokenization of old-token inputs, frozen old embeddings and other parameters, and the same decoding settings. Since the new token cannot be generated, induction gives equality of complete sequence distributions. It does not require independently sampled strings to coincide. An illustrative three-logit calculation returned zero probability difference [E1]. Removing the mask would change the assumptions and is not a counterexample to the stated experimental procedure.

Diversity calculation. Section 5.4 and Figure 6 (p. 8) consider nine possible target numbers. Under a uniformly selected correct target and iid guesses from probabilities p_1,...,p_9, the average success probability is:

> P(hit by n) = 1 - (1/9) sum_{i=1}^9 (1-p_i)^n; P_uniform = 1-(8/9)^n.

Convexity of (1-p)^n and Jensen’s inequality show that uniform guessing maximizes this success probability among fixed iid guessing distributions. Independent computation yields 0.6920538523 at n=10 and 0.9910071137 at n=40, consistent with 69% and above 99% [E1]. Concentrating all mass on two targets has limiting average success 2/9, which explains the approximate low baseline. Adaptive sampling without replacement is outside this iid comparison. Appendix B.2 (p. 16) says the Gemma curves are analytic functions of empirical token probabilities; this review did not reproduce those probabilities.

Preference loss and self-scoring. Equation (2), Appendix A.1 (p. 14), adds a term -log sigmoid(beta log[p_theta(y_c|x)/p_theta0(y_c|x)]) to the DPO loss. At fixed other probabilities it rewards increasing chosen-response likelihood. It is not a proof of monotonic improvement under shared-parameter optimization, and the paper reports empirical stabilization rather than claiming such a theorem. Section 5.5 (pp. 8-9) and Appendix B.3 (pp. 16-17) explicitly target the model’s own expressed preferences. Using that model as scorer matches this target; it does not establish human-assessed quality. The reported 3.8 versus 3.2 means remain descriptive and were not recomputed from raw responses.

# 3. Scope, dependence and unresolved evidence

The paper is a position article with proof-of-concept experiments. Section 3.2 (p. 5) allows that existing language could describe concepts at greater length; Section 4 (p. 6) states that neologisms do not solve faithfulness. Consequently, the experiments should support a proposed interface for communication and control, not a formal impossibility result for all existing vocabulary or proof of human understanding. No distinct substantive defect was demonstrated within this interpretation.

Length-control plots and the example tables were checked for internal consistency with their stated purpose, not independently reproduced. Exact held-out splits, raw generated responses, numerical token probabilities, parsing behavior, prompt variants and training logs were not available in the reviewed materials. Resolving generalization and performance uncertainty would require these artifacts or fresh model experiments. No author code was executed.

arXiv:2502.07586 lists only v1, submitted 11 February 2025 [S3]; it is an earlier preprint and was not substituted for the publisher PDF. The authors’ October 2025 blog identifies a separate follow-on work on controllability and self-verbalization [S4]. That follow-on is not an erratum and was not audited here. No correction was identified in the inspected sources; inaccessible discussion means this is not an exhaustive errata search.
