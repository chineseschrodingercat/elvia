Abstract: The original 38-page paper and its embedded supplement were reviewed. The overall assessment is Major: a stated Theorem 4.1 bound fails on an admissible scalar instance; the SAGA proof uses a false independence step and its unrestricted variance lemma has a counterexample; and the advertised nonmonotone example violates essential assumptions. Restricted repairs and counterevidence are supplied. Two application mappings also need correction. These findings do not establish algorithmic divergence or loss of the headline asymptotic order. Historical experiments remain unverified. Scientific adjudication is pending.

# Definitions and core dependencies

The paper solves \(0\in Gx+Tx\), with \(G=n^{-1}\sum_iG_i\). Assumptions 1.1–1.4 require a solution, maximal monotonicity of \(T\), averaged Lipschitz continuity \[\frac1n\sum_i\|G_ix-G_iy\|^2\le L^2\|x-y\|^2,\] and a weak-Minty solution \(x^\star\) satisfying \[\langle Gx+v,x-x^\star\rangle\ge-\kappa\|Gx+v\|^2
 \quad(x\in\operatorname{dom}(G+T),\ v\in Tx).\] For \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\) the reflected quantity is \(S_\gamma^k=Gx^k-\gamma Gx^{k-1}\). Definition 2.1 requires an unbiased estimator and \[\mathbb E\|\widetilde S_\gamma^k-S_\gamma^k\|^2\le\Delta_k,\qquad
 \Delta_k\le(1-\rho)\Delta_{k-1}+CU_k+\widehat C U_{k-1},\] where \(U_k=n^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb E\|G_ix^k-G_ix^{k-1}\|^2\). VFR updates \(x^{k+1}=x^k-\eta\widetilde S_\gamma^k\); VFRBS adds the implicit term \(-\eta[\gamma v^{k+1}-(2\gamma-1)v^k]\).

The variance recurrences feed the Lyapunov arguments in Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 and their SVRG/SAGA complexity corollaries. Their quantitative correctness is central to the contribution. A valid proof repair, or survival of an asymptotic rate, does not erase a false published derivation; it does bound its impact.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Theorem 4.1 has a false bound and an incomplete initial-potential calculation).  Major. This directly concerns the stated convergence guarantee for the new inclusion method.

## Claim and admissible counterexample

Equation reference: eq:bad. Theorem 4.1, Eq. 12 and the definition of \(\widehat\Theta_2\) (p. 7), state \[
 \frac1{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^K\mathbb E\|x^k-x^{k-1}\|^2
 \le\frac{\widehat\Theta_2\widehat R_0^2}{K+1},
 \quad
 \widehat\Theta_2=
 \frac{4(3\gamma-1)\widehat R_0^2}
 {(1-\gamma)(1-ML^2\eta^2)}.\] Thus the right side is quartic in initialization scale.

Take any number of identical components \(G_i\equiv0\) on \(\mathbb R\), \(Tx=(8/3)x\), \(L=1\), \(\kappa=0\), \(\gamma=3/4\), \(\eta=1/2\), \(\rho=1\), \(C=\widehat C=0\), and the exact zero estimator with \(\Delta_k=0\). Let \(x^\star=0\), \(x^{-1}=x^0=a=1/100\) and \(y^0=2a\). The zero set is nonempty; \(T\) is continuous, positive linear and maximally monotone; the Lipschitz inequality is \(0\le|x-y|^2\); and weak Minty is \((8/3)x^2\ge0\). Initialization satisfies \(x^0=J_{\gamma\eta T}y^0\). Moreover \[M=9/4,\quad \delta=1/5,\quad
 0<\eta=1/2\le2/3=1/(L\sqrt M).\] Every original hypothesis is satisfied; an upper Lipschitz bound need not be minimal.

The actual resolvent update gives \(x^1=5a/6\), while \(\widehat R_0^2=a^2+\gamma^2\eta^2\|Gx^0+v^0\|^2=2a^2\). At \(K=1\), \[\text{left side of \text{(eq:bad)}}=\frac{a^2}{72}
 =\frac1{720000}>
 \frac1{1093750}=\frac{640a^4}{7}
 =\text{printed right side}.\] This is a false stated bound, rather than only an undefined constant or a conjectured convergence failure.

## Separate proof defect and bounded repair

Appendix D, Eq. 30 (p. 23), defines \[\mathcal L_k=\|x^k+\gamma\eta(Gx^{k-1}+v^k)-x^\star\|^2
 +\mu\|x^k-x^{k-1}+\gamma\eta(Gx^{k-1}+v^k)\|^2.\] With \(\mu=(1-\gamma)/(3\gamma-1)\), p. 26 drops the positive second term at \(k=0\) when asserting \(\mathbb E\mathcal E_0\le2\widehat R_0^2\). In the same deterministic example, \(\mu=1/5\) and \[\mathcal E_0=21a^2/5>4a^2=2\widehat R_0^2.\] Deleting the extra \(\widehat R_0^2\) in [eq:bad] (#eq:bad) does not fix this independent derivation error.

Equation reference: eq:resrepair, eq:steprepair. A verified local replacement, by Young’s inequality, is \[\mathcal E_0=\|A+B\|^2+\mu\|B\|^2
 \le(2+\mu)(\|A\|^2+\|B\|^2)=(2+\mu)\widehat R_0^2,\] where \(A=x^0-x^\star\) and \(B=\gamma\eta(Gx^0+v^0)\). Using the correctly derived descent Eq. 36 gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \frac1{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^K\mathbb E\|Gx^k+v^k\|^2
 &\le(1+\mu/2)\frac{\widehat\Theta_1\widehat R_0^2}
 {\eta^2(K+1)},\\
 \frac1{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^K\mathbb E\|x^k-x^{k-1}\|^2
 &\le\frac{2(2+\mu)(3\gamma-1)\widehat R_0^2}
 {(1-\gamma)(1-ML^2\eta^2)(K+1)}.\end{aligned}\] For [eq:steprepair] (#eq:steprepair) the upper step restriction is strict. Equation [eq:resrepair] (#eq:resrepair) retains the paper’s strict lower step condition. These replacements require the variance recurrence and unbiasedness with respect to the full algorithmic history, as used in the descent proof. They repair the displayed estimate under those conditions; they do not certify the original iterate-only abstract filtration or historical runs. No divergence or failure of the residual order is inferred. Evidence: original pp. 7, 23–26 and , .

Problem 2 (The SAGA variance argument and quantitative specialization need correction).  Major. The unrestricted estimator lemma is false as stated, and a core step-size lower bound is false. The batch regime used for the main complexity result has a verified restricted repair.

## Source claim and dependence error

Lemma 2.3 (p. 5) and Appendix B.2, Eq. 20 (p. 18), prescribe \[\Delta_k=\frac1{nb}\sum_i\mathbb E\|G_ix^k-\gamma G_ix^{k-1}
 -(1-\gamma)\widehat G_i^k\|^2\] with \(\rho=b/(2n)\), \(C=[2(n-b)(2n+b)+b^2]/(nb^2)\) and \(\widehat C=2(n-b)(2n+b)\gamma^2/(nb^2)\). The proof replaces the updated table by a probability-weighted mixture of refreshed and unrefreshed expressions while leaving \(x^k\) inside an unconditional expectation. But \(x^k\) and that table update use the same batch \(B_{k-1}\).

This equality fails even on an admissible VFR trajectory. Set \(n=2\), \(b=1\), \(G_1x=x\), \(G_2x=2x\), \(x^0=1\), \(\gamma=3/4\) and \(\eta=1/20\), with the required initial table. Then \(x^1=157/160\) and the two \(x^2\) values are \(3083/3200\) and \(1543/1600\). Exact enumeration yields \[\Delta_2=\frac{4041}{4096000},\qquad
 \text{factorized expression}=\frac{1611}{1638400},
 \qquad \text{difference}=\frac{27}{8192000}>0.\] Here \(x^\star=0\), \(\kappa=0\), \(L^2=5/2\), and \(\eta^2L^2M=2983/3840<1\); all VFR hypotheses hold. Crucially, the recurrence’s right side is \(23823/3276800>\Delta_2\). This check refutes the intermediate equality, not convergence or the recurrence on this trajectory.

## Counterexample to the unrestricted stated recurrence

For Lemma 2.3 itself, take \(n=20\), \(b=19\), \(\gamma=3/4\), \(G_1x=x\) and \(G_ix=0\) for \(i>1\). Initialize \(x^{-2}=x^{-1}=x^0=0\) and the table at zero. Draw independent uniform 19-subsets. Choose the adapted sequence \[x^1=28/3,\qquad
 x^2=\begin{cases}28/3&1\in B_1,\\8&1\notin B_1,\end{cases}\] and let \(x^3=x^2\), except that \(x^3=8+760/99\) if both \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) omit index 1. Use precisely the paper’s table update. These are valid adapted iterates for the generic estimator lemma, which imposes no VFR update. The operators are Lipschitz (\(L^2=1/20\)) and their average is monotone with root zero. This is explicitly not a VFR trajectory.

Because each omission has probability \(1/20\), direct four-branch enumeration gives \[\Delta_2=\frac1{7600},\quad U_2=\frac1{225},\quad
 \Delta_3=\frac{229441}{372438000},\quad U_3=\frac{361}{49005}.\] With \(\rho=19/40\), \(C=479/7220\), \(\widehat C=531/57760\), \[\Delta_3-\{(1-\rho)\Delta_2+CU_3+\widehat C U_2\}
 =\frac{9943}{571824000}>0.\] Thus the asserted recurrence for the specified \(\Delta_k\) is false in its unrestricted generality. This does not show that no alternative variance sequence could work.

## Restricted repair and its exact scope

The counterexample uses \(b>n^{2/3}\), outside the complexity corollaries. Their variance recurrence can be proved without the false independence step. Set \(q=b/n\) and let \(I_i\) indicate non-refresh. Writing \(r_i\) for the previous component residual, \(s_i=G_ix^{k-1}-G_ix^{k-2}\) and \(d_i=G_ix^k-G_ix^{k-1}\), the exact identity is \[a_i^k=d_i+I_i(r_i-\gamma s_i).\] Only \(r_i,s_i\) are fixed before the previous draw; \(d_i\) may depend on it. For \(D=Cb=4/q-2-q>1\), Young’s inequality bounds this by \(D\|d_i\|^2+D(D-1)^{-1}I_i\|r_i-\gamma s_i\|^2\). After averaging \(I_i\), its second coefficient is \(A=(4-2q-q^2)/(q+4)\). The required domination \[A\|r-\gamma s\|^2
 \le(1-q/2)\|r\|^2+\gamma^2(4/q-2-2q)\|s\|^2\] has first principal minor \(q(q+2)/[2(q+4)]\) and determinant \(\gamma^2q(8-6q-3q^2)/[2(q+4)]\). It therefore holds for \(0<q\le(\sqrt{33}-3)/3\). Dividing by \(nb\) recovers the printed constants. Every \(b\le n^{2/3}\) with \(n\ge2\) lies in this range; \(n=b=1\) is checked directly. This is a verified repair of the relevant recurrence, not of the unrestricted lemma.

## A separate false step-size specialization

Corollary 3.3 (p. 6) asserts \(\eta=1/(L\sqrt M)\ge0.1494b^{3/2}/(nL)\). For \(n=1000\), \(b=100=n^{2/3}\), \(\gamma=3/4\), all \(G_ix=x\), \(L=1\) and \(\kappa=0\), its own constants give \[\rho=1/20,\quad C=379/1000,\quad
 \widehat C=1701/8000,\quad M=54913/1200.\] Hence \(\eta=0.14782675297<0.1494\), although all assumptions hold. The symbolic \(\sigma\) on pp. 22–23 evaluates to \(0.1341808523\), not \(0.14948\). The symbolic \(\Gamma\) evaluates to \(841.8947368\), not \(2815.8\); the chosen 2816 is nevertheless conservative.

Equation 9 additionally contains \(bp^2\) although the SAGA corollary defines no \(p\). A supported replacement at \(\gamma=3/4\) follows from \[\Lambda=\frac2b+\frac{4(1+\gamma^2)(2n^2-nb-b^2)}{b^3}
 \le\frac{8(1+\gamma^2)n^2}{b^3},
 \quad M\le\frac{1157n^2}{24b^3}.\] Theorem 3.1 then gives the valid rounded bound \[\frac1{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^K\mathbb E\|Gx^k\|^2
 \le\frac{526L^2\|x^0-x^\star\|^2n^2}{b^3(K+1)}\] under its hypotheses and the repaired variance recurrence. The exact intermediate coefficient is \(4724/9\). This preserves the stated asymptotic scaling; no trajectory violating an appropriately repaired residual rate is claimed. Evidence: pp. 5–6, 18, 22–23 and independent `e/saga.py`, `e/edge.py` with their exact JSON outputs.

Problem 3 (The advertised admissible nonmonotone example is ineligible).  Major. Appendix A.2 supplies the explicit argument, cited in the main assumption discussion, for the central extension beyond monotonicity.

A.2 (pp. 15–16) proposes, for any \(\epsilon>0\), \[G=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}-\epsilon&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \kappa=\epsilon.\] It repeatedly asserts \(G\succeq0\) and infers weak Minty and arbitrarily small admissible \(L\kappa\). In fact \(G\) has eigenvalues \(\pm1\), and \(T\) is not monotone, violating Assumption 1.2. With zero affine offset and solution \(x^\star=0\), the matrix that must be PSD is \[S=(G+T)+\epsilon(G+T)^\top(G+T)
 =\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^3&1-\epsilon^2\\1-\epsilon^2&\epsilon\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det S=2\epsilon^2-1.\] For \(\epsilon=1/100\) and \(x=(1,-1)\), the actual weak-Minty test is \(-2.01\ge-0.020201\), which is false. The paper’s intermediate matrix is PSD; adding the indefinite \(G\) is the invalid inference. These failures concern the claimed eligibility of this example, not a counterexample satisfying the abstract theorem.

A verified replacement shows the class is nonempty: \[T=0,\quad G=-\epsilon I+
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 L=\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2},\quad
 \kappa=\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon^2}.\] The symmetric part is \(-\epsilon I\) and \(G^\top G=(1+\epsilon^2)I\), so weak Minty holds with equality at zero. \(T\) is maximally monotone, zero is a solution, and \(L\kappa\to0\). Replicating \(G_i=G\) supplies a finite sum satisfying the averaged Lipschitz condition. Thus the core illustrative derivation fails, while the broader nonmonotone research claim survives this precise replacement. Evidence: pp. 15–16 and .

Problem 4 (The quadratic objective and its printed saddle operator differ).  Minor. The application mapping is wrong; no historical implementation binding establishes damage to the generic solver conclusions.

Equation 38 and the following display in E.2 (p. 32), also used to explain main Eq. 15, map \(u^\top A_i u+u^\top L_i v-v^\top B_i v+b_i^\top u-c_i^\top v\) to a block operator with diagonal blocks \(A_i,B_i\) and lower-left block \(-L_i\). For the stated symmetric \(A_i,B_i\), differentiation instead gives \[G_i(u,v)=
 \begin{pmatrix}2A_i&L_i\\-L_i^\top&2B_i\end{pmatrix}
 \binom uv+\binom{b_i}{c_i}.\] The displayed lower block is also dimensionally invalid for permitted \(p_1\ne p_2\). For an explicit unconstrained admissible case, set \(A=B=1\), \(L=2\), \(b=1\), \(c=0\), and replicate components if desired. The printed operator has root \((-1/5,-2/5)\), where the correct saddle gradient is \((-1/5,-2/5)\ne0\). Both smooth maps are Lipschitz and monotone; this comparison needs no nonmonotonicity assumption.

The correction above is verified directly. Absorbing a factor of two into differently defined matrices would change the published objective, and the reported equal dimensions do not repair the omitted transpose for nonsymmetric \(L_i\). Actual saved matrices or code could establish a correct implementation; they are absent. The finding does not show that the historical curves came from the wrong operator. Evidence: p. 32 and .

Problem 5 (The logistic reformulation changes the stated uncertainty model).  Minor. A local modeling equivalence fails, while the displayed alternative remains a legitimate optimization problem.

E.3(a), p. 34, defines \(f_i(w)=\max_j\ell(\langle X_{ij},w\rangle,y_i)\), but Eq. 39/main Eq. 16 uses one common \(z\in\Delta_m\) for all observations. Consequently its maximized loss is \[\max_j\frac1N\sum_i\ell_{ij}(w),
 \quad\text{instead of}\quad
 \frac1N\sum_i\max_j\ell_{ij}(w).\] For \(N=m=2\), \(w=1\), \(y_1=y_2=0\) and \(X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&-2\\-2&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), the specified logistic loss gives \(h=\log(1+e^2)\) and \(l=\log(1+e^{-2})\). The intended samplewise worst-case value is \(h=2.1269280110\); the printed shared-scenario value is \((h+l)/2=1.1269280110\). The gap is exactly one, and adding the same \(\tau|w|\) leaves it unchanged. All inputs and binary labels satisfy the model’s definitions.

A precise repair uses separate \(z_i\in\Delta_m\) and \(\max_{z_1,\ldots,z_N}N^{-1}\sum_{ij}(z_i)_j\ell_{ij}\). Alternatively, explicitly assume a shared scenario index across the dataset; that is a different uncertainty model. The generic convergence analysis can apply to a correctly specified problem of either kind. There are no reported classification or robust-accuracy results to refute here, and the historical effect requires a source-bound rerun. Evidence: p. 34 and .

Problem 6 (Experimental nonmonotonicity and oracle-cost comparisons need run bindings).  Needs Evidence. The inspected record does not settle these empirical claims.

Section 5.1 and E.2 (pp. 8, 32–33) assert or motivate nonconvex–nonconcave tests by generating possibly indefinite component matrices. This does not certify indefinite averages or an admissible weak-Minty constant for each actual instance. For example, \(\operatorname{diag}(-0.1,1)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-0.1)\) are both indefinite but average to \(0.45I\). Actual aggregate spectra, Lipschitz estimates, \(\kappa\) tests, seeds and saved instances are missing. This example does not prove that any historical instance was monotone.

Figures 1–13 use epochs, but the full-snapshot, multiple-minibatch and diagnostic-residual costs lack a recoverable common plotting convention. E.1 describes \(\lfloor n/b\rfloor\) inner iterations per double-loop round, which alone does not make that round cost \(n\) component evaluations. E.3 uses different algorithm step sizes without clearly identifying a common evaluation parameter for the FBS residual \(G_\eta\). The correct empirical cost and residual-comparison bindings require code and logs, not a guessed correction.

Substantial counterevidence limits any allegation of unfairness: synthetic results average ten instances; batch size and refresh probability are varied; enlarged steps and manual tuning are disclosed; the unstable MNIST baseline is explicitly omitted; and some competitive SAGA/VEG behavior is acknowledged. These are optimization-residual demonstrations. The logistic objective is convex–concave on its feasible domain. The synthetic WGAN has component form \(G_i(x)=Ax+c_i\), so same-batch SVRG cancels sampling noise exactly; that valid simple example has limited variance-reduction stress, while the other experiments broaden coverage. No benchmark execution or ranking reversal is established.

# Overall dependence, retained results and remaining work

Theorem 4.1’s false quantitative statement and proof, the SAGA lemma/specialization, and the failed eligibility argument are separately tied to core theoretical claims. Their supported assessment is Major under the assigned standard, even though restricted repairs preserve substantial content. The quadratic and logistic mappings are separately assessed Minor because historical damage to the solver comparison has not been demonstrated. Missing experiments do not raise any supported severity.

The VFR residual proof in Appendix C and the main descent algebra in D were checked without finding a further decisive counterexample. The implementable resolvent form is consistent with the main VFRBS update. Fresh uniform batches, conditioned on the full pre-draw state including memory and \(v^k\), give unbiasedness. The paper’s narrower \(\mathcal F_k=\sigma(x^0,\ldots,x^k)\) does not alone justify every VFRBS product cancellation involving \(v^k\); the broader abstract class remains unverified. Uniform sampling without replacement changes an i.i.d. variance equality to an upper bound, which is sufficient.

Oracle counts \(np+3b\) for loopless SVRG are expected counts; SAGA’s three-minibatch accounting is conservative relative to two fresh evaluations. The replacements of \(b=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor\) by \(n^{2/3}\) are not exact identities, but bounded rounding preserves the order. Constants with \(1-ML^2\eta^2\) in their denominators are not finite at the allowed endpoint; this is not evidence of divergence. No separate current-literature priority audit, later-version comparison or erratum was available in the frozen source set. No known correction has been silently attributed to the original.
