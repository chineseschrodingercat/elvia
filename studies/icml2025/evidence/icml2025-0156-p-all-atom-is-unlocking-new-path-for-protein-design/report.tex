Abstract: The inspected public sampler adds churn noise and performs its reverse update at the inflated noise level, while conditioning the denoiser on the original level. This conflicts with Algorithm 1 and its preconditioning formulas. A legal default-parameter step gives noise arguments 16 and 19.2, so the discrepancy is substantive rather than a field-name ambiguity. The supported assessment is Minor for the pinned release; effects on published protein-design metrics remain Needs Evidence. Counterevidence excludes broader allegations about unacknowledged representation degeneracy, universally invalid exported structures, or absent stereochemical evaluation. No author code or model inference was executed. Adjudication remains pending.\par

\# Scope and necessary background\par
Pallatom models protein coordinates using an atom14 representation, a diffusion denoiser and a sequence decoder. The main scientific text, key metric definitions and tables, sampling and representation appendices, and the source paths needed to trace noise conditioning were inspected. Coverage of dense graphical panels is limited to their descriptions and reported comparisons; there was no new quantitative image analysis or protein generation.\par

\# Substantive finding\par
Problem 1 (The released sampler does not propagate churn inflation to the denoiser).  Assessment: Minor. Algorithm 1, lines 12–24, p. 5, specifies that the denoiser receives the same inflated level used to form its noisy input and reverse update. In the pinned source, the model receives the uninflated level. This is a static specification-to-release discrepancy, with unresolved impact on the historical experiments.\par

\#\# Why the argument can matter\par
The preconditioning formulas already show that replacing 16 by 19.2 changes the denoiser computation. An elementary illustration makes the distinction explicit. If a scalar clean coordinate has prior variance \(v=256\) and is corrupted with independent Gaussian noise of variance \(\sigma^2\), its posterior-mean denoiser is \[D(x;\sigma)=\frac{v}{v+\sigma^2}x.\] At \(x=1\), the two arguments yield \[D(1;16)=0.5,\qquad
D(1;19.2)=0.4098360655737705.\] This analytic example proves that the arguments are not interchangeable in general. It is not a measured error of Pallatom, a model of its exact training distribution, or an estimate of a protein-design score change.\par
\#\# Alternative readings and counterevidence\par
When \(\gamma=0\), the discrepancy reduces to the release’s small epsilon. Outside the churn window there is no substantial inflation. The documented defaults activate the discrepancy for the legal example above.\par
A deliberately different sampler heuristic could be valid or useful. However, the paper’s Algorithm 1 passes the inflated level, and the release does not document this particular departure. Weights and historical run logs were not inspected. Consequently the result supports a current-release reproducibility concern; it does not establish that Tables 1–16 used this implementation or that the published metrics are wrong.\par
There are further correspondence questions: the printed algorithm uses two within-step trunk calls with updated self-conditioning, whereas the inspected source uses one and updates the template for the next step. Its Fourier time embedding also overwrites an initially logarithmic quantity with a raw-noise expression. These are retained as historical provenance limitations, not additional unbounded experimental allegations.\par
\#\# Correction and what it would establish\par
For correspondence with the published noise argument, update the denoiser’s noise-level field and its derived features from \(\widehat{\sigma}\), preserving masks, shapes and numerical types. This restores that one argument relationship algebraically. It does not validate a complete repaired sampler or guarantee better samples. A full reconciliation must also address the self-conditioning and time-embedding differences, followed by evaluation with the actual trained weights. No code modification, model rerun or author adoption was performed.\par
\# Checked counterevidence and surviving conclusions\par
Appendix C.1, p. 13, explicitly acknowledges that the atom14 geometry can be ambiguous among chemically different residues. Main §3.3 and its SeqHead address sequence decoding. This review therefore does not allege an undisclosed one-to-one geometry-to-sequence theorem.\par
The terminal source step can form an internal zero-noise reverse expression. However, exports the pre-update denoised `px0` and sequence logits. This dataflow is counterevidence to an inference that all exported structures must contain NaNs. No such empirical failure is established.\par
Appendix G.2, pp. 23–28, reports bond-length, bond-angle, chi-angle and clash analyses. It would be inaccurate to describe the study as lacking all stereochemical checks. Table 1 also discloses the special C-alpha basis of the Multiflow comparison. The paper’s numerical designability and self-consistency results were not independently reproduced; wet-lab success is not inferred from them or imposed as an unstated requirement.\par
The training-count arithmetic \(7459+27697=35156\) agrees with the reported examples per epoch. These bounded consistency checks and explicit limitations are retained independently of the identified release discrepancy.\par
