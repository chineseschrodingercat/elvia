# 4. Problem 1: the stated test rule and distance range cannot both hold

## Original claim and suspect step

On p.7, section 6.2 describes seven ordered stimuli A through G and novel test pairs that exclude the terminal elements. Section 7.3 on the same page reports tests at symbolic distances 2-6. The distance definition in Figure 2, p.8, is rank separation. [Rane-PMLR (2025)]

## Complete finite check

After removing A and G, the available ranks are B, C, D, E, F. Enumerating all unordered nonadjacent pairs gives exactly:

Pair | Distance

B,D | 2

B,E | 3

B,F | 4

C,E | 2

C,F | 3

D,F | 2

Thus the admissible distance set is {2,3,4}, with maximum 4. Distance 5 requires A,F or B,G; distance 6 requires A,G. All of those pairs contain a terminal element. Reversing the display order of a pair does not change its rank separation. The finite enumeration was independently executed using the standard library and saved in verification/0018/independent_results.json.

## Counterevidence and alternative readings

The reported range could be a carryover from Experiment 1, which includes all pairs. Alternatively, the actual Experiment 2 tests may have included endpoints. The abbreviated Figure 1 schematic is not a trial log and cannot decide between these possibilities.

A nearby statement assigning B an always-incorrect status is also inconsistent with B being an interior member of the adjacent chain; it plausibly intends G. This is treated as related descriptive ambiguity, not as a second independent defect or evidence that the experiment used incorrect labels.

## Impact and conditional repair

Judgment: Minor, a bounded methods/results incompatibility. Endpoint exclusion matters because endpoints have uniformly reinforced status, so choices involving them can be answered without general nonadjacent inference. The inconsistency prevents a precise reconstruction of the intended control. It does not show that the reported 0.40 accuracy is false.

If endpoint exclusion describes the implementation, the admissible range is 2-4; this conditional mathematical correction is verified. If distances 5 or 6 were actually tested, the method description and endpoint-free interpretation require revision. The actual pair list must determine the repair, followed by recalculation of any affected summaries. No empirical repair has been verified or adopted.

# 5. Problem 2: chance-performance and uncertainty claims cannot yet be independently verified

## Claim, missing information, and precise uncertainty

Section 7.3 characterizes Experiment 2 performance as at chance and reports no distance differences. Table 1 gives accuracy 0.40 and SD 0.50. Figure 2 presents 95% intervals and fitted lines for Experiment 1. The text specifies ten task iterations and sixty training trials per iteration, but does not establish the independent test-response denominator or the SD’s aggregation unit. The inspected materials also do not identify a reproducible model snapshot, inference settings, complete response history, or sufficient GLM family/link and uncertainty details. [Rane-PMLR (2025), pp.6-8]

Ten iterations cannot automatically be equated with ten test observations, and the six hundred training presentations are not test responses. An SD near 0.50 can be compatible with binary outcomes near accuracy 0.4; it is not evidence of an arithmetic error. The Experiment 2 paragraph’s pointer to Figure 2 does not supply its missing trial data because that figure describes Experiment 1.

## Independent check of what accuracy alone can establish

To illustrate dependence on the missing denominator, consider hypothetical independent Bernoulli responses under a known null probability 0.5. An exact two-sided binomial test sums probabilities no greater than the observed probability. Two hypothetical samples have identical accuracy 0.40:

Hypothetical sample | Accuracy | Exact two-sided p-value

4 correct of 10 | 0.40 | 0.75390625

400 correct of 1000 | 0.40 | 2.7284641560660184e-10

These are independently calculated illustrations, not reconstructions of the paper’s data or its SD. They prove that an accuracy value alone does not determine its statistical relation to chance. Actual observations may also be clustered within conversations or task instances, so an independent-binomial calculation could be inappropriate even after obtaining a raw count.

Failure to reject a chance null is not, by itself, an equivalence result. A formal equivalence claim would additionally require a justified tolerance and suitable uncertainty calculation.

## Counterevidence, impact, and repair status

Judgment: Needs Evidence for independent statistical verification. The authors may intend a descriptive characterization of 40% as roughly near a 50% baseline; that reading is acknowledged and is not disproved. Likewise, all six displayed fitted slopes are visually positive, consistent with their sign claim. No contrary numerical result is asserted.

The unresolved material is needed to assess sampling uncertainty, distance effects, and reproducibility. Release the actual test pairs and responses, independent units, aggregation rules, model/inference metadata, and uncertainty procedure. Reanalysis should respect dependence and distinguish estimation, a difference test, and equivalence. The published estimates, intervals, and fitted coefficients have not been independently reproduced.

# 6. Mechanism and scope checks: no additional demonstrated defect

## Inductive transfer versus deduction from declared ordinal premises

Figure 1C asks the system to learn correct word choices through feedback; it does not announce that the hidden relation is transitive. A concrete countermodel shows the resulting inference boundary. Let one rule choose the earlier item in A>B>C>D>E>F>G. Let a second rule agree everywhere except for the unseen B,D pair, where it chooses D. Both rules make identical predictions for all six trained adjacent pairs, and hence for all sixty repetitions, but they disagree on B,D. The second rule has a cycle B>C>D>B.

Therefore, the training labels do not uniquely entail the intended nonadjacent answer without a structural assumption. The independent checker confirms equal training predictions and different B,D predictions. This does not invalidate the task: spontaneous adoption of the intended structure is a legitimate behavioral capacity to test. It means that failed transfer can reflect failure to infer or deploy the hidden ordering, rather than absence of an ordinal computation once suitable premises are represented.

The same reasoning limits the assertion on p.7 that using a common mechanism across contexts would necessarily give similar performance. Encoding, memory, instruction comprehension, and elicitation can affect the observed outcome even when a reasoning component is available. The paper’s P4 and P5 explicitly recognize related behavior/mechanism and performance/competence distinctions. Its discussion also favors context-dependent capability statements. A claim about the observed protocol is supported more directly than a universal mechanism claim.

## A symbolic-distance pattern does not uniquely identify TI

For Experiment 1, consider an illustrative rule that memorizes the adjacent choices, always selects A if present in a novel pair, avoids G if present, and otherwise guesses. It has no general solution for interior novel comparisons. At a nonadjacent distance d, there are 7-d unordered pairs; the expected accuracy is (endpoint_pairs + 0.5 * other_pairs)/(7-d).

Distance | Total pairs | Endpoint-containing pairs | Expected accuracy

2 | 5 | 2 | 0.70

3 | 4 | 2 | 0.75

4 | 3 | 2 | 0.8333333333333334

5 | 2 | 2 | 1.00

6 | 1 | 1 | 1.00

The checker independently enumerates this curve. It demonstrates an alternative source of a positive distance association, without claiming that GPT-4o actually used this strategy or that the curve matches every plotted value. Endpoint-free and position-controlled comparisons would help discriminate mechanisms.

The authors describe behavior analogous to the symbolic distance effect; they do not prove a unique animal-like mechanism from the curve alone. All six fitted lines in Figure 2B point upward. Intermediate dips in Figure 2A do not contradict a positive fitted association. These are scope clarifications, not further confirmed errors.

## Causality motivation

The cited Halpern paper studies sufficient conditions for causal transitivity and explicitly discusses failures of transitivity in general. It does not establish ordinal TI as a necessary substrate of every causal judgment. The reviewed article’s motivation should be read with that qualification; it does not explicitly claim that every causal relation is transitive. This source check therefore does not refute the ordinal experiments or create another finding. [Halpern (2016), author PDF pp.2,6-8]
