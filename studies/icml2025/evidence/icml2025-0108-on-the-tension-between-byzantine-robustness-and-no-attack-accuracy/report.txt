Abstract: The sharp aggregation lower bound and the trimmed-mean upper bound are supported by their exact constructions and corrected variance decomposition. Theorem 4.6 nevertheless states a false finite-time convergence guarantee: an admissible three-worker quadratic example gives \(4/9\leq0\). The lower-bound proof also contains an invalid universal divergence step; its theorem can be recovered by an explicit case analysis supplied here. These are Major findings concerning core convergence statements and derivations, with surviving results and repairs distinguished. A separate main-text variance normalization error is local. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Identity, scope, and mathematical setting

Paper 0108, I25-43477, by Yi-Rui Yang, Chang-Wei Shi, and Wu-Jun Li, is the supplied ICML 2025/PMLR publication [paper], 22 pages. Main text and Appendix A.1–A.7 were read. Page references below are 1-based PDF pages, identical to printed article pages. The critical expressions on pages 4, 6, 20, and 21 were additionally checked against page images.

The supplied manifest identifies no acquired author repository, and separate OpenReview supplement availability is unresolved. The appendix is present in the original PDF. No author code, raw experiment outputs, or later author correction was inspected. The review is an exact mathematical/static assessment with independent scalar diagnostics, not a reproduction of the ResNet experiments.

An \((f,\kappa)\)-robust aggregator must satisfy its error inequality for every subset of \(n-f\) inputs. An \(\epsilon\)-accurate aggregator bounds squared distance to the all-worker mean by \(\epsilon\) times all-worker variance. Here \(f\) is a tolerated number of Byzantine workers; all examples in this review have no actual Byzantine workers. The convergence assumptions are a lower-bounded \(L\)-smooth global objective, globally bounded gradient heterogeneity \(G^2\), and, for the PL conclusions, the stated PL inequality. Theorem 4.5 uses a constant positive step size and an arbitrarily-late-existence quantifier for \(T\), rather than a lower bound at every finite \(T\).

# Numbered findings

Equation reference: eq:bad. Problem 1 (Major: Theorem 4.6 has a false finite-time upper bound).  On page 6, the first display in Theorem 4.6 asserts, for step size \(1/L\), \[
 \frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2
 \leq \frac{2L[F^*-F(w_T)]}{T}+\epsilon G^2 .\] The initial objective value has been replaced by a lower bound in the wrong position. The printed nonpositive transient term is not a valid upper bound.

#### Counterexample and every operative assumption.

Set \(n=3\), \(f=1\), \(d=1\), and use the scalar median. For any selected pair of inputs, their median with the third input lies between the pair’s endpoints; its squared deviation from that pair’s mean is at most the pair variance. Thus the aggregator is \((1,1)\)-robust. It is also \(1/2\)-accurate, as follows from the checked trimmed-mean bound with \(n=3,f=1\).

Choose \[F_1(w)=\tfrac12w^2,\qquad F_2(w)=F_3(w)=\tfrac12w^2-w .\] Then \[F(w)=\tfrac12(w-\tfrac23)^2-\tfrac29,\qquad
 F^*=-\tfrac29,\qquad L=\mu=1 .\] The three gradients are \(w,w-1,w-1\), so \[\nabla F(w)=w-\tfrac23,\qquad
 \tfrac13\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\nabla F_i(w)-\nabla F(w)|^2=\tfrac29=G^2\] for every real \(w\). All assumptions, including global bounded heterogeneity, smoothness, bounded loss, and PL, hold. The functions are convex, which is permitted by the general smooth-objective theorem.

Let \(w_0=0\), \(\eta=1=1/L\), and \(T=1\). The aggregated gradient is \(-1\), hence \(w_1=1\) and \(F(w_1)=-1/6\). The two sides of [eq:bad] (#eq:bad) are \[\text{left}=\tfrac49,\qquad
 \text{right}=2(-\tfrac29+\tfrac16)+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac29=0 .\] The independently written exact-rational check records this discrepancy. It involves neither a limiting argument nor a vanishing-heterogeneity edge case.

#### Correction and counterevidence.

Equation reference: eq:correct. Appendix A.7, page 21, correctly derives \[F(w_{t+1})-F(w_t)
 \leq-\frac{\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2}{2L}+\frac{\epsilon G^2}{2L}\] and then the valid telescoping bound with \(F(w_0)-F(w_T)\). The next appeal to \(F(w_T)\geq F^*\) supports \[
 \frac1T\sum_{t<T}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2
 \leq\frac{2L[F(w_0)-F^*]}{T}+\epsilon G^2,\] not [eq:bad] (#eq:bad). The telescoping version is exactly tight in the counterexample: both sides equal \(4/9\); the uniform lower-bound version has right side \(5/9\).

Equation [eq:correct] (#eq:correct) is proved by summing the displayed one-step bound and substituting the lower bound in the correct term. It uses the same assumptions and restores the asymptotic constant \(\epsilon G^2\). The separate PL upper bound follows from the usual scalar recurrence and is not refuted. The error is plausibly an endpoint substitution mistake, but it changes a principal finite-time guarantee in the paper’s core tightness argument. Its short repair and survival of the qualitative tradeoff do not reduce this finding below Major.

Problem 2 (Major: the lower-bound proof’s unstable-step argument is invalid as stated).  Appendix A.6, case (iii), page 21, claims \(w_t\to+\infty\) whenever \(\eta>2\sqrt{f(n-f)}/(nG)\), and concludes the lower bound from divergence. This omits an admissible fixed initialization and also ignores alternating signs. Theorem 4.5 explicitly quantifies over every initial point.

The proof has already obtained \[w_t=1+r^t(w_0-1),\qquad
 r=1-\frac{nG\eta}{\sqrt{f(n-f)}}.\] Use the same quadratic example above, for which \(nG/\sqrt{f(n-f)}=1\), but set \(\eta=3>2\) and \(w_0=1\). Then \(r=-2\) and \(w_t=1\) for all \(t\). Neither the objective nor the squared gradient diverges. Their actual values are \[|\nabla F(w_t)|^2=\tfrac19,\qquad F(w_t)-F^*=\tfrac1{18}.\] The invalid divergence step is used to complete the arbitrary-step, arbitrary-initialization lower-bound proof, a core contribution. This is a demonstrated proof defect, not a counterexample to the theorem’s conclusion.

#### Full case repair for the construction.

For \(f>0,G>0\), put \[a=\frac{nG}{\sqrt{f(n-f)}}=\mu,\quad
 d=\frac fn,\quad u=w_0-1,\quad
 c=a^2d^2=\frac{f}{n-f}G^2,\quad r=1-a\eta.\] Then \[|\nabla F(w_t)|^2=a^2(d+r^tu)^2,\qquad
 F(w_t)-F^*=\frac a2(d+r^tu)^2 .\] If \(u=0\), these equal \(c\) and \(c/(2\mu)\) at every time, for every positive step size. If \(|r|<1\), they converge to those same limits, so both required strict inequalities hold eventually for every \(C_1<c\). If \(r=-1\), the average squared gradient converges to \(a^2(d^2+u^2)\geq c\); choosing the infinitely repeated parity with the larger terminal gap gives at least \(c/(2\mu)\). If \(r<-1\) and \(u\ne0\), \(|w_t|\) diverges, and both squared-gradient averages and objective gaps diverge. This proves the desired simultaneous inequalities at arbitrarily large \(T\). The zero-floor cases can be handled separately since \(C_1<0\) and both relevant quantities are nonnegative.

This case analysis also avoids Appendix A.6’s self-referential formula for \(T\) on page 20. The proof there treats a lower bound on each two-step squared-gradient sum as an equality. Using the period average as above supplies the missing justification without that formula. Thus the lower-bound result survives the explicit repair. Major attaches to the invalid core derivation, not to a claim that its underlying theorem is false.

Problem 3 (Minor: the main-text middle-group variance uses the wrong divisor).  Page 4, Section 3.4.1, defines \(s_{I_0}^2\) with divisor \(f\), even though \(I_0\) has \(n-2f\) elements. Equation (7) on page 5 then weights it by \((n-2f)/n\), as though it were the usual middle-group variance. The resulting printed identity is false when the middle group has nonzero variance and \(n\ne3f\).

Let \(n=5,f=1\) and \(x=(0,1,2,3,4)\). Both the full mean and trimmed mean are 2. The full variance is 2, the two mean gaps are both 2, and the printed middle variance is 2. Equation (7)’s right side is \[0+\frac25(2\cdot2)+\frac35(2)=\frac{14}{5}\ne2 .\] The correct middle variance is \(2/3\), giving the correct right side 2. Appendix A.3, page 16, explicitly uses the correct divisor \(n-2f\), and its subsequent derivation is valid. Thus this is a local inconsistency in the proof sketch with a valid supplied full proof as counterevidence. Correct the main-text definition; the trimmed-mean theorem and its equality conditions remain supported.

# Results checked without a substantive issue

For Theorem 3.1, the \(n-f\) identical zero inputs force the robust aggregator to return zero. With the remaining \(f\) inputs equal to \(A\), its squared error is \(f^2A^2/n^2\), and all-input variance is \(f(n-f)A^2/n^2\). Their ratio is exactly \(f/(n-f)\). This establishes the universal no-attack floor under the stated robustness definition.

For Theorem 3.2, averaging over all subsets of size \(m=n-f\) gives expected within-subset variance \[\frac{n(m-1)}{m(n-1)}\,V\leq V,\qquad
 V=\frac1n\sum_i\|x_i-\bar x\|^2 .\] Jensen’s inequality for the average subset mean then proves the \(\kappa\)-accuracy statement. The exact finite subset check agrees with the identity.

With the corrected variance definition, Appendix A.3 establishes \[V=\frac{n-f}{f}(\mathrm{TM}_{f/n}-\bar x)^2+
 \frac{2f}{n}\Delta_-\Delta_++\widetilde s^2\] in one dimension, with all remainder terms nonnegative. Summing coordinate inequalities gives the vector bound. A finite exact-arithmetic check over sorted inputs from \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\), \(3\leq n\leq9\), and every positive integer \(f<n/2\) finds no violation and attains the claimed ratio for each \((n,f)\). This diagnostic supplements the proof rather than replacing it. The \(f=0\) case is the exact mean and should be treated separately from formulas dividing by \(f\).

The coordinate median result follows for the usual convention averaging the two central values at even \(n\). The geometric-median upper bound follows from its minimizing property, the triangle inequality, and Cauchy–Schwarz, as in Appendix A.5. Its displayed tightness example allows any minimizer in the nonunique scalar even-sample case; it should not be read as proving that every fixed tie-breaking rule attains that same example.

# Empirical scope and overall impact

Section 5 and Tables 3–6 were read and checked for their reported direction and arithmetic. They show worse displayed no-attack accuracies as the specified robustness parameters increase in the tested setups. They do not prove monotonic degradation on every dataset. The mathematical result concerns the sharp worst-case envelope, rather than an ordering of every possible pair of robust algorithms.

The paper explicitly selects the best final test accuracy among initial learning rates. For claims beyond those reported tuned comparisons, per-seed results, fixed validation selection, uncertainty, and exact aggregation/NNM configurations are needed. No code or run logs were acquired, so experimental robustness conditions, historical implementation choices, and reproducibility remain unverified. External provenance for inherited Table 1 constants and the full priority claim was not independently audited.

The strongest supported assessment is Major because a central upper guarantee is false and a central lower-bound proof contains invalid case reasoning. The corrected upper bound, repaired lower construction, aggregation floor, trimmed-mean optimal constant, and qualitative worst-case tension survive. No benchmark-score loss or whole-paper failure is claimed.
