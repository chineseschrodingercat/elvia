Abstract: The published spectral formula and the condition-number decrease guarantee are checked by exact two-dimensional calculations. Proposition 3.2 mistakes diagonal entries for eigenvalues; Theorem 4.3(1) permits a step that increases the condition number from 4 to 9. Both affect core theoretical arguments and support Major findings. A separate transpose inconsistency in the released preconditioner is Minor because its effect on the published experiments is unknown. These findings do not invalidate the reported empirical observations. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and checked dependencies

The model uses a square linear feature map \(x^\top\theta\) and \(K=X^\top X\succeq0\), with \(H(\theta)=\theta^\top K\theta\). The paper defines \(\kappa(S)=\|S\|_2\|S^+\|_2\); all counterexamples below have full rank, so zero-singular-value conventions are immaterial. The least-squares Hessian has an inessential factor of two under the unhalved loss in (8); we use the paper’s exact \(H\) convention. The minimizing regularizer is \[R_{\rm well}(H)=\tfrac12\|H\|_2^2-\tfrac1{2d}\|H\|_F^2.\] At a unique largest eigenvalue \(a\) with eigenvector \(v\), its feature gradient is \[\nabla_\theta R_{\rm well}=2K\theta(avv^\top-H/d),\] which follows directly by \(dH=(d\theta)^\top K\theta+\theta^\top Kd\theta\). This validates the gradient used in the second counterexample independently of the disputed step-size argument. Section 4.4 explicitly says separate regularizer guarantees do not automatically combine for the full objective; the review respects that limitation.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The spectrum in Proposition 3.2 is not given by row norms).

Source and assumptions. Proposition 3.2, (10), p. 3, and B.1, pp. 13–14, purport to give every singular value of \(H\) as \[\sigma_i=\sum_j[\sigma_{\theta,i}(u_{\theta,i}^\top q_j)\gamma_j]^2.\] The text calls \(\gamma_j\) the singular values of \(K\), whereas B.1 writes \(K=Q\Gamma^2Q^\top\). We first use the more favorable latter interpretation, so \(\gamma_j^2\) are the eigenvalues of \(K\).

Exact check. Set \(d=N=2\), \(\theta=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\) and \[K=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad X=K^{1/2},\qquad
H=\begin{pmatrix}8&2\\2&2\end{pmatrix}.\] \(K\) is positive definite with eigenvalues 3 and 1; \(\theta\) is invertible with distinct singular values. This is an admissible square linear feature extractor with a genuine data Gram matrix. Labels may be zero, as they do not affect \(H\). Choosing \(U_\theta=I\) and \(q_1=(1,1)^\top/\sqrt2\), \(q_2=(1,-1)^\top/\sqrt2\), the formula gives 8 and 2. But \[\det(tI-H)=t^2-10t+12,\qquad
\operatorname{spec}(H)=\{5+\sqrt{13},5-\sqrt{13}\},\] approximately \(\{8.605551,1.394449\}\). Both predicted eigenvalues are wrong. If the statement is instead read literally with \(\gamma_j=3,1\), it gives 20 and 5 and remains false.

Failed step and counterevidence. With \(M=\Sigma_\theta U_\theta^\top Q\Gamma\), B.1 correctly obtains \(H=V_\theta MM^\top V_\theta^\top\). It then calls the row-normalized \(M\) an orthonormal matrix and treats row norms as singular values. In this example the normalized row inner product is \(1/2\), not zero. Normalizing lengths does not remove correlations. The proposed factorization also places the row scaling on the wrong side. The expression is valid in aligned cases where \(U_\theta^\top KU_\theta\) is diagonal; alignment is not a hypothesis of Proposition 3.2.

Impact and supported correction. This is Major for the spectral derivation used in Section 3.1 to explain the role of singular-vector angles. The exact general replacement is the eigenvalue list of \(\Sigma_\theta U_\theta^\top KU_\theta\Sigma_\theta\), equivalently the squared singular values of \(K^{1/2}\theta\). It is not the list of its diagonal entries. This identity is proved by orthogonal similarity and restores the spectrum, but does not establish every subsequent qualitative existence claim. The independent numerical output is `check_output.json`, entry `proposition_3_2`.

Problem 2 (Theorem 4.3(1) admits a condition-number increase).

Source. Theorem 4.3(1), pp. 4–5, repeated in B.4, pp. 20–22, asserts strict decrease after \(\theta'=\theta-\eta K^{-1}\nabla_\theta R_{\rm well}\) when the largest eigenvalue \(a>b\) is unique and \[0<\eta<\min\left\{\frac1{(1-1/d)a},\frac{\sqrt{ab}-b}{(2/d^2)b^2}\right\}.\] Exact check of every condition. Take \(d=2\), \(X=K=I_2\), \(\theta=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\), \(H=\operatorname{diag}(4,1)\), rank two, and \(\eta=1/5\). The maximum 4 is unique, \(K^{-1}\) exists, and the displayed upper bound is \(\min\{1/2,2\}=1/2\), strictly exceeding \(1/5\). If a harmful-task covariance is needed to instantiate the common notation, set \(K_H=I_2\) also; no constraint from part (2) is needed for part (1). The valid gradient above gives \[\nabla_\theta R_{\rm well}=\operatorname{diag}(8,-1),\quad
\theta'=\operatorname{diag}(2/5,6/5),\quad
H'=\operatorname{diag}(4/25,36/25).\] Thus \(\kappa(H')=9>4=\kappa(H)\). Both old and new matrices are positive definite, and even the feature diagonal entries stay positive. No singular limit, sign ambiguity, stochasticity or combined-loss interaction is used.

Proof diagnosis and alternatives. The expansion on p. 21 is correct: the old largest mode becomes \(a[1-2\eta(1-1/d)a]^2\) and the other modes become \(b_i(1+2\eta b_i/d)^2\). Page 22 then claims the stated bound preserves their order. Here the old largest mode is \(4/25\) and the other is \(36/25\), so that step fails. A smaller step can work: for this example \(0<\eta<3/17\) yields strict decrease, excluding the singular step \(1/4\) automatically. This example-specific interval is not offered as a universal repaired theorem.

Impact. Major: monotone regularizer updates are an explicit contribution in the introduction, and Section 4.3 provides the theoretical justification of Algorithm 1. The invalidity is already present in the isolated regularizer step, beyond the acknowledged caveat about combining gradients. It neither disproves empirical effectiveness nor proves that the published learning rates caused the failure. Evidence: `check_output.json`, `theorem_4_3_1`.

Problem 3 (The dummy-layer preconditioner acts on the transposed axis).

Figure 4, p. 26, and released `models/linear.py`, lines 15 and 31–38, use \(Y=XW^\top\) and compute the weight gradient \(G_W\) as the upstream gradient transposed times \(X\). They return \((K+\lambda I)^{-1}G_W\). Since \(\theta=W^\top\), Algorithm 1’s left multiplication in \(\theta\) corresponds instead to \(G_W(K+\lambda I)^{-1}\) in \(W\). For a square, full-rank example, take \(X=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt2,1)\), \(\lambda=1\) and \(G_W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Such an upstream gradient is attainable because \(X\) is invertible. The two returns are \[\begin{pmatrix}0&1/3\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\1/3&0\end{pmatrix},\] with Frobenius difference \(\sqrt2/6\). They coincide in commuting special cases, but not generally. `models/model.py` activates this path for linear regularization; the relevant forward convention rules out silently treating \(W\) as \(\theta\). A right solve, equivalently solving against \(G_W^\top\) and transposing, repairs this local transpose mismatch. The ridge approximation still differs from the theorem’s exact inverse. This is a Minor implementation/pseudocode discrepancy: the operator mismatch is established, but its effect on reported experiments and the identity of their historical code remain Needs Evidence. No author program was executed. The unused `S2_gradients` helper was also read; its formula is not used to infer an experimental defect.

# Overall impact and conclusion

The two Major findings concern separate core mathematical derivations. The gradient chain rule and the exact spectral congruence remain valid. Figures 1 and 3 visually show the reported slower harmful-task probing trends, and Figure 2 shows the authors’ success coloring; these observations cannot rescue the false universal claims or be rejected by the synthetic counterexamples. Tables 1–4 and the experimental settings were read, but seed-level statistics, data processing, checkpoint selection and the 20-group Hessian estimates were not reproduced. The Appendix C statement about early stopping with test accuracy requires clarification before interpreting reported generalization without selection bias; no quantitative bias is established here. No later correction or historical code provenance has been independently verified.
