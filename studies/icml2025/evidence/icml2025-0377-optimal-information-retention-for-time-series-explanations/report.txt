Abstract: The central information-theoretic derivation is tested using exact finite distributions. Replacing information in the retained signal by information in the mask is invalid, even under perfect predictive fidelity, and a zero mask-prior penalty can coexist with positive retained nuisance information. This is a major gap in the claimed derivation, not a refutation of empirical utility. The completeness criterion and contrastive justification have narrower implications than claimed. A separate static-source check shows that the released temperature hook is not the stated backward relaxation. Historical evaluation paths remain unverified.

# P1: retained values and selection masks are different information variables

Location and classification. Section 2.2, Eqs. (3)–(5), and Appendix B.1, Eqs. (19)–(21), pp. 4 and 13–14. Major: a central derivation of the optimization objective is invalid.

The identity \(I(X;X^m\mid Y)=\mathbb E_{X,Y}D_{\rm KL}(p(X^m\mid X,Y)\Vert p(X^m\mid Y))\) is valid. It does not permit replacing \(X^m=X\odot M\) by \(M\). The map from \(M\) to \(X^m\) depends on the very random variable \(X\) whose information is being measured; it is not a fixed invertible transformation of one variable. Equality of conditional distributions cannot be obtained by merely renaming the argument.

Take a length-two univariate sequence \(X=(Y,Z)\) where \(Y,Z\) are independent uniform signs, and let the black-box classifier read the first coordinate. All quantities are finite and discrete, so no differential-entropy singularity is involved. Two admissible fixed masks give

\(M\) | \(I(X;X^m\mid Y)\) | \(I(X;M\mid Y)\) | \(I(X;Y\mid X^m)\)

\((1,1)\) | 1 bit | 0 | 0

\((1,0)\) | 0 | 0 | 0

Both masks preserve the classifier’s predictions exactly, so the semantic JS constraint in Appendix B.1 cannot remove this counterexample. The full mask retains one bit of independent nuisance information; the mask itself is constant and conveys none. With the reported prior \(r=1/2\), both deterministic mask laws have the same finite prior penalty \(2\log 2\), so the proposed mask penalty does not distinguish their different nuisance retention.

The same mismatch can be exhibited with strictly interior mask probabilities. Compare masks independent of \(X\) with retention probabilities \((3/4,1/4)\) and \((3/4,3/4)\) under prior \(r=1/2\). Both have mask penalty \(2[(3/4)\log(3/2)+(1/4)\log(1/2)]\), while their retained nuisance information is respectively \(1/4\) and \(3/4\) bit, by the erasure channel on \(Z\). The first-coordinate mask distribution is identical, so the expected semantic penalty for a classifier of that coordinate is also identical. This pair has equal semantic penalty, rather than the perfect fidelity of the deterministic pair.

The issue is not confined to deterministic endpoints. If both mask entries are independent Bernoulli\((1/2)\) variables independent of \(X\), then \(p(M\mid X)=q(M)\) and \(L_{\rm mask}=0\), while \(I(X;X^m\mid Y)=1/2\) bit. The nonzero signs make an erased coordinate distinguishable from an observed coordinate. The independent script enumerates all 16 equally likely joint outcomes to verify these values. This second case isolates the regularizer; unlike the fixed-mask pair, it is not claimed to satisfy perfect semantic fidelity.

A valid variational identity would instead be \[\mathbb E D_{\rm KL}(p(M\mid X,Y)\Vert q(M\mid Y))
=I(X;M\mid Y)+\mathbb E_YD_{\rm KL}(p(M\mid Y)\Vert q(M\mid Y)).\] It concerns the mask, not the retained values. An actual derivation for the criterion must keep \(X^m\) in the conditional distribution, or add and justify assumptions linking its information to the mask. An alternative honest repair is to call \(L_{\rm mask}\) a mask-prior regularizer and withdraw the asserted derivation/equivalence. Sparsity and fidelity penalties may still improve the practical model, but empirical usefulness does not restore the missing identity. No complete replacement guarantee is verified here.

# Completeness and contrastive interpretation

Equation (6), \(I(X;Y\mid X^m)=0\), expresses sufficiency of the retained signal for \(Y\) under the assumed channel; it does not generally imply that the physically deleted complement is uninformative. For \(X=(Y,Y)\) with \(M=(1,0)\), the conditional mutual information is zero but \(I(X\odot(1-M);Y)=H(Y)=1\) bit. Repeated task-relevant temporal information is not excluded by the paper’s setting. Thus the narrative that removal necessarily destroys label information requires an additional condition or a direct residual criterion. This example does not deny that retaining one duplicate can already be information-sufficient.

The chain rule in Appendix B.2 Eq. (31) is valid. Treating \(I(X;Y,\widehat X)\) as constant requires the separately assumed condition \(I(X;\widehat X\mid Y)=0\); this is precisely a no-nuisance condition, not a consequence of reconstruction or marginal distribution matching. Also, equality or similarity of marginals does not by itself preserve conditional mutual information. The jump to Eq. (33) does not state a contrastive mutual-information bound or the needed negative-sampling distribution. Here negatives are produced from the same input through inverse masking and Gaussian filling, rather than established independent draws from the positive marginal. The loss is a plausible pair-separation surrogate, but an InfoNCE-style label alone would not prove the claimed criterion is optimized under that sampling scheme. The empirical ablations remain relevant evidence of usefulness; they are not a replacement for the missing probabilistic derivation.

# P2: the released gradient hook differs from the stated relaxation

Location and classification. Appendix C Algorithm 1 versus `maskgen.py`:67–100. Minor confirmed release/specification discrepancy; applicability to the reported runs is Needs Evidence.

Algorithm 1 forms a backward softmax using the backward temperature. In the released function, the lines constructing that softmax are commented out; a hook instead multiplies the gradient at the forward logits by \(\tau_f/\tau_b\). Freeze the temperatures by taking the permitted adaptation rate zero, let \(l=(1,-1)\), \(g=(0,0)\), \(\tau_f=1\), and \(\tau_b=3\). If \(J(p)=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top\), the source then yields \(J(p_f)/\tau_b\), where \(p_f=\operatorname{softmax}(l/\tau_f)\), whereas Algorithm 1 yields \(J(p_b)/\tau_b\). Their first diagonal entries are approximately 0.0350 and 0.0747. Scaling the forward Jacobian cannot generally substitute the differently shaped backward softmax Jacobian. The independent calculation verifies the discrepancy; it does not execute autograd or the author program. With equal temperatures the two coincide, which limits the affected scope.

There is also a noise-scaling difference. The source uses \((l+g)/\tau_f\), so its hard argmax is unchanged by any positive forward temperature for fixed \(l,g\). The paper uses \(l/\tau_f+g\). For \(l=(1,-1)\), \(g=(-0.4,0.4)\), the latter changes its chosen index between temperatures 1 and 3, while the source does not. These strict comparisons persist in a neighborhood of the noise values. The reference implementation therefore cannot be assumed to validate the written two-temperature algorithm. A repair needs one specified estimator and a matching forward/backward implementation, followed by new gradient and empirical checks. No such author-code repair was run.

# Supporting checks

The released `consistency.py`:320–339 has a consequential conditional evaluation path: when its argument named `test` is supplied, ground-truth explanation AUPRC on that argument becomes the checkpoint selection score. If this argument is the held-out test set, selection leaks test explanation labels; if it actually contains an independent validation set, that concern does not apply. The invocation and split provenance are absent, so this is Needs Evidence, not a finding that the published experiments leaked test data. The file also includes Gaussian KL and configurable raw/latent contrastive terms beyond the short displayed objective, underscoring the need for the exact run configuration.

The source prints uncertainty as sample standard deviation divided by the square root of the number of evaluated explanations; this does not by itself establish run-to-run uncertainty. Neither seed-level results nor raw saliency maps were reconstructed. Synthetic truth masks, ECG interval heuristics and perturbation AUROC evaluate different notions and should not be read as direct empirical estimates of the conditional information criteria. The paper does provide CNN/LSTM checks, multiple loss ablations and explicit hyperparameters; these were considered, not ignored.

Figures 1–5 and 7–10 were actually viewed, together with the original B.1/B.2 equations and Algorithm 1 typography. Figure 4’s insertion experiment retains bottom-ranked features; lower AUROC at a small retained fraction can support attribution selectivity, despite the generic caption saying higher is better. The accompanying prose recognizes this distinction. In the qualitative panels each heatmap has its own color scale, so visual darkness alone cannot quantitatively compare methods. The supplied plots can illustrate localization but do not authenticate the numerical tables. Figure 6 was read through its text/caption but not separately image-inspected. OpenReview metadata access was unavailable with a recorded 403; no later correction or complete implementation was established.

# Conclusion

The overall assessment is Major because the claimed derivation of nuisance-information minimization substitutes non-equivalent random variables. Exact finite counterexamples survive the semantic fidelity constraint. The method can still be a useful empirical regularized explainer. The code estimator discrepancy is separately confirmed within the pinned release, while historical protocol impact remains unresolved. Algebraic and finite checks are complete; a corrected objective theorem, implemented repair and benchmark rerun are not. Scientific adjudication is pending, human reviews are empty, and adoption is unknown.
