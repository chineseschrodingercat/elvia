# 2. Scope, definitions, and evidence limits

An algorithmic explanation describes operations and their composition; a behavioral result records an answer; a mechanistic explanation links computation to model components. Agreement between any two requires an explicit mapping. In particular, a token representation, a graph node, a search state, and an attention weight are different objects. A search rollout also depends on neighbor ordering, stopping rules, and whether one records visited nodes, traversed edges, or frontier updates.

AlgEval is presented as a methodological program, not a proved identification theorem. The paper includes no formal theorem/proof appendix. Its proposed causal interventions and future tests must not be misrepresented as completed validation. Similarly, proposed improvements in sample efficiency, emissions, safety, and architecture are prospective, rather than measured benefits of the case study.

# 3. Problem 1 — Algorithm exclusion depends on an unvalidated decoder

Judgment: Needs Evidence / inferential scope qualification.

## Source claim and suspect step

Section 4, p. 6, assumes a layer-to-search-step correspondence. Section 4.2, p. 7, selects the node pair with greatest representation inner product at each layer and compares that sequence with BFS/DFS rollouts. Figure 2, p. 6, labels the comparison as hypothesis rejection. The interpretation on p. 8 extends the attention observations toward absence of exhaustive search or a full world model. [R1]

The missing link is necessity: if a model implemented a particular search procedure, must the chosen maximum-inner-product pair or attention summary reveal its state? A mismatch rejects the procedure together with its assumed observable encoding. It cannot separately reject every implementation of the procedure.

## Complete analytic check

Consider seven token representations at each layer i. For tokens A and B set a_A = a_B = 2; for each other token set a_x = 1/2. Let

Code:
u(i,x) = (a_x, z(i,x)), with -1 <= z(i,x) <= 1.
score(i,x,y) = u(i,x) dot u(i,y), for distinct x and y.

Every A–B score is 4 + z(i,A)z(i,B), hence at least 3. For a pair containing exactly one of A/B, the score is at most 1 + 1 = 2. For a pair containing neither, it is at most 1/4 + 1 = 1.25. Thus the selected distinct pair is always A–B, strictly and without a tie.

A changing finite search state can be encoded by distinct bounded values of z(i,C) for another token C, with downstream computation reading that coordinate and ignoring the fixed first coordinate. For example, three successive states can be assigned 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3. Each layer may still correspond to one search step, while the observed maximum pair never changes. The decoder reports a constant pair; the encoded state changes.

This construction satisfies the stated raw-inner-product definition, bounded finite representations, and a layerwise state progression. It grants exclusion of self-pairs, which is favorable to the decoder. It is an abstract countermodel to an unrestricted proxy-to-state implication, not a construction of the inspected pretrained Llama weights and not a refutation of the authors’ explicitly conditional assumption. No assumption that the model must place algorithmic state in its dominant similarity component has been established.

An independent finite check evaluated all 3^7 = 2,187 assignments with z in {-1, 0, 1}. The minimum A–B score was 3, the maximum competing score 2, and the minimum margin 1. The inequalities above establish the continuous case; the grid is only a check on their implementation.

A separate attention boundary follows from the standard head output A V, where A = softmax(Q K-transpose / sqrt(d_k)). [R5, Eq. 1, p. 4] Setting a head’s value output to zero makes its contribution zero for every attention pattern permitted by its queries, keys, and mask. Such heads can influence an unweighted attention average without contributing to the answer. Multi-head output projection further separates attention weight from causal contribution. This does not imply that the actual models contain such zero-value heads.

## Counterevidence, impact, and defensible repair

The paper is substantially more cautious than a universal exclusion theorem: p. 6 states the correspondence as an assumption; p. 7 calls the sequence possible; p. 8 uses tentative interpretation and requests further mechanism verification; p. 5 includes causal intervention in the proposed framework. Figure 6 also treats geometric changes as a candidate signature. These qualifications preserve a useful exploratory case study. The cited attention literature likewise does not justify a blanket claim that attention can never explain a model. [R6]

The defensible conclusion is that the extracted sequences show poor agreement with the tested rollouts under the chosen decoder. A stronger exclusion requires validated state decoding or causal evidence, with sensitivity to representations, token occurrences, layer grouping, stopping rules, and candidate implementations. Positive controls with known algorithms and tests of value/output contributions would help distinguish the possibilities; none was performed in this review.

Repair status: the conditional wording is analytically justified. An empirically verified identification method is not supplied. Impact: the reach of the illustrative mechanism inference is limited; the central research agenda is not invalidated.

# 4. Problem 2 — The direction and family of layer tests need specification

Judgment: Needs Evidence, with a fully specified conditional recalculation.

## Source claim and complete numerical check

Section 4.1, p. 7, counts 14 layers favoring the correct pathway and three favoring the incorrect pathway. Appendix A.1, p. 20, describes Bonferroni correction over layer tests but does not explicitly give sidedness, alpha, or separate directional families. [R1]

Let T have Student’s t distribution with 31 degrees of freedom. Recomputing P(T >= abs(t)) from each printed statistic approximately reproduces the Tables 2–3 p-values, allowing for rounded t values. This is a one-sided tail in the observed direction. For a single two-sided family of 32 layers at an explicitly assumed alpha = .05, the required raw two-sided threshold is .05/32 = .0015625; the two-sided p is twice that tail.

Layer and direction | Printed t; df | Recomputed one-tail p | Two-sided p | Bonferroni 32 adjusted p

26, correct pathway | 3.38; 31 | .000986578 | .001973157 | .0631410

23, incorrect pathway | -3.23; 31 | .001463540 | .002927080 | .0936665

These two layers do not pass that two-sided family criterion. Every other listed layer does. Among the published significant layers, the resulting counts are 13 positive and two negative. This is not a reanalysis of omitted layers or a claim that the paper specified the assumed test family.

For a symmetric null, selecting the one-sided tail after observing the sign covers both tails. Controlling a combined family requires accounting for both directions. Equivalently, the two-sided 32-test calculation uses a one-tail threshold .05/64. Bonferroni does not require independence, although the validity of each original t-test still depends on its sampling/model assumptions.

## Strongest alternative and bounded impact

Two separately prespecified directional families could justify a 32-test correction within each family. This would not by itself provide a single .05 bound for their union. The published description does not resolve the intended claim, so the reviewer cannot infer the authors’ prior decisions or declare the 14/3 result unconditionally false. The original observation unit, pairing, full p-value vector, and handling of dependence also require the underlying data and analysis specification.

Table 1 is consistent at the level that can be checked: t = 4.51, df = 2015 yields two-sided p = 6.85561e-6, agreeing with the rounded 7.0e-6. The displayed coefficient .33 and SE .07 need not divide to exactly 4.51 because they are rounded; their rounding intervals permit ratios from about 4.33 to 5.15. No extra error is inferred from that ratio.

Defensible resolution: disclose the planned hypotheses, sidedness, alpha, family definition, analysis unit, and raw/adjusted p-values. If the intended claim is one two-sided 32-layer family at .05, the conditional changes above follow from the printed statistics. If separate directional families were intended, describe them and limit the associated error-control statement.

Repair status: tail arithmetic verified; the original inferential design remains unresolved. Impact: two marginal layer classifications depend on the interpretation. The displayed aggregate-model tail is not contradicted, and none of these significance tests alone identifies a search algorithm.

# 5. Problem 3 — Environmental quantities change workload and accounting scope

Judgment: Minor for the source-attribution/accounting mismatch; the carbon magnitude remains Needs Evidence.

## Claim, primary-source comparison, and units

The p. 10 emission-efficiency paragraph presents half a liter of cooling water and gasoline-equivalent emissions for one interaction, then compares projected global water use with UK consumption, citing Li et al. (2025). [R1]

Li et al. v5, Introduction p. 2, instead estimates 500 mL for approximately 10–50 medium-length responses, dependent on place and time. Table 1, p. 5, includes on-site and electricity-related off-site water. Section 3.3.2, pp. 5–6, describes inputs up to roughly 800 words and outputs of 150–300 words, and acknowledges higher possible consumption. Its 2027 projection distinguishes 4.2–6.6 billion cubic meters of withdrawal from .38–.60 billion of consumption; the UK comparison concerns withdrawal. [R4]

The reviewer’s arithmetic is 500/50 = 10 mL and 500/10 = 50 mL per response on the source’s aggregate approximation. Recasting 500 mL as a single medium-response quantity changes that denominator by a factor of 10–50. This calculation is neither a fresh measurement nor a universal bound on heavy requests.

The cited PDF supplies no located per-interaction estimate equivalent to five gallons of gasoline. Full-text checks found no occurrence of gallon, gasoline, petrol, or carbon equivalent. No independently verified replacement carbon number is proposed. Water volume also cannot be converted to carbon emissions without workload, energy, location, and accounting information.

## Alternative reading, impact, and precise repair

An interaction might mean a long session of many responses, and a difficult workload might consume much more than the source’s reference request. These readings prevent an impossibility claim about half a liter or the carbon magnitude. They do not supply the omitted workload definition or justify substituting consumption for withdrawal. Nor does a comparison described as more than half logically disprove every larger fraction; the paper’s near-two-thirds fraction remains untraced rather than independently falsified.

A defensible local revision is to preserve the source’s response count and combined operational-water scope, explicitly distinguish withdrawal from consumption, and remove or separately substantiate the gasoline-equivalent number with a workload-specific calculation. This corrects the attribution without promising that AlgEval itself reduces resources.

Repair status: the workload and accounting clarification is source-verified; a carbon replacement and any emissions benefit require additional evidence. Impact: the quantitative environmental motivation is affected, not the validity of the proposed algorithmic research program.
