# Scope and mathematical setting

DCS samples a binary mask with \(n\) retained points in a \(k\times k\) convolutional boundary, with stride \(S\). Equations (6)–(8), p. 4, relate the input-gradient cosine similarity of two independently sampled paths to the retained points. Lemma 1 gives \(n\le S^2\epsilon_c\), and the surrounding text says this guarantees small expected gradient similarity. The combined interval in Eq. (12) is subsequently used as a guarantee governing the robustness/natural-accuracy trade-off. The counterexample below concerns this sufficiency claim, with uniformly sampled, spatially shared convolution masks.

# Substantive finding

Problem 1 (The receptive-field upper bound does not guarantee the stated gradient similarity).  For \(k=5\), \(S=2\), \(n=1\) and \(\epsilon_c=1/4\), the claimed condition holds, but a valid masked convolution has expected gradient cosine \(4096/15625=0.262144>1/4\). The failure affects the core data-independent guarantee and supports Major impact.

## Construction and verification of conditions

Use a single-channel \(103\times103\) input, a \(5\times5\) kernel, stride two, zero padding of width zero (valid convolution), and weights all equal to one. There are \(50\times50\) output locations. At each forward pass choose one of the 25 offsets \((a,b)\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}^2\) uniformly and independently of the preceding pass; apply that same one-point mask across all spatial positions. Thus \(n=1<S^2=4\), and \(n\le S^2\epsilon_c=1\). This is a shared-weight convolution of the form in Definition 1. There is no circular padding, asymptotic image limit or nonuniform mask sampling.

Let the scalar logit be the sum of all convolution outputs, with a second logit zero. Evaluate cross-entropy for class zero of the zero-logit class at the all-zero input. Its derivative with respect to the summed logit is \(1/2\). Consequently, apart from this common positive scalar, the input gradient for mask \((a,b)\) is the indicator of \[G_{a,b}=\{(2i+a,2j+b):0\le i,j<50\}.\] Each set has 2500 distinct entries, so both gradient norms are nonzero and the cosine for a mask pair is exactly \(|G_{a,b}\cap G_{c,d}|/2500\). This supplies a legitimate classifier loss, not merely an artificial unnormalized dot product.

## Exact expectation

In one dimension write \(H_a=\{2i+a:0\le i<m\}\) with \(m=50\). Odd differences in offsets give zero intersection; equal offsets give \(m\); differences two and four give \(m-1\) and \(m-2\). Among the 25 ordered one-dimensional offset pairs there are five equal pairs, six pairs with difference two, and two pairs with difference four. Hence \[\sum_{a,c=0}^4 |H_a\cap H_c|=5m+6(m-1)+2(m-2)=13m-10.\] The two coordinate dimensions factor independently, giving \[\mathbb E\cos(\nabla_X L,\nabla_X L')=
\left(\frac{13m-10}{25m}\right)^2
=\left(\frac{64}{125}\right)^2=\frac{4096}{15625}>\frac14.\] The reviewer-written script constructs all 25 two-dimensional support sets, enumerates all 625 ordered pairs using rational arithmetic, checks their common size and independently matches this closed form. It imports no author code. The excess \(0.012144\) is exact, not Monte Carlo noise.

## Defective implication in the source proof

Appendix A.1, p. 13, Eq. (20), obtains the stricter expression \[n\le \frac{k^2(\epsilon_c-1)}{\lfloor k/S\rfloor^2}+S^2.\] It then relaxes it to \(n\le S^2\epsilon_c\) in Eq. (21). For the example, the former right-hand side is \(-11/16\) and the latter is one. A weaker necessary restriction on \(n\) does not retain a sufficient guarantee obtained from the earlier worst-case inequality. The proof therefore cannot justify using every point in the enlarged interval as safe. The exact convolution calculation demonstrates that this is a real failure of the asserted sufficiency, not just an unproved implication.

## Alternative readings, counterevidence and impact

If Lemma 1 is interpreted only as a necessary upper restriction, the counterexample does not refute that one-way reading. The main text, however, explicitly uses it to guarantee small similarity and Eq. (12) to guarantee the trade-off. That interpretation is what fails. The public `MaskedConv2d` in `model/randpos_resnet.py`, lines 321–359, multiplies one weight tensor by a mask and calls `conv2d`; the mask is spatially shared. Lines 685–733 also form a spatial mask and tile it across channels. This supports the shared-mask interpretation used here. Some released calls choose three points rather than one; the universal lemma and Table 5 explicitly include \(n=1\), so the counterexample is not a claim to reproduce the default experiment. If a different formulation independently resamples a separate mask at every spatial location, it would be a different stochastic operator requiring its own proof; no such independence assumption repairs the published shared-convolution guarantee.

The method’s introductory contribution and Section 4 explicitly rely on this bound to claim data-independent control of gradient similarity. This is a central theoretical dependency even though the empirical method may remain useful. Figures 3–4 and the grid-search heatmaps in Figure 6 are finite observations and do not establish the universal claim. The counterexample does not show a drop in CIFAR or ImageNet robustness, and it is not an attack on the trained networks. No general corrected bound is claimed: keeping an intermediate inequality is not sufficient until the earlier probability and gradient decompositions are also verified.

# Other inspected claims and limitations

Appendix A.2 was read through its normalization and probability bounds. An additional issue requiring careful rederivation is the expectation/absolute-value interchange around Eqs. (9)–(10); the present report does not assign a separate theorem finding without a fully specified normalization convention. The lower bound is therefore not independently certified.

The source reports PGD, AutoAttack, EOTPGD, transfer and adaptive attacks. The inspected `BPDA.run` in `evaluate_nonrepeated_eotbpda.py`, lines 258–323, averages independently optimized perturbations after each run rather than accumulating transformation-averaged gradients before each update. This does not by itself establish the provenance of Table 6 or quantify any robustness overestimate. A fresh appropriately adaptive evaluation, with exact checkpoints and stochastic attack settings, would be needed; no new adversarial accuracy is asserted here. Reading these code paths statically is not an execution or reproduction. The report’s supported severity rests solely on the fully checked core-bound counterexample.
