Abstract: The complete original paper and embedded appendix were reviewed. Three Major findings concern core mathematical foundations: an invalid variational decomposition and inconsistent loss signs; a contrastive objective minimized by equal matched-pair scores, including collapsed representations; and a false nuisance-information upper bound under the stated hypotheses. These defects affect the justification of MCVAE and invariant relational learning. Historical benchmark execution is unavailable, so the findings do not establish that corrected implementations lose reported accuracy. Conflicting CompSolv results and incomplete experimental bindings remain Needs Evidence. Adjudication is pending.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The variational identity and minimized MCVAE loss are inconsistent).  Major. The discrepancy affects a central conditional modeling module and its claimed derivation.

## Claim and location

Equation reference: eq:printed. Equations 5–6 (pp. 5–6), A.2 Equations 21–24 (pp. 12–13), and Proposition 4.2 (p. 7) motivate a conditional evidence objective. Writing \(x=\widetilde H_1\), Eq. 23 claims \[
 \log p_\theta(x\mid e)
 =-\mathrm{KL}\{q(z\mid x,e)\Vert p_\theta(z\mid x)\}
   +\mathbb E_q\log p_\theta(x\mid z,e).\] Equations 6/24 combine negative KL with a positive reconstruction/variance penalty, and enter the minimized loss [eq:joint] (#eq:joint).

## Exact counterexample and alternative interpretation

Use a constant environment and a proper Gaussian joint: \[z\sim N(0,1),\quad x\mid z\sim N(z,1),\quad
 x_{\rm obs}=0,\quad q=p(z\mid x=0)=N(0,1/2).\] All variances are positive and distributions normalized. The printed KL is zero, meeting any positive threshold \(\delta\), but \[\begin{aligned}
 \log p(0)&=-\tfrac12\log(4\pi)=-1.2655121235,\\
 \text{right side of \text{(eq:printed)}}
 &=-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)-\tfrac14=-1.1689385332.\end{aligned}\] The asserted expression exceeds the evidence by \(\frac12\log2-\frac14=0.0965735903\). Under its literal conditioning it is not even an evidence lower bound.

Equation reference: eq:correct. The prose on p. 13 instead mentions \(p(z\mid e)\). Granting that intended prior still does not justify equality for arbitrary \(q\). In the same joint, \(q=N(0,1)\) gives a proper ELBO of \(-1.4189385332\), below the evidence by \(0.1534264097\). The correct decomposition is \[\begin{aligned}

 \log p_\theta(x\mid e)
 &=\mathbb E_q\log p_\theta(x\mid z,e)
   -\mathrm{KL}\{q\Vert p_\theta(z\mid e)\}\\
 &\quad+\mathrm{KL}\{q\Vert p_\theta(z\mid x,e)\}.\nonumber\end{aligned}\] This refutes the particular asserted marginal-likelihood identity. It does not claim every data-conditioned prior in a different generative model is invalid.

## Optimization direction and finite diagnostic

Negating likelihood-minus-KL for minimization changes both terms, giving negative log likelihood plus KL. Equations 6/24 change the reconstruction sign but retain negative KL. The appendix repeats this discrepancy.

Set \(x=0\), comparison prior \(N(0,1)\), \(q=N(0,v)\) and fixed reconstruction variance parameter \(\sigma^2=1\). This explicitly separates posterior and reconstruction variances; the paper gives no equation binding them. With its reconstruction coefficient, \[K(v)=\tfrac12(v-1-\log v),\quad
 J_-(v)=-K(v)+v,\quad J_+(v)=K(v)+v.\] Independent symbolic calculations give:

\(v\) | \(K(v)\) | Printed \(J_-(v)\) | Corrected \(J_+(v)\)

\(1/3\) | 0.215973 | 0.117361 | 0.549306

\(1/4\) | 0.318147 | \(-0.068147\) | 0.568147

Both satisfy \(\mathrm{KL}<0.4\), yet the losses order them oppositely. Without a hard KL bound, \(J_-(v)=\frac12(v+1+\log v)\to-\infty\) as \(v\downarrow0\). If \(\sigma^2\) is tied to \(v\), the reconstruction term itself has another degeneracy; that alternative is not used to attribute all unboundedness to the KL sign. The identity and sign findings do not depend on this diagnostic’s variance interpretation.

## Impact and checked repair

The published derivation does not establish its ELBO interpretation or prior-matching regularization, undermining MCVAE’s theoretical justification and Proposition 4.2. This supports Major independently of a benchmark rerun. It does not prove historical training used these signs or diverged.

Equation [eq:correct] (#eq:correct) restores the exact decomposition. A coherent minimized objective is \(-\mathbb E_q\log p_\theta(x\mid z,e)+\gamma\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert p_\theta(z\mid e))\), \(\gamma>0\); \(\gamma=1\) gives the usual negative ELBO. Specify decoder, prior and variance roles. These local repairs do not prove predictive sufficiency or OOD invariance and were not tested in a neural implementation.

Problem 2 (The contrastive loss only equalizes matched-pair scores).  Major. Equation 12 does not implement the discrimination used to motivate the central mutual-information mechanism.

## Claim and exact calculation

Equation reference: eq:equalize. Section 4.3 presents Eq. 12 (p. 7) as an approximation to Eq. 11’s mutual-information objective. Original-image inspection confirms that its denominator uses \(\operatorname{sim}(\widehat H_{\rm inv}^{\,j},H_1^j)\) for every \(j\ne i\), rather than anchor \(i\) against sample \(j\). Set \(s_i=\operatorname{sim}(\widehat H_{\rm inv}^{\,i},H_1^i)\). Then \[
 L_{\rm MI}=\log\sum_{j=1}^M e^{s_j}
             -\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M s_i
 \ \geq\ \log M.\] Jensen’s inequality gives equality whenever all matched-pair scores are equal. A common additive score shift leaves the loss unchanged; its score gradient is \(\operatorname{softmax}(s)_i-1/M\), zero at equality.

Take two equally likely inputs \(H_1^1=(1,0)\) and \(H_1^2=(0,1)\), and set both learned representations to the same nonzero unit vector \(u=(1,1)/\sqrt2\). With either dot-product or cosine similarity, both scores equal \(1/\sqrt2\) and the loss attains its global minimum \(\log2\), although the representation is constant and its mutual information with \(H_1\) is zero. The informative choice \(\widehat H_{\rm inv}^{\,i}=H_1^i\) also attains \(\log2\) while retaining \(\log2\) information. For constant \(Y\), the same distinction applies to the conditional information in Eq. 11. No undefined cosine or numerical approximation is involved.

## Counterevidence, impact and repair limits

The supervised term can oppose collapse; this does not prove collapse of the complete predictor. An intended anchor-versus-negative formula is a plausible correction, but no appendix correction or acquired implementation supplies it. The demonstrated failure concerns the contrastive/MI mechanism central to the invariant-learning argument, so Major does not require showing benchmark loss.

A candidate repair compares anchor \(\widehat H_{\rm inv}^{\,i}\) against every \(H_1^j\) in its denominator. A bound on the conditional information of Eq. 11 additionally needs appropriate conditioning and negative sampling. An index change alone does not prove that bound or invariance. For deterministic \(w\), moreover, \(I(w(H_1);H_1\mid Y)=H(w(H_1)\mid Y)\); maximizing it can retain nuisance variation. If \(H_1=(Y,C)\) for independent fair bits, retaining \((Y,C)\) preserves more conditional information than retaining \(Y\) alone, despite equal predictive information. This limits the noise-removal interpretation.

Problem 3 (The mixup nuisance-information upper bound is false).  Major. Appendix A.1 is explicitly invoked as theoretical justification for the proposed mixup conditional modeling.

## Claim and complete admissible counterexample

Equation reference: eq:bound. A.1 (p. 12), Eqs. 14–20, assumes a Lipschitz predictor with constant \(L_f>0\), bounded nonnegative loss \(B\), and \(\widetilde H=\lambda H_1+(1-\lambda)H_2\), \(\lambda\in[0,1]\). It asserts \[
 \mathcal E_{\rm mixup}(\lambda)
 \leq \mathcal E^*+c_2\sqrt{I(\widetilde H;C_{\rm nuisance})},\] where \(\mathcal E^*\) is optimal achievable error and \(c_2>0\) depends on \(L_f,B\).

Let \(Y\) be a fair bit and set \(H_1=H_2=e_1=e_2=Y\). Then \(\widetilde H=e=Y\) for every \(\lambda\), including a Beta-distributed one. Use prediction/target domains \([0,1]\), squared loss, \(B=1\), and \(f(h)\equiv1/2\). This satisfies the displayed Lipschitz inequality with \(L_f=1\). Let \(C_{\rm nuisance}\) be an independent fair bit. Then \[I(\widetilde H;C_{\rm nuisance})=0,\quad
 \mathcal E^*=0,\quad
 \mathcal E_{\rm mixup}=\mathbb E(1/2-Y)^2=1/4.\] The zero optimum is attained by \(f^*(h)=\min(1,\max(0,h))\), also globally 1-Lipschitz. Equation [eq:bound] (#eq:bound) therefore requires \(1/4\leq0\), independently of \(c_2\).

A one-hot version removes any concern about environmental encoding. Let \(Y\) be uniform on the two unit vectors in \(\mathbb R^2\), set all four representations/labels equal to \(Y\), predict \((1/2,1/2)\), and use half squared Euclidean loss on the probability simplex. The loss is bounded by one and the risk is \(1/4\); Euclidean projection onto the simplex is 1-Lipschitz and attains zero risk on the support. Both versions are in the independent calculation record.

## Counterevidence and consequence

A.1 never assumes that the evaluated predictor already minimizes risk. Adding that premise changes the claim; nuisance independence alone cannot remove predictive or optimization error. The qualitative need to preserve task information remains sensible, but Eq. 19 and the two-sided Eq. 20 do not supply the advertised generalization justification. This is a demonstrated invalid derivation tied to a core module, hence Major. It does not refute all mixup applications or the reported scores. No replacement upper bound is verified: substantive assumptions and an excess-risk or approximation term would be required.

# Additional checks and overall impact

The three Major findings independently invalidate mathematical support for central components. No unavailable implementation or score rerun is needed to demonstrate them; conversely, they do not imply every displayed experiment is false.

Proposition 4.2’s bridge to conditional invariance is not supplied by A.3’s marginal conditions. Let \(S,U,E\) be independent fair bits, input \(X=(S,U,E)\), representation \(S\), residual \((U,E)\) and target \(Y=S\oplus E\). Representation–residual independence, within-environment factorization and equal representation marginals hold, yet \[P(Y=1\mid S=0,E=0)=0,\qquad
 P(Y=1\mid S=0,E=1)=1.\] An invariant sufficient alternative \(S\oplus E\) exists, so this does not deny its existence. A.3 explicitly concludes marginal stability and is not silently promoted to a separate false theorem. The implication from optimization to label-conditional invariance remains unproved. Its unrestricted additive-noise definition is also not a substantive sufficiency test.

B.1’s intended scaffold- and solvent-group-disjoint splits are appropriate safeguards. Actual partitions, five-solvent QM9Solv allocation, DDI shift definitions and synthetic spurious-feature interventions remain unavailable. C.2’s dielectric scatter plot does not specify the intervention or establish that a chemically predictive property is spurious. Table 4 explicitly labels its extensions IID. Bounded prose corrections are also needed: removing MCAR is worse than the stated baseline on both scaffold columns of Table 2, and Table 4’s FreeSolv winner is CMRL, not the CGIB named in C.3. These do not create additional core-failure claims. Figure 4 mentions validation-based checkpoint selection; sensitivity plots of test errors alone do not establish test-set tuning.
