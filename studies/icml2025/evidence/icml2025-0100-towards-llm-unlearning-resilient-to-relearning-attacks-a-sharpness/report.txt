# 2. Definitions and valid reasoning retained

Write ell for the forget loss, g for its gradient, H for its Hessian, and v=g/||g|| where g is nonzero, as explicitly assumed on p. 4. The robust formulation restricts weight perturbations to a norm ball, with default Euclidean radius rho>0, and relates relearning to an objective counteracting the forget objective. This is a conditional threat model, not an equivalence to every fine-tuning attack.

Equation (4) explicitly linearizes the inner maximization; rho*v correctly optimizes that linearized problem. Equation (6) is explicitly a first-order Taylor approximation. Equation (7), gradient of the gradient norm equals H*v, is correct for nonzero g. Equation (8) is a local finite-difference approximation. Equations (9)-(11) define coherent smoothing/regularization objectives under ordinary integrability/differentiability conditions. These valid steps survive.

Algorithm A1 perturbs the forget loss, computes its gradient at perturbed weights, and combines it with the retain gradient at original weights. The public SAM paths follow this ordering: WMDP base.py lines 764-775, restoration/retain backward at 834-849, combination at 853-864; MUSE sam_iterative.py lines 129-138 and helpers through 226. This positive static check assumes nonzero forget gradients, relevant gradient entries for trainable parameters and one accumulation step, explicitly configured in WMDP. General multi-microbatch accumulation, AMP and distributed equivalence were not certified [R3,R6].

WMDP UE is 1 minus benchmark accuracy, so higher is better; UT is MMLU accuracy. MUSE forget-set VerbMem and KnowMem are lower-is-better; retain-set KnowMem is higher-is-better. Relative resistance, utility, total information erasure and universal attack robustness are distinct claims.

# 3. Problem 1 - Stationarity does not force curvature to vanish

Source claim and location. Section 4, p. 4, immediately after Eq. (8), concludes from solving Eq. (5) toward stationarity that ||H*v|| tends to zero. This is the mathematical bridge from SAM optimization to curvature suppression and the subsequent CR/smoothing motivation, closely tied to a principal contribution [R1,R2].

Suspect step. A zero gradient of the full surrogate does not force one component to vanish. For the Eq. (6) surrogate G, stationarity permits cancellation between g and rho*H*v. For F in Eq. (5), the derivative contains the shifted gradient and the Jacobian of v. The correct identities appear below. Adding a retain gradient cannot justify dropping the other terms.

Complete counterexample. Fix any rho>0, for example 0.01, and take the scalar polynomial ell displayed below. Choose lambda=0, permitted by the nonnegative retain coefficient, or a constant retain loss with any lambda. The loss is smooth, coercive and globally nonnegative. Its only critical points are -rho, rho/2 and rho, with values 5*rho^2/12, 215*rho^2/192 and 13*rho^2/12, so its global minimum is positive.

The factored gradient is positive on (-rho,rho/2), hence v=1 near x=0. The source’s nonzero-gradient condition holds at and near the solution. At zero, g=rho/2, H=-1 and ||H*v||=1. Locally Eq. (5) is exactly F(x)=ell(x+rho). Thus F’(0)=ell’(rho)=0 and F”(0)=ell”(rho)=1>0: zero is a strict local minimum of the stated surrogate, but directional curvature does not vanish. No undefined normalization or stationary maximum is used.

Independent verification. With rho=0.01, step size 0.25 and x0=0.001, 80 reviewer scalar iterations yield x=7.45278e-14, g=0.00499999999993 and ||H*v||=1.00000000000745. At zero, the Eq. (8) finite difference with mu=1e-6 is -1.00004999, tending to -1. Exact symbolic checks and the complete replay script are preserved [R6].

Alternative readings and counterevidence. This tests Eq. (5) as the paper’s approximate-SAM surrogate; it does not claim its perturbation is an exact nonlinear inner maximizer. Algorithm A1’s deliberate omission of higher derivatives cannot remove the example because v is locally constant. Appendix A supplies a second smooth nonnegative loss with strict local minima for both Eq. (5) and Eq. (6), while ||H*v||=1, closing the alternative interpretation of the paragraph. These are generic mathematical witnesses, not simulated LLM losses or NPO training runs.

Impact and repair. Major: a core-connected inference is false even if the experiments and broader smoothing hypothesis survive. Valid local approximations and conditional robust optimization remain available. The derivative identities below are verified scoped replacements; no complete argument restoring the claimed zero-curvature conclusion has been verified.

\[g=\nabla\ell,\quad v=g/\|g\|_2,\quad Dv=(I-vv^\top)H/\|g\|_2.\] \[\begin{aligned}F(\theta)&=\ell(\theta+\rho v(\theta)),&
\nabla F&=(I+\rho Dv)^\top\nabla\ell(\theta+\rho v),\\
G(\theta)&=\ell(\theta)+\rho\|g\|_2,&
\nabla G&=g+\rho Hv.\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}\ell(x)&=\rho^2+\frac{x^4}{4\rho^2}-\frac{x^3}{6\rho}-\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{\rho x}{2},\\
\ell'(x)&=\frac{(x-\rho/\allowbreak{}2)(x-\rho)(x+\rho)}{\rho^2}.\end{aligned}\]

# 4. Problem 2 - The large-radius ablation confuses forgetting with utility

Source claim. The rho-ablation discussion on p. 9 and Appendix E, p. 15, attributes rho=0.1 to ineffective forgetting. Table A3 instead shows identical pre-attack forget scores, substantial utility deterioration and mixed post-attack changes [R1,R6].

Complete check. Pre-attack forget VerbMem and KnowMem are both 0.00 at both radii. Increasing rho from 0.01 to 0.1 reduces retain KnowMem by 11.57 points; after attack, forget VerbMem improves by 5.42 while KnowMem worsens by 2.19. Directions follow the paper’s explicit UE/UT definitions.

Alternative reading and impact. If effectiveness informally means the joint forget/retain objective, the utility loss supports a worse tradeoff; the specific assertion about preventing forgetting does not follow. Minor: a bounded interpretation error, with no demonstrated failure of the selected rho=0.01 comparison. A verified replacement is: pre-attack forgetting is unchanged at displayed precision, retain utility falls substantially, and post-attack metrics show a mixed tradeoff. This repairs the description, without asserting new experimental results.

# 5. Problem 3 - The public GP penalty is detached

Source claim and location. Equation (10), p. 5, defines a differentiable gradient-norm penalty. The public WMDP path is NPO_FT_GNR, WMDP/src/unlearn/GA.py lines 218-264, reachable through __init__.py lines 24-25 and the supplied GP command [R3].

Complete static check. Lines 255-257 request differentiable gradients with create_graph=True, but line 258 converts squared-gradient sums using .item(). The accumulated norm and its square root are Python scalar values. Adding that value at line 262 contributes zero parameter derivative. The official Tensor.item documentation confirms nondifferentiability [R5]. For the reviewer example ell(theta)=theta^2/2 at theta=1 and rho=0.01, the intended derivative of ell+rho*|ell’| is 1.01; a detached penalty leaves 1.0. No author or Torch training code was executed.

The command supplies gnr_rho=10, while this loss hardcodes 0.01. A changed logged loss is distinct from a changed optimization gradient. The expression is present in the first code release, so it is not only a recent edit.

Impact and counterevidence. Minor for the demonstrated release defect; published GP-result implications remain Needs Evidence because run-source linkage is missing. Objective identity would matter even if scores remained favorable after such linkage were established. Preserving a differentiable tensor norm and using the intended coefficient are necessary local requirements, but no complete repaired trainer or empirical restoration was verified.

No command was run, no training failure was reproduced, and no complete repair is claimed.

# 7. Problem 5 - The released MUSE objective is not likelihood-ratio NPO

Source claim and location. The paper identifies NPO as the MUSE baseline on pp. 3 and 7-8. The original NPO definition depends on the conditional probability of a forget response relative to a reference, Eq. (3) in Zhang et al. [R4]. The saved MUSE/baselines/baselines/iterative.py lines 155-157 and sam_iterative.py lines 256-258 instead apply logsigmoid to reference.logits-current.logits over the full tensor, without probability normalization or selecting the forget labels for that term [R3,R6].

Complete invariant check. Set beta=0.1, reference and initial current logits (1,-1), and subsequent current logits (-1,-3), a common shift of -2. All logits are finite, beta is positive, and the reference and forget label remain fixed. Both current softmax distributions equal (0.88079707798,0.11920292202), hence every response likelihood is unchanged. Proper likelihood-ratio NPO remains 13.8629436112; the released loss changes from 13.8629436112 to 11.9627773876. This establishes objective mismatch without assuming a real model follows a common-offset trajectory.

Counterevidence and impact. Both MUSE baseline and SAM use the expression, so a relative advantage can survive. WMDP uses cross-entropy loss differences and is outside this raw-logit counterexample; its reduction convention is a separate scope question. Minor for the released objective mismatch; actual published MUSE objective remains Needs Evidence. If the released expression generated the named NPO comparison, that identity failure would need assessment without requiring score deterioration. No run mapping or complete replacement with verified label shifting, masking, sequence reduction and beta convention was obtained.

\[L_{\rm NPO}=-\frac{2}{\beta}\mathbb E\log\sigma\!\left(-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\rm ref}(y\mid x)}\right),\qquad \beta>0.\]

# Appendix A. A witness also covering Eq. (6)

Let t=x/rho, h(t)=1+3t+(9/2)t^2+7t^3+100t^4, q(t)=(t-1)^2*h(t), and ell(x)=rho^2*q(x/rho). The identity h(t)=(1+3t/2)^2+t^2*(9/4+7t+100t^2) proves h>0: the quadratic has positive leading coefficient and discriminant -851. Thus ell is smooth and nonnegative.

Exact derivatives: q’(0)=1, q”(0)=-1, q”’(0)=6, q’(1)=0, q”(1)=231. At x=0, g=rho>0, H=-1 and v=1 nearby. The Eq. (5) surrogate ell(x+rho) has derivative zero and second derivative 231. The Eq. (6) surrogate ell(x)+rho*ell’(x) has derivative rho*(1-1)=0 and second derivative -1+6=5. Both have strict local minima while ||H*v||=1. Constant retain loss leaves the calculation unchanged. This closes the Eq. (6) reading without invoking a zero base gradient [R6].
