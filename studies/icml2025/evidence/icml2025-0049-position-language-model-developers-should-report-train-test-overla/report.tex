\# 2. Summary of checked claims\par
The paper advocates reporting training/test overlap when publishing model evaluations. Its main descriptive result is a survey of thirty selected flagship models: nine receive a positive transparency score. Recounting the actual Table 1 rows gives 30 total, 9 positive, and 21 zero scores. The three outreach-linked additions are included in those nine. A score measures the paper’s minimum transparency threshold; it is not evidence that every benchmark was covered or that a model is free of overlap. [E1, pp. 4-6, 18-19; E3]\par
Two bounded concerns are supported. First, the released pipeline does not implement the paper’s concatenated-reference convention consistently. A complete finite example demonstrates missing or understated reference overlap. Second, the Codeforces discussion links a separate old/new-problem anecdote to the published GPT-4 result and then gives a causal interpretation not established by those data. Neither concern demonstrates that the thirty-model census or the general disclosure recommendation is false.\par
Short-sequence domain handling, scoring completeness, pseudocode variable names, and inaccessible anecdote sources are recorded separately with their counterevidence. They do not raise the supported severity. The review treats the normative recommendation on its own terms and does not demand an experiment merely to justify a transparency norm.\par

\# 4. Problem 1: reference boundaries differ between the specification and released implementation\par
Source claim and locations. Appendix B.1, p. 19, concatenates references; Appendix C, p. 21, computes reference n-gram intersections; Appendix D.2, p. 22, converts those intersections into scores. The official repository is linked directly in the caption of Table 2, p. 7. [E1]\par
Suspect implementation step. In the pinned compute\_data\_overlap\_metrics.py, lines 100-108 index each reference separately. Lines 174-186 only record matches against that index, and lines 239-249 emit the recorded entries. By contrast, compute\_metrics\_from\_ngrams.py, lines 278-294, processes emitted entries and joins references at line 292 before computing scores. Therefore the matcher omits cross-reference n-grams that the paper and downstream denominator include. [E2]\par
Complete case A. Let r1 be ’alpha beta gamma delta epsilon zeta eta’ and r2 be ’theta iota kappa lambda mu nu xi’. Set n = 13 and let the sole training document be their fourteen-token concatenation. Under the paper’s definition, the concatenated reference has two 13-grams, both present in training. Binary overlap is 1, n-gram fraction is 2/2 = 1, and the union of their token positions covers all fourteen tokens, giving token overlap 14/14 = 1. Each separately indexed seven-token reference has zero 13-grams. The released matching stage therefore emits no reference-overlap entry for this instance. Missing output is the demonstrated static consequence; it is not mislabeled as a numerical zero returned by a helper. [E3]\par
Complete case B avoids short individual references. Use the twenty-six-token sequence ’alpha beta gamma delta epsilon zeta eta theta iota kappa lambda mu nu xi omicron pi rho sigma tau upsilon phi chi psi omega aleph beth’. Split after token thirteen into two references, and use the entire sequence as the training document. The paper’s concatenation has 26 - 13 + 1 = 14 distinct 13-grams, all overlapping, so its n-gram fraction is 1. Per-reference indexing retains only two 13-grams. A reference entry now exists, but downstream scoring uses the concatenated denominator fourteen and only two recorded matches: 2/14 = 1/7. Binary and token coverage remain 1 in this case. All tokens are distinct and both references have length thirteen, so neither zero denominators nor multiplicity conventions explain this discrepancy. [E3]\par

Alternative reading and counterevidence. Preserving reference boundaries is a legitimate design that avoids artificial matches between separate answer choices. It is not the convention stated in B.1, and it should not be combined silently with a concatenated downstream denominator. The finding is a specification/implementation mismatch, not an assertion that concatenation is scientifically preferable.\par

No Table 1 classification is shown to change. The missing publication-run linkage remains Needs Evidence; it is not filled by assuming current public code is the historical executable. Alternatively, explicitly adopt per-reference matching and make definitions and downstream denominators consistent. These are precise alternatives with different measurement meanings. No author file was changed, neither alternative was adopted, and no historical result was recomputed.\par
\# 5. Problem 2: the Codeforces example does not establish the claimed causal contribution\par
Source claim and location. The Introduction, p. 1, and Section 3, p. 3, connect the published GPT-4 Codeforces evaluation to a separate report of success on ten pre-2021 problems and failure on recent problems. Section 3 then states that overlap reporting would have made overlap’s significant contribution to the described breakthroughs clear. The issue is this inference and the linked evaluation populations, not a claim that the anecdotal counts are fabricated. [E1]\par
Primary-source check. The GPT-4 Technical Report’s Table 1, p. 5, reports a Codeforces rating of 392, below the fifth human percentile. Appendix A.6, p. 25, describes ten recent contests, about six problems each, with ten attempts per problem. Table 9, p. 30, already reports zero measured contamination for that evaluated set. This is a different evaluation from ten old problems. The source’s own contamination test is not treated as proof that every possible form of overlap is absent. [E4]\par
Complete inference check. Let A be the report’s recent-contest evaluation and B the separate historical-problem comparison. Evidence about scores on B does not establish membership of A in the training data or the causal contribution of overlap to A’s scores. Even accepting the paper’s old/new accuracy numbers, time also changes the sampled problems and potentially their difficulty and selection. The reported comparison contains no identification argument that holds these factors fixed. Furthermore, the specific assertion that missing reporting prevented understanding A is not supported when the cited report already supplies an overlap analysis.\par
Alternative reading. The historical-problem result can be a useful warning signal and motivation for more transparent evaluation. The original cited social-media source was inaccessible in this review, so the counts were not independently authenticated. Low human percentile also does not by itself rule out best performance among contemporary language models. Neither of those stronger accusations is made here.\par
Impact, repair, and judgment. Minor is supported for this bounded empirical illustration: the survey count and the protocol definition are not derived from this anecdote, and the demonstrated failure is a local population/causal attribution. A faithful narrowing would present the anecdote as a separate concern about historical problems and avoid treating it as proof that contamination caused the published result. This logical narrowing is supported; no causal effect has been estimated, no author correction has been adopted, and no new experiment was run.\par
\# 6. Counterevidence, domain clarification, and unperformed work\par

The p. 2 phrase giving 23 remaining models is a local numerical/editorial inconsistency, not evidence that the 9/30 count is wrong. These presentation issues do not create additional substantive findings. [E1; E3]\par
Partial transparency versus complete coverage. Section 5.2 asks about each reported benchmark, but also states an operational minimum of some meaningful information. Appendix A discloses coverage of 6/8 benchmarks for GPT-4, 8/14 for Qwen2, and prefiltering for 12/24 listed Apple benchmarks. The positive scores are defensible as partial-transparency scores. They must not be interpreted as certification of complete benchmark coverage. No unambiguous scoring error is claimed from the broader aspirational criterion. [E1, pp. 4, 18-19]\par
Short-sequence domain. The stated family of metrics maps token sequences into [0,1], but the n-gram fraction has denominator zero when a nonempty sequence contains fewer than thirteen tokens. For x = (’alpha’), its printed expression is 0/0. This is an omitted domain/convention, preserved as a clarification. Static tracing supplies crucial counterevidence to a crash allegation: no full-length n-gram is indexed for such a component, no match entry is emitted, and the ordinary downstream path therefore does not call the unguarded division on it. No end-to-end crash was demonstrated. A total mathematical definition needs a stated short-sequence convention or restricted domain. [E1-E3]\par
