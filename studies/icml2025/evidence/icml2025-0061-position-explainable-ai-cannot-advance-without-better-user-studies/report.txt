# 3. Problem 1 - Released subsample does not fully recover the stated analysis input

Source claim and location. Appendix A.2, p.16 describes two time-based samples of 30 draws each, plus all 56 top-4 papers, for 116 sample memberships. The captions of Figures 4, 6 and 7 also describe 30-draw temporal subsamples. Table 1, p.17 identifies SubsamplingGroup as the released membership information.

Complete check. The independent parser reads the semicolon-delimited CSV, expands every comma-separated SubsamplingGroup token, and preserves repeated labels on the same row. This is necessary because sampling was with replacement. It yields 30 earlier-group memberships on 29 distinct rows, 29 newer-group memberships on 29 rows, and 56 top-4 memberships. The sum is 115, with 111 distinct rows across overlapping groups. Thus the linked public dataset does not encode one of the declared newer-group draws. This is not a mistaken comparison of 116 with the number of unique papers.

Additional replay check. For the explicit newer-group memberships, CatObjSubj gives 4 Objective, 11 Objective and Subjective, and 14 Subjective observations (n=29). The corresponding raw proportions are 0.138, 0.379 and 0.483. The p.7 Figure 6 orange points are approximately 0.32, 0.25 and 0.44, respectively; they do not follow directly from that released subsample. Visual readings are approximate, not digitized exact numbers.

Alternative reading and counterevidence. Figure 6 may have used a different, figure-specific filter: all annotated earlier-year rows give 13/42 Objective, 9/42 both and 20/42 Subjective, closely matching its red points. The analogous newer-year set has 69 rows (22 Objective, 18 both, 29 Subjective). This plausible alternative must not be confused with proving that the figure is incorrect. It does, however, leave the documented sample-to-figure mapping unresolved. The repository has no plotting code or immutable figure-generation record to settle the issue.

Impact and judgment. Minor: a bounded release and documentation defect blocks exact replay of the stated subsample and some figure inputs. The evidence does not identify which data actually generated the published figures, and therefore does not demonstrate a false published empirical conclusion. The key top-4 reporting-completeness count (3/56) is reproducible and does not depend on the missing temporal-group membership. This is a claim-level impact assessment, not a downgrade based on the article being a position paper.

Repair status. No repaired dataset or analysis is asserted. A defensible repair requires the actual sampled indices (including multiplicities), the figure-specific filters, missing-value rules and archived generation inputs, or an explicit correction of the sampling description if the release was the intended input. Adding an invented thirtieth row would not be a valid repair. Author adoption has not been requested.
