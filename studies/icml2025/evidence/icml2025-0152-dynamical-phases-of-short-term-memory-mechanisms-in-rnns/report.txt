# Concerns requiring evidence

Problem 1 (PCA-sign dependence of the released discriminator).  The statistic is not an intrinsic property of the neural trajectory. The exact algebra is established; its effect on the paper’s successful networks and empirical conclusions is unresolved.

## Exact calculation

For rows \(z_t=(u_t,v_t)\) with \(u_t\ne v_t\), centering the two coordinates gives \[z_t-\overline z_t(1,1)
=\tfrac12(u_t-v_t)(1,-1).\] Thus the Pearson matrix is exactly \[C_{ts}=\operatorname{sgn}(u_t-v_t)
        \operatorname{sgn}(u_s-v_s).\] It has rank one and discards the magnitudes and most geometry of the scores. A permissible sign change of the second PCA eigenvector replaces this sign signal by \(\operatorname{sgn}(u_t+v_t)\). Distinct eigenvalues do not remove eigenvector-sign freedom.

For a reproducible finite example, let \(N=400\) and \[\theta_j=2\pi(j+0.123)/400,\quad
u_j=0.2625(\sin\theta_j+\sin8\theta_j),\quad
v_j=0.25(\sin\theta_j-\sin8\theta_j).\] Set the first three columns of a 100-column trajectory to \((u_j,v_j,0.1\cos\theta_j)\) and all others to zero. Its columns are centered and orthogonal; their sample variances are approximately \(0.06907895\), \(0.06265664\), and \(0.00501253\). The first two PCs therefore have distinct leading eigenvalues. All entries lie in \([-1,1]\); the largest absolute entry is \(0.519961\). The minimum absolute coordinate differences are nonzero under both signs, so no undefined Pearson entry is used.

The auxiliary’s direct two-dimensional FFT and the parent’s independent factorization of the Fourier transform of \(ss^\top\) agree: \[D(u,v)=8.8729396253\,10^{-7},\qquad
D(u,-v)=0.6570498839.\] The same data and principal subspace therefore receive opposite classes under the released threshold. Independent outputs are and .

## Assumption boundary and counterevidence

This is a valid counterexample to PCA-sign invariance of the statistic. It is not a verified counterexample satisfying every condition of the complete trained-RNN experiment: no weights realizing this trajectory under the paper’s tanh dynamics were constructed, and the reaction-accuracy/reliability gates were not demonstrated. The published claim describes reliable separation in sampled successful networks; it does not state a universal coordinate-invariance theorem.

Consequently, a Major finding against the phase diagram is not established by this construction. A deterministic sign convention could make one implementation reproducible, and the actual sampled trajectories could be insensitive to signs. Representative dynamical examples and the toy-model theory provide evidence independent of the bulk classifier. Conversely, reproducible signs alone would not establish that the resulting classification tracks the intended dynamics.

The supported outcome is Needs Evidence for empirical classification reliability. Resolving it requires the historical trajectory/provenance records and a sign-sensitivity assessment against a dynamical reference classification. A sign-invariant Gram/distance statistic is a possible alternative, not a verified correction offered by this review.

Problem 2 (Delay-dependent normalization complicates the theory-to-training comparison).  The toy loss and an inspected released training loss differ by a delay-dependent factor. The algebra is exact; its contribution to reported empirical exponents remains unresolved.

Equations (S7) and (S17) add separately normalized delay- and response-window mean losses. In , lines 144–148 assign response weight \(1-M/N\) and other-time weight \(M/N\), with \(M=10\) response samples and \(N\) total samples. Line 65 averages the weighted loss over the whole sequence.

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the respective mean squared errors outside and inside the response window, using the same batch/output averaging. The release computes \[L_{\mathrm{release}}
=\frac{1}{N}\left[(N-M)\frac{M}{N}A
+M\left(1-\frac{M}{N}\right)B\right]
=\frac{M(N-M)}{N^2}(A+B).\] For \(N=20,40,80\), the factors are \(0.25,0.1875,0.109375\). Rebalancing the windows thus does not keep the overall loss scale fixed as delay varies. Under otherwise identical gradient dynamics, a nominal learning rate \(\eta\) corresponds to balanced-loss rate \(\eta M(N-M)/N^2\). The finite scale values are recorded in .

This is a comparison requiring explanation, not proof that the empirical exponent is wrong: cue windows, momentum and different toy/full-RNN dynamics remain, and the historical execution-to-commit correspondence is unverified. Dividing by the displayed factor removes this algebraic scale difference only; it is not a validated full training repair.
