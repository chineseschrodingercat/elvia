# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The step-count breakdown does not satisfy the partition identity).  Appendix A.14, Figure 4 (p. 43), reports mutually inconsistent all-task, successful-task and failed-task mean step counts under the table’s stated common-population interpretation.

## Claim, assumptions and calculation

Section 5.1 (p. 7) specifies binary complete-task success on 165 WebArena-Lite test cases. Figure 4 reports \(N=165\), success rate \(53.9\%\), all-task mean \(11.12\), success mean \(7.52\), and failure mean \(13.43\). Its original rendered page confirms these values.

For one finite population partitioned exhaustively into \(S\) successes and \(F=N-S\) failures, ordinary task means necessarily obey \[N\overline n_{\mathrm{all}}
=S\overline n_{\mathrm{success}}+F\overline n_{\mathrm{failure}}.\] This is an identity of sums, with no distributional assumption. The displayed success percentage and denominator imply \(S=89\), \(F=76\). The other two means consequently imply \[\overline n_{\mathrm{all}}
=\frac{89(7.52)+76(13.43)}{165}
=\frac{14083}{1375}
=10.2421818\ldots,\] not \(11.12\). The discrepancy is \(0.8778182\). Each displayed mean rounded to two decimals has error at most \(0.005\); the combined discrepancy tolerance is \(0.01\). Rounding cannot account for the difference. ## Counterevidence, impact and correction

Conditional means averaged separately across multiple runs, different denominators, or different step conventions could yield different aggregates. Such conventions are not stated for this table and would require revised labels and denominators. The website success counts themselves are consistent: \((16,16,17,25,12,3)\) sum to \(89\). Therefore the check does not contradict the principal \(53.94\%\) success rate.

Problem 2 (Released matching erases key–value associations).  The pinned exact-match utility has a definite semantic false positive for the monthly-count output form illustrated on pp. 19–21. Historical benchmark impact remains unresolved.

## Source and complete finite check

In `plan_and_act/cot/utils.py`, lines 1037–1051, failed normalized-string comparisons fall back to equality of alphanumeric token multisets. Lines 1011–1025 similarly use token-set inclusion for `must_include`. The consuming `plan_and_act/previous_rounds_evaluators.py`, lines 136–141 and 174–201, routes exact and inclusion evaluations through these helpers.

Take the ordinary ASCII reference and prediction \[r=\texttt{01:11, 02:16},\qquad
p=\texttt{01:16, 02:11}.\] The task requests month-to-count associations. The independent mapping/token diagnostic is . The prediction assigns both counts to the wrong month. Both strings are unaffected by case, Unicode or separator normalization relevant here; string equality fails. Their token sequences are respectively \[(01,11,02,16),\qquad(01,16,02,11),\] which have identical multisets. The release therefore returns \(1.0\) through the fallback, although the mappings differ. For the inclusion branch, both required token sets \(\{01,11\}\) and \(\{02,16\}\) are subsets of the wrong prediction’s token set, so that fallback also accepts. The independent scripts enumerate these strings and mappings without importing or calling author code.

## Alternatives and bounded consequence

Order-insensitive matching is valid for an unordered set of independent answers. A month-to-count mapping requires preserving each internal association; sorting complete pairs would preserve this information, whereas sorting all tokens does not. Punctuation normalization cannot justify swapping values.

This is a supported Minor defect in the release. The run script imports an evaluator under `evaluation_harness`, while the inspected file is delivered under `plan_and_act`; runtime path mapping and publication-time commit correspondence are not established. The existence of the helper is therefore not proof that it scored the historical runs, nor that any particular published answer exploited it. No false-positive count or adjusted benchmark rate was measured.

For a fixed month:count schema, comparing parsed mappings restores the illustrated semantic requirement. This is an example-specific correction, not a universal replacement or a validated rerun of the benchmark evaluator.
