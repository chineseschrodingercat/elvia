# 2. Definitions, assumptions and reasoning that survives

At a fixed positive Gaussian noise level \(\sigma\), let p and q denote the real and generated noised densities, m\(\alpha\)=\(\alpha\)p+(1\(-\)\(\alpha\))q, and s_r=\(\nabla\)log r. The data distribution before noising is p0; a differentiable generator g\(\theta\) maps an independent latent variable to generated clean samples. Expectations in noised gradient formulas also average over the injected Gaussian noise. Function-space regression claims require realizability and attainment; pathwise differentiation additionally requires differentiation/integration interchange and transport regularity.

\[D_\alpha=\frac{\alpha p}{m_\alpha},\qquad s_{m_\alpha}=D_\alpha s_p+(1-D_\alpha)s_q,\qquad \Delta=s_q-s_p.\]

Proposition 3.1 and Appendix A.1 [R1, pp.3,12] are consistent with direct differentiation of the paper’s scaled \(\alpha\)-JSD. Mass conservation cancels explicit score-mean terms, yielding the pathwise score-difference update. The \(\alpha\)\(\to\)0 and \(\alpha\)\(\to\)1 limits are forward and reverse KL under the needed integrability. At \(\alpha\)=1/2 the paper’s normalization is four times conventional JSD; its own gradient remains internally consistent.

\[\mathcal D_\alpha=\frac{\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert m_\alpha)}{\alpha}+\frac{\mathrm{KL}(p\Vert m_\alpha)}{1-\alpha},\quad
\partial_\theta\mathcal D_\alpha=\frac1\alpha\int(\partial_\theta q)\log(q/m_\alpha).\]

Completing the square verifies Proposition 3.2’s mixture-score regression. Gaussian DSM in Proposition A.1 identifies \(-\)E[\(\epsilon\)|Y]/\(\sigma\)=s_m\(\alpha\)(Y). Proposition A.2 follows by differentiating log m\(\alpha\) [pp.13–14]. These facts identify the combined score, not an arbitrary learned factorization of it. Four independent Gaussian quadrature/finite-difference checks of Proposition 3.1 differ by at most 4.58\(\times\)10^\(-\)10 [E1]. These finite checks support the analytic calculation; they are not a general proof.

Tweedie’s relation s=(f\(-\)y)/\(\sigma\)\({}^{2}\) and the denoiser conversion in Appendix C are correct in the inspected Gaussian setting. Static source inspection confirms the real/fake DSM mixture weights, generator detachment in score training, generator vector-field sign, frozen teacher and correct logistic gate [E2]. Appendix D.3’s discriminator argument assumes both component scores are already correct. Its weighted Brier optimum identifies the gate where their difference is nonzero, under unrestricted realizability; it cannot establish simultaneous recovery of both unknown factors.

# 3. Problem 1 — adaptive weighting eliminates the claimed KL interpolation

Source claim and location. Section 3.5, p.5, Eq.(9)–(10), defines a samplewise adaptive weight and states that it preserves the forward-KL limiting behavior as \(\alpha\)\(\to\)0. This supports the multi-divergence explanation in Section 3.1 and the interpretation of the ablation in Section 5.3. The printed new normalization contains no additive pseudo-Huber constant. Write w=w_DMD w\(\alpha\) and retain the common DMD weight exactly.

\[w_\alpha=\alpha\frac{\|s_q-s_p\|}{\|s_q-s_{m_\alpha}\|},\qquad
\gamma^w=\mathbb E_q\!\left[J_\theta g\, w_{\mathrm{DMD}}w_\alpha\frac{s_q-s_{m_\alpha}}{\alpha}\right].\]

Complete check. For 0<\(\alpha\)<1, positive p,q and \(\Delta\)\(\ne\)0, the valid mixture-score identity from Proposition A.2 gives s_q\(-\)s_m\(\alpha\)=D\(\alpha\)\(\Delta\) with D\(\alpha\)>0. Homogeneity of the norm then gives w\(\alpha\)=\(\alpha\)/D\(\alpha\). Substitution yields the following pointwise identity, before any expectation or limit.

\[w_\alpha\,\frac{s_q-s_{m_\alpha}}{\alpha}=\Delta,\qquad
\gamma^w=\mathbb E_q[J_\theta g\,w_{\mathrm{DMD}}\Delta]\quad(0<\alpha<1).\]

This is the same DMD-weighted reverse-KL direction as at \(\alpha\)=1. Before the new normalizer, the forward limit is (p/q)\(\Delta\): its density ratio changes the generated-data expectation to a real-data expectation. The new normalizer removes precisely that factor. Calling the update weighted does not preserve the missing \(\alpha\) dependence.

Implementation check. At pinned [R2], main/sjsd/sjsd_guidance.py:263–276 computes \[R=\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{mean}((p_\alpha-p_0)^2)}
                   {\operatorname{mean}((p_1-p_0)^2+10^{-6})}},
 \qquad \mathrm{grad}=\frac{p_\alpha-p_0}{A R},
 \qquad A=\operatorname{mean}|p_1|.\] Exact mixture denoisers imply p\(\alpha\)\(-\)p0=D\(\alpha\)(p1\(-\)p0). Hence D\(\alpha\) cancels even with this stabilizer: the remaining factor depends on the reference difference, not \(\alpha\). The current implementation therefore does not rescue the claimed limit [E2].

Supported impact: Major. The failure directly affects the asserted divergence geometry of a central practical update. It leaves the unweighted derivation intact. Alpha can still affect learned-score approximation, amortization and the optional GAN regularizer. The finding does not assert that the whole algorithm, its finite training trajectories or its empirical ablation are \(\alpha\)-independent.

# 4. Problem 1 — complete full-weight example and precise limits

Parameters and assumptions. Use two dimensions, \(\sigma\)=1, p0=\(\tfrac12\)\(\delta\)_(1,0)+\(\tfrac12\)\(\delta\)_(\(-\)1,0), and g\(\theta\)(z)=\(\theta\) at \(\theta\)=(2,1). The latent may be standard normal and ignored. The noised laws are a two-Gaussian mixture p and q=N((2,1),I), both smooth, strictly positive and finite-moment. E_q[\(\partial\)\(\theta\) log q]=0. Only their noised scores are used; the unnoised point masses do not enter the density differentiation.

\[f_p(y)=(\tanh y_1,0),\quad
\Delta(y)=(2-\tanh y_1,1),\quad
w_{\mathrm{DMD}}=\frac1{\|(2,1)-f_p(y)\|_1}=\frac1{3-\tanh y_1}.\]

The second coordinate of \(\Delta\) is one, so the score-difference norms never vanish. The DMD weight lies strictly between 1/4 and 1/2, so all weighted expectations exist. Eq.(32)’s summed L1 norm is used. Replacing that sum by the released per-coordinate mean supplies a common dimension factor and does not restore \(\alpha\) dependence.

Let u(y)=((2\(-\)tanh y)/(3\(-\)tanh y), 1/(3\(-\)tanh y)). The actual weighted update for every interior \(\alpha\) is E_[N(2,1)]u. The forward limit before the new \(\alpha\) normalizer, retaining the same DMD weight, is E_[\(\tfrac12\)N(\(-\)1,1)+\(\tfrac12\)N(1,1)]u. Dominated convergence applies: for \(\alpha\)\(\leq\)1/2, qp/m\(\alpha\)\(\leq\)2p and u is bounded.

The vectors both sum to one and differ, so even a global rescaling cannot reconcile them. Their inequality also has a direct proof: u2 is strictly increasing. Couple Yq=Z+2 with Yp=Z+B, where Z is standard normal and B is an independent equiprobable \(\pm\)1. Then Yq>Yp always, so E_q u2>E_p u2. Quadrature reports the size; it does not establish the existence of the discrepancy.

Scoped repair: describe this normalization as an ideal-score collapse to the common reverse-KL direction. Changing/removing it changes the algorithm; no performance-preserving replacement or fresh empirical repair is verified.

# 5. Problem 2 — Corollary 4.1 does not identify its learned components

Source claim and dependency. Corollary 4.1, p.6, fixes \(\alpha\) and noise and states that a minimizer of Eq.(14) recovers both the fake score and log(p/q). The following text interprets this as density-ratio learning; Eq.(15) uses the factors separately, and Section 4.2 uses the ratio in a generator regularizer. Appendix A establishes only the combined mixture-score optimum. Existence of one correct factorization does not identify all minimizers.

Interior counterexample. Let p0=N(0,1), q0=N(1,1), \(\sigma\)=1 and \(\alpha\)=1/2. A generator is g\(\mu\)(z)=\(\mu\)+z with standard-normal z, at \(\mu\)=1. Then p=N(0,2), q=N(1,2), and m=(p+q)/2. All densities are positive and smooth, moments and DSM risks are finite, and the usual differentiation and score-mean conditions hold. Choose the following smooth, wrong learned factors.

\[\ell_\psi(y)=0,\quad D_\psi=\tfrac12,\quad
s_{\mathrm{fake}}(y)=2s_m(y)-s_p(y),\quad
s_{\mathrm{exp}}=\tfrac12s_p+\tfrac12s_{\mathrm{fake}}=s_m.\]

The DSM conditional-mean decomposition is a constant plus E_m||s_exp\(-\)s_m||\({}^{2}\). Thus this pair attains the global minimum of Eq.(14), not merely a stationary point. It is also a legitimate fake density score, not an arbitrary nonintegrable vector field. With c=e^(1/2), h=N(2,2), one has q\({}^{2}\)/p=c h and

\[r(y)=\frac{p(y)+2q(y)+c h(y)}{3+c},\qquad
\nabla\log r=\nabla\log(m^2/p)=2s_m-s_p.\]

The mixture weights are positive and sum to one. Moreover r is the \(\sigma\)=1 convolution of the same weighted mixture of N(0,1), N(1,1), N(2,1). Even requiring a normalized density score obtained under the same noising process does not exclude the construction. The regression theorem states no architectural or additional identifying constraint that forbids these function-space factors.

Both claimed conclusions fail: the true log ratio is 1/4\(-\)y/2, not zero; at y=0, s_q=0.5 while s_fake=0.437823499114. The replay verifies the density normalization and exact combined-score identity, with numerical score residual below 8.89\(\times\)10^\(-\)16 [E1]. The scientific defect is the invalid inference from mixture-score identification to separate component identification.

# 6. Problem 2 — generator consequence, counterevidence and scoped repair

Generator consequence. Let D*=p/(p+q). For the location parameter \(\mu\), the Jacobian is one and \(\Delta\)=s_q\(-\)s_p=1/2. The correct divergence gradient is E_q[D*]. At the wrong global Eq.(14) minimizer above, Eq.(15) uses D\(\psi\)=1/2 and s_fake separately, producing 1\(-\)E_q[D*].

\[\gamma_{\rm true}=2\mathbb E_q[D^*\Delta]=\mathbb E_q[D^*],
\qquad \gamma_{\rm wrong}=\mathbb E_q[(D_\psi/\alpha)(s_{\rm fake}-s_p)]
=1-\mathbb E_q[D^*].\]

Thus even exact estimation of the combined mixture score does not suffice for the proposed separate-factor gradient. Major is supported by a false central identification guarantee and its direct methodological dependency. The fact that the correct pair is also a minimizer is important counterevidence to a stronger allegation of impossible learning, but does not establish the corollary’s identification claim.

Practical counterevidence. The paper trains across \(\alpha\) and emphasizes mass at \(\alpha\)=0. More decisively, the released SMD trainer sums a separate fake-only DSM loss with real/fake interpolated losses (main/sjsd_dist/train_sjsd_dist.py:376–379). Its interpolation detaches component predictions while training the gate [R2,E2]. This richer objective is not identical to the fixed-\(\alpha\) premise. The counterexample is not an empirical failure of that implementation.

A precise restricted replacement is available. Assume exact realizability and simultaneous attainment of a joint objective with positive weight on \(\alpha\)=0 and at least one interior \(\alpha\). The \(\alpha\)=0 term forces s_fake=s_q almost everywhere. The interior term then forces (D\(\psi\)\(-\)D*)(s_p\(-\)s_q)=0. Therefore the log ratio is identified wherever s_p\(\ne\)s_q. This is an analytic statement about exact minima, not a finite-training convergence guarantee.

The nondegeneracy condition matters. If p=q, choosing s_fake=s_p gives the correct mixture score for every \(\alpha\) and any ratio head, so the objective cannot force \(\ell\)=0. The score-based generator gradient is nevertheless zero. Appendix D.3 explicitly assumes accurate component scores and is consistent with this limitation; it is not a proof of the original simultaneous-identification claim. Author adoption and empirical verification of any modified theorem or training protocol remain pending.

# 7. Problems 3 and 4 — bounded current-release defects

Problem 3: covariance accumulation (Minor). In [R2], main/sjsd/test_folder_edm.py:188–190 initializes the 2048\(\times\)2048 second-moment accumulator with ones. Lines 204–213 add feature outer products, subtract N\(\mu\)\(\mu\)\(^{\mathsf T}\) and divide by N\(-\)1. For one evaluation process and N>1, the result is \(\Sigma\)_sample+J/(N\(-\)1), where J is the all-ones matrix. This identity holds regardless of the feature values. The vendored third_party/edm/fid.py instead initializes with zeros.

\[\widehat\Sigma_{\rm release}=
\frac{J+\sum_i x_ix_i^\top-N\bar x\bar x^\top}{N-1}
=\widehat\Sigma_{\rm sample}+\frac{J}{N-1}.\]

Independent two-dimensional illustration [E2]: features (1,0),(\(-\)1,0) give the correct covariance diag(2,0), whereas the released formula gives [[3,1],[1,1]]. The universal offset proof does not depend on these being actual Inception outputs. At the configured N=50,000, the added matrix has trace 2048/49,999; that trace is not a predicted FID change. No reference covariance or generated samples were evaluated. A zero initializer precisely removes this algebraic offset, but no evaluator patch or end-to-end repair was run.

Problem 4: incompatible public entry-point contracts (Minor). In main/sjsd/sjsd_guidance.py:207, compute_density_ratio accepts three arguments besides self; compute_cls_logits:396 supplies four. The enabled GAN generator path therefore has a direct Python call mismatch if preceding initialization succeeds. The guidance classifier call also supplies real_image/fake_image/real_label/fake_label keywords to a function declared with x_real/x_fake/label/step. Reviewer-owned argument-binding demonstrations produce TypeError; no author function was executed.

Additional release constraints are preserved in [E2]: SMT reads undeclared args.distillation; the SMD launchers pass parser-unsupported flags; imports reference modules absent from the archived tree. An external overlay could supply modules, so that absence is not treated as proof that historical runs were impossible. Earlier initialization failures do not invalidate the static signature mismatch, but limit claims about the first runtime failure.

Impact and repair limits. These are demonstrated current-source defects, with no established link to the evaluator or training snapshot used for published tables. Their supported impact is bounded release reliability. Historical source/configuration and raw logs are needed to determine paper-level empirical effects. No score deterioration, fabrication, complete repair or author adoption is asserted.

# 8. Coverage, configuration discrepancies and preserved history

Empirical claims were read against Table 2, Figure 2, Section 5 and Appendix E [R1]. ImageNet SMT reports 3.23 FID versus iCT 4.02 and iCT-deep 3.25; SMD reports 1.48 versus DMD2 with GAN 1.51, while the separately identified expensive finetuned DMD2 result is 1.23. CIFAR-10 SMT 3.13 is above iCT 2.83; SMD 2.22 is above SiD 1.92. The paper describes competitiveness and acknowledges important comparison categories. This review does not convert those qualified comparisons into an invented universal-best claim.

Ablations support empirical benefits from the adaptive weighting, multiple \(\alpha\) values and GAN regularization in the reported runs. Such benefits can coexist with Problem 1 because approximate amortized scores and auxiliary losses remain \(\alpha\)-dependent. Stable training is treated as an observation in these experiments, not as an unstated universal convergence theorem. Swiss-roll and interpolation claims were inspected descriptively; samples, metrics and trajectories were not regenerated.

Release configuration: the current SMT score sampler uses a probability-1/2 branch uniform on {0,1/2,1}, otherwise a 1001-point grid. Its \(\alpha\)=0 mass is 1/6+1/2002=502/3003\(\approx\)0.167166, differing from p.5’s 25%. SMD uses a different augmentation helper; the SMT discrepancy is not transferred to it. This is a current configuration difference, with historical-run provenance unresolved [E2].

The p.5 head-only discriminator-training statement is not enforced by the released live-backbone feature graph and all-parameter guidance optimizer. Existing signature failures prevent claiming that this branch actually ran. The resulting paper impact remains Needs Evidence. Main-text batch 40 versus Table 3 batch 280 plausibly reflects seven processes at 40 per process in the launchers; the exact historical distributed configuration and compute comparison remain unverified.

The same-data denoising warmup is explicitly disclosed and is compatible with training from scratch. The release’s checkpoint/warmup configuration is incomplete, but this is not evidence of a secret external teacher. Known corrections are preserved: author commit f1b1fbf2ae2f58776d0739469e788647691575fd (19 August 2025) changes adaptive-weight multiplication to division, present in the pinned current code. Commit 2be705cfbea6f19ddff6076b3c1d29dcc4259d14 corrects an initializer spelling. These are historical author fixes, not current defects.

No OpenReview discussion was inspected. Missing items include exact historical training/evaluation commits, complete launch environments, warmup/checkpoint provenance, raw FID statistics, generated samples, seed variability and complete compute accounting. These omissions do not raise supported severity.

# 9. Dependence, versions and conclusion table

These central failures survive even if the empirical results and the broader utility of one-step mixture-score methods remain intact.
