Abstract: The reported scaling experiments, released selection procedure, and worked examples were examined. One Major concern is supported: the ambiguity-conditioned analysis does not identify the higher-quality-response mechanism attributed to it in Section 2.2. An explicit fixed-quality model with the same 50 independent binary verification attempts gives increasing conditional selection accuracy. This invalidates the identifying inference, not the observed accuracy trend. A separate Minor concern qualifies the smaller-model comparison. Scientific decisions remain pending.

# Problem 1: the claimed control does not identify response quality

Judgment: Major for the core identifying argument; Needs Evidence for the proposed mechanism in the actual data.

## Claim, dependency, and suspect step

Section 2.2 (p. 3), supported by Figures 2–3 (pp. 4, 6), conditions accuracy on at least one correct candidate. It presents this as isolating the burden of discriminating among more candidates after accounting for the increased chance of finding a correct answer. The ensuing increase is taken to demonstrate that the larger pool contains higher-quality correct responses, where quality means rigor or freedom from reasoning flaws. This is one of the three principal explanatory factors introduced on p. 3 and contributes to the abstract’s interpretation of scalability.

The condition fixes existence of a correct candidate, not their number or the opportunities for a fixed stochastic verifier to assign a high score. Thus the reported conditional trend does not distinguish improved objective response quality from repeated chances to verify equally good responses.

## A complete countermodel for the identifying inference

Consider independent candidates, each correct with probability \(p=0.1\), with all correct candidates of identical reasoning quality. All correct candidates share one correct final answer and all incorrect candidates one wrong final answer. Each candidate receives 50 independent binary checks, approving a correct candidate with probability \(0.7\) and an incorrect one with probability \(0.6\). These laws do not change with \(k\). Retain the maximum score within each final-answer group, then select the higher-scoring group, breaking a tied maximum uniformly between groups. This is the no-comparison selection rule in the inspected `create_verifications_minimal.py`, lines 633–684; selection uses no answer key.

Equation reference: eq:conditional. Let \(U_s\) and \(V_s\) be the cumulative distribution functions of \(\operatorname{Bin}(50,0.7)\) and \(\operatorname{Bin}(50,0.6)\), with \(U_{-1}=V_{-1}=0\), and define \[H_{r,t}=pU_r+(1-p)V_t .\] For \(k\) candidates, \(H_{r,t}^k\) is the joint probability that the correct and incorrect group maxima are at most \(r,t\), respectively; a missing group has maximum \(-1\). A strict correct-group win at score \(s\) has probability \(H_{s,s-1}^k-H_{s-1,s-1}^k\). Adding half the probability of a tied group maximum gives \[\begin{aligned}
 A_G(k)&=\frac12\sum_{s=0}^{50}
 \left[H_{s,s}^k+H_{s,s-1}^k-H_{s-1,s}^k-H_{s-1,s-1}^k\right],\\
 B_G(k)&=\frac{A_G(k)}{1-(1-p)^k}.
 \end{aligned}\] Independent parent evaluation at 80 decimal digits, cross-checked by conditioning on the number of correct candidates, gives \[\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 k&20&50&100&200\\ \hline
 100B_G(k)&58.116&65.456&73.579&79.980
\end{array}\] to three decimal places. All correct responses remain equally rigorous, and both per-query verification laws remain fixed. Meanwhile the expected number of correct candidates under the conditioning is \(kp/[1-(1-p)^k]\), which grows with \(k\). Averaging uniform subsamples of an initially independent pool does not remove this mechanism: marginally, each subsample still has the corresponding independent candidate law.

## Counterevidence, impact, and repair boundary

This is a counterexample to the sufficiency of the proposed identifying argument, not a claim that the synthetic score distributions reproduce Gemini’s measured errors, question filters, or curves. The authors may be right that larger pools contain better proofs. The empirical rise in conditional accuracy, the headline benchmark gains, and the usefulness of comparison/rewrite interventions all remain possible. The abstract’s partial attribution is cautious, but the more specific isolation and demonstration language in Section 2.2 requires evidence that this control does not supply. No direct candidate-quality measurement or analysis fixing the number of correct candidates was located in the inspected release or relevant appendix.

The Major judgment is tied to a demonstrated invalid inference explaining a principal scientific result; it does not require refuting the method or decreasing its reported scores. The supported immediate correction is to describe the curves as conditional selection accuracy and the quality explanation as a hypothesis. Establishing that explanation would require distinguishing objective response quality, the count of correct candidates, question composition, and verification-score noise. No replacement mechanism is asserted as the empirically established one. Evidence: original pp. 3–6; `grouped_tie_check.py`, `calc.json`, and the pinned selection source. The earlier uniform-candidate-tie calculation is retained as predecessor evidence; the group-tie calculation above matches the inspected release.

# Problem 2: smaller-model superiority has explicit exceptions

Judgment: Minor, local comparative interpretation. Table 6’s caption (p. 15) and Appendix B.1’s continuation (p. 16) say that Pro+Flash verification matches or exceeds Pro consistency, while verification strictly exceeds consistency for all model choices. The displayed MATH comparison is \[\frac{456}{500}=91.2\% < \frac{460}{500}=92.0\% ,\] so Pro+Flash trails Pro consistency by four answers, or 0.8 percentage points. Flash verification and Flash consistency also tie on LiveBench Reasoning at \(84/140\). These are direct comparisons of the stated results, without assumptions about statistical significance.

The other three Pro+Flash columns improve over Pro consistency, by 3, 1, and 9 answers, respectively. Flash improves on its other three tasks. Consequently a precise repair is to acknowledge the MATH exception and Reasoning tie. This leaves the principal Pro-only Table 1 results and the broader feasibility of cheaper verification intact. The numerical repair is verified; historical outcomes were not rerun and author adoption has not been requested. Evidence: source image `s15.png`, printed tables, `calc.json`, and `a_code.json`.

\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(a-100)(b-100)(c-100)=0 .\] For ordered nonnegative integers, each of the three coordinate-100 sets has 201 elements; their pairwise and triple intersections are the single triple \((100,100,100)\). Inclusion–exclusion gives \(3(201)-3+1=601\). Parent enumeration of all 45,451 admissible triples confirms this and the displayed multiplicities sum to 200.

The dodecagon reference answer 315 in Appendix F.1 was independently checked by enumerating the intersections of perpendicular chord segments: the six orientation-pair counts are \(51,54,51,54,51,54\). Parent floating-point checks at three tolerances agree with the auxiliary’s exact symbolic enumeration. Appendix E’s truth-puzzle, square-count, and unit-conversion answers also check out. The displayed reasoning is deliberately flawed while its final answer is correct. Appendix F’s false named theorems are explicitly model-generated rewrites of incorrect responses; they are not scientific theorems asserted by the authors.

## Released procedure and scoring

The static source maps the main settings to 200 candidates, 50 checks, and up to three distinct final answers within 0.05 of the best score for comparison. Ground truth enters final grading after selection in the minimal driver. A notebook shortcut skips verification for already graded homogeneous all-correct/all-wrong sets; any candidate then gives the same accuracy, although such oracle-dependent compute savings cannot be assumed at deployment.

The relevant prompt-tuning cell uses the stated equal-error-rate denominators. A different diagnostic cell has different denominators; attributing that diagnostic to Table 4 would be unsupported. The source’s documented training/validation split logic does not itself demonstrate leakage. The stated \(200\times50\times13{,}000=130\) million output-token arithmetic checks out; the 8192-token limit is per query, whereas a verification thread contains several queries. Historical cost totals were not reproduced.

Table 3 uses AIME denominator 14, while Tables 1 and 6 use 15. In `verification/benchmarks.py`, lines 120–178, the default excludes question 2024-II-9 but explicitly instructs that it still count as an unanswered question. The notebooks retain that default, whereas the minimal scripts request all 15. This explains the discrepancy and supports the conservative headline denominator; it does not establish score inflation. The denominator convention should be stated consistently.

Cross-model protocol matching for the disclosed public o1 scores remains unverified. The manual grading audit was not repeated. Power-law descriptions concern the tested regime.
