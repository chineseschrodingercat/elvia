Abstract: Three Major findings concern the identification of ground-truth posteriors, the strict CE-bias proposition, and test-set independence in the released CIFAR evaluation path. Two Minor findings concern loss normalization and metric implementation. Finite counterexamples, exact arithmetic, and static control-flow arguments establish the stated scopes without executing author code. The L2 two-point balance result survives independent verification. Historical table corruption, a reversal of the reported empirical advantages, and failure of semantic augmentation in general are not established.

# Supported Problems

Problem 1 (Major: posterior identification does not follow from the reannotation argument).  Claim and dependency. The abstract, Sections 3.2–3.3, pp. 3–5, and the conclusion describe soft annotations as ground-truth uncertainty or accurate class posteriors. Section 3.3 introduces a classification-optimal encoder \(E\), asserts Eq. (7), and then removes prototype norm/distance terms to obtain the allegedly bias-free Eq. (11). This is a core justification for the proposed reannotation, not merely an ancillary motivation.

First implication: classification optimality does not identify Eq. (7). Consider a finite population of 40 equally weighted observations with \(E(x)=x\), ten observations at each of \(x=-2,-1,1,2\). Their class-1 counts are \((1,4,6,9)\), and class-0 counts are \((9,6,4,1)\). The true posteriors are therefore \[\eta(-2)=0.1,\quad\eta(-1)=0.4,\quad
 \eta(1)=0.6,\quad\eta(2)=0.9.\] The prototype sums specified in Eq. (7) are \(P_1=18\) and \(P_0=-18\). Class counts and prototype norms are equal. The prototype classifier predicts class 1 precisely when \(x>0\), attaining Bayes-optimal classification accuracy. Yet Eq. (7) prescribes \[\eta_{\rm proto}(x)=\sigma(36x/\tau).\] No single \(\tau>0\) gives both \(\eta_{\rm proto}(1)=0.6\) and \(\eta_{\rm proto}(2)=0.9\). Matching the former forces odds \(3/2\) at \(x=1\), hence odds \(9/4\) and probability \(9/13\) at \(x=2\), rather than \(9/10\). The discrepancy is \(27/130\). Dividing both prototypes by their equal class counts changes the scale but not this impossibility. Thus the example satisfies the stated classification-optimality premise and even equal prototype norms; it refutes the claimed implication to calibrated probabilities.

Second implication: removing norm terms can change a correct posterior. For a separate test, grant the stronger premise that Eq. (7) already equals the true posterior. Use finite distinct prototypes and a nonzero residual \[P_i=(2,0,0),\quad P_j=(0,1,0),\quad
 r=(0,0,1),\quad \tau=1,\quad
 E=\lambda_E P_i+(1-\lambda_E)P_j+r.\] The residual is orthogonal to both prototypes, so it meets the orthogonality requirement in Eq. (8). Equations (7) and (10) give \[\lambda_{\rm true}=\sigma(5\lambda_E-1),
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\rm CSM}=\sigma\bigl(s(\lambda_E-\tfrac12)\bigr)
 \quad\text{from Eq.~(11)}.
 \tag{2.1}\] At \(\lambda_E=1/2\), the correct probability is \(\sigma(1.5)=0.8175744762\), while Eq. (11) gives \(1/2\) for every \(s>0\). At \(\lambda_E=1/4\) and \(s=4\), the two values are \(0.5621765009\) and \(0.2689414214\). More generally, every \(\lambda_E\in(0.2,0.5)\) reverses the preferred class for any positive \(s\). The failure therefore extends beyond an isolated midpoint.

Under the mean-prototype convention used in the source, a probabilistic realization is available: take class-conditional Gaussians with these means and common identity covariance, and prior odds \(e^{3/2}\). Bayes’ log odds are \(E^\top(P_i-P_j)\), because the prior log odds cancel half the norm difference. Equation (7) is then an exact posterior, while the norm-removal rule changes it.

Alternative readings and boundary of the objection. Appendix A.1 explicitly postulates Eq. (15), equivalent to Eq. (7). If this is intended as an additional probability-calibration assumption, the first counterexample does not refute that assumption-conditioned identity; it shows why classification optimality alone cannot justify it. Similarly, equal prototype norms plus a common squared inter-prototype distance \(d^2\), with \(s=d^2/\tau\), make Eq. (11) agree with Eq. (10). The second example does not satisfy equal norms and is not presented as a counterexample under that extra restriction. It tests the advertised removal of unequal-norm effects in the general setting that motivates the method.

The norm-invariant rule may be a useful heuristic for imperfect CLIP features. Reported downstream gains do not establish that its labels equal true conditional probabilities, and the counterexamples do not disprove those gains. The precise correction is to state the extra assumptions or describe the labels as model-derived targets, retaining the norm/scale terms when the assumed posterior model is used. Independent probability validation is needed for the stronger ground-truth claim. This limits a central theoretical/evidentiary justification while leaving practical augmentation benefits open.

Problem 2 (Major: the CE-bias proposition fails at an allowed constraint value).  Claim, assumptions, and location. Proposition 3.2, p. 5, asserts \(\beta<0\) for every \(\delta<\|q_1-q_2\|_2^2\), under Definition 3.1’s \(\delta\geq0\) and \[\min_{p_1,p_2\in\Delta^K}
 L_{\rm CE}(p_1,q_1)+L_{\rm CE}(p_2,q_2)
 \quad\text{subject to }\|p_1-p_2\|_2^2\leq\delta.\] It supplies the claimed sharp-label preference in the core CE-versus-L2 comparison. Use \(K=2\), \(q_1=(9/10,1/10)\), \(q_2=(7/10,3/10)\), and \(\delta=0\). These meet \(0.5\leq\lambda_2<\lambda_1<1\) and \(0<\|q_1-q_2\|^2=2/25\).

Exact calculation. Feasibility forces \(p_1=p_2=p\). The objective is \[-\frac85\log p_s-\frac25\log(1-p_s),\] whose unique minimum is \(p_s=4/5\). Both squared errors equal \(1/50\), so \[\beta(p,p)=\|p-q_1\|^2-\|p-q_2\|^2=0,\] contradicting strict negativity. This is the exactly indistinguishable case that the proximity model explicitly allows.

Proof diagnosis and checked repair. Lemma A.1, p. 17, makes a strict-negative conclusion from a factor proportional to \(-\epsilon\), while allowing \(\epsilon=0\). With \(S=\alpha_1+\alpha_2\), \(d=\alpha_1-\alpha_2\), and \[(D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4)=(2-S-\epsilon,S+\epsilon,2-S+\epsilon,S-\epsilon),\] the correctly expanded expression is \[T_{\rm CE}
 =\frac{-8\epsilon(S-1)(d-\epsilon)}{D_1D_2D_3D_4}.
 \tag{2.2}\] It vanishes at zero and is negative for \(0<\epsilon<d\). The last line of Eq. (43), p. 16, also prints two denominators with \(1-S\) instead of \(2-S\); Eq. (44) uses the correct 2. For \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\epsilon)=(0.9,0.7,0.1)\), the actual \(h\) in Eq. (39) equals \(-32/255\), while the printed last line of Eq. (43) equals \(-6208/1785\). Both defects were checked against the rendered original.

Counterevidence, impact, and correction scope. Restricting the strict-negative branch to \(0<\delta<\|q_1-q_2\|^2\), and including equality at \(\delta=0\), repairs this boundary claim. Positive-\(\delta\) checks support the intended sharper-label tendency, and Proposition 3.4 remains valid as shown below. The finding does not establish that CE is balanced for every \(\delta\), that L2 lacks practical advantages, or that the ablation scores are wrong. Its Major severity concerns the demonstrated invalid derivation and false statement in the central loss-selection argument; the local nature of the correction does not remove that dependency.

Problem 3 (Major: the released test path includes the training pool).  Claim and exact path. Appendix B.2, p. 20, describes separate CIFAR training/validation/test data. In both released `configs/data/cifar10.yaml` and `configs/data/cifar100.yaml`, training/validation and test loaders receive the same `data.data_root`. In `calibrate/data/cifar100.py`, lines 187–198, the former partitions indices from that directory; lines 268–277 construct the test dataset from the whole same directory without a held-out sampler. The trainer’s `test()` uses this configured loader.

Decisive set argument. Assume an unchanged readable directory with unique contiguous image IDs and the supplied `valid_size=0.1`, with no unrecorded test-path override. Let its image identities be \(S\), the training sampler pool \(T\), validation pool \(V\), and test pool \(U\). Then \[T\mathbin{\dot\cup}V=S,\qquad U=S,\qquad T\cap U=T,\quad V\cap U=V.\] For \(50{,}000\) images this is a \(45{,}000\)-image training pool, \(5{,}000\)-image validation pool, and \(50{,}000\)-image test pool. Thus 90% of test identities belong to the training pool and all validation identities are reused in testing. Shuffling changes membership selection but not these inclusions. Dropping a partial training batch does not make the resulting test set independent; different input normalizations do not create new image identities.

Alternatives and bounded impact. This is a static result about the released path, not proof that the proceedings tables used overlapping data. A historical override of the test directory could avoid it. CIFAR-100 additionally encounters a hardcoded ten-class target size, so no completed CIFAR-100 execution is inferred; the same overlap argument applies to the supplied ten-class configuration when its prerequisites are supplied. No author training was run.

Core dependency and correction. Independent test evaluation is essential to the central accuracy/calibration comparison. Following the displayed path does not measure that quantity, giving a Major validity defect in the released evaluation route. A separate test directory or immutable disjoint identity lists, with verified intersections, repairs the route. Historical split IDs and configurations are required to determine whether any published table needs recomputation; no score decrease is asserted without them.

# Local implementation and definition discrepancies

Problem 4 (Minor: the combined loss has an unspecified relative normalization).  Location and calculation. Equation (13), p. 5, defines \(L_2=K^{-1}\|p-q\|^2\), and Eq. (14) sums CE and this loss. Released `calibrate/losses/csm.py`, lines 18–19 and 35–44, instead averages the sum of squared class errors over augmented examples and adds mean CE. For \(B\) originals and \(gB\) augmentations, the code uses \[\frac1B\sum_i L_{{\rm CE},i}
 +\frac1{gB}\sum_a\|p_a-q_a\|^2 .\] Relative to a literal minibatch-sum version of Eq. (14), the L2-to-CE coefficient differs by \(K/g\), not a common multiplicative scale. The supplied loader returns one endpoint plus one interior augmentation, so \(g=2\): factors 5 and 50 arise for \(K=10\) and 100, respectively. The latter is an algebraic comparison, not a claim that the unmodified 100-class path runs.

Why the distinction can matter. For one hard label of class 1 and two identical soft labels \((0.6,0.4,0,\ldots)\), suppose all share \(p=(t,1-t,0,\ldots)\). With \(K=10\), the literal paper objective is \(-\log t+0.4(t-0.6)^2\), minimized at \(t=1\) on the closed simplex. The source objective is \(-\log t+2(t-0.6)^2\), minimized at \[t=(0.6+\sqrt{1.36})/2=0.8830951895.\] These are probability-space calculations, not trained-network measurements.

Alternative interpretation and scope. An intended expectation/mean convention or an explicit loss weight could reconcile the implementation, but the exact coefficient must be stated. For a literal full-dataset sum with \(N\) originals and \(A\) augmentations, the analogous relative factor is \(KN/A\). The standalone two-point L2 balance theorem is invariant under positive scaling and is not refuted. This is a local specification/reproducibility discrepancy; its effect on historical comparisons is unresolved. Correct the objective’s reductions and coefficient, and record the actual training weight.

Problem 5 (Minor: metric formulas and adaptive-bin coverage need correction).  Locations. Section 3.1, p. 3, Eq. (2), Appendix B.3, pp. 20–21, and `calibrate/evaluation/metrics.py` do not fully agree.

ECE normalization and counterevidence. Eq. (2) weights raw bin counts by \(1/M\), where \(M\) is the number of bins, instead of \(1/N\). Thirty wrong predictions of confidence \(0.8\), with 15 bins, give 1.6 under that formula and 0.8 under normalized ECE. Appendix Eq. (65) and the source ECE implementation use the correct sample normalization. Thus this printed definition error does not establish that the ECE tables were computed incorrectly.

Adaptive-bin omission. The source’s `AdaptiveECELoss`, lines 216–237, starts its quantile boundaries at the minimum confidence but tests \(\texttt{lower}<c\leq\texttt{upper}\) for every bin. Every minimum-confidence observation is excluded. With thirty identical confidences \(c=0.8\) and all predictions wrong, every boundary is 0.8, all bin predicates are false, and the implemented accumulation is zero. Correct bin coverage gives a discrepancy of 0.8. This conclusion follows from the predicates without calling the author function.

If the omitted minimum group has size \(m_{\min}\), correcting only that omission with fixed boundaries changes the metric by at most \(m_{\min}/N\). With a unique minimum among 5,000 cases, the bound is 0.02 percentage points; with ties the effect can be larger. This is not a bound on arbitrary alternative rebinning methods. Raw logits are needed to quantify the actual table impact.

OE/UE convention and repair. The source also multiplies each OE/UE contribution by bin confidence, unlike Eqs. (66)–(67). A bin with confidence 0.8 and accuracy 0.5 contributes 0.24 under the source convention and 0.30 under Eq. (66). Either convention can be named, but they are distinct metrics. Specify the definitions consistently and assign every sample exactly once, including tied minima. These local discrepancies do not by themselves demonstrate a changed historical method ranking.

# Verified results and remaining evidence limits

L2 balance: scoped No Issue Found. Let \(d=q_1-q_2\), \(m=(q_1+q_2)/2\), and \(t=\min(1,\sqrt{\delta}/\|d\|)\). Direct projection of the pair onto the distance constraint gives \[p_1=m+\tfrac12td,\qquad p_2=m-\tfrac12td,\qquad
 \|p_1-q_1\|^2=\|p_2-q_2\|^2
 =\tfrac14(1-t)^2\|d\|^2.\] Both outputs lie on the segment joining the labels and hence in the simplex. This independently verifies Proposition 3.4’s balance conclusion, including \(\delta=0\). It does not imply calibrated predictions on a new distribution. A separate numerical minimization of the binary Focal objective at \(\gamma=1\), over 60 specified label/constraint cases, found positive \(\beta\) throughout; this finite check is not a universal independent proof of Proposition 3.3.

One-hot labels and uncertainty. One-hot observations need not imply a conditionally deterministic label. For \(Y\mid X=x\sim{\rm Bernoulli}(\eta)\), expected CE has derivative \((p-\eta)/(p(1-p))\) and is minimized at \(p=\eta\). Thus finite-data overfitting or missing soft annotations should not be conflated with an information-theoretic impossibility of learning uncertainty from one-hot observations. This qualification preserves the possible practical value of additional synthetic supervision.

Release completeness. The annotation utility defines but never invokes `main`; its helper reads `model`, `preprocess`, and `device` as globals although they are local to `main`. In addition, line 84 reuses the last filename from the image loop for every feature coefficient, leaving one key per intended group. These are static obstacles to the advertised command producing complete annotations; they do not establish corruption of the historical labels. The loader hardcodes ten-class targets despite the 100-class configuration. The four-process generation command and the sampler’s single-device assumptions also require reconciliation before reproducing generation. No distributed failure or historical loss of samples is inferred from an unexecuted run.

Experimental accounting. The main CIFAR-10/100 augmentation counts \(159{,}840/158{,}400\) match the seed counts in the run script. Appendix B instead gives \(550\cdot45\cdot8=198{,}000\) for CIFAR-10 and describes five sets per CIFAR-100 pair, which yields \(5\cdot4950\cdot8=198{,}000\), not its printed \(198{,}400\). Different versions or subsets could explain part of this; an immutable sample ledger is needed. Table 10 also has CIFAR100-LT, \(\rho=10\), ACC 62.35 for CSM versus 63.83 for RankMixup, so an across-all-settings accuracy-superiority claim is too broad, while the displayed ECE advantage survives.

The visual examples show semantic transitions, but appearance alone is not an oracle for numerical class probabilities. Table 6 supports a benefit from the reported reannotation/loss combination within its measured setup; it cannot establish exact posterior truth. The EQ-DATA comparison usefully addresses training exposure, but generation/annotation cost and the experiment-time settings remain separate quantities. Historical logits, seeds, full configurations, model checkpoints, and the ImageNet evaluation implementation are needed to resolve remaining reproducibility and uncertainty questions.

# Assessment

The supported outcome is Major, based independently on the core posterior argument, the false CE-bias statement/derivation, and the conditional but fully specified released evaluation path. Local loss and metric discrepancies are reported separately with bounded impact; unresolved historical questions do not increase their supported severity.
