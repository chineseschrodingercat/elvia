Abstract: A stationary conditional-softmax counterexample refutes the printed directional-improvement theorem, and a norm-ratio inversion identifies a repair to its sufficient condition. A separate finite example exposes a support and fallback gap in the claimed DPO connection. Selected continual-editing averages materially exaggerate local gains. The locality argument remains defensible as a first-order result under isotropy. These findings do not establish failure of the empirical method as a whole; adjudication remains pending.

Title: Technical Verification: Mitigating Heterogeneous Token Overfitting in LLM Knowledge Editing

# Paper identity and reviewed version

This report concerns Mitigating Heterogeneous Token Overfitting in LLM Knowledge Editing, by Tianci Liu et al., ICML 2025, PMLR volume 267; identifier `I25-43678`, OpenReview `vOu5K93z4f`. The reviewed object is the frozen published proceedings PDF, retrieved on 25 October 2026. Page references below are one-based PDF pages, which coincide with the internal page numbers. They are not the continuous PMLR volume pagination.

# Scope, contribution and assumptions

OVERTONE replaces a hard token target with a mixture of that target and a filtered current prediction, with a fallback to the hard target if the mixture changes the most probable token. A lower loss threshold suppresses updates on already fitted tokens. The paper studies four editing methods and several benchmarks. Main Sections 1–5 and Appendices A–D were read, including the proofs, implementation choices and continual-editing tables. The frozen source search did not locate an author implementation; this is a bounded availability result, not a claim that no implementation exists. No author code or LLM training was executed.

The method is a plausible regularizer and the component ablations are useful. The reduction to ordinary cross-entropy at \(\epsilon=0,\lambda=1\) is sound. Its additional per-token filtering cost is linear in vocabulary size, apart from the model forward/backward pass. These observations do not imply the stronger directional or preference-optimization guarantees. The following calculations distinguish a false theorem under its listed assumptions from limits on an empirical explanation.

# A realizable counterexample to the directional theorem

Problem 1 (Assumption A.7 does not imply Theorem A.10).  The strict directional improvement claimed in Theorem A.10 fails even when the pretrained loss is stationary and the filtered target is locally constant.

Use the paper’s notation \(a=\nabla\ell_{\rm CE}(z^{\rm new})\), \(b=-\nabla\ell_{\rm CE}(z^{\rm old})\), and \(c=\nabla\operatorname{CE}(\pi_{\rm flt},\pi_\theta)\). Set \[a=c=(1,0),\qquad b=(-1,1/10),\qquad a+b=(0,1/10).\] Assumption A.6 makes \(G=(a+b)/N\). Then \(\cos(a,G)=0\), whereas Assumption A.7 only requires \[\cos(b,c)>1-\frac{\|b\|^2}{8\|a+b\|^2}
                  (1-\cos(a,a+b))^2=-11.625.\] The actual cosine is \(-1/\sqrt{1.01}\simeq-.995037\), so the assumption holds. Nevertheless \(\lambda a+(1-\lambda)c=a\) for every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\). Its cosine remains zero: no strict improvement exists. The negative threshold also shows why the verbal description of A.7 as requiring close directions is not generally valid.

This is realizable by a small conditional softmax model, rather than an unattached vector construction. At \(\theta=0\), choose new-context probabilities \(p=(.5,.3,.2)\), preferred token 0, and logits \(\log p+J\theta\), where \[J=\begin{pmatrix}-2&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.\] With the published filter parameter \(n=.5\), only token 0 survives, giving \(q=\pi_{\rm flt}=(1,0,0)\). Thus \(J^T(p-e_0)=a=c\), and every mixture passes the most-probable-token check. For the old context use uniform probabilities and logit Jacobian \[J_o=\begin{pmatrix}-1.5&.15\\0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad -J_o^T((1/3,1/3,1/3)-e_0)=b.\] A pretraining set with one old-context example of each label and new-context label counts \((5,3,2)\) has zero gradient. Its summed-loss Hessian eigenvalues are approximately \(.0130413\) and \(11.502\), so this is a strict local minimum. Distinct old/new teacher-forced contexts can be formed by different first tokens after the same input, with fixed first-token probabilities \((.6,.3,.1)\) independent of \(\theta\); their filters are defined and their gradients vanish. The two-token sequences therefore realize the context change in the paper’s setup. The independent script checks stationarity, curvature, filtering and a finite-difference CE gradient \((1.00000000008,0)\). This finite model addresses the stated gradient assumptions, not the measured behavior of a particular LLM.

The error is visible on page 17. With \(A=\|a+b\|\), \(B=\|b\|\), \(k=\cos(a,a+b)\) and \(d=\|c/\|c\|-b/\|b\|\|\), solving \((A-Bd)/(A+Bd)>k\) gives \(d<(A/B)(1-k)/(1+k)\) when \(k>-1\). The proof instead uses \(B/A\). A sufficient repaired assumption, for nonzero vectors and a locally fixed target, is \[\cos(b,c)>1-\frac{\|a+b\|^2}{8\|b\|^2}
                       (1-\cos(a,a+b))^2.\] Indeed it gives \(r=Bd/A<(1-k)/2\leq1\), hence a positive numerator and cosine at least \((1-r)/(1+r)\geq1-2r>k\), at the proof’s chosen \(\lambda=\|c\|/(B+\|c\|)\). The repaired condition rejects the counterexample: its threshold is \(.998762\). It is substantially stronger in this example and has not been verified empirically. The original theorem is Major; changing the ratio repairs this sufficient vector argument when the selected mixture is actually used, not the full fallback algorithm or its assumptions.

# Target dependence and the preference interpretation

Problem 2 (The DPO connection does not cover the stated algorithm’s support and fallback).  Theorem A.16 and Equation (17) use a filtered reference probability in a logarithmic ratio, although Algorithm 1 deliberately assigns exact zeros and Equation (3) can fall back to a label outside that support.

For current probabilities \((.8,.15,.05)\), \(n=.5\) retains only the first token. If the desired token is the third and \(\lambda=.1\), the proposed mixture is \((.9,0,.1)\) and fails the argmax check. The actual target becomes \(e_2\), with finite loss \(-\log(.05)=2.995732\) at \(\epsilon=0\). But the reference probability of the preferred token in Equation (17) is zero, so its log-ratio is undefined or infinite. The independent calculation reproduces this exact algorithmic branch. The theorem’s unconditional interpretation therefore needs support restrictions and explicit treatment of fallback cases. This is a Major gap in the theoretical connection, not a counterexample to finite OVERTONE training loss.

The derivation also treats the target entropy and reference as constants while Algorithm 1 recomputes them from the current logits. Equation (6)’s displayed KL expansion has the wrong entropy sign: \(D_{\rm KL}(q\|p)=\operatorname{CE}(q,p)-H(q)\). Omitting that entropy in a gradient calculation requires a detached, fixed target for the current update. The clipping constant can likewise be dropped for gradients only within a fixed active set. These conventions, and the dependence of the argmax fallback on \(\lambda\), should be explicit. The counterexample above does not rely on this ambiguity because its target is locally a fixed point mass.

On pages 19–20 the proposed preference probability is a clipped exponential. At zero margin it assigns \(1/e\) to each direction, totaling \(2/e\simeq.73576\), rather than complementary probabilities for an exhaustive binary comparison. A tie or abstention model could account for leftover mass, but none is specified in that derivation. A valid repair must define the pairwise probability model, positive reference support, update-time detachment and fallback objective; merely calling the algebra DPO is insufficient. Those repairs have not been implemented or validated here. Finally, equal logits have standard deviation zero, so the literal filter removes all tokens. A deterministic keep-at-least-one rule is a small necessary boundary repair.

# What the locality argument does establish

The absolute values in Theorem A.15 are essential and are present in the rendered PDF. Under the stated isotropic gradient-direction assumption, with radius independent of direction, \(\mathbb E|v^TW|=C\|v\|\). Consequently, if \(\|c\|<\|a\|\) and \(\lambda<1\), the triangle inequality proves a smaller expected absolute first-order probability perturbation for the mixture. A 20,000-angle deterministic quadrature on the unit circle, with \(a=(2,0)\), \(c=(0,.5)\) and \(\lambda=.1\), gives respectively \(1.273240\), \(.318310\) and \(.313499\) for the two directions and their mixture. The ratio for \(c\) versus \(a\) is exactly \(.25\) to numerical precision, agreeing with the norm ratio.

This supports the local linearized calculation. It does not establish a finite-step inequality without a sufficiently small learning rate and control of higher-order terms; \(\lambda=1\) gives equality. Nor does lack of prior knowledge about gradient directions establish isotropy in an LLM. The empirical locality gains remain relevant evidence, but are distinct from verifying those distributional assumptions.

# Continual-editing aggregates and empirical scope

Problem 3 (Incorrect table averages inflate some continual-editing gains).  Several displayed averages in Tables 4–5 cannot be obtained from their component scores. Their corrections materially change individual claimed improvements.

For \(T=100\), Table 4’s LLaMA 2 MELO WikiBio row gives reliability \(48.88\) and locality \(97.61\), but average \(48.88\). The arithmetic mean is \(73.245\). Its OVERTONE row averages \(74.805\), so the gain is \(1.56\) percentage points, rather than the displayed \(25.93\). In Table 5, LLaMA 3 MELO on Wikicounterfact lists \((22.2,22.9,97.85)\) and average \(22.55\); the correct mean is \(47.65\). The corresponding OVERTONE mean is \(47.7333\), making the gain approximately \(.0833\) rather than \(25.18\) points. Table 5’s FT-M plus OVERTONE WikiBio row similarly averages \((94.23+85.83)/2=90.03\), not \(94.23\).

These are directly checked numerical discrepancies, also verified in the rendered source tables. They are Major for those local improvement magnitudes; they do not erase the many larger LoRA gains elsewhere. Recomputing every aggregate from saved per-example outputs is needed to establish the table as a whole. This review corrects the named cells and does not certify every other entry.

Claims of preserving reliability or uniformly improving portability also need qualifications already visible in Table 1. LLaMA 2 LoRA on WikiBio changes reliability from \(100\) to \(97.68\), while locality improves from \(48.84\) to \(68.45\). LLaMA 3 WISE portability changes from \(49.27\) to \(47.54\), despite a better overall average. These are tradeoffs, not universal dominance. Appendix B additionally shrinks WISE’s activation threshold by a factor \(.6\) under OVERTONE. A matched-threshold ablation is needed before attributing the whole WISE difference to the loss alone.

Figure 1’s rising portability loss and Figure 2’s token-wise heterogeneity motivate the method. The proposed underfitting-degree statistic compares an edited target’s confidence with a pre-edit greedy confidence; a negative value by itself is not a held-out generalization error or a causal identification of heterogeneous overfitting. The paper itself proposes repeated runs and acknowledges unresolved knowledge conflicts. These uncertainties should remain in the empirical interpretation rather than being filled by the invalid directional theorem.

# Conclusion

The contribution remains a useful empirical editing regularizer with informative ablations. The direction guarantee is false under its printed assumptions; the preference connection omits operative algorithm branches; and selected continual-editing averages substantially exaggerate local gains. The locality result has a defensible first-order interpretation. Independent finite calculations and source-page inspection establish the specific findings and the limited vector repair. Training replication, gradient-assumption measurements, implementation detachment, raw-score verification, later corrections and author adoption remain unverified. Scientific adjudication is pending.
