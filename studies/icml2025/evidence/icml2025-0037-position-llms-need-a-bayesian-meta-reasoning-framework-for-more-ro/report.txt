# Problem 1: a conditional posterior is replaced by a marginal posterior

Source claim and exact location. At the bottom of p. 3, Section 3.1, under ’Updating knowledge priors at the meta-level’, the paper labels the following quantity as the posterior over knowledge priors. The original rendered page was visually checked. [E1]

\[p(\theta_I,\theta_E\mid O,F)\ \propto\ \left[\int p(O\mid f)\,p(f\mid\theta_I,\theta_E)\,df\right]p(\theta_I)p(\theta_E).\] Suspect step. Integrating over f removes the realized strategy from the evidence. Under the stated Markov simplification, the right side is an unnormalized p(theta|O), not p(theta|O,F). Its dependence on theta cannot generally be repaired by a scalar normalizing constant. This is a target-distribution mismatch.

Dependency. This is the second level of the paper’s central two-level Bayesian learning scheme, immediately following Eq. (2) and Table 1. Section 3.2 uses meta-reflection to update knowledge and strategies, and Section 4.4 describes refining F and then optimizing the meta-parameters. Consequently the incorrect expression affects the proposed mechanism for consolidating knowledge across tasks. It is not a peripheral numerical example or a bibliography defect.

# Problem 1 continued: a complete positive-probability counterexample

All five variables I=theta_I, E=theta_E, F, A and O are binary. I and E are independent Bernoulli(1/2). The remaining conditional tables are below; probabilities of value zero are the complements. E is a genuine random variable but its effect on F is set to zero.

Conditional probability | Parent value 0 | Parent value 1

P(F=1 | I,E), parent I | 1/10 | 9/10

P(A=1 | F), parent F | 1/10 | 9/10

P(O=1 | A), parent A | 1/8 | 7/8

\[p(i,e,f,a,o)=p(i)p(e)p(f\mid i,e)p(a\mid f)p(o\mid a).\] Assumption check. Each conditional table sums to one. Exact enumeration gives total joint mass 1, all 32 joint assignments strictly positive, and minimum mass 1/3200. No zero-likelihood event, undefined conditioning, dependent prior, invalid strategy value or negative weight is used. The event O=1,F=1 has probability 2/5. The independent reviewer script uses Python Fraction arithmetic and does not import or execute author code. [E4]

\[\Pr(O=1\mid F=0)=\frac9{10}\frac18+\frac1{10}\frac78=\frac15,\qquad \Pr(O=1\mid F=1)=\frac45.\] Correct result when F=1 and O=1 are given. Conditional independence makes the O likelihood common to both values of I. The posterior probability of I=1 is therefore 9/10. Equivalently, its numerator is (1/2)(9/10)(4/5)=9/25 and the event probability is 2/5.

\[\Pr(I=1\mid O=1,F=1)=\frac{9/25}{2/5}=\frac9{10}=0.90.\] Printed-right-side result. Marginalizing F yields likelihood 13/50 for I=0 and 37/50 for I=1. Multiplying each by its 1/2 prior and normalizing gives 37/50=0.74 for I=1. The exact joint meta-parameter probability is also different: the correct P(I=1,E=1|O=1,F=1)=9/20=0.45, whereas the printed normalized right side gives 37/100=0.37.

\[\frac{(1/2)(37/50)}{(1/2)(13/50+37/50)}=\frac{37}{50}\ne\frac9{10}.\] The disagreement survives normalization: the correct odds for I=1 versus I=0 are 9, while the printed odds are 37/13. When the conditioned strategy is changed to F=0, the correct posterior is 0.10, but the integrated expression still gives 0.74. Thus the formula loses information that its left side explicitly conditions on. Analytic Bayes calculations and exact finite enumeration agree.

# Alternative interpretations, scoped corrections, and impact

Strongest alternative reading. The left side may be mislabeled and the authors may intend the marginal posterior p(theta|O). Under the simplifying conditional-independence assumptions, that reading makes the integral mathematically appropriate. It changes the inferential target and must be stated explicitly. Table 1 samples F from a strategy prior and Eq. (2) updates its posterior, so neither passage makes the conditioned F on the left side a harmless dummy integration variable.

If F is instead a controlled intervention, or a distribution over strategies rather than an observed latent value, the update must specify that interpretation and the corresponding probability measure. Those are reasonable design choices, but the paper does not supply such a reinterpretation of the displayed conditional posterior. It is not valid to silently use them to equate two demonstrably different distributions.

Two precise mathematical repairs are available; they represent different algorithms. For a strategy fixed as evidence, retain F=f and remove the integral. For an uncertain strategy marginalized out, remove F from the left-side conditioning. The general likelihood may depend on theta; retain that dependence when the graph permits it.

\[p(\theta\mid O,F=f)\propto p(O\mid f,\theta)\,p(f\mid\theta)\,p(\theta).\] \[p(\theta\mid O)\propto p(\theta)\int p(O\mid f,\theta)\,p(f\mid\theta)\,df.\] Both identities are analytically supported and distinguish the counterexample’s 0.90 and 0.74 results. They do not establish which learning rule the authors intend, how repeated evidence should be managed, how rewards become calibrated likelihoods, or that an eventual implementation improves LLM performance. Those parts of a repaired framework are unverified.
