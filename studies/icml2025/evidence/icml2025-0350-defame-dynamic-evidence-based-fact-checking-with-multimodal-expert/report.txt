Abstract: DEFAME combines multimodal evidence retrieval with iterative planning and produces inspectable fact-checking reports. The comparisons with its own GPT-4o baselines are promising. However, a principal literature comparison substitutes a baseline F-score for accuracy, and the published implementation does not enforce its claimed temporal restriction for reverse image search. Its accuracy calculation also excludes refusals and can finalize incomplete runs without an explicit full-population score. These are substantive defects in the comparison and reproduction contracts, although they do not establish that historical runs used contaminated evidence or produced inflated accuracy scores. Additional configuration, dataset-count, and human-study details need reconciliation. All 35 physical pages, applicable appendices, relevant original figures, and the available central implementation paths were examined. No author program was executed. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

Title: Technical Verification of DEFAME: Dynamic Evidence-based FAct-checking with Multimodal Experts

# Identity, scope, and checks that survive

This report concerns Tobias Braun, Mark Rothermel, Marcus Rohrbach, and Anna Rohrbach, ICML 2025, I25-43719 (0350) [paper]. The source is the frozen proceedings PDF.

All 35 physical pages, including Appendices A–O, were read. The acquired source packet’s 218 files passed their frozen hashes. Code claims below concern only the released `icml` branch at commit

.

That release is presented as the ICML version; nevertheless, the actual historical run commit, prediction files, retrieved pages, and rating data were not established. Current code defects are not evidence that those defects affected reported experiments.

The main dynamic procedure does implement repeated planning, execution, evidence summarization, development, and judgment, with continuation on NEI and a bounded iteration count. The GPT formatter includes both image references and image data. The MOCHEG loader explicitly deduplicates claim identifiers and removes missing rulings, consistent with the declared restricted evaluation population. The text-search backend carries a date restriction into its query. These positive checks constrain the concerns below.

Appendix J’s identity is valid for a single-label classifier evaluated on the same complete population: if \(N\) decisions contain \(C\) correct predictions, then aggregated \(TP=C\) and \(FP=FN=N-C\), so \[F_{1,\mathrm{micro}}=\frac{2C}{2C+2(N-C)}=\frac CN.\] It does not convert an arbitrary reported F-score into accuracy, or equate metrics computed on different subsets.

# Supporting checks

Problem 1 (Major: the MOCHEG literature comparison mixes metrics).  Table 3 (physical page 7) places MetaSum’s 48.6 in an accuracy column and compares it with DEFAME’s 59.2. Appendix J does not justify that substitution.

The cited MetaSumPerceiver primary paper [msp], physically inspected at pages 6–7, labels 48.6 as an F-score in Table 4. Its Table 3 separately reports 55.6 accuracy for the same named setting: both critics, text-plus-image evidence, and Llama 2. Thus the source itself distinguishes these quantities. No assumption about whether its F-score is macro, weighted, or another aggregation is needed to identify the unsupported relabeling.

The subtraction \(59.2-48.6=10.6\) percentage points therefore does not establish an accuracy improvement. Subtracting the two published accuracy values instead gives \(3.6\) points, but this is not a repaired matched-population estimate: DEFAME evaluates 1,689 retained claims, and equivalent baseline filtering and evidence conditions have not been demonstrated. A source-table error is another possible explanation, but then the comparator is unresolved rather than established by the micro-F1 identity. Release aligned sample identifiers and recompute both methods under a common metric and evidence protocol, or label the comparison as non-comparable. This finding materially affects a principal benchmark improvement; it neither refutes DEFAME’s own baseline comparison nor establishes that its measured accuracy is wrong.

Problem 2 (Major: reverse image search lacks the stated temporal restriction).  Page 4 states that all web tools restrict sources to dates preceding the claim when its date is known. The pinned implementation does not enforce this for reverse image search (RIS).

The complete inspected path is

`FactChecker` \(\to\) `Actor` \(\to\) `Searcher` \(\to\) `GoogleVisionAPI`.

The first stages preserve the claim date and set `ImageQuery.end_date`. But `defame/tools/search/google_vision_api.py`, lines 28–78, sends only the image to `web_detection`, then scrapes matching pages after domain filtering. It neither reads the date constraint nor assigns publication dates to its `WebSource` objects. The inspected cache/API wrappers and `Searcher.search` remove known URLs and truncate results; they do not supply a missing date filter. An independent AST inspection confirms that this backend never accesses `end_date` or `start_date`.

This is a conditional but direct implementation counterexample: an allowed matching page newer than a known claim date, if returned by the service and successfully scraped, is admitted by the inspected path. In contrast, `query_serper.py` does send a date-limited text/image-search query. Therefore the finding is specific to RIS, not an assertion that all date handling is absent. Search-provider behavior or an occasional date-aware summary is not a software guarantee of exclusion. The inspected publication-date query also does not by itself archive the earlier contents of subsequently edited pages.

The defect concerns the temporal evaluation premise, a central motivation of the work. Repair RIS with recorded publication-time validation and an explicit unknown-date policy; preserve evidence snapshots and query/run timestamps. Historical impact remains unknown without the actual retrieved pages. I do not claim that any particular reported prediction used future information.

The benchmark’s stronger “leakage-free” characterization also needs qualification. Selecting fact-check articles after the backbone’s cutoff does not establish that their claims, images, or semantic contents were absent before it. Page 5 expressly allows earlier events to be revisited, and substitutes the review date when the claim date is missing. This is a reasonable post-cutoff article collection, but verified novelty or a quantified overlap audit is needed for the stronger interpretation. This logical limitation is not evidence of actual training contamination.

Problem 3 (Major: released accuracy is conditional on accepted, completed outputs).  The evaluation code’s denominator differs from ordinary accuracy over all selected benchmark claims when a refusal or worker failure occurs.

In `defame/eval/evaluate.py`, `compute_metrics` counts refusals and returns \[\widehat A_{\rm release}=\frac{C}{M-R},\] where \(M\) is the number of saved predictions and \(R\) the number marked `REFUSED_TO_ANSWER`. `compute_accuracy` uses the same exclusion. This path is reachable: `Judge._generate_verdict` returns that label when verdict extraction fails, and the logger saves the enum name used by the metric. Separately, a worker exception sends an error message without a prediction; the main loop can terminate after all workers finish and finalize the saved CSV. It does not enforce one terminal outcome for every requested claim before reporting this score.

An independent ten-claim example with eight correct decisions, one wrong decision, and one refusal yields \(8/9=88.89\%\) under the release formula, compared with \(8/10=80\%\) over all attempts. This is an exact denominator effect, not a reconstruction of the paper’s runs. Selective accuracy is legitimate if explicitly named and accompanied by coverage and a common abstention policy. Here it must not silently stand in for the ordinary accuracy and micro-F1 population discussed in the paper. Save a complete claim ledger, distinguish refusal from operational failure, report \(C/N\) and coverage alongside selective accuracy, and make incomplete runs visibly incomplete. If all historical runs had full coverage and no refusals, their accuracy values would be unaffected; that remains a plausible alternative requiring the run artifacts.

The original plots reveal small but concrete population discrepancies worth reconciling independently of the code. Figure 8(f)’s MOCHEG matrix totals 1,688 rather than the declared 1,689; its NEI row totals 521 rather than 522. Figure 8(i)’s VERITE matrix totals 1,000 rather than 1,001. These do not prove that refusal exclusion caused either omission. In Figure 4, all three ClaimReview matrices sum to 300, but their supported/refuted/misleading/NEI row totals are \((89,136,55,20)\), whereas page 5 gives \((89,129,61,21)\). A dataset revision could explain this, and should be versioned. By contrast, the actual AVERITEC diagonal is \(84+4+259+6=353\), giving \(70.6\%\), compatible with Table 3’s \(70.5\pm0.6\); an apparent discrepancy from text extraction was rejected after inspecting the image.

# Method specification and inferential qualifications

Problem 4 (Minor: the released ablation and model configurations disagree with the description).  Several explicit release settings do not reproduce the named experiment without clarification or repair.

Page 8 defines “w/o Planning” as a fixed schedule executing each tool exactly once without the planner. The released `no_planning.yaml` files instead select `summary/static`, whose `apply_to` calls the planner once and executes its chosen actions. The alternative `summary/all_actions` still calls the planner and can repeat while the verdict is NEI. Neither inspected implementation is the stated fixed schedule. The ClaimReview configuration also names the unregistered benchmark `claimreview2024q`. Moreover, MOCHEG’s main configuration selects `test`, whereas its no-planning and all-actions configurations select `dev`; the loader actually uses this string in the dataset path. These are present-release reproduction discrepancies, not proof that the historical ablation used those configurations.

The geolocator defaults to `geolocal/StreetCLIP`, with image–country-text scoring, whereas page 4 and Figure 2 name GeoCLIP. The inspected main configurations leave that default in place. Appendix H (page 21) also says MOCHEG has no extra judgment rules and assigns the warning against argument from ignorance to AVERITEC. In the release, MOCHEG carries that warning; AVERITEC instead permits absence-of-expected-evidence reasoning, and ClaimReview adds a rule always suggesting geolocation. Release the exact run-specific model, prompts, split identifiers, and ablation schedules. This finding is conservatively Minor because the observed discrepancies establish an inadequate released specification, while their relation to the historical experiments is unverified.

Problem 5 (Minor: the human-study significance needs a stated analysis unit).  The reported \(p\approx9.1\times10^{-9}\) does not come with enough design information to assess generalization across claims and raters.

Pages 9 and 25 report 185 ratings over 36 claims, at least five ratings per claim, split into 154 DEFAME and 31 CoT ratings, and a Mann–Whitney test. Repeated ratings of the same claim/report share an item; the number of raters, assignment, pairing, and repeated-rater structure are not supplied. A pooled independent-observation analysis is not automatically a valid test of an effect over new claims. For illustration only, an exchangeable cluster of five ratings with within-claim correlation \(\rho\) has variance inflation factor \(1+4\rho\); the actual correlation is unknown.

Provide anonymized claim/report/rater identifiers, group assignment, and the exact tested units. Use an item-aware paired/permutation analysis or a suitable crossed/clustered model for the intended population. A test on already aggregated independent units could resolve this concern, but the paper does not establish that this was done. The descriptive completeness advantage can survive such a correction; the present record does not establish that the reported \(p\) is numerically false. The release’s study helper selects successful cases by default and targets a different sample layout; this is not enough to allege that the published 36-claim study used that selection.

Two further wording corrections are warranted. Appendix M says the one-shot variant improves all metrics even against fine-tuned systems, but Table 8 gives BLEU \(7.31<11.40\) and BERTScore \(87.42<88.10\), although its ROUGE-L is larger. Improvement over DEFAME’s own zero-shot row is supported; superiority on every metric over the fine-tuned row is not. Also, an updated real-world fact does not alone invalidate a time-indexed historical label. Appendix K’s proposed timestamped evaluation is therefore preferable to treating all old labels as currently wrong. The claimed individual annotation errors in Appendix I were viewed, but their full external source histories were not independently adjudicated.

# Assessment and evidence limits

Recommendation: Major revision. The metric substitution affects a principal performance comparison, and the current release lacks an important temporal safeguard and a complete-population scoring contract. Correcting these issues may preserve substantial benefits from multimodal retrieval; the evidence here does not show a collapse of the entire approach.

The review read the whole proceedings paper and relevant core code, traced queries, judgment, logging, scoring, benchmark loaders, model formatting, prompts, and ablations, and performed independent integer and denominator calculations. All Figures 1–18 were actually inspected on their physical pages, together with relevant tables; the cited comparator’s two metric tables were separately viewed. The accompanying coverage record distinguishes these checks from unperformed work. No LLM inference, search-provider call from author code, model download, benchmark rerun, raw-rating reanalysis, or historical artifact reconstruction was performed. Separate OpenReview attachments remained unavailable in the acquired packet. Utility-only and unrelated benchmark paths were not exhaustively audited. Evidence files bind calculations, line anchors, original images, and the exact final report to their hashes. This is an initial review, not independent acceptance; adjudication is pending and the human-review list is empty.
