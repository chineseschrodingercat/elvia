Complete counterexample families satisfy the stated convexity, comparator and rank requirements. The vector family even has a fixed global gradient cap. # 2. Claims, assumptions and favorable conventions

Write G_t=sum_{i<=t} g_i g_i^T, bar g_t=sum_{i<=t} g_i, and rho_{1:t}=sum of the FD-discarded eigenvalues. FTSL uses A_t=B_t B_t^T+rho_{1:t} I and x_{t+1}=-eta A_t^{-1/2} bar g_t. FTFSL implements the analogous update through a larger basis and delayed compression. Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 assume convex losses and ||x*||<=D, choose eta=D/sqrt(2), and claim O(tr(G_T^{1/2})+sqrt(rho_{1:T})). D is fixed throughout the counterexamples.

Appendix A, p. 12, explicitly defines inverse notation to mean the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse when a matrix is singular. We honor that convention. Startup singularity is therefore not alleged as an undefined-algorithm defect. The counterexamples either use an ordinary positive inverse after the first gradient or act wholly within the observed gradient span.

The matrix theorem assumes convexity, gradient rank at most r, and ||X*||_F<=D_M. It uses eta=D_M/sqrt(r), epsilon>0 and the cumulative-gradient update X_{t+1}=-eta Ltilde_t^{-1/4} bar G_t^X Rtilde_t^{-1/4}. No gradient-norm cap is included among those theorem assumptions.

The uniform interpretation of big-O is essential: with the stated parameters and assumptions fixed, its hidden multiplicative constant cannot grow with the horizon or the realized gradient/preconditioner ratio. Merely renaming an unbounded sequence-dependent factor as a constant changes the claimed data-dependent guarantee.

# 3. Complete vector counterexample to Theorems 4.1 and 4.2

Fix any d>=2, sketch size tau=1, D=1 and eta=1/sqrt(2). Let T=N+1, e1 be the first coordinate vector, and choose the following differentiable convex losses:

\[f_t(x)=-\frac{x^{(1)}}{N}\quad(1\le t\le N),\qquad f_{N+1}(x)=x^{(1)}.\] Every loss is convex and globally Lipschitz with gradient norm at most 1. The cumulative loss of every fixed x is zero, so x*=0 is an attained optimum and satisfies the comparator assumption. Nonuniqueness is permitted by the source. A smooth unique-optimum strengthening is given below.

In FTSL with tau=1, FD discards the only eigenvalue each round, so B_t=0 and rho_{1:t}=sum_{i<=t}||g_i||^2. For the first k gradients, rho_{1:k}=k/N^2 and bar g_k=-(k/N)e1. Hence A_k=(k/N^2)I and the actual next decision is x_{k+1}=eta sqrt(k)e1. These matrices are positive definite for k>=1.

In FTFSL the gradients stay in one direction, so the maintained rank remains 1<2tau and no compression occurs. Its escaped mass is zero; the preconditioner is the exact rank-one G_k. Its prescribed pseudoinverse gives exactly the same decision trajectory. Thus the example also covers the fast method without any sketch approximation error.

\[R_N=\eta\left(\sqrt N-\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sqrt k\right)\ge\frac{\eta}{3}\sqrt N.\] The inequality follows from sum_{k=0}^{N-1}sqrt(k)<=integral_0^N sqrt(u)du=(2/3)N^{3/2}. At the final horizon G_T=(1+1/N)e1 e1^T. The expression in Theorem 4.1 is exactly 2sqrt(1+1/N)<=2sqrt(2), while that in Theorem 4.2 is sqrt(1+1/N)<=sqrt(2). Actual regret diverges like sqrt(N), while each claimed controlling expression stays bounded. No horizon-independent constant can make the advertised orders hold.

The table is reviewer arithmetic for the displayed formulas, not an author-code experiment. The analytic unbounded ratio is the proof; a finite ratio exceeding one alone would not refute an unspecified big-O constant.

The failure is not restricted to a nonunique optimum. Replace only the last loss by x^(1)+sqrt(1+||x+e1||^2)-1. The total fixed-comparator objective is then sqrt(1+||x+e1||^2)-1, uniquely minimized at x*=-e1, with ||x*||=D=1. All losses remain smooth and convex, and every gradient is globally bounded by 2. The prefix trajectory is unchanged; terminal squared-gradient mass is at most 5, while regret is the preceding R_N plus a nonnegative term and still diverges. This removes both a uniqueness objection and a concern about choosing D larger than a zero comparator’s norm.

For fixed sketch sizes tau>=2, the same rank-one sequence is retained exactly, with zero discarded mass and the same pseudoinverse trajectory. Thus the mechanism is not a one-column sketch artifact. It concerns the unconstrained cumulative-gradient update and its missing stability control.

# 4. Where the proof fails

Appendix A.2, p. 13, compares the lagged preconditioner A_{t-1} with the current covariance G_t through a_t, then sets C1=max_t 1/sqrt(a_t). The publisher PDF actually prints a_t=max{rho_{1:t-1}/(||g_t||^2+rho_{1:t-1}),1} and claims a_t<=1. For nonnegative masses that maximum is 1; the required matrix comparison generally fails. Replacing max by min corrects the immediate expression but does not bound C1.

For the normalized tau=1 counterexample, before the last gradient rho_{1:N}=1/N and ||g_{N+1}||^2=1. Even after ignoring the initial zero-mass issue, the corrected ratio is a_{N+1}=1/(N+1), so C1>=sqrt(N+1). It cannot be absorbed into a uniform constant. In the no-compression fast case rho remains zero, making the same escaped-mass comparison even less informative.

There is also an initialization problem in the imported lagged-regret lemma as printed. For T=1, tau=1, D=1, eta=1/sqrt(2), take f1(x)=|x^(1)+1|, x1=0 and x*=-e1. The queried gradient is e1, A1=I and A0=0. Actual regret is 1. Lemma A.1’s right-hand side is Psi1(x*)/eta+(eta/2)||g1||^2_{A0^{-1/2}}=1/sqrt(2), because the prescribed zero pseudoinverse makes the second term zero. The displayed lemma therefore fails in this admissible instance. A positive initial regularizer requires a different statement and proof treatment.

These diagnostics explain the theorem failure, but the complete trajectory counterexample is independently decisive. Correcting the max/min symbol or an omitted transpose does not alter that trajectory and cannot repair its divergent ratio.

# 5. Matrix extension: Theorem 4.4 fails under its stated assumptions

Fix m=n=2 and tau=r=D_M=epsilon=eta=1. Let E11 denote the matrix with a single 1 in position (1,1). For N>=8 use the first N losses -X_11 and the final loss N X_11+(1/2)||X||_F^2. Their sum is exactly (1/2)||X||_F^2, so X*=0 is the unique optimum. Every gradient queried along the trajectory has rank one, satisfying Assumption 3.3. No gradient-norm bound is assumed by Theorem 4.4.

With tau=1, each left/right sketch discards its sole nonzero eigenvalue. Consequently Lhat=Rhat=0, rho^L=rho^R=S_t (the cumulative squared scalar-gradient mass), and Ltilde=Rtilde=(1+S_t)I_2. After k of the first gradients, the exact decision is X_{k+1}=k/sqrt(k+1) E11. All inverse powers are ordinary positive definite matrix functions.

\[R_N=\frac{N^2}{\sqrt{N+1}}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{k}{\sqrt{k+1}}+\frac{N^2}{2(N+1)}\ge\frac14N^{3/2}\quad(N\ge8).\] Let q=N+N/sqrt(N+1) be the final scalar gradient and S=N+q^2. The true terminal left and right covariance matrices each have eigenvalues 1+S and 1. With rho^L=rho^R=S and r=1, the factor displayed in Theorem 4.4 is:

\[B_N=\left((1+S)^{1/4}+1+S^{1/4}\right)^2\le25N,\qquad \frac{R_N}{B_N}\ge\frac{\sqrt N}{100}\longrightarrow\infty.\] A simple bound suffices: q<=2N and S<=5N^2, so the displayed factor is O(N); meanwhile k/sqrt(k+1)<=sqrt(k) and N^2/sqrt(N+1) is at least sqrt(8/9)N^{3/2} for N>=8, giving the stated regret lower bound. Reviewer calculations independently reproduce the exact recurrence. At N=512, 2,048, 8,192 and 32,768 the exact regret/factor ratios are approximately 1.853, 3.734, 7.503 and 15.045.

Page 22 identifies the same missing control explicitly: C3=[min_t sqrt(r epsilon/(||g_t||^2+r epsilon))]^{-1}. Here it grows with N. A separately imposed, horizon-independent gradient cap, or an explicit retained C3 term, would change the matrix theorem and can block this family. The source’s current convexity/rank/comparator assumptions do not supply that cap. This is grouped with the vector failure as one fundamental issue, not counted as a second unrelated discovery.

# 6. A verified restricted repair for a modified vector algorithm

A concrete repair is available under additional conditions, but it changes the original vector method. Add a fixed ridge delta>0 before taking the square root: A_t=delta I+B_t B_t^T+rho_{1:t}I, with A0=delta I, and retain x_{t+1}=-eta A_t^{-1/2} bar g_t. Assume a fixed bound ||g_t||<=G and eta>0. Let C=sqrt(1+G^2/delta). Then the following bound is valid:

\[R(T)\le\frac{D^2}{2\eta}\left(\sqrt\delta+\sqrt{\rho_{1:T}}+\operatorname{tr}G_T^{1/2}\right)+\eta C\,\operatorname{tr}G_T^{1/2}.\] Proof of scope: FD gives A_{t-1}>=delta I+G_{t-1}. For a=delta/(delta+G^2), the ridge implies A_{t-1}>=a(delta I+G_t). Positive definiteness permits inverse-square-root order, so the lagged gradient penalty is bounded by C times the current regularized penalty. Concavity of the trace square root gives sum_t g_t^T(delta I+G_t)^{-1/2}g_t<=2 tr(G_T^{1/2}).

For completeness, monotone H_t=A_t^{1/2} and the conjugate potential Phi_t(s)=(eta/2)s^T H_t^{-1}s give the lagged FTRL bound by telescoping: each linearized loss is at most Phi_{t-1}(bar g_{t-1})-Phi_t(bar g_t)+(eta/2)g_t^T H_{t-1}^{-1}g_t. Fenchel’s inequality bounds the endpoint minus comparator term by x*^T H_T x*/(2eta). Its spectral norm is at most sqrt(delta)+sqrt(rho_{1:T})+tr(G_T^{1/2}), proving the displayed repair.

This derivation was independently checked. It requires both the positive initial ridge and an explicit fixed gradient cap, and keeps their dependence visible. It does not validate the original zero-ridge update, preserve its exact scale behavior, prove a repaired matrix theorem, or establish unchanged experimental performance. Simply clipping gradients is insufficient to fix the original vector result: the first counterexample already has gradient norm at most 1.

# 7. Surviving checks, empirical scope and excluded concerns

The FD covariance sandwich and escaped-mass mechanism remain useful. The spectral compensation preserves PSD monotonicity; the rank/trace handling in the matrix comparator term, Kronecker vectorization, and the structural memory advantage are not refuted by the counterexamples. Appendix A contains local notation slips, including an omitted transpose in Algorithm 3 and a common left/right rank label; favorable symmetric formulations were used rather than treating these as separate central failures.

The advertised O(tau d) fast-update cost is amortized, with an O(tau^3) small-matrix decomposition each round and an O(tau^2 d) basis rotation after sufficiently many basis insertions. The condition tau<=O(sqrt(d)) is needed to absorb the cubic term. A compression round can be more expensive than the amortized cost. This is a complexity qualification, not a refutation of the overall acceleration mechanism.

Theorem 4.3’s mirror-descent variant has a different assumption involving D1=max_t||x_t-x*||. The present cumulative-gradient examples do not establish a false statement of that theorem under its own conditions. Nor do they demonstrate failure on the reported classification datasets or neural-network workloads.

The original experiments compare online classification on Gisette/Epsilon, ResNet training on CIFAR-10/100, and a small Transformer on WikiText-2. The source reports a learning-rate/regularizer grid, eight RTX 3090 GPUs, no momentum in the image comparison, gradient clipping at 0.5 in language modeling, and no preconditioner for the embedding layer. Those empirical settings differ from the unrestricted theorem class. Raw curves, seeds, exact selected settings and author implementation were unavailable, so runtime, statistical repeatability and train/test selection were not reproduced.

Iteration/epoch labels require clarification: CIFAR is described as 200 iterations, while the language-model description uses 40 epochs and the corresponding graph labels iterations. These could be reporting conventions; without logs they do not prove incorrect training. The empirical figures cannot establish a worst-case online regret theorem, and an invalid theorem does not itself show that the plotted comparisons are false.

Appendix B’s reduction was checked against the cited Agarwal et al. supplement and visually inspected at p. 23. Its adaptive-ratio normalization and inner gradient-query notation differ from the cited presentation. These are retained as clarification/provenance limitations, without asserting an independently proved failure of every nonconvex conversion. Any application relying on the refuted online guarantees must first use a valid replacement.
