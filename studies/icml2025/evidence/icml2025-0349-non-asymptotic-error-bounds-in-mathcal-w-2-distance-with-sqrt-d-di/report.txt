Abstract: The block-restart argument correctly converts finite-time strong error and convergence toward the invariant distribution into a uniform sampling bound. The advertised \(\sqrt d\,h\) scaling is also consistent with an exact Gaussian calculation. The published proof contains a false intermediate inequality in the projected-sampler analysis, incorrect derivatives in its example verification, and several inconsistent moment indices and constants. The missing moment range is a Major gap in the published general-SDE proof; the explicitly repaired local inequalities and example constants are Minor. These checks do not refute either Langevin application rate: the most conspicuous false inequality admits a weaker replacement of the order needed downstream. All 25 physical pages and the original numerical figure were examined. No implementation was available in the acquired packet, and no author code was run. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

Title: Technical Verification of Non-asymptotic Error Bounds in \(\mathcal W_2\)-Distance with Sqrt(d) Dimension Dependence and First Order Convergence for Langevin Monte Carlo beyond Log-Concavity

# Identity, scope, and positive checks

The paper is by Bin Yang and Xiaojie Wang, ICML 2025, I25-43718 (0349) [paper]. The source is the frozen PDF.

All 25 physical pages, including Appendices A–G, were read. The acquired publisher metadata and PDF passed their frozen hashes. Physical pages 3, 5, 8, 17, 19, 21, and 23–25 were actually viewed; page 8 contains all four panels of the original numerical figure. No author release was located in the source search recorded in the frozen manifest. That is a bounded availability statement, not a claim that no implementation exists anywhere. Separate OpenReview attachments remained unresolved after the source phase’s access barrier.

The key recurrence in Appendix D is sound in its intended homogeneous Markov setting. If a block of length \(T=mh\) gives a uniform discretization error \(A h^q\), and \(K e^{-\eta T}\le e^{-1}\), then \[W_2(\nu\widetilde P_n,\pi)\le A h^q+e^{-1}W_2(\nu\widetilde P_{n-m},\pi).\] Iterating the recurrence gives a geometric transient and an \(O(h^q)\) error floor. Pairwise contraction between arbitrary initial laws is unnecessary; the assumed contraction toward \(\pi\) suffices. Uniform numerical moments control the random initial point at each restart. The ceiling in the block length is correctly included in (114) and (119).

For \(U(x)=|x|^2/2\), the Euler chain has invariant covariance \((1-h/2)^{-1}I_d\) for \(0<h<2\). Therefore \[W_2\!\left(N(0,(1-h/2)^{-1}I_d),N(0,I_d)\right)
 =\sqrt d\bigl((1-h/2)^{-1/2}-1\bigr)
 =\tfrac14\sqrt d\,h+O(\sqrt d\,h^2).\] Independent arithmetic at \(d=1,10,100\) and \(h=0.1,0.01,0.001\) confirms this scaling. This validates a nontrivial special case, not the full nonconvex theorem. Dimension-independent log-Sobolev and growth constants remain essential hypotheses of the general claim.

# Corrections required in the mathematical argument

Problem 1 (Minor: the last inequality of (169) is false).  On physical page 24, the weighted increment integral is bounded by \(C(d^{2\gamma+1}+|x|^{4\gamma+2})^{1/2}h^2\). The square root cannot hold uniformly in \(x\) under the stated assumptions.

Take the one-dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck potential \(U(x)=x^2/2\) and use the admissible polynomial upper-growth exponent \(\gamma=2\). This smooth potential satisfies dissipativity, the one-sided condition, log-Sobolev, and the required third-derivative bound (its third derivative vanishes). For deterministic \(X_0=x>0\), \[\mathbb E|X_s-x|^2=x^2(e^{-s}-1)^2+1-e^{-2s}.\] Since \((1+x^2+X_s^2)^2\ge x^4\), the left side of (169), with \(t=0\), is at least \[x^4\{x^2 A(h)+B(h)\},\quad
 A(h)=h-2(1-e^{-h})+\tfrac12(1-e^{-2h}),\quad
 B(h)=h-\tfrac12(1-e^{-2h}).\] For fixed \(h=1/2\), \(A(h)>0\), so this grows as \(x^6\), whereas the printed right side is \(O(x^5)\). No constant independent of \(x\) repairs that inequality. The required constant’s rigorous lower bound grows from approximately \(1.24\) at \(x=10\) to \(1164.86\) at \(x=10000\); the unbounded asymptotic ratio, rather than these samples, proves failure.

The likely correction is to remove the final exponent \(1/2\). Indeed, applying the preceding Hölder step with an increment estimate of the displayed order gives a polynomial of order \(d^{2\gamma+1}+|x|^{4\gamma+2}\), not its square root. This larger polynomial is still bounded, by a dimension-weighted Young inequality, by \[C\bigl(d^{5\gamma+1}+d^{-4}|x|^{10\gamma+10}\bigr),\qquad d\ge1,\ \gamma>0.\] Thus the final local-error target (170) has enough slack to absorb this correction. The unexplained final dimension simplification in (168) is not needed for this repair. Put \(\tau=s-t\le h\le1\) and \(q=(2\gamma+1)/(\gamma+1)>1\). The preceding integral estimate in (168), the growth bound (18), and the available moments give directly \[\bigl(\mathbb E|X_s-x|^{2q}\bigr)^{1/q}
 \le C\{(d^{\gamma+1}+|x|^{2\gamma+2})\tau^2+d\tau\}.\] The other Hölder factor in (169) is at most \(C(d^\gamma+|x|^{2\gamma})\). Multiplication, Young’s inequality for the mixed powers, and integration yield \[\int_t^{t+h}\mathbb E[(1+|x|^\gamma+|X_s|^\gamma)^2|X_s-x|^2],ds
 \le C(d^{2\gamma+1}+|x|^{4\gamma+2})(h^3+h^2).\] This proves the needed weaker bound under the stated local-error assumptions, without adding a one-sided assumption to Lemma F.3 or claiming its printed (168) has been proved. To see absorption explicitly, set \(z=|x|/\sqrt d\): the larger target is \(d^{5\gamma+1}(1+z^{10\gamma+10})\), whereas the repaired polynomial is \(d^{2\gamma+1}(1+z^{4\gamma+2})\), which it bounds up to a constant for \(d\ge1\). The factor \(h\) in (166) then gives (170). This verified surviving local-error argument, rather than ease of repair, is the reason Problem 1 is Minor. It does not refute Theorem 2.16.

Problem 2 (Minor: Appendix G does not verify the example with its stated constants).  The double-well derivatives in (188)–(189), and the parameters asserted on page 8, are incorrect.

For the verification’s \(\alpha=\beta=1\) potential \(U(x)=|x|^4/4-|x|^2/2\), direct differentiation gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \nabla^2U&=(|x|^2-1)I+2xx^\top,\\
 \Delta U&=(d+2)|x|^2-d,\qquad
 \nabla\Delta U=2(d+2)x.\end{aligned}\] The printed Hessian omits a factor two; the Laplacian and its derivative then have further coefficient/sign errors. In particular, the proposed \(4d|x|\) bound fails at \(d=1\). With \(\gamma=2\), the page-8 choice \(\gamma_1=\gamma_2=1\) also violates the explicit condition \(\gamma_1+\gamma_2=\gamma+1\) and would only supply a \(\sqrt d|x|\) term in Assumption 2.14. A valid repair is \(\gamma_1=2\), \(\gamma_2=1\), and \(L_0=6\) for all \(d\ge1\). The stated \(L_1=1\) in Assumption 2.12 fails already in one dimension as \(y\to x\to\infty\), where the derivative is \(3x^2-1\) but the bound is \(1+2x^2\). Taking \(L_1=2\) is sufficient.

The Gaussian-mixture expression (186) also changes the denominator’s last coefficient from one to three in its second equality. Its intended linear bound nonetheless survives: writing \(z=\langle x,a\rangle\) gives \(\nabla\Delta U_1=2|a|^2\operatorname{sech}^2(z)\tanh(z)a\), whose norm is at most \(2|a|^4|x|\), tighter than (187). These corrections establish admissibility with modified constants; they do not overturn the examples or establish any defect in the historical simulations. The actual numerical double well uses \(\beta=16\), so its constants should be documented separately from the \(\beta=1\) illustration.

Problem 3 (Major: the general-SDE proof uses an unprovided moment range).  The proof of Theorem 3.3 uses exact-solution moments beyond the range guaranteed by its stated Condition (A1) and Lemma 3.1. The remaining indexing and identity corrections listed below are Minor and should be distinguished from this hypothesis gap.

Condition (A1), physical page 6, provides one \(p^*\ge1\); Lemma 3.1, page 7, only bounds exact-solution moments of order \(2p\) for \(p\le p^*\). Yet (79) and (94)–(103) use orders \(2r_0\) and \(2r+r_0\) without relating \(p^*\) to \(r_0\) or \(r\). Condition (A2) supplies numerical-solution moments; the published argument does not derive the required exact-solution bounds from it. This is not resolved by changing the argument of a moment constant.

The gap is Major for the general framework: Theorem 3.4 explicitly invokes Theorem 3.3 in (112), so the unprovided bound is used in a principal result, not an isolated example. A sufficient repair for this proof is to assume \[p^*\ge\max\{r_0,r+r_0/2\}\] and the corresponding finite initial moment, or provide a different proof deriving all needed exact moments under the original assumptions. This review establishes a gap in the supplied proof, not a counterexample to the fully stated theorem with (A2). The additive-noise dissipative Langevin applications explicitly admit arbitrary finite moment orders from deterministic starts, as (172) confirms, and their prescribed initial moments cover the application. Their survival does not validate the broader published proof.

The following associated corrections can be checked without executing a sampler.

- In (94)–(103), a moment of order \(2r+r_0\) calls for \(\widehat C_i^*(r+r_0/2)\) under (37)/(39), not \(\widehat C_i^*(2r+r_0)\). The latter would give a larger dimension power than (177). The generic theorem must also require enough exact-solution moments: \(p^*\ge\max\{r_0,r+r_0/2\}\) suffices for the moments used in this proof. Condition (A1) as printed only states \(p^*\ge1\). This is the Major proof-range issue above; the corrected index itself is a Minor bookkeeping change. The Langevin applications admit the needed moments, so their rates are not refuted by this issue.

- The gradient Itô identity (128) should have drift \(-\nabla^2U\nabla U+\nabla\Delta U\). The printed exterior minus applies to both terms. Subsequent triangle-norm bounds can use the same sum of norms after correcting the sign; the same issue occurs in (159).

- Equation (19) follows from (17) with \(1+2|x|^\gamma\), not \(1+|x|^\gamma\), unless the Lipschitz constant is enlarged. This is a constant change, not an exponent change.

- In (141) and (176), \(K_2^*\) is assigned the hatted quantity although it should be the unhatted coefficient multiplying the power of \(|x|\). Equation (144)’s expanded second line contains an extra factor \(d\) inside a bracket already multiplied by \(d\). Its first line has the intended \(O(d)\) size. The \(r_0=0\) case of (103) should be stated separately to avoid a formal division by zero and to use only second moments.

- The supremum in (24) cannot coexist with a right-hand side decreasing in the free index \(n\) for arbitrary large initial data. State a pointwise decaying bound, or replace the decaying initial term by its value at zero. The argument in Appendix A already has the appropriate pointwise form for ordinary LMC. The constants in (149) exchange the transient and bias labels; selecting \(h\) with the corrected labels preserves the asserted complexity order.

These are version-bounded claims about the published equations. Some are plainly compatible with transcription errors. The corrected moment indexing reproduces the power \(d^{11\gamma/2+1}\) before taking the square root in the projected bound; the literal printed indexing does not. A revised proof should state these repairs explicitly rather than asking the reader to silently change constants and indices.

# Evidence limits and assessment

The four panels on page 8 are compatible with qualitative density approximation and finite-time strong order one. The error study uses \(T=6\), a reference discretization \(h_{\rm ref}=2^{-13}\), and 3000 samples. Such a coupled finite-time experiment is not a direct empirical measurement of invariant-distribution \(W_2\) error or a dimension-asymptotic validation. No raw trajectories, seeds, implementation, or uncertainty analysis was available for independent numerical reproduction. No historical computational failure is alleged.

The recommendation is Major revision for the unprovided moment range in the general-SDE proof and its dependent uniform theorem. The local pLMC repair, derivative corrections, and bookkeeping findings remain Minor for the reasons demonstrated above. The nonconvex restart mechanism and the main LMC rate have not been refuted by these checks. The false bound (169) is an actual mathematical error, but its explicit weaker replacement fits the downstream polynomial target, which limits its severity. The projected local-error gap has been closed here by the explicit weaker estimate; the original simplified increment claim is bypassed. The generic moment-range issue still requires a stated qualification or a replacement derivation. Focused independent AI verification is recorded with the preserved predecessor; adjudication is pending and there are no human reviews.
