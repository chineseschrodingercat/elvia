Abstract: The central vector-space construction is invalid because addition depends on the representative of a decision-equivalence class. Exact examples using normalized nonnegative densities and signed valid logits demonstrate the problem. The zero-density limit and the alternative appendix do not establish a uniform probability distribution over the whole feature space. These theoretical objections do not invalidate finite-box noise training as an empirical method. Static inspection also finds bounded issues in one released classifier-combination variant and in training-mode restoration, together with a local noise-contrastive density formula error. Distinct implementations, the reported scalability failures, and unverified historical experiment provenance are explicitly retained.\par

\# P1: decision equivalence does not define the claimed vector space\par
Location and classification. Sections 2–3, Eqs. (1), (6)–(12), pp. 2–5; dependent zero and basis claims in Section 4 and Appendices C/F. Major: the purported vector structure and its derived zero-class interpretation are not established.\par
For an operation on equivalence classes, changing a representative must not change the result. Section 3 verifies familiar identities of functions but never verifies this prerequisite. Consider two unit-volume bins and normalized piecewise-constant densities, written as two-entry vectors: \[p=(0.7,0.3),\quad p'=(0.9,0.1),\quad q=(0.15,0.85),
 \qquad \tau=0.5.\] All three densities are nonnegative and integrate to one. Both \(p\) and \(p'\) classify just the first bin; moreover \(p'=2(p-\tau)+\tau\), precisely an allowed transformation in Eq. (1), with normalization constant one. Nevertheless the equal-mixture construction in Eq. (6) gives \[(p+q)/2=(0.425,0.575),\qquad (p'+q)/2=(0.525,0.475).\] At the same threshold \(0.5\), these classify opposite bins. Equivalently the unnormalized sums at threshold one classify opposite bins. Thus even restricting inputs to normalized genuine densities does not make the asserted class operation independent of the representative. The mixture identity in Appendix A is correct for specified component densities and equal mixing weights; it is not a well-defined union operation on their decision-equivalence classes.\par
The direct Eq. (11) failure is equally explicit at zero threshold. Let \(h=(1,-1)\), \(h'=2h\), and \(g=(-1.5,1.5)\). Then \([h]=[h']\), but \(h+g=(-0.5,0.5)\) and \(h'+g=(0.5,-0.5)\) induce opposite decisions. Signed functions are permitted by the valid-logit set \(S\) in Section 2; they can also be obtained by centering and scaling nonnegative densities and their thresholds. They are unavoidable if the claimed negative scalar multiplication is retained. There are no equality-boundary cases in these examples. More abstractly, at zero threshold the proposed scalar rule gives \(2[h]=[2h]=[h]\) for every such nontrivial decision class. In a real vector space subtraction would force \([h]=0\), contradicting the claimed distinct class-vectors.\par
Alternatives considered. Page 3 explicitly acknowledges that arbitrary representative logits cannot be merged. Passing to their equivalence sets does not remove the problem when addition is then defined through those same representatives. Picking a distinguished density would require additional information and a different construction, rather than prove the present quotient well-defined. Applying Boolean union/complement to already computed indicators is legitimate (the four rows of Table 1 were checked); it does not identify union with vector addition. In particular, union is idempotent whereas real-vector addition is not, except at zero.\par
Appendix F introduces threshold tuples and monotone transforms, but retains the same representative addition in Eq. (44). Strictly increasing transforms already include the rescaling counterexample. As literally written, allowing non-decreasing constant transforms also destroys decision preservation by collapsing both the logit and its threshold to the same constant. The appendix expressly calls its argument intuition and omits constraints; restricting these transforms would remove that latter degeneracy but would not repair the additive counterexample.\par
Dependent zero and basis interpretations. The ordinary zero function has integral zero, so normalization is undefined. A sequence \(\varepsilon r(x)\to0\) normalizes to any chosen probability density \(r\), not uniquely to a uniform one. Independently checking \(r=(0.8,0.2)\) at three positive \(\varepsilon\) values gives the same nonuniform normalized result. The constant-path choice in Eqs. (19)–(20) does give a uniform law on each fixed finite box. However, \(q_R=(2R)^{-1}\mathbf1_{[-R,R]}\) has total mass one and converges pointwise to zero, with \(q_R([-1,1])=1/R\to0\). It has no probability limit that is uniform on the whole real line. The same obstruction holds in higher dimensions. Section 4 itself labels the derivation an indication, and Appendix F also disclaims a formal proof; those qualifications must be preserved. They do not support the stronger discovery claim in the conclusion.\par
Appendix C’s independence assertion is also unsupported. In particular, Appendix F’s monotone decision equivalence gives \([x]=[x^3]\) at zero threshold, since cubing and its inverse preserve the threshold decisions. Even before quotienting, its ordered multivariate tensor coefficients are redundant: nonzero coefficients \(m_{12}=1\), \(m_{21}=-1\) give \(x^\top m x=0\) for every \(x\). The stored exact/finite checks include this cancellation. Symmetric multi-index coefficients and explicit approximation/domain conditions would be needed for a different polynomial parameterization; a learned quadratic separator remains usable without the claimed universal basis.\par
Consequence and repair scope. The proposed real vector space, its additive-identity distribution, and the associated basis theorem require replacement. A finite-domain noise classifier and ordinary Boolean operations remain coherent empirical constructions. This review verifies the counterexamples, not a replacement theory or a claim that noise training cannot help.\par
\# P2: a released unary combiner compares non-identifiable raw logits\par
Location and classification. and , especially lines 26–30 of the latter. Minor, restricted to the two-output cross-entropy variant in the inspected revision; the exact historical experiment route is unverified.\par
That variant trains two output logits for each unary model. The combiner selects the largest last-column raw logit across models, without subtracting the corresponding noise logit. Let two models output \(A=(0,2)\) and \(B=(0,1)\) for one input. The combiner selects \(A\). Adding 10 to both outputs of \(B\) gives \((10,11)\), so it selects \(B\), although every probability, cross-entropy loss and within-model prediction of \(B\) is unchanged. The independent calculation obtains the same \(B\) class probability \(0.7310586\) and loss \(0.3132617\) before and after the shift. Adding a common final-layer bias realizes this invariance for all inputs. Uniform noise in the loss does not fix this free offset.\par
Compare a log-odds margin or probabilities with explicit prior/noise calibration, and authenticate the executed configuration. This suggested local change removes the additive-offset ambiguity but does not prove general cross-model calibration. Crucially, the separately inspected specifies a single-output BCE variant, to which this two-logit invariance does not apply. Its configuration also differs from the current helper’s binary-branch selector, so execution provenance is needed. The grouped route uses softmax probabilities and masks a model’s noise-class win; it likewise is not subject to the raw-offset example. The finding therefore is not assigned indiscriminately to Figures 41–44 or their reported accuracies.\par
\# P3: evaluation mode persists into later training epochs\par
Location and classification. , lines 69–107, 127–159; . Minor implementation issue, with unmeasured historical effect.\par
Each epoch invokes training followed by three evaluation routines. Those routines call , but the training routine and surrounding loop never restore . Static syntax inspection finds evaluation calls at lines 107, 129 and 159 and no model-training-mode call. Consequently, after the first evaluation a newly constructed model stays in evaluation mode for subsequent optimization epochs. The CIFAR architectures contain both batch normalization and dropout, so dropout is disabled and running batch-normalization statistics cease to update. Gradients and optimizer updates still occur; this is not a claim that training stops.\par
The installed framework’s actual mode implementation was read and hashed. A separately created BatchNorm/Dropout probe confirms the behavior: the batch counter is 1 after the training forward, remains 1 after an evaluation-mode forward, and becomes 2 after explicitly restoring training mode. Restore the mode at the start of each training epoch and then compare affected runs. Both compared CIFAR models share the helper; the direction and magnitude of any benchmark effect cannot be inferred. The inspected notebook uses 100 epochs while the script and Figure 29 use 50, further limiting historical attribution. Pure linear/ReLU MNIST models do not acquire the same dropout/normalization consequence.\par
\# P4: constant in the noise-contrastive density formula\par
Location and classification. Section 6, Eqs. (26)–(29), pp. 8–9. Minor algebraic error in density normalization.\par
With the paper’s definition \(p_n(u)=k\), substituting in Eq. (26) gives \(G=\log p_d-\log k\), not \(\log p_d-k\). Hence \(p_d=k e^G\), not \(e^{G+k}\). For \(p_d=0.4\) and \(k=0.25\), \(G=0.4700036\) gives the correct density 0.4, while the printed expression gives 2.0544407. One could instead define a new symbol \(k=\log p_n\), but that is not its definition in the text. The score identity is unchanged because the missing term is constant in \(u\).\par
More generally, the Bayes binary log-odds is \(\log p_d-\log p_n+\log(\pi_d/\pi_n)\) when the sampling priors differ. For equal priors and uniform noise on a finite box, the positive region is a density superlevel set \(p_d>1/V\); it is not automatically the full distributional support or an arbitrary future class’s complement. This does not contradict the paper’s conditional mode-seeking generation discussion, but does not establish exact recovery of a true manifold either.\par
\# What the experiments and code do support\par
The image generator samples independent pixel values uniformly on \([0,1]\), and the data loaders create separate training, test and occupancy-noise draws. The inspected MNIST/CIFAR preprocessing uses official training/test splits. Their accuracy test loader excludes noise whereas purity includes separately generated test noise, consistent with the figure captions. Thus the source does not justify a training/test-noise reuse allegation. Synthetic noise instead uses a data-dependent bounding box with ten-percent padding; its test-noise box is calculated from test data, so its evaluation distribution should be explicitly documented.\par
Figures 6–7 and 17 show finite synthetic rejection regions. Figures 18, 29 and 40 display low uniform-noise occupancy with broadly comparable closed-set accuracy; this is useful empirical evidence for rejecting that chosen noise. An exclusive classifier with no reject output has occupancy one by construction. Purity is sample precision, and both quantities depend on the noise population and region. Appendix D.2.2 explicitly acknowledges this dependence and requests its disclosure. These metrics alone do not certify rejection of semantically novel classes or coverage of all empty space.\par
The unary plots (Figures 19–28 and 30–39) show high individual accuracy against uniform noise, while the actual repository diagrams report 81.65\% on MNIST and 25.39\% on CIFAR10. The grouped repository falls from 97\% to 85\% to 73\% as datasets are added. The main text and conclusion acknowledge poor scaling; these failures are not hidden and should not be mistaken for evidence of universally compatible class boundaries. The generated-digit panel is qualitative mode-seeking evidence; no new image-quality statistic was inferred from it.\par
Raw predictions, repeated-seed uncertainty, historical configurations/checkpoints, the ImageNet embedding pipeline and the remaining source tree were not reproduced or exhaustively audited.
