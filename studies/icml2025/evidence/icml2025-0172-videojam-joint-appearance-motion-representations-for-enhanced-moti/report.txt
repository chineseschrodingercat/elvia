Abstract: The joint training objective and the probabilistic justification of Inner-Guidance were inspected in the ICML 2025 proceedings version. A central derivation confuses differentiation with respect to the sampled state with differentiation with respect to model parameters. Consequently, dependence of a conditioning signal on model weights does not establish the claimed obstruction to ordinary conditional-score algebra. An explicit smooth Gaussian model verifies this distinction, even with correlated conditions whose distribution depends on the weights. The supported impact is Major for the justification of a principal method component; the empirical advantages of VideoJAM are not refuted by this check.

# Definitions and checks that survive

Equation reference: eq:target. For a fixed model parameter \(\theta\) and realized condition \(c\), the state score is \(\nabla_x\log p_\theta(x\mid c)\). It lies in the state space. The parameter score \(\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(x\mid c)\) lies in parameter space and is a different derivative. The cited classifier-free guidance paper explicitly uses the noisy-state derivative [cfg]. The paper uses the interpolation \(x_t=t x_1+(1-t)x_0\), with velocity \(x_1-x_0\). Equations (1)–(3) follow from differentiation in time, and the concatenated squared-error objective in Equation (6) is a coherent joint appearance–motion regression target. There is no contradiction in learning correlated appearance and motion, or in employing the generated motion state as an auxiliary signal. Write \(z=(x,d)\) and suppress time. For positive differentiable densities, Equation (7) defines the unnormalized quantity \[
\widetilde p_\theta(z\mid y)\ \propto\
p_\theta(z\mid y)\,p_\theta(y\mid z)^{w_1}\,
p_\theta(d\mid x,y)^{w_2}.\] Whenever it is normalizable, it is a legitimate modified density. Its state-score calculation can be performed without independence from model weights.

# Substantive finding

Problem 1 (Parameter dependence does not invalidate the conditional-score identity).  Section 2 (p. 3), Section 4.3 (p. 5), and Appendix A (p. 12) argue that existing conditioning constructions require independence of the conditions from model weights, and that generated motion violates this requirement. Appendix A ties omission of \(p_\theta(c_1,c_2)\) in Equations (9)–(11) to that independence. Equation (8) on p. 5 then explicitly writes \(\nabla_{\theta'}\) while identifying the result as a diffusion score and subsequently substituting state-sized velocity predictions.

## Exact mathematical check

Equation reference: eq:bayes, eq:statescore. For any positive differentiable joint density and fixed realized \(c\), \[
\log p_\theta(x\mid c)=\log p_\theta(x,c)-\log p_\theta(c).\] Thus, with \(\theta\) and \(c\) held fixed, \[
\nabla_x\log p_\theta(x\mid c)=\nabla_x\log p_\theta(x,c),
\qquad \nabla_x\log p_\theta(c)=0.\] The marginal may depend arbitrarily on \(\theta\) and the components of \(c\) may be dependent. Neither changes Equation [eq:statescore] (#eq:statescore). In contrast, \[\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(x\mid c)
=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(x,c)-\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(c),\] and the last term need not vanish. This identity concerns a parameter derivative, not a sampling direction. For a fully specified example take independent standard Gaussian noises and \[C_1=\theta+\varepsilon_1,\qquad
C_2=C_1+\varepsilon_2,\qquad
X=C_1+C_2+\varepsilon_x.\] All conditional variances are one. The joint density is smooth, strictly positive and normalized, the two conditions are correlated, and their marginal depends on \(\theta\). At \[\theta=0.4,\qquad c_1=1,\qquad c_2=0.2,\qquad x=2,\] direct evaluation gives \[\begin{aligned}
\partial_x\log p_\theta(x\mid c_1,c_2)
&=\partial_x\log p_\theta(x,c_1,c_2)=-0.8,\\
\partial_x\log p_\theta(c_1,c_2)&=0,\\
\partial_\theta\log p_\theta(x\mid c_1,c_2)&=0,\\
\partial_\theta\log p_\theta(x,c_1,c_2)
&=\partial_\theta\log p_\theta(c_1,c_2)=0.6.\end{aligned}\] An independent finite-difference calculation with step \(10^{-5}\) returned \(-0.8000000000008\), \(0\), and \(0.5999999999950\) for the corresponding nonzero/zero derivatives. These numerical results confirm the exact algebra; they are not its proof. The input model, source script and actual output are retained in `verification/0172/independent_checks.json`.

## Alternative interpretations and counterevidence

Treating the subscript \(\theta'\) in Equation (8) as a notational error repairs that symbol but does not repair the repeated verbal argument about weight independence in Sections 2, 4.3 and Appendix A. Conversely, reading it literally as a parameter derivative makes the immediate substitution of velocity predictions invalid: the vectors live in different spaces, and a parameter-dependent normalizing constant can no longer simply be omitted. A generated conditioning value can change between inference steps. Its origin does not alter a partial derivative at a fixed realized condition. If instead one deliberately differentiates through a state-dependent function \(c(x)\), the chain rule introduces \(D c(x)\) terms. That is a different derivative and is not justified merely by saying that the condition depends on model weights. The joint state interpretation matters. For \(z=(x,d)\), a marginal involving \(d\) can depend on the differentiated coordinates. This can distinguish a joint-state construction from a conditional appearance score; it does not validate the stated weight-independence argument. Likewise, the product factorization in Appendix A’s Equation (9) requires conditional independence of conditions given \(x\). Dropping the conditioning marginal from an \(x\)-derivative requires no such factorization. Appendix A’s Equation (10) is an exact chain factorization without an independence assumption.

## Verified correction and boundaries

Equation reference: eq:correct. Taking the state gradient of Equation [eq:target] (#eq:target) and applying Bayes’ rule gives the precise identity \[
\begin{split}
\nabla_z\log\widetilde p_\theta(z\mid y)
={}&(1+w_1+w_2)\nabla_z\log p_\theta(z\mid y)\\
&-w_1\nabla_z\log p_\theta(z)
-w_2\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_x\log p_\theta(x\mid y)\\0_d\end{pmatrix}.
\end{split}\] The omitted normalizer and \(p_\theta(y)\) are constant in \(z\) at fixed \(\theta,y\). Equation [eq:correct] (#eq:correct) restores the density-level derivation. It makes no claim that an arbitrary motion output of the flow-dropped network equals the padded marginal score. Section 4.3 states that the dropped-motion branch is trained with appearance loss only, so its motion component requires a separate convention if the whole concatenated update is to implement this exact joint-score identity. The staged materials do not resolve that implementation question. The score-to-velocity relation also uses state derivatives. For the stated Gaussian interpolation, the exact conditional-mean velocity at \(0<t<1\) satisfies \[u_t(z)=\frac{z+(1-t)\nabla_z\log p_t(z)}{t}.\] This follows by differentiating the Gaussian convolution and substituting \(\mathbb E[z_0\mid z_t=z]\) into the interpolation. In particular, the padded marginal score \((s_x,0_d)\) maps pointwise to \((u_x,d/t)\), not \((u_x,0_d)\). The independent native challenge checked this affine mapping with exact rational arithmetic. This pointwise identity alone does not show that an arbitrary tilted score family transports its proposed marginals. A complete corrected sampler requires consistent treatment of these relations. No fully repaired implementation is asserted here.

# Core dependence and impact

Inner-Guidance is one of the paper’s two principal method components. The failed reasoning is used to declare existing guidance inapplicable, motivate the new construction, and attribute the IP2P ablation difference to a theoretical gap (Appendix A). This is a demonstrated invalid argument directly tied to that contribution, supporting Major under the review contract. The density in Equation (7), the joint regression architecture, and reported preference improvements can remain useful. A different empirical result from another weighting rule cannot establish the erroneous derivative premise. The comparison tests complete update rules and coefficients; it does not isolate independence from model parameters as a causal mechanism. No video-quality loss, false benchmark value, or whole-paper failure is inferred. The available arXiv v2 contains the same targeted reasoning; no correction was found in the inspected version and no exhaustive erratum search is claimed. The inaccessible reference fetches and absence of staged VideoJAM implementation are recorded as missing evidence, not additional defects.
