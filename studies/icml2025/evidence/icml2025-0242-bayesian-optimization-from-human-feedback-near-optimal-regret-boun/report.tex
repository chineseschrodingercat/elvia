Abstract: Two core derivation problems are established in the proceedings version. The coefficient-space objective is not the stated RKHS-regularized likelihood, and a curvature inequality essential to the confidence-bound proof fails in an exact one-observation example satisfying the assumptions. A further concentration step retains outcome-dependent weights despite fixed design. The supported assessment is Major because these steps support the main regret guarantee. The checks do not prove that the proposed regret rate is impossible, or that the numerical experiments are incorrect.\par

\section*{Definitions and dependencies}
Write \(\mu(s)=(1+e^{-s})^{-1}\) and \(\bar k\) for the dueling kernel induced by the normalized base kernel \(k\). For \(z=(x,x')\), \(h(z)=f(x)-f(x')\) and \(Y\mid z\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mu(h(z)))\). Assumption 2.1 requires \(f\in\mathcal H_k\) with norm at most \(B>0\). Theorem 4.7 additionally requires observation locations independent of their labels.\par
Equation reference: eq:kappa. Equation (7) defines the unconstrained minimizer of the summed logistic loss plus \(\lambda\|h\|_{\mathcal H_{\bar k}}^2/2\), with \(\lambda>0\). Equation (2) defines \[
\kappa=\sup_{x,x'\in X}\frac1{\mu'(f(x)-f(x'))}.\] This is curvature at the true utility differences, not a uniform bound over fitted functions.\par
Theorem 4.7 supplies confidence intervals used in Appendix B to retain the optimum, eliminate inferior actions, and establish Theorem 4.1. That theorem and Corollaries 4.4–4.5 carry the central near-optimal regret and sample-complexity claims. Thus defects in this confidence argument have a direct core dependency.\par
\section*{Substantive findings}
Problem 1 (The representer expansion changes the regularizer).  Equations (8)–(9), p. 5, and (36)–(37), p. 16, identify the RKHS estimator with an objective penalizing the Euclidean norm of its kernel coefficients. Those objectives are generally different.\par
\subsection*{Original step and exact calculation}
For \(h=\sum_i\theta_i\bar k(\cdot,z_i)\) and \(K_{ij}=\bar k(z_i,z_j)\), the reproducing identity gives \[\|h\|_{\mathcal H_{\bar k}}^2
=\sum_{i,j}\theta_i\theta_j
\langle\bar k(\cdot,z_i),\bar k(\cdot,z_j)\rangle
=\theta^\top K\theta.\] It does not give \(\theta^\top\theta\), as used in (9) and (36).\par
Take \(X=\{-1,1\}\), \(k(x,x')=xx'\), \(f=0\), \(B=1\), one deterministic duel \(z=(1,-1)\), and \(\lambda=1\). The base kernel is normalized by \(k(x,x)\le1\); the linear RKHS and deterministic design satisfy the assumptions. Under the BTL model, \(Y=1\) is an allowed event of probability \(1/2\). The dueling Gram matrix is \(K=[4]\). Setting \(s=h(z)=4\theta\), the correct objective is \[\log(1+e^{-s})+\frac{s^2}{8},\] whereas (9) gives \(\log(1+e^{-s})+s^2/32\). Their strictly convex first-order equations are, respectively, \[\mu(s)-1+s/4=0,\qquad \mu(s)-1+s/16=0.\] Both roots are positive and cannot coincide: subtracting the equations would require \(3s/16=0\). Independent scalar bisection gives \(s=1.0425969140\) and \(s=1.9656720992\), with predicted probabilities \(0.7393507715\) and \(0.8771454938\). This finite calculation confirms the analytically established distinction.\par
\subsection*{Implementation, alternatives, and consequence}
The pinned CPU source `clean\_test\_algos\_synthetic\_cpu.py`, lines 263–298, forms logits \(K\alpha\) and penalizes \(\sum_i\alpha_i^2\). Its gradient likewise uses \(\lambda\alpha\). The synthetic GPU and Yelp files use the same penalty. The CPU objective also averages the likelihood by the number of observations; at fixed \(\lambda\) this introduces a sample-size-dependent regularization rescaling relative to the paper’s summed loss. The one-observation example avoids that additional distinction.\par
Euclidean regularization is valid in an orthonormal RKHS feature coordinate system, but (8) explicitly uses kernel sections, which are not generally orthonormal. Whitening would require changing both coordinates and the prediction expression. Appendix C’s later switch to RKHS feature parameters therefore does not justify the displayed finite-coefficient objective.\par
This is a Major estimator/proof consistency problem: the estimator specified in (7), the claimed equivalent finite problem, and the implemented fit are not generally identical. The valid local correction is to use \(\lambda\theta^\top K\theta/2\) in the coefficient expansion, with consistent likelihood scaling. That identity repairs the conversion only; it does not repair the confidence proof below. The available repository’s README associates its CPU variant with the paper, but the inspected commit is not cryptographic evidence of the exact historical run.\par
Problem 2 (The central confidence-bound proof uses invalid curvature and concentration steps).  Equation (41), Appendix C, p. 17, states \(G_t(\theta^\star,\theta_t)\succeq\kappa^{-1}V_t\). The stated \(\kappa\) does not control the estimator segment. Moreover, the subsequent independent-noise argument treats fitted, outcome-dependent coefficients as fixed.\par
\subsection*{Exact counterexample to the curvature inequality}
Use \(X=\{0,1\}\), \(k(x,x')=xx'\), \(f=0\), \(B=1\), one deterministic duel \((1,0)\), and \[\lambda=\frac1{1+e}>0.\] The dueling feature is one and \(K=[1]\), so the two regularizers in Problem 1 coincide in this example. The true RKHS norm is zero, the base kernel is normalized, the observation point is independent of its label, and \(Y\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) is precisely the assumed model. Linear kernels are explicitly included in the paper.\par
For \(Y=1\), the derivative of the strictly convex penalized objective is \(\mu(a)-1+\lambda a\). Its unique zero is exactly \(a=1\). For \(Y=0\), symmetry gives \(a=-1\). In either case the integrated sigmoid derivative between the true parameter zero and the fitted parameter is \(\mu(1)-1/2\). Equation [eq:kappa] (\#eq:kappa) gives \(\kappa=4\), and therefore \[\begin{aligned}
G_t&=\lambda+\mu(1)-\tfrac12=\tfrac12,\\
\kappa^{-1}V_t&=\tfrac14+\lambda
=\tfrac14+\frac1{1+e}>\tfrac12 ,\end{aligned}\] because \(e<3\). Thus (41) fails for both labels, hence with probability one. Numerically the two sides are \(0.5\) and \(0.51894142137\).\par
This does not depend on allowing a singular linear Gram matrix on the original domain. With the strictly positive-definite delta kernel on \(\{0,1\}\), take \(\lambda=2/(1+e)\). Along the dueling-feature direction, whose squared norm is two, the corresponding eigenvalues are \(1\) and \(1/2+2/(1+e)>1\), again contradicting (41). The fitted difference is still \(\pm1\).\par
A curvature bound uniform over the entire true-to-estimated segment would imply an appropriate related inequality. That is an additional condition, different from (2). Even bounded fitted parameters do not make the true-function curvature bound uniform over that segment. Shrinking true preference differences in later rounds similarly does not by itself control fitted differences.\par
\subsection*{A separate obstacle to the concentration argument}
The stochastic term on pp. 17–18 contains \[\varphi(z)^\top G_t(\theta^\star,\theta_t)^{-1}
\sum_i\varepsilon_i\varphi(z_i),\qquad
\varepsilon_i=Y_i-\mu(h(z_i)).\] Although fixed design makes the label noises independent, \(G_t\) depends on the fitted \(\theta_t\), hence on those same labels. Bounding the realized coefficient norm does not justify the fixed-coefficient centered sub-Gaussian argument invoked there.\par
For a concrete diagnostic, use \(X=\{0,1\}\), \(k(x,x')=xx'\), \(f(x)=x\), \(B=1\), the same fixed duel, and \(\lambda=1\). Let \(p=\mu(1)\). The fits are \(\pm a\), where \(\mu(a)+a=1\). Direct scalar evaluation gives \[a=0.4010581375,\quad G_1=1.2205835398,\quad G_0=1.2355365793.\] Consequently \[\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{Y-p}{G_Y}\right]
=p(1-p)\left(\frac1{G_1}-\frac1{G_0}\right)
=0.00194946856\ne0 .\] Conditioning on the design does not restore centering; conditioning on the two distinct \(G_Y\) values reveals the label. This numerical diagnostic illustrates the invalid inference about fixed weights. It is not a numerical counterexample to the final confidence interval itself.\par
The roundwise reset of observations in the released algorithm supports design independence conditional on previous rounds. Cross-round adaptation is therefore not the objection. The remaining dependence arises within the fitted estimator.\par
\subsection*{Core impact and repair status}
The proof explicitly uses (41) in both (42) and (43), which combine into (45) and Theorem 4.7. The latter feeds Appendix B and the main regret and sample-complexity statements. This demonstrated invalid derivation supports Major without requiring the whole algorithm or every empirical result to fail. Correcting the curvature step alone would leave the concentration obstacle. No complete replacement confidence proof, corrected regret guarantee, or verified implementation repair is supplied by this review.\par
\section*{Overall assessment}
The reported proof does not establish the central guarantee as written. Its ultimate truth under some alternative argument remains unresolved. The variance-based sampling and elimination idea, and the recorded experimental curves, are not refuted by these checks. The paper explicitly qualifies its scalar-feedback lower-bound comparison as informal because of the noise-model difference; this report does not mischaracterize that qualification as a missing formal proof.\par
