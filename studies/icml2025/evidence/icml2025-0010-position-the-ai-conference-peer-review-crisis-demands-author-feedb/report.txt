Position: The AI Conference Peer Review Crisis Demands Author Feedback and Reviewer Rewards

Paper I25-40108 | Kim, Lee and Lee | PMLR 267:81634–81651 (2025). Reviewed: the 18-page publisher-linked proceedings PDF. Page references below are physical PDF pages and match the printed page numbers. Review date: 24 October 2026.

Outcome: Minor — one confirmed, bounded empirical-survey correction. No Major defect is demonstrated. The central feedback-and-rewards proposal remains an untested intervention whose benefits require pilots; this review does not establish that it fails. Human scientific decision is pending; author adoption has not been requested.

# Problem 1. The compensation survey omits a documented ICML 2024 benefit

Location and original claim. Appendix C, Table 2, p.17, records ICML 2024 as O / - and gives a 2024 in-kind-compensation total of one. The table counts benefits restricted to outstanding reviewers, and p.18 explicitly includes registration tickets as in-kind compensation. Section 4.3, p.6, uses the survey to support limited availability of reviewer rewards. The claim being checked is the venue-year classification and its count, not whether a reward improves review quality. [S1]

Evidence. The ICML 2024 program chairs state that financial constraints reduced the group receiving free registration from the originally planned top decile to a smaller group. The official conference reviewer-instructions page links that explanation. The target paper itself recounts this reduced-pool arrangement in §5.2, p.6, footnote 7, so the discrepancy does not depend on a newly introduced post-publication program. [S1, S3]

Quantity | Published coding | Minimal correction

ICML 2024 compensation | Not positively marked | Mark present; annotate limited recipient pool

2024 positive venue count | 1: ICLR | 2: ICLR and ICML, holding other cells fixed

Distinct positive venues over 2020–2024 | 5 in Table 2: ACL, CVPR, ECCV, ICLR, NeurIPS | 6 after adding ICML, if other entries remain unchanged

Reasoning and strongest alternative interpretation. A reduced number of recipients does not remove a benefit from a survey that includes selective awards. The caption acknowledges incomplete public information; a dash could therefore mean not identified rather than proven absent. Even under that interpretation, the table omits a positive program already recognized in the paper and understates its positive count. Section 4.3’s separate statement of four compensated venues also needs a defined time window and reconciliation with the table. That count discrepancy is included in this correction, rather than treated as a second problem.

Bounded impact and repair. This is a Minor empirical classification error in a supporting survey, with no demonstrated damage to the central proposal. Correct the ICML cell, update the 2024 total, define the survey cutoff and counting window, and distinguish unknown entries from verified absence. Do not infer that rewards are widespread or effective from this correction. The remaining venue-year classifications have not been exhaustively revalidated.

Verification class: direct primary-source comparison plus independent exact recounting of the published table. This is not an empirical replication of a reward intervention. Source snapshots, a web-evidence note for the program-chair page, and the reviewer-authored recount script are recorded in provenance.

# Checks that passed and interpretation boundaries

Scope | Result and limit

Figure 1, p.1 | ICLR’s official 2025 fact sheet gives 11,603 submissions and an increase of 4,341. The implied prior count is 7,262; growth is 59.7769%, correctly rounded to 59.8%. Other venue/year points were not independently reconstructed. [S4]

Appendix A, pp.12–14 | The seven top-20 changes sum to 24: 3+4+3+3+4+2+5. The mean turnover is 24/(7\(\times\)20)=17.1429%, supporting the reported 17%. This describes ICLR keyword slots, not a measured loss of reviewer knowledge. [S1]

Appendix B, pp.15–16 | The prompt, model family and error categories are disclosed. The paper explicitly allows spell-checking confounding and describes the evidence as weak and non-conclusive. No LLM-authorship prevalence or causal quality decline follows from Figure 6. The 28.8% versus 28.883% difference from rounded endpoints is immaterial and excluded. [S1]

Figures 2–3, pp.3 and 5 | The diagram matches staged release; the badge image is explicitly illustrative. Neither is evidence of an implemented system’s effectiveness. [S1]

Staging and aggregation. Under §3, authors commit feedback before weaknesses and ratings are released. A legitimate first-stage question about a missing mathematical assumption can still reveal skepticism and induce punitive feedback. This establishes a limit on an absolute prevention guarantee, not that staging cannot reduce bias. Averaging also need not remove shared directional bias. The abstract’s mitigation wording, §5.1’s proposed pilots, §5.3’s gaming discussion and §6’s acknowledged burdens support treating these as Needs Evidence, not additional confirmed Problems. [S1]

Rewards and measurement. Subsequent impact of reviewed papers measures a reviewer’s portfolio, not necessarily the contribution of that review; reviewers of the same papers can provide different-quality reviews. The cited educational badge study reports a favorable course-level observation in a qualitative case study. Its transfer to conference reviewers remains untested. These are measurement and external-validity limits, consistent with the paper’s call for pilots. [S1, S8]

# Selected source corrections and pending evidence

NeurIPS definitions. The 2021 study reports 203/882=23.0% raw accept/reject disagreement; 16.0% results from treating specified borderline opposing decisions as consistent. The 2014 table gives 43/166=25.9%. Clarify these definitions and years when presenting §7.1’s 16–23% range. The primary 2021 report does not establish a temporal increase in noise. This is a scope clarification, not a demonstrated failure of the qualitative inconsistency argument. [S5]

Goldberg study timing. Section 7.1, p.8, describes review evaluations as occurring after the whole submission process. The primary study’s §2 places them after reviews were shown to authors but before final conference decisions. Its author bias concerns reviewer recommendations; the randomized length experiment is a separate analysis. Correcting the timing does not invalidate a proposal that hides recommendations before feedback. [S6]

Liang prevalence attribution. The accessible arXiv v1 uses sentences as its main unit and reports an estimated 17.5% for arXiv computer-science abstracts by February 2024. It does not directly measure an annual paper-level lower bound above 17.5%. A constructed corpus with 35 modified sentences out of 200 can have only two of twenty papers affected, showing why units matter. This is a logical example, not a reconstruction of the data. The cited COLM final version was inaccessible; exact version correspondence remains unresolved, so this attribution is retained as Needs Evidence / Scope Clarification. [S7]

Pending subitems without severity escalation: P01 — effectiveness, residual retaliation, calibration and false-positive LLM flags under staged feedback; P02 — reward alignment and whether paper-impact metrics measure review contributions; P03 — reconstruction of keyword/typo analyses from dated data, mappings, model outputs/settings and detector validation; P04 — final-version verification of the Liang attribution. The exact CVPR count of 19 desk-rejected papers was not independently verified from a primary announcement; the underlying policy was verified and no contrary evidence was found. [S9]
