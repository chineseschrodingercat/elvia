Abstract: The central potential argument does not cover its stated radius domain. On a fixed three-point metric, valid fractional solutions produce arbitrarily small positive service radii, making the claimed one-step amortized inequality false. Zero optimal radii also make the printed potentials undefined. This supports Major for the recourse derivation, without a counterexample to the algorithm’s actual competitive recourse. The LP transformation and geometric separation lemmas pass the inspected checks. Empirical evidence and several lower-bound conventions retain explicit scope limits. Adjudication is pending.

# The recourse potential lacks a valid small-radius treatment

Equation reference: eq:potential, eq:claimed. Problem 1 (Major: the claimed amortized inequality is false for legal fractional service radii).  The facility-location algorithm on p. 6 assigns a newly selected client radius \(r_j=\gamma R_j\), where \(R_j=\sum_i d_{ij}y_{ij}\). With \(\alpha=11,\gamma=10/9,\delta=2\), Eq. (3.4), p. 7, has the form \[
\Phi=K\sum_{j\in A}\max\{0,1/10-x(B_j)\}
-\eta\sum_{j\in A}\log_2(\Delta/r_j),\] where \[\eta=\frac1{\log_2(69/20)},\qquad
K=110(1+\eta\log_2\Delta).\] Equation (3.5) asserts \[
\Delta N_1+\Delta N_2+\Delta\Phi
\le O(\log\Delta)\|x^t-x^{t-1}\|_1.\] The proof also asserts a lower bound \(\Phi\ge-C\log\Delta\sum_i x_i\). Both assertions fail under the printed assumptions.

The minimum positive distance between distinct points being one does not imply \(R_j\ge1\): \(R_j\) is a fractional weighted average that can include self-distance zero. The following construction keeps \(\Delta,\beta\), the number of points and all opening costs fixed.

## Admissible first insertion

Let \[M=F=\{a=0,b=1,c=2\},\qquad d(u,v)=|u-v|,\qquad f_a=f_b=f_c=1.\] This is a metric with minimum positive distance one and aspect ratio \(\Delta=2\). Set \(\beta=2\), start from the prescribed empty state, and insert the single client \(a\). For any \(0<s<0.1\), choose \[x_a=y_{aa}=1-s,\quad x_b=y_{ba}=s,\quad x_c=y_{ca}=0.\] Assignments sum to one, \(0\le y_{ia}\le x_i\), and the cost is \(1+s\le2\,\mathrm{OPT}^1\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}^1=1\). The fractional optimum is exactly one because coverage requires \(\sum_i x_i\ge1\), all opening costs are one, and opening at \(a\) attains one. Initial fractional movement is one.

Equation reference: eq:firstphi. The algorithm adds \(a\), whose service cost is \(s\), radius is \(r_a=\gamma s<1\), and ball is the singleton \(B_a=\{a\}\). Its mass \(1-s>1/10\) satisfies all mass invariants; separation is vacuous and the sole client is covered by the cheapest facility \(a\). The nonnegative term in [eq:potential] (#eq:potential) vanishes, so \[
\Phi^1=-\eta\log_2\frac{2}{\gamma s}.\] As \(s\downarrow0\), this tends to minus infinity while \(\Delta=2\) and \(\sum_i x_i=1\). Therefore no claimed universal terminal bound of that form holds.

## A legal deletion falsifies the one-step inequality

Equation reference: eq:failure. Delete \(a\), leaving no clients, and set \(x^2=0\). The empty-instance optimum is zero and this is a valid exact solution. The fractional movement is one. The old ball has mass zero and is dropped, so \(\Delta N_1=1,\Delta N_2=0,\Phi^2=0\). Equation [eq:claimed] (#eq:claimed) would require a universal constant to bound \[
1+\eta\log_2\frac{2}{\gamma s}
\le O(\log2)\cdot1.\] The left side is unbounded as \(s\downarrow0\), whereas the right side is constant. Thus the actual inductive inequality, not just its terminal estimate, is false. Empty states are explicitly used in the paper’s initialization and lower-bound phases; excluding them would change the stated model.

The same failure can be obtained without an empty second state. Insert \(c\) instead, and set \(x_b=1,\ y_{ba}=y_{bc}=1\). The cost is 3, within the \(\beta=2\) budget \(2\mathrm{OPT}^2=4\). Here \(\mathrm{OPT}^2=2\): opening at both endpoints attains 2, and dual client values \(1,1\) certify the lower bound since \(\sum_j\max\{1-d_{ij},0\}\le f_i=1\) for every facility. The old singleton ball is dropped. Whichever current client is selected next has radius \(\gamma\) and a ball containing \(b\) with mass one. Any cheapest facility chosen there covers the other client within distance \(2<11\), so exactly one replacement ball suffices. The resulting change is \[\Delta N_1+\Delta N_2+\Delta\Phi
=1+\eta\log_2(1/s),\qquad
\|x^2-x^1\|_1=2(1-s)<2.\] This also contradicts a universal bound [eq:claimed] (#eq:claimed), independently of tie choices.

## Independent numerical check and proof dependency

For \(s=2^{-400}\), direct reviewer-authored arithmetic gives \(\eta=0.5597235175\), \(\Phi^1=-224.3640508\), whereas even the weaker explicit bound \(-110(1+\eta)\sum_i x_i\) is \(-171.5695869\). The deletion-step left side is 225.3640508, and the insertion-step left side is 224.8894070. The symbolic family \(s=2^{-m}\), rather than any single large value, establishes failure for every universal constant. Exact rational inputs and numerical outputs are retained in `check.py`/`calc.json`.

The faulty potential step is used to prove Theorem 3.4’s principal facility-location recourse guarantee. Theorem 3.7 explicitly inherits the \(k\)-median recourse bound from Theorem 3.4. The error therefore concerns the core competitive-recourse derivation, supporting Major under the specified calibration.

## Related boundaries, counterevidence and repair limits

The \(k\)-center potential (3.2), p. 5, has the same logarithm. For one client and \(k=1\), the allowed \(F=M\) gives \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\), so its created radius is zero and \(\log(\Delta/r)\) is undefined. Exact self-service also produces zero radii in the facility-location and \(k\)-median formulations. This is not fixed by separating distinct metric points.

At the other end, facility-location radii are not capped: \(r=\gamma R\) may exceed \(\Delta\). The p. 8 add-only argument \(-\eta\log_2(\Delta/r)\le0\) then also needs a separate justification. For example, on the same metric a singleton at \(a\) can be fractionally served entirely from \(c\) with opening cost 1 and service cost 2; with \(\beta=3\) this is feasible, and its new radius is \(2\gamma>2\).

These observations do not demonstrate excessive actual recourse. The deletion construction makes just one opening and one closing, total recourse 2, matching its total fractional movement 2. Nor does the review show that the particular positive-body-chasing implementation must produce this fractional path. Theorem 3.4 and its proof purport to round any fractional input satisfying the stated conditions, which the example does.

A bounded scale potential together with a separate singleton-ball argument may repair the proof. Merely replacing \(r\) inside the logarithm is not a verified repair: replacement-event credits and all boundary transitions must be re-established. No complete corrected amortization is claimed. The geometric coverage and approximation arguments may survive independently.

# Checks that survive and remaining scope

Appendix B’s covering–packing conversion is valid. For a fixed client, \[\min_{F'\subseteq F}\left(\sum_{i\in F'}y_{ij}
+\sum_{i\notin F'}x_i\right)=\sum_i\min\{y_{ij},x_i\}.\] Clipping \(y'_{ij}=\min\{y_{ij},x_i\}\) therefore preserves coverage, enforces \(y'\le x\), decreases nonnegative service cost and leaves \(x\)-recourse unchanged. Selecting \(F'=\{i:y_{ij}\le x_i\}\) gives the separation oracle. Finite subset enumeration independently checks illustrative cases; the coordinatewise identity proves the claim.

The geometric arguments in Lemmas 3.3 and 3.6 also check out. For \(k\)-center the exact constants \(\alpha=3+2\sqrt2,\delta=\sqrt2\) give \((2+2\delta)/(\alpha-\delta-1)=\delta\). For facility location the shrink factor is 3.45 and the separation comparison is \(12/6.9<2\). Disjoint balls of mass at least \(1/11\) give the stated \(11k'\) center count for \(k\)-median. These checks do not repair the potential argument.

The HST lower-bound displays on pp. 14–15 incorrectly bound \(\sum_{k=0}^{m}(4c)^k\) by its last term. The correct upper bound is \(\frac{4c}{4c-1}(4c)^m\le\frac43(4c)^m\) for \(c\ge1\). The subsequent separation still exceeds the required comparison by a factor at least \((4c-1)/(2c)\ge3/2\), so that local arithmetic can be repaired without losing the logarithmic order. Counting an opening plus a closing as one recourse instead of two likewise changes only a constant.

Two lower-bound scope questions remain: the facility-location construction uses repeated clients at one node despite the set notation \(C^t\subseteq M\), and the displayed \(k\)-median argument directly uses a 2-approximate offline comparator whereas the theorem is phrased for arbitrary input \(\beta\ge1\). A multiplicity convention or checked distinct-copy construction, and the missing parameter extension, are needed for a literal full-scope proof. These are Needs Evidence, not asserted counterexamples to the existence of the lower bounds. Selecting an adversarial path using a randomized algorithm’s marginal distribution does not by itself require observing its realized random seed.

Appendix D studies 100-step streams on Glass, Wine and Airfoil, with insertion/deletion probabilities \(0.9/0.1\), \(k=4,\beta=3/2,\epsilon=1/4\). The displayed low-recourse traces support those short streams; they do not prove constant recourse for arbitrary long streams. Lower online recourse than the fractional comparator is possible under resource augmentation. The original plots were visually inspected, but not digitized or independently reproduced. Facility costs, preprocessing, random seeds, repetitions, solver details and per-step numeric traces are not supplied in the acquired material. The blue \(\alpha\beta\mathrm{OPT}\) reference also needs parameter clarification: direct composition for facility location and \(k\)-median includes the fractional relaxation’s \(1+\epsilon\) factor.
