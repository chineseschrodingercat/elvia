Abstract: Exact Gaussian updates give counterexamples to the stated main Lyapunov bound and to the uniform one-step entropy-based estimate underlying it. Missing temperature factors and omitted initial-moment dependence are material to the proof. The Wasserstein conversion and continuous-time noise scaling also need local corrections. The supported overall assessment is Major. These findings concern the published statements and derivation; they do not rule out a corrected near-linear-in-accuracy complexity result.

# Framework and a fully admissible test family

The paper studies \(E(\mu,\nu)=J(\mu,\nu)+\tau\mathrm{KL}(\mu\mid\rho)-
\tau\mathrm{KL}(\nu\mid\rho)\) for a standard Gaussian reference \(\rho\), with convex-concave \(J\). Its assumptions bound spatial derivatives of first variations through order four and derivatives of second variations, assume LSI for the associated Gibbs measures, and require finite initial energy and fourth moments. Algorithm 1 uses alternating Euler updates with independent Gaussian noises. The stochastic extension adds unbiased oracle noise with a bounded fourth moment. These are distribution-level statements; the paper explicitly leaves finite-particle error analysis for future work.

Equation reference: eq:params. Set \(d=1\), \(J\equiv0\), and \(\rho=N(0,1)\). Every Gibbs operator and both equilibrium distributions equal \(\rho\), with LSI constant \(\alpha=1\). All derivatives of \(J\) vanish, so one may take \(M_i=0\) and the valid upper bounds \(C_1=C_2=1/2\) (also any nonnegative \(C_0\)). Choose \[
 \tau=\tfrac12,\quad\lambda=\tfrac1{10},\quad
 \eta_2=\tfrac1{100},\quad\eta_1=\tfrac1{2000}.\] Then \(\tau<1/(2C_1^2)=2\), \(\eta_1<1/C_1=2\), \(\eta_2<1/(2\tau\alpha)=1\). The time-scale relation holds exactly: \[\eta_1=\min\{\lambda,0.2,1/(\tau\alpha)\}\tau\alpha\eta_2.\] Exact gradients are a permissible stochastic oracle with \(\zeta=0\). Every Gaussian initial law below has finite entropy, KL divergence and fourth moment. The requirements are thus checked without relying on nonsmooth densities or unbounded functional derivatives.

Equation reference: eq:OU. For \(a_i=\eta_i\tau\), each marginal follows the elementary Gaussian update \[
 \begin{gathered}
 X_{k+1}=(1-a_i)X_k+\sqrt{2a_i}\,\xi_k,\\
 m_{k+1}=(1-a_i)m_k,\qquad s_{k+1}=(1-a_i)^2s_k+2a_i,
 \end{gathered}\] where \(s_k\) denotes variance. The exact KL is \(\mathrm{KL}(N(m,s)\mid\rho)=(m^2+s-1-\log s)/2\). Also \(L_1=\tau\mathrm{KL}(\mu\mid\rho)\), \(L_2=\tau\mathrm{KL}(\nu\mid\rho)\). These identities support all checks below.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The stated main contraction bound is false).  Theorems 1–2, pp. 6–7, assert contraction \((1-2\eta_1\tau\alpha)^K\) with the explicit remainders in Appendix A.3. The following counterexample disproves that quantitative statement. This is Major because it directly concerns the main last-iterate theorem.

Use [eq:params] (#eq:params), \(\mu_0=N(10^5,1)\) and \(\nu_0=N(0,1)\). The constants in A.3 give \(r_{g4}=r_{h4}=48\) and do not include the initial mean. To avoid depending on ambiguous parentheses in the displayed \(\Gamma_0\), enlarge each \(\Gamma_j\) to \(100\). Every surviving displayed term is smaller than this bound: even taking the cross term outside the \(M_3\) bracket gives \(\Gamma_0\leq(3/4)\sqrt{48}<5.20\), and \(\Gamma_1=\Gamma_2=(1/2)\sqrt{48}+1/16<3.53\). The enlarged Theorem 1 remainder is \[\overline R_1=
 \frac{\lambda\{100\eta_2^2+(1-2\eta_2\tau)200\eta_1^2\}
       +100\eta_1^2}{\eta_1\tau}=4.1198.\] Replacing positive remainder constants by these larger ones only weakens the asserted upper bound.

Let \(q_i=(1-a_i)^2\). After \(K\) updates, \(m_{\mu,K}=(1-a_1)^K10^5\) and \(s_{i,K}=q_i^K+2a_i(1-q_i^K)/(1-q_i)\). Substituting these values in the Gaussian KL formula gives the exact Lyapunov value. Already after one update its excess over the enlarged claimed bound exceeds \(152\); after ten updates the excess exceeds \(1500\). The independent 60-digit calculation records both complete values. This is not particle simulation and contains no sampling error.

The mechanism is explicit. The mean contribution contracts by \((1-a_1)^2=1-2a_1+a_1^2\), strictly slower than \(1-2a_1\). The extra \(a_1^2L_1(\mu_0)\) cannot be bounded uniformly by a remainder that omits arbitrary finite initial moments. Appendix C, p. 22, actually concludes the weaker factor \(1-\eta_1\tau\alpha\), not the displayed factor in the theorem. Lemma 10, pp. 15–16, keeps initial moments in its moment estimate, but A.3 drops them when defining the final constants. These are concrete sources of the mismatch.

Using the weaker factor eliminates this particular Gaussian mean counterexample, and retaining appropriate initial-moment dependence is another necessary accounting repair if a sharper factor is pursued. Neither observation verifies the general repaired theorem, because the next problem affects its one-step entropy estimate independently. With \(\zeta=0\), the same example applies to Theorem 2’s stated bound. There is no implication here that the algorithm fails to approach a small discretization neighborhood in this example: [eq:OU] (#eq:OU) shows that it does.

Problem 2 (The required uniform one-step estimates do not follow from the assumptions).  Lemma 1, Proposition 1 and the entropy argument in Lemmas 6–7 are affected. These are Major defects in the core elementary discretization proof.

Equation reference: eq:badlemma. First, retain the same problem and step sizes but set \(\nu_0=N(0,s)\) with \(s=10^{-4}\), and use any Gaussian \(\mu_0\). The next variance is \(s'=(1-\eta_2\tau)^2s+2\eta_2\tau=0.0100990025\). Lemma 1’s ascent inequality, p. 5, requires \[
 \frac{\tau}{2}\{s-s'+\log(s'/s)\}
 \ \geq\ \eta_2\tau^2\frac{(1-s)^2}{s}-\Gamma_2\eta_2^2.\] The left side is approximately \(1.151255\); the right side is at least \(24.98500025\) even after inflating \(\Gamma_2\) to \(100\). Thus the inequality is false with a smooth, everywhere positive density satisfying all initial assumptions. The Fisher information of this iterate is \(1/s\); LSI of the reference Gibbs distribution does not bound it or its log-density Hessian. Small moments alone do not bound these quantities either.

Lemma 6 introduces a uniform log-density Hessian bound that Assumptions 1–3 do not provide. Its convexity observation about heat-flow entropy does not by itself give a quadratic Taylor upper remainder. For a Gaussian of variance \(s\), heat-flow entropy has second derivative \(1/[2(s+\epsilon)^2]\) in the negative-entropy convention, so its Taylor constants necessarily depend on the density scale. In addition, the equality in (26) omits the inverse transpose Jacobian: \[\nabla_y\log(T_\#\nu)(T(x))
 =(DT(x))^{-T}\{\nabla_x\log\nu(x)-\nabla_x\log|\det DT(x)|\}.\] Even for \(T(x)=a x\), Fisher information scales as \(a^{-2}\), not as the expression in (26). An elementary proof may still be possible, but the present uniform remainder and transport identity require replacement.

Second, Proposition 1 uses \(f_k+\nabla\log\mu_k\), whereas the energy’s Wasserstein gradient is \(f_k+\tau\nabla\log\mu_k\). At \(\mu_0=\nu_0=\rho\), \(f_0=g_0=\tau x\). The true next \(L_1\) is positive, since the Euler step changes its variance to \(1+(\eta_1\tau)^2\). The proposition’s right side is at most \(-\eta_1(\tau-1)^2/2+100\eta_1^2=-0.0000375\). Thus the missing temperature factor is not harmless under the theorem’s arbitrary \(\tau\) convention. The LSI identity at the top of p. 22 repeats the same missing factor. Correcting it to \(\tau\nabla\log\mu_k\) resolves this equilibrium contradiction, but does not resolve [eq:badlemma] (#eq:badlemma).

The smooth Gaussian examples avoid all integration-by-parts boundary and initial-singularity ambiguities. A defensible repair must either prove the needed density regularity uniformly, state stronger initial/iterate assumptions with their constants, or use a different discretization estimate that does not require such uniform Fisher/Taylor control. No corrected general complexity theorem is claimed by this review.

Problem 3 (The Wasserstein coefficient is inverted).  Corollary 1, p. 7, writes \((2\tau/\alpha_1)(W_2^2(\mu,\mu^*)+
W_2^2(\nu,\nu^*))\leq\tau(\mathrm{KL}(\mu\mid\mu^*)+
\mathrm{KL}(\nu\mid\nu^*))\). This local conversion error is Minor.

For \(\mu=N(1,1)\), \(\nu=\rho\) and both targets \(\rho\), the distance sum is \(1\) and the KL sum is \(1/2\). With \(\alpha_1=1\), the displayed inequality requires \(2\tau\leq\tau/2\), which fails. The correct coefficient is \(\tau\alpha_1/2\). Appendix C, p. 23, states the correct underlying Talagrand inequality \(W_2^2\leq(2/\alpha_1)\mathrm{KL}\), providing direct counterevidence to an alternative LSI convention. The local coefficient repair is verified. The corollary also invokes the stochastic setup while retaining \(R_1\) rather than its noise-dependent analogue; a complete repaired corollary must inherit whatever valid stochastic remainder is ultimately established.

Problem 4 (Continuous-time noise uses the wrong time-scale factor).  Equation (4), p. 3, multiplies both drift and Brownian increment for \(\omega\) by \(\eta\). This is Minor relative to the discrete-time contribution.

For \(J=0\) the printed equation is \(d\omega=-\eta\tau\omega\,dt+\eta\sqrt{2\tau}\,dB\), whose invariant variance is \(\eta\), rather than the target Gaussian variance \(1\). For example \(\eta=2\) gives \(N(0,2)\). Scaling the noise by \(\sqrt{\eta}\), giving \(\sqrt{2\eta\tau}\,dB\), restores the target. Algorithm 1 uses the correct square-root dependence on its own ascent step size, so this observation does not itself refute that algorithm.

# Positive checks, dependence and remaining work

For the uncoupled Gaussian family, the best-response operators, nonnegative Lyapunov terms and their KL identities are exact. The bounded-perturbation LSI reasoning in Propositions 3–4 has the expected form: a perturbation bounded in oscillation by \(2G_0/\tau\) or \(2F_1m_0/\tau\) gives the stated exponentially reduced Gaussian LSI constant. The nonlinear best-response dependence and its smoothness must still be controlled in a repaired proof; the finite computations here do not certify all those steps.

The core contribution is the discrete-time last-iterate convergence analysis. Both a final stated theorem and its foundational one-step estimates fail on admissible cases, supporting Major independently of typographical defects. A weaker rate statement might survive after substantive proof repair; it is not established merely by replacing the factor two in Theorem 1. The application discussion supplies formulations and regularity examples, not empirical validation or a finite-particle convergence theorem.
