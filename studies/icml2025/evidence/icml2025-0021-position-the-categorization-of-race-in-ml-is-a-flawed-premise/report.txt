# 3. Problem 1 - PUMS availability is conflated with a benchmark recode

Source claim and location. Section 4, Approach E, PDF p. 4, states that multiple-race responses collected by the Census are subsequently simplified into single-label categories in ACS PUMS, and invokes this as a data-availability obstacle to multi-label treatment. Appendix C, p. 19, describes the coarse folktables race categories. The suspect step is extending the properties of the RAC1P recode to the information available in the underlying public microdata.

Complete check. The official 2022 ACS 1-year PUMS metadata independently defines RACWHT, RACBLK and RACASN as binary indicators for White, Black and Asian membership respectively, including membership in combination with other races. Each has distinct 0/1 values. RAC1P is a different variable with a combined multiple-race category. The public schema therefore retains combination-capable information in parallel with the coarse recode. These are metadata for a pre-publication data year, not a hypothetical newly proposed representation. [S2-S5]

The sampled public folktables implementation, acs.py lines 84-102, configures ACSIncome with RAC1P as its group variable. This supports the narrower statement that the default benchmark uses a coarse group label. It does not support a claim that PUMS itself lacks the separate indicators. The exact inspected code bytes are saved and hashed; the snapshot was retrieved from the current main branch and is not evidence of which historical revision the paper’s authors used. [S6]

Alternative reading and impact. Read strictly as a statement about a particular RAC1P-only benchmark export, the criticism is reasonable. Read as the stated property of ACS PUMS, it is false. The demonstrated impact is bounded: one practical argument against multi-label analysis is overstated. This check neither proves that all mixed identities are adequately represented nor refutes the paper’s ethical critique or establishes the performance of any alternative fairness method. Severity: Minor.

Supported correction. Specify that standard RAC1P-based folktables tasks collapse multi-race responses, while ACS PUMS also supplies separate race indicators; retaining and analyzing those indicators is distinct from selecting the default benchmark recode. This factual correction is verified against the official schema. No claim is made that it restores all social or methodological objectives of the paper. Author adoption has not been requested.

# 5. Checked reasoning and scope clarifications

Combinatorial statement (§4, p. 4). If each of k base labels may be included or excluded independently, and the empty combination is inadmissible, the possible distinct nonempty combinations number 2^k - 1. This follows by subtracting the empty subset from all 2^k subsets. Independent enumeration for k = 1,...,7 returned 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, 127, agreeing with the formula. The formula does not by itself prove that every combination must be instantiated or that all analyses require an exponentially large computation. No issue found in the stated counting step.

Subgroup fairness (§2, p. 2; §6.1, pp. 6-7). Kearns et al.’s formal setup accepts a protected feature vector and a family of Boolean subgroup indicators; categorical race variables are one possible instantiation, not a mathematical prerequisite. Its experimental example does use census-derived attributes. This distinction qualifies a broad reading of the target paper’s characterization but does not resolve which protected features are normatively appropriate. This is recorded as a scope clarification, not a separate central defect. [S8, §2 and §5.2]

Illustrative experiments (§§4-6; Appendices D-E). The reported mean cosine similarities have the stated ordering: the generated-minus-real differences are 0.0811 and 0.0382. These are arithmetic checks of printed summaries, not validation of the underlying embeddings or identification of phenotypic diversity. Pair construction, cropping, image-selection decisions, uncertainty and controls would be needed for that stronger inference. The Figure 1 significance markers could not be reproduced from the supplied summaries.

The VQA illustration records repeated responses to one face pair. It supports a description of those responses under that stimulus and prompt; it does not identify a latent perceptual mechanism or establish population-level discrimination. The text acknowledges important limits, including that the illustration does not imply systematic discrimination. No additional demonstrated error is assigned merely because fresh experiments would be needed to test a broader claim.
