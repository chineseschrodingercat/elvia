# 2. Operative definitions, assumptions and coverage

For an undirected graph with binary adjacency A and positive degree matrix D, write Lhat = I - D^(-1/2) A D^(-1/2), with spectrum in [0,2]. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, matrix norms in the perturbation arguments are spectral norms. Appendix A assumes connected graphs. The witnesses below are connected, unweighted, loop-free graphs with positive degrees. The paper distinguishes the following two architectures [R1, pp. 16–17, Eqs. (6)–(7)]:

\[H(G;w)=\phi\!\left(\sum_i[h_\alpha(P)\sigma(\sigma(XW_1)W_2)]_i\right),\qquad P=D^{-1}A,\] \[H(G;w)=\operatorname{Softmax}\!\left(h_\alpha(\widehat L)\sigma(\sigma(XW_1)W_2)\right).\] The inductive readout is real-valued; it is not constrained to [0,1]. The assumptions on sigma and phi in Theorems 4.8/D.5 are Lipschitz continuity, without sigma(0)=0, positive homogeneity, or a restriction to ReLU. Weight norms are bounded by c_W when the main bound is used. The risk setup allows a nonnegative loss, without the loss conditions from Shen et al. being stated [R1, pp. 9, 16–18]. The separate softmax in the transductive architecture cannot supply an unstated probability-range assumption for the inductive one.

The modified tree mover’s distance, TMD^L, uses depth-L computational trees, a zero-feature single-root blank tree for cardinality padding, and recursively transported child trees. At depth one its cost is the norm of the difference of features divided by accumulated ancestor degrees. At larger depths it does not add a root-feature comparison. The accumulated degree is 1 at the original root and is multiplied by the parent’s degree along each edge [R1, pp. 17–18, Definitions D.1–D.4]. The transport constraints give each tree unit mass: row and column sums are 1, not 1/n. This distinction is used in every distance calculation below.

Main Theorem 4.8 bounds the graph-output difference by c_W^2 K_phi K_sigma^2 max_k|alpha_k| times the sum of TMD depths 1 through K+1. D.5 uses the more precise coefficient-weighted sum. In Eq. (4), the same summed-depth ground cost defines the Wasserstein discrepancy between domains. We denote the hypothesis Lipschitz constant by K_Lip to distinguish it from polynomial order K. A stray d_min divisor appears in the prose after Eq. (13); it is 1 in the relevant witnesses and cannot remove them.

Coverage includes the main argument in Sections 1–6, definitions and operator conventions in Appendices A–C, the complete D.5 proof, figures and captions in Sections 3–4 and Appendix E, and the operative claims F.1, F.3–F.6. References directly needed to test imported assumptions were consulted as pinned primary-source versions [R2–R5]. The full bibliography was not exhaustively checked. The page images retained from the predecessor and the newly inspected p. 8 resolve formulas that text extraction can distort. # 3. Problem 1 — Theorem 4.8/D.5 fails for allowed activations (Major)

## Claim and suspect step

Theorem 4.8 [R1, pp. 8–9, Eq. (3)] and D.5 [p. 18, Eqs. (12)–(13)] claim a TMD control on the output of Eq. (6) for Lipschitz activation and readout. The proof’s decisive unsupported inference is the last inequality of Eq. (20), p. 20: it moves unequal accumulated degree divisions through the nonlinear feature transform using only its Lipschitz constant. That property controls F(x)-F(y), not F(x)/a-F(y)/b when a and b differ. Eq. (18) must not itself be condemned merely for using zero padding: padding missing transformed embeddings by zero can justify that identity. Compatibility with the raw-feature blank tree and the later nonlinear scaling remains unproved.

## Complete witness and assumption audit

Take G_a = K2 and G_b = P3, with scalar raw features zero on every vertex. Set W1=W2=1, phi(t)=t, sigma(t)=1/(1+exp(-t)), and h(t)=t^2. Both graphs satisfy the binary, connected, undirected, positive-degree setting. Their degree lists are (1,1) and (1,2,1). Sigma is globally 1/4-Lipschitz; phi is 1-Lipschitz; both weight norms are 1; the polynomial has genuine degree 2. The original definition permits different graph sizes and explicitly supplies padding for this case.

Write c = sigma(sigma(0)) = sigma(1/2) > 0. Since P maps the all-ones vector to itself, the transformed constant survives P^2. Sum readout therefore gives 2c and 3c. Every leaf feature of every computational or blank tree is zero, so every terminal cost is zero. Recursion and optimal transport give TMD^1=TMD^2=TMD^3=0, irrespective of a possible degree-reset interpretation. Thus both the weighted D.5 bound and the main summed-depth bound have a zero right side.

\[\big|H(G_a)-H(G_b)\big|=c=\frac{1}{1+e^{-1/2}}>0,\qquad \text{claimed upper bound}=0.\] The local failure can also be evaluated directly. For F(x)=sigma(sigma(x)), x=y=0, a=1 and b=2, |F(0)/1-F(0)/2|=c/2>0 whereas any proposed Lipschitz multiple of |0/1-0/2| is zero. These denominators occur in depth-three K2/P3 walks. As an exact-arithmetic corroboration, the allowed affine activation sigma(t)=t+1 gives node value 2 and graph outputs 4 and 6, while the same raw-tree distances remain zero.

## Alternative readings, dependency and repair status

Replacing the actual sum readout with a mean, silently requiring a zero-preserving homogeneous activation, or requiring equal node counts would change the claim. The present witness uses the published architecture and assumptions. The report does not infer experimental failure for an implementation that happened to use a more restricted activation. A separate zero-preserving clipped-activation calculation in Appendix B shows why simply adding sigma(0)=0 has not been verified as a complete repair of D.5.

This is Major because D.5 is the new stability derivation, its corollary is main Theorem 4.8, and Section 4.3 explicitly substitutes its coefficient-dependent constant into the inductive generalization argument. A core theoretical guarantee is false even though other perturbation bounds and localization observations survive. No full corrected theorem is certified. # 4. Problem 2 — Theorem 4.9 imports a bound without its loss conditions (Major)

## Claim and faithful comparison

The risk setup on p. 9 permits an arbitrary nonnegative loss. Theorem 4.9, Eq. (4), then bounds target risk by source risk plus 2 K_Lip W1 and the best joint source/target risk, citing Shen et al. The imported result uses an absolute-disagreement loss, [0,1]-valued hypotheses, a shared labeling function and a common Lipschitz constant over the class [R2, p. 5, Lemma 1 and Theorem 1]. Those are operative assumptions, not optional provenance details. The counterexample below targets the broader published claim in [R1], not [R2].

\[R_T(w)\le R_S(w)+2K_{\rm Lip}W_1(\mu_S,\mu_T)+\inf_{w'}\{R_S(w')+R_T(w')\}.\]

## Parameters, uniform constant and complete calculation

Use K2 in both domains, source features (2,2), target features (5/2,5/2), and common label 0. The architecture has identity activation and readout, W1=W2=1, and filters h_a(t)=a t^2 for 0<=a<=1. Evaluate the hypothesis a=1 with squared error loss. The graphs, bounded real features, weights, Lipschitz maps and nonnegative loss all meet the stated conditions. The evaluated polynomial has genuine degree 2.

A valid uniform K_Lip=1 is supplied independently of the failed D.5. Let the input space be all K2 graphs with scalar feature entries in [0,5/2]. Because both degrees are 1 and the depth trees are single paths, each of TMD^1, TMD^2 and TMD^3 equals the optimal unit-mass matching cost d_OT between the two feature multisets. For every a in the class, the difference between a times the two feature sums is at most d_OT, hence at most the summed-depth cost 3d_OT. Thus the whole class satisfies the required Lipschitz upper bound; this is not merely a two-point estimate.

Here P^2=I. Outputs are 4 and 5, so risks are 16 and 25. Every depth has two terminal feature differences of 1/2, hence TMD^1=TMD^2=TMD^3=1. The two domain measures are point masses, and W1=3. The joint-risk infimum is zero at a=0. Even if one insists every class member have strictly positive degree-two coefficient, use 0<a<=1 and the same infimum is approached as a tends to zero.

\[R_S=16,\quad R_T=25,\quad W_1=3,\quad \inf_{a}(R_S+R_T)=0,\qquad 25>16+2(1)(3)=22.\]

## Counterevidence, dependency and repair boundary

The labels do not change between domains; label shift is not the explanation. The error does not rely on Problem 1 because a common Lipschitz constant has been established directly. Squared error is allowed by [R1]’s nonnegative-loss setup and its real-valued inductive readout. Imposing the separate transductive softmax or the unstated [0,1] restriction would change that setup. Shen et al.’s theorem remains valid within the assumptions inspected here.

The source’s absolute-disagreement proof uses both a triangle inequality and a 2 K_Lip Lipschitz bound for disagreement. Those steps cannot simply be reused for arbitrary loss with the same constant. The failed conversion is central to Section 4.3’s claimed out-of-domain guarantee and its interpretation in terms of polynomial coefficients, so the supported impact is Major. A repair must state a defensible loss/class restriction and rederive constants, in addition to obtaining valid stability assumptions. No complete repaired generalization theorem is claimed. Evidence: [R1, p. 9; R2, p. 5], independent_checks.json and completion_checks.json.

# 5. Problem 3 — F.6 removes sampling fluctuation at zero radius (Major)

## Claim and assumptions

Theorem F.6 [R1, p. 23, Eq. (25)], explicitly invoked on p. 9, defines the closed parameter ball W_R={w: ||w-w0||<=R} and states a high-probability transductive gap bound proportional to R. It states no positive lower bound on R. Training/test indices are a uniform partition without replacement [p. 17]. The claimed term is:

\[\begin{aligned}
& O\!\left(\frac{(m+u)^{3/2}R\sqrt{L_1^2+L_2^2}}{mu}\right),\\
& L_1=\sqrt2\,c_Xc_W^2K_\sigma^2\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^K\|\widehat L^k\|_\infty\right)^{1/2},
\qquad L_2=2c_Xc_WK_\sigma^2\|h_\alpha(\widehat L)\|_\infty .
\end{aligned}\]

## An admissible fixed predictor

Take K2 with m=u=1, scalar feature 1 at both nodes, identity activation, W1=1, W2=(1,0), and h(t)=1+t^2. Let w0 contain exactly these parameters and R=0. The closed ball is a nonempty singleton; feature, weight and activation constants can all be 1. The graph is connected, the polynomial has genuine degree 2, and the weight dimensions match Eq. (7). Assign opposite binary labels to the two nodes and use standard nonnegative cross-entropy.

The normalized Laplacian is [[1,-1],[-1,1]], annihilating the constant feature. Both logits are (1,0) and both softmax probability vectors are (e/(e+1),1/(e+1)). The two losses are log(1+e^(-1)) and log(1+e), whose difference is exactly 1. Consequently the test-minus-train gap is +1 or -1 with probability 1/2 each.

\[\|I\|_\infty=1,\quad\|\widehat L\|_\infty=2,\quad\|\widehat L^2\|_\infty=4,\quad\|I+\widehat L^2\|_\infty=5;\qquad L_1=\sqrt{14},\quad L_2=10.\] All constants are finite, but the displayed bound is zero because R=0. At delta=1/4, a positive gap violates even a favorable one-sided upper-bound interpretation with probability 1/2, exceeding the allowed failure probability 1/4. Interpreting big-O as an absolute bound does not help.

## Asymptotic and source-theorem checks

The failure is not confined to a two-node finite-size case. For any odd m>=3, use the connected regular cycle C_(2m), the same constant features and predictor, m nodes of each label, and m training nodes. If J is the number of lower-loss nodes sampled for training, then J is hypergeometric with population 2m and m lower-loss nodes; the gap is (2J-m)/m. Symmetry under J -> m-J and odd m give P(gap>0)=1/2 at arbitrarily large graph sizes. R=0 still forces the displayed term to zero. Exact finite checks for m=1,3,5,9,21 agree with this analytic argument.

Even a positive-radius reading requires qualifications: under the usual interpretation that the displayed dependence is uniform in R, choose R=1/m and retain w=w0 in that ball. Bounds c_W=2 and fixed feature/activation constants work; the normalized-cycle operator norms stay bounded. The claimed term is O(m^(-3/2)), whereas every positive gap for odd m is at least 1/m and occurs with probability 1/2. This auxiliary observation is conditional on that uniform big-O interpretation; the primary R=0 witness uses the literal stated closed ball.

Tang and Liu [R3, p. 3] work with an open ball of radius r>=1, so the R=0 witness is outside their assumptions. Their Proposition 4.15 supplies loss Lipschitz constants; it is not by itself the displayed complete probabilistic statement. Their Eq. (28), p. 22, retains radius-independent sampling terms. Accordingly, this finding does not refute their original theorem. Adding appropriate radius restrictions and sampling/concentration terms would require a new complete derivation; none is certified here.

The supported severity is Major: main Section 4.3 uses F.6 as the transductive generalization guarantee supporting the central complexity/generalization position. A fixed predictor still has random train/test loss fluctuation, which the displayed bound eliminates. No deterioration of trained scores or author-code defect is inferred. Evidence: [R1, pp. 9, 17, 23; R3, pp. 3, 7, 22], independent_checks.json and completion_checks.json.

# 6. Problem 4 — Exact-k nodal-domain existence is false (Minor)

Source claim: the main discussion on p. 6 and Theorem F.3 on p. 23 assert that an eigenvector corresponding to the k-th eigenvalue exists with exactly k nodal domains [R1]. F.3 gives no simple-spectrum assumption and defines domains as maximal connected subgraphs on which sign does not change. The claim is stronger than the qualified upper-bound results in Davies, Leydold and Stadler [R4, pp. 2–3], which distinguish weak and strong nodal domains and multiplicity.

Use the connected unweighted triangle K3. Its normalized Laplacian is (3/2)I-(1/2)J, with spectrum 0,3/2,3/2. Every nonzero vector in the lambda_3 eigenspace has zero coordinate sum and therefore at least one positive and one negative entry. Both sign supports are connected cliques. There are exactly two strong sign domains; maximal weak domains also number two, with any zero entries allowed in the relevant maximal sign sets. A zero cannot create a third maximal domain. The eigenspace is spanned by (1,-1,0) and (1,1,-2), but the argument applies to every vector in it.

\[k=3,\qquad \lambda_3=3/2,\qquad \max_{0\ne v\in\ker(\widehat L-(3/2)I)}\{\text{nodal-domain count of }v\}=2<3.\] Repeated eigenvalues are allowed by the published statement. Choosing a different basis within that eigenspace cannot restore the exact-three assertion. This is a bounded background strengthening rather than a premise used in D.5, Eq. (4), or F.6. Qualified nodal-domain upper bounds can still support a narrower discussion of low-frequency structure. The supported impact is Minor on that dependency analysis, not because the claim is in an appendix.

Deleting the exact-existence assertion removes the demonstrated false subclaim. A complete replacement must still state the weak/strong-domain and eigenvalue-multiplicity qualifications; this review does not certify the remainder of F.3 verbatim. Evidence: [R1, pp. 6, 23; R4, pp. 2–3], independent_checks.json.

# 7. Problem 5 — A signed filter requires an absolute value (Major)

At the end of the first perturbation inequality on p. 7, the feature-perturbation term replaces ||h(Lhat)|| by sup over lambda in [0,2] of h(lambda) [R1]. The filter is real-valued, not constrained to be nonnegative; the paper also discusses signed responses. The preceding decomposition with the operator norm is valid.

Set Lhat’=Lhat to the normalized K2 Laplacian, h(lambda)=-1, x=(1,0), x’=0, and take the feature-norm bound at least 1. The left side is 1, the structure-perturbation term is zero, and the printed feature term is -1. Thus the displayed inequality asserts 1<=-1. The K>=2 condition in the later Theorem 4.4 does not govern this earlier general decomposition. If desired, the same failure with genuine degree 2 uses h(lambda)=-1-lambda^2 and x=(1,1)/sqrt(2), since Lhat x=0 and sup h=-1.

\[\|h(\widehat L)\|_2=\max_i|h(\lambda_i)|\le\sup_{\lambda\in[0,2]}|h(\lambda)|.\] This replacement follows directly from the symmetric spectral decomposition and restores the operator-norm step; the constant-filter witness then has corrected right side 1. The supported impact is Major: Section 4.1 identifies filter-response size as one of three stability concerns, and Section 4.2 immediately uses the unsigned maximum in its feature-perturbation conclusion. Although the preceding norm decomposition permits the intended magnitude reading, the accompanying maximum-value prose repeats the error, so this is an invalid derivation in a core stability argument. The preceding decomposition and the separate graph-structure bounds in Theorems 4.2 and 4.4 remain valid; the latter do not use this false replacement. The correction does not repair Problems 1-3 or turn an upper bound into necessary instability. Evidence: [R1, p. 7], source-p07.png and independent_checks.json.

# 8. Problem 6 — F.1 omits the operative cut-size restriction (Minor)

Theorem F.1 [R1, bottom of p. 22 continuing on p. 23] states lambda_2/2 <= h(G) <= sqrt(2 lambda_2), but defines h(G) as min over S subset V of |E(S,Sbar)|/(d|S|), with no half-size or half-volume restriction. To give the statement its most favorable meaningful reading, exclude empty and full subsets. A regular graph removes any ambiguity in the scalar degree d.

For connected unweighted K3, d=2 and lambda_2=3/2. A singleton has two cut edges and ratio 2/(2*1)=1. Any two-vertex proper subset also has two cut edges and ratio 2/(2*2)=1/2. These exhaust the six nonempty proper subsets. The printed minimum is therefore 1/2, while lambda_2/2=3/4. The lower inequality fails: 3/4 is greater than 1/2. The normalized-Laplacian convention and all regular-graph conditions hold.

The standard volume definition is h_vol=min cut(S,Sbar)/vol(S) over 0<vol(S)<=vol(V)/2, equivalently using min(vol(S),vol(Sbar)) in the denominator over all nonempty proper subsets. On a d-regular graph this requires 0<|S|<=|V|/2. It gives h_vol=1 on K3. The corrected conventional Cheeger statement is supported by Chung’s primary account [R5, printed pp. 754, 756; PDF pp. 4, 6]. The review independently verifies the lower inequality as follows.

\[\begin{aligned}
z&=D^{1/2}\!\left({\bf1}_S-\frac{\operatorname{vol}(S)}{\operatorname{vol}(V)}{\bf1}\right),
\qquad z\perp D^{1/2}{\bf1},\\
\lambda_2&\le\frac{z^\top\widehat Lz}{z^\top z}
=|\partial S|\!\left(\frac1{\operatorname{vol}(S)}+\frac1{\operatorname{vol}(\bar S)}\right)
\le\frac{2|\partial S|}{\operatorname{vol}(S)} .
\end{aligned}\] The last step uses precisely the missing half-volume condition. The upper Cheeger inequality is checked against the primary source, not independently reproved here. This is a local repair with explicit conditions, not a new generalization result. The defect changes a stated background theorem but is not used in the new stability or risk derivations; its supported severity is Minor. The predecessor left the normalization concern unresolved; this completion adds the actual regular-graph counterexample and supported definition repair. Evidence: completion_checks.py/json and [R5].

# Appendix A. Independent positive checks of the perturbation bounds

## A.1 Theorem 4.2 in the polynomial setting

Let Lhat=U Lambda U^T and E=V B V^T with ||E||<=epsilon. Let d=||V-U||, delta=(d+1)^2-1, E0=U B U^T, and F=E-E0. Orthogonality and expansion give F=(V-U)B(V-U)^T+(V-U)BU^T+UB(V-U)^T, hence ||F||<=delta epsilon. E0 commutes with Lhat.

For a fixed real polynomial h that is scalar C-Lipschitz on [0,2], the matrix polynomial expansion gives h(Lhat+E)-h(Lhat)=Dh_Lhat[E]+O(epsilon^2). In the U basis, the derivative acts entrywise by divided differences of h; each has absolute value at most C, including derivative limits at repeated eigenvalues. The commuting part has norm at most C epsilon. Bounding the remaining part first in Frobenius norm gives:

\[\begin{aligned}
\|Dh_{\widehat L}[F]\|_2&\le\|Dh_{\widehat L}[F]\|_F\le C\|F\|_F\le C\sqrt n\,\delta\varepsilon,\\
\|h(\widehat L+E)-h(\widehat L)\|_2&\le C(1+\delta\sqrt n)\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2).
\end{aligned}\] This independently verifies the stated form [R1, p. 7] in the polynomial context. The remainder constant may depend on the fixed polynomial and dimension; it is not asserted to depend only on C. This proof does not establish the same second-order remainder for an arbitrary merely Lipschitz nonpolynomial function, and it is not represented as a replay of every argument in the paper’s cited source.

# Appendix A continued. Theorem 4.4

For normalized symmetric Laplacians A and A’, both spectral norms are at most 2. The telescoping identity for A’^k-A^k does not require A and A’ to commute, and yields ||A’^k-A^k||<=k 2^(k-1) epsilon when ||A’-A||<=epsilon. Consequently:

\[\|h(A')-h(A)\|\le\sum_{k=1}^K|\alpha_k|\,k2^{k-1}\varepsilon
\le\|\widetilde\alpha\|_1K2^{K-1}\varepsilon.\] The p. 8 source image confirms that Theorem 4.4’s coefficient is ||alpha_tilde||_1 (K^2-1)/8 [2(K+1)/(K-1)]^K. For nonzero nonconstant coefficient norm, its ratio to the tighter coefficient just obtained is:

\[\frac{K^2-1}{4K}\left(\frac{K+1}{K-1}\right)^K
\ge\frac38\left(1+\frac2{K-1}\right)^K
>\frac98>1,\qquad K\ge2.\] Indeed, the first factor is at least 3/8, and Bernoulli’s inequality bounds the power below by 1+2K/(K-1)>3. A zero nonconstant coefficient norm is the trivial constant-filter case. Thus the printed looser bound is valid in the normalized symmetric setting. Exact finite-ratio checks for K=2 through 12 corroborate, but do not replace, this all-K proof. No extension to an arbitrary nonnormal operator is claimed.

# Appendix B. Scoped repair diagnostic and actual calculations

A preserved diagnostic tests adding only sigma(0)=0 to D.5. Use sigma(t)=clip(t,0,1), which is zero-preserving, 1-Lipschitz and idempotent; identity readout; unit weights; and h(t)=t. G_a is K2 with features (1,0). G_b is P3 with center feature 0 and leaf features 2 and 0. Under the accumulated-degree definition, the depth-two terminal feature multisets on K2 are {0}, {1}, and a blank; those on P3 are {0}, {1,0}, {0}. Zero padding admits a matching of zero cost, so TMD^2=0. After clipping, the graph sums of P times the transformed features are 1 and 1/2. The coefficient-weighted D.5 bound is zero and fails.

This diagnostic has two explicit limits. It uses degrees inherited from the original computation tree; a resetting interpretation changes that calculation.
