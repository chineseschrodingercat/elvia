Abstract: The fixed finite-difference step in the manuscript does not support its claimed local error order. The acquired code instead implements a valid midpoint method. Empirical comparisons and feature sharing are examined with explicit limits on causal attribution and historical-version linkage.\par
\section*{Scope and assessment}
This review covers I25-43737, Taming Rectified Flow for Inversion and Editing, in its 15-page PMLR version and the acquired RF-Solver-Edit release. Assessment: Major, specifically for the claimed local error order of the fixed-difference algorithm described in Section 3.2. The inspected release uses a different, valid second-order midpoint construction. This is important counterevidence: the mathematical defect below does not establish that the released implementation or historical experiments had first-order convergence. Empirical advantages remain unreplicated and partly confounded by backbone and evaluation choices.\par
\section*{Differencing error changes the stated order}
Problem 1 (Major: fixed \(\Delta t\) does not give the claimed \(O(h^3)\) local error).  Section 3.2, page 4, specifies \(\Delta t=0.01\) for estimating the first derivative and then claims reduction of each step’s error from \(O(h^2)\) to \(O(h^3)\). The derivative approximation introduces an additional \(O(h^2\Delta t)\) term. It cannot be discarded when \(\Delta t\) is fixed as \(h\to0\).\par
Write \(v=v(x,t)\) and \(h=t_{i-1}-t_i\), allowing either sign. Along the exact ODE solution, the total derivative is \[Dv=\partial_t v+(\partial_xv)v.\] The Euler probe \(x_\delta=x+\delta v(x,t)\) gives, for smooth \(v\), \[\frac{v(x+\delta v,t+\delta)-v(x,t)}\delta=Dv+O(\delta).\] Substitution in (8) therefore gives a local defect \(O(h^3)+O(h^2\delta)\). The integral identity (3), Taylor integration with exact total derivatives, and the signed \(h^2/2\) term are valid; it is the replacement of the exact derivative by a fixed finite difference that changes the argument.\par
An exact scalar witness is \(x'=t^2\). From any \((x,t)\), the numerical increment is \(ht^2+h^2t+\delta h^2/2\), whereas the exact increment is \(ht^2+h^2t+h^3/3\). Their difference is \[E(h,\delta)=\frac{\delta h^2}{2}-\frac{h^3}{3}.\] For \(\delta=0.01\), \(E/h^2\to0.005\), rather than zero, and \(|E|/|h|^3\) is unbounded. The scalar network field need not be exotic for the issue to occur. As a specifically rectified-flow example, independently draw \(X_0,X_1\sim N(0,1)\). Conditional regression of \(X_1-X_0\) on \(X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1\) gives \[v(x,t)=\frac{2t-1}{t^2+(1-t)^2}x,
 \qquad x(t)=\sqrt{t^2+(1-t)^2}\quad(x(0)=1).\] Independent integration to \(t=0.8\) with \(N=128,\ldots,4096\) confirms that the fixed-\(0.01\) scheme approaches first-order global convergence (last observed order \(0.987\)). Setting \(\delta=h/2\) is at least second order; this particular RF field exhibits additional cancellation and an observed order near 3, which is not a general guarantee. For the polynomial field \(v=t^2\) on \([0,1]\), the exact midpoint global error is \(-1/(12N^2)\), confirming its general second-order behavior. Both exact polynomial calculations and the smooth population-RF check are saved; no author code was executed. As usual, local-to-global order statements require appropriate smoothness, stability and bounded derivatives over the interval.\par
Dependency and repair. The improved local order is the central mathematical justification for RF-Solver and its inversion form (9), so this is more than a cosmetic remainder typo. Taking \(\delta=O(h)\), with a suitable signed probe, repairs the missing order condition. The paper should state that condition, reconcile the experimental step specification, and distinguish a formal order result from guaranteed improvement at a fixed coarse NFE. A higher-order method can have a larger error constant or stability restriction on a particular coarse grid. Also, the fixed positive probe evaluates \(t+0.01>1\) at the first denoising point \(t=1\), beyond the stated training-time interval; whether a trained model behaves well there is not established.\par
\section*{The acquired implementation is an explicit midpoint method}
The acquired commit is `7ac380143379b656eca5398157f41f97ef1ef2f9`, dated 1 May 2025. In the FLUX implementation, `src/flux/sampling.py` lines 118–134 set \(\delta=h/2\), evaluate \(k_1=v(x,t)\) and \(k_2=v(x+hk_1/2,t+h/2)\), and use \[x+h k_1+\tfrac12h^2\frac{k_2-k_1}{h/2}=x+h k_2.\] This is exactly the explicit midpoint Runge–Kutta formula in exact arithmetic; the derivative correction is an algebraic rewriting. The HunyuanVideo scheduler’s midpoint and final updates (lines 323–326 and 389–394) use the same construction, with the pipeline evaluating the midpoint at lines 1038–1070. The signed midpoint remains inside each integration interval and satisfies the order condition identified above. Floating-point cancellation in the expanded form is a separate issue not quantified here; the simpler \(x+hk_2\) expression avoids that subtraction/division.\par
The implementation is thus evidence for a plausible repair, not evidence that the paper’s fixed-\(0.01\) version has the asserted order. Which version generated each table needs paper-matched configuration or commit evidence. Calling the release a second-order solver is justified under the usual smooth-ODE assumptions. Its relationship to the midpoint formula should be explicit when describing the solver contribution; this algebraic observation alone is not a literature-priority judgment.\par
Reversing the sign of \(h\) gives an approximation to the inverse flow, not an exact discrete inverse. For \(x'=x\), midpoint has multiplier \(1+h+h^2/2\); a forward/backward pair multiplies by \(1+h^4/4\), which is \(1.000025\) at \(h=0.1\). This agrees with the paper’s small-step qualification and does not contradict reduced reconstruction error. In addition, identical forward/reverse vector fields and conditioning are necessary to isolate integration error. The editing entry point uses guidance 1 for inversion and a configurable target guidance for denoising (edit.py 157–164), as is reasonable for editing, but that path alone cannot establish a pure inversion-error experiment.\par
\section*{Feature sharing and empirical evidence}
The FLUX value replacement in `src/flux/modules/layers.py` 247–255 implements the key idea of (14): retain current queries and keys while substituting stored inversion values. Figure 3’s distinction between FLUX single blocks and OpenSora spatial attention was checked visually. The inspected entry point and sampler also confirm two network calls per integration step, supporting the stated equal-NFE comparison with Euler: 5 two-stage steps correspond to 10 calls for generation and 25 correspond to 50 for one reconstruction direction. Memory and feature-transfer overhead are not captured by NFE. The published OpenSora experiment implementation was not in the acquired source-only release, so its historical details cannot be inferred from the HunyuanVideo extension.\par
Needs Evidence: attribution of the sampler comparison. Appendix B.1 explicitly states that DPM-Solver is run on Stable Diffusion, whereas RF, Heun and RF-Solver use FLUX. Table 1’s DPM-Solver++ comparison therefore changes model, training and default generation settings as well as numerical integrator. It is a whole-system comparison, not evidence that the proposed sampler outperforms DPM-Solver on a common vector field. The FLUX-versus-FLUX comparisons remain relevant. FID changes from 24.40 for Heun to 23.98 for RF-Solver and CLIP score from 31.03 to 31.09; paired seeds, schedule/guidance details and uncertainty are needed to assess how stable these smaller differences are. No arithmetic contradiction in those reported values was found.\par
Table 5 controls the feature-sharing mechanism across its solver ablation and holds NFE fixed, which is useful. Its third-order variant has slightly lower FID (23.94 versus 23.98) and equal displayed CLIP score, so the prose that higher order gives no enhancement should be read as no consistent overall advantage. Reconstruction and editing metrics favor the second-order choice. The higher-derivative construction and its independent order verification are not supplied in sufficient detail here for a complete audit.\par
Needs Evidence: editing success versus appearance preservation. Table 3 correctly acknowledges that low source-to-edit LPIPS can reward failure to edit. Table 4’s subject consistency, motion smoothness, aesthetic quality and imaging quality do not directly measure target-prompt edit success or preservation specifically outside edited regions. A source-video identity output can score well on temporal and appearance dimensions while not making the requested edit. Figures 5–9 supply useful examples but do not establish dataset-wide semantic success. Target-aware metrics or blinded judgments with exact prompts and selection rules would resolve this. Appendix B also allows manually refined prompts and tuned sharing steps; a held-out tuning protocol and raw item list are needed before attributing aggregate gains solely to the method. This is an unresolved evaluation limitation, not a claim of manipulated data.\par
\section*{Scope and limitations}
The scientific main text on pages 1–8 and Appendices A–F on 13–15 were read. Figures 2–10 and Tables 1–5 were actually inspected at the cited pages; Figure 1’s caption was read but its panel details were not independently inspected. References on 9–12 were not exhaustively checked. Static source coverage includes the FLUX schedule, two-stage sampler, value injection and editing entry point, plus the HunyuanVideo midpoint scheduler and associated pipeline calls. The remaining code, alternate guidance branches, model checkpoints, quantitative evaluation scripts and original videos were not executed or fully audited. Viewing selected video frames is not a temporal playback inspection. No claim is made about historical experiment failures.\par
Adjudication is pending, human reviews are empty, and no independent acceptance is claimed.\par
