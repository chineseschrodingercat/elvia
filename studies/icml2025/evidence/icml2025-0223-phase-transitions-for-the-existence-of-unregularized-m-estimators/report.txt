Abstract: The reviewed publisher version contains an inconsistent negative-part convention in the projection underpinning its phase-transition proofs. A balanced null logistic model satisfying the stated assumptions makes the displayed projection infeasible and contradicts its claimed moment and distance identities exactly. This supports a Major assessment of the core derivation. It does not refute the threshold formula or the final theorems: a local sign correction restores the inspected projection identities. Full verification of a revised proof remains outstanding; scientific adjudication is pending.

# A demonstrated error in the central projection

Problem 1 (Major: the displayed projection violates its defining cone).  The paper explicitly defines \(z_-=\max(-z,0)\) on p. 3. Lemma A.1, p. 9, requires \(tU+p\leq0\) on \(\Omega_\nearrow\), but defines \(p(t)=-tU+(G+tU)_-\) there. It asserts cone membership, \(\varphi(t)=\mathbb E[(G-p(t))^2]\), and at the optimizer \(\mathbb E[p_*^2]=\mathbb E[Gp_*]\). These assertions are incompatible with the stated convention.

## An admissible complete test

Let \(U,G\) be independent standard normals and let \(Y\) be an independent balanced sign. Use \[\ell_y(u)=\log(1+e^{-yu}),\qquad
\Omega_\nearrow=\{Y=-1\},\quad\Omega_\searrow=\{Y=1\}.\] Each loss is \(C^1\), strictly convex and nonconstant. Its infimum is zero and its Gaussian-input expectation is finite, since \(\ell_y(u)\leq\log2+|u|\). There are no coercive losses, so the probability requirement is satisfied. The two positive expectations in Assumption 2.4 each equal \(1/2\): independence gives one half of the second moment of \(U\). The growth condition holds with \(b=1,D(Y)=1\), using \(\log(1+e^u)\geq u\) and \(\log(1+e^{-u})\geq-u\). Both required moments of \(D\) are finite. This is an allowed single-index model with a constant response link, not a noiseless separable exception.

Symmetry and independence give \(\varphi(t)=(1+t^2)/2\), so \(t_*=0,\delta_\infty=2\). The printed candidate is \[p_*=G_-I_{\{Y=-1\}}+G_+I_{\{Y=1\}}.\] On \(\{Y=-1,G<0\}\), of probability \(1/4\), it is positive where the cone requires it to be nonpositive. Exact half-normal moments give \[\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[p_*^2]&=\tfrac12,&
\mathbb E[Gp_*]&=\tfrac12(-\tfrac12)+\tfrac12(\tfrac12)=0,\\
\mathbb E[(G-p_*)^2]&=\tfrac32\ne\varphi(0)=\tfrac12,&
\mathcal G(p_*)&=1/\sqrt2>0.\end{aligned}\] Thus the asserted direction is infeasible even at the allowed boundary \(\delta=\delta_\infty=2\). An independently written 60-node Gauss–Hermite calculation in `check.py`/`calc.json` confirms respectively \(0.5,\ 2.03\cdot10^{-17},\ 1.5\). The analytical identities, rather than quadrature accuracy, establish the contradiction.

## Dependency and consequence

The proof of Lemma A.1 on p. 10 also uses \(z_-^2=z_-z\), which is false for the defined nonnegative negative part. The finite-dimensional projection on p. 13 repeats the same sign. Lemma E.1, pp. 18–19, uses the false moment identity to certify the nonexistence direction’s feasibility in [eq:constraint] (#eq:constraint). Its claimed bound on the ray loss fails too: at \(Y=-1,G=-1\), \(\ell_{-1}(p_*)=\log(1+e)=1.313262>\ell_{-1}(0)=0.693147\). The strict inequality persists on a positive-probability set.

For the existence branch, Lemma E.2, p. 20, forms \(\widetilde v=-aU+(aU+v)_-\) on increasing losses and asserts \((a,\widetilde v)\in C\). With \(a=0,v=G,\delta=3\), the input is feasible because \(\mathcal G(G)=1-\sqrt{3/2}=-0.224745<0\). Its expected loss is at most \(\log2+\sqrt{2/\pi}<2\), so it lies in an allowed sublevel set. Yet the constructed \(\widetilde v\) again violates the cone on \(\{Y=-1,G<0\}\). The projection inequality (25), followed by (26)–(27) on p. 21, therefore lacks its asserted premise. These steps support both sides of Theorem 3.4 and hence the nonlinear system’s phase transition in Theorem 2.7; the finite-dimensional proof of Theorem 2.6 also uses the projection. This is a consequential invalid core derivation, even though it has an evident local repair.

## Counterevidence and verified local correction

The surrounding formulas evidently intend the signed convention \(z_-=\min(z,0)\). That alternative explains the error and is strong counterevidence against treating the theorem statements as false. The original rendered pages confirm that the conflict is not text extraction.

Equation reference: eq:correct. Keeping the explicit nonnegative convention, the pointwise projection is \[
p_c(t)=-tU-(G+tU)_-I_{\Omega_\nearrow}
                    +(G+tU)_+I_{\Omega_\searrow}.\] The additive term is zero on \(\Omega_\vee\). Directly projecting \(G\) onto the respective half-lines/equality proves cone membership and \(\|G-p_c(t)\|_2^2=\varphi(t)\). At the null-model optimum, the corrected squared norm, correlation and squared distance are all \(1/2\); feasibility at \(\delta=2\) is restored. The same minus sign is required in the increasing-loss branch of \(\widetilde v\). The threshold [eq:phi] (#eq:phi) is unchanged because it uses squared negative parts.

A global convention swap alone is insufficient to verify every proof line. On p. 19 the final contradiction uses equal positive expectations for \(G_-\) and \(G_+\); signed negative parts instead have opposite expectations. With the nonnegative convention each mean is \(1/\sqrt{2\pi}\), not the printed \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\). A valid local replacement is to square the asserted zero random variable on the disjoint loss-type events, yielding \[0=\tfrac12\{\mathbb P(\Omega_\nearrow)+\mathbb P(\Omega_\searrow)\}>0,\] a contradiction. These local repairs restore the inspected algebra; they are not a certification of a completely revised proof.

# Other checks and scope boundaries

For a response independent of \(U\), with \(c=\mathbb P(\Omega_\vee)\), symmetry gives \(\varphi(t)=(1+c)(1+t^2)/2\). Thus the critical plotted ratio is \(p/n=\delta_\infty^{-1}=(1+c)/2\). For balanced binomial responses with \(q\) trials it is \(1-2^{-q}\); for Poisson mean one it is \(1-e^{-1}/2=0.8160602794\). The binomial values at \(q=1,2,4,8,16\) are \(0.5,0.75,0.9375,0.99609375,0.9999847412\). Loss classifications and the reciprocal conversion between the theory’s \(n/p\) and plots’ \(p/n\) are coherent.

The KKT-to-proximal-system calculations on pp. 15–16 give \(\|\sigma G-v\|^2=\delta^{-1}\sigma^2\) and \(\mathbb E[G(v+aU)]=\sigma(1-\delta^{-1})\) as stated. No additional substantive defect was established in the inspected nondegeneracy or uniqueness strategy. Theorem 2.6 omits the critical equality case for asymptotic estimator existence, whereas Theorem 2.7 addresses a different infinite-dimensional object and includes equality on its nonexistence side; this is not a contradiction.

Figure 2’s caption states \(n=1500\), while Section 4 states \(n=1000\). The actual simulation configuration and linear-programming tolerance remain unresolved; no change in a numerical conclusion is established. No raw experiments were reproduced.
