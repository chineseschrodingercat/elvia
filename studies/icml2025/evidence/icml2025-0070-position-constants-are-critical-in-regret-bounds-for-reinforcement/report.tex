\#\# Outcome: Major\par
The published derivation of the central refined regret guarantee contains a false bonus substitution in the optimism proof and incorrect reciprocal-root summation constants. The first failure has an explicit admissible MDP execution: the claimed intermediate lower bound is about 0.05396 while the actual value difference is about 0.01350. \# Problem 1 | A larger bonus enters the proof\par
\#\# Source claim and exact mismatch\par
Theorem 3.2, pp.3-4 and its Appendix F restatement on p.35, defines the Bernstein-Freedman bonus with correction term C below. Lemma F.1 also omits an exterior factor L in its correction, although its minimum is outside the expectation. Write N=max\{N\_k(x,a),1\}, and let expectation be under the empirical next-state distribution.\par
\[\begin{aligned}L&=\log(5HSAT/\delta),\\ b'(y)&=\min\!\left\{\frac{84^2H^3S^2AL^2}{\max\{1,N'_{k,h+1}(y)\}},H^2\right\},\\ C&=\sqrt{\frac{4\mathbb E_{\widehat P}b'}{N}}.\end{aligned}\] In Eq.(74), p.40, the substitution for this same bonus instead contains sqrt(4 L E\_Phat[b’]/N)=sqrt(L) C. The subsequent variance comparison on p.41 relies on this enlarged term to cancel the term containing L times the squared value-estimation gap. Since L>1 and b’ is positive for finite counts, the inserted term is strictly larger. The L\textasciicircum{}2 already inside b’ does not supply the additional exterior L.\par
\[\begin{aligned}\text{Stated:}\quad &\sqrt{4L\widehat{\mathcal V}/N}+C-\sqrt{2L\widehat{\mathcal V}^{*}/N},\\ \text{Eq. (74):}\quad &\sqrt{4L\widehat{\mathcal V}/N}+\sqrt L\,C-\sqrt{2L\widehat{\mathcal V}^{*}/N}.\end{aligned}\]\par
\#\# Dependency and severity\par
Lemma F.1 is the optimism step used to replace true regret by optimistic regret and invoke the bonus/model-error bounds in Theorem 3.2. The substitution changes the quantity being analyzed. A complete execution on the next page makes the printed lower bound itself false, rather than merely identifying a notational ambiguity. Major is warranted by this invalid derivation of a central technical guarantee; empirical score deterioration is not required for that judgment.\par
\#\# Alternative readings and historical attribution\par
There is no stated convention that absorbs the extra L: the main bonus, repeated lemma statement and released correction term agree in omitting it. The counterexample is also insensitive to the H\textasciicircum{}2/H\textasciicircum{}3 inconsistency and min/expectation order because it has one state and the b’ cap is active. Retaining a nonnegative future-value gap cannot justify the enlarged lower bound in that execution, where the future gap is zero.\par
Azar et al. (2017), main Algorithm 4 and supplement Lemma 19, already contain a related missing-L step: their variance-difference lower bound drops L. The current issue therefore has inherited lineage; the 2025 proof inserts L into the correction instead. Both primary versions and exact locations are preserved. No allegation about intent or historical discovery priority is made.\par
\#\# Repair status\par
Adding L to the algorithm’s correction would restore this particular cancellation but changes the algorithm and its bonus sum. It does not validate the published constants. A new proof for the stated bonus, or a fully reanalyzed changed bonus, is needed. Neither complete repair was established by this review.\par

\# Problem 2 | Incorrect summation constants\par
\#\# Confirmed scalar failures\par
Lemma D.7, p.25 after Eq.(34), turns 2H\textasciicircum{}2 sqrt(SL) times a state-action sum of n\textasciicircum{}(-1/2) into H\textasciicircum{}2 S sqrt(A T\_k L). With the displayed balanced upper limit N=T\_k/(SA), this requires the false bound sum\_\{n=1\}\textasciicircum{}N n\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)<=sqrt(N)/2. The same factor error occurs in term (c) on that page and in D.9, p.31, Eqs.(56)-(57). Appendix E, pp.34-35, instead effectively uses sum n\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)<=sqrt(N).\par
\[\begin{aligned}\sum_{n=1}^N n^{-1/2}&\ge\sqrt N>\frac{\sqrt N}{2}&& (N\ge1),\\ \sum_{n=1}^N n^{-1/2}&>\sqrt N&& (N>1).\end{aligned}\] The first inequality holds because every summand is at least 1/sqrt(N); no probabilistic or MDP assumption can make this scalar step valid. For a concrete large-count choice, take H=2,S=2,A=1,K=1000000,delta=0.05. Then T=2000000, N=1000000 and L=20.5001223; K exceeds 250HS\textasciicircum{}2AL. The sum is 1998.54014549 while the D.7 implied upper bound is 500. This diagnostic concerns the displayed intermediate sum, not an assertion that a stochastic variance attains that sum.\par
\#\# Verified local correction and downstream dependency\par
\[\sum_{n=1}^Nn^{-1/2}\le1+\int_1^N x^{-1/2}\,dx=2\sqrt N-1\le2\sqrt N.\] Using this bound in the displayed reindexing changes D.7’s coefficients from 1+4+2 to 4+4+8, giving a conservative combined coefficient 16 instead of 7. D.7 feeds the empirical-variance sum in D.9, which feeds Eq.(75) and the 24e/616e constants in Theorem 3.2. Appendix E has an additional bonus substitution: its summation uses 2H sqrt(L/N), although Theorem 3.1 specifies 2HL/sqrt(N). These are material errors in a contribution explicitly focused on constants.\par
\#\# Counterevidence and partial repair limits\par
The final constants are not proved false by these failures. Conditional on the remaining counting steps, replacing 7 by 16 still gives an Eq.(51) normalized envelope of about 2.1438 at the typical threshold, below its stated 4. A corrected CH envelope can also fit within the main 10HL term for nontrivial H>=2 and L>=log(20). These explicit slack checks are retained rather than asserting that every final coefficient must increase.\par
Other intermediate counting conventions need separate work: D.9 drops a stage index when aggregating stage-specific harmonic sums, and its b’ definition varies between H\textasciicircum{}2 and H\textasciicircum{}3. The scalar correction alone does not validate those steps, the probability event, or Problem 1. Major describes the invalid derivation of central constant-sensitive results; a complete repaired theorem remains unverified.\par

\# Problem 3 | Released implementation differences\par
\#\# A different variance is computed\par
The official repository linked by the publisher is pinned to commit `3434b55b735d674d144eefce392b545425816ca1`. In agents.py, UCBVI.newEpisode (lines 79, 83) and MVP.newEpisode (line 216) compute population variance across the entries p\_i V\_i. The formulas in the paper require the variance of V under probability weights p.\par
\[\mu=\sum_i p_iV_i,\qquad \operatorname{Var}_{p}(V)=\sum_i p_i(V_i-\mu)^2.\]\par
{\ttfamily\footnotesize Released statistic: np.std(Phat[s,a,:] * Vstar[:,h+1])**2}\par
Take p=(1/2,1/2), V=(1,0), admissible next-stage values with deterministic rewards and H>=2. The required variance is 1/4. The entrywise products are (1/2,0), whose population variance is 1/16. More generally, for uniform p over S states, the released statistic is the required variance divided by S\textasciicircum{}2; the variance-dependent bonus component is therefore divided by S. This is a mathematical function mismatch, not a numerical tolerance issue.\par

\#\# Concerns narrowed by primary counterevidence\par
The allegation that the compared MVP never discards samples was rejected. Zhang et al. (2024), Section 3, Remark 1, explicitly describes a modified MVP using the newest half of the data at each doubling, while distinguishing the original cumulative-data variant. The released baseline resets its batch counts consistently with that description. This supports the concrete cited variant; it does not imply all doubling schemes must discard data.\par
Lemma D.2, p.17, removes stage-dependent weights from a sum containing signed martingale terms. A scalar recurrence shows that the generic algebraic inference needs justification, and the same step appears in Azar et al. (2017). However, a fully admissible UCBVI witness to that lemma was not established. It is retained as Needs Evidence, with a potential weighted-martingale repair unverified, rather than counted as a false theorem.\par
