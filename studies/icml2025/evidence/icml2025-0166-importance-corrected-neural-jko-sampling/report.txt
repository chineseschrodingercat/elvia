# Scientific reconstruction and assumptions

Equation reference: eq:jko. The paper represents a JKO update by a continuous normalizing flow and interleaves learned transport with importance-based rejection corrections. The exact proximal update is \[
 \mu_\tau^{k+1}\in\arg\min_\mu
 \left\{\mathcal F(\mu)+\frac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\mu,\mu_\tau^k)\right\}.\] The theoretical velocity fields in Theorem 3.3 are minimizers of the exact dynamic problem. They should not be identified with arbitrary approximate neural-network optimizers.

Assumption 3.1 requires \(\mathcal F:\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\) to be proper, narrowly lower semicontinuous, coercive, bounded below and \(\lambda\)-convex along generalized geodesics. Its finite-slope domain is required to lie in \(\mathcal P_2^{ac}\), and the initial absolutely continuous measure must have finite metric slope. Theorem 3.3 asserts strong convergence of the exact concatenated velocities as \(\tau\downarrow0\). Corollary 3.2 identifies their interpolated curves with geodesic interpolation.

The rejection correction is a separate contribution. For acceptance function \(\alpha\) and \(a=\mathbb E_\mu\alpha\), its density has the form \[\widetilde p(x)=p(x)\bigl(\alpha(x)+1-a\bigr).\] It is normalized, and can be written as \(a\eta+(1-a)\mu\) with \(\eta\) the normalized accepted distribution when \(a>0\). The auxiliary review checked the corresponding mixture algebra and the stated KL argument under the paper’s exact-distribution assumptions. This does not certify numerical ODE densities, estimated normalizers or trained neural flows.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Missing time-step factor in the strong-convergence proof).  Assessment: Major. Appendix B.3, PDF p. 20, uses an invalid action identity in the proof supporting Theorem 3.3, a central theoretical contribution.

## Original claim and dependency

Equation reference: eq:action, eq:correctaction. Corollary 3.2(i), p. 5, correctly states \[
 W_2^2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1})
 =\tau\int_{k\tau}^{(k+1)\tau}
     \|v_\tau(\cdot,t)\|_{L^2(\gamma_\tau(t))}^2\,dt .\] The velocity convention in Eq. (9), p. 16, is also \((T_\tau^k-I)/\tau\). Nevertheless, the p. 20 proof replaces the total integral by an unscaled sum of squared distances. With \(K\) steps the correct identity is \[
 A_\tau:=\int_0^{K\tau}\|v_\tau\|^2\,dt
 =\frac1\tau\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}
       W_2^2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1}).\] There is also a local index mismatch in the displayed sum. The missing factor, rather than the index typography, is the material issue.

## An admissible exact witness

Take \(d=1\), \(\nu=\mathcal N(0,1)\), \(\mathcal F(\mu)=\mathrm{KL}(\mu\Vert\nu)\), and \(\mu^0=\mathcal N(1,1)\). Relative entropy to this Gaussian is nonnegative, proper, narrowly lower semicontinuous, coercive and \(1\)-convex in the stated sense. Its finite-slope domain is absolutely continuous; the initial squared slope is \(1\). Thus this is within the paper’s assumptions.

The global minimizer in [eq:jko] (#eq:jko) is \(\mathcal N(m/(1+\tau),1)\) when the previous measure is \(\mathcal N(m,1)\). This is not merely a restriction to an arbitrarily chosen Gaussian subfamily. For a competitor of mean \(a\), write \(\bar\mu\) for its centered translate. Then \[\begin{aligned}
 W_2^2(\mu,\mathcal N(m,1))
  &=(a-m)^2+W_2^2(\bar\mu,\mathcal N(0,1)),\\
 \mathrm{KL}(\mu\Vert\mathcal N(0,1))
  &=\frac{a^2}{2}+\mathrm{KL}(\bar\mu\Vert\mathcal N(0,1)).\end{aligned}\] The centered terms are nonnegative and jointly minimized by \(\bar\mu=\mathcal N(0,1)\). Minimizing the remaining scalar quadratic gives \(a=m/(1+\tau)\).

For \(\tau=1/2\), \(K=2\) and \(T=1\), the successive means are \(1,2/3,4/9\). Consequently \[\sum_{k=0}^1W_2^2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1})
 =\frac19+\frac4{81}=\frac{13}{81},
 \qquad A_\tau=\frac{26}{81}.\] The p. 20 equality is therefore false by a factor of two in this instance. More decisively, for \(\tau=1/K\), \[A_\tau=\frac{1-(1+\tau)^{-2K}}{2+\tau}
 \longrightarrow \frac{1-e^{-2}}2>0,
 \qquad \sum_k W_2^2=\tau A_\tau\longrightarrow0.\] The unscaled sum and the actual action have different limits.

## Why restoring the factor does not complete the printed proof

The proof uses endpoint error \(W_2(\mu_\tau^k,\gamma(k\tau))\le C\tau\). Put \(b_k=W_2(\gamma(k\tau),\gamma((k+1)\tau))\) and \(I_\tau=\int_0^{K\tau}\|\widehat v\|^2\,dt\). After restoring [eq:correctaction] (#eq:correctaction), the displayed triangle-inequality strategy yields only \[\begin{aligned}
 A_\tau
 &\le 4KC^2\tau+4C\sum_k b_k+\frac1\tau\sum_k b_k^2\\
 &\le 4KC^2\tau+4C\sqrt{K\tau I_\tau}+I_\tau.\end{aligned}\] Here \(b_k^2\le\tau\int_{k\tau}^{(k+1)\tau}\|\widehat v\|^2dt\) follows from the dynamic distance bound. The first two excess terms do not vanish merely from \(K\tau\to T\). The square expansion in the original proof also loses a factor of two in its cross term. Thus the printed reasoning does not establish the limsup estimate used in its next, strong-convergence step.

## Counterevidence, impact and correction

The Gaussian witness does not refute strong convergence; its exact velocities converge to the Gaussian gradient flow. A different energy-dissipation argument may prove the theorem. No complete repaired proof is certified here. The supported Major conclusion is that a demonstrated invalid derivation lies directly in the proof of the core velocity-convergence claim. Neither failed benchmarks nor impossibility of repair is needed for that assessment. The rejection-density identity and empirical results are not refuted by this finding.

Problem 2 (The appendix’s explicit velocity has the ascent sign).  Assessment: Minor. Appendix B.1, pp. 15–16, gives \(v=+\nabla(\delta\mathcal F/\delta\mu)\) and, for reverse KL, \(v=\nabla\log p-\nabla\log q\).

The main definition on p. 4 instead uses the descent convention \(v\in-\partial\mathcal F\), with continuity equation \(\partial_t p+\nabla\cdot(pv)=0\). For \(p=\mathcal N(m,1)\) and \(q=\mathcal N(0,1)\), \[\nabla\log p-\nabla\log q=m.\] The appendix’s sign therefore gives \(m'=m\) and \(\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{KL}(p\Vert q)=m^2>0\) for \(m\ne0\); descent requires \(m'=-m\). This directly checks the stated formula under smooth positive densities satisfying the assumptions.

The main negative-subgradient definition and variational minimization are counterevidence against alleging that the trained algorithm follows ascent. The local correction is a minus sign in the explicit gradient-flow formulas. No observed implementation reversal or additional core dependency was established, so the assessment remains Minor.

Problem 3 (The neural objective does not implement the stated JKO step).  Assessment: Major. The objective in Eq. (5), p. 5, and the first objective on p. 6 contains \(\frac12\int\|v\|^2\). On p. 6, \(\dot\omega=\|v\|^2\), but Eq. (6) adds \(\omega\) rather than \(\omega/2\).

For the same one-dimensional Gaussian family, the defined JKO objective minimizes \[\frac{a^2}{2}+\frac{(a-m)^2}{2\tau},
 \qquad a_{\rm JKO}=\frac{m}{1+\tau}.\] Doubling its transport penalty instead gives \(a=m/(1+\tau/2)\). At \(\tau=1/2\), \(m=1\), these are \(2/3\) and \(4/5\).

Algorithm 3 in Appendix D.1 (p. 23) explicitly optimizes Eq. (6), so this objective is on the central neural-JKO training path. The release statically corroborates the discrepancy: accumulates the squared velocity norm; with its unit integration duration, , lines 98–104, adds the resulting penalty divided by `tau`, without a factor \(1/2\). No author code was executed.

This is a Major derivation defect in the correspondence between the defined JKO step and the central neural training objective: Eq. (6) doubles the kinetic term derived from Eq. (5), and the Gaussian witness changes the exact optimizer at the same stated step size. Adding the missing half, or explicitly setting \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tau/2\), supplies a local reconciliation. This reparameterization preserves a JKO family and may preserve convergence and practical performance; it does not make the printed derivation valid. The severity follows the demonstrated core dependency, without requiring benchmark loss or a difficult repair. No benchmark degradation or complete verified implementation patch is claimed.

Problem 4 (Shifted latent densities are evaluated without recentering).  Assessment: Minor, release-specific. In , the zero-centered `latent_energy` expression is also used by `energy_evaluation` on a recovered latent coordinate without subtracting `latent_mean`.

Lines 22–39 define the latent mean, zero-centered energy, and sampling path. Sampling computes energy before translating the sample and is consistent. Lines 91–92 of the evaluation path instead pass the shifted coordinate directly to the zero-centered expression.

A valid zero-layer instance with \(d=1\), scale \(1\), latent mean \(1\) and evaluation point \(x=1\) has density \(\mathcal N(1,1)\). The correct negative log density is \[\frac12\log(2\pi)=0.9189385332,\] whereas the released evaluation expression gives \(\frac12[1+\log(2\pi)]=1.4189385332\). Its reported-to-true density ratio is \(e^{-1/2}=0.6065306597\).

The default zero-mean configuration is unaffected. The LGCP configuration enables finding a nonzero latent mean, making the option substantive; however, the inspected evaluation routine in , lines 156–162, calls `model.sample`, whose latent-energy handling is different and correct for this translation. Historical benchmark harm is consequently unproved. A local correction is to recenter the argument at the `energy_evaluation` call. Changing the shared energy function without adjusting sampling would require additional review.
