\section*{Problem 1: Countable pointwise convergence is insufficient for the claimed consistency}

Source: Current author copy Theorem 3, PDF p.6; Appendix F, Assumptions 2–4 and Properties 7–8, Eqs.(29)–(30), pp.19–22; final proceedings corresponding locations

Assessment: supported; impact: major

\subsection*{Source claim (paraphrase)}

Theorem 3 claims that an accepted safe policy is returned with probability tending to \(1\). The stated hypotheses allow a countable class, piecewise Lipschitz costs, a strictly feasible policy, and pointwise sample-loss convergence. The proof upgrades pointwise convergence to eventual uniform accuracy on a countable cover.

\subsection*{Analysis}

Countable simultaneous convergence still permits parameter-dependent indices: \(\forall\theta\,\exists N_\theta\) is not \(\exists N\,\forall\theta\). For instance, \(f_n(k)=\mathbf1\{k\geq n\}\) converges pointwise to zero while its supremum remains \(1\). A full algorithmic counterexample follows, including the theorem’s assumptions.

Let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]\), \(Y=0\), logging prediction \(A^\beta\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) independent of \(X\), and repercussion \(R^\psi=A^\psi\). Use threshold \(\tau=1/2\), confidence parameter \(\delta=1/10\), margin \(\xi=1/10\), and cross-entropy for label zero. Include the safe policy \(q_\star(x)=1/4\). For each finite union \(U\) of rational intervals of total measure at most \(1/4\), include \(q_U=1/8\) on \(U\) and \(7/8\) outside. This class is countable and can be integer-encoded.

The importance estimate is \(2q_\theta(X)A^\beta-1/2\in[-1/2,5/4]\). Predictions depend only on observed \(X\), logging has full support, and the deterministic repercussion mechanism is invariant. Singleton partitions meet the printed Lipschitz and cover conditions. Fixed-policy losses are bounded, so the strong law supplies pointwise convergence. The safe policy has \(g(\star)=-1/4\), permitting \(\epsilon=1/5>\xi\). Every other policy is unsafe: \[g(U)=7/8-(3/4)|U|-1/2\geq3/16.\]

For every finite candidate sample, choose a rational-interval union covering all its feature values with total measure at most \(1/4\). Its empirical loss is \(\log(8/7)\), and every constraint estimate is at most \(-1/4\). The inflated bound obeys \[U^+(U)\leq-1/4+\tfrac72\sqrt{\log10/(2n_f)}\leq-1/40\quad(n_f\geq279).\] Thus it enters the feasible branch of Algorithm 2. The sample maximum loss is \(\log8\), attained by the empty union; every infeasible-branch cost is greater than \(\log8-1/40>\log(8/7)\). Every feasible-branch loss is at least \(\log(8/7)\), while the safe policy has \(\log(4/3)\). A global minimum is attained and every minimizer is unsafe, irrespective of tie breaking.

The independent test can return that unsafe candidate or abstain; it cannot restore the safe policy discarded by selection. Consequently the probability of returning an accepted safe policy is zero for all sufficiently large split sizes. The independent-holdout safety guarantee is compatible with this counterexample because it may reject.

\subsection*{Finding and scope}

The central consistency theorem is false under its printed hypotheses, in both the final proceedings and current author copy. The unbiased importance estimate and independent-test safety result are not refuted.

\subsection*{Proposed correction}

The supplied correction requires genuine uniform convergence and attained minimizers, or a restricted finite class, together with vanishing confidence radii and a strict feasible margin. Countable covers alone cannot provide the missing uniform law.

The existing correction is retained as a bounded proposal; author adoption is not established.

\section*{Problem 2: The multi-constraint penalty rewards cancellation by satisfied constraints}

Source: Current author copy and final proceedings Algorithm 4, PDF p.24, line 16; Appendix F multi-constraint consistency extension, p.19

Assessment: supported; impact: major

\subsection*{Source claim (paraphrase)}

The infeasible branch returns \(\ell_{\max}+\sum_jU_j^{\rm inflated}\), using signed inflated upper bounds rather than positive violations.

\subsection*{Analysis}

Use the same valid logging/repercussion model but just two constant policies \(q_s=1/4,q_b=3/4\), thresholds \(\tau_1=1/2,\tau_2=10\), and confidence parameters \(1/10\). Importance estimates have common width \(3/2\). A finite class gives uniform convergence; the safe policy has both constraints strictly negative. Population values are \[(g_1(s),g_2(s),\ell_s)=(-1/4,-39/4,\log(4/3)),\quad(g_1(b),g_2(b),\ell_b)=(1/4,-37/4,\log4).\] The safe branch cost tends to \(\log(4/3)\), while the unsafe penalty tends to \(\log4-9\), which is much smaller. Hence selection chooses the unsafe policy with probability tending to \(1\), despite an available strictly feasible member.

The strongly satisfied second constraint offsets the first violation. Its generous threshold is permitted; the superscript denotes inflation rather than positive truncation. The independent test correctly rejects with probability tending to one, so eventual safe return still fails without compromising test independence.

\subsection*{Finding and scope}

The displayed multi-constraint selector has a separate finite-class consistency obstruction, even when uniform convergence holds. No implementation or experimental rerun is inferred.

\subsection*{Proposed correction}

The supplied separated cost retains feasible losses and otherwise uses \(\ell_{\max}+C+\sum_j\max\{0,U_j^++\xi/4\}\), with \(C>0\). Every infeasible cost then exceeds every feasible loss. For finite classes, uniform convergence and vanishing radii make a strictly feasible branch available; selection has margin and the independent test accepts with probability tending to one. The same argument needs explicit uniform laws for infinite classes.

The existing correction is retained as a bounded proposal; author adoption is not established.
