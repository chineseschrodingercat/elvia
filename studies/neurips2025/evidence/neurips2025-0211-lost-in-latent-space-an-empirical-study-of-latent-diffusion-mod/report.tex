\section*{Problem 1: Timing normalization counts channels rather than states}

Source: Table 1, p.7; experiments/eval.py, lines 258–266 and 361; lola/emulation.py, decode\_traj, lines 44–70

Assessment: supported; impact: minor

\subsection*{Source claim (paraphrase)}

Table 1 describes inference time per state, including generation and decoding. The evaluator divides elapsed time by the first two dimensions of a decoded trajectory whose layout is \((B,C,L,\ldots)\).

\subsection*{Analysis}

The denominator is therefore \(BC\), while the number of output states is \(BL\). For elapsed time \(1\) second and shape \((2,3,5,4,4)\), the release reports \(1/6\) second; the per-output-state value is \(1/10\), or \(1/8\) per newly generated state if each trajectory has one context state. Doubling channels alone changes the exported field to \(1/12\) without changing the number of states. The decoder’s explicit rearrangement establishes the axis identity independently of timing execution.

\subsection*{Finding and scope}

The exported unit is not per temporal state. Whether the published table used this field, and the corrected benchmark values, remain unestablished. A per-channel-per-trajectory interpretation would require a different label.

\subsection*{Proposed correction}

For total output states divide by \(BL\); for newly generated states divide by \(B(L-c)\) with an explicit context count \(c\). State the selected unit and timing synchronization/warmup protocol.

A proposed correction is described; author adoption is not established.

\section*{Problem 2: The context sampler uses wrapping rather than Poisson truncation}

Source: Appendix B, p.17, before Eq.(15); lola/emulation.py, random\_context\_mask, lines 184–221; experiments/configs/train\_diffusion.yaml, lines 12–18

Assessment: supported; impact: minor

\subsection*{Source claim (paraphrase)}

The paper describes a Poisson count with parameter \(2\), truncated to allowable positive context lengths. The released configuration samples \(N\sim\operatorname{Pois}(1)\) and sets \(c=1+(N\bmod4)\) for a window of length \(5\).

\subsection*{Analysis}

Wrapping sends every count in the same residue class to one length. Its probabilities for \(c=1,2,3,4\) are approximately \[(0.383216876,0.370946117,0.184450766,0.061386241)\]. In contrast, conditioning \(\operatorname{Pois}(2)\) on \(1\le c\le4\) gives \((1/3,1/3,2/9,1/9)\). Clipping would also produce a different distribution. Adding a minimum-count offset changes the mean but does not make a wrapped law into the stated conditional law.

\subsection*{Finding and scope}

The source’s training mask distribution differs from the written distribution. This does not determine whether its empirical performance is better or worse.

\subsection*{Proposed correction}

Document the wrapped law if it is intended, or sample the finite conditional categorical probabilities. Rejection from \(\operatorname{Pois}(2)\) until \(1\le c\le4\) has acceptance probability \(6e^{-2}\).

A proposed correction is described; author adoption is not established.
