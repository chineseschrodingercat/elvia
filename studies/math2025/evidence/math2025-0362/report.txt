A replacement argument under the stated regularity is supplied below. Two further displayed formulas require local corrections. None of these findings supplies a counterexample to the principal embedding or rigidity theorems. # Scope and proof structure

All 33 source pages, including the appendical topology argument and references, were read. The review traced the hypotheses of Theorems 1.1, 1.3 and 1.4 through the spinorial equality case, flat level sets, global coordinates, the construction of the pp-wave metric, the decay-dependent rigidity argument and the borderline examples. Source page numbers below are one-based PDF pages and agree with the printed numbering.

The asymptotic-flatness definition is substantive: the manifold is connected, complete and without boundary, the ends are finite in number, the metric and second fundamental form have the specified weighted Hölder regularity, \(\mu,J\) are integrable, and \(q\in((n-2)/2,n-2]\). The nonzero equality case in Theorem 1.1 also requires spin, the dominant energy condition and \(E=|P|\). Theorem 1.3 uses the strict additional threshold \(q>n-1-s-\alpha\); the \(E=0\) case has its separate threshold. These hypotheses have not been dropped in assessing the main results.

The equality spinors yield the bilinear field \(X\), its norm \(N\), and the spacetime-harmonic equation \(\nabla^2u=-k|\nabla u|\). The leaf-flatness argument uses the family of parallel spinors obtained from varying the asymptotic spinor, rather than the existence of just one parallel spinor. Codazzi identities and decay eliminate the transverse curl of the shift, giving \(Y=\nabla^\Sigma\ell\). The resulting metric has profile \[F=|\nabla u|^{-2}+|\nabla^\Sigma\ell|^2-2\ell_u,
 \qquad \mu=-\tfrac12|\nabla u|^2\Delta_\Sigma F.\] The latter identity, proved in Lemma 6.6, is the correct general-slice identity and is relevant to the second finding.

The checks below include exact symbolic matrix multiplication, a complete Christoffel/Ricci calculation for the pp-wave ansatz, and an explicit interpolation calculation. The retained script was authored for this review and executed with exit code zero. No author code was executed.

# Major finding: the interpolation remainder has the wrong derivative order

## Location and decisive calculation

Equation reference: eq:printed. On p. 23, equations (7.5)–(7.7) interpolate \(F(\,\cdot\,,\rho)-1\) at \(s-1\) nodes by a polynomial of degree \(s-2\), but state \[
 R(u,\rho)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{s-1}(u-u_i)}{(s-2)!}
             \partial_u^{s-2}F(\Phi(u,\rho),\rho).\] The asserted intermediate point lies in \([u_0,u_s]\). This identity is false. It is then used explicitly to obtain (7.10) and (7.11) on p. 24, whose derivative comparison is the essential contradiction for \(s\geq3\).

For a concrete calculation, take \(s=3\), \(u_0=0,u_1=3,u_2=9,u_3=33\) and \(f(u)=e^{-u}\). The two interpolation weights at zero are \(L_1(0)=3/2\) and \(L_2(0)=-1/2\). Therefore the actual residual is \[R_f(0)=1-\tfrac32e^{-3}+\tfrac12e^{-9}
       =0.92538110235024742531\ldots>0.\] The right side of the printed formula is instead \[(0-3)(0-9)f'(\Phi)=-27e^{-\Phi}<0
 \qquad(0\leq\Phi\leq33).\] No intermediate point can make the two equal. These nodes also satisfy the mass-value and spacing inequalities in (7.4) with \(c(u)=e^{-u}\) on this interval. A smooth nonnegative compactly supported extension agreeing with this function on the entire interval can be chosen when integrability on \(\mathbb R\) is desired. Multiplication by a positive radial factor does not change the sign contradiction. Even reproduction of a linear polynomial fails for [eq:printed] (#eq:printed), although the true interpolation residual then vanishes.

This is an independent calculation disproving the interpolation identity used in the proof. It is not an asymptotically flat initial-data counterexample to Theorem 1.3: no assertion is made that this test profile satisfies its strict weighted decay threshold. That distinction is essential.

## Effect and a repair at the available regularity

Replacing the derivative in [eq:printed] (#eq:printed) by one of order \(s-1\) would give the usual classical remainder only with an extra derivative. The manuscript has \(F-1\in C^{s-2,\beta}_{-q}\) at this step, so an index correction alone is not an adequate justification.

Equation reference: eq:repair. The intended contradiction can instead be proved directly in the available Hölder class. Set \(k=s-2\) and fix the chosen nodes. On a fixed compact interval \(I\) containing them and \(u_0\), define \[\Lambda h=h(u_0)-\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}L_i(u_0)h(u_i).\] This functional annihilates every polynomial of degree at most \(k\). For \(h\in C^{k,\beta}(I)\), subtract its degree-\(k\) Taylor polynomial at any fixed point of \(I\). The integral Taylor estimate, with the top derivative replaced by its increment, gives \[\|h-T_kh\|_{L^\infty(I)}
       \leq C_{k,\beta,I}[h^{(k)}]_{C^{0,\beta}(I)}.\] For \(k=0\) the same estimate is just the Hölder difference estimate. Thus, for the fixed interpolation nodes, \[
 |R(u_0,\rho)|=|\Lambda(F(\,\cdot\,,\rho)-1)|
 \leq C[\partial_u^k F(\,\cdot\,,\rho)]_{C^{0,\beta}(I)}
 \leq C'\rho^{-q-k-\beta}.\] The last bound follows from (7.1) and Definition 2.1: \(u\) is restricted to a bounded interval, \(r\asymp\rho\), and its fixed differences are within the permitted weighted Hölder comparison scale for large \(\rho\).

The manuscript’s lower bound, obtained before invoking (7.7), is \[R(u_0,\rho)>\tfrac14c(u_0)\rho^{3-n}.\] Together with [eq:repair] (#eq:repair), this is impossible when \(q+k+\beta>n-3\), equivalently \(q>n-1-s-\beta\). The strict threshold permits the appropriate choice of \(\beta<\alpha\). This replaces (7.7), (7.10) and (7.11), and also avoids assigning derivative values to unspecified intermediate points.

The repair is conditional on the upstream weighted-coordinate estimate (7.1) and the radial mass asymptotics already established in the paper. It repairs the identified analytic step; it is not an independent reconstruction of all global geometric estimates. The finding is classified as Major because an essential step of a principal theorem’s proof is invalid as written, even though a repair is available under the same regularity.

# Minor finding: the energy density is slice dependent

## Location and admissible example

Theorem 3.3 on p. 6 quantifies over any initial data set in the pp-wave spacetime, while its displayed energy formula uses \(\mu=-\tfrac12F^{-1}\Delta_\Sigma F\). The factor \(F^{-1}\) is appropriate for the \(t=0\) slice, not for an arbitrary spacelike graph.

For the ambient metric \[\bar g=-2\,\mathrm du\,\mathrm dt+F\,\mathrm du^2+|\,\mathrm dy|^2\] and graph \(t=-\ell(u,y)\), the induced metric and inverse \(uu\) coefficient are \[\begin{aligned}
 g&=(F+2\ell_u)\,\mathrm du^2+2\ell_a\,\mathrm du\,\mathrm dy_a+|\,\mathrm dy|^2,\\
 |\nabla u|_g^2&=(F+2\ell_u-|\nabla_y\ell|^2)^{-1}.\end{aligned}\] The exact Ricci calculation gives \(\operatorname{Ric}_{uu}=-\Delta_yF/2\) and scalar curvature zero. The normal contraction therefore gives \[\mu=-\tfrac12|\nabla u|_g^2\Delta_yF.\]

For a global example, take \(y\in\mathbb R^3\), \[F(y)=1+(1+|y|^2)^{-1/2},\qquad \ell(u,y)=u/2 .\] Here \(\Delta_yF=-3(1+|y|^2)^{-5/2}<0\) and the graph metric is \((F+1)\,\mathrm du^2+|\,\mathrm dy|^2\), uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean metric and complete on \(\mathbb R^4\). It is a smooth spacelike initial data set in the specified pp-wave spacetime; its null-dust energy satisfies the dominant energy condition. At \(y=0\), \(F=2\) and \(\Delta_yF=-3\). The actual energy density is \(1/2\), whereas the displayed formula gives \(3/4\).

Theorem 3.3 does not impose asymptotic flatness on this arbitrary-slice assertion. The example satisfies that assertion’s hypotheses, but is not claimed to satisfy the asymptotic-flatness assumptions of Theorems 1.1 or 1.3.

## Impact and correction

Replace \(F^{-1}\) by \(|\nabla u|^2\), or explicitly restrict the displayed formula to the \(t=0\) slice. The later Lemma 6.6 (p. 20) and the borderline construction (p. 26) already use the correct factor. This counterevidence limits the demonstrated impact to the earlier general-slice statement; it does not establish a defect in the principal construction. The finding is therefore Minor.

# Minor finding: a factor is missing in the inverse metric

Equation (6.8), p. 18, displays the metric and its inverse. Writing \(f=|\nabla u|^{-1}\) and the shift as a column \(Y\), the metric is \[G=\begin{pmatrix}f^2+|Y|^2&Y^T\\Y&I\end{pmatrix}.\] Its correct inverse is \[G^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}
 f^{-2}&-f^{-2}Y^T\\
 -f^{-2}Y&I+f^{-2}YY^T
 \end{pmatrix}.\] The printed lower-right block is \(I+YY^T\), missing \(f^{-2}\). For the admissible positive-definite test \(f=2\), \(Y=(1,0)^T\), exact multiplication of \(G\) by the printed candidate gives \[\begin{pmatrix}1&3/4&0\\0&7/4&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\] rather than the identity. Multiplication by the corrected matrix gives the identity exactly. The first row of the printed inverse, used to recover \(|\nabla u|\) and the normal/shift relation, is correct. No downstream failure of the embedding has been demonstrated from the erroneous lower block. Correcting this displayed matrix is a Minor revision.

# Coverage, dependencies and limits

The analysis included the dimension and regularity thresholds, the finite-end and integrability hypotheses, the leaf-spinor argument, the closed-shift calculation, the constraint identity, the interpolation contradiction, and the coordinate cancellation in the borderline construction. The topology appendix and both figures were read or visually inspected in their actual source pages. In particular, the borderline example is a \(C^{2,\alpha}\) construction; its dimension restriction includes \(n-3-\alpha>(n-2)/2\), so in dimension five one must have \(\alpha<1/2\). It has not been represented as proving optimality for all higher \(s\) by the same construction.

External positive-mass, weighted elliptic and Reeb-stability results were checked for their roles as cited dependencies, but were not each independently reproved or fully retrieved. The global coordinate estimates and every term of the lengthy asymptotic-coordinate calculation were followed in the supplied manuscript; this review does not certify a separate formal derivation of each estimate.

The arXiv version record and the primary publisher landing page were inspected, together with a targeted search for corrections, supplements and associated code. No explicit supplementary file, correction or author-code repository was located in that bounded search. The publisher page requires access for the article; the reviewed bytes remain the bound arXiv v2 PDF. Absence of located code is not a mathematical defect.
