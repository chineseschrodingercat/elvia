Abstract: The specified manuscript contains a false compatibility assertion in the spectral scaling argument, a reversed parameter choice in the spectral identification used for the geodesic theorem, and sharpness statements that fail in the expressly allowed dimension one. We give an exact linear-algebra witness, complete local replacement arguments for the first two steps, and direct dimension-one calculations. These findings do not supply counterexamples to the principal capacity coincidence or geodesic theorems. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Major: the global radial stretch need not preserve compatibility).  The proof of Theorem 10.1 on p. 49 asserts that \(f_r(a,p)=(ra,p)\) induces a bijection \(f_r^*:\mathcal J(r\alpha)\longrightarrow\mathcal J(\alpha)\). This is false for the class defined on p. 48, although the end adjustment transforms correctly.

## Exact check

Equation reference: eq:badmatrix. Work at \(a=0\) in symplectic tangent coordinates \((a,R_\alpha,x,y)\), with \(\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(W,W)\) and \(W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For \(r=2\), set \[J_b=\operatorname{diag}
 \left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix},
       \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\right),
 \qquad
 S=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0&0\\0&1&0&-1\\1&0&1&0\\0&0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}.\] Here \(S^T\Omega S=\Omega\) and \(J=S J_bS^{-1}\) is compatible. For example, the leading principal minors of the symmetric matrix \(\Omega J\) are \(3/2,3,1,1\), all positive. With \(D=df_2=\operatorname{diag}(2,1,1,1)\), however, \[\Omega(D^{-1}JD)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&0&-1&0\\
 0&1&0&1\\
 -2&0&1&0\\
 0&2&0&3
 \end{pmatrix},
 \] which is not symmetric. Thus \(f_2^*J\) is not compatible.

This is an admissible middle-region example, not an inadmissible end condition. Start with a \(2\alpha\)-adjusted structure and implant the displayed compatible value in a small interior chart, keeping the structure fixed outside that chart. One may use a symplectic frame and a smooth path from the identity to \(S\) with a bump function. Equivalently, the local quadratic Hamiltonian \(a y\) has the shear \(S\) as its linearized time-one map; a cutoff realizes it locally. Compatibility is preserved by symplectic conjugation and the two ends remain adjusted. The four-dimensional example also embeds as a direct summand in higher dimensions. Exact rational matrix calculations were independently run and retained with exit status zero.

## Verified replacement

For each given \(J\in\mathcal J(r\alpha)\) choose a compact interval containing its entire non-adjusted region. Choose a smooth increasing diffeomorphism \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) which is the identity on that interval and has derivative \(r\) outside a larger interval. Its transition regions can be placed entirely where \(J\) is adjusted. Put \(F(a,p)=(f(a),p)\).

On the identity region \(F^*J=J\). In either adjusted transition region, write the tangent splitting as \(\langle\partial_a,R_\alpha\rangle\oplus\xi\). Then \[(F^*J)\partial_a=\frac{f'}r R_\alpha,
 \qquad (F^*J)R_\alpha=-\frac r{f'}\partial_a.\] Since \(f'>0\), this block is compatible with \(da\wedge\alpha\), and the \(\xi\)-block remains compatible with \(d\alpha\). The positive factor \(e^a\) does not change compatibility. At both ends \(f'=r\), so \(F^*J\) is \(\alpha\)-adjusted and belongs to \(\mathcal J(\alpha)\).

The diffeomorphism \(F^{-1}\) gives an exact bijection between the corresponding holomorphic curves with transformed point constraints. It preserves nonconstant components, punctured-sphere domains and the existence of a negative end. The affine reparameterizations at infinity preserve finite energy and asymptotic geometric orbits; on the compact transition band the relevant positive energy norms are uniformly comparable. Their positive actions satisfy \(A^+_{r\alpha}(u)=r A^+_\alpha(F^{-1}u)\). For each original auxiliary datum this gives an infimum bounded by \(r c_k(\alpha)\). Taking the supremum yields \(c_k(r\alpha)\le r c_k(\alpha)\); the same construction with \(1/r\) gives the reverse inequality. This proves scaling without the false fixed-map bijection.

The severity concerns an invalid derivation of a structural property used for continuity, degenerate extensions and the lower bound in Theorem 5 (p. 58). The replacement repairs this scaling step. It does not independently establish the other analytic properties of Theorem 10.1 or refute its final scaling conclusion.

Problem 2 (Major: the parameter limit in the spectral identification is reversed).  In Section 11, p. 59 defines \(\beta_t=((1-t)S+t)\alpha_0\) and claims that its short closed orbits are independent of \(t\in(0,1]\). Page 60 then invokes Proposition 10.4 for small positive \(t\) on the ground that this coefficient is \(C^2\)-close to \(1\). These are the wrong endpoint and limit for the displayed family.

Equation reference: eq:norm. For nonconstant invariant \(S\), at \(t=1\) the form is \(\alpha_0\) and every Zoll fiber is an orbit. For \(t<1\), a fiber through a point with \(dS\ne0\) is not a Reeb orbit of \(\beta_t\); the short fibers are the critical fibers. Also, exactly, \[\|((1-t)S+t)-1\|_{C^2}=(1-t)\|S-1\|_{C^2}.
 \] The available normal-form control at this step is \(C^1\)-closeness of \(S\). Taking \(t\) near zero does not provide the required \(C^2\) control. This is the step identifying \(c_0(\alpha),c_1(\alpha)\) with the minimum and maximum short periods, hence it is directly used in Theorem 5, not an unrelated notation issue.

## Verified replacement for this step

Write \(T_0=\operatorname{sys}(\alpha_0)\) and use \(0\le t<1\). The normal-form relations \(dS=-B[V]\), with \(V\) small in \(C^1\), and the short-orbit argument of Proposition 4.3 and Lemma 4.4 apply uniformly to \(V_t=(1-t)V\). They identify the short orbits with the critical fibers of \(S\) for every \(t<1\), with actions \(((1-t)S(\gamma)+t)T_0\).

Here only \(k=0,1\) are needed. Choose the uniform closeness so that \(1-\delta\le S\le1+\delta\) with \(0<\delta<1/3\). Theorem 10.1 and Proposition 10.3 put \(c_k(\beta_t)\) in \([(1-\delta)T_0,(1+\delta)T_0]\). The short/long separation excludes long orbits in this interval, and \((1+\delta)T_0<2(1-\delta)T_0\) excludes sums of two or more orbit actions. Thus \[q_k(t)=\frac{c_k(\beta_t)-tT_0}{1-t}
 \quad\hbox{belongs to}\quad T_0 S(\operatorname{Crit}S),\qquad 0\le t<1.\] The right-hand set has measure zero by Sard’s theorem. The left-hand side is continuous on the connected interval \([0,1)\), hence constant. Consequently \[c_k(\beta_t)=(1-t)c_k(S\alpha_0)+tT_0,\qquad k=0,1.\] For the fixed smooth \(S\), take \(t<1\) sufficiently close to \(1\) in [eq:norm] (#eq:norm). Proposition 10.4 now applies. Dividing the two resulting identities by \(1-t>0\) gives \[c_0(S\alpha_0)=T_0\min S,\qquad
 c_1(S\alpha_0)=T_0\max S.\] This repairs the local parameter argument, conditional on the spectral properties and the earlier normal-form/short-orbit inputs. The scaling repair above supplies one of those properties; a complete independent reconstruction of all SFT and Gromov–Witten inputs is outside this review. The geodesic theorem is not refuted.

Problem 3 (Major for the unrestricted sharpness claims: dimension one must be excluded).  Page 7 explicitly allows contact dimension \(2n-1\) with \(n\ge1\). Proposition 3 on p. 9 has no exclusion of \(n=1\) and asserts a strict increase of the systolic ratio near every Zoll form. Proposition 1 on p. 4 likewise states the strict capacity inequality without excluding the planar case.

On the circle, any positive form \(\alpha=f(\theta)d\theta\) has \(R_\alpha=f^{-1}\partial_\theta\). Its minimal period and contact volume both equal \(\int_{S^1}\alpha\), so \[\rho_{\rm sys}(\alpha)
 =\frac{\operatorname{sys}(\alpha)}{\int_{S^1}\alpha}=1.\] In particular \(\alpha_0=d\theta\) is Zoll and admits no strict improvement whatsoever. This directly contradicts Proposition 3 in an expressly included dimension, independently of any regularity or limiting argument.

For completeness, Proposition 1 also fails in dimension two of the ambient symplectic space. A planar smooth star-shaped domain \(D\) has boundary \(I=b(\theta)>0\) in action-angle coordinates \(I=r^2/2\), with \(\omega=dI\wedge d\theta\) and area \(A=\int_0^{2\pi}b(\theta)d\theta\). Choose a circle diffeomorphism \(g\) with \(g'=2\pi b/A\). The annular map \[(I,\theta)\longmapsto \bigl(I/g'(\theta),g(\theta)\bigr)\] preserves \(dI\wedge d\theta\) and sends the boundary to \(I=A/(2\pi)\). It extends to an ambient compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphism: interpolate the lifts by \(g_t=(1-t)\operatorname{id}+tg\), whose derivatives stay positive; write \(\eta_t=\dot g_t\circ g_t^{-1}\) and use \(H_t=-I\eta_t(\theta)\). Its vector field is \(-I\eta_t'\partial_I+\eta_t\partial_\theta\). Cut off this Hamiltonian on an annulus containing all boundary trajectories, with cutoff identically one on a neighborhood of those trajectories and zero near the origin and infinity. The boundary map is unchanged, and the bounded component maps to the disk of area \(A\). Normalization and ambient invariance give \(c(D)=A\) for every normalized capacity; Stokes gives \(\operatorname{sys}(D)=A\). The claimed strict inequality is impossible.

The verified correction is to restrict these strict sharpness statements to \(n\ge2\). Section 2 uses a nonempty displaceable open set in the transverse ball in \(\mathbb C^{n-1}\) (pp. 18–20), which requires that restriction. The positive capacity coincidence and local systolic results are not contradicted in dimension one; on a connected circle the latter is equality. Related dimension-free claims of arbitrarily large systolic ratio need the same restriction. This is a false boundary case of the asserted sharpness, not evidence against the higher-dimensional construction.
