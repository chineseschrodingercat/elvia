Abstract: The acquired preprint proves a structural-coefficient version of Berthelot’s conjecture by a motivic realization and comparison with classical rigid cohomology. Three proof dependencies fail as written: a formal open immersion is used as an algebraic immersion, a formal-model indexing functor is asserted to be final despite a disconnected comma category, and an incorrect torus realization is used to justify stabilization. Explicit admissible examples and a split-cofiber calculation establish these Major proof defects. They do not constitute counterexamples to the final Berthelot theorem or to the intended pushforward compatibility. Two additional local assertions concerning dagger neighborhoods and a terminal object have bounded corrections. The scope of the all-frame comparison also requires clarification. Scientific adjudication remains pending; no human review is asserted.

# Exact source and scope of the claim

The work reviewed is Berthelot’s conjecture via homotopy theory, by Veronika Ertl and Alberto Vezzani [paper], associated with DOI https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2025-0021. The actual source is the 34-page arXiv:2406.02182v2 dated 1 April 2025. The corresponding author TeX was read as source, not executed. The publisher PDF was not obtained through the attempted authorized route. The journal metadata identifies a 54-page article; equivalence to that version is not assumed. All locations below refer to one-based pages of the acquired PDF, which agree with the printed pagination on the inspected pages.

Fix a perfect field \(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\) and \(K=W(k)[1/p]\). For a smooth proper morphism \(f:X\to S\), the main theorem on page 2 and Corollary 3.45 on page 25 claim that the structural-coefficient groups \[R^q f_{\mathrm{rig}*}\bigl(X/(T,\overline T,\mathfrak P)\bigr),
 \qquad q\geq0,\] as the proper frames over \(S\) vary, are realizations of a canonical overconvergent \(F\)-isocrystal. They are asserted to be vector bundles on every such frame, including frames not assumed smooth. The theorem does not establish preservation of arbitrary overconvergent coefficient objects. Remark 3.49, pages 25–26, explicitly postpones the classical comparison for coefficients of motivic origin; its proposed use of Proposition A.13 is conditional on a further equivalence of coefficient categories.

The main dependency chain is the dagger de Rham realization (Proposition 2.22), locally free cohomology of dualizable motives (Corollary 2.24), the Monsky–Washnitzer realization (Propositions 3.9–3.10), its Frobenius enhancement (Proposition 3.18 and Corollary 3.19), and the comparison with Berthelot’s complex (Propositions 3.33 and 3.39). The appendix investigates a separate pushforward compatibility. This distinction is essential when assessing the three Major findings.

# A formal open is not the asserted scheme immersion

Problem 1 (The algebraization step in Proposition 3.39).  Step 2 of Proposition 3.39, pages 22–23, takes \(\mathfrak T=\operatorname{Spf}A\subseteq\mathfrak T'=\operatorname{Spf}A'\) and a smooth \(A\)-algebra \[R=A[X_1,\ldots,X_M]/(f_1,\ldots,f_m).\] It then asserts that the chosen presentation gives an embedding \(\operatorname{Spec}R\subset\mathbb P^M_{A'}\), and uses its schematic closure to construct a geometric materialization. That embedding need not exist.

## A counterexample satisfying the local hypotheses

Take \(k=\mathbb F_p\) and \[A'=\mathbb Z_p\langle t\rangle,\qquad
 A=\mathbb Z_p\langle t,t^{-1}\rangle.\] The formal scheme \(\operatorname{Spf}A\) is the principal formal open \(D(\bar t)\) of \(\operatorname{Spf}A'\). Both are reduced, and \(A'\) is the ring of power-bounded functions on the rigid closed unit disc \(\mathfrak T'_{\mathbb Q_p}\). Thus the special hypotheses of Step 1, retained in Step 2, hold. Choose the identity smooth object over \(\mathfrak T\). It has \(R=A\), with no variables or relations, so \(M=0\). The claimed embedding becomes \[\operatorname{Spec}A\longrightarrow\mathbb P^0_{A'}=\operatorname{Spec}A'.\] This morphism is not an immersion.

Here is a direct algebraic verification. The convergent Laurent series \[z=\sum_{n\geq1}p^n t^{-n^2}\] belongs to \(A\). Suppose that \(z\in\operatorname{Frac}(A')\). There would then be a nonzero \(g=\sum_{j\geq0}g_jt^j\in A'\) with \(gz\in A'\). Put \[a=\min_j v_p(g_j),\qquad v_p(g_d)=a\] for some index \(d\). If \(2n>d+1\), the coefficient of \(t^{d-n^2}\) in \(gz\) is \[p^n g_d+\sum_{m>n}p^m g_{\,d-n^2+m^2}.\] All terms with \(m<n\) vanish because their \(g\)-index is negative. The first displayed term has valuation \(a+n\), whereas the remaining sum lies in \(p^{a+n+1}\mathbb Z_p\). This coefficient is therefore nonzero. For arbitrarily large \(n\) its exponent is negative. Consequently \(gz\) cannot belong to \(A'\), a contradiction.

Thus \[\operatorname{Frac}(A')\subsetneq\operatorname{Frac}(A).\] Both rings are domains and the generic point of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) maps to the generic point of \(\operatorname{Spec}A'\). An immersion induces an isomorphism on the residue field at each point of its image. The strict generic-field extension therefore excludes the asserted immersion.

## Impact, contrary evidence and repair boundary

This is a Major defect in the supplied comparison proof. The false embedding is the stated construction that realizes the local generators as geometric materializations. Step 3 then glues the comparisons obtained in Step 2, and Corollary 3.43 and the proof of Corollary 3.45 invoke this comparison. The issue affects an essential derivation of the principal result, even if a correct replacement can be supplied.

There is substantial contrary evidence to any stronger conclusion. The formal open immersion in the example is perfectly valid. It can have a different geometric materialization. Moreover, Remark 3.42 on page 24 supplies an alternative comparison route when the generic fiber is smooth, as it is in this example. The calculation does not disprove the comparison functor or the Berthelot conclusion.

A repair must distinguish completed formal localization from ordinary algebraic localization. One possible route is an appropriate weak algebraization or an algebraic model before completion, followed by a justified compactification. Another is an independent local comparison with its descent and pullback compatibilities proved. Merely replacing embedding by morphism does not establish that the stated schematic-closure construction has all the required geometric-materialization properties. The general reduced-base argument still needs an actual replacement.

# The formal-model functor is not final

Problem 2 (The projective-line branch of Proposition A.13).  The proof of Proposition A.13, page 31, asserts that, for \(X=\mathbb P^1_S\), the functor \[r:\mathrm{FSch}/S\longrightarrow\mathrm{FSch}/X,
 \qquad \mathfrak T\longmapsto\widehat{\mathbb P}^1_{\mathfrak T},\] is final. Here a formal model over \(X\) has a map of its reduced special fiber to \(X\), rather than a specified lift of that map to a formal projective line. This distinction makes the assertion false.

## An explicitly disconnected comma category

Set \[k=\mathbb F_p,\qquad S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_p,\qquad
 B=\operatorname{Spf}\mathbb Z_p,\qquad X=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb F_p}.\] Let \(Y=B\) be an object of \(\mathrm{FSch}/X\) by sending its special fiber to \(\infty=[1:0]\). These formal schemes satisfy the paper’s conventions at the start of Section 3. For any formal \(\mathbb Z_p\)-scheme \(\mathfrak T\), let \[q_{\mathfrak T}:\widehat{\mathbb P}^1_{\mathfrak T}
       \longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb P}^1_B\] be the structural map. An object of the comma category \((Y/r)\) is a map \(a:Y\to\widehat{\mathbb P}^1_{\mathfrak T}\) reducing to the prescribed special-fiber map. Attach to it the invariant \[h(\mathfrak T,a)=q_{\mathfrak T}\circ a.\] If \(b:(\mathfrak T,a)\to(\mathfrak T',a')\) is a comma morphism, then \(\widehat{\mathbb P}^1(b)\circ a=a'\), hence \[q_{\mathfrak T}\circ a
   =q_{\mathfrak T'}\circ\widehat{\mathbb P}^1(b)\circ a
   =q_{\mathfrak T'}\circ a'.\] The invariant is unchanged along every arrow and every zigzag.

Now take \(\mathfrak T=B\) and the two sections \[a_0=[1:0],\qquad a_p=[1:p].\] They both reduce to \(\infty\), but their invariants differ. On the formal chart with coordinate \(u=X_1/X_0\), they are the continuous homomorphisms \(\mathbb Z_p\langle u\rangle\to\mathbb Z_p\) sending \(u\) to \(0\) and \(p\), respectively. Thus the comma category has at least two connected components and its nerve is not contractible. In fact, within each fixed invariant \(h\), the object \((B,h)\) is terminal, since \(\mathfrak T\to B\) is the unique structural map. The defect is precisely the multiplicity of lifts.

This also checks the variance. The displayed coefficient diagrams are indexed contravariantly. Initiality of \(r^{\mathrm{op}}\), needed for their limit restriction, is equivalent to finality of \(r\). Taking the opposite of the disconnected comma category does not make its nerve contractible.

## Impact and the limit of the counterexample

This is a Major issue in the appendix’s algebraic pushforward comparison. The proof uses the asserted finality to assemble the projective-line calculation over formal bases and identify the resulting object in the limit category. A false categorical reduction cannot support that identification without an additional argument.

The example does not contradict the preceding rigid-analytic Proposition A.11: there the full map to the rigid projective line is already fixed. Nor does it contradict the étale branch of Proposition A.13, where formal étaleness supplies unique lifting. The two sections can be joined by the formal family \([1:pt]\). This is relevant evidence that a suitable homotopy-based proof may work, but it is not a zigzag of the asserted comma category. Localization inside the category of motives does not by itself prove finality of the ordinary formal-scheme indexing functor.

A valid repair could prove that the particular diagrams and comparison maps are invariant under those homotopies, or could work with chosen compatible lifts and then establish descent between them. The present computation does not show that the conclusion of Proposition A.13 is false. It also does not independently refute Corollary 3.45: that theorem is proved earlier without this appendix. The sole earlier reference to Proposition A.13 in Remark 3.49 concerns a future coefficient comparison with additional hypotheses.

# Further findings on dagger realization

Problem 3 (Smooth surjectivity does not remove the relative boundary).  Remark 2.7, page 5, asserts that a smooth surjective dagger morphism can be straightened with \(X_h=X_0\times_{S_0}S_h\), using \(\operatorname{Int}(X_0/K)=f_0^{-1}\operatorname{Int}(S_0/K)\).

Take the standard dagger closed unit disc over \(K=\mathbb C_p\) mapping to the point \(S=\operatorname{Spa}K\). This is a smooth surjective morphism of affinoid dagger varieties. The point has a cofinal constant presentation. The claimed formula would make its source presentation constant as well. But no fixed strict outer neighborhood of the closed unit disc is cofinal among its strict neighborhoods: if it contains a closed disc of radius \(\rho>1\), a smaller radius \(1<\rho'<\rho\) gives a strict neighborhood not containing that outer disc. Equivalently, the boundary of a closed outer disc does not lie in its interior over \(K\), although it lies in the inverse image of the interior of the point.

The cited Corollary A.11 of Vezzani [vezzani], actually checked in the acquired reference, instead contains \[\operatorname{Int}(X/S)=\operatorname{Int}(X/Y)\cap f^{-1}\operatorname{Int}(Y/S).\] The missing relative-interior factor cannot be discarded from smoothness alone. Partial properness is an appropriate condition for the boundary term to disappear, but an arbitrary smooth affinoid disc is not partially proper. This is Minor in the present dependency assessment: no essential later use of this false stationary straightening was identified, and Remark 2.20 retains shrinking source neighborhoods. Remove the assertion or give the required boundary hypothesis. The main realization is not disproved by this example.

Problem 4 (The unreduced torus does not realize to the unit).  In Step 2 of the proof of Proposition 2.22, page 9, the realization of the dagger torus \(\mathbb T^{1\dagger}\) is identified with the unit. The torus of Example 2.3 is the unreduced space, whereas the Tate object in Definition 2.14 is a cofiber.

Over \(\mathbb Q_p\), put \(A=\mathbb Q_p\langle T,T^{-1}\rangle^\dagger\). The complex is \[A\xrightarrow{\,d\,}A\,dT.\] Every exact form has residue zero, while \(dT/T\) has residue one. The kernel consists of constants. Conversely, a residue-zero overconvergent Laurent form has an overconvergent primitive after shrinking the annulus: division by a nonzero integer causes at most polynomial coefficient growth, which is absorbed by the available exponential decay. Thus \[H^0=\mathbb Q_p,\qquad H^1=\mathbb Q_p[dT/T],
 \qquad \operatorname{dR}(\mathbb T^{1\dagger})\simeq\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf1[-1].\] The cited Le Bras–Vezzani Corollary 4.37(6) gives this two-summand formula, not the displayed unit [lbv].

This is a Major defect in the supplied stabilization argument. Write \(M=\mathbb Q(\mathbb T^{1\dagger})\) and \(T_{\mathrm{red}}=\operatorname{cofib}(e:\mathbf1\to M)\). The unit section \(e\) has a retraction \(p:M\to\mathbf1\), with \(pe=\mathrm{id}\). Contravariant realization therefore identifies \(\operatorname{dR}(T_{\mathrm{red}})\) with \(\operatorname{fib}(\operatorname{dR}(e):\operatorname{dR}(M)\to\mathbf1)\). Under the printed equality \(\operatorname{dR}(M)\simeq\mathbf1\), this split epimorphism is an isomorphism over \(\operatorname{Spa}\mathbb Q_p\): its endomorphisms are scalars in \(\mathbb Q_p\). The resulting fiber would be zero, which is not tensor-invertible. Thus the stated computation cannot justify the ensuing extension to the stabilized category in Proposition 2.22. This proposition is an actual input to the main realization and comparison chain.

The corrected two-summand calculation instead gives \(\operatorname{dR}(T_{\mathrm{red}})\simeq\mathbf1[-1]\), which is tensor-invertible and supplies the required stabilization argument. This explicit repair is substantial counterevidence to any claim that the intended realization or the main theorem is false. It does not remove the core proof defect in the displayed argument or certify all the remaining comparison steps.

Problem 5 (Properness does not give the stated terminal object).  Remark 2.27, page 10, calls the canonical dagger structure of a proper rigid variety \(S\) terminal in \(\mathrm{Ad}^{\dagger}_{/S}\). Definition 2.25 allows an arbitrary analytic map from the completed source, which need not extend to a dagger map.

Take \(S=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Q_p}\), the dagger unit disc \(T^\dagger\), and the analytic map into the affine chart given by \[f(T)=\sum_{n\geq1}p^nT^{n!}.\] It converges on the closed unit disc, but its radius of convergence is exactly one, since \(p^{-n/n!}\to1\). A morphism to the alleged terminal object would extend \(f\) to a map from some larger closed disc to \(\mathbb P^1\). Such a map is a meromorphic function with finitely many poles on that affinoid disc: the inverse image of infinity is a proper zero-dimensional analytic subset of a one-dimensional affinoid. There are no poles on the unit disc. A slightly larger radius therefore avoids all poles and gives an analytic extension of \(f\), contradicting its convergence radius.

The ordinary rigid category \(\mathrm{Ad}_{/S}\) does have its identity as terminal object. The actual proof of Corollary 2.28 on page 11 begins with that ordinary category and then applies the forgetful restriction; it does not require the false dagger terminal assertion. This is therefore a Minor local categorical correction in the reviewed proof chain. Deleting Remark 2.27, or restricting the objects to maps admitting a dagger extension, resolves this particular obstruction.

# Positive checks and remaining boundaries

The definition immediately preceding Corollary 2.24 is important: \(\mathrm{Vect}\) consists of perfect complexes with locally free cohomology, not just objects in degree zero. Shifts of dualizable motives are therefore not counterexamples. For the actual mixed-characteristic application, the argument first obtains perfectness by monoidal duality, then invokes faithfully flat dagger completion and the locally free cohomology theorem of Le Bras–Vezzani. The latter’s Theorem 4.46 is explicitly stated over \(\mathbb Q_p\). The more general characteristic-zero formulation in Section 2 needs an extension of that cited input if equal-residue-characteristic zero is intended; this is a scope request, not a counterexample to its Witt-vector application.

The duality convention is also consistent: \[(f_*M)^\vee\simeq f_\sharp(M^\vee)\] for smooth proper \(f\). Consequently applying the dualized realization to \(f_*\mathbf1\) supplies the intended cohomological realization of \(f_\sharp\mathbf1\). The Frobenius construction uses invariance under universal homeomorphisms and the semilinear Witt-vector Frobenius. It does not require \(k=\mathbb F_p\) or an identity Frobenius on an arbitrary perfect field.

The proof does not silently assume that the underlying-module functor \(u\) is always \(t\)-exact. Remark 3.44 explicitly warns against that inference. In Corollary 3.45, local splitting of a perfect complex with projective cohomology is the stated justification for comparing the ordinary cohomology groups.

A separate Needs Evidence boundary remains: Proposition 3.39 assumes reduced formal schemes and a reduced generic fiber, and Corollary 3.43 is explicitly for proper reduced frames. Corollary 3.45 then says every proper frame. The review did not locate an explicit reduction or nilpotent base-change argument covering this change of scope. No admissible counterexample to the resulting rigid-cohomology claim has been established. A nonreduced base alone is insufficient to produce one: the identity smooth proper family still realizes the free rank-one module. The report therefore requests the missing reduction or the intended frame convention, rather than classifying the broad theorem as false.
