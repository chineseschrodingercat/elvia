Abstract: Three explicit checks identify defects in the proof of the spherical construction: the stated elimination map fails the required parity and Neumann conditions, the differentiated equation used for Fredholm regularity is false, and the derivative formula for the Neumann eigenvalue omits a nonzero boundary term. The last omission also gives an incorrect sign assertion. These defects affect the core proof of Theorem 1.5 and warrant Major assessment. They do not supply counterexamples to the asserted existence of Schiffer domains. We give precise corrections and distinguish them from local coefficient and nodal-count errors in the cylinder discussion. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Major: the sphere elimination map is not well defined

Equation reference: badmap. On p. 25, equations (6.9)–(6.11) define \[
h_u(x)=\frac{\partial_tu(1,x)}{U_\lambda''(1)},\qquad
M_1u(t,x)=u(t,x)-tU_\lambda'(|t|)h_u(x),\] and assert that \(M:\mathcal X_2^D\to\mathcal X_2^{DN}\times C_p^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb R)\) is well defined. The same multiplier occurs in the derivative computation on p. 26. Actual page inspection confirms that the factor is \(t\), not \(|t|\).

Fix \(\lambda=1/2\) and choose \(0<\varepsilon<1/100\). Put \[u(t,x)=\varepsilon\mu(1-t^2).\] This smooth function satisfies every condition defining \(\mathcal X_2^D\); it is constant in \(x\), hence even and periodic. Formula [badmap] (#badmap) gives \(h_u=2\varepsilon\), which belongs to the open admissible set \(h>2/\pi-1\) on p. 24. The example can be made arbitrarily small in the domain norm.

Equation reference: boundaryfail. For \(g(t)=tU'(|t|)\), the one-sided endpoint derivatives are \[g'(1)=g'(-1)=U''(1)=-\mu.\] Consequently, \[
\partial_tM_1u(1,x)=0,\qquad
\partial_tM_1u(-1,x)=4\varepsilon\mu\ne0.\] Moreover, \(g\) is odd, so subtracting its nonzero multiple from the even \(u\) destroys evenness. There is also a regularity failure at the interior point \(t=0\). By uniqueness for [ode] (#ode), \(U(0)\ne0\), and hence \(U''(0)=-\mu U(0)\ne0\). Since the even extension of \(U\) is smooth, \[tU'(|t|)=U''(0)t|t|+O(|t|^4).\] Its second derivative has different one-sided limits. Thus the printed \(M_1u\) is not even a \(C^2\) function in this example.

## Dependency and repair

The failure occurs before the definition \(G_\lambda=F_\lambda\circ M\) in (6.12). It invalidates the asserted map into the stated Banach spaces, the use of Proposition 6.1 for that map, and the corresponding bifurcation application on p. 31. This is a defect in a core construction, irrespective of how short its correction is.

The multiplier must be \[t\,\partial_t\bigl[U_\lambda(|t|)\bigr]
   =|t|U_\lambda'(|t|).\] This is smooth and even, and its endpoint derivatives have opposite signs, restoring both Neumann conditions. Likewise, \[\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}
 U_\lambda\!\left(\frac{|t|}{1+sh(x)}\right)
 =-|t|U_\lambda'(|t|)h(x),\] which corrects the displayed derivative preceding (6.16). One must make this replacement consistently in the map, its derivative, and the recovered solution. No counterexample to the existence conclusion of Theorem 1.5 is claimed.

# Major: the Fredholm regularization equation is false

Equation reference: correctfredholm. The proof of Proposition 6.2, pp. 26–27, must upgrade a Dirichlet solution \(u\in C^{2,\alpha}\) of \[Au=f,\qquad
A=\partial_{tt}-b(t)\partial_t+a(t)\partial_{xx},\quad
b(t)=\lambda\tan(\lambda t),\quad
a(t)=\lambda^2\sec^2(\lambda t),\] to \(\partial_tu\in C^{2,\alpha}\). With \(v=\partial_tu\) and \(L_\lambda^0=A+\mu\), the printed (6.19) is \[L_\lambda^0v=\mu v+\partial_tf-b'(t)\partial_tv+a'(t)\partial_{xx}v.\] The actual differentiated identity is instead \[
L_\lambda^0v=\mu v+\partial_tf+b'(t)v-a'(t)\partial_{xx}u.\]

There is an exact admissible test. Set \(u(t,x)=1-t^2\) and \[f=Au=-2+2\lambda t\tan(\lambda t).\] The function \(u\) is smooth, even, periodic, and Dirichlet at \(t=\pm1\); \(f\) is smooth and even, so \(f\in C_p^{1,\alpha}\subset\mathcal Y\). At \(t=0\), \(v=-2t\) gives \[L_\lambda^0v=0,\qquad
\text{right side of the printed (6.19)}=2\lambda^2.\] For the allowed value \(\lambda=1/2\), the residual is \(-1/2\). The original symbolic calculation reproduces this value and gives identically zero residual for [correctfredholm] (#correctfredholm).

The printed equation also places \(\partial_{xx}v\) on its right side when only \(v\in C^{1,\alpha}\) is known, so the claimed \(C^{0,\alpha}\) regularity does not follow from the available hypotheses. This directly damages the Fredholm step required by the spherical bifurcation argument.

With [correctfredholm] (#correctfredholm), every term on the right is \(C^{0,\alpha}\). The boundary relation (6.20) supplies \(C^{1,\alpha}\) normal derivative data, because \(f|_{\partial\Omega_*}\) inherits that regularity from its \(\mathcal Y\) decomposition. Standard boundary regularity then gives the desired upgrade. This repairs the argument, rather than validating the printed identity. The incidental assertion \(v\in C_p^{1,\alpha}\) should also lose its even-in-\(t\) restriction: the derivative of an even \(u\) is odd. Ordinary Schauder regularity does not require that parity.

# Major: a missing Neumann boundary term in the crossing argument

The derivative formula (6.28) on p. 28 is used on p. 29 to assert \(\mu'(\lambda)\le-2\lambda\) and to establish the spectral crossing in Lemma 6.3. It omits an endpoint term.

Equation reference: correctmu. Let \(w(t)=\cos(\lambda t)\) and \[\rho(\tau)=-\left(\cos\tau+\frac{1+\tau\tan\tau}{\cos\tau}\right).\] Differentiate [ode] (#ode) in \(\lambda\). The differentiated eigenfunction still has homogeneous Neumann data, so self-adjointness at fixed \(\lambda\) gives \[\mu'(\lambda)\int_0^1 U^2w\,dt
 =\int_0^1 \partial_\lambda\!\bigl(\lambda\tan(\lambda t)\bigr)U'U w\,dt.\] Integrating this expression by parts yields the exact identity \[
\mu'(\lambda)\int_0^1 U^2\cos(\lambda t)\,dt
 =\frac{\sin\lambda+\lambda\sec\lambda}{2}
   +\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_0^1\rho(\lambda t)U^2\,dt.\] The endpoint at zero vanishes, but \(U(1)=1\) makes the displayed endpoint at one nonzero. It is strictly positive for every \(0<\lambda<1\). Equation (6.28) contains only the integral term and is therefore false for the actual normalized eigenpair throughout that interval. The vanishing of \(U'(1)\) does not remove a boundary term involving \(U(1)^2\).

Equation reference: muasymptotic. There is also a decisive analytic check on the asserted sign. At \(\lambda=0\), the normalized eigenfunction is \(U_0(t)=-\cos(\pi t)\) and \(\mu(0)=\pi^2\). The eigenvalue family is even and \(C^2\). Since \[-\partial_{tt}+\lambda\tan(\lambda t)\partial_t
 =-\partial_{tt}+\lambda^2t\partial_t+O(\lambda^4),\] simple-eigenvalue differentiation gives \[\mu''(0)=
 \frac{2\int_0^1 U_0(t)\,tU_0'(t)\,dt}
      {\int_0^1 U_0(t)^2\,dt}=1.\] Both integrals were independently evaluated symbolically. It follows that \[
\mu(\lambda)=\pi^2+\tfrac12\lambda^2+o(\lambda^2),
\qquad \mu'(\lambda)=\lambda+o(\lambda).\] Thus \(\mu'(\lambda)>0\) for all sufficiently small positive \(\lambda\), contradicting the printed \(\mu'(\lambda)\le-2\lambda\). This is an analytic contradiction inside the stipulated parameter interval, not a numerical conjecture based on a few sample eigenvalues.

## Impact and a sufficient replacement

The faulty sign is used in the proof of the crossing estimate (6.25), which feeds the unique crossing in Lemma 6.3(iii), transversality in Proposition 6.4, and Theorem 1.5. The claimed estimate itself is not refuted here. A replacement argument is available: smooth even dependence of the simple Neumann eigenvalue gives \[|\mu'(\lambda)|\le C_\mu\lambda\] on each compact interval \([0,\lambda_0]\), and also on \([0,1]\) using the regular Sturm–Liouville family beyond \(1<\pi/2\). Combine this with the valid Dirichlet formula (6.27), the lower bound for \(\rho(\lambda t)/\cos(\lambda t)\), and \[\partial_\lambda\!\left(\lambda^2\sec^2(\lambda t)\right)\ge2\lambda\] to recover \(\sigma_\ell'(\lambda)\ge(2\ell^2-C)\lambda\), with an adjusted constant independent of \(\ell\). The required crossing for large \(\ell\) can then be justified. A false central derivative formula and the proof that relies on it remain Major defects even though an alternative estimate is attainable.

# Separate local corrections

## The cylinder coefficient \(\gamma\)

The coefficient identified in Remark 1.3(ii), p. 4, and again on p. 22 has the opposite sign to the expansion actually derived on p. 21. With \(\nu=N/2-1\), \(j=j_{N/2,m}\) and \(d=j_{\nu,1}\), the latter gives \[\gamma_{\rm correct}
 =\frac{d^2I_\nu(j)I_{\nu+1}(d)}{j^2I_\nu''(j)},\] without the additional minus sign printed in the two summaries.

For \(N=m=1\), set \(C=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). Then \[U_1(r)=-\cos(\pi r),\qquad
\phi_1(r)=C\cos(\pi r/2),\qquad
\gamma_{\rm printed}=\frac{C}{2\pi}.\] The radial derivative at \(r=1\) of the printed first-order correction is \[\left.\frac{d}{dr}\right|_{r=1}
 \{\phi_1(r)+\gamma_{\rm printed}rU_1'(r)\}=-C\pi\ne0.\] Replacing \(\gamma\) by \(-C/(2\pi)\) makes it zero. The source explicitly obtains the expansion in \(C^1\), so this is a genuine inconsistency with its Neumann data. This is assessed as a local transcription error because the correct coefficient is already present in the preceding derivation; the existential assertion in Theorem 1.2 does not prescribe the erroneous sign.

## The nodal count

Remark 1.3(i), p. 4, says that \(U_m\) has \(m-1\) zeros in \((0,1)\) and the solution has \(m\) nodal domains. The interlacing (4.2) instead places exactly \(m\) zeros of \(J_{N/2-1}\) below \(j_{N/2,m}\). For \(N\ge2\), the radial solution therefore has \(m+1\) nodal domains. For \(N=1\) the two sides of each positive radial zero are distinct, giving \(2m+1\) domains. In particular, \(N=m=1\) gives the explicit zeros \(t=\pm1/2\) of \(-\cos(\pi t)\) and three connected sign regions on \((-1,1)\times(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)\). The simple zeros persist along the small \(C^1\) branch. This corrects a supplementary description, not the Schiffer boundary conditions.

## Formulas whose required repairs are local

In (3.6), p. 12, \(h''/h-h'^2/h^2\) must be \(h''/(1+h)-h'^2/(1+h)^2\), consistently with (2.17) and the definition of \(F_\lambda\). The printed split is undefined at the trivial branch \(h=0\) even though the defining pullback operator is regular there.

On p. 25, the displayed split of \(F_\lambda\) omits the base function \(u_\lambda\). It sums to \(L_\lambda^hu\), whereas (6.7) defines \(L_\lambda^h(u+u_\lambda)\). For example, at \(u=0\) and constant \(h=c\ne0\), its actual value at \(t=1\) is \(\mu[1-(1+c)^2]\ne0\), while the displayed split is zero. To repair the split while keeping the asserted target spaces, include \(u_\lambda\) in the derivative terms and put \(\mu u_\lambda\) in the \(C_p^{1,\alpha}\) part; merely putting it in the part claimed to vanish on the boundary would introduce another error.

On p. 30, the first equality expressing the second mixed-boundary eigenvalue as an infimum over functions orthogonal to constants must be a lower bound. The min–max principle gives precisely that lower bound: every two-dimensional trial space contains a vector orthogonal to constants. The constrained infimum is strictly above the first positive Neumann eigenvalue, since equality would require a Neumann eigenfunction that also vanishes at \(1\). This is excluded by the ODE and normalization. This corrected inequality suffices to exclude the unwanted Fourier modes. Finally, on p. 31, the eigenrelation should be \(L_\lambda^0w_\lambda=-\sigma_\ell(\lambda)w_\lambda\) by (6.21). Changing that sign does not destroy transversality, which uses its nonvanishing derivative.

# Successful checks, materials, and limits

For the cylinder, the coordinate chain rule in (2.14)–(2.17) was independently checked. The choice \(D_t=t\cdot\nabla_t\) explains the boundary-compatible sum space \(\mathcal Y\). The distributional calculation in Lemma 3.7 correctly gives \[(\Delta_t+\lambda\partial_{xx})D_tu
 =2f+D_tf-2\lambda u_{xx},\] and its boundary data have the regularity needed for the upgrade. Compact embedding through \(C^{1,\alpha}\) explains the index-zero perturbation in Proposition 3.8.

The cylinder kernel calculation has exactly the required isolated mode. If \(\lambda_m=j_{N/2,m}^2-j_{N/2-1,1}^2\), modes \(k\ge2\) lie below the first radial Dirichlet eigenvalue; \(k=0\) is excluded by strict interlacing; \(k=1\) leaves the first radial Dirichlet eigenfunction. The range condition and \(\partial_\lambda L_\lambda v_*=-v_*\) give transversality. The return to the physical domain yields \(\mu_s=j_{N/2,m}^2/\lambda(s)\) and \(h_s=(1+h_{\varphi_s})/\sqrt{\lambda(s)}\), as required.

For the sphere, the coordinate differential operator and the reciprocal-height substitution in (6.6) were checked independently. The positivity of the mixed-boundary ground state, the corrected min–max comparison, and the nonzero endpoint derivatives support the intended spectral and reparametrization arguments after the corrections described above.
