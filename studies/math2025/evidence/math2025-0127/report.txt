Abstract: The acquired author manuscript of Boxer and Pilloni is examined through its support formalism, fixed-weight construction, interpolation and comparison theorems. The stated bilinear duality over the full weight algebra is incompatible with its central-character specialization and requires an involution on the second factor. Two further errors affect explicit steps in the core construction: the displayed support window is empty, and a Weyl-shift placement gives an incorrect ordinary-normalization exponent. Exact replacements are derived. Auxiliary boundedness, dyadic quotient and stratum-range defects have narrower effects. The overall assessment is Major for the specified structural statement and derivations; the existence of higher Hida complexes and the corrected control and comparison statements are not disproved.

Author: AI-assisted technical review

Title: Duality, support conditions and normalization in higher Hida theory

# Paper identity, scope and principal claims

The reviewed paper is George Boxer and Vincent Pilloni, Higher Hida theory for Siegel modular forms, associated with Inventiones mathematicae (2025), DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01393-2 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01393-2). The exact source is the 65-page manuscript acquired from the Imperial College author page [1]. Its intrinsic revision date was not established. All page references below are one-based physical PDF pages; they agree with the printed page numbers. No equivalence with the publisher’s version is asserted.

The central objects are indexed by the minimal representatives \({}^{M}W\) of \(W_M\backslash W\) for \(G=\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}\). Write \(d=g(g+1)/2\), and retain the paper’s opposite-Siegel-parabolic and Borel conventions. The complex \(\operatorname{R\Gamma}_w(K^p)\) is an ordinary part of a cohomology with partial support on an Igusa tower. A canonical theory before taking that ordinary part is not claimed. The tame level is neat, and the family construction uses the cofinal decomposable compact subgroups of §5.1. At \(p=2\), decomposability expressly includes a semidirect-product requirement.

The main assertions must be kept separate. Theorem 1.4.3 gives admissibility, fixed-weight control, and a Cousin spectral sequence. Its integral amplitude statement is proved for \(g=1,2\), while the all-\(g\) assertion remains Conjecture 5.6.1. The general amplitude result is rational. Classicality is obtained when \(C(\kappa)\) has one element; singular weights retain a spectral sequence. Comparison with higher Coleman theory is a normalized slope-zero comparison. The arithmetic-group vanishing of Theorem 1.5.1 is over \(\mathbb Q_p\), not an integral vanishing statement.

Selected source pages were actually viewed, and reviewer-written exact checks supplement the arguments below. The deep solid-category, toroidal, degree-theoretic and earlier Coleman inputs remain named dependencies. This is not a claim to have reconstructed every external theorem.

# Problem 1: full-weight duality requires an involution

## The incompatible formulations

Equation reference: eq:printedpair. Theorem 1.4.8 [1, p. 5] explicitly asserts a perfect \(\Lambda\)-bilinear pairing \[
 M_w^\bullet\otimes_\Lambda M_{w',\mathrm{cusp}}^\bullet
 \longrightarrow\Lambda[-d],
 \qquad w'=w_{0,M}ww_0,\quad
 \Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[T(\mathbb Z_p)]],\] with adjoint \(t\mapsto w_0(t^{-1})\). Theorem 5.5.2, p. 57, repeats the pairing. The preceding definitions, pp. 55–57, use the same full torus and weight algebra for every \(w\), with \[M_w^\bullet=\operatorname{RHom}_{T(\mathbb Z_p)}
 \bigl(1,\operatorname{R\Gamma}_w(K^p)\widehat\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p}\Lambda\bigr).\] The preceding \(w\)-twist of the torus action fixes its center. No involution on the second \(\Lambda\)-action is specified in these definitions.

Equation reference: eq:dualweight. By contrast, the fixed-weight Theorem 5.5.1 correctly pairs \[\kappa \quad\hbox{with}\quad
 \kappa'=-w_{0,M}\kappa-2\rho_{\mathrm{nc}},
 \qquad \chi\quad\hbox{with}\quad w_0\chi^{-1}.\] With the common specialization parameter \[\nu=-w^{-1}w_{0,M}(\kappa+\rho)-\rho,\] a direct calculation gives \[
 \nu'=-w_0\nu.\] Indeed \(w_{0,M}\rho_{\mathrm{nc}}=\rho_{\mathrm{nc}}\), \(w_{0,M}\rho=2\rho_{\mathrm{nc}}-\rho\), and \(w_0\rho=-\rho\). Substitution in the formula for \(w'\) proves [eq:dualweight] (#eq:dualweight). The map \(-w_0\) is the identity on the derived symplectic character space, but negates the scalar-center character. The full \(GSp\) torus in §3.1 therefore matters.

## An admissible nonzero fiber

Take \(g=1\), \(p=5\), \(w=1\). In the paper’s coordinates \(\rho=(-1;0)\), and Remark 4.3.3, p. 47, identifies the Hodge line with weight \((1;-1)\). Thus \[\kappa=(4;-4),\qquad \nu=(-2;4),\qquad
 \kappa'=(-2;4),\qquad \nu'=(-2;-4).\] The parameter \(\nu\) is dominant in the convention \(0\geq\nu_1\). At the same \(\Lambda\)-point \(-\nu\), the \(w'=w_0\) factor instead has weight \((-2;-4)\). Its central component differs from the required Serre partner.

There is a nonzero ordinary first fiber. Let \[E_4(q)=1+240\sum_{n\geq1}\sigma_3(n)q^n,\qquad
 f(q)=E_4(q)-5^3E_4(q^5).\] The classical Eisenstein series and its degeneracy pullback are modular of weight four. Their combination has constant coefficient \(-124\), hence is nonzero. The identity \[\sigma_3(5n)=(1+5^3)\sigma_3(n)-5^3\sigma_3(n/5)\] with the last term zero when \(5\nmid n\), proves \(U_5f=f\) coefficient by coefficient. Pullback to sufficiently small neat tame level preserves this nonzero class. Modularity of \(E_4\) is the standard classical input; checking finitely many Fourier coefficients would not prove it.

Under the diagonal-invariant construction of \(M_w^\bullet\), an inherited unit-torus operator \(u\) acts as \([u]^{-1}\). At specialization \(-\nu\), choose \(u=6I_2\). Then \(u\) acts by \(6^4\), whereas its prescribed adjoint \(w_0(u^{-1})=u^{-1}\) on the second factor at that same point acts by \(6^{-4}\). A bilinear pairing \(P\) satisfying the asserted adjoint relation must obey \[(6^4-6^{-4})P=0,\qquad
 6^4-6^{-4}=\frac{1679615}{1296}\ne0.\] Over \(\mathbb Q_5\) it is zero, contradicting perfectness at the nonzero fiber. A uniform inverse convention for the regular \(\Lambda\)-action changes both factors together and does not remove this incompatibility. The exact weight arithmetic and coefficients \(0\)–\(20\) are saved in `aux_families/exact_duality_checks.json`; the all-coefficient identity is the proof.

## Correction and impact

Equation reference: eq:correctpair. Define the algebra involution \[\iota:\Lambda\longrightarrow\Lambda,\qquad
 [t]\longmapsto[w_0(t^{-1})].\] The compatible formulation uses \[
 M_w^\bullet\otimes_\Lambda
 \iota^*M_{w',\mathrm{cusp}}^\bullet\longrightarrow\Lambda[-d],\] or states explicitly that the original pairing is \(\iota\)-sesquilinear. The twist changes the second specialization to \(-\nu'\), as required by [eq:dualweight] (#eq:dualweight). It is an exact repair of the character and adjoint obstruction. The fixed-weight Serre pairing, transposition of support and finite-level perfectness supply the corresponding construction; their background duality remains a dependency. The character correction and its specializations are verified here; a complete new proof of the global twisted perfect pairing, including every transition-map compatibility, is not claimed.

A relevant countercheck is the authors’ earlier modular-curve paper [2, pp. 17–18], which explicitly changes the weight-algebra action before forming its \(\Lambda\)-linear Serre pairing. Its one-variable parametrization differs from the full-torus formula here, so it is supporting evidence for the need to specify a twist, not a claim that the two formulas are identical.

This is Major: [eq:printedpair] (#eq:printedpair), with the displayed actions, is a false main structural guarantee at an allowed nonzero fiber. The corrected duality can coexist with the construction, control and amplitude theorems. No failure of their existence statements follows. A silently intended twist would be a substantive clarification of the statement, not a validation of the untwisted formula.

# Problem 2: the displayed support window is empty

Equation reference: eq:emptywindow. Section 4.4.1 [1, p. 50] defines the space used in Lemma 4.4.2 by \[
 S_{\mathbb F_p,(w-1,w+1)}^{\mathrm{tor}}
 =\bigcap_i Z_{i,<w_i+1}\cap Z_{i,>w_i+1}.\] Proposition 4.1.13(1), p. 42, says that \(Z_{i,<j}\) and \(Z_{i,>j}\) have disjoint support. Consequently [eq:emptywindow] (#eq:emptywindow) is empty whenever the paired indices are defined. This is confirmed by the source image, not an extraction ambiguity.

For a concrete allowed case take \(g=1\), depth \(n=1\), and the longest representative, for which \(w_1=0\). Both indices in [eq:emptywindow] (#eq:emptywindow) are \(1\), so the space is empty. The corresponding ordinary étale stratum is not empty. The smooth curve \(y^2=x^3-x\) over \(\mathbb F_5\) has eight points and Frobenius trace \(-2\), hence is ordinary. Over an algebraic closure it admits the required étale subgroup and neat prime-to-five level. The saved exact count is an independent finite witness; the ordinary-stratum description comes from the stated moduli problem.

An empty scheme cannot be a compactification of that stratum. Thus the base case of Lemma 4.4.2, invoked by Proposition 4.4.4 and then by the definitions of the fixed-weight and family complexes, is invalid as printed.

## A verified replacement

Replace the second endpoint by \(w_i-1\): \[Y_w=\bigcap_i
 \bigl(\operatorname{supp}Z_{i,<w_i+1}\cap\operatorname{supp}Z_{i,>w_i-1}\bigr).\] At endpoints use full-special-fiber support for \(Z_{i,>-1}\) and \(Z_{i,<i+1}\). These conventions agree with the extreme positive or negative parts of \(Z_i-njV(p)\), since \(0\leq Z_i\leq niV(p)\). The corrected \(Y_w\) is closed in the proper special fiber. Removing \(D_{w,+}\cup D_{w,-}\) gives the ordinary \(w\)-chart by Proposition 4.1.13. If density is required, take its closure, as in Proposition 4.4.4.

It is necessary to verify strict dynamics, rather than infer it from pointwise monotonicity. The degree inequalities of 4.2.10–4.2.11 supply the local relation \[a=q b,\qquad a=p_1^*f_+,\quad b=p_2^*f_+,\quad
 q\in\sqrt{(a)}\] on the corrected window. The two boundary supports for a fixed index are disjoint. On a finite cover choose a common \(N\) with \(q^N=u a\). Then, without cancelling a possible zero divisor, \[a^N=q^Nb^N=u q b^{N+1}.\] Thus \((a)^N\subseteq(b)^{N+1}\), which is exactly \[p_2^*D_+\leq \frac{N}{N+1}p_1^*D_+\] in the paper’s ideal-power ordering. The other sign gives strict contraction of \(D_-\); finite sums admit a common strict bound. The nilpotent-thickening lemma then gives the higher reductions modulo \(p^s\).

In Corollary 4.4.3 the support containment needed for the factorization is sufficient; the printed stronger equality need not hold. On \(Y_w\), the excess divisor contains the support of \(p_1^*D_++p_2^*D_+\), since outside the excess one is on an ordinary correspondence chart, and the window selects only the \(w\)-chart. The source’s square-and-glue argument consequently supplies the required global map.

The assessment is Major for this explicit invalid construction in the core chain \(4.4.2\to4.4.4\to5.1.4\to5.2\). The replacement above verifies the missing local input, conditional on the named degree and compactification results. It does not claim that higher Hida theory itself is false.

# Problem 3: the ordinary-normalization exponent has the wrong shift

Remark 4.3.12 [1, p. 48] gives an exponent comparing the global normalization with the local \(w'\)-normalization and asserts strict positivity for \(w'\notin C(\kappa)\). Its displayed difference puts the extra \(\rho\) inside both Weyl actions. That placement changes the calculation.

Equation reference: eq:correctexponent. Put \(A=w_{0,M}(\kappa+\rho)\). Proposition 4.3.9, on the same page, gives the correct normalization character \[\nu_w=-w^{-1}A-\rho.\] Therefore the ratio of the global normalization, with \(w\in C(\kappa)\), to the \(w'\)-normalization is the character \[\nu_w-\nu_{w'}=(w')^{-1}A-w^{-1}A.\] Its valuation is \[
 n_{w'}=\bigl\langle (w')^{-1}A-w^{-1}A,\ v(t)\bigr\rangle.\] The common \(-\rho\) cancels before the Weyl actions. For \(t=\lambda(p)\) the ratio is \(p^{n_{w'}}\); for general \(t\), retain the character ratio, including its unit factor. This unit has no effect on the ordinary projector.

Take \(g=1\), \(\kappa=(2;0)\), \(\rho=(-1;0)\), \(w=1\), \(w'=s\), and \(t=\operatorname{diag}(1,p)\in T^{++}\). Then \(C(\kappa)=\{1\}\), \(A=(1;0)\), \(\nu_1=(0;0)\), and \(\nu_s=(2;0)\). The character \((2;0)\) evaluates on \(t\) as \(p^{-1}\). The correct ratio is \(p\), so \(n_s=1\). In the printed expression the inner weight is \(\kappa+2\rho=0\), producing zero. It therefore contradicts the stated positivity in this permitted example. The exact calculation is saved in `aux_fixed/exact_checks.json`.

Formula [eq:correctexponent] (#eq:correctexponent) also restores the general argument: \(w^{-1}A\) is the anti-dominant orbit representative. Pairing with a strictly dominant cocharacter is uniquely minimized there, modulo the stabilizer. The difference is positive precisely for \(w'\notin C(\kappa)\). Multiplication by \(p^{n_{w'}}\) then removes those terms from the ordinary part. This is the normalization step that selects the \(C(\kappa)\)-terms in Theorem 4.5.2 and its regular-weight classicality consequence.

The assessment is Major for the invalid essential calculation, not for an alleged failure of the corrected positivity theorem. The neighboring Proposition 4.3.9 is strong counterevidence against broader damage and directly supplies the verified repair.

# Problem 4: coherent cohomology alone does not ensure finiteness

Lemma 2.10.1(4) [1, p. 29] allows an arbitrary complex \(F\) in the displayed derived category, assumes coherent cohomology and proper \(Z\), and concludes finite cohomology for \(\operatorname{R\Gamma}_Z(Y,F)=\operatorname{R\Gamma}(Z,i^!F)\). No lower boundedness is imposed.

Let \(k=\mathbb F_2\), \(A=k[e]/(e^2)\), \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}A\), \(Z=\operatorname{Spec}k\), and \[F=\bigoplus_{n\geq0} k[n].\] Each \(H^{-n}(F)=k\) is coherent, and \(Z\) is proper over \(k\). A bounded-above free resolution of \(k\) is \[\cdots\xrightarrow{e}A\xrightarrow{e}A\xrightarrow{e}A\longrightarrow k.\] Multiplication by \(e\) has kernel equal to image. In the Hom complex into \(F\), every differential vanishes, while degree zero is \[H^0\operatorname{RHom}_A(k,F)=\prod_{n\geq0}k.\] This is not finite-dimensional. Thus every stated hypothesis holds and the asserted finiteness fails. Finite truncations \(F_N\) give dimensions \(N+1\); exact binary-ring checks for \(0\leq N\leq8\) corroborate the explicit infinite-product argument.

Adding \(F\in D^+_{\mathrm{coh}}(Y)\), in the surrounding noetherian setting, repairs the assertion: a fixed total degree receives only finitely many coherent Ext contributions, and proper coherent cohomology is finite. A use involving derived pullback must preserve this boundedness or impose the appropriate finite Tor-dimension.

This is Minor. The fixed-weight applications use locally free automorphic bundles, boundary twists, and nebentypus bundles invertible on the relevant ordinary charts; their required pullbacks are bounded. No failure of the main Hida construction is established by this unbounded-complex example. The concern is the unrestricted auxiliary lemma.

# Problem 5: the dyadic Levi quotient need not be the intersection

On p. 37, §3.4 defines \(U_K=K\cap U_P(\mathbb Q_p)(1)\) and \(M_K=K\cap M(\mathbb Q_p)\). It proves \(K=M_K\ltimes U_K\) when \(p>2\), but then uses, without that restriction, \[1\longrightarrow U_K\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow M_K\longrightarrow1\] and identifies the group in Proposition 3.4.9(5) with \(M_K\). At \(p=2\) the Levi projection can be larger than this intersection.

Here is an explicit genus-one compact. Write the lower parabolic in coordinates \(U(u)\operatorname{diag}(a,d)\), whose Levi action on \(u\) is \(d/a\). For \(a,d\in\mathbb Z_2^\times\) put \(\epsilon(a)=(a-1)/2\bmod2\), and define \[K=\{(a,d,u):u\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z_2,\quad 2u\bmod2=\epsilon(a)\}.\] The function \(\epsilon\) is a multiplicative-group character into \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). Every \(d/a\) is odd and acts trivially on \(\tfrac12\mathbb Z_2/\mathbb Z_2\). These facts prove closure under multiplication and inversion. The subgroup is compact and open, and is stable under the Galois action multiplying \(u\) by the odd cyclotomic character. It therefore has exactly the descent required in §3.4.5.

Its unipotent intersection is \(\mathbb Z_2\). Its Levi projection is all \((\mathbb Z_2^\times)^2\), whereas its Levi intersection requires \(a\equiv1\pmod4\). For example \((-1,-1,\tfrac12)\in K\), but \((-1,-1,0)\notin K\). The natural map from \(K\cap M\) to \(K/U_K\) is not surjective. The reviewer-written quotient calculation modulo four has eight group elements and projection/intersection sizes four and two, respectively; the analytic construction above proves the full claim.

Replace the quotient by \(\operatorname{pr}_M(K)\), or restrict this assertion to split compacts. The later §5.1 explicitly imposes the latter condition, even at two, and uses a cofinal collection of such compacts. Thus the family control theorem does not rely on the false general intersection identity. The assessment is Minor for the unrestricted finite-level formulation; no failure of the smooth quotient or of the later decomposable tower is established.

# Problem 6: stratum assertions need their positive-level and special-fiber ranges

## Depth zero

Section 4.1 [1, pp. 40–41] starts with \(n\geq0\), then defines strata by requiring certain graded subgroups \(\operatorname{Gr}_i\) of order \(p^n\) to be multiplicative and asserts that they lie in the ordinary locus. At \(n=0\) all these groups are trivial, hence multiplicative. The condition selects the entire special fiber.

For example the smooth elliptic curve \(y^2=x^3+1\) over \(\mathbb F_5\) has six points and trace zero, so it is supersingular. It nevertheless satisfies all the depth-zero graded-group conditions. The exact point count and discriminant check are saved with the ordinary example in `aux_fixed/exact_checks.json`. Restrict this Iwahori-stratum construction to \(n\geq1\). Positive depths are cofinal in the family tower, so the resulting limit construction is unaffected.

## Whole schemes versus special fibers

Proposition 4.1.13(2), p. 42, states that \[\bigcap_{i=1}^k
 \bigl(Z_{i,>j_i}^{\,c}\cap Z_{i,<j_i}^{\,c}\bigr)\] is empty unless every increment \(j_i-j_{i-1}\) is zero or one. As an assertion about the whole scheme, it is false. Take \(g=2\), \(n=1\), \(j_1=0,j_2=2\). Both indices satisfy \(0\leq j_i\leq i\), and the sequence is increasing, but its second increment is two. Every displayed divisor is vertical: it is a positive or negative part of \(Z_i-nj_iV(p)\), with \(Z_i\) supported over \(p=0\). The complementary open therefore contains the entire nonempty generic fiber, unchanged by these vertical blow-ups.

The empty-stratum assertion belongs to the special fiber or its formal completion. This correction leaves the valid zero/one-increment charts and Corollary 4.1.14 available. Both range defects are Minor: they are explicit false auxiliary assertions at the stated boundary, with no demonstrated damage to the positive-level formal construction.

# Positive checks, local corrections and dependency limits

## Support formalism and interpolation

The distinction between ideal-power inequalities, set inclusion and topological compactness in §2 is essential. The support limits are not freely interchanged. Strict contraction supplies the off-diagonal maps in 2.5.3–2.5.4; after taking the ordinary part, their composites become invertible and give the comparison equivalences. The Artinian hypotheses are used for finite direct factors.

Base-change and perfectness in §2.6 retain flatness of the coefficient sheaf. The finite-group version uses an actual finite étale torsor and group-ring flatness, not division by the group order. The transposed-correspondence duality of 2.7.4 retains smoothness, local freeness and the Artinian base. Flatness of both correspondence maps is used in the iteration argument. The Cousin spectral sequence comes from a finite filtration and retains its total degree \(a+b\).

In §3 the cyclotomic base extension reconciles the multiplicative and constant Tate-module level structures. The manuscript explicitly warns that the chosen integral models need not commute with this base change. The ordinary models, rather than arbitrary deep-level integral models, provide the smooth local input.

In 5.2.6–5.2.7 the order \(\lim_m\operatorname{colim}_n\) modulo \(p^m\) produces continuous functions. The finite \(p\)-group rings used for minimal complexes are local; the final quotient \(T(\mathbb F_p)\) has order prime to \(p\), including \(p=2\). The shift \(-w^{-1}w_{0,M}\rho-\rho\) in the control theorem is absorbed by the normalized trace and must not be dropped.

## Comparison, slopes and vanishing

The source image on p. 59 confirms the closure condition \(T(\overline U)\subset U\) in Definition 6.1.3. Its omission in plain-text extraction was not a paper defect. That condition supplies the compactness reduction in Lemma 6.1.5. The two kinds of neighborhoods in 6.1.10–6.1.11 contract to the same ordinary stratum; their finite-slope comparison is the bridge to Coleman theory.

The integral comparison preserves a lattice on each finite-slope part. It does not assert that all overconvergent cohomology is finite-dimensional. In 6.2.14, the bound on poles is independent of the reduction exponent before passing to the inverse limit.

The passage from strict to weak dominance in 6.2.10 and 6.2.16 can be completed precisely. For \(t\in T^+\), fix \(s\in T^{++}\); then \(t^ns\in T^{++}\) for every positive \(n\). On any common finite-slope eigencharacter \(\lambda\), \[n\,v(\lambda(t))+v(\lambda(s))\geq0\quad(n\geq1)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v(\lambda(t))\geq0.\] Group generation alone would not give this inequality, but the available all-strict-element bounds do. This is a verified omitted-step completion, not another asserted defect.

The bounded external checks of [2] and [3] concern the stated duality and vanishing dependencies. More precisely, [3, Theorem 6.7.3, p. 162] gives locally analytic finite-slope amplitudes \([0,\ell(w)]\) for cusp coefficients and \([\ell(w),d]\) otherwise. It imposes no regular-weight or strongly-small-slope hypothesis for these ranges; a domain hypothesis belongs only to its additional torsion-free conclusion. Selecting slope zero through [1, 6.2.15] therefore supports its rational amplitude assertion.

For the arithmetic application, [3, Theorem 5.12.11 and Remark 5.12.12, p. 132] permits the strongly-small-slope condition to be weakened after proving its Conjecture 5.9.2. That is the bound supplied by [1, 6.2.10]. Trivial coefficients are allowed: in [3, Definition 5.11.2, p. 126], put \(\kappa_w=-w_{0,M}w\rho-\rho\). For \(v\ne w\), the excluded threshold is \(\rho-v^{-1}w\rho\), a nonzero sum of positive roots. The zero slope does not dominate that threshold. The opposite-sign condition follows from [3, Proposition 5.11.3, p. 127]. Non-neat principal levels may be treated rationally by pullback and trace at an auxiliary neat tame level; this does not give integral torsion vanishing.

The complete earlier proofs, the solid six-functor foundations, Fargues’ degree theory, Raynaud quotient theory, toroidal comparison, and the lowest-weight-kernel input from Pilloni’s earlier work were not independently reconstructed. Those limits are retained in the coverage ledger.

## Localized notation

Several further slips can be corrected from adjacent formulas without creating additional substantive Problems. On p. 2, \(M_\mu\) must be the Levi, as correctly defined on pp. 33 and 36. On p. 34 the minimal-representative test is \(w^{-1}\Phi_M^+\subset\Phi^+\), with Levi positive roots, not the displayed non-Levi set \(\Phi^{+,M}\). At \(g=1\) the printed test excludes the reflection although \(W_M=1\); the correct dominant-cone and permutation descriptions on that page remain intact. Exact enumeration for \(g=1,2,3,4\) gives \(2,4,8,16\) minimal representatives and the expected length symmetry.

The line-bundle map in 2.5.3 needs its negative \(D_-\) sign; the ensuing formula has it. The induced-representation action in 4.3.1 should read \((m'\cdot f)(m)=f((m')^{-1}m)\). The normalization in 4.3.17 is supplied unambiguously by 4.3.9 and the following global formula. The singleton-\(C(\kappa)\) corollaries 4.5.4–4.5.5 follow for \(M\)-dominant weights from their preceding theorem, despite the narrower printed dominance label. In the proof of 5.5.1 the pre-conjugation Serre partner needs weight \(-\kappa-2\rho_{\mathrm{nc}}\) and the cuspidal twist. Finally, the last defining line of 6.2.8 needs \(U_{\geq w-\epsilon}\), and 6.2.13 needs \(A/p^r\), as its proof states. These corrections are documented, but isolated typography is not used to inflate the assessment.

# Overall impact and review state

The full-weight untwisted pairing is contradicted under the displayed action convention. The empty window and incorrect exponent invalidate two explicit core derivations; their replacements were checked at the required scheme and character levels. The remaining numbered defects concern auxiliary domains. None of these findings is a counterexample to the existence of higher Hida complexes, the corrected regular-weight control theorem, or the rational comparison framework.
