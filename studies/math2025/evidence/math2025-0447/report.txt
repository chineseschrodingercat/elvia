Abstract: The acquired preprint contains four Major problems in the route to its spectral and pointwise convergence estimates. The uniformly stated point-sampling input estimate is false for smooth concentrated data; the empirical estimates do not justify the variational maxima; repeated eigenvalues are not handled by the scalar eigenvector argument; and random local factors and a corrector term are lost in the pointwise argument. A local negative-norm definition also requires correction. These findings concern the specified arXiv v1. They do not establish that the final journal version or a correctly formulated homogenization result is false.

Optimal Convergence Rates for the Spectrum of the Graph Laplacian on Poisson Point Clouds

# Exact source and intended mathematical setting

The source is Scott Armstrong and Raghavendra Venkatraman, Optimal convergence rates for the spectrum of the graph Laplacian on Poisson point clouds, arXiv:2312.08149v1 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08149v1), submitted 13 December 2023; the PDF author date is 14 December 2023. It has 23 numbered pages. The acquisition manifest connects this work to the assigned journal DOI 10.1007/s10208-025-09696-9 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-025-09696-9). Equivalence to the journal text is not asserted.

The intended model is the unit-radius geometric graph at a fixed intensity \(\alpha>\alpha_c(d)\), restricted to its selected connected component, with zero values in a boundary shell. The paper does not include \(\alpha=\alpha_c(d)\). Write \(R=3^m\) and \(U_m=RU_0\). Its intended rate is \[|R^2\lambda_{m,k}-\lambda_{0,k}|
 \lesssim R^{-1}\lambda_{0,k}^{3/2}
 \begin{cases}1,&d\geq3,\\ \sqrt m,&d=2.\end{cases}\] The allowed spectral range is correspondingly \(R^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda_{0,k}}\ll1\), with the additional \(\sqrt m\) in dimension two.

Several evident transcription errors occur in the kernel branches and boundary inequalities in (1.2), (1.3), and (2.1). The checks below use the intended nonnegative symmetric kernel, interior equation, and zero boundary shell. They persist after those local corrections. The barred sums are read as the normalized averages defined on p. 8; no mismatch is inferred merely from the appearance of the summation glyph.

# P1. The uniform point-sampling input estimate is false (Major)

Theorem 1.1 and (1.3)–(1.4), pp. 2–3, assert one random scale and one finite constant for every \(\bar u\in H^2(U_m)\cap H_0^1(U_m)\): \[-\mathcal Lu(x)=-\nabla\cdot\bar a\nabla\bar u(x),
 \qquad
 \|u-\bar u\|_{\underline L^2(\eta_*(U_m))}
 \leq C\|\bar u\|_{\underline L^2(U_m)}R^{-1}L_m,\] where \(L_m=1\) for \(d\geq3\) and \(L_m=\sqrt m\) for \(d=2\). No averaging of the forcing or frequency restriction is stated.

Fix any nontrivial realization with an interior vertex \(x_0\) and invertible Dirichlet matrix \(A_g=-\mathcal L\). Choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi\) supported in the unit ball and equal to one near its center. For \(\rho\) smaller than the distance from \(x_0\) to the other sampled points and to the boundary, put \[\bar u_\rho(x)=
 -\frac{|x-x_0|^2}{2\,\operatorname{tr}\bar a}\,
 \chi\!\left(\frac{x-x_0}{\rho}\right).\] This function is smooth and compactly supported in \(U_m\). At every sampled point its value is zero. Its sampled forcing is one at \(x_0\) and zero everywhere else. Hence \[u_\rho=A_g^{-1}e_{x_0}\ne0\] is independent of \(\rho\), and the left side of the claimed estimate is a fixed positive number. By contrast, \[\|\bar u_\rho\|_{\underline L^2(U_m)}
 =O(\rho^{2+d/2})\longrightarrow0.\] This contradicts (1.4) for every finite \(C\), even at one fixed allowed scale.

The witness uses smooth data, so the objection is stronger than the lack of a canonical point evaluation for a general Sobolev equivalence class. Choosing it after fixing the graph is legitimate for the stated event that is uniform over every \(\bar u\). The restated input is used in (2.15), p. 12.

The cited companion result [AV23b] was not independently retrieved and is not being refuted here. A repair requires the actual sampling or coarse-graining operator, the correct data norm and regularity, and verification that the corrected estimate applies to the trial functions used below. A change of norm label alone is not a verified repair.

# P2. The empirical estimates do not support the variational maxima (Major)

## The selected cluster is not an independent sample

On p. 20, the proof of Lemma A.2 calls the variables indexed by the selected connected component independent and identically distributed, and applies (A.3). This is not the conditional uniform sample formed by all Poisson points. Connectivity-based selection introduces dependence. For example, a selected two-vertex connected component must have its two vertices within unit distance, whereas two independent uniform points in a domain of diameter greater than one need not.

Moreover, the displayed variance-only exponential bound is not the general Bernstein inequality for bounded variables: a range-dependent term, or a justified restriction to its quadratic regime, is needed. Lemma A.3 on p. 21 refers back to the same argument. Thus the stated proofs do not supply the cluster concentration estimates they use.

## Uniform normalization over the trial class is impossible

There is a separate pathwise obstruction to (2.12)–(2.13), pp. 10–11. Those formulas retain a maximum over continuous, continuum-normalized functions with upper bounds on their supremum and gradient energy. The passage from a fixed-function probability estimate to that maximum requires simultaneous control that is false on the stated class.

Take an allowed dimension \(d\geq3\) and a finite realized point set. Choose smooth cutoffs equal to zero at each point and one outside disjoint balls of radius \(\rho\). Their product \(h_\rho\) satisfies \[h_\rho\to1\ \hbox{in }L^2(U_m),\qquad
 \|h_\rho\|_\infty\leq1,\qquad
 \frac{1}{|U_m|}\int_{U_m}|\nabla h_\rho|^2=O(N\rho^{d-2})\to0.\] Normalize \(f_\rho=h_\rho/\|h_\rho\|_{\underline L^2(U_m)}\). Then its continuum normalized \(L^2\) norm is one, its supremum tends to one, and its energy tends to zero, while its empirical squared norm is zero. These are admissible competitors whenever the stated supremum cap has slack above one, as occurs for allowed domains with sufficiently large \(\lambda_{0,1}\), or in the class \(\mathcal A_T\) for sufficiently large \(T\). The competitors are not required to have zero boundary trace. Taking a small positive value at the sample points instead of zero gives nonzero denominators arbitrarily close to zero.

This refutes the required uniform empirical normalization, including under the energy restriction (2.8). It does not refute every possible fixed-function concentration theorem. Restricting one comparison to a deterministic ground state might avoid one maximum, but does not establish the opposite comparison over graph-dependent trial functions.

## The probability and induction closure also need justification

On p. 13 the proof maximizes over \(T\) although the displayed failure probability depends on \(T^{-2}\) in its exponent. A bound with a \(T\)-independent numerical right side does not itself hold on one uniform event. The subsequent choice of \(T\) uses an eigenvector supremum estimate whose derivation already invokes the conditional eigenvalue bound.

The orthogonality transfer (2.33)–(2.34), p. 18, is explicitly granted and is not subsequently established before the proof ends. In (2.36), the extra \(R^{-2}\) in the supremum bound even makes the continuum-normalized class empty for fixed \(K\) and large \(m\); this factor must at least be corrected. These are further missing closure steps, rather than additional claimed counterexamples to the final eigenvalue rate.

A complete repair needs controlled comparison maps or finite-dimensional trial spaces, valid concentration for the selected cluster, and simultaneous estimates with the advertised dependence on \(m\), \(k\), and \(T\). The review does not supply that replacement proof.

# P3. The eigenvector argument needs spectral-subspace control (Major)

The gap in (1.6), p. 4, is the distance to the nearest distinct eigenvalue. Theorem 1.2 does not impose simplicity, and the allowed domains can have repeated Dirichlet eigenvalues. Nevertheless, the induction declares the optimizer in (2.35) unique (p. 18) and extends the ground-state eigenvector argument by changes of notation (p. 17).

A gap to the rest of the spectrum controls a spectral subspace, not an arbitrarily fixed vector within a repeated eigenspace. The exact finite-dimensional diagnostic \[A_0=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\lambda,\nu),\qquad
 A_\varepsilon=
 \begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\varepsilon&0\\
 \varepsilon&\lambda&0\\0&0&\nu\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon<\lambda<\nu,\] has perturbation norm \(\varepsilon\) and a fixed external gap. Its split eigenvectors, scaled to have inner product one with \(e_1\), are \(e_1\pm e_2\), both at distance one from \(e_1\). A scalar normalization cannot remove the rotation. This is a diagnostic of the perturbation argument, not a claim that these matrices are themselves Poisson graph Laplacians.

There is also a resonance issue in the displayed residual equation. Let \[\bar\lambda=R^{-2}\lambda_{0,k},\qquad
 c=\sum_i\partial_i\phi_{0,k}\varphi_{e_i},\qquad
 v=\phi_{0,k}+c,\qquad w=\phi_{m,k}-v.\] Using the stated eigenvalue equations, the correct identity is \[(\mathcal L+\lambda_{m,k})w
 =-\mathcal Lv+\nabla\cdot\bar a\nabla\phi_{0,k}
   +(\bar\lambda-\lambda_{m,k})\phi_{0,k}
   -\lambda_{m,k}c.\] Equation (2.25), p. 14, has a minus sign on the left and a plus sign in the final term. The corrected operator has a kernel at the eigenvalue. Testing with \(w\) therefore does not give an unrestricted coercive estimate; projection and normalization conditions must be supplied.

For a simple isolated eigenvalue, an appropriately oriented eigenvector estimate may be recovered from a projected resolvent argument. For repeated eigenvalues, the theorem should state a cluster/subspace estimate and an explicit basis-selection convention. Neither repair, with all the claimed constants and probability quantifiers, is completed in the reviewed text.

# P4. Random local factors and a corrector are lost in the pointwise proof (Major)

The proof on pp. 15–17 starts with a local regularity radius \(\mathcal X(x)\) having stretched-exponential integrability. The estimate requires \[R_x=\tfrac12 R\,\operatorname{dist}(\zeta,\partial U_0)
 \geq\mathcal X(x),\qquad x=R\zeta.\] The global condition \(R>\mathcal X\) alone does not establish this for every vertex, particularly near the boundary.

The edge estimate on p. 16 retains a factor \(\mathcal X(x)^{d/2}\). The next page’s pointwise estimate retains a factor of order \(\mathcal X(x)^2\) after taking square roots. Stretched-exponential integrability of these factors does not turn them into the deterministic constant \(C(d,\alpha)\) stated in Theorem 1.3. Uniform control over all centers and all stated scales requires a separate argument, with its resulting factors or probability qualification retained.

A second omission is exact. The proof defines \(w=\phi_m-\phi_0-c\) but later uses \[\|\phi_m\|_\infty\leq\|w\|_\infty+\|\phi_0\|_\infty\] on p. 17. The term \(\|c\|_\infty\) is missing. Controlling \(w\) does not by itself control \(\phi_m-\phi_0\) or produce the deterministic \(T\) used to close P2.

The regularity scaling on p. 14 also needs correction: the wavelength is \(R/\sqrt{\lambda_{0,k}}\), whereas the displayed substitution omits \(R\) and reverses this scale. Interior Hessian estimates cannot simply be applied globally up to every allowed convex-domain boundary. An obtuse planar sector has Dirichlet eigenfunctions behaving like \(r^\nu\sin(\nu\theta)\), \(1<\nu<2\), with unbounded Hessian at the corner; a boundary treatment is required.

These observations invalidate the written derivation of the deterministic pointwise and every-edge estimates. They do not prove that every appropriately qualified replacement theorem is false. The local random factors, the corrector, the boundary regime, and spectral-gap dependence must all be tracked in such a replacement.

# P5. Correct the negative-norm pairing (Minor)

On p. 8 the displayed definition is \[\sup_{g\ne0}\frac{\langle f,g\rangle_{H^1}}{\|g\|_{H^1}},\] with zero-boundary test functions. For a Dirichlet function \(f\), Cauchy–Schwarz and the choice \(g=f\) make this exactly \(\|f\|_{H^1}\), not its negative Sobolev norm. The numerator should use the intended distributional or \(L^2\) pairing. Later applications, including p. 16, use that corrected pairing implicitly. This is a local definitional repair; it does not resolve P1–P4.

# Verified structure, limitations and assessment

The graph energy identity, positivity of the intended Dirichlet inverse, ordinary minmax principle, and continuum scaling \(\mu_{0,k}=R^2/\lambda_{0,k}\) were checked. Conditional on a valid estimate \[|\mu_{m,k}-\mu_{0,k}|\leq C L_m\sqrt{\mu_{0,k}},
 \qquad |\mu_{m,k}-\mu_{0,k}|\leq\tfrac12\mu_{0,k},\] taking reciprocals indeed gives \[|R^2\lambda_{m,k}-\lambda_{0,k}|
 \leq 2C L_m R^{-1}\lambda_{0,k}^{3/2}.\] Thus the desired exponent is consistent with the inverse-eigenvalue scaling. The missing steps are the analytical and stochastic comparisons needed to obtain that premise.

Appendix A.1’s eigenfunction supremum scaling and the formal product identities were checked within their stated regularity scope. The general homogenization, corrector, and large-scale regularity theorems cited from other works were not independently reproduced. Nor was optimality against every admissible random model established. The discussion of higher-order convergence, manifolds, and Neumann conditions was not treated as a proved extension of the complete Dirichlet argument. No missing implementation is counted as a mathematical defect.

The assessment for the acquired v1 is Major. P1 includes a direct smooth-data counterexample, and P2 includes a direct obstruction to the required uniform trial-class estimate. P3 and P4 identify central statement/proof gaps with exact diagnostics and necessary repair conditions. The intended spectral convergence theorem itself is not declared false for the Poisson model, and no conclusion is transferred to uninspected journal bytes.
