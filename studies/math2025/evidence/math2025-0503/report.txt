Abstract: The frozen 54-page arXiv version 2212.03362v2 by Elchanan Mossel, Allan Sly and Youngtak Sohn is examined. Two major problems are established: the self-averaging lemma fails for an admissible offspring family, and the printed numerical certificate omits a Gaussian normalization factor. Several local corrections concern message conventions, a lower-bound proof, conflicting cubic coefficients, and the precise ranges of auxiliary assertions. Counterevidence and possible repairs are supplied. The principal stochastic-block-model theorem is not disproved. Scientific decision remains pending; this initial report is not independent acceptance.

The paper studies the symmetric broadcast channel \(K_\lambda=\lambda I+(1-\lambda)J\), with \(-1/(q-1)\leq\lambda<1\), on Galton–Watson trees of mean offspring \(d>1\). For the stochastic block model, \(d=(a+(q-1)b)/q\) and \(\lambda=(a-b)/(a+(q-1)b)\). Theorem 1.5 asserts impossibility of weak recovery for \(q=3,4\), large fixed \(d\), and \(d\lambda^2\leq1\). The proof passes through tree nonreconstruction, small-magnetization expansions and a large-degree Gaussian approximation, followed by a tree-to-graph coupling.

# A false self-averaging lemma

Problem 1 (Major: Lemma 5.2).  On pp. 37–38, Lemma 5.2 claims, under Assumptions 1.10 and 1.11, that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), sufficiently large \(d\) gives \[\mathbb E\left|\widehat x_n-x_n\right|<\varepsilon,\qquad
 \widehat x_n=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^{\gamma}x_n(T_j).
 \tag{P1}\] This statement is false even at \(n=1\), for \(q=3\) or \(4\) and \(\lambda=d^{-1/2}\).

Proof. For all real \(d>1\), set \(a_d=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\), \(b_d=\lceil3d/2\rceil\), and put mass \((d-a_d)/(b_d-a_d)\) at \(b_d\), with the remaining mass at \(a_d\). The mean is exactly \(d\). Since \(X_d/d\leq5/2\), Assumption 1.10 holds with \(K(\theta)\leq e^{5\theta/2}\). For \(d\geq4\), \(1/4\leq X_d/d\leq7/4\) surely, so Assumption 1.11 also holds. At every even integer \(d\), this is the equal mixture of \(d/2\) and \(3d/2\).

Equation reference: eq:jensen, eq:nonvanish. Conditionally on \(\gamma\), the subtrees are independent copies of \(T\). Consequently \[\mathbb E(\widehat x_n\mid\gamma)=\frac{\gamma}{d}x_n,\qquad
 \mathbb E|\widehat x_n-x_n|
 \geq x_n\mathbb E|\gamma/d-1|=\frac{x_n}{2}.
 \] To bound \(x_1\) away from zero uniformly, fix root degree \(D\), put \(U=\mathbf1_{\{\sigma_\rho=1\}}-1/q\), \(v=(q-1)/q^2\), and \(S=\sum_{j=1}^D(\mathbf1_{\{\sigma_j=1\}}-1/q)\). Direct conditioning gives \[\operatorname{Cov}(U,S)=D\lambda v,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(S)=Dv\{1+(D-1)\lambda^2\}.\] Projection onto \(S\) is no better than conditioning on all child spins. By colour symmetry, \[x_1(T)=q\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[U\mid\sigma(1)])
 \geq \frac{q-1}{q}\frac{D\lambda^2}{1+(D-1)\lambda^2}.\] For \(\lambda^2=1/d\) and \(D\geq d/2\), the last ratio is at least \(1/3\). Thus \[\mathbb E|\widehat x_1-x_1|\geq\frac{q-1}{6q},
 \] for arbitrarily large even \(d\), contradicting (P1). The channel is admissible and the near-critical condition (16) holds for all sufficiently large such \(d\). ◻

The calculation is not an objection merely at depth zero. For example, independent enumeration at \(q=4,d=16,\lambda=1/4\) gives one-generation magnetizations approximately \(0.292802\) and \(0.572411\) at degrees 8 and 24. Their average is \(0.432606\); the conditional Jensen lower bound is \(0.216303\). These floating checks illustrate the exact proof above.

#### Dependency and repair.

The centering in the proof on p. 38 should be \((\gamma/d)x_n\), not \(x_n\). The valid estimate is \[\mathbb E\left|\widehat x_n-\frac{\gamma}{d}x_n\right|
 \leq \sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Var}(x_n(T))}{d}}.\] This exposes the missing offspring fluctuation term. Lemma 5.2 is used in Proposition 5.5, equation (124), on p. 39, to obtain the deterministic approximation \(x_{n+1}\approx g_q(d\lambda^2x_n)\). That approximation is then used on p. 40 in Theorem 1.12. The failure therefore concerns a core derivation, not an unused estimate.

One repair is to strengthen the offspring hypothesis to \(X_d/d\to1\), with the uniform exponential bound providing uniform integrability. A less restrictive approach retains a mixture such as \(\mathbb Eg_q(\lambda^2\gamma x_n)\) and proves its required gap. Assumption 1.11 can supply a positive-probability compact interval on which a strictly positive gap is retained, but the approximation and its constants must be re-established. This report does not supply that full revised proof. Regular and Poisson offspring concentrate after division by \(d\); hence they are counterevidence to extrapolating this defect into a disproof of the SBM theorem.

# The numerical certificate as printed cannot work

Problem 2 (Major: Appendix B, equation (140)).  Equation (138), p. 52, correctly includes \((2\pi)^{-3/2}\) in both Gaussian integrals. The definitions of \(S_n,T_n\) and their sum in (140), p. 54, omit this factor. The subsequent claim that this sum certifies the inequalities (137) is impossible for the printed formula.

Equation reference: eq:normalization. Let \[Q_s(x)=\left[1+e^{-16s/3}
       \sum_{1\leq i<j\leq3}e^{\sqrt{16s/3}(x_i+x_j)}
       \right]^{-1}.\] The paper has \(g_4(s)+1/4=\mathbb EQ_s(G)\), for standard Gaussian \(G\in\mathbb R^3\). Let \(A_n(s)\) denote the sum of all the \(S_n\) and \(T_n\) terms in (140), before \(-1/4+2\cdot10^{-6}\). The \(\phi\) of Lemma B.2 bounds integrals of \(e^{-x^2/2}\), without a Gaussian normalizing constant. The monotonicity and cell bounds in Lemmas B.1–B.2 therefore imply \[A_n(s)\geq
 \int_{[-5,5]^3}Q_s(x)e^{-\|x\|^2/2}\,dx.\] Also \(g_4(s)\geq0\): in the equivalent four-coordinate softmax, the centred Gaussian vector is exchangeable, and the mean of coordinate 1 is increased relative to every other coordinate by \(16s/3\geq0\). Its expected softmax is therefore at least \(1/4\). Since \(0\leq Q_s\leq1\), the tail bound on p. 52 gives \[A_n(s)\geq(2\pi)^{3/2}(1/4-2\cdot10^{-6}).\] Thus the printed right side of (140) is at least \[-1/4+2\cdot10^{-6}
 +(2\pi)^{3/2}(1/4-2\cdot10^{-6})>3.68.
 \] It cannot establish any of the bounds below \(0.75\) in (137), regardless of choosing \(n=100\) or \(200\). This does not say that (140), regarded merely as a loose upper bound, is false.

#### Counterevidence and correction.

The normalization is present in (138), so an omitted factor is a plausible explanation. Restore it to the complete sum and supply a new rigorous computation, including rounding control. The exterior integration domain should also be written \([-5,5]^3\setminus[-1,1]^3\): the earlier indicator in (138), read literally as all three absolute coordinates being at least one, omits mixed regions. The subsequent sum uses the correct complementary cube domain.

There is substantial affirmative evidence for the intended lemma. An independent rational expansion of the Gaussian exponential moments exactly reproduced every coefficient of (135), and every coefficient and remainder bound in the polynomial \(h\) of (136). It gives \[h(0.0612)
 =-\frac{5448232471317579703973864934488503}
          {201366841793060302734375000000000000}
 \approx-0.02705625426<-0.02.\] Independent Gauss–Hermite checks of orders 24 and 40 also give \(g_4(s)<s\) at the seven points \[s\in\{0.0612,0.0615,0.08,0.1,0.2,0.5,0.75\}.\] at \(s=0.75\), the order-40 value is about \(0.4967738574\). These floating evaluations are counterevidence to a false-lemma interpretation, not certified replacements for the full grid. No author code was executed, and unavailable author code is not itself classified as a mathematical defect.

The dependency is Lemma 5.6 \(\to\) the Gaussian contraction in Theorem 1.12 \(\to\) the \(q=4\) SBM result. An invalid certificate for this crucial input warrants Major severity. Neither Lemma 5.6 nor the final theorem is disproved here.

# Local proof and formulation corrections

Problem 3 (Minor: zero-offspring message convention).  The convention \(Y_{i1}=1/q\) on \(\{\gamma=0\}\), p. 8, conflicts with the unconditional independence in Proposition 2.3(b), p. 9, and the first moment in Lemma 3.4, equation (23), p. 13.

For \(p_0=\mathbb P(\gamma=0)>0\), \(\lambda\ne0\), and \(x_n>0\), conditioning gives, under that convention, \[\mathbb E(Y_{11}-1/q)=(1-p_0)\lambda x_n,\] rather than \(\lambda x_n\). Poisson offspring, one of the paper’s principal cases, have \(p_0=e^{-d}>0\). The factorization in Lemma 3.5, p. 15, uses the same unconditional independence. Define an infinite sequence of potential child subtrees and messages independently of \(\gamma\), and use only its first \(\gamma\) entries. This restores the intended identities without changing the actual broadcast process or its empty-product posterior.

Remark 3.3 on p. 13 also needs a nonempty level \(n\) for its strict inequality \(x_n(T)>0\). A singleton tree has no observations at level \(n\geq1\) and \(x_n(T)=0\). The averaged magnetization can still be positive at finite depth. These are locally repairable construction and boundary errors, not counterexamples to the intended expansion.

Problem 4 (Minor: quadratic bound used as a linear bound).  The last inference in Lemma 3.8, p. 18, obtains \(x_{n+1}\geq c\lambda^4x_n^2\) and then claims \(x_{n+1}\geq c\kappa^4x_n\) from \(|\lambda|\geq\kappa\). The power of \(x_n\) cannot be dropped.

There is a stronger valid argument. For fixed \(T\) with a first child, condition first on the depth-\(n\) observations in that child’s subtree only. If its posterior is \(Q_i\), the root posterior is \(1/q+\lambda(Q_i-1/q)\). Conditional-variance monotonicity when the other branches are added gives \[x_{n+1}(T)\geq\lambda^2x_n(T_1),\qquad
 x_{n+1}\geq\mathbb P(\gamma\geq1)\lambda^2x_n.\] Assumption 1.10 bounds \(\mathbb E\gamma^2/d^2\); Cauchy–Schwarz therefore bounds \(\mathbb P(\gamma\geq1)\) away from zero. Since \(|\lambda|\leq1\), this proves the stated \(c\lambda^4x_n\) lower bound as well. The lower bound is used on p. 19 and in the barrier proof on pp. 35–36. Its printed derivation is invalid, but this complete repair supports Minor severity.

Problem 5 (Minor: inconsistent cubic coefficient).  The bracket multiplying \(\{\mathbb E\binom{\gamma}{2}\}^2\lambda^6x_n^3\) in equation (15), p. 10, is one quarter of the bracket in equation (105), p. 35.

The later expression is \[\frac{4q^2(q+1)}{(q-1)^2d(d-1)}
 -\frac{24q^2(q-2)}{(q-1)^2d(d\lambda-1)}.\] For Poisson offspring with \(d=16,q=4,\lambda=-1/4\), all channel and near-critical constraints are admissible. Exact substitution gives cubic coefficient \(-44/15\) in (15), but \(32/45\) in (105). The discrepancy even changes its sign. Equation (106), the displayed \(d^*\) criterion, and the subsequent proof use the later coefficient. Correcting the overview to agree with that derivation is therefore the indicated local repair; this mismatch alone does not invalidate the threshold ultimately stated.

Problem 6 (Minor: antiferromagnetic threshold interpretation).  Theorem 1.13 and the paragraph following it, p. 6, equate \(\lambda^-(q,d)>-d^{-1/2}\) with reconstruction somewhere below the Kesten–Stigum bound for all the stated \(d>1\). The admissible channel interval must be retained.

When \(d<(q-1)^2\), the point \(-d^{-1/2}\) is outside \([-1/(q-1),0)\). The displayed inequality may then be true solely because of this domain endpoint. For a concrete check, take Poisson mean \(d=2\) and \(q=4\). For any admissible negative \(\lambda\), the one-edge total variation contraction is \(|\lambda|\leq1/3\). A coupled broadcast started from different root colours has expected number of disagreements at depth \(n\) at most \((2|\lambda|)^n\leq(2/3)^n\). Thus reconstruction is impossible throughout the admissible negative interval. Nevertheless \(\lambda^-(4,2)=-1/3>-1/\sqrt2\). Poisson offspring satisfy Assumption 1.10, and \(2<d^*_{\rm Poi}\).

This disproves the blanket reconstruction interpretation, not the formal inequality for \(\lambda^-\). The argument on pp. 35–36 that chooses \(\lambda\) near \(-d^{-1/2}\) needs an admissible neighbourhood. Distinguish the vacuous range and restrict that existence interpretation, for example to \(d\geq(q-1)^2\) when the rest of the hypotheses apply. The large-\(d\) nonreconstruction theorem is unaffected by this boundary correction.

Problem 7 (Minor: Appendix A replacement variables).  On p. 49, the definitions of \(S_k,T_k\) use initial segments of the Gaussian variables instead of tails. The displayed Taylor estimates also omit \(\mathbb E\phi(T_k)\).

With the printed sums, \(T_k\) contains \(Z_k\), so their asserted independence fails whenever \(Z_k\) is nondegenerate. Even for zero increments and \(\phi\equiv1\), the printed Taylor remainder inequality becomes \(1\leq0\). Use instead \[S_k=\sum_{j<k}V_j+\sum_{j\geq k}Z_j,\qquad
 T_k=\sum_{j<k}V_j+\sum_{j>k}Z_j,\] and subtract \(\mathbb E\phi(T_k)\) in both Taylor estimates. The constant terms cancel in their difference, while the matched first and second moments leave the usual cubic remainder. The Frobenius-norm identity above (132) likewise requires the norm, not its square, after taking the square root. These explicit corrections recover the standard Lindeberg argument under bounded increments and bounded third derivatives. They concern the proof of Proposition 5.3, used in Proposition 5.5, and do not provide a counterexample to the intended Gaussian approximation.

Problem 8 (Minor: distinct vertices in Corollary 1.6).  The phrase any fixed vertices \(u,v\) on p. 4 must exclude \(u=v\).

For \(u=v\), \(\mathbb P(\sigma_u=i\mid G,\sigma_v=1)=\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}\) for every \(n\), not \(1/q\), even with \(q=3,4\), large \(d\) and \(d\lambda^2\leq1\). The intended two-point statement and its relation to weak recovery on pp. 44–45 concern distinct vertices. Adding this qualifier repairs the literal counterexample without changing the main theorem.

# Coverage, counterchecks and remaining limits

The posterior identities and reverse-martingale argument were checked against the channel’s colour symmetry, including extinction and the empty-level posterior. The product expansion was followed through the \(u_n,v_n,w_n\) recursions, the weighted polynomial truncation, the near-critical constraints \(d^5|\lambda|^9>1+c_0\), \(1-c_0\leq d\lambda^2\leq1\), and the nonzero denominators \(d-1,d\lambda-1\). An independent check of the \(q=4\) specialization links (105) to (106); the overview mismatch is separated above. The uniform remainder estimates depend on the exponential moment bound. This is a substantive reading and targeted calculation audit, not a symbolic certification of every high-order expansion.

For the Gaussian step, the covariance is positive semidefinite with a zero direction corresponding to the all-ones vector. The softmax is invariant in that direction. Positivity of its mean bias and the exact Gaussian exponential-moment formula were used in the independent Appendix B checks. The small-\(s\) polynomial check succeeded; the large-\(s\) certificate needs repair. Equation (119)’s increment maximum should range over all \(D\) increments in the intended Lindeberg application, as is required in the proof.

The tree-to-SBM argument was read through the coupling, finite neighbourhood types, conditional exchangeability, permutation maximization, and the two-point reformulation. There are additional local bookkeeping corrections: on p. 41 the exact Bernoulli–Poisson distance is \(p(1-e^{-p})\), not \(p-e^{-p}\); on p. 42 the Markov estimate concerns \(\mathbb P(A_1^c)\); the bound on the remaining colour count requires subtraction of \(n/q\). Summing per-vertex \(O(n^{-2/5})\) errors over at most \(n^{1/4}\) revealed vertices gives \(O(n^{-3/20})=o(1)\), and collision probabilities are \(O(n^{-1/2})\). These checks support the coupling conclusion after correction. The tower-property step on p. 44 should be formulated using an observable good event or unconditional conditional expectations with an \(o(1)\) exceptional-event error, rather than silently assuming measurability of a coupling event.
