Abstract: A polynomial proper map satisfying the stated boundary regularity and Shilov-boundary preservation assumptions contradicts the second-order osculation assertion in the core proof. The same example contradicts a later dimension equality used for parallelism and product decomposition. This is a Major proof-level defect in the supplied version, not a counterexample to the main rationality theorem. The rationality criterion and the transversality argument have independent repairs. Several frame and dimension formulas require bounded corrections, and the proper-factor argument needs further justification. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Exact source, hypotheses and scope

The reviewed source is Sung-Yeon Kim, Rationality of proper holomorphic maps between bounded symmetric domains of the first kind, M25-0166, arXiv:2305.01875v1, 3 May 2023, 33 pages [source]. The assignment’s title uses type one and associates the paper with DOI 10.1215/00127094-2024-0060 (https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2024-0060). This review does not identify the supplied author manuscript with the journal-final text or any later correction. Page references are one-based physical PDF pages; printed numbers agree on all inspected pages.

Write \[D_{p,q}=\{Z\in M_{p,q}(\mathbb C):I_q-Z^*Z>0\},\qquad p\ge q.\] The rank-\(r\) boundary orbit \(S_r\) has \(r\) strictly negative directions and \(q-r\) null directions in its Grassmannian realization. Thus \(S_0\) is the Shilov boundary; for \(p=q\) it is \(U(q)\). Theorem 1.2 assumes \(q,q'\ge2\), properness, and only a local \(C^2\) extension near a Shilov point with local Shilov-boundary preservation. It asserts a totally geodesic product factorization with a proper rational factor and a nonproper factor, and rationality of the whole map for \(p>q\). Corollary 1.3 assumes global smooth boundary regularity, \(q\ge3\), and \(q'\le2q-1\).

# A counterexample to the core osculation assertion

Problem 1 (Major: page 22 and its use on page 28).  The asserted containment of the nullspace image in its affine second-order osculating space is false, even for a polynomial proper map preserving the entire Shilov boundary.

Take \[f:D_{2,2}\longrightarrow D_{8,8},\qquad f(Z)=Z^{\otimes3}.
 \tag{1}\] The operator-norm identity \(\|f(Z)\|=\|Z\|^3\) proves that it maps the source into the target and is proper: any sequence approaching the source boundary has image norm tending to one. The map is polynomial, extends smoothly across the closure, and sends unitary matrices to unitary matrices. It therefore satisfies every relevant hypothesis of Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 5.1. The generic characteristic-direction rank used later is also seven: for invertible \(Z\), factor \(df_Z(A)\) by \(Z^{\otimes3}\) and put \(B=Z^{-1}A\). For rank-one \(A\) with \(\operatorname{tr}B\ne0\), \(B\) is similar to \(\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{tr}B,0)\). The sum of its actions in three tensor slots has a one-dimensional kernel.

Put \(P=I_2\), \(\lambda=-1/10\), and, for real \(|t|<1/2\), set \[v(t)=(1,t)^t,\quad w(t)=(-t,1)^t,\quad H(t)=v(t)v(t)^*,
 \qquad Z(t)=I_2+\lambda H(t).\] Here \(Z(t)\) has eigenvalues \(a(t)=1+\lambda(1+t^2)\) and \(1\), with \(0<a(t)<1\). It lies on \(S_1(D_{2,2})\) in the family of boundary components through \(P\). Define the two curves appearing in the page-22 argument by \[\begin{split}
 x(t)&=f(Z(t)),\\
 y(t)&=I_8+\lambda\,df_{I_2}(H(t))\\
 &=I_8+\lambda\bigl(H(t)\otimes I_2\otimes I_2+
 I_2\otimes H(t)\otimes I_2+I_2\otimes I_2\otimes H(t)\bigr).
 \end{split} \tag{2}\] The eigenvalues of \(x(t)\) are \(a(t)^k\), and those of \(y(t)\) are \(1+\lambda k(1+t^2)\), for \(k=0,1,2,3\), with multiplicities \(\binom3k\). Every eigenvalue except the \(k=0\) eigenvalue lies strictly between zero and one. Consequently both curves lie in \(S_7(D_{8,8})\) and have the same null line. In the identification of \(V_{I_8}\) with \(\mathbb C^8\), that line is \[Z_0(x(t))=Z_0(y(t))
 =\mathbb C\,w(t)^{\otimes3},\qquad
 w(t)^{\otimes3}
 =(-t^3,t^2,t^2,-t,t^2,-t,-t,1)^t.
 \tag{3}\] Thus this is also a test of the claimed equality of the two nullspace curves, not an example violating an earlier prerequisite.

The last coordinate in (3) is one. In the corresponding big Schubert cell of \(\operatorname{Gr}(1,V_{I_8})=\mathbb P^7\), the nullspace curve is \[\kappa(t)=(-t^3,t^2,t^2,-t,t^2,-t,-t).\] Both \(\kappa'(0)\) and \(\kappa''(0)\) have first coordinate zero, whereas \(\kappa(t)\) has first coordinate \(-t^3\). Hence \[\kappa(t)\notin
 \kappa(0)+\operatorname{span}\{\kappa'(0),\kappa''(0)\}\quad(t\ne0).
 \tag{4}\] The full complex null-base family is the cubic Veronese curve \([\omega]\mapsto[\omega^{\otimes3}]\) on \(\mathbb P^1\). Its tangent plus second fundamental form in this affine chart has dimension two. It does not contain the curve. This directly refutes the last displayed containment on page 22, including its stated flat-connection interpretation.

## The earlier differential inference is also invalid

A quadratic curve on a nonlinear Schubert variety need not have ambient acceleration tangent to that variety. Already for the identity map of \(D_{2,2}\), the same \(y(t)=I_2+\lambda H(t)\) lies on \[W_P=\{Y:\operatorname{rank}(Y-I_2)\le1\}.\] At \(y(0)=I_2+\lambda\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), its tangent space requires the \((2,2)\) entry of an increment to vanish. But \(y''(0)=2\lambda\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) is not tangent. Thus the page-22 inclusion \(\operatorname{span}\{y'(0),y''(0)\}\subset T_{y(0)}W_P\) is itself false. Moreover a nonlinear projection of a quadratic curve need not be quadratic: the second derivative of a composition includes the Hessian of the projection. Equation (3) demonstrates the actual failure, rather than merely pointing out a missing chain-rule term.

The preceding page-21 justification of tangent parallelism along an entire target boundary component also needs correction. For the proper linear map \(f(Z)=\operatorname{diag}(Z,Z)\) from \(D_{2,2}\) to \(D_{4,4}\), its null-base family through \(I\) is \(A\mapsto A\oplus A\). At \(A=\mathbb Ce_1\), order the target as \((A\oplus A)\oplus(A^\perp\oplus A^\perp)\) and write a leaf point as \(Y_B=\operatorname{diag}(I_2,B)\). Differentiating \(A_\chi=\mathbb C(e_1+\chi e_2)\) shows that the complex span of the saturated-family tangent has off-diagonal blocks \(a(I_2-B)\) and \(b(I_2-B)\), with \(a,b\in\mathbb C\), and an arbitrary lower-right block. For \(B=0\) and \(B=\operatorname{diag}(0,1/2)\) these off-diagonal lines differ although the leaf is the same. Ambient block parallelism therefore does not imply this smaller tangent space is parallel. For the particular linear example the chosen first-order and true image points can coincide; the example refutes the general justification, while (3)–(4) separately refute the final containment.

## The downstream dimension equality fails for the same map

For (1), the space \(L_P\) defined in (5.19), page 26, is \(\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^2\), of dimension four, because the cubes of lines span the symmetric tensor space. At a fixed point the null line \(Z\) has dimension one, so \[\dim(L_P/Z)=3.\] There is one source horizontal CR direction (\(q-1=1\)). The Veronese map is immersive, so the notation of Section 5.2 gives \(J_1=1\). The page-28 equality \[\operatorname{span}\{V_u:u\le J_1\}
 +\operatorname{span}\{h_u^a:u\le J_1,\ a\le q-1\}=L_P\pmod Z
 \tag{5}\] has left side of dimension at most two and right side of dimension three. It is false. This is precisely the degree-two-curve step used to select \(J_2\), establish independence of \(P\), and prove the main technical Lemma 5.4. The subsequent use in Corollary 5.5 and Theorem 1.2 therefore lacks the required proof.

Replacing second order by a universal fixed order cannot suffice: \(Z\mapsto Z^{\otimes d}\) gives the degree-\(d\) Veronese null curve for every \(d\). A different argument using stabilized higher osculating spans might be possible; none is supplied or certified here.

## Counterevidence against overstating the conclusion

The map (1) is rational and is consistent with the main theorem. Indeed, the symmetric tensor subspace and its orthogonal complement are reducing subspaces for every \(Z^{\otimes3}\), since the operators and their adjoints commute with tensor permutations. A fixed unitary basis therefore gives a block decomposition \[f(Z)=F_1(Z)\oplus F_2(Z),\qquad
 F_1=\operatorname{Sym}^3 Z\in D_{4,4},\quad F_2\in D_{4,4}.\] A maximizing singular vector \(u\) gives the symmetric tensor \(u^{\otimes3}\), so \(\|F_1(Z)\|=\|Z\|^3\); \(F_1\) is proper and polynomial. Along \(Z_\epsilon=\operatorname{diag}(1-\epsilon,0)\), \(F_2(Z_\epsilon)=0\), so \(F_2\) is nonproper. The standard block product is totally geodesic. Thus the counterexample refutes the central derivation, not Theorem 1.2 or Corollary 1.3.

# Two arguments that can be repaired completely

## Rationality from the singleton condition: Lemma 3.1

Assume the cited rationality of \(g=f_1^\sharp\). The lemma’s finite singleton condition then gives a direct proof of rationality without the problematic chain-avoidance step on pages 12–13.

For target interval subgrassmannians \([A_j,B_j]_{q'}\) containing a common \(q'\)-plane, put \(A=\sum A_j\) and \(B=\bigcap B_j\). Their intersection is \([A,B]_{q'}\). In exterior coordinates their minimal linear spans are described by \[a\wedge\omega=0\ (a\in A_j),\qquad
 \iota_\phi\omega=0\ (\phi\in\operatorname{Ann}B_j).\] Combining these equations replaces \(A_j\) by \(A\) and \(\operatorname{Ann}B_j\) by \(\operatorname{Ann}B\). If the intersection is a singleton, either \(\dim A=q'\) or \(\dim B=q'\); the intersection of the exterior linear spans is exactly its one-dimensional Plücker line. There is no arbitrary-linear-section counterexample to this particular inference.

Choose a source chart \(V_x=\operatorname{graph}Z\) centered at \(x_0\), and set \(T_Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\Z&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For the finitely many prescribed flags \(\sigma_j=(C_j,D_j)\) through \(x_0\), the flags \(\sigma_j(x)=(T_ZC_j,T_ZD_j)\) are algebraic sections through those exact flags. On a small neighborhood inside the source, \(f(x)\in X'_{g(\sigma_j(x))}\); all \(g(\sigma_j(x))\) are defined after shrinking because the original flags lie in its domain. Their Plücker spans give a rational linear system \(M(x)u=0\). At \(x_0\) it has rank \(N'\) in \(N'+1\) homogeneous unknowns. Signed maximal minors give a rational projective point equal to \(f(x)\) on a nonempty open set. The Plücker equations then hold identically, so this is a rational map from \(X\) to \(X'\). Connectedness gives agreement with \(f\) on its original domain.

The printed generic-chain argument does not by itself show that all flags can avoid the bad set while retaining a prescribed endpoint. The construction above bypasses that issue and avoids taking jets of \(f\) outside its known domain. Lemma 2.3’s finite-jet argument is valid on the connected parameter locus actually needed: analyticity turns vanishing of all jets into an identity, and finite-dimensional linear algebra selects finitely many rows. A disconnected parameter manifold needs this qualification.

The moduli-map premise is corroborated by Lemmas 6.1–6.2 of the later primary version [KMS], whose Section 6 assumes properness but no small rank gap. The exact cited 2022 preprint and the full Mok–Tsai Proposition 2.6 proof were not retrieved. This remains an identified external dependency, not a fully reproduced external proof.

## Transversality: Proposition 5.1

The Taylor expansion on pages 18–19 omits a factor \(1/2\). With \(h=A_0+rA_1+r^2A_2+o(r^2)\), the displayed differential identity yields \[A_2\cdot\overline{A_0}=\tfrac12\|A_1\|^2,\qquad
 \|h\|^2=1+2\|A_1\|^2r^2+o(r^2),\] rather than coefficients one and three. Its sign contradiction survives. The following proof also removes the need to select a point simultaneously for all rotated target bases.

On a Shilov disc let \(F=f\circ\varphi\). For each unit \(u\in\mathbb C^{q'}\), \(F(\zeta)u\) lies in the unit ball inside the disc and has norm one on the available boundary arc. The nonnegative superharmonic function \(1-\|F(\zeta)u\|^2\) is strictly positive inside. The Hopf lemma gives a strictly positive outward derivative of \(\|F(\zeta)u\|^2\) at every point of that arc. After normalizing \(F(P)=(I_{q'},0)^t\), the radial derivative of its upper block is Hermitian by differentiation along the boundary. Its quadratic form is positive on every nonzero \(u\), so it is positive definite and has rank \(q'\). This proves the asserted full-rank direction and, by nonvanishing of the corresponding minor, full rank for general directions. Only the local boundary arc is needed. This repair does not imply the false osculation statement.

# Bounded geometric and frame corrections

These issues are grouped as Minor; their particular intended conclusions have local repairs, unlike the osculation step.

For arbitrary \(M=[V_M,W_M]_q\), the parameter space in Lemma 2.1 has second factor \(\operatorname{Gr}(p+r-\dim W_M,H/W_M)\), not an undefined \(r-s\). In Lemma 2.2, \(\dim(A^*/V_P)=r\), so the second factor is \(\operatorname{Gr}(r,H/V_P)\), not \(\operatorname{Gr}(q-r,H/V_P)\). For example \(q=3,r=1\) gives a line, not a two-plane. The Hermitian form identifies \(A\mapsto A^*/V_P\) anti-holomorphically between the two factors in tube type; its graph is maximally totally real. The lemma’s intended conclusion is preserved. Empty flag intersections must be excluded when defining the nonempty boundary-component orbits.

The fixed-coordinate complementary-vector assertion on page 7 is false. Set \(b=1/2\), \(Z_t=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&b\\0&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and use the fixed flag \[A=\mathbb Ce_1,\qquad B=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2+b e_3,e_4).\] The characteristic subgrassmannian has constant tangent \(\mathbb CE_{22}\) in the graph chart, but its complementary characteristic vector for the compact canonical metric is, up to scale, \[v_t=\begin{pmatrix}1+b^2&0\\bt&0\end{pmatrix}.\] This follows by moving \(e_1\) toward \(B^\perp=\mathbb C(e_3-b e_2)\) and restoring the identity upper graph block. Its directions at \(t=0,1/2\) differ. The weaker constancy of the tangent space of a fixed interval subgrassmannian is correct, because its graph-chart incidence conditions are affine linear.

Definition 4.1(i), page 14, lacks a quadratic correction ensuring that the transformed \(Y\)-vectors remain null. In the scalar block take \(W_0=V_0=\widetilde W=1\), \(W_u^\beta=t\) and \(V_\alpha^v=-t\) with real \(t\ne0\). All printed constraints hold, but \(Y'=Y-t\widetilde Z\) has norm \(-t^2\) and \(\langle Y',\widetilde Z+tZ\rangle=2t\). Thus adding a quadratic term alone does not fix the printed signs. For this real scalar block the complete Gram-preserving replacement is \[Z'=Z,\qquad \widetilde Z'=\widetilde Z+tZ,\qquad
 Y'=Y+t\widetilde Z+\tfrac12t^2Z .\] Indeed \(\langle Z',Y'\rangle=1\), \(\|\widetilde Z'\|^2=-1\), \(\|Y'\|^2=0\), and \(\langle\widetilde Z',Y'\rangle=0\); all other pairings remain as prescribed. The general change must likewise preserve the full Gram matrix. In Lemma 4.2, page 15, \(\|Z_\alpha+c_\alpha Y_\alpha\|^2=2\Re c_\alpha\); the asserted null vector requires purely imaginary \(c_\alpha\), not arbitrary real \(c_\alpha\), in the printed normalization. The curvature-nullspace conclusion has the direct complementary-block proof. Lemmas 4.3–4.4 agree with linearized Schubert rank conditions.

The strictly increasing pivot indices on page 24 must allow empty blocks. For the identity of \(D_{q,q}\) with \(q\ge3\), \(r=J_1=1\) and \(q'-r=q-1\); there cannot be \(q-1\) strictly positive increments ending at one. Nondecreasing indices give the usual row-kernel elimination. The type separation used in (5.12) is valid: the relevant \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\) components are independent.

With only \(C^2\) regularity, the second-jet expansion on page 25 has an \(o(t^2)\) remainder, not necessarily \(O(t^3)\). That weaker remainder suffices for the second-derivative identities. If the coefficients \(C_\alpha^u\) vary, their differentiated terms lie in the already known first-order span; exact constancy needs a gauge choice but is unnecessary modulo that span.

On page 26, \(V=\widetilde Z-X\) and \(\widehat V=\widetilde Z+X\) imply \(X=(\widehat V-V)/2\), with the opposite sign to that printed. Consequently the coefficient of \(\widehat V_W\) in \(dV_u\) is \[\tfrac12(\widetilde\Omega_u^W-\Omega_u^W+
             \Delta_u^W-\overline{\Delta_u^W}),\] and the vanishing relation corresponding to (5.18) must use this combination. After substitution, the subsequent expression \(dV_u=\sum_W(\widetilde\Omega_u^W-\overline{\Delta_u^W})V_W
\pmod Z\) survives. These sign repairs do not establish (5).

# Remaining obligations and final theorem assembly

The factor-properness argument in Corollary 5.5, pages 29–30, needs additional evidence even if the preceding geometric construction is replaced. From properness of \((f_1,f_2)\), a failure of properness of \(f_1\) gives a boundary-approaching sequence on which \(f_2\) approaches its boundary. It does not alone show that \(f_2\) is proper on every boundary-approaching sequence. The printed contradiction step needs a global propagation argument, especially under only a local \(C^2\) extension. The proof then invokes Corollary 5.5 itself for \(f_2\), without an explicit induction or a separately established weaker decomposition statement.

There is a partial noncircular repair. If a fixed global product has already been obtained and \(M_P=\{A_P\}\times M\) is defined by the span of the actual nullspaces in (5.19), then block diagonality gives \(Z_0(f(y))=Z_0(f_1(y))\oplus Z_0(f_2(y))\). Retaining the entire second factor in \(M_P\) forces the second nullspace component to vanish for all nearby \(y\) in the family. A proper \(f_2\), continuous at these boundary points, would have a nonzero nullspace there, a contradiction. This can exclude properness of \(f_2\) without citing the corollary recursively. It does not prove the missing implication that \(f_1\) is proper. No counterexample to that intended conclusion is claimed.

Local boundary hypotheses are not automatically fatal to the global factorization. In tube coordinates the Shilov edge is the space of Hermitian matrices. A holomorphic defining function that vanishes on a local edge patch also vanishes inside: along \(W=A+\zeta I\), use one-variable zero-boundary reflection/uniqueness, then uniqueness on the translated maximal real slice. Holomorphic identity extends a resulting fixed product constraint over the connected source. Nearby unitary points can also be joined by rank-one phase steps using a spectral decomposition, all within a small germ. These observations can localize the chain argument once correct parallelism and product constraints exist; they do not prove those missing premises.

For the non-tube argument in Lemma 6.1, the rank-one boundary component is a ball of dimension \(p-q+1\ge2\). A smooth CR map from its sphere to the totally real Shilov boundary of a tube factor is constant: its differential kills complex tangent directions, their brackets span the remaining tangent direction, and local holomorphic uniqueness applies. This part of the mechanism is valid. The product to which it is applied still depends on the unproved geometric reduction.

In Corollary 1.3, \(m\ge1\) and \(r>m\) imply \(q'-r\le2q-3<2(q-1)\), as required by the displayed numerical comparison. The exact rigidity application needs the stated embedding and transversality hypotheses matched to its boundary stratum. The accessible primary preprint [KimCR] distinguishes a local transversal CR embedding theorem from a global corollary with an additional numerical condition. The published 2021 version cited in the source was not retrieved, so no published numbering mismatch or false corollary is asserted.

Finally, an established standard isometric factor would imply Kobayashi isometry of the graph map: projection to that factor gives a holomorphic left inverse, and the two distance-contraction inequalities give equality. The retraction and isometry results in [Mok] support this step. They do not supply the earlier factorization.

# Assessment and required revision

The supported assessment is Major. The decisive evidence is the exact counterexample (1)–(5) to a central derivation, not the number of typographical corrections. A revision needs a replacement for the second-order containment and the page-28 span argument, followed by a noncircular proof of the proper rational factor. The bounded corrections and the two independent repairs above should be incorporated and the local-to-global uses made explicit.

No conclusion that the main theorem is false is supported. No author code was supplied or executed, and its absence is not a mathematical defect.
