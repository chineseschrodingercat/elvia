Abstract: The acquired manuscript is examined through its local-density, cone-summation, geometric-approximation and sieve arguments. The stated cutting proposition has an explicit counterexample, and the radial compactness hypothesis used in the passage to boxes is not satisfied by those boxes. Several local normalization formulas also require correction. Corrected fixed-cone computations provide counterevidence to a blanket rejection of the prime-counting strategy. The findings concern this author-manuscript version; they do not establish that the three final matrix asymptotics are false.

Title: A verification of selected arguments in
Prime Number Theorems for Polynomials from Homogeneous Dynamics

# Identity, statements and scope

The source is Giorgos Kotsovolis and Katharine Woo, Prime number theorems for polynomials from homogeneous dynamics, arXiv:2312.11445v1, 18 December 2023, 38 physical pages; the manuscript date is 19 December 2023 [KW]. The assigned DOI is 10.1007/s00039-025-00716-y. Equivalence with the 53-page journal article has not been established. All page references concern the acquired PDF; its physical and printed numbering agree.

Theorems 1.4, 1.6 and 1.8 concern, respectively, determinants on \(\operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb Z)\), Pfaffians on alternating \(2n\)-by-\(2n\) matrices (\(n\ge2\)), and determinants on symmetric \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrices (\(n\ge3\)). Their positive-prime constants are \[\prod_{j=2}^n\zeta(j)^{-1},\qquad
 \prod_{\substack{3\le j\le2n-1\\j\ {\rm odd}}}\zeta(j)^{-1},\qquad
 \prod_{\substack{3\le j\le n\\j\ {\rm odd}}}\zeta(j)^{-1}.\] Positive primes and the two signs counted by the prime-ideal formulation are kept distinct. A statement for positive level-one cones does not alone prove its negative-level counterpart.

The primary executor checked the geometric hypotheses and counterexample, the sieve error scales, and the integration of these findings. Full reading is not a complete audit of the cited equidistribution, mass-formula or sieve theorems. Their exact external normalizations remain declared inputs.

# Problem 1: the cutting proposition and its application

Assessment: Major, geometric implication not justified as stated. Section 4 explicitly drops the dynamical assumptions of §2. Theorem 4.1 assumes that a compact set \(R_0\) is star-shaped about zero and that \[[0,\infty)R_0\cap V_1\] is a nice compact subset of \(V_1=\{F=1\}\). Here nice means that the boundary has measure zero on \(V_1\) (Definition 2.2, p. 6). Definition 4.4 requires a finite collection of compact subsets \(\Omega\subset V_1\) such that \[R_0=E\ \cup\!\bigcup_\Omega[0,1/\operatorname{ht}(\Omega)]\Omega,
 \qquad |E|\le\epsilon,\qquad
 \operatorname{ht}(\Omega)=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\omega\|_\infty .\] Proposition 4.6 asserts that such a cutting exists under the two stated hypotheses (pp. 14–15).

Take \(V=\mathbb R^2\), \(F(x,y)=x\), and \[R_0=\{(x,y):0\le x\le\tfrac12,\ |y|\le x\}.\] This is a compact convex star-shaped set of area \(1/4\); the polynomial is homogeneous and irreducible. Its radial projection is the nice compact segment \(\{(1,s):-1\le s\le1\}\). Every nonempty \(\Omega\) in a purported cutting must lie in this segment, since its cone is required to be contained in \(R_0\). Consequently \(\operatorname{ht}(\Omega)=1\), and the cone contains a point with \(x=1\), outside \(R_0\). No nonempty cutting piece is possible. An empty collection leaves \(|E|=1/4\), contradicting every \(\epsilon<1/4\). This directly refutes Proposition 4.6 with its printed definition; it is not a numerical example or a failure of an antecedent about prime distribution.

There is a separate failure of the cited application. For the centered unit box in \(\operatorname{Mat}_2(\mathbb R)\), the radial projection onto \(\det=1\) is all of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\), hence is noncompact. Already \[\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\0&t^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
                  \qquad t\ge1,\] is an unbounded sequence in that projection. The same sequence lies in the radial projection of the nonnegative unit box used in Remark 4.2. Thus the assertion in §5.2 (p. 19) that the matrix box satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 4.1 is incorrect.

## Effect and necessary repair

The cutoff \(1/\operatorname{ht}(\Omega)\) encodes a unit norm boundary, not the radial boundary of an arbitrary \(R_0\). A repaired general construction must use the radial length allowed by \(R_0\), approximate it on the angular pieces, and control the exceptional region. For boxes, compact pieces can instead be obtained after removing a neighborhood of \(F=0\); the theorem and its application must be stated to permit this exhaustion. The asserted finite union of convex exceptional pieces in §5.1 is not supplied by the printed construction. A finite convex outer cover with controlled total volume would suffice for the sieve step, but must be proved with the corrected cutting.

These are defects in the bridge from fixed cones to the advertised regions. They do not give a counterexample to a matrix prime asymptotic. Even for the triangular example above, the actual prime count is \[\sum_{p\le T/2}(2p+1)\sim\frac{T^2}{4\log T},\] which agrees with its prime integral by the ordinary prime number theorem. The invalid cutting statement must therefore be distinguished from the truth of a prime-counting conclusion for a particular region. Remark 1.5’s additional boundary-measure-zero condition is also meaningful and should not be silently replaced by niceness of the radial projection. The ledger records the boundary and zero-measure-cone quantifiers separately; no theorem is refuted by assuming an unverified antecedent.

# Problem 2: inconsistent local normalization formulas

Assessment: Minor, explicitly repairable intermediate formulas. The following discrepancies are confirmed on the actual source pages. They are grouped because each concerns the compatibility of the local normalizations with the later displayed constants.

## Radial shells and level shells

Put \(D=\dim V\), \(d=\deg F\), and \(C=[0,1]\Omega\) for a positive-measure compact patch in \(F=1\). The radial-shell definition on p. 7 gives \[\mu_{\rm rad}(\Omega)
 =\lim_{\epsilon\to0}
   \frac{\operatorname{vol}([1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\Omega)}{2\epsilon}
 =D\operatorname{vol}(C).\] For the coarea measure \(\mu_F(\Omega)=\int_\Omega|\nabla F|^{-1}\,dS\), homogeneity gives \(\operatorname{vol}(C)=(d/D)\mu_F(\Omega)\), so \(\mu_{\rm rad}=d\mu_F\). Consequently \[\int_{T\Omega}\frac{dx}{\log^+F(x)}
 \sim\frac{\mu_F(\Omega)T^D}{D\log T}
 =\frac{\mu_{\rm rad}(\Omega)T^D}{dD\log T}.\] Lemma 2.5 (p. 8) lacks the last factor \(1/d\) when read with the preceding radial definition. Its first coarea step substitutes an \(F\)-level shell for a radial shell.

For a concrete determinant patch \[\Omega=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&(1+bc)/a\end{pmatrix}:
           1\le a\le2,\ 0\le b,c\le1\right\},\] the coarea element is \(da\,db\,dc/a\). Thus \(\mu_F(\Omega)=\log2\) and \(\mu_{\rm rad}(\Omega)=2\log2\). The true integral coefficient is \((\log2)/4\), whereas the literal Lemma 2.5 gives \((\log2)/2\). Replacing the shell endpoints by \((1-\epsilon)^{1/d},(1+\epsilon)^{1/d}\), or dividing the radial measure by \(d\), resolves the discrepancy. Remark 2.6 already adopts this coarea normalization.

## Finite-place factors and prime powers

The unit-determinant density in Lemma 3.3 (pp. 10–11) must be \[q^{-(n^2-1)}\frac{\#\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)}{q-1}
       =\prod_{j=2}^n(1-q^{-j}).\] The printed equalities omit \(q^{-(n^2-1)}\). At \(n=q=2\), the actual density is \(3/4\), not \(6\). The denominator in Theorem 3.5 (p. 12) must instead use the Pfaffian fiber dimension: \[q^{\,k(2n^2-n-1)}
             \quad\hbox{rather than}\quad q^{\,k(n^2-1)}.\] For \(n=2\), \(\operatorname{Pf}=af-be+cd\) in six independent coordinates. There are \((q^3-1)q^2\) solutions to \(\operatorname{Pf}=1\) modulo \(q\), and smoothness gives \(q^{5(k-1)}\) lifts modulo \(q^k\). At \(q=3\), the printed normalization therefore gives \((26/27)3^{2k}\), which diverges; the corrected density is \(26/27\). Finally, the determinant prime sum on p. 11 needs \(T^{n^2}\) in place of \(T^n\). These repairs agree with the ambient dimensions and the following coarea formulas.

## Symmetric-matrix formulas

For \(S=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,p)\), substitution into the stated mass formula (11), p. 21, makes the ratio of \(\alpha_p(S)^{-1}\) to \((2p)^{-1}(\alpha_p^{\rm std}(S))^{-1}\) equal to \[1+\frac{(-1/p)}p .\] Thus Lemma 6.2’s relative \(O(p^{-2})\) needs \(O(p^{-1})\) at rank three. The weaker error is sufficient and is already used later.

Further displayed factors must be reconciled with their own derivations: the last branch in Proposition 7.4 (p. 24) needs \(1+2^{1-n}/(1+2^{-n/2})\), as in (18); the extra \(1/2\) on p. 29 must cancel against Corollary 7.3’s factor \(2\); and the first even-rank level-count display on p. 36 needs \(p^{(n-1)/2}\). The statements of Lemmas 7.5 and 7.7 must retain the odd-zeta product present in their preceding derivations.

There is concrete counterevidence to interpreting these slips as a failure of the corrected density. An exact enumeration of all \(2^{10}=1024\) symmetric binary \(4\)-by-\(4\) matrices found \(448\) invertible ones: \(28\) alternating and \(420\) nonalternating. A separate exact determinant expansion and a lifting argument give \[\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 p\bmod8&1&3&5&7\\ \hline
 \delta_2(p)&245/256&105/128&231/256&105/128 .
\end{array}\] Their mean is \(7/8=(448/1024)/(1-1/2)\), the directly counted Bateman–Horn local factor. These values agree with corrected (16)–(18). The retained script exhausts the finite cases; the passage to stable \(2\)-adic densities is justified separately by the analytical lifting argument, not inferred from a finite experiment.

For even \(n=2r\ge4\), the source’s \(L\)-series convention omits the Euler factor at \(2\). A fixed-cutoff expansion of that absolutely convergent series, followed by PNT in fixed residue classes, leaves only odd squares and gives \((1-2^{-n})\zeta(n)\) as the mean. The corrected four residue-class multipliers cancel \(1-2^{-n}\). The gamma, even-zeta and parity factors in the definite and indefinite formulas likewise cancel to the stated odd-zeta product. These algebraic implications are checked conditionally on the displayed general mass and measure formulas; no uniform growing-modulus remainder or all-rank genus classification is certified.

# Checks that support the corrected strategy

## Determinant local stability and summation

Write \(\beta_q=\prod_{j=2}^n(1-q^{-j})\). For target determinant \(p\), every mod-\(p\) matrix of rank at most \(n-2\) has all its lifts divisible by \(p^2\) in determinant. Rank \(n-1\) matrices are smooth points of the determinant-zero fiber. Counting their kernels and injective quotient maps gives \[\#\{A\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb F_p):\operatorname{rank}A=n-1\}
 =\frac{p^n-1}{p-1}\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}(p^n-p^i).\] After normalization and smooth lifting this yields the exact relation \[\frac{\mathfrak S_p}{\mathfrak S_{\det}}
                   =1+p^{-1}+\cdots+p^{-(n-1)}.\] Thus the needed stability is valid with \(O(p^{-1})\).

For each fixed \(\Omega\), a finite-head/tail split justifies summing the level-count error \(o_\Omega(1)\) over primes; no uniformity over all possible cones is required at this step. Ordinary PNT and partial summation give \[\sum_{p\le T^n}p^{n-1}\sim\frac{T^{n^2}}{n^2\log T}.\] Together with the corrected coarea measure this verifies the determinant cone implication, conditional on the cited level-set equidistribution theorem. The corresponding Pfaffian exponent is \(T^{n(2n-1)}/[n(2n-1)\log T]\), as correctly printed on p. 13.

## Sieve scales and order of limits

For a convex region in a box of scale \(T\), the translated lattice discrepancy per residue class is \(O_D(T^{D-1})\). Even the trivial bound of at most \(d^D\) residue classes gives, with \(z=T^{1/(10D)}\), \[\sum_{d\le z}|r_d(R)|
   \ll_D T^{D-1}z^{D+1}
   \ll_D T^{D-1/2},\qquad D\ge2.\] This avoids the source’s unsupported uniform \(O_F(d^{D-1})\) composite modulus count. Slicing along a coordinate on which \(F\) depends bounds the small-prime contribution by \(O_F(T^{D-1}z+T^{D-1})\), also below the needed error scale. The sieve inequality and the required finite-field prime-density input remain external ingredients. The proof uses absolute irreducibility although the opening formulation says irreducibility over \(\mathbb Q\); the checked application is restricted to the stated matrix families.

If a corrected construction supplies \(N(\epsilon)\) convex covering pieces of total volume \(O(\epsilon)\), the remainder is \[O_F\!\left(\frac{\epsilon T^D}{\log T}
               +N(\epsilon)T^{D-1/2}\right).\] For fixed \(\epsilon\), the second term is negligible. One first takes \(T\to\infty\), then \(\epsilon\to0\). This checks the error bookkeeping conditional on the missing geometric construction, without certifying that construction.

# Conclusion and verification limits

One Major defect in the geometric implication and one grouped Minor normalization issue are supported in this acquired version. The explicit repairs and conditional fixed-cone checks are retained as counterevidence. The full matrix asymptotics, all signature cases and all external equidistribution and mass-formula conventions have not been independently certified by this report.
