# Checked reasoning and dependencies

## Light bulb smoothing

On pages 5–8, the argument first equips a topological normal bundle \(V\) of \(R\) with its intrinsic smooth structure. Quinn’s theorem is applied to the inclusion into the ambient smooth manifold, not as an assertion that the original inclusion is smooth. The relative construction on page 6 keeps the isotopy fixed near the smooth dual \(G\). This is what allows the maps on \(V\) and on \(\nu(G)\) to be glued.

Gabai’s lemma then supplies a surface \(R''\) smoothly isotopic to \(R\) in the plumbed neighborhood \(N=V\cup\nu(G)\) and lying where the terminal embedding is smooth. Composing the isotopy in \(N\) with the topological embedding, followed by the terminal ambient isotopy, gives the claimed topological isotopy in \(M\). Smoothness is asserted only for the final surface. Shrinking \(V\) and \(\nu(G)\) gives the stated support control. There is no inference here that the intermediate Whitney disks were already smooth in \(M\).

Quinn’s theorem, the extension of topological normal bundles, and Gabai’s lemma are substantive external inputs. Their original proofs were not independently reproduced. The manuscript’s explanation of their use was checked; the pictures of the Norman moves were not visually inspected because of the recorded image-interface failure.

## Stable smoothing and the complement fundamental group

Lemma 3.2, pages 9–10, has the necessary compactness and embedding assumptions. If \(g(x)=g(y)\) and \(g\) is \(\epsilon\)-close to the embedding \(f\), then \[d(f(x),f(y))<2\epsilon.\] Uniform continuity of \(f^{-1}\) yields a short path from \(x\) to \(y\) in the domain. The selected constants place its image under \(g\) in a contractible neighborhood in the target. This proves existence of a null-homotopic double point loop; it does not assert that every arbitrary choice of a loop is null-homotopic for a nonsimply connected domain.

That distinction is respected in Lemma 3.4. The controlled regular homotopy selects null-homotopic loops first. The bundle exact sequence and the cited complement isomorphism identify the relevant kernel with a cyclic group generated by a meridian. Twisting the push-off changes its meridional power and makes the chosen parallel null-homotopic in the complement of the immersed surface and the Whitney disks. Null-homotopy merely in the ambient manifold would not suffice; the proof explicitly supplies the stronger complement statement.

Pages 12–13 construct immersed geometric duals, use separate \(S^2\times S^2\) summands to remove their intersections, and then apply Theorem C in disjoint neighborhoods of each Whitney disk and its dual. The resulting smooth Whitney disks remain topologically isotopic to the disks that reverse the original finger moves. Thus the argument retains the original surface’s isotopy class. Simply resolving double points of an arbitrary approximation by the Norman trick would not establish this, as the manuscript itself notes in Remark 3.1. The controlled form of Quinn’s theorem and the complement isomorphism cited from earlier work remain external dependencies.

## Dax invariance and the unstabilized indeterminacy

Lemma 4.5, page 15, uses a sufficiently close generic approximation of an embedded honest track. Lemma 3.2 supplies trivial double point labels. Here the domain \(I^3\) is simply connected, so changing the connecting path does not introduce another label. Definition 4.1 counts only nonidentity labels, and hence the sum is zero. This establishes the needed vanishing for a topological isotopy between smooth disks without claiming a general topological jet-transversality theorem.

Definition 4.6 deliberately takes values modulo \(d(\pi_3(M))\), although the smoothings lie in a stabilization. Theorem 4.7 does not silently enlarge that denominator. For fixed \(H\), changing the smoothing isotopies inserts topological isotopies between smooth disks, whose Dax values vanish by Lemma 4.5. For two choices \(H,K\) in \(M\), the difference is \[\operatorname{dax}(h_0^{-1}HK^{-1}h_0).\] The reparametrization on pages 16–17 cancels the smoothing paths and produces a kernel map in the original \(M\). Its value lies in \(d(\pi_3(M))\), as required. This is the decisive distinction between the original and stabilized indeterminacies.

The point-avoidance argument of Lemma 4.11 uses the nonempty boundary to place a point outside a collared isotopy and move it to the prescribed omitted point while fixing the endpoint surfaces. The passage to an almost smooth manifold then uses Lemma 4.12 to identify the Dax image after puncturing. The almost smooth structure is fixed in Theorem F; independence of an arbitrary choice of such a structure is not claimed by that theorem and has not been inferred here. The smooth Dax theory and its generic-track theorem are accepted as the paper’s cited inputs, not freshly proved in this review.
