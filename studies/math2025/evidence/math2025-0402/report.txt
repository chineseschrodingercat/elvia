Abstract: The reviewed source is the 52-page Wiley version of record of Cancès, Faulstich, Kirsch, Letournel, and Levitt, DOI 10.1002/cpa.22244. The review identifies five groups of local defects: an insufficiently specified interaction-tensor convention; a sign and normalization error in the response formula; inconsistent numerical rank diagnostics and dimension conventions; a fixed-point bifurcation criterion written for the wrong derivative; and transcription errors in the high-level and first-order calculations. Exact finite-dimensional examples establish the failed statements. Explicit repairs preserve the principal local existence and first-order arguments. No counterexample to Theorems 4 or 5, or unresolved Major defect in their core argument, was established. The initial assessment is Minor, subject to the corrections and numerical clarifications below. The scientific decision remains pending.

# Definitions, quantifiers, and main-theorem boundaries

The real one-particle space is \(\mathbb R^L\), with \(1\leq N<L\) and \(\mathcal D=\{D=D^T=D^2:\operatorname{Tr}D=N\}\). Coordinate fragments partition the \(L\) sites, with sizes \(L_x<N\). The block-density space has tangent space \(\mathcal Y\), consisting of symmetric block-diagonal matrices of total trace zero. The low-level map minimizes the Hartree–Fock energy over projectors with prescribed block density; the high-level map solves the interacting impurity problems with frozen occupied cores and a common chemical potential imposing the total particle number. The fixed-point equation is \(P=F^\mathrm{DMET}_\alpha(P)=F^\mathrm{HL}_\alpha(F^\mathrm{LL}_\alpha(P))\).

The actual hypotheses, pp. 1367–1369 (physical 9–11), are:

1. \(\epsilon_N<0<\epsilon_{N+1}\) for the one-body Hamiltonian \(h\), with \(D_0=1_{(-\infty,0]}(h)\).

2. Both \(D_0X_x\) and \((I-D_0)X_x\) have dimension \(L_x\) for every fragment. Equivalently, every fragment block of \(D_0\) has all eigenvalues strictly between 0 and 1.

3. \(B=\operatorname{Bd}|_{T_{D_0}\mathcal D}:T_{D_0}\mathcal D\to\mathcal Y\) is surjective.

4. The true frozen-impurity density response \(R:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal Y\) to a perturbation \(h+Y\) is invertible.

Theorem 4 asserts existence of a sufficiently small two-sided interval in \(\alpha\) and a neighbourhood of \(D_0\) with a unique real-analytic solution branch there. It does not assert global uniqueness of all DMET solutions, a uniform interval over all Hamiltonians, or convergence of the elementary fixed-point iteration. Theorem 5 asserts \(D^\mathrm{DMET}_\alpha=D^\mathrm{exact}_\alpha+O(\alpha^2)
=D^\mathrm{HF}_\alpha+O(\alpha^2)\) in this local regime. Equality of first derivatives does not require the exact interacting density to remain a projector.

A closing spectral gap, a fragment eigenvalue at 0 or 1, or loss of either surjectivity or response invertibility lies outside these conclusions. In particular, numerical branch behaviour where (A4) fails is not a counterexample to Theorem 4. Assumption (A2) also entails \(L_x\leq \min(N,L-N)\); the prescribed fragmentation must satisfy this feasibility restriction. The paper’s additional convention \(L_x<N\) is retained in every example below.

# Problem 1: the stated tensor symmetry does not justify the effective one-body formulas

## Location and exact failure

On p. 1363 (physical 5), the stated interaction symmetry is only \(V_{ab\,cd}=V_{cd\,ab}\). Equations (21), (25), and (30), pp. 1366, 1368, and 1376 (physical 8, 10, 18), use \(J(D)-K(D)\) as the effective one-body term. Proposition 8 assumes that \(D\) is compatible with the fragmentation; it does not assume (A3) or (A4).

Take \(L=4\), \(N=2\), four singleton fragments, and \[D=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&0\\1&1&0&0\\
 0&0&1&1\\0&0&1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad h=I-2D.\] This is a rank-two real projector, every fragment population is \(1/2\), and \(h\) has eigenvalues \(-1,-1,1,1\). Thus all assumptions used in Proposition 8 hold, as do (A1)–(A2). For fragment 1, the impurity space is \(\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)\) and the frozen core is \(c=(e_3+e_4)/\sqrt2\).

Let \(S_{13}=S_{31}=1\), \(A_{23}=1\), \(A_{32}=-1\), all other entries zero, and set \[V_{ab\,cd}=S_{ab}A_{cd}+A_{ab}S_{cd}.\] This real tensor satisfies exactly the stated pair-Hermitian symmetry. Its entire two-body operator \(\frac12\sum V_{ab\,cd}a_a^\dagger a_b^\dagger a_d a_c\) vanishes: each summand factorizes with a symmetric pair of creation or annihilation indices, which the CAR relations annihilate. The independent calculation checked all entries of the full \(16\times16\) Fock-space matrix, with exact fractions.

Nevertheless, for the core density \(cc^T\), the restriction of the paper’s \(J(cc^T)-K(cc^T)\) to the impurity space is \[\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.\] It is nonzero and not symmetric. Its expectation in \((e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2\) is \(1/2\), whereas the exact interaction and its frozen-core restriction are zero. Changing to the paper’s orthonormal impurity basis conjugates this discrepancy and does not remove it. This is an operator-formula counterexample satisfying the proposition’s assumptions, not a counterexample to the main theorems.

## Impact, repair, and counterevidence

A precise harmless normalization of the interaction tensor is needed: \[V^c_{ab\,cd}=\tfrac12(V_{ab\,cd}+V_{ba\,dc}).
 \tag{R1}\] Simultaneously swapping the two creation and the two annihilation indices produces two minus signs. Hence replacing \(V\) by \(V^c\) leaves the many-body Hamiltonian, all variational impurity problems, and the HF energy unchanged. It preserves pair-Hermiticity and imposes \(V^c_{ab\,cd}=V^c_{ba\,dc}\). Wick contraction then gives the \(J-K\) expressions with \(J,K\) formed from \(V^c\). In the example, \(V^c=0\) exactly.

Thus this is Minor: it requires an explicit representative convention, or the corresponding symmetrized contractions throughout, but no restriction on the underlying many-body operator or on (A1)–(A4). Physical interaction conventions may already contain the additional symmetry; it was not part of the displayed mathematical hypothesis. Using the raw nonsymmetric effective operator is not a valid repair.

# Problem 2: response sign and chemical-potential normalization

## Location and derivation

Equations (75)–(76), p. 1397 (physical 39), have a positive overall response sign and identify \(\widetilde\ell(Y)\) with \(\operatorname{Tr}(GY)\). The same page’s last Taylor expansion has another incompatible chemical- potential sign. In contrast, (54), p. 1389 (physical 31), correctly defines the positive operator \(\mathcal L_x^+\) through \(\delta 1_{(-\infty,0]}(h_x)=-\mathcal L_x^+(\delta h_x)\).

Equation reference: eq:mu, eq:response. Write \(C_x=C^x(D_0)\) and \(p_x=C_x^T\Pi_x C_x\). Differentiating the particle-number constraint gives the corrected formulas \[\begin{aligned}
 t&=\sum_x\langle p_x,\mathcal L_x^+(p_x)\rangle>0,\\
 \mu'[Y]&=\frac{\sum_x\langle p_x,
             \mathcal L_x^+(C_x^T Y C_x)\rangle}{t}
          =\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(GY)}{t},\\
 R[Y]&=-\sum_x\Pi_x C_x\mathcal L_x^+
       \bigl(C_x^T Y C_x-\mu'[Y]p_x\bigr)C_x^T\Pi_x.
       \end{aligned}\] Here \(t\) is exactly the positive derivative of the total impurity particle number with respect to chemical potential. There is no hypothesis fixing it to 1. These formulas enforce \(\operatorname{Tr}R[Y]=0\).

## Exact example satisfying all four hypotheses

Let \(L=4\), \(N=2\), with singleton fragments, and set \[u=\tfrac12(1,1,1,1)^T,\quad
 v=\tfrac1{\sqrt{18}}(1,3,-2,-2)^T,\quad
 D=uu^T+vv^T,\quad h=I-2uu^T-3vv^T.
 \tag{R2}\] The spectrum of \(h\) is \((-2,-1,1,1)\). The fragment populations are \(p=(11/36,3/4,17/36,17/36)\), all strictly interior. All off-diagonal entries of \(D\) are nonzero, so a diagonal matrix commuting with \(D\) is scalar; this proves (A3). Define \[z_i=p_i(1-p_i),\quad M_{ii}=z_i,\quad M_{ij}=-D_{ij}^2\ (i\ne j),
 \qquad \sigma=(11/24,3/8,17/42,17/42).\] The positive \(\sigma_i\) are the reciprocal impurity occupied–virtual gaps. Direct two-level perturbation theory gives, for \(Y=\operatorname{diag}y\), \[R[Y]_{ii}=-2\sigma_i\big((My)_i-\mu'[Y]z_i\big).
 \tag{R3}\] If this response is zero, \(My=\mu'z\); summing gives \(\mu'=0\), since \(M\) is a graph Laplacian and \(\sum_i z_i>0\). Its graph is connected, so \(y\) is constant. On the trace-zero space this proves (A4). The exact response matrix also has rank 3 in the independent calculation.

For \(y=(1,-1,0,0)\), exact arithmetic gives \[n:=\operatorname{Tr}(GY)=\frac{95}{1512},\qquad
 t=\frac{6701}{9072},\qquad
 \mu'[Y]=\frac{570}{6701}.\] Using the printed (75)–(76) instead gives \[\operatorname{Tr}R_{\rm printed}[Y]=n(1-t)
       =\frac{225245}{13716864}\ne0.\] It therefore fails even to map \(\mathcal Y\) into \(\mathcal Y\). The corrected response has trace zero exactly. Independently, the overall negative sign follows from the spectral-projector derivative; it is not repaired merely by inserting the denominator.

## Effect on the main proof

The preceding factorization can be retained. With \(A Q=-[D_0,[h,Q]]\) and \(B^*Y=D_0Y(I-D_0)+(I-D_0)YD_0\), the actual operator \(L A^{-1}B^*\) defined in (70)–(71) equals the corrected [eq:response] (#eq:response). Substitution gives the minus sign and [eq:mu] (#eq:mu); it does not give the printed (75)–(76). Consequently \[d_P(F^\mathrm{DMET}_0-I)(P_0)=R(BA^{-1}B^*)^{-1}\] still proves invertibility under (A3)–(A4). The IFT argument is repairable at this displayed calculation. The exact example disproves the printed formula, not invertibility of the true response or Theorem 4. Classification: Minor, with both corrections required.

# Problem 3: the numerical surjectivity diagnostic uses the wrong product

Figure 6 and the accompanying paragraph, p. 1373 (physical 15), identify the lowest eigenvalue of \(S=B^*B\) with (A3). Surjectivity of \(B:T_{D_0}\mathcal D\to\mathcal Y\) instead requires \(BB^*\) to be positive definite on \(\mathcal Y\). When \(\dim T_{D_0}\mathcal D>\dim\mathcal Y\), \(B^*B\) has a zero eigenvalue even for surjective \(B\).

Example (R2) satisfies all four hypotheses and has dimensions 4 and 3. The nonzero matrix \[Q=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&3&-3\\0&0&5&-5\\3&5&0&0\\-3&-5&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}\] satisfies \(DQ+QD=Q\) and \(\operatorname{Bd}Q=0\). Thus \(Q\) is a tangent vector in the kernel of \(B^*B\), while \(B\) is surjective. The exact computation verifies these identities. This decisively invalidates the stated diagnostic.

There is also a dimension-convention inconsistency to resolve. Page 1372 (physical 14) specifies \(N_f=3\), \(L_x=2\), \(\dim\mathcal Y=8\), and \(N=10\). Under the definitions in Section 2, \(\sum_x L_x=L=6\) would then violate \(N<L\). Appendix B, pp. 1409–1410 (physical 51–52), instead displays five occupied and seven virtual orbitals and a \(7\times5\) excitation block. If those are the real one-particle spaces and the three fragments have equal size, they imply \(L=12\), \(N=5\), \(L_x=4\), \(\dim\mathcal Y=29\), and \(\dim\mathcal D=35\). This is a conditional inference from the displayed orbital counts, not a claim to have inspected the implementation. Spin summation or spin-paired density conventions may explain the differing numbers, but a precise map to the theorem’s conventions is needed.

The theoretical rank test in Lemma 11, p. 1378, and the adjoint calculation (41), p. 1385, are correct counterevidence: the analytical criterion itself is not disproved. Replace the numerical diagnostic by \(BB^*\) on the declared target space, or state explicitly that only the appropriate nonzero singular values are being measured, and reconcile the dimensions before interpreting Figure 6. Classification: Minor. The published implementation and the corrected numerical curve remain unverified.

# Problem 4: zero versus unit eigenvalue in the fixed-point bifurcation claim

On p. 1374 (physical 16), the discussion asserts a simple eigenvalue of \(dF^\mathrm{DMET}_0(P_0)\) crossing zero, with all remaining eigenvalues having negative real parts, and uses this as evidence for a transcritical bifurcation of \(P=F^\mathrm{DMET}_0(P)\). The exact differentiation is \[\Phi(\Theta,P)=F^\mathrm{DMET}_0(\Theta,P)-P,\qquad
 d_P\Phi=d_PF^\mathrm{DMET}_0-I.
 \tag{R4}\] Under the printed spectral condition, the crossing eigenvalue of \(d_P\Phi\) is \(-1\) and every other eigenvalue is also nonzero. The implicit function theorem then gives a unique local branch; that spectral condition cannot establish the claimed local fixed-point bifurcation. The required singularity is an eigenvalue 1 of \(dF^\mathrm{DMET}_0\), or equivalently 0 of \(d\Phi\).

The paper itself uses the correct residual in Section 7.6.3, equation (67), p. 1394 (physical 36), so this may be a notational substitution in the numerical section. State explicitly which Jacobian was calculated and which equation was solved by Newton’s method. A simple zero crossing of the residual derivative alone also does not certify the transcritical type; the reduced nonlinear coefficients or equivalent nondegeneracy information must distinguish it from other bifurcations. The branch plots in Figure 8 and the orbital discussion are numerical evidence, not that missing nondegeneracy argument.

Classification: Minor, affecting the stated numerical inference. No claim is made that the reported branches cannot occur, that the code used the wrong residual, or that the perturbative theorems fail. The actual computational convention must be clarified.

# Problem 5: local Hamiltonian, charge, and normalization transcription errors

## High-level Hamiltonian and charge constraint

On p. 1393 (physical 35), (64) defines \(\widetilde h_x=C_x^{\sim T}hC_x^\sim\), but the displayed impurity Hamiltonian below (66) has neither the core contraction \(\alpha C_x^{\sim T}(J(D^x)-K(D^x))C_x^\sim\) nor the factor \(1/2\) on its two-body term. These are not scalar environment-energy shifts. Equation (30), and the later first-order calculation on pp. 1403–1404, supply the missing terms.

For the compatible \(L=4,N=2,D\) in Problem 1, now use the canonical tensor \(V_{13\,13}=V_{31\,31}=1\), with all other entries zero. Its interaction is \(n_1n_3\). Freezing \(c=(e_3+e_4)/\sqrt2\) gives the nonzero impurity term \(\frac12 n_1\); the displayed Hamiltonian on p. 1393 omits it. The same example checks the relabelling of \(a_7\) on p. 1382 (physical 24): its four-contraction sum must retain the preceding factor \(1/2\). In a basis containing the normalized core orbital, that inner sum is 1 for the occupied \(e_1\) state, so its energy is \(1/2\), not 1. Restoring the factor reconnects the calculation to the final \(J-K\) expression on p. 1383, after the tensor convention in Problem 1 has been imposed. Separately, \(V_{12\,12}=V_{21\,21}=1\) gives \(n_1n_2\), whose energy on the doubly occupied impurity is 1. Omitting the factor \(1/2\) gives 2. These are exact CAR computations with all compatibility and gap assumptions used in Lemma 13 satisfied.

The same page and p. 1394 impose particle number by summing \(\langle c_\kappa^\dagger c_\lambda\rangle\) over both fragment indices. The sum must be over \(\kappa=\lambda\). An assumption-compliant check is available with \(L=6,N=3\). Let \[P=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 D_4=\begin{pmatrix}P&(\sqrt3/4)I_2\\
                    (\sqrt3/4)I_2&I_2-P\end{pmatrix},\quad
 D=D_4\oplus\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Since \(P(I_2-P)=3I_2/16\), \(D\) is a rank-three projector. Use fragments \(\{1,2\},\{3,5\},\{4,6\}\), reordered into consecutive blocks if desired, and \(h=I-2D\). The block eigenvalues are \((1/4,3/4)\), \((1/2,1/2)\), \((1/2,1/2)\), so (A1)–(A2) hold and \(L_x=2<N\). At the noninteracting solution the actual total fragment trace is 3, but the printed sum of all fragment entries is \(7/2\). This is a mathematical difference, not notation for a trace.

## First-order normalization and signs

On p. 1400 (physical 42), the displayed \(\Psi_0=(N!)^{-1/2}c_N^\dagger\cdots c_1^\dagger|0\rangle\) contains an extra factor for the canonical creation operators defined in Section 2. Their product applied to normalized vacuum already has norm 1. For \(N=2\), the displayed vector has squared norm \(1/2\), contradicting the claimed orthonormal excitation basis. Remove the extra factorial factor. The first-order expansion must also retain the negative excitation-energy denominators. On p. 1401 (physical 43), the two positive intermediate formulas for \([d_1]_{ai}\) lose the minus sign from the preceding sum-over-states formula. Equation (77) already restores the correct negative sign.

The direct and exchange contractions actually printed on that page are \[[J_{W_2}(d)]_{mn}=\sum_{p,q}[W_2]_{npmq}d_{pq},\qquad
 [K_{W_2}(d)]_{mn}=\sum_{p,q}[W_2]_{npqm}d_{pq}.\] Under the tensor normalization (R1), these agree with (21). For the first expression, interchange the dummy indices \(p,q\) and use \(d_{pq}=d_{qp}\). For the second, pair-Hermiticity and simultaneous pair exchange give \([W_2]_{npqm}=[W_2]_{qmnp}=[W_2]_{mqpn}\). Thus there is no additional contraction-index defect under that convention. For the canonical interaction \(n_1n_3\) and the block projector of Problem 1, both expressions give \(J-K=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,0,1/2,0)\). With \(h=I-2D\), the negative response in (77) gives \[D_1=\operatorname{diag}(-1/8,1/8,-1/8,1/8),\] in agreement with the retained exact perturbation calculation in the full six-dimensional two-particle sector. The contrary index counterexample was based on a transcription of the formulas that does not match the source and is withdrawn.

The rewritten impurity Hamiltonian on p. 1404 (physical 46) also already keeps \(-\mu(\alpha)\) outside the factor \(\alpha\): the closing parenthesis follows the two-body term and precedes the chemical-potential term. The subsequent \(W_1\) consistently uses \(\mu'(0)\). No chemical-potential factoring defect is supported by this page; that proposed subclaim is withdrawn. A nonzero \(\mu'(0)\) establishes the term’s first-order importance, but does not show an error in the correctly printed formula.

## Repair boundary

Restore the core term and the half factor, replace the charge sums by diagonal sums, and normalize the determinant according to the CAR convention. The corrected finite-dimensional impurity Hamiltonian remains analytic; the noninteracting gap and positive charge derivative persist locally, so Lemma 13’s analytic IFT argument still works. The singles-only contribution to the one-body first derivative also survives the normalization correction. Classification: Minor. These errors should be corrected explicitly; merely invoking analyticity of the incorrectly displayed Hamiltonian would not prove the result for the variationally defined DMET map.

# Core proof and dependency checks after the specified repairs

## Compatibility, representability, and noninteracting consistency

Lemma 7’s rank/interior equivalences follow from \(x^TDx=\|Dx\|^2\) and the corresponding identity for \(I-D\). The occupied and virtual impurity columns in (28) are orthonormal, mutually orthogonal, and span the asserted \(2L_x\)-dimensional space. The core formula (31) subtracts precisely the occupied impurity projector.

For Lemma 9’s singleton-fragment case, Kadison’s Theorem 6 gives the diagonal characterization, and his Theorem 7 supplies a real projection with the prescribed diagonal [kadison]. The latter’s constructive proof was checked, so passage to the real setting is supported. For two fragments, writing a projector in blocks gives \(CC^T=P_1-P_1^2\) and \(C^TC=P_2-P_2^2\); the off-diagonal projection equation forces complementary fractional eigenvalues, as claimed. The commutant test in Lemma 11 follows by identifying the kernel of the adjoint of \(B\); it tests surjectivity, not injectivity of \(B\).

For Proposition 1, \(D_0\) commutes with \(h\). Compression to the occupied and virtual impurity spaces therefore retains strictly negative and strictly positive spectral sectors. The chemical potential 0 yields the required density. The total impurity particle number is globally nondecreasing in chemical potential, and its local derivative at 0 is strictly positive by (A2). This also excludes another chemical-potential root with the same total particle number. The possible impurity level crossings away from 0 do not invalidate this monotonicity argument.

## Analytic maps and local uniqueness

The noninteracting energy obeys the gap estimate \[\operatorname{Tr}(h(D-D_0))\geq \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|D-D_0\|_F^2,
 \qquad \gamma=\epsilon_{N+1}-\epsilon_N>0.\] Together with the local submersion from (A3), this localizes every global low-level minimizer for small \(|\alpha|\) and \(P-P_0\). In the displayed estimates on pp. 1391–1392, \(\alpha C_{\rm nl}\) must be bounded by \(|\alpha|C_{\rm nl}\) for the advertised two-sided interval, and the submersion bound may require a fixed local constant. These harmless bound corrections do not affect localization.

In a basis diagonalizing \(D_0\) and \(h\), a tangent vector is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&X^T\\X&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The Hessian acts on its lower-left block by \(X\mapsto h_+X-Xh_-\) and is positive with lower bound \(\gamma\). This independently verifies the cited Grassmann-Hessian identity, without assuming an external theorem with mismatched hypotheses. The saddle matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B^*\\B&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) is invertible because \(A>0\) and \(B\) is onto. Hence \[dF^\mathrm{LL}_0=A^{-1}B^*(BA^{-1}B^*)^{-1}.\] For the corrected high-level Hamiltonian, finite-dimensional gapped spectral projectors are analytic by the resolvent formula. The scalar charge equation has derivative \(t>0\), so its chemical potential and density are analytic locally. Global monotonicity of the ground-state charge rules out a second admissible root. Combining these derivatives yields the corrected residual factorization in Problem 2, to which (A4) applies. No assertion of contractivity is needed.

## First-order exactness and limits of numerical evidence

In the determinant expansion, a one-body observable couples the reference determinant only to single excitations. Double excitations therefore do not contribute to the first derivative of the one-body density. The corrected Wick contraction produces the same first-order effective one-body perturbation for the exact and HF densities. In the impurity calculation, the core and occupied impurity densities sum to \(D_0\), giving the same first-order interaction term for the interacting and HF high-level maps. Their scalar chemical-potential derivatives agree by the same charge equation. The invertible fixed-point residual then gives equality of the block-density derivatives, and the low-level map gives equality of the full-density derivatives. This verifies the mechanism of Theorem 5 after the stated local corrections.

The H\(_{10}\) and H\(_6^{4-}\) plots illustrate first-order agreement and second-order disagreement; they are not needed to prove the analytical first-order assertion. The admitted noise near poorly conditioned hypotheses does not itself establish a theorem defect. The qualitative DET remark is not a replacement for an independently stated quantitative invertibility hypothesis; no counterexample to that variant was established here, and it is not counted as a Problem.
