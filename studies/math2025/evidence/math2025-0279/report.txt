# F1. Major: the radius estimate and its stated sufficient bounds fail

Location. Lemma 3.8, p. 13, the displayed estimate below the bound for \(|a_O|\), its following sufficient conditions, and (3.10) on p. 14. The constants on p. 17 and the uniqueness completion on p. 18 were also checked.

Take \(X=\{0,1\}\), \(d(0,1)=1\), and \(T=\mathrm{id}\). ASP holds with \(\delta=1/2,C=L=1,\lambda=\log2\): every pair of \(\delta\)-close points is equal, and every allowed periodic pseudo-orbit is constant. MCGBP holds with \(K=1,v=0\), since \(u-\min_Xu\ge0\). Set \[\alpha=\varepsilon=1,\quad \psi=1,\quad O=\{0\},\quad
 u(0)=\frac3{50},\quad u(1)=0,\quad h=\frac1{10}.\] Then \(\bar u=u\), \(Z_{u,\psi}=\{1\}\), \(\|\bar u\|_1=3/25\), \(D(O)=1/2\), \(L_O=1/2\), and \(a_O=4/25\). The negative-region radius used in the paper is \[R=\frac{|a_O|\|\psi\|_0+\|h\|_0}{\varepsilon}=\frac{13}{50}.\] The printed intermediate requirements hold: \[L_O=\frac12>\frac{12}{25}
 =2(2\operatorname{Lip}_T)\|\bar u\|_1,\qquad
 \|h\|_0=\frac1{10}<\frac18.\] Nevertheless \(R=13/50>1/4=D(O)/(2\operatorname{Lip}_T)\). The displayed radius inequality itself gives the false comparison \(26/25\le22/25\).

This example tests the explicit intermediate conditions, not the final stronger choice on p. 17. A separate exact-rational check with \(u(0)=10^{-6},u(1)=0,h=-10^{-4}\), on the same system, satisfies even those final printed parameter restrictions while still falsifying the intermediate displayed inequality: \[\frac{R}{D/2}=\frac{199}{250000}>
 \frac{51}{125000}=\text{its printed right-hand side}.\] Here the radius conclusion itself does hold. In both examples the resulting \(G\) is zero on \(O\) and strictly positive at the other point. Thus neither example is misrepresented as a counterexample to Proposition 3.2. The initial review’s rational-arithmetic script and its actual successful execution are retained. The radius condition controls the escape construction in Lemma 3.8 and Proposition 3.2, the second main step in the proof of Theorem 2.1(I) on p. 7. Its false quantitative derivation therefore concerns a core proof step. The smaller example does not refute the final radius conclusion, and neither example is a counterexample to the theorem.

## A complete replacement retaining the original quantifiers

Write \(N=\#O\), \(D=D(O)\), \(r=d_{\alpha,Z_{u,\psi}}(O)\), and \[m=\psi_{\min},\quad M=\|\psi\|_0,\quad P=\|\psi_K\|_\alpha,\quad
 B=\|\bar u\|_\alpha,\quad b=\|\bar u\|_0.\] For the prescribed coefficient \(\varepsilon>0\), set \(t=\varepsilon/2\) and \(H=10\varepsilon\). Define \[A=\frac{4C^\alpha}{t(1-e^{-\lambda\alpha})}
       \left(B+H+\frac{b+\delta^\alpha}{m}P\right),
 \qquad L_1=\frac{t}{(2\operatorname{Lip}_T)^\alpha},\] \[L_2=(A+2)\frac{MB}{m},\qquad
 L_3=(A+2)\left(1+\frac{M}{m}\right),\qquad
 \widehat L=1+\frac{3L_2}{L_1},\qquad
 \widehat\delta=\min\left\{1,\frac{L_1}{3L_3}\right\}.\] These constants are independent of \(O\). Assume \(D^\alpha>\widehat Lr\), \(\|h\|_\alpha\le10\varepsilon\), and \(\|h\|_0<D^\alpha\widehat\delta/N\), the correct elementary bound is \[Q:=|a_O|M+\|h\|_0
 \le \frac{MB}{mN}r+\left(1+\frac{M}{m}\right)\|h\|_0.\] In particular \(Q<L_1D^\alpha/3\), so the radius for coefficient \(t\) is less than \(D/(2\operatorname{Lip}_T)\).

For \(G_t=\bar u+t\,d^\alpha(\cdot,O)+h-a_O\psi_K\), the same escape construction as on pp. 14–17 gives a comparison-distance sum at most \(4C^\alpha Q/[t(1-e^{-\lambda\alpha})]\). The periodic remainder is at least \(-NQ\), and the escape contribution is at least \(L_1D^\alpha-Q\). Hence the block sum is at least \[L_1D^\alpha-(A+N+1)Q
 \ge L_1D^\alpha-L_2r-L_3N\|h\|_0
 >\frac13L_1D^\alpha.\] Here \(A+N+1\le N(A+2)\) uses \(N\ge1\). Orbits staying close to a fixed periodic phase forever are generic for \(\mu_O\), by ASP. For other generic points, concatenation of positive blocks gives \(\int G_t\,d\mu\ge0\).

Positive finite blocks alone do not establish a strictly positive asymptotic mean. The reserved coefficient does: \(a_O\) is independent of \(t\), since the distance term vanishes on \(O\), so \[G_\varepsilon=G_t+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}d^\alpha(\cdot,O).\] Every invariant probability other than \(\mu_O\) has positive integral of \(d^\alpha(\cdot,O)\). This proves strict uniqueness at the originally prescribed \(\varepsilon\), retaining the entire \(10\varepsilon\) perturbation budget. It also makes precise the informal modification on p. 18, where \(d\), rather than \(d^\alpha\), is printed in the added term. The source’s calibration and escape mechanism are valid counterevidence against an interpretation that the main locking conclusion has been disproved.

# F2. Major: the smoothing proof mixes a norm and a seminorm

Location. Norm definition on p. 5; Proposition 3.9 and Theorem 3.10, pp. 18–19; (3.19)–(3.20) and the subsequent perturbation condition, p. 20. The defined norm is \(\|v\|_1=\|v\|_0+[v]_1\). A bound on the gradient controls the second term, not both terms.

For a complete admissible model take the Riemannian circle \(M=\mathbb R/(2\mathbb Z)\), \(f=\mathrm{id}\), \(X=\{0\}\), \(u=0,\psi=1,O=\{0\}\). The subsystem has ASP and MCGBP automatically, and the multiplicative condition (3.18) holds. Put \(q=\varepsilon d(\cdot,O)\). Then \(\|q\|_0=[q]_1=\varepsilon\), so \(\|q\|_1=2\varepsilon\). If \(\|w-q\|_0<\eta\), evaluation at \(0\) and its antipode gives \[\|w\|_0\ge\varepsilon-\eta,\qquad [w]_1\ge\varepsilon-2\eta.\] Consequently \(\|w\|_1+\|q\|_1\ge4\varepsilon-3\eta>2\varepsilon+\eta\) when \(0<\eta\le\varepsilon/8\), contradicting the middle estimate in (3.20), with \(h=0\). Positive smooth periodic mollification supplies such a \(w\) with \(\|w\|_1\le\|q\|_1\), so the example respects the approximation theorem.

Moreover, the proposition allows \(\|Dh\|_0<5\varepsilon\), whereas the end of its proof imposes \(\|h\|_1<5\varepsilon\). These are different even at an arbitrarily small allowed uniform radius. On the same circle take \[h(x)=a\sin(\pi kx),\qquad
 a=\frac{5\varepsilon}{\pi k+1/2}.\] Then \(\|Dh\|_0=5\varepsilon-a/2<5\varepsilon\), but \(\|h\|_1=5\varepsilon+a/2>5\varepsilon\). Its uniform norm meets any positive prescribed radius when \(k\) is large. Thus the proof does not cover every perturbation allowed by Proposition 3.9. The source explicitly uses that proposition together with Proposition 3.1 to obtain Theorem 2.1(II) (p. 18). The false norm estimates and altered quantifier affect that core passage from Lipschitz locking to the claimed \(C^1\) neighborhood; they are assessed Major. This does not assert that the repaired \(C^1\) genericity conclusion is false.

Counterevidence. Theorem 3.10 explicitly defines its Lip-dense property as uniform approximation with an almost preserved norm bound. It does not assert convergence of the difference in Lipschitz norm. A cusp example against that stronger density would not refute the stated result.

Repair. The two actual approximation inequalities imply \[[w]_1=\|w\|_1-\|w\|_0
 <\|q\|_1+\eta-(\|q\|_0-\eta)
 =[q]_1+2\eta.\] Let \(c_O=D(O)/N\le\delta\) and \(a_0=c_O\widehat\delta\), using corrected Proposition 3.2 constants from F1 with \(\alpha=1\). Choose \[\eta=\min\{\varepsilon/4,a_0/4\},\qquad
 \widehat\delta_1=\min\{\widehat\delta/2,\varepsilon\sigma_*/(\delta+1)\},
 \qquad \widehat\delta'_1=\operatorname{diam}(M)+1.\] Here \(\sigma_*=1\) on a connected manifold. For a disconnected manifold with a fixed compatible distance agreeing with geodesic distance on components, put \(\sigma_*=\min\{1,\sigma\}\), where \(\sigma>0\) is the minimum intercomponent distance. Compactness gives finitely many components. Then \([w]_1<1.5\varepsilon<2\varepsilon\) and \(\|w\|_0<(\operatorname{diam}M+1)\varepsilon\). For every perturbation allowed by Proposition 3.9 with the new \(\widehat\delta_1\), set \(g=w-q+h\). On each component \([h]_1\le\|Dh\|_0<5\varepsilon\). For different components the quotient is at most \(2\|h\|_0/\sigma<2\varepsilon\), by the strengthened uniform radius. Consequently the global seminorm is less than \(5\varepsilon\), and \[[g]_1<7.5\varepsilon,\qquad
 \|g\|_0<1.25\varepsilon,\qquad
 \|g\|_0<3a_0/4.\] Thus \(\|g\|_1<8.75\varepsilon<10\varepsilon\), while the required uniform locking radius also holds. The orbit-dependent accuracy \(\eta\) is permitted by the quantifiers; all constants required to be independent of \(O\) remain so. The perturbation is \(C^1\)-small as \(\varepsilon\to0\), and the main \(C^1\) conclusion is retained. The additional factor \(\sigma_*\) depends only on the ambient metric, not on \(O\), and is unnecessary when \(M\) is connected.

# F3. Minor: local formulas in the higher-regularity argument

Locations. Proposition 4.1 on p. 21, (4.7) and the following block calculation on p. 24, and the final sentence of its proof on p. 25. The correctly stated proposition includes \(u+w+h\).

A common nondegenerate test system is \(M=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), \(f(x)=3x\bmod1\), with geodesic distance, \(\psi=1\) and \(u=0\). It has ASP with \(C=L=1,\lambda=\log3,\delta=1/8\). Indeed, signed differences of successive \(\delta\)-close iterates satisfy \(q_{i+1}=3q_i\), because the possible integer discrepancy has modulus less than one. The endpoint exponential bound follows. A periodic pseudo-orbit is corrected by the geometric inverse-branch series with error at most half its pseudo-orbit error.

For completeness, MCGBP with \(K=1\) also has a direct verification. The operator \[\mathcal Lv(x)=\min_{fy=x}\{u(y)+v(y)\}\] satisfies \([\mathcal Lv]_\alpha\le3^{-\alpha}([u]_\alpha+[v]_\alpha)\). After normalization at a base point, it preserves the compact convex Hölder ball with seminorm bound \([u]_\alpha/(3^\alpha-1)\). A fixed point modulo constants gives \(\mathcal Lv=v+c\). Integration gives \(\beta(u)\ge c\), and empirical limits of calibrated backward orbits give \(\beta(u)\le c\). Thus the examples below satisfy the full system assumptions.

## The perturbation \(w\) cannot be dropped

Take \(O=\{0\}\), \(w(x)=\kappa(1-\cos2\pi x)\), with \(\kappa>0\) small enough for the prescribed \(C^{s,\alpha}\) bound, and \(h(x)=-\tau(1-\cos2\pi x)\), where \[0<\tau<\min\{\kappa/2,\varrho/[4(1+\pi)]\}.\] All required perturbation bounds hold. The function \(u+w+h\) is nonnegative and vanishes on \(O\), as required by the proposition. But the other fixed point \(1/2\) has \((u+h)(1/2)=-2\tau<0=(u+h)(0)\). Hence the final p. 25 sentence asserting minimization of \(u+h\) is false. Restore \(w\); the proposition on p. 21 already gives the correct conclusion.

## The escape threshold uses \(\operatorname{Lip}_f\), not \(C\)

The lower bound in (4.3), p. 23, uses \(\theta(D/(2\operatorname{Lip}_f))\), correctly. It is changed on p. 24 to \(\theta(D/(2C))\). In the preceding system, keep \(O=\{0\}\), \(w\) as above and \(h=0\). Choose \(\rho=1/100\) and \(z=1/300\). Then \(D=1/8\), \(\operatorname{Lip}_f=3\), and the first escape time is \(m_2=2\): the distances are \(1/300,1/100,3/100\); the last return to \(\mathrm{Area}_1\) is \(m_3=1\). The printed block bound would imply \[w(1/300)+w(1/100)+w(3/100)\ \ge\ \theta(1/16).\] Its left side is at most \(182\pi^2\kappa/90000<\kappa/32\), whereas \(1-\cos x\ge2x^2/\pi^2\) on \([0,\pi]\) gives \(\theta(1/16)\ge\kappa/32\). Thus the displayed bound is false. Restore \(2\operatorname{Lip}_f\) throughout; the initial choice of \(\varrho\) on p. 22 already uses that correct threshold.

## The sign and the periodic remainder

Below (4.7), the claimed pointwise bound must be \(G\ge-\|h\|_0-|a_O|\|\psi\|_0\), not the printed plus sign. In the same system take \(h(x)=c-rd(x,O)\), with \(c=r=\varrho/4\). It is Lipschitz, \(\|h\|_1=\varrho/2<\varrho\), and \(a_O=c\). For sufficiently small positive \(x\), \[G(x)=\kappa(1-\cos2\pi x)-rx<0,\] while the printed lower bound equals \(-c+c=0\). The corrected bound is precisely the one required for the periodic remainder.

If \(m_3=pN+q\), \(0\le q<N\), that remainder starts at \(pN=m_3-q\), not \(m_3-q-1\) as printed in (4.7). For a concrete period-two orbit use \(O=\{1/8,3/8\}\), with \(h(x)=a\cos(4\pi(x-1/8))\), \(a>0\) arbitrarily small, and a positive smooth \(w\) vanishing exactly on \(O\). Then \(G\) has successive orbit values \(a,-a\). For \(m_3=1\), the true sum is zero, whereas the printed range, interpreted by periodic continuation, gives \(-a\). This value of \(m_3\) is realized by \(z=1/8+3/1000,\rho=1/100,m_2=2\) in the same escape construction. The corrected remainder has at most \(N\) terms, so its stated lower bound with factor \(N\) is retained.

These defects are local formula errors, not counterexamples to Proposition 4.1. The correct conclusion is already in the proposition on p. 21, the correct escape threshold is in (4.3) and the original choice of \(\varrho\), and the needed negative remainder bound is already printed in (4.7). These internal checks delimit the effect of F3 independently of the length of its repair. With the correct threshold, sign and remainder, the exponential comparison bounds the total negative contribution by a constant times \(\varrho\). The prescribed positive escape cost dominates for sufficiently small \(\varrho\); concatenation gives the required nonnegative integral. Adding \(u\ge0\), zero on \(O\), preserves the weighted minimum. Robust minimization for every sufficiently small perturbation also forces uniqueness in the interior of the perturbation ball: otherwise a smooth test function separating two minimizing measures contradicts that robustness. This verifies the local-locking implication needed in Theorem 4.2.

# Positive coverage and external dependencies

The symbolic coding on p. 9 explicitly uses the closure of the coded set; the overline is visible in the source image. Its finite words are unchanged. With the stated Bressaud–Quas combinatorial bound, candidate block periods grow subexponentially and shadowing errors decay exponentially. Taking the running maximum of candidate periods justifies the passage from the constructed subsequence to all large period bounds.

The short-gap splitting in Proposition 3.1 halves the period at each step. Applying ASP to the available indices \(0,\ldots,n_1-1\) and summing the two geometric tails gives a period-independent error multiplier. At most \(\log_2 n\) splittings occur; the initial approximation exponent can be chosen larger than the logarithm of that multiplier. If the closure of \(Z\) already contains a periodic orbit, its deviation is zero and the multiplicative inequality \(D^\alpha>\widehat Ld_{\alpha,Z}\) handles the case without division by zero. Otherwise compactness forces approximation periods to tend to infinity.

Strict positivity of \(\psi\) is preserved in every ratio and calibration. The normalized potential is nonnegative and zero on the closed union of minimizing supports; integral identities use invariance under \(T\). For Appendix A, the useful identity is also \[\beta(u_K;f^K)=\beta(u;f).\] Given an \(f^K\)-invariant measure \(\nu\), averaging its first \(K\) pushforwards gives an \(f\)-invariant measure \(\mu\) with \(\int u_K\,d\nu=\int u\,d\mu\). Conversely an \(f\)-invariant minimizing measure is \(f^K\)-invariant. This independently checks the change of iterate needed before applying the cited calibrated-subaction result.

The full external Bressaud–Quas combinatorial theorem, Greene–Wu global smoothing theorem, and Bousch’s theorem with the general Anosov adapted-iterate construction were not independently reproved. The explicit tripling-map verification above is not presented as a proof of that general Anosov theorem. The higher-regularity result is relative density in the periodic-minimizer class, not a claim of density in all \(C^{s,\alpha}\) observables.
