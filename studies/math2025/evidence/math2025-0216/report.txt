Abstract: The 47-page second arXiv version of Hogancamp, Rose, and Wedrich is examined. The comparison in Theorem C with the full \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) skein lasagna module is incompatible with the zero-framed unknot: its degree-zero dimension is infinite, whereas the total Kirby color gives dimension two. The fixed-class statement in Theorem 6.8 has a narrower scope. Other corrections concern finality and compact objects in general background statements, a reversed triangular inequality, a multiplicity and a grading shift in completed tensor products, and the displayed cabling limit. Several central finite algebra calculations and the filtered-colimit logic receive positive checks. The findings do not refute the Kirby object construction or the elementary handle-slide isomorphism. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

Title: Integer homology classes, completion, and grading conventions in a Kirby color for Khovanov homology

# Identity, scope, and proof structure

The reviewed work is M. Hogancamp, D. E. V. Rose, and P. Wedrich, A Kirby color for Khovanov homology, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1589. The actual primary source is arXiv:2210.05640v2, dated 25 October 2022, with 47 pages [paper]. The journal records submission on 6 February 2023, acceptance on 14 July 2024, and publication on 17 February 2025 [journal]. This review does not assert that the preprint and subscription journal PDF are equivalent. All 2,799 extracted-text lines and all 47 source pages were read and viewed. The retained author TeX was consulted for specific formulas, without compiling or executing author code. No article-specific computational supplement or executable code was located in the bounded source search; their absence is not a defect.

The paper first establishes graded category and completion conventions in Section 2, then the dotted Temperley–Lieb presentation, symmetrizers, quiver relations, and the faithful polynomial functor in Section 3. Section 4 constructs the Kirby ind-objects and handle-slide isomorphisms. Section 5 develops graphical rules, Hom representability, locally finite sums, and tensor-product decompositions. Section 6 extends colored Khovanov homology and compares it with skein lasagna modules. Section 7 describes further directions. This order matters: finite-dimensional polynomial checks do not by themselves establish coherence of all four-dimensional cobordism maps.

Throughout the calculations below, \(\mathbb K\) has characteristic zero, as assumed in the manuscript. Page numbers refer to the primary PDF. For the substantive comparison issue, the author-hosted 35-page Manolescu–Neithalath source [MN] was additionally obtained, and its first two pages were read and actually viewed. Only that bounded external dependency was checked; no complete review of that other paper is claimed.

# A substantive scope obstruction

Problem 1 (Major: Theorem C compares with too large a module).  Theorem C on p. 3 identifies the total-color invariant with the \(N=2\) skein lasagna module of the attached two-handlebody, without fixing an integer relative homology class. The preceding discussion specifies the \(\mathfrak{gl}_N\) theory. This is stronger than Theorem 6.8 on p. 42, whose index lies in \(\{0,1\}^r\) and denotes a particular class in \(H_2(B^4(L_2),L_1;\mathbb Z)\).

Take \(L_1=\varnothing\) and let \(L_2=U\) be the zero-framed unknot. Then \(B^4(U)=S^2\times D^2\). Proposition 4.6 (p. 23) and Example 6.7 (p. 41) give \[\operatorname{Kh}(U^{\omega_i})\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq0}q^{-2n}\mathbb K,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Kh}(U^\omega)\cong
 \bigoplus_{n\geq0}q^{-2n}(\mathbb K\oplus\mathbb K),
 \quad \omega=\omega_0\oplus\omega_1.\] In particular its homological and quantum degree \((0,0)\) has dimension \(2\).

In contrast, [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 1.2, p.~2]{MN}] states \[\mathcal S^2_{0,0,*}(S^2\times D^2;\varnothing)
 \cong \mathbb Z[A_1,A_0,A_0^{-1}],
 \qquad \deg_q A_1=-2,\quad \deg_q A_0=0.\] After extension to \(\mathbb K\), the vectors \(A_0^m\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\), are linearly independent in degree \((0,0)\). Thus that degree is countably infinite-dimensional. This contradicts the bigraded isomorphism in the literal full-module reading of Theorem C. Merely forgetting the integer homology grading does not identify different vectors or reduce this dimension.

The evidence against overextending this counterexample is also explicit in the manuscript. Example 6.7 first compares each \(\omega_i\) to a fixed-class calculation. Theorem 6.8 restricts the integer class to the chosen parity representatives. Remark 6.9 (p. 43) acknowledges that other integer classes can have grading shifts and that \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) foams would remove the discrepancy. Remark 1.4 (p. 4) discusses a different \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) version with a \(\mathbb Z/2\) grading; it does not define the full \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) module to be a mod-two quotient. None of these passages identifies all the Laurent monomials in the displayed external ring.

The necessary repair is to state exactly which module is being compared. One may retain the fixed-class conclusion of Theorem 6.8, with the normalization correction in Problem 6, and restrict the global conclusion accordingly. A genuinely mod-two or \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) version requires a precise definition and a comparison proof. Alternatively, a construction retaining all integer classes could target the full \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) module. Simply changing its name is insufficient. Nor does the fixed-class comparison alone show that selecting the particular integer lifts \(0,1\) is canonical under all handle changes. The local handle-slide construction may have its own invariance argument; the invalid full-module identification cannot supply that missing implication. This is a central theorem-scope issue, not a counterexample to existence of \(\omega_i\).

# Background hypotheses and finite algebra

Problem 2 (Minor: the finality criterion omits a connectivity hypothesis).  Definition 2.18 (p. 8) omits nonemptiness from filteredness. Lemma 2.20 (p. 9) then states a finality criterion without assuming that its source indexing category \(I\) is filtered. Its two printed conditions are insufficient.

Let \(I\) consist of two isolated objects, \(J\) of one object, and \(F:I\to J\) be the unique functor. The first condition holds, and all relevant pairs of parallel maps in \(J\) are equal, so the second holds too. For the constant diagram \(\alpha(*)=\mathbb K\) in degree zero, the canonical comparison is \[\operatorname*{colim}_{I}\alpha F=\mathbb K\oplus\mathbb K
 \longrightarrow \operatorname*{colim}_{J}\alpha=\mathbb K,
 \qquad (a,b)\longmapsto a+b.\] It has the nonzero kernel vector \((1,-1)\) and is not an isomorphism. Thus \(F\) is not final.

Add nonemptiness to Definition 2.18, and assume \(I\) filtered in Lemma 2.20, or use the standard nonempty-connected comma-category criterion directly. With the filtered assumption, two arrows \(j\to F(i_1),j\to F(i_2)\) can first be moved to a common successor in \(I\); the second printed condition then equalizes them. This proves the needed connectedness. The tail inclusions used in Lemma 4.3 and the diagonal/cofinal sequences in Sections 4–5 already have nonempty filtered sources, so the counterexample does not invalidate those applications.

Problem 3 (Minor: compact objects require idempotent completion).  Remark 2.24 (p. 10) identifies an arbitrary graded linear category \(C\) with the compact objects of \(\operatorname{Ind}(C)\). This identification needs an idempotent-completeness hypothesis, or replacement of \(C\) by its Karoubi envelope.

Let \(C\) have a single object \(X\) with \(\operatorname{End}(X)=M_2(\mathbb K)\), concentrated in degree zero, and put \(e=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\). The ind-object \[E=\operatorname*{colim}(X\xrightarrow e X\xrightarrow e X
 \xrightarrow e\cdots)\] splits \(e\) and is a retract of the compact object \(X\), hence is compact. But \(\operatorname{End}(E)=eM_2(\mathbb K)e\cong\mathbb K\), so \(E\) cannot be isomorphic to the only original object \(X\). This is a failure of the asserted identification, not of compact generation. Convention 4.1 (p. 22) already takes the finite graded additive and Karoubi completions before forming the main ind-category. The intended main setting therefore has an available repair.

Problem 4 (Minor: triangular direction and two algebraic notations).  Proposition 3.26, especially (19) on p. 20, reverses the zero condition in its triangular pairing. Lemma 3.20 (p. 17) has an ill-typed index in one composition, and the proof of Corollary 3.23 (p. 19) prints a tensor product where a product is required.

For the first error use four boundary points and undotted caps \[d_{\mathrm{par}}=(1,2)(3,4),\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{nest}}=(1,4)(2,3).\] The manuscript’s monomials are \(\delta_{\mathrm{par}}^*=x_1x_3\) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{nest}}^*=x_1x_2\). In the prescribed lexicographic order, the nested monomial is larger, but \[d_{\mathrm{nest}}(x_1x_3)=1.\] Thus the claim of vanishing for \(i>j\) is false. Ordering parallel before nested, the pairing matrix is \[\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.\] The correct condition is vanishing for \(i<j\). Indeed, a nonzero evaluation chooses exactly one endpoint of every undotted cap and no endpoint of a dotted cap. The ordered left endpoints are componentwise no larger than the chosen endpoints; their monomial is therefore lexicographically no smaller. Equality and the greedy reconstruction give the diagonal entries. This repairs the triangular proof and preserves faithfulness.

The two composition relations should be written separately: \[U_{n-2}D_n=-z_n^2\quad\hbox{in }\operatorname{End}(P_n),\qquad
 D_nU_{n-2}=-z_{n-2}^2\quad\hbox{in }\operatorname{End}(P_{n-2}).\] The proof already supplies the second correct subscript. For the center, the truncation is \(z_n^{n+1}\) rather than \(z_n^n\), and the even and odd inverse systems give \(\mathbb K[z]\times\mathbb K[z]\), not \(\mathbb K[z]\otimes\mathbb K[z]\). The correct map is \[(f,g)\longmapsto \frac{1+s}{2}f+\frac{1-s}{2}g
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb K[s,z]/(s^2-1).\] The printed tensor rule is not bilinear: \(f\otimes0=0\), but its proposed image is generally nonzero. The graded-limit convention in Remark 3.24 is sound; it gives polynomials, not an unrestricted power-series ring. These corrections leave the final center description intact.

There is positive evidence for the core finite algebra. Set \(y_a=(-1)^{a-1}x_a\) and \(z_n=\sum_a y_a\). The symmetrizer acts by ordinary permutations of the \(y_a\), and characteristic zero gives \[z_n^j=j!\,e_j(y_1,\ldots,y_n),\qquad
 \operatorname{Pol}(P_n)=q^{-n}\langle1,z_n,\ldots,z_n^n\rangle.\] With the specified dotted cup and cap normalization, \[U_m(z_m^j)=-\frac{z_{m+2}^{j+2}}{(m+1)(m+2)},\qquad
 D_{m+2}(z_{m+2}^j)=(m+1)(m+2)z_m^j,\] where powers beyond the truncation are zero. The two compositions are exactly the correctly indexed \(-z^2\). For \(n-m=2r\geq0\), \[(U_{n-2}\cdots U_m z_m^j)(1)
 =(-1)^r\frac{m!}{n!}z_n^{j+n-m},
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq m.\] These nonzero, distinct powers prove independence of the quiver spanning family. They support the stated Hom formula; they do not independently prove the external topological presentation of the annular category.

# Completion, cabling, and a boundary case

Problem 5 (Minor: multiplicity and grading in completed tensor products).  Section 5.4 (p. 35) twice writes \(\omega\otimes\omega\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq0}q^{-2n}\omega\). Proposition 5.27 has a separate extra shift in its proof on p. 38.

The degree-zero dimension of \(\operatorname{Pol}(\omega)\) is \(2\). Hence the degree-zero dimensions in the first displayed isomorphism are \(4\) and \(2\). Summing the four correct parity-component formulas in Corollary 5.25 gives instead \[\omega\otimes\omega
 \cong\bigoplus_{n\geq0}q^{-2n}(\omega\oplus\omega).\] This correction is consistent with the component formula.

In the proof of Proposition 5.27, the definition \(Y_n=q^{-i-j-2n}\omega'_{i+j}\) disagrees with the proposition’s \(q^{-2n}\omega'_{i+j}\). The merge followed by \(n\) pink dots has degree \(2n\), so into the printed shifted target its degree is \(-i-j\), whereas Definition 2.6 requires a degree-zero projection. For \((i,j)=(0,1)\) the left side has a one-dimensional degree-zero polynomial image, but the erroneously shifted right side has none. Remove the additional \(-i-j\) in \(Y_n\) and in the ensuing degree calculations. This is a repair of the proof’s normalization, not a refutation of the correctly stated proposition.

The convergence restriction on sums remains essential: Definition 5.21 permits a family only if its restriction along each canonical finite-stage inclusion has finite support. It does not allow arbitrary formal sums. Together with the finite-stage factorization in Lemma 5.20, this explains why composition of the admissible families is defined. The use of graded Hom also prevents replacing \(\operatorname{End}(\omega_i)=\mathbb K[z]\) by \(\mathbb K[[z]]\).

Problem 6 (Minor: the cabling colimit omits its grading shifts).  Equation (41), p. 42, omits the quantum shifts present in the construction, in Example 6.7, and in (42). It also prints \(i_r+n_r\) in the final cable exponent, where the indexing requires \(i_r+2n_r\).

The consistent expression is \[\operatorname*{colim}_{\underline n\in\mathbb N^r}
 q^{-|\underline i|-2|\underline n|}
 \operatorname{Kh}\!\left(L_1\cup
 K_1^{(i_1+2n_1)}\cup\cdots\cup
 K_r^{(i_r+2n_r)}\right).\] Without the shifts, the dotted-annulus transition has nonzero degree and is not the degree-zero diagram in the graded category required by Section 2. The correction is already supported by the neighboring expression (42); it addresses the fixed-class formula, not the larger scope obstruction in Problem 1.

Problem 7 (Minor: a future-direction vanishing claim includes identities).  Section 7.7 (p. 45) claims that all maps between its two-term complexes \(C_l\) and \(C_{l'}\) are null-homotopic when \(l\leq l'\), with \(P_l=0\) for negative indices. The equality case \(l=l'=0\) disproves this.

Here \(C_0\) is just \(P_0\) in degree zero. Its identity is nonzero because \(\operatorname{End}(P_0)=\mathbb K\). Both adjacent terms vanish, so every putative chain homotopy has \(dh+hd=0\) and cannot equal the identity. The statement must exclude equality or specify a different class of maps; this review does not claim that merely changing \(\leq\) to \(<\) proves every remaining case. The displayed arrow label also needs the source-compatible \(U_{l-2}\) under the earlier convention \(U_m:P_m\to P_{m+2}\). This future-direction error is not used in the main Kirby color construction.

# Checks that remain valid and limits of the assessment

The compact-test-object argument in Lemma 2.26 was checked: degreewise finiteness permits the sums, and the stipulated identity decomposition on compact generators identifies the biproduct. The elementary handle-slide argument uses an ind-object identity correctly. Composing the two finite slides gives a transition map, which becomes an isomorphism under the cofinal-tail identification; it need not be the identity on an unchanged finite stage.

The Hom representability argument extends from finite objects using duality and the polynomial tensor description, then converts filtered colimits into inverse limits. The graded dual convention is necessary here. Theorem 4.15 supplies a higher-categorical center construction as a proof sketch. This review has not independently rebuilt every two-dimensional cobordism relation or all higher coherence data. The introduction itself distinguishes handle-slide naturality from a direct Kirby-movie-coherent four-manifold functor. That acknowledged distinction is preserved rather than treated as an additional proved defect.

The reviewer’s retained exact-arithmetic script checked 15 cup columns, 25 cap columns, 40 composition columns, the four-endpoint triangular counterexample, and the finite Hilbert coefficients of the tensor-product identities. It terminated with exit code zero. The infinite-dimensional comparison uses the explicit Laurent basis and the cited ring theorem, not extrapolation from those finite computations. No author code was run. The external annular-presentation, colored-homology, and skein-lasagna theorems were not independently reproved in full.

Problem 1 warrants a major scientific revision of the comparison’s stated scope. Problems 2–7 admit narrower corrections or require a precise qualification. None establishes that all intended corrected results are false. Conversely, positive checks in selected dependencies do not certify the entire paper. These are initial-review findings; the formal scientific decision remains `pending`, and the human-review list is empty.
