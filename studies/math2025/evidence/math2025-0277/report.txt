Assessment: Major in the inspected core proof, with local Minor corrections. The averaging estimate used to derive the local limit theorem needs a substantive correction to its admissible domain and moment exponent. The literal printed domain and its natural intended correction are distinguished below: no spread-out counterexample is falsely claimed to satisfy a singleton domain. Several auxiliary statements are also false as written, with exact admissible tests given here. No counterexample to the qualitative local limit conclusion itself is established. The full text was read, but this is not a certification of every external input. Scientific decision remains pending; human reviews are empty.

# F1: The core averaging estimate and its use (Major)

## Literal domain and actual failed application

Equation (10), p. 5, prints \[\nu\big((a,b)+[M\sqrt n,M\sqrt n]^{2d}\big)=1.\] Both interval endpoints are positive and identical in the actual page image. The displayed set is a singleton. Thus every member of the printed \(\mathcal E_n(\alpha,M)\) is a point mass, if the singleton lies in the lattice; otherwise that choice gives no member. This cannot supply the spread-out endpoint estimate described immediately below (13).

The actual use is on p. 20, in the proof of Corollary 1.6: the law \(\nu_{\omega,r}\) is the distribution of \((X_r,X_{n-r})\) under the bridge weighted only in the initial and terminal strips. For \(x=y=0\), \(r\geq1\), and sufficiently large even \(n\geq r^{4(1+\varepsilon)}\), at least two such pairs are reachable. All finite real environments give every admissible path strictly positive weight. Consequently \(\nu_{\omega,r}\) has at least two atoms and is not a member of the literal \(\mathcal E_{n-2r}(\alpha,M)\) for any centers and any \(M\). This is a direct failure of the hypothesis required in the displayed application of Theorem 1.1(ii). Small positive Gaussian disorder as specified below satisfies all disorder and moment assumptions of Corollary 1.6. The counterexample here is to that claimed applicability, not to (13) on its printed singleton domain.

There is a second precise mismatch on pp. 18–19: \(p'\) is chosen close to the full \(p^*\) when invoking Lemma 3.2, although that lemma, p. 16, only allows \(p'<p^*\wedge2\). Likewise (11) is invoked for orders approaching \(p^*>2\), beyond its stated range. This restriction cannot simply be erased.

## Independent test of the intended spread-out statement

Suppose only the evident missing minus sign in (10) is supplied. Then the full-\(p^*\) rate in (12)–(13) is false for the following explicit class of models.

Take \(d=3\) and independent standard Gaussian \(\omega_{t,x}\). Let \(\pi\in(0,1)\) be the eventual return probability, after time zero, of the difference of two independent three-dimensional simple random walks, and set \(\beta^2=-\log(\pi)/90>0\). Transience gives \(\pi<1\); the number \(N\) of positive-time returns has the geometric tail \(\mathbb P(N\geq j)=\pi^j\). For ten replicas, \[\mathbb EW_n^{10}
 =E^{\otimes10}\exp\!\left(\beta^2
       \sum_{1\leq i<j\leq10}N_{ij}(n)\right)
 \leq \prod_{i<j}
       \left(E^{\otimes2}e^{45\beta^2N_{ij}(n)}\right)^{1/45}.\] The last expression is bounded uniformly in \(n\), since \(\pi e^{45\beta^2}=\sqrt\pi<1\). Thus \(P:=p^*(\beta)\geq10\). It is finite: a single straight path gives \[\mathbb EW_n^q\geq
 \exp\!\left\{n\left(\frac{q(q-1)\beta^2}{2}-q\log6\right)\right\},\] which diverges for \(q>1+2\log6/\beta^2\). All absolute exponential moments required in (2) exist. These facts also put the model in weak disorder, by the martingale’s bounded \(L^{10}\) norm and its usual zero–one law.

Choose \(p=9/5\). For every \(P\geq10\), this lies in the first branch of (12), and \[\xi(P,p)=\frac{P-1}{P+1}\left(1-\frac{5}{3P}\right)
 \geq\frac{15}{22}.\] Let \(n=2k^2\), let \(B_k\) be the even-parity lattice sites in \([0,k]^3\), and let \(\mu_k\) be uniform on \(B_k\), with \(N_k=|B_k|\asymp k^3\). Set \(\nu_k=\mu_k\otimes\mu_k\). For \(k\) large, every pair is reachable in time \(n\), the center separation is zero, and the support lies in the corrected box with \(M=1\). Both marginal maxima are \(N_k^{-1}\).

Condition the averaged pinned partition function on the first environment layer. Independence and normalization of every other layer give exactly \[\mathbb E[W_{0,n}^{\beta,\nu_k}-1\mid\omega_{1,\cdot}]
   =S_k:=\sum_z a_z\eta_z,\qquad
 \eta_z=e^{\beta\omega_{1,z}-\beta^2/2}-1,\] where \(a_z\geq0\), \(\sum_z a_z=1\), and at most \(Ck^3\) coefficients are nonzero. The centered independent variables \(\eta_z\) have variance \(\sigma^2>0\) and finite fourth moment. Expansion of the fourth moment yields \[\mathbb ES_k^2=\sigma^2\sum_z a_z^2,\qquad
 \mathbb ES_k^4\leq C\left(\sum_z a_z^2\right)^2.\] Paley–Zygmund applied to \(S_k^2\), followed by conditional Jensen, therefore gives \[\|W_{0,n}^{\beta,\nu_k}-1\|_p
 \geq \|S_k\|_p
 \geq c\left(\sum_z a_z^2\right)^{1/2}
 \geq c k^{-3/2}.\] At the admissible value \(\varepsilon=1/22\), (13) would instead give an upper bound \(C k^{-3(\xi-\varepsilon)}\leq Ck^{-21/11}\). Since \(21/11>3/2\), these estimates contradict each other for large \(k\). This proves that fixing the domain typo alone does not restore the intended quantitative estimate. It is explicitly not a counterexample satisfying the uncorrected singleton definition.

## Impact, counterevidence and bounded repair

The damage concerns a central quantitative estimate and its actual invocation in the local limit proof. That is the basis of the Major grade; neither global falsity nor irreparability is asserted. Counterevidence is the correctly restricted exponent range in Lemmas 2.2, 3.1, 3.2 and Theorem 1.1(i). The title’s beyond-\(L^2\) regime has \(p^*\leq2\); the counterexample tests the larger range that the theorem also states.

A bounded repair is to use the two-sided box and, for a requested \(p\), select \[p<q<p^*\wedge2.\] Repeating the optimization on pp. 18–19 with \(q\) in every auxiliary moment bound yields a positive norm exponent \[\xi_q=\min\left\{\frac{q-1}{q+1},\frac{q-p}{q}\right\}
          \left(1-\frac{1+2/d}{q}\right).\] This formula includes the meeting point of the two branches. Indeed the competing powers of the marginal size \(u\) in the \(p\)th moment are \(ap/2\), \(\frac p2(q-1)(1-(1+2/d)/q-a/2)\) and \((q-p)(1-(1+2/d)/q-a/2)\). Equating the increasing power with the smaller decreasing one and then taking the \(p\)th root gives the displayed formula. Using \(q<p^*\wedge2\) respects every cited moment range. This smaller positive exponent is enough for the qualitative averaging argument, conditional on the corrected bridge comparison discussed next. The original full-\(p^*\) rate for \(p^*>2\) is not recovered.

# F2: The bridge comparison needs its actual hypotheses (Minor)

Locations. Lemma 2.3, p. 12, defines \(f,g\) as real-valued measurable functions and uses \(|x-y-nm(\lambda)|\leq M\sqrt n\); its proof is on p. 13.

## A signed-function counterexample with positive denominator

Take \(d=3\), \(\lambda=0\), \(n=4\), \(x=x'=y=y'=0\), \(s=1\), \(t=3\), \(\varepsilon=1/4\), \(M=2\). Let \(f,g\) depend only on the first of the two walks, with \[f=\mathbf 1_{\{X_1=e_1\}}-\frac16+\delta,\qquad
 g=\mathbf 1_{\{X_3=e_1\}},\qquad \delta>0.\] All stated hypotheses hold, including measurability and the time gap; the denominator is \(\delta/6>0\). There are 90 four-step return paths, 15 with \(X_1=e_1\), 15 with \(X_3=e_1\), and 6 with both. Consequently the ratio in Lemma 2.3(i) equals \[\frac{1/15-1/36+\delta/6}{\delta/6}
       =1+\frac{7}{30\delta},\] which is unbounded as \(\delta\downarrow0\). The second walk simply integrates out. An independently authored exact enumeration of all \(6^4=1296\) paths verifies these counts.

## The printed drift sign fails even for positive functions

Take \(\lambda=(\log2,0,0)\), so \(m(\lambda)=(3/13,0,0)\). For an integer \(\ell\geq1\), let \(n=26\ell\), \(x=x'=0\) and \(y=y'=-6\ell e_1\). These endpoints are reachable and satisfy the printed drift condition with equality zero. Put \(s=n/2\), \(t=n\), \(g=1\) and \(f=4^{-X_s^{(1)}}\) on the first walk. Every function is positive and finite. Under \(P^\lambda\) the one-step probabilities in the first coordinate are \(4/13,1/13\), with the other four directions having probability \(2/13\) each. Hence \(E^\lambda4^{-X_s^{(1)}}=1\). Conditioned on \(X_n=y\), increments are exchangeable, so \(E[X_s^{(1)}\mid X_n=y]=-3\ell\). Jensen’s inequality gives a bridge numerator at least \(4^{3\ell}=64^\ell\). No uniform constant in the stated lemma can bound this ratio.

The repair is to require nonnegative functions, with positive finite denominators, and the drift condition \(|y-x-nm(\lambda)|\leq M\sqrt n\) (and likewise for the second walk). With that condition the bridge-kernel ratio in the source proof has a denominator of order \(n^{-d/2}\) and a numerator at most \(C(t-s)^{-d/2}\). The resulting positive-kernel comparison proves the corrected part (i). For part (ii), actual possible positions satisfy \(|z_1-x|\leq s\) and \(|z_2-y|\leq n-t\). Using these bounds, rather than the smaller powers printed on p. 13, gives the required errors \(O(n^{-1/4-3\varepsilon})+O(n^{-2\varepsilon})\) in the Gaussian ratio, conditional on the correctly normalized moderate-deviation local limit input.

The actual Boltzmann functions on pp. 18 and 20 are nonnegative, and p. 16 already chooses \(m(\lambda)=(b-a)/n\), with the corrected direction. This is decisive counterevidence to treating the two examples as a disproof of the intended core polymer comparison. The local lemma’s excessive hypotheses are false; its corrected version supplies the needed use. The grade is Minor on that actual impact.

# F3: The strong-disorder liminf assertion is false (Minor)

Location. Theorem B(ii), p. 3, claims that under strong disorder the quantity \[\liminf_n\max_x\mu^\beta_{\omega,n}(X_{n+1}=x)\] is almost surely bounded below by a strictly positive constant.

Here is a self-contained admissible counterexample. Take \(d=1\), \(\mathbb P(\omega=1)=1/4\), \(\mathbb P(\omega=0)=3/4\) and \(\beta=\log64\). The environment is nonconstant and satisfies every absolute exponential moment condition. For \(\theta=1/2\), subadditivity over the \(2^n\) nearest-neighbor paths gives \[\mathbb EW_n^{1/2}
 \leq\left[
   \frac{\sqrt2\,\mathbb Ee^{\beta\omega/2}}{(\mathbb Ee^{\beta\omega})^{1/2}}
 \right]^n
 =\left(\frac{11}{\sqrt{134}}\right)^n.\] Markov’s inequality and Borel–Cantelli imply \(W_n\to0\) almost surely, proving strong disorder without an external phase-transition theorem.

Fix \(\epsilon>0\). Choose an integer \(L\) with \(\|P_L\|_\infty<\epsilon/2\) and let \(\delta=(\epsilon/2)e^{-\beta L}\). At an arbitrary block-start time \(m\), let \(\rho\) be the polymer endpoint distribution. There are at most \(1/\delta\) sites with \(\rho(x)>\delta\). Require every future environment value in the \(L\)-step reachable tubes from these heavy sites to equal zero. The number of sites in this event is bounded by a constant depending only on \(L,\delta\), not on \(m\) or \(\rho\). Conditioned on the past its probability is therefore at least a fixed \(q_\epsilon>0\).

On this event, work with the unnormalized weights \(e^{\beta H}\); the common \(\lambda(\beta)\) normalization cancels. The total future normalization is at least one because all weights are at least one. Heavy starting sites contribute at most \(\|P_L\|_\infty\) to the mass at any final site. The remaining sites contribute at most \(e^{\beta L}\|\rho_{\rm light}*P_L\|_\infty\leq e^{\beta L}\delta\). Thus the maximum endpoint probability at time \(m+L\) is at most \(\epsilon\). Its subsequent unweighted step also has maximum at most \(\epsilon\).

For disjoint blocks, iterated conditional probabilities bound the chance of avoiding this event for \(N\) consecutive blocks by \((1-q_\epsilon)^N\). It therefore occurs infinitely often almost surely. Taking the intersection over \(\epsilon=1/j\) proves \[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_x\mu^\beta_{\omega,n}(X_{n+1}=x)=0
 \quad\hbox{almost surely}.\] This contradicts precisely the printed assertion.

The core weak-disorder proof uses Theorem B(i), not this strong-disorder liminf. The actual damage is to the stated background localization result, and the grade is Minor. Replacing liminf by the appropriate limsup localization statement requires the correct cited theorem and hypotheses; the argument above proves falsity of liminf, not a new universal localization theorem.

# F4: Strict moment monotonicity omits the constant-environment case (Minor)

Locations. Assumption (2), p. 2; Theorem 1.2(ii), p. 6; its proof, pp. 19–20. The stated iid and exponential-moment assumptions allow \(\omega_{t,x}=0\) deterministically. Then \(\lambda(\beta)=0\), \(W_n^\beta=1\) and \(p^*(\beta)=+\infty\) for every \(\beta\geq0\). Taking \(\beta=1\) and \(\beta'=1/2\) satisfies the finite-support hypothesis and \(p^*(\beta)>1\), but the claimed strict inequality would be \(+\infty>+\infty\). The related assertion \(\beta_{\rm cr}=0\) in dimensions one and two also excludes this permitted case.

Nondegeneracy would exclude this counterexample. For completeness, finite \(p^*(\beta)\) for a nonconstant finite-support law and \(\beta>0\) can be checked directly. Let \(b\) be its largest value, of probability \(q_b\in(0,1)\). Then \(c=\beta b-\lambda(\beta)>0\). Choose a finite box whose killed-walk spectral radius \(\rho_B\) satisfies \(e^c\rho_B>1\). On the event that every environment variable in this box through time \(n\) equals \(b\), whose probability is \(q_b^{|B|n}\), the partition function is at least \(c_0(e^c\rho_B)^n\). For sufficiently large moment order its expectation therefore grows exponentially. This proves the finiteness needed in the hypercontractive argument.

There is also a repairable parameter-selection error on p. 20: \(\epsilon_0=(1+P)/2\) need not lie in \((0,P-1)\), and the stated monotonicity of \(p(P-\epsilon)/(P-\epsilon)\) has the opposite direction to that supplied by Theorem D. Given finite \(P=p^*(\beta)>1\), choose \(1<q_0<P\) and write \(r_0=p(q_0)/q_0>1\). For \(q<P\) sufficiently close to \(P\), with \(q>q_0\) and \(q>P/r_0\), Theorem D yields \(p(q)\geq q r_0>P\). Together with (49), this verifies the required strict moment improvement. The external hypercontractivity theorem itself is retained as an input. The verified repair concerns this boundary and parameter choice; no failure for nonconstant finite-support disorder has been established.

# F5: The introductory exact density omits the terminal weight (Minor)

Locations. The exact equality at the foot of p. 1, the pinned definition on p. 4, and Remark 1.7 on p. 8. The pinned \(W_{0,n}^{\beta,0,x}\) omits time \(n\), whereas \(\mu^\beta_{\omega,n}\) includes it. The correct equality is \[\mu^\beta_{\omega,n}(X_n=x)
 =\frac{e^{\beta\omega_{n,x}-\lambda(\beta)}
             W_{0,n}^{\beta,0,x}}{W_n^\beta}\,P(X_n=x).\] For an admissible small positive Gaussian \(\beta\) from F1, already \(n=1\), \(x=e_1\) gives \(W_{0,1}^{\beta,0,e_1}=1\). The left side is \(e^{\beta\omega_{1,e_1}-\lambda(\beta)}/(6W_1^\beta)\), rather than \(1/(6W_1^\beta)\), almost surely. This is an exact counterexample in weak disorder. The counterevidence is Remark 1.7, which correctly uses \(\mu_{\omega,n-1}\) and \(W_{n-1}\). Adding the terminal factor, or consistently using that shifted convention, repairs the identity. The formal pinned local limit statement is not refuted by this introductory normalization error.

# F6: Editorial clarification of the upper-bounded condition

Location. The display labelled (u-bd.), p. 3, says \(\exists K>0:\mathbb P(\omega_{0,0}\leq K)=0\). An upper-bounded Bernoulli law on \(\{0,1\}\) fails this condition for every \(K>0\). Conversely \(\omega=2+|Z|\), with \(Z\) standard Gaussian, satisfies the printed condition with \(K=1\) while having no finite upper bound. Both laws satisfy (2). Thus the display is not equivalent to the property it names. It should read \(\mathbb P(\omega_{0,0}>K)=0\), or equivalently \(\mathbb P(\omega_{0,0}\leq K)=1\). The correct boundedness condition used in Theorem E on p. 24 and the later general-environment inputs are counterevidence to a resulting failure of the main theorem. The surrounding prose and that later formulation identify the intended assumption. No invalid derivation is attributed to this display alone. It is retained as an editorial clarification, not counted as a substantive Minor finding.

# F7: The lattice and Gaussian formulas in Appendix A3 (Minor)

Locations. The assertion \(X_{2i}/2\in\mathbb Z^d\) on pp. 13 and 25; equation (63), p. 25; the Gaussian integral on p. 26.

In \(d=3\), the two-step path \(e_1,e_2\) has positive probability and gives \(X_2/2=(e_1+e_2)/2\notin\mathbb Z^3\). Thus the claimed integer-lattice Fourier inversion cannot be applied to that variable as written. For \(\lambda=0\) its covariance is \(\Sigma=I/6\). The lower bound \(r^{-1}|t|\leq t^\mathsf T\Sigma t\) printed in (63) fails for every \(r>1\) at \(t=e_1/r\): its two sides are \(r^{-2}\) and \((6r^2)^{-1}\). Finally the Fourier integral is \[\frac1{(2\pi\sqrt n)^d}
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{-t^\mathsf T\Sigma t/2-ix\cdot t/\sqrt n}\,dt
 =\frac{e^{-x^\mathsf T\Sigma^{-1}x/(2n)}}
 {(2\pi n)^{d/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma}},\] with \(\Sigma^{-1}\) in the exponent, not \(\Sigma\). For \(x=\sqrt n\,e_1\), \(\Sigma=I/6\), the two exponential factors are \(e^{-3}\) and \(e^{-1/12}\), respectively.

A correct lattice is \(H=\{z\in\mathbb Z^d:\sum_jz_j\text{ is even}\}\) for the two-step walk. Use a lattice basis of \(H\), for example \(e_i-e_d\) for \(i<d\) and \(2e_d\), to identify it with \(\mathbb Z^d\); the two-step walk has a self-loop and is aperiodic on this lattice. Replace the lower side of (63) by \(r^{-1}|t|^2\) and use the inverse covariance in the transform. On the compact tilt cube the corrected covariance eigenvalues remain uniformly bounded above and away from zero. These corrections preserve the coarse upper and central lower orders \(n^{-d/2}\) of Theorem F. The counterevidence is that those orders do not depend on the erroneous linear norm or the interchange of covariance and inverse covariance. The local formulas are false, but no false conclusion of Theorem F is established; grade Minor. This report checks these repairs and the kernel normalization, not every remainder estimate in the cited random-walk monograph.

# Positive checks, dependencies and limits

The main summability threshold was independently recomputed: Young’s inequality and the heat-kernel bound give \[\sum_z(\mu*P_t^\lambda(z))^p
 \leq\min\{\|\mu\|_p^p,Ct^{-d(p-1)/2}\}.\] Splitting at \(T\) of order \((\max_x\mu(x))^{-2/d}\) gives \((\max_x\mu(x))^{p-1-2/d}\); the tail is summable precisely when \(p>1+2/d\). The exponent appearing as \(p-(1-2/d)\) in (27), p. 12, must be replaced by this \(p-(1+2/d)\) in that derivation. Both are positive in the working range, so the smallness/continuity argument still works with the derived exponent.

The separated-collision argument was traced on pp. 14–18: independence occurs across disjoint time slabs, the \(p/2<1\) power permits subadditivity, the small return mass gives geometric decay in the number of separated collisions, and choosing sufficiently large fixed \(K,L\) absorbs the polynomial cost of pinning. At least one macroscopic interval avoids collisions when these counts are controlled; one can take \(L\geq2\) in the covering step. The bridge comparison is used only with positive Boltzmann factors. This tracing supports the mechanism after the explicit local corrections above, rather than validating every printed equality.

For Corollary 1.6, the inner environment is independent of the random endpoint law determined by the outer strips. A positive averaging exponent, and weak-disorder endpoint delocalization from Theorem B(i), then give convergence of the normalized ratio. The unnormalized version uses a slightly higher moment below \(p^*\wedge2\) and Hölder; the two outer strips are independent. For \(n\geq r^{4(1+\varepsilon)}\), choose a smaller bridge parameter \(\varepsilon'<\varepsilon/(4(1+\varepsilon))\) to ensure \(r\leq n^{1/4-\varepsilon'}\). Reachability and parity must remain in each supremum. This argument does not include \(p^*=1+2/d\), which the source itself excludes.

For the overlap estimates on pp. 22–23, the finite reachable cone has only \(O(n^d)\) sites. The exponential far-endpoint bound is therefore summable. The near-endpoint bound reduces to \(\sum_n n^{-d(p-1)/2}<\infty\). One must use \(\sup_n(W_n^\beta)^{-p}<\infty\) in weak disorder rather than pull a varying \(W_n^{-p}\) outside a sum. Likewise finite-cone restrictions must be retained when replacing a zero endpoint weight by an exponential upper bound. These bounded corrections establish the claimed summability mechanism conditional on the corrected pinned moment bound. The rate comparison in (58) correctly uses \(p^*\wedge2\).
