Abstract: The assessment of the exact acquired author version is Major. A random-operator domination step cancels coefficients before applying distinct temporal translations; an explicit two-phase construction disproves that step. The transported-density formula also has an incorrect sign and normalization under the Gaussian law explicitly fixed in the paper. These defects affect the local probabilistic estimates and the central density bootstrap. They are not counterexamples to the main quasi-invariance theorem. The density identification admits a consistent scalar repair; a replacement proof of the random-operator estimate remains necessary. Local normalization, time-localization, moment, and flow-identity corrections are proved separately. All 64 physical source pages were read, and the numerical threshold was independently rederived. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Core proof defects

Problem 1 (Major: temporal translations lost in the random kernel).  In the proof of Proposition 3.6, p. 25, the first inequality after the dyadic definition of \(K_{\tilde\tau_1}^{N_1,N_2}\) is false. It replaces a sum of differently translated test functions by a norm of the scalar sum of their coefficients. The same reduction is invoked in Lemma 5.6, pp. 45–46.

## An all-hypotheses counterexample to the displayed step

Write \(\tau=\tilde\tau_1\) and \(\Phi=n_1^{2\alpha}-n_2^{2\alpha}+n_3^{2\alpha}-n_4^{2\alpha}\) with absolute values in the general definition. Choose \[\alpha=2,\quad s=\tfrac25,\quad \sigma=0,\quad q=2,
 \quad b=\tfrac38,\quad b'=\tfrac13,\quad r=48.\] These satisfy \(b_J=1/(2\alpha)=1/4<b'<b<1/2\) and \(r>1/(b-b')=24\). Choose the small increase in \(q'+\) consistent with this Hölder exponent. Both \(\langle 10\rangle\) and \(\langle 12\rangle\) lie in the same dyadic block. In this finite block set \[g_{10}=g_{12}=g_{-12}=1,\qquad g_{-10}=-1,\] and set its other coordinates to zero. Fix \((n_3,n_4)=(-1,1)\). The only nonzero summands satisfying \(n_1-n_2+n_3-n_4=0\) and the nonresonance exclusions are \[(n_1,n_2)=(12,10),\quad(-10,-12).\] Their phases are \(\Delta\) and \(-\Delta\), where \(\Delta=12^4-10^4=10736\), and their coefficients are \(c,-c\), where \(c=(\langle 10\rangle\langle 12\rangle)^{-s}>0\). The indicators involving \(\langle \Phi\rangle\) are identical. Consequently \[K_\tau^{N_1,N_2}(-1,1)=0,\qquad
 G^{N_1,N_2}(-1,1)=0\] for all \(\tau\), with exactly the definitions on p. 25.

Let \(\eta\ge0\) be a nonzero smooth function supported in \((-1,1)\), choose \(L\gg\Delta\) inside the permitted modulation region, and set \[f(-1,\tau)=\eta(\tau-L),\qquad
 g(1,\lambda)=\eta(\lambda-L-\Delta),\] with all other spatial components zero. The original dyadic pairing in that display equals \[c\int_\mathbb R\frac{\eta(\tau-L)^2}{\langle \tau\rangle^b}\,\mathrm d\tau>0.\] The contribution with phase \(-\Delta\) vanishes by disjoint support. The proposed first upper bound, \[\sum_{n_3,n_4}G^{N_1,N_2}(n_3,n_4)
       \|f(n_3,\cdot)\|_{L^q}
       \|g(n_4,\cdot)\|_{L^{q'+}},\] is zero. All test norms are finite; inverse temporal Fourier transforms are Schwartz functions. This is a failure of the specific domination inequality, not merely a complaint about an omitted estimate.

The exact Gaussian coordinate assignment has probability zero, but that does not limit the evidence to an exceptional input. For this fixed finite block the original pairing and the \(L^r_\tau\) scalar-kernel norm depend continuously on its finitely many Gaussian coordinates. The latter vanishes at the displayed point while the former is positive. For every fixed proposed implicit constant there is therefore an open neighborhood where the inequality fails. Such a neighborhood has positive Gaussian probability. Coordinates outside the block are irrelevant to this dyadic inequality.

## Propagation and repair boundary

The Hilbert–Schmidt estimates on pp. 25–26 estimate the collapsed kernel, whose norm need not dominate the actual operator. The same issue affects the truncation estimate (3.36) on p. 27. These bounds enter the enhanced-data norm (3.16), the contraction time (3.50), regularity persistence, and Lemma 4.1 on p. 38. They are essential inputs to the global construction.

For the energy version on pp. 45–46, take instead all four displayed Gaussian coordinates equal to \(1\). The factors \(\Psi_s\) are \(\langle 12\rangle^{2s}-\langle 10\rangle^{2s}\) and its negative. Thus the scalar energy kernel again cancels at \((-1,1)\), whereas the temporal translations remain different. The same tests isolate one term; a unit complex rotation of a test makes its \(\operatorname{Re}(i\,\cdot)\) pairing nonzero. The parameters \(b=2/5\), \(\alpha=2\), \(s=2/5\), \(\sigma=0\), \(q=2\) satisfy the energy condition \(4b\alpha+4\sigma>3\). Lemma 5.6 explicitly invokes the preceding reduction, so its proof inherits the defect.

A repair must estimate a random operator on joint spatial and temporal frequencies, retaining the translations \(\tau\mapsto\tau+\Phi\) until after the appropriate operator norm is taken. Summing absolute values discards the cancellation that supplies the intended random gain, and is not a verified substitute at the stated exponents. No complete replacement estimate, including its truncation convergence, was established in this review. Neither Proposition 3.6’s final moment statement nor Theorem 1.1 is asserted to be false. The Major assessment concerns a disproved essential proof step and its demonstrated dependencies.

Problem 2 (Major: the printed function is not the transported density).  Equations (5.1)–(5.2), p. 40, are incompatible with the explicit Gaussian covariance on p. 3 and with the equation’s sign. The opening half-energy identity on p. 42 is also incorrect. Lemma 5.10, pp. 53–54, uses the same function both as a Radon–Nikodym density and through the incorrect formula.

## Exact derivation

Equation reference: eq:true-density. For the truncated low modes let \[W_N(z)=\sum_{|n|\le N}\langle n\rangle^{2s}|z_n|^2,\qquad
 A(z)=\sum_{\rm NR}\langle n_4\rangle^{2s}
       z_{n_1}\overline z_{n_2}z_{n_3}\overline z_{n_4}.\] All four frequencies in this sum are truncated. The explicitly specified Gaussian law has density \(C_Ne^{-W_N}\). The four weighted sums in \(\sum_{\rm NR}\Psi_s z_1\bar z_2z_3\bar z_4\) are \(\bar A,A,\bar A,A\), respectively. Hence \[Q_{\rm print}=4\operatorname{Im}A,\qquad
 \frac{\,\mathrm dW_N}{\,\mathrm dt}
 =2\operatorname{Re}(iA)=-2\operatorname{Im}A
 =-\tfrac12 Q_{\rm print}.\] The linear and diagonal resonant contributions to this derivative are zero. Finite-dimensional Hamiltonian volume preservation, invariance of the high-mode Gaussian factor, and invariance of the full Wick mass give the actual density \[f_t^N(u)=
 \exp\!\bigl(W_N(P_Nu)-W_N(P_N\Phi_{-t}^Nu)\bigr)
 =
 \exp\!\left(-\frac12\int_0^t
 Q_{\rm print}(P_N\Phi_{-r}^Nu)\,\mathrm dr\right).
 \] The function printed in (5.2) is therefore \((f_t^N)^{-2}\). The reverse half-energy derivative on p. 42 is \(Q_{\rm print}(\Phi_{-t}^Nu)/4\).

## A finite-mode witness within the strict main range

Take \(\alpha=2\), \(s=1/2\), \(\gamma=4\), \(N=1\), and \[z_{-1}=z_0=1,\qquad z_1=i.\] Here \(\theta_*=1/2+\), \(c=4\), and \(2\gamma=8>4+(8/3)(1/2+)\) for small losses; the upper \(\gamma\) restriction is infinite. Thus even the strict hypotheses of Proposition 5.11 are satisfied. There are four nonresonant quartets. With \(w=\sqrt2\), direct exact computation gives \[A=2i(1-w),\quad Q_{\rm print}=8(1-w),\quad
 W_N'=4(w-1).\] The true value of \(\partial_t\log f_t^N|_{t=0}\) is \(4(w-1)\); the printed formula gives \(8(1-w)\), with opposite sign and twice the magnitude. The discrepancy is a nonzero finite-dimensional polynomial and persists on an open set. This set has positive Gaussian, and hence positive \(\rho\), measure after adjoining almost every admissible high-mode tail. The example is therefore not a null-set objection to a measure-theoretic identity.

## Impact and a verified algebraic repair

The change of variables in Lemma 5.10 requires the actual density. Substitution of (5.2) into that calculation breaks the identification on which the uniform-density bootstrap and Proposition 5.11 depend. This is a core proof error even though it admits a fixed scalar correction.

Retain the input law and set \(Q_{\rm density}=-Q_{\rm print}/2\) in all transport formulas, or insert \(-1/2\) in their exponents. Correct the formal Gaussian density and the p. 42 identity accordingly. The estimates in Section 5.2 control absolute values; the variational energy argument also permits either sign, after taking the absolute value of the purely Gaussian term. Scaling by \(-1/2\) changes constants and the parameter \(\lambda\), not \(\theta_*\), \(c\), the strict \(\gamma\) inequalities, or the time-growth exponent in (5.31). This verifies the scope of the normalization repair. It does not supply the missing random-operator argument in Problem Problem 1 (#pr:kernel). Changing the variance of every Gaussian coordinate is not an appropriate silent repair of the frozen input law.

# Local corrections with demonstrated boundaries

Problem 3 (Minor: exact variational normalization).  Lemma 2.4, equation (2.5), p. 14, assigns a drift cost \(\frac12\int_0^1\|\theta\|_{L^2}^2\,\mathrm dt\) to the complex Brownian covariance fixed in its footnote. The correct cost for its unchanged drift map is \(\int_0^1\|\theta\|_{L^2}^2\,\mathrm dt\).

For any \(N\ge1\), take the real functional \(F(v)=\lambda\operatorname{Re}\widehat v(0)\), \(\lambda\ne0\). It has every required polynomial and exponential moment. If \(X=\operatorname{Re}\widehat Y(1,0)\), then \(\operatorname{Var}X=1/2\), and \[-\log\mathbb Ee^{-\lambda X}=-\lambda^2/4.\] On the printed right side, completing the square in the zero-mode drift gives the exact infimum \[\inf_\theta\mathbb E\int_0^1
 \left(\lambda\operatorname{Re}\theta_0+
       \tfrac12\|\theta\|_2^2\right)\,\mathrm dt
 =-\lambda^2/2.\] The deterministic real drift \(\theta_0=-\lambda\), with other modes zero, is adapted and square integrable and attains it. With cost coefficient \(1\), the infimum is \(-\lambda^2/4\).

This exact-formula error has a smaller downstream scope than Problem Problem 2 (#pr:density). The simplified upper bound in Lemma 2.6 remains valid: the correctly derived bound has the stronger subtraction \(\|V\|_{H^s}^2\), and weakening that subtraction to \(\frac12\|V\|_{H^s}^2\) only increases the right side. Thus the later upper-bound applications do not lose their claimed parameter range from this coefficient alone.

Problem 4 (Minor: support statement and Wick-tail inference).  The abstract on p. 1 says that \(\mu_s\) is supported on \(H^s\). Equation (2.9), p. 16, prints a growing upper bound \(C_pN^{-1+4s}\) and uses it to infer a Cauchy property.

For the law actually defined on p. 3, \[\|u_0\|_{H^s}^2=\sum_n|g_n|^2=\infty
 \quad\hbox{almost surely},\] by the strong law, so \(\mu_s(H^s)=0\). The correct almost-sure regularity is \(H^\sigma\) for every \(\sigma<s-1/2\), with exclusion of the critical space, as already stated in the body of the paper. The abstract should use that support regularity; the main theorem’s quantifiers already do so.

For \(M\ge N\) the Wick-tail variance is exactly \[\mathbb E\left|\sum_{N<|n|\le M}
          \frac{|g_n|^2-1}{\langle n\rangle^{2s}}\right|^2
 =\sum_{N<|n|\le M}\langle n\rangle^{-4s}
 \lesssim N^{1-4s}.\] Second-chaos moment comparison therefore gives the valid \(L^p\) norm estimate \(C_pN^{1/2-2s}\), which tends to zero when \(s>1/4\). The printed \(N^{-1+4s}\) bound need not itself be false, since it is weaker and grows in this range; it simply does not imply the claimed Cauchy conclusion. The displayed calculation repairs that inference and the construction of the positive weight. Neither correction changes the main parameter range.

Problem 5 (Minor: incompatible time cutoff and a false endpoint).  Lemma 3.2, pp. 18–19, uses the global cutoff \(\chi_T=1_{[-T,T]}\) with a restriction norm on \([0,T]\) in (3.11), and includes the critical zero-trace endpoint \(b=1/q'\) in its additional claim about (3.9).

For (3.11), take spatial frequency zero and any nonzero smooth \(h\) supported in \((-3T/4,-T/2)\). Its restriction to \([0,T]\) is zero, but \(\chi_Th=h\). With \(q=2\), \(b=0\), and a small allowed \(\delta\), the right side is zero and the left side is positive. The assertion on p. 19 that multiplying any extension by \(\chi_T\) gives the same function is likewise false for this example. Replacing the cutoff by \(1_{[0,T]}\), or defining restriction spaces on the symmetric interval, removes the mismatch. The subsequent integrals over \([0,T]\) are compatible with the first correction.

For the endpoint, take \(q=p=2\), \(b=1/2\), \(b'=0\), and spatial frequency zero. For \(N\to\infty\) put \(T=N^{-1}\), \(M=e^{N^2}\), and \[f_N(t)=\frac1{\log N}\int_1^N\frac{\cos(\lambda t)}{\lambda}
          \,\mathrm d\lambda,\qquad u=f_N-f_M.\] These are smooth functions, \(u(0)=0\), and \[\|f_N\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb R)}\lesssim(\log N)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \|f_M\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}\lesssim(\log M)^{-1}.\] Moreover \(f_N(t)=1+O((\log N)^{-1})\) for \(0\le t\le T\): substitute \(\lambda t\) in the integral of \(1-\cos(\lambda t)\). Thus \[\|u\|_{L^2(0,T)}\ge cT^{1/2},\qquad
 \|u\|_{H^{1/2}(0,T)}\lesssim(\log N)^{-1/2}.\] The claimed endpoint version of (3.9) would force \(cT^{1/2}\le CT^{1/2}(\log N)^{-1/2}\), a contradiction. This verifies failure under the explicit zero-trace hypothesis, rather than merely observing that a generic critical trace is undefined. The upper zero-trace range must exclude \(b=1/q'\); the actual Duhamel estimates use the strict value \(b>1/q'\). No contradiction to those strict estimates is asserted.

Problem 6 (Minor: the moment power in Lemma 5.5).  Equation (5.6), p. 44, states \(\mathbb E\|\chi_TY^{(3),N}\|^p\lesssim_{p,q}T\) for every \(p\ge1\), uniformly in \(N\). This is false at \(p=1\). The proof supports \(T^{p/2}\) instead.

An actual high-frequency counterexample uses \[\alpha=2,\quad s=\tfrac25,\quad q=2,\quad
 \sigma_3=0,\quad p=1,\quad
 N=4M,\quad T=(1024M^4)^{-1}.\] All hypotheses of Lemma 5.5, including (5.5), hold. In spatial output frequency zero, choose \(n_1=a,n_3=b\in[M,2M]\), \(a<b\), and \(n_2=a+b\). These are nonresonant truncated triples. For large \(M\), \[\Psi_s=\langle a\rangle^{4/5}+\langle b\rangle^{4/5}
       -\langle a+b\rangle^{4/5}-1\ge cM^{4/5}.\] Strict concavity of \(x^{4/5}\), after scaling \((a,b)/M\) to \([1,2]^2\), supplies a uniform positive constant. The three Gaussian denominators have product at most \(CM^{6/5}\).

Apply the bounded linear functional \(L(F)=\int_0^1\widehat F(\tau,0)\,\mathrm d\tau\) to \(F=\chi_TY^{(3),N}\) in \(X_2^{0,-1/2-\varepsilon}\). Its operator norm is independent of \(M,T,N\). The relevant phase obeys \(|\Phi_2|\le288M^4\), so on \(0\le\tau\le1\), \(\widehat\chi_T(\tau-\Phi_2)\ge cT\). Each chosen monomial \(g_ag_b\overline g_{a+b}\) consequently has coefficient at least \(cTM^{-s}\) in \(L(F)\). There are at least \(cM^2\) distinct unordered monomials. They are orthogonal third-order Gaussian chaos terms; interchanging \(a,b\) reinforces the coefficient, and no different triple cancels that same monomial. It follows that \[\|L(F)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\ge cTM^{1-s}.\] Third-chaos hypercontractivity gives \(\|L(F)\|_4\le C\|L(F)\|_2\). Interpolation between \(L^1\), \(L^2\), and \(L^4\) then gives \(\mathbb E|L(F)|\ge c\|L(F)\|_2\). Therefore \[\frac{\mathbb E\|F\|_{X_2^{0,-1/2-\varepsilon}}}{T}
 \ge cM^{3/5}\longrightarrow\infty.\] This is an analytic lower bound; the saved finite numerical coefficient sums are only illustrations and are not used as an asymptotic proof.

The proof on pp. 44–45 yields \((\mathbb E\|F\|^p)^{2/p}\lesssim_pT\) for \(p\ge2\). Jensen’s inequality extends the resulting \(\mathbb E\|F\|^p\lesssim_pT^{p/2}\) to \(1\le p<2\). This correction supplies exactly the bounded moments of \(T^{-1/2}\|F\|\) used on p. 52, and does not change the energy threshold. The original weaker \(CT\) consequence remains valid for \(p\ge2\), \(T\le1\).

Problem 7 (Minor: equation conventions and local scope).  The gauge on p. 7 lacks a factor \(2\); the contraction functional on p. 30 lacks the Duhamel operator; and the last phase assertion on p. 12 is too broad in \(\alpha\).

For the gauge, the spatially constant solution \(u(t)=e^{it}\) solves the positive unrenormalized equation (1.16), with mass \(1\). The printed map \(e^{-itM(u)}u\) gives the constant \(1\), whose residual in the renormalized equation is \(-1\). The correct map is \(e^{-2itM(u)}u=e^{-it}\). These smooth \(L^2\) data satisfy the scope of the stated gauge equivalence. They are not offered as random data for the rough-data theorem.

The functional \(\Gamma\) at the start of Section 3.4 must be \[i\int_0^t S(t-t')
  \{\mathcal N(S(t')(u_0+v_0)+v(t'))+
      \mathcal R(S(t')(u_0+v_0)+v(t'))\}\,\mathrm dt'.\] With \(u_0=0\) and a constant \(v_0=a\ne0\), the correct nonlinear correction is \(v(t)=a(e^{-i|a|^2t}-1)\), with \(v'(0)=-i|a|^2a\); the printed functional gives \(-|a|^2a\). Finally, after (2.1) the text claims \(|\Phi_\alpha|\gtrsim1\) on nonresonant integer quadruples throughout \(\alpha>1/2\). For \(1/2<\alpha<1\) take \[(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)=(N+1,N,N-1,N).\] The frequency relation and exclusions hold, but \[\Phi_\alpha=(N+1)^{2\alpha}+(N-1)^{2\alpha}-2N^{2\alpha}

On p. 61 the upper-bound argument must start with \(+\log\int e^{r(u)}\,\mathrm d\rho\), not its negative. Likewise the good event in Corollary 6.2, p. 58, must use all \(t\ge0\) to be the complement of the bad event in (6.2). # Independent threshold and global-argument audit

## Elimination of the auxiliary parameter

The piecewise threshold in Theorem 1.1 was checked independently from (5.20)–(5.21) and (5.30), rather than accepted from Remark 5.12. Write \(a=\alpha\) and, for \(s<1/2\), \(U=(1-2s)^{-1}\). Ignoring only the explicitly arbitrarily small strict losses, put \[D(\gamma)=2\gamma-c-\frac{4a}{2a-1}\theta_*.\] In the branch \(a\le5/4+s/2\), \[\theta_*=\frac{4a+2s-3}{4a},\qquad
 c=4+\frac{(\gamma-1)(5+2s-4a)}{2s}.\] In the other branch, \(\theta_*=(1+2s)/(2a)\) and \(c=4\). Exact simplification gives \[D_A(U)=\frac{2F_A}{(1-2s)(2a-1)},\qquad
 D_B(U)=\frac{4F_B}{(1-2s)(2a-1)},\] where \[\begin{split}
 F_A&=2s^2+(10a-7)s+4a^2-11a+5,\\
 F_B&=2s^2+(4a-2)s-a.
 \end{split}\] These polynomials increase in \(s\) on the admissible range. Their relevant roots are exactly \[s_A=\frac{\sqrt{68a^2-52a+9}-10a+7}{4},\qquad
 s_B=\frac{\sqrt{4a^2-2a+1}-2a+1}{2}.\] At the branch boundary \(s=2a-5/2\), \(F_A=2F_B=32a^2-70a+35\), giving \(a_0=(35+\sqrt{105})/32\) as printed. Under \(a+s>3/2\), \(D_A'(\gamma)>0\); \(D_B'=2\). A positive limiting gap therefore permits a finite \(\gamma<U\) with room for the required strict losses. It also forces \(\gamma>2\).

The extra condition \(a+s>3/2\) follows automatically from the main range. For \(a\ge5/4\), use \(s>1/4\). For \(1<a<5/4\), \(F_A(3/2-a)=-(4a-1)(a-1)<0\), so \(s>s_A\) places \(s\) above that boundary. The endpoint \(s=1/2\) is handled separately: for \(1<a\le3/2\), take \(\gamma>a/(a-1)\); for \(a>3/2\), take \(\gamma>2+2/(2a-1)\), with a strict margin. No substitution of an infinite \(\gamma\) is needed.

The comparison with the stated deterministic regularity is also consistent. Substituting \(s=(5-2a)/6\) gives \[F_A=\frac{(8a-5)(a-1)}9,\qquad
 F_B=\frac{-20a^2+34a-5}{18}.\] The relevant crossover is \((17+3\sqrt{21})/20\). The local \(q\) interval is nonempty because \(4a/(3-2s)>2\). The actual choice of \(q\) in Lemma 5.8 fits the stricter local-theory interval, despite a larger interval being printed at the start of Section 4.

For the removal of localizations, put \(D=2\gamma-c-4a\theta_* /(2a-1)>0\). Both positive powers of the dyadic parameter in Proposition 5.11 are then strictly below \(2\gamma\), so the negative tail absorbs them after small losses. ## What the terminal argument does establish conditionally

Assuming a valid local convergence theorem and uniform bounds for the actual densities, the weak-limit argument on p. 59 is coherent. Since \(\rho\) is finite, a uniform \(L^2(\rho)\) bound supplies a weakly convergent subsequence for each fixed \(t\). Bounded continuous functions on \(H^{-1}\) are valid \(L^2(\rho)\) tests. Pathwise truncated convergence identifies the weak limit with the transported measure, and preserves positivity and total mass. A common weak subsequence for uncountably many times is not required for this identity.

For integer-step continuation, absolute continuity pulls the local good set back to a full-measure set. Countable iteration and local uniqueness permit patching. Complex conjugation preserves the measures and conjugates positive to negative time, giving the backward construction. For a fixed \(t\), the inverse identities from uniqueness and backward absolute continuity imply the reverse absolute continuity as well. The paper defines quasi-invariance using the forward relation; the stronger equivalence follows with this additional step.

The one-unit threshold in the first event inclusion on p. 59 should also be kept consistent: use \(\|\mathcal V_N\|\le C=e^M\), with \(N\ge N_0(u_0,C)\), to deduce \(\|\mathcal V\|\le C+1\). The printed occurrence of \(e^M+1\) for the truncated norm can be repaired by this choice or by enlarging \(C\) consistently. It is not a demonstrated failure of the limiting global solution.

A fully specified invariant measurable flow domain, and the passage in footnote 9 from the finite-dimensional variational bound to the infimum defining \(r\), were not completely reconstructed here. In particular, the assertion that \(r(P_Nu)\) has the requisite limiting property is not proved by merely writing that approximation. These are recorded reconstruction limits, not promoted into counterexamples. The full-time \(J\) norms are invariant under free time shifts by translating both test functions. Together with the corrected cubic-iterate identity, this provides the needed local time-shift mechanism.

The finite-mode integrability statement in Remark 5.2 also needs care because the weight contains a high-mode Wick tail. It is not refuted here. For that tail \(Z=\sum_{|n|>N}a_n(|g_n|^2-1)\), \[\mathbb Ee^{-\lambda Z}
 =\prod_{|n|>N}\frac{e^{\lambda a_n}}{1+\lambda a_n}<\infty
 \quad(\lambda>0),\] since \(\sum a_n^2<\infty\). The exact quadratic density formula has growth at most \(e^{C_N\|P_Nu\|_2^2}\). Splitting according to whether the tail cancels at least half the low-mode mass, and choosing \(\lambda\) sufficiently large on the cancellation event, supplies a valid fixed-\(N\) integrability route. The full-weight variational applications similarly call for a separate larger cutoff followed by a justified limit. These observations distinguish an omitted justification from an established false integrability conclusion.
