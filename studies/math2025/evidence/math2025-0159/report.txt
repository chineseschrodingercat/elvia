Title: Initial mathematical review: M25-0159
The monodromy of families of subvarieties on abelian varieties

Authors: Ariyan Javanpeykar, Thomas Krämer, Christian Lehn, and Marco Maculan. Publication: Duke Mathematical Journal 174 (2025), no. 6, 1045–1149; DOI 10.1215/00127094-2024-0053 (https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2024-0053).

Reviewed source: the exact 105-page journal typeset PDF obtained from Maculan’s author website. Printed page numbers are used below; physical PDF page \(p\) has printed number \(1044+p\). The separately retained arXiv:2210.05166v3 PDF and TeX, dated 18 March 2025, are ancillary sources, not presumed to be the journal’s typesetting source.

Initial assessment: Major. The signed-cycle definitions omit the permutation normalization required by their asserted degrees and by Claim 8.9. An admissible eight-point cycle gives the exact coefficient discrepancy \(552\ne0\). This affects the cycle-to-spin-representation bridge used in the main classification; the paper itself later displays the correct factor, and the normalized calculation can be repaired. Separately, the unexcluded zero-dimensional case contradicts the stated main monodromy equivalence and simplicity criterion. Three local defects concern arithmetic twists, line-valued pairings, and the \(r=1\) endpoint of an auxiliary wedge-power theorem. These findings do not refute the intended positive-dimensional classification after the identified corrections. The scientific decision remains `pending`; `human_reviews=[]`.

# Scope, assumptions, and the proof chain

The monodromy theorem concerns a smooth proper family \(\mathcal X\subset A\times S\) with connected fibers, where \(A\) is constant and the characteristic is zero. The stated dimension range is \(d<(g-1)/2\), with explicit Euler-characteristic exclusions for exceptional and certain half-spin representations. The theorem is an equivalence: geometric nonconstancy, nondivisibility, and the absence of product or symmetric-power decompositions are equivalent to big monodromy for most torsion tuples of characters. Most excludes a finite union of torsion translates of proper character subtori. It is not a claim about every character.

The Tannaka formulation concerns the derived group of the connected component, rather than the entire group. The chain is generic vanishing and Galois detection; characteristic cycles and their weight interpretation; simplicity; classification of minuscule representations; reconstruction of wedge powers from curves; and exclusion of spin representations in the stated range. The distinction between a finite Gauss map, mere nondegeneracy, and an ample normal bundle was retained. General singular statements were not silently replaced by smooth ones.

The primary reviewer read the introduction and Section 2 in full, checked the numerical and geometric interfaces, and independently examined the affected later statements. Thus the journal text was covered collectively, while the detailed proof audit remains the bounded one documented in the coverage records. Selected primary dependencies were retrieved and inspected. Deep generic-vanishing, microlocal Tannakian, and classification foundations were not all re-proved.

# Problem 1: ordered signed cycles have the wrong multiplicities

Location. Definition 5.21 and Lemma 5.22, pp. 1106–1107; the definitions in Section 8.3, pp. 1132–1133; the degree assertions on p. 1130; and Claim 8.9, p. 1134. The affected bridge is Theorem 8.5, used in the proof of Theorem 8.3 on p. 1132.

The definitions and their numerical consequence. Let \(\Lambda_Z\) be a reduced symmetric clean cycle of Gauss degree \(2n\), with a general fiber \[\{p_1,-p_1,\ldots,p_n,-p_n\}.\] The source defines \(\Lambda_{Z,S}^{[r]}\) inside \(A^r\times\mathbb P_A\), by removing the big diagonal and antidiagonal from the ordered fiber product. Its elements are ordered tuples, not unordered subsets. Section 8.3 then defines \[\Lambda_{Z,S}^{\alpha}:=\sigma_{\alpha *}\Lambda_{Z,S}^{[r]},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Alt}_S^r\Lambda_Z:=\Lambda_{Z,S}^{(1^r)}
 \tag{1}\] without the factor \(1/N(\alpha)\) used elsewhere. Definition 5.21 likewise omits that factor for the parity pieces.

For \(\alpha=(1^r)\), there are \(r!\) orderings of each signed subset. Consequently (1) has Gauss degree \[r!\,2^r\binom nr,
 \tag{2}\] not the stated \(2^r\binom nr\). When \(r=n\) and the Gauss monodromy is even, each parity piece has degree \(n!2^{n-1}\) rather than \(2^{n-1}\). Pushforward multiplicities are not discarded when two sums coincide; such coincidences cannot fix the total degree discrepancy.

A complete counterexample to Claim 8.9. Let \(E/\mathbb C\) be an elliptic curve, let \(p\in E(\mathbb C)\) have infinite order, and set \(A=E^4\). Let \(p_i\) have coordinate \(p\) in position \(i\) and zero in the other three positions. Then \[\sum_{i=1}^4 a_i p_i=0,\quad a_i\in\mathbb Z
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_1=\cdots=a_4=0.\] Take the reduced effective zero-cycle \(Z=\sum_{i=1}^4([p_i]+[-p_i])\). Its conormal cycle is eight disjoint copies of \(\mathbb P_A\). The Gauss map is finite of degree eight, with trivial, hence even, monodromy. Here \(n=4\), \(m=2\), and both parity-sum supports have dimension zero. Thus the hypothesis \(m-1>\min_\epsilon\dim\operatorname{Alt}_{S,\epsilon}^nZ\) in Lemma 8.8 is satisfied.

Let \[S_\pm=\sum_{\substack{a_i\in\{\pm1\}\\\prod a_i=\pm1}}
       [a_1p_1+\cdots+a_4p_4]\] denote the multiplicity-one fiber cycles. Under the printed ordered-tuple definitions, the fiber of \(\Lambda_\pm:=\operatorname{Alt}_{S,\pm}^4\Lambda_Z\) is \(24S_\pm\).

Claim 8.9 asserts \[\Lambda_+\circ\Lambda_+-[2]_*\Lambda_+
 =
 \Lambda_-\circ\Lambda_--[2]_*\Lambda_-.
 \tag{3}\] Evaluate the fiber cycles at \(q=2(p_1+p_2+p_3+p_4)\). Independence of the \(p_i\) forces both sign vectors in any sum yielding \(q\) to be \((+,+,+,+)\). Therefore the coefficient of \([q]\) on the left of (3) is \[24^2-24=552,\] whereas on the right it is zero. The witness satisfies the cycle, finiteness, parity, and dimension assumptions. It is not a floating-point diagnostic or a hypothetical representation without a geometric cycle. The retained reviewer-written finite calculation reproduces these coefficients exactly.

Counterevidence and repair. The intended normalization is unusually well evidenced within the paper. Definition 5.19 uses \(1/N(\alpha)\), the ordinary wedge definition on p. 1120 uses \(1/r!\), and the proof on p. 1140 explicitly writes \(1/N(\alpha)\) with \(\alpha=(1^n)\). Thus the problem is an inconsistency among actual definitions and calculations, not an allegation that the intended signed-weight identity is false.

Insert \(1/N(\alpha)\) into the affected definitions; for the spin application this is \(1/n!\). The quotient by permutations of equal-weight entries explains integrality: on the diagonal-free tuple locus the corresponding permutation group acts freely, and the sum map factors through its quotient. The normalized parity cycles have fibers \(S_\pm\) and degrees \(2^{n-1}\).

For these normalized cycles, (3) is valid. Its off-diagonal terms are paired by changing the first coordinate in which the two sign vectors differ. This reverses both parities, preserves their sum, and is an involution. The diagonal terms are exactly \([2]_*S_\pm\). This proves the repaired identity for every \(n\), rather than only the finite samples. Positive rescaling leaves support and dimension arguments unchanged, while the restored multiplicities also restore the stated starting degrees in the Segre-class recursion. With \(s=s(\Lambda_+)\), that recursion must use \(\delta=s(\Lambda_-)-s(\Lambda_+)\) to obtain \((2s+\delta)*\delta=[2]_*\delta\); the definition on p. 1135 reverses this sign. This uniquely determined sign correction is recorded with the normalization repair, without counting another Problem.

Impact. The raw cycle cannot be identified with the multiplicity-one spin-weight cycle as asserted in Theorem 8.5, and Claim 8.9 is false with the printed definitions. These are calculations in the exclusion of spin groups, hence in Theorem C and the main Tannaka classification. This is a Major invalid core derivation under contract 0.7.1, even though a specific normalization repair is available. The repaired counts, parity identity, and unchanged support calculations are verified here; an unconditional independent certification of every other geometric and Tannakian input is not claimed.

The type-\(D\) orbit formula on p. 1106 has a separate local wording issue: when the weight has length \(\ell<n\), an unused zero coordinate can absorb the sign parity, so the nonzero entries are not restricted to even sign changes. The \(\ell<n\) branch of Lemma 5.22 already uses the full signed cycle. This clarification is not counted as another core defect.

A further certification limit concerns the first reducedness conclusion of Lemma 5.22, p. 1107. Abstractly, sending \(\epsilon_4\) to zero and \(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3\) to independent lattice elements keeps all eight even all-ones weights distinct, while the standard weights \(\pm\epsilon_4\) coincide. A parity-compensating subgroup can still act transitively on those eight weights. Thus the quotient-lattice interface and abstract transitivity alone do not supply the type-\(B\) collision argument in this full-length type-\(D\) case. No perverse-sheaf or conormal realization satisfying all geometric hypotheses has been constructed here. This is an unclosed limitation, not a counterexample or an additional Problem; the verified normalization repair is not a certification of that separate assertion. For general signed type-\(D\) coefficients, a complete edited definition must also state the absolute-value and parity-relabeling convention.

# Problem 2: the zero-dimensional case contradicts the stated equivalences

Location. The setup on p. 1046, big-monodromy definition on p. 1048, and main monodromy theorem on p. 1049; Theorem A on p. 1053 and Theorem 6.1 on p. 1108. Lemma 2.12 on p. 1064 reflects the same missing restriction.

Take any complex abelian variety \(A\) of dimension \(g\ge3\), let \(S\) be a smooth connected variety, and set \[\mathcal X=\{0\}\times S\subset A\times S.\] This is a closed smooth proper family with connected fibers of dimension \(d=0\). The normal bundle of a fiber is the vector space \(T_0A\) on a point and is ample: its projectivization carries the ample bundle \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{g-1}}(1)\). The bound \(0<(g-1)/2\) holds. Its Euler characteristic is \(1\), and all conditional exclusions in Assumption 1.1 are vacuous. The source’s conventions on p. 1056 do not require a variety to have positive dimension; later statements explicitly impose that condition when needed.

The geometric generic fiber is constant up to translation, so condition (1) of the main monodromy theorem is false. On the other hand, for every character, \[V_\chi=R^0f_*\pi^*L_\chi\] is the trivial rank-one local system. The point is symmetric with translating section zero, and \(d\) is even. Hence the group used in the definition is \(\operatorname{SO}_1=\{1\}\). Its product for any tuple is a normal subgroup of the trivial monodromy group. Condition (2) is true for every torsion tuple. This directly contradicts the printed equivalence.

For the same fiber, \(\delta_{\{0\}}\) is the tensor unit. Its Tannaka group and derived group are trivial, with zero Lie algebra. Section 6.1 defines simplicity through a simple Lie algebra, so the derived group is not simple. Yet a point cannot be a product of two positive-dimensional smooth varieties. This also contradicts Theorem 6.1, not just an informal description in the introduction. Finally, Lemma 2.12 would give \(1=|e_X|\ge g\); its proof assumes \(H^1(X,\mathbb C)\ne0\), which fails for the point.

Repair, impact, and boundary. An explicit restriction \(d>0\) in the monodromy and simplicity statements, and in the relevant numerical lemma, excludes this example. The corresponding nontriviality restriction should also be stated when calling the representation minuscule. These are manifestations of one missing boundary hypothesis, not separate counted errors.

The finding is Major at the level of the stated main equivalence and its simplicity input: admissible parameters give opposite truth values for the two sides. Its scope is exactly the omitted zero-dimensional case. It establishes no failure of the intended positive-dimensional theorem, and adding \(d>0\) is not represented as a new proof of all the remaining cases.

# Problem 3: an arithmetic character twist need not preserve the tensor unit

Location. Lemma 3.12, p. 1077, with the non-algebraically-closed-field conventions of Section 3, p. 1067.

The character group there is defined using the étale fundamental group \(\pi_1(A,0)\), while the section explicitly permits arbitrary characteristic-zero fields. With that arithmetic fundamental group, the assertion that twisting by \(L_\chi\) is a tensor equivalence, and hence preserves the Tannaka group, needs a normalization at the identity.

Let \(k=\mathbb R\), let \(A/k\) be any abelian variety, and let \(\chi\) be the nontrivial character of \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb C/\mathbb R)\), pulled back along \(\pi_1(A,0)\to\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb C/\mathbb R)\). Take coefficients in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) and \(P=\delta_0\). Then \[G(P)=1,\qquad P\otimes L_\chi=i_{0*}(\mathrm{sgn}),\qquad
 G(P\otimes L_\chi)=\mu_2.\] Both skyscraper sheaves have geometric Euler characteristic one, so passage to the negligible quotient does not identify them. The second object is a nontrivial invertible object of order two. In particular, the twist sends the tensor unit to a nontrivial object and cannot be a strong tensor equivalence.

The corrected hypothesis is that the restriction \(e^*L_\chi\) at the identity section be the trivial Galois representation, with the normalized multiplicative structure. Then \[m^*L_\chi\simeq \operatorname{pr}_1^*L_\chi
                    \otimes\operatorname{pr}_2^*L_\chi\] and tensoring with \(L_\chi\) respects both convolution and the unit; twisting by \(L_{\chi^{-1}}\) is its inverse. Over an algebraically closed base the arithmetic obstruction is absent.

This normalization is explicitly present in Definition 2.4 of the exact cited Lawrence–Sawin arXiv v4. The characters actually used after passage to the function field in Theorem 4.10 descend from an algebraically closed constant base and have trivial identity restriction. Thus the displayed counterexample establishes a Minor defect in the general lemma, without invalidating that normalized application.

# Problem 4: a line-valued pairing gives a similitude group

Location. The pairing and ensuing upper bound on pp. 1047–1048, together with the assertion on p. 1048 that a family constant up to translation has finite algebraic monodromy.

The pairing has values in the line local system \(a^*L_\chi\): \[V_\chi\otimes V_\chi\longrightarrow a^*L_\chi.
 \tag{4}\] Compatibility with monodromy means preservation with the character of this target line as multiplier. It does not imply preservation of a fixed scalar-valued bilinear form.

For an explicit positive-dimensional example, let \(C\) be a hyperelliptic curve of genus four and \(A=J(C)\). Embed \(C\) using a Weierstrass basepoint, so \(C=-C\). Over \(S=A\), consider the translating family \(X_t=C+t\). It is smooth and proper, and its symmetry section is \(a(t)=2t\). Multiplicativity of the normalized character local system gives \[V_\chi\simeq H^1(C,L_\chi|_C)\otimes L_\chi
 \quad\text{on }S.\] For a nontrivial character \(\chi\) of order three, \(H^0\) and \(H^2\) on \(C\) vanish and \(\dim H^1=6\). The monodromy contains \(\zeta I_6\), where \(\zeta^3=1\) and \(\zeta\ne1\). Its multiplier in the alternating pairing is \(\zeta^2\ne1\). Thus even a torsion character gives a full monodromy group not contained in the asserted symplectic group.

For an analytic character with infinite cyclic image generated by \(2\in\mathbb C^\times\), the same computation gives scalar algebraic monodromy \(\mathbb G_m\). The family is constant up to translation, but its monodromy is not finite. Finiteness of this scalar character is restored by the torsion restriction.

Correction and bounded impact. The unrestricted upper bound is the appropriate similitude group. For torsion \(\chi\), the multiplier has finite image and becomes trivial after a finite étale cover; for arbitrary \(\chi\), every derived subgroup kills the multiplier character. The definition of big monodromy requires the special orthogonal or symplectic group as a normal subgroup, which is compatible with a similitude ambient group. Therefore this is a Minor error in the introductory full-group statements, not a counterexample to the intended positive-dimensional main equivalence.

# Problem 5: the wedge reconstruction statement includes \(r=1\)

Location. Theorem 7.6, p. 1119; its use immediately below, and the end of its proof on p. 1127.

The theorem permits \(e,r\ge1\) and asserts that \(Z\) is a curve if \[\operatorname{Alt}^r\Lambda_Z=\Lambda_{[e](X)},\qquad r\dim Z<\dim A,
 \tag{5}\] with \(X\) smooth and nondivisible and both Gauss maps finite. For \(r=e=1\), (5) is the identity \(\Lambda_Z=\Lambda_X\) and imposes no curve condition.

For a concrete positive-dimensional witness, take a smooth plane quartic \(C\), let \(A=J(C)\), and set \(X=Z=\Theta\simeq\operatorname{Sym}^2C\). The source itself discusses this smooth genus-three theta surface in Proposition 2.9(2). The theta divisor is ample and principal. Its normal bundle is ample, so its Gauss map is finite by Theorem 2.8. Its stabilizer is trivial: a translation preserving \(\Theta\) belongs to the kernel of its principal polarization. Thus every hypothesis of Theorem 7.6 holds with \[e=r=1,\qquad \dim Z=2<3=\dim A,\] but \(Z\) is a surface.

The actual classification theorem, Theorem 7.3, uses \(1<r\le n/2\), and the final curve deduction uses the smoothness criterion for symmetric powers of exponent at least two. Requiring \(r\ge2\) in Theorem 7.6 excludes the example and matches that application. This is a Minor endpoint error in the auxiliary statement; no failure of the actual \(r>1\) reconstruction is established.

# Positive checks, counterevidence, and limitations

The conormal and numerical arguments in Section 2 were checked with their hypotheses. In the proof that a finite Gauss map implies nondegeneracy, both strict dimension inequalities are needed: one gives a positive-dimensional fiber of a quotient map, while the other supplies a nonzero conormal covector common to that fiber. The isogeny pushforward coefficient is the generic degree; nondivisibility forces this degree to be one.

For complete intersections of \(r=g-d\) ample divisors, the Chern-class computation gives \[|e_X|=\sum_{\substack{a_i\ge1\\\sum a_i=g}}D_1^{a_1}\cdots D_r^{a_r}
 \ge g!\binom{g-1}{d}.\] The mixed-intersection inequality supplies the lower bound term by term. When \(d\ge1\), some \(a_i\ge2\), and factorial divisibility in integral cohomology proves evenness. These checks support the stated exclusion of \(27\) and \(56\) in the actual positive-dimensional complete-intersection range. They do not justify extending Lemma 2.12 to points.

Debarre’s original Theorem 4.5 was inspected for the exact relative-cohomology range used in Proposition 2.9. Ein–Ilic–Lazarsfeld’s Proposition 2.4 was checked with \(n=d\) and bundle rank \(g-d\), including its convention \(\delta(t)=2^{\lfloor\sqrt t\rfloor}\) and the nef-cotangent hypothesis. Thus the square-root exponent in Lemma 2.12 is not an OCR or rounding error. The full classical foundations behind these theorems were not reconstructed.

The Galois/monodromy check traced observability and fixed-part inputs to the retrieved primary papers. In the exact Lawrence–Sawin v4, several results cited with Section 5 numbers occur in Section 4; the matching statements, rather than an unsupported citation-number match, were checked. The normalized geometric characters used in the main application avoid Problem 3. Nondivisibility supplies the relevant irreducibility of the connected derived action.

The symplectic wedge range was also stress-tested. For a positive-dimensional nondegenerate support, the identity relating \(\operatorname{Alt}^{n+1}V\) and \(\operatorname{Alt}^{n-1}V\) for \(\dim V=2n\), together with support dimensions, rules out \((n+1)d<g\). This explains why the primitive-wedge argument is used in its permissible range. An intermediate generic degree described as exactly two need only be at least two; no separate substantive issue is inferred from that overstatement.

The independent finite weight checks reproduce the normalized parity identity and the exceptional minuscule orbit sizes \(27,27,56\). They check those counts and duality properties, not the completeness of the classification of simple Lie algebras. In the spin exclusion, the possible Euler characteristic eight is ruled out using parity and the surface bound: the relevant dimensions force a surface and the bounds exceed eight. No missing low-rank exception was established.

The support-union formula on p. 1130 needs its stated range restricted to \(r\le n\), or its lower summation index changed to \(\max(0,r-n)\). The subsequent use is at \(r=2\le n\), so this out-of-range wording does not supply another main-theorem failure. The negative-valued second branch in Example 7.8 is similarly a transparent formula correction, not a separately counted Problem.

Appendix A’s field-reduction interfaces and Appendix B’s symmetric-power and birational arguments were checked in their stated characteristic-zero settings. The full external theories of constructible sheaves, microlocal characteristic cycles, generic vanishing, and Tannakian duality remain imported. No missing author code is treated as a mathematical defect, and no search is claimed to prove first discovery or the absence of corrections.
