Abstract: This review concerns the acquired 118-page Wiley version of record by Emmanuel Michta, Jiwoon Park and Gordon Slade, DOI 10.1002/cpa.22256. The complete source was read. Three problems affect displayed core proof steps, and three affect peripheral ranges or constants. The evidence establishes the indicated false inequalities, invalid implications or incorrect prefactors; it does not establish that the principal finite-size scaling theorems are false. Each problem includes its hypotheses, proof, dependency and repair boundary. The disposition remains pending independent human review.

# Scope, notation and principal dependencies

Source page numbers below are the internal numbers 1–118 of the acquired version, rather than journal pagination 2001–2118. Its first page records acceptance on 2 April 2025. The separate arXiv record 2306.00896v3 is dated 17 March 2025 and describes a 105-page manuscript with editorial improvements; equality with that file is not asserted.

The main model has integer \(n\geq1\), integer \(d\geq4\), sufficiently large \(L\) and sufficiently small positive \(g\), with the smallness depending on \(L\). The hierarchical volume has \(L^{dN}\) sites. Periodic and free boundary conditions are distinguished by \[-\Delta_N^{\mathrm F}=-\Delta_N^{\mathrm P}
       +qL^{-2N}Q_N,\qquad
 q=\frac{1-L^{-d}}{1-L^{-(d+2)}}.\] Here \(Q_N\) projects onto constant fields. The covariance decomposition uses \(P_j=Q_{j-1}-Q_j\) and coefficients \(L^{2(j-1)}/(1+aL^{2(j-1)})\); in finite volume the admissible real mass satisfies \(a>-L^{-2(N-1)}\). The block fluctuation has zero sum. This last property is essential, but has the limited consequence explained in Problem 1.

Theorem 1.1 treats the fixed supercritical Gaussian regime. Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 treat, respectively, the non-Gaussian critical window and the larger Gaussian window. Their centre under free boundary conditions is the exact quantity \[\nu_N^{\mathrm F}=\nu_{0,N}(-qL^{-2N})-qL^{-2N}.\] In dimensions at least six, discarding the displayed error in a simpler asymptotic centre would exceed the critical window. The paper retains the exact centre there; Remark 3.5 explicitly recognizes this issue. It is not a finding against the paper.

For the critical profile, put \[I_k(s)=\int_0^\infty x^k e^{-x^4/4-sx^2/2}\,dx,\qquad
 f_n(s)=\frac{I_{n+1}(s)}{nI_{n-1}(s)}\quad(n>0).\] Integration by parts gives \(nI_{n-1}=I_{n+3}+sI_{n+1}\) and the stated recursion for \(f_n\). The \(n=0\) complete-graph statements are separate from the main integer-spin theorems. The paper’s Euclidean-lattice extrapolations and several broader universality statements are identified as conjectural. They are not promoted to theorems in this review.

The proof proceeds through the finite-volume Gaussian identity, RG estimates and critical-point construction, followed by the last-scale integrals. Problems 1–3 concern this chain. Problems 4–6 concern an introductory asymptotic, an unnecessarily broad auxiliary lemma, and an appendix prefactor. The classification Major identifies an invalid core derivation even where a local repair is available; it does not mean that a final theorem has been disproved.

# Problem 1: false quartic separation in the Gaussian identity

Equation reference: eq:quartic-false. Severity: Major. Source: Lemma 2.1, pp. 29–30, especially the final paragraph following (2.34) on p. 30. The proof of analytic continuation uses, for a zero-sum field, the inequality \[
 \sum_x|\varphi_x+y|^4\geq
 \sum_x\bigl(|\varphi_x|^4+|y|^4\bigr).\] All fields in the following example are real, so the issue precedes any choice of interpretation for complex parameters.

Claim 1.  Inequality [eq:quartic-false] (#eq:quartic-false) fails for a scalar zero-sum field in every volume with at least three sites.

Proof. Take \(n=1\), \(\varphi=(2,-1,-1,0,\ldots,0)\) and \(y=-1/10\). Then \(\sum\varphi_x=0\), \(\sum\varphi_x^2=6\) and \(\sum\varphi_x^3=6\). Expanding the fourth powers, the left side minus the right side of [eq:quartic-false] (#eq:quartic-false) is \[4y\sum_x\varphi_x^3+6y^2\sum_x\varphi_x^2
       =-\frac{12}{5}+\frac9{25}=-\frac{51}{25}<0.\] The zero-sum condition cancels the linear sum, not the cubic sum. ◻

This example fits the allowed hierarchical volumes by adding zero entries. For any fixed sufficiently large \(L\), \(d\geq4\) and \(N\geq1\), the zero-mean Gaussian subspace contains these real configurations in its support. Neither small \(g>0\) nor an admissible mass repairs the asserted deterministic inequality.

#### Dependency and counterevidence.

The bound is used to justify integrability and holomorphy when the mass is continued to the negative finite-volume range. Lemma 2.1 feeds Corollary 2.2 and the last-scale representation used in Section 4. Lemma 8.4, pp. 90–91, uses the valid Jensen inequality \(\sum_x|\varphi_x+y|^4\geq M|y|^4\), where \(M\) is the number of sites. That statement does not imply the additional \(\sum|\varphi_x|^4\) term.

#### Repair and impact.

A weaker coercive bound suffices and can be proved directly. Write \(A=\sum_x|\varphi_x+y|^4\). Jensen gives \(M|y|^4\leq A\). The elementary inequality \(|u-v|^4\leq8(|u|^4+|v|^4)\) then gives \(\sum|\varphi_x|^4\leq16A\). Consequently \[A\geq \frac1{17}\left(\sum_x|\varphi_x|^4+M|y|^4\right).\] Positive quartic coercivity dominates every quadratic and linear term uniformly on compact sets of the complex mass half-plane \(\Re a>-L^{-2(N-1)}\). This supplies the required domination. An analytic continuation argument should first cross-multiply the normalized identity; one need not assume that a complex partition function has no zeros. For real admissible parameters its denominator is positive. Thus the displayed inequality is false, while the Gaussian identity has a concrete repair and is not itself disproved.

# Problem 2: an unjustified high-dimensional decay exponent

Severity: Major. Source: (3.3), (3.12), pp. 31–32; (5.27)–(5.29), p. 57; (5.38), p. 58; and (6.10) with the proof of Theorem 3.1(ii), p. 63.

Equation reference: eq:actual-K. Write \(\rho_j=L^{-(d-4)j}\), and distinguish the paper’s exponent \(\mathfrak p\) from an integer polynomial degree. The domain and norm have the form \[\|K_j\|_{\mathcal W_j}\leq C_{\rm RG}\widetilde\vartheta_j^3
             \widetilde g_j^3\rho_j^{\mathfrak a},\qquad
 \|K_j\|_{\mathcal W_j}
 =\|K_j\|_{T_0(\ell_j)}
       +\widetilde g_j^{9/4}\rho_j^{\mathfrak b}
                       \|K_j\|_{T^{G_j}(h_j)},
 \quad \mathfrak b=\mathfrak a-\mathfrak p .\] For \(d\geq12\) the actual definition is \[\mathfrak p=\frac{d}{2(d-4)}(1-\varepsilon),\qquad
 0<\varepsilon<\frac1{d-3}.\] Directly dividing by the norm weight gives \[
 |K_j(\varphi)|\leq C_{\rm RG}\widetilde\vartheta_j^3
       \widetilde g_j^{3/4}\rho_j^{\mathfrak p}G_j(\varphi).\] It does not give the power \(\rho_j^{3/4}\) asserted for all dimensions in (6.10). Bounded comparisons between \(g_j\) and \(\widetilde g_j\) cannot replace an exponentially different power of \(\rho_j\).

#### A counterexample to the stated domain implication.

The distinction can be witnessed inside the norm domain, rather than merely by failure to derive an estimate. Set \(n=1\) and mass zero, so \(\widetilde\vartheta_j=1\) and, for \(d>4\), \(\widetilde g_j\) stays bounded away from zero for fixed small initial \(g\). Let \[H(t)=t^6e^{-ct^4},\quad c>\kappa,\qquad
 K_j(\varphi)=\epsilon_0\widetilde g_j^3\rho_j^{\mathfrak p}
                         H(\varphi/h_j).\] Increasing \(c\) if necessary accommodates the fixed regulator convention. Every fixed-order derivative has finite regulated norm. If an analytic derivative norm is used, Cauchy’s formula on a fixed complex circle gives the same conclusion: the negative quartic term dominates the cubic perturbation in its real part. At the origin, \(\|H\|_{T_0(r)}=O(r^6)\) as \(r\downarrow0\). Since \(\ell_j/h_j=C\widetilde g_j^{1/4}\rho_j^{1/4}\), the two contributions obey \[\|K_j\|_{\mathcal W_j}\leq C\epsilon_0
 \left(\widetilde g_j^{9/2}\rho_j^{\mathfrak p+3/2}
              +\widetilde g_j^{21/4}\rho_j^{\mathfrak p+\mathfrak b}\right).\] Here \(\mathfrak b=\tfrac32(1-\varepsilon)<3/2\) for \(d\geq12\). For small \(\epsilon_0\) this lies in the required \(K\) domain. At \(\varphi=h_j\) its value is a fixed positive multiple of \(\widetilde g_j^3\rho_j^{\mathfrak p}\). Because \(\mathfrak p<3/4\), its ratio to the claimed bound in (6.10) diverges with \(j\).

This construction disproves the claimed implication from domain membership. It is not asserted to be the RG trajectory from the paper’s initial condition \(K_0=0\).

#### Quantitative consequence and repair.

For the permissible choice \(\varepsilon=1/(2d-6)\) made on p. 63, the exponential rate supplied by [eq:actual-K] (#eq:actual-K) is \(d(1-\varepsilon)/2\). It equals \(17/3\) at \(d=12\), rather than \(6\), and \(247/40\) at \(d=13\), rather than \(13/2\). The particular \(e_N\) estimate used in (3.12) therefore does not follow from the domain estimate cited in its proof. The strict exponent margins in the contraction argument, pp. 99–102, prevent simply setting \(\varepsilon=0\) without further proof.

Retaining \(\rho^{\mathfrak p}\) still gives a positive exponential decay rate. In the last-scale estimates of pp. 50–51, this decay dominates the factor \(\exp(C\sqrt N)\) allowed by the negative critical-window range. Thus weakening \(e_N\) consistently is a plausible repair of the proof of the main scaling limits. Alternatively, the authors could prove a sharper bound for the actual trajectory. Neither the sharper trajectory estimate nor a counterexample to the final scaling theorems is established here.

# Problem 3: mass decay incorrectly attached to arbitrary directions

Equation reference: eq:S1false. Severity: Major. Source: Proposition 9.4 and (9.14), (9.18), p. 101; (9.35)–(9.44), pp. 104–105. The extended norm is defined in Section 8.1, p. 87, and includes \(\lambda_K\) times the norm of the Fréchet derivative in an arbitrary unit \(K\) direction. Proposition 9.4 places the factor \(\widetilde\vartheta_+^3\) outside both terms: \[
 \|S_1\|_{\mathcal W_+(\vec\lambda)}
 \leq C_L\widetilde\vartheta_+^3
           \bigl(\widetilde g_+^3\rho^{\mathfrak a}+\lambda_K\bigr).\]

#### A mass-limit contradiction under the stated hypotheses.

Fix \(n=1\), \(d\geq4\), a sufficiently large \(L\), sufficiently small \(g>0\), scale \(j=0\), \(V(\varphi)=g\varphi^4/4\), and \(K=0\). Let the mass centre and actual mass coincide, \(\widetilde a=a\to\infty\). Put \(\lambda_V=0\) and fix \(0<\lambda_K\leq g^{9/4}\), for instance \(g^{9/4}/2\). These are within the proposition’s restrictions. With \(M=L^d\), \[C_1(a)=\frac1{1+a}\left(I-\frac1M J\right)\longrightarrow0 .\] Choose an even direction \(H(\varphi)=c_0\varphi^6e^{-c\varphi^4}\) with \(c\) sufficiently large and \(c_0>0\) chosen to give unit \(\mathcal W_0\) norm. It has \(\operatorname{Loc}(e^V H)=0\), because its Taylor terms of degree at most four vanish. Hence the local counterterm has zero derivative in this direction. Differentiating the product defining \(S_1\) at \(K=0\), and then letting \(a\to\infty\), gives at any fixed real \(\varphi>0\) \[D_KS_1(V,0)[H](\varphi)
 \longrightarrow M e^{-(M-1)V(\varphi)}H(\varphi)>0 .\] The perturbative constant tends to zero and \(\widetilde g_+\to g\). Dominated convergence is justified by the quartic decay of \(H\) and \(e^{-V}\) and the Gaussian covariance tending to zero. The evaluation term in the regulated output norm, with its positive weight, therefore gives a positive lower bound for the left side of [eq:S1false] (#eq:S1false). The right side tends to zero, because \(\widetilde\vartheta_+=(2a)^{-1}\) in this mass regime.

The shrinking domain for the base value \(K\) cannot exclude this counterexample: \(K=0\) is interior for each finite \(a\), and arbitrary Fréchet directions are not required to carry the small domain radius. The distinction is also stated in the discussion of derivatives on p. 59.

#### Counterevidence, correction and dependency.

The paper’s own estimates (9.35), (9.39), (9.40), (9.43) and (9.44) place \(\lambda_K\) outside the mass-decay factor. Combining them supports \[\|S_1\|_{\mathcal W_+(\vec\lambda)}
 \leq C_L\bigl(\widetilde\vartheta_+^3
                 \widetilde g_+^3\rho^{\mathfrak a}+\lambda_K\bigr),\] with the harmless fixed-scale comparisons absorbed in \(C_L\). The corresponding expression in the acquired RG book [book], physical PDF p. 166, printed p. 156, equation (10.4.26), likewise leaves \(\lambda_K\) outside that factor.

The false estimate occurs in the proof of the derivative bounds needed for RG iteration. Its correction leaves the \(q=0\) bounds with mass decay and the general \(q\geq1\) bounds without it, as in the actual statement of Theorem 9.1. The crucial \(q=1\) contraction is supplied separately by Proposition 9.5. Thus the printed uniform estimate and its use in (9.18) are invalid, but the structure of the desired theorem accommodates the corrected estimate. This is not evidence that the main finite-size theorem fails.

# Problem 4: a logarithmic restriction missing from an asymptotic

Severity: Minor. Source: (1.54), p. 16, compared with (1.32), p. 11, and (1.35), p. 12. For \(d=4\) let \(\widehat\gamma=(n+2)/(n+8)>0\) and \(\widehat\theta=1/2-\widehat\gamma\). The critical window has \[w_N=A_4(\log L^2)^{\widehat\gamma}B^{-1/2}
                N^{-\widehat\theta}L^{-2N}.\] The infinite-volume susceptibility satisfies \(\chi_\infty(\nu_c+\epsilon)\sim
 A_4\epsilon^{-1}(\log\epsilon^{-1})^{\widehat\gamma}\). Equation (1.54) asserts the resulting simplification to \(s_N^{-1}\sqrt{BN}\,L^{2N}\) for \(s_N\geq\epsilon_0>0\) and \(s_Nw_N\to0\).

Take \(s_N=L^N\). Both stated restrictions hold, but substitution in the infinite-volume formula shows that the ratio to the proposed expression tends to \[\left(\frac{\log(1/(s_Nw_N))}{2N\log L}\right)^{\widehat\gamma}
 \longrightarrow 2^{-\widehat\gamma}\neq1 .\] More generally \(s_N=L^{cN}\), \(0<c<2\), gives \((1-c/2)^{\widehat\gamma}\). This is a direct application of the paper’s own fixed-parameter susceptibility asymptotic along a sequence tending to criticality, not a change in model.

The simplification is valid under \(\log s_N=o(N)\), or the original logarithm can be retained. The actual positive growth range in Theorem 1.2, \(s_N\leq L^{\sqrt N}\) in dimension four, satisfies this additional condition. Section 1.5 is not used to prove the main results. The displayed assertion is therefore false in its printed range, with no demonstrated damage to Corollary 1.4 or the main proof.

# Problem 5: a covariance lemma is too broad in polynomial degree

Severity: Minor. Source: Lemma 8.3 and (8.20), pp. 89–90. The lemma allows even mass-independent polynomials of degree at most \(2p\) for every integer \(p\geq1\), and bounds the covariance by a constant times a field-growth factor \(P_h(\varphi)^{2p}\).

Set \(n=1\), \(j=0\), \(a=0\), \(M=L^d>1\) and \(C=I-J/M\). Take \[V_1(B)=V_2(B)=\sum_{x\in B}\varphi_x^6,\qquad p=3,
 \quad \lambda_V=\lambda_K=0,\] and any fixed permitted \(h\geq\mathfrak c_+\). These polynomials are even and mass-independent. If \(S_r=\sum_x\zeta_x^r\), then \(S_1=0\) almost surely and \[\sum_x(\varphi+\zeta_x)^6
 =M\varphi^6+15\varphi^4 S_2+20\varphi^3 S_3
               +15\varphi^2 S_4+6\varphi S_5+S_6 .\] For the stated Gaussian, \(\operatorname{Var}S_2=2\operatorname{tr}(C^2)=2(M-1)\). The covariance of the polynomial with itself is consequently \[450(M-1)\varphi^8+O(\varphi^6)
       \qquad (|\varphi|\to\infty).\] The possible degree-seven cross term vanishes by Gaussian parity. The left norm contains the zeroth-order evaluation, while the claimed right side grows only as \(|\varphi|^6\). No fixed constant can give (8.20).

The quartic instances actually used on pp. 94, 98 and 103 have \(p=2\); their maximum degree \(4p-4\) is precisely four and this example does not refute them. The cited RG book’s Lemma 7.6.1, physical pp. 122–123, has a quartic scope; it does not supply the printed arbitrary-degree extension. A safe repair is to restrict the lemma to the needed degrees \(p=1,2\) and prove those cases. For general \(p\), growth at least \(4p-4\) is necessary even for equal scalar block polynomials. This necessary power is not asserted sufficient under every more general coefficient convention. The classification is Minor because the demonstrated overstatement is outside the operative quartic uses.

# Problem 6: a factor of two in the endpoint Laplace prefactor

Severity: Minor. Source: Appendix B, pp. 117–118, especially (B.7), (B.9)–(B.11); the definition of \(f_0=I_1\) on p. 18. Here \(g>0\) is fixed and the complete-graph weakly self-avoiding walk has the effective potential of (B.4). At criticality set \[P=\tfrac12 V_c''(0)>0,\qquad b=\dot V_c'(0)>0.\] The exact integration-by-parts representation (B.7) is \[G_{01}(\nu)=\int_0^\infty e^{-NV_\nu(t)}(1-V_\nu'(t))^2\,dt .\]

#### Direct asymptotic and operative assumptions.

At \(\nu=\nu_c+sN^{-1/2}\) and fixed real \(s\), put \(t=u/\sqrt{PN}\). The quadratic critical minimum and \(V_\nu(0)=0\) for every \(\nu\) give \[NV_\nu(t)=u^2+\frac{bs}{\sqrt P}u+o(1),\qquad
 (1-V_\nu'(t))^2=1+o(1).\] The Taylor remainder in \(\nu\) has an additional factor \(t\) near zero; thus its contribution after multiplication by \(N\) tends to zero on this scale. It is not necessary to treat an unspecified \(O(s^2)\) exponent as vanishing.

For completeness, the critical minimum in this specific model can be verified directly. Put \(f(x)=e^{-gx^2-(\nu_c+1)x}\) and \(M_k=\int_0^\infty x^k f(x)\,dx\). There is a unique \(\nu_c\) with \(M_0=1\) by strict monotonicity and the two limits as \(\nu\) tends to \(\pm\infty\). Writing \(S(x)=\int_x^\infty f(t)\,dt\), the identity \(f(x+u)=f(x)f(u)e^{-2gxu}\) gives \(S(x)<f(x)\) for \(x>0\). Consequently \(S'(x)<-S(x)\), \(S(0)=1\), and \(S(x)<e^{-x}\). Integration by parts gives \(M_k<k!\) for \(k\geq1\). The Bessel series in (B.4) now gives \[v(t)=\sum_{k\geq0}\frac{M_k t^{k+1}}{k!(k+1)!}<e^t-1
       \quad(t>0).\] Thus \(V_c(t)>0\) off zero, \(V_c''(0)=1-M_1>0\), and \(\dot V_c'(0)=M_1>0\). The tail growth supplied by [bs] and positivity away from zero justify endpoint localization, also in a fixed \(s\) window.

Equation reference: eq:correct-Laplace. Since \(f_0(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-u^2-zu}\,du\), the result is \[
 NG_{01}(\nu_c+sN^{-1/2})
 \sim \frac{\sqrt N}{\sqrt P}\,
                 f_0\!\left(\frac{bs}{\sqrt P}\right).\] At \(s=0\) this is \(\sqrt{\pi N}/(2\sqrt P)\). Equation (B.11) is twice [eq:correct-Laplace] (#eq:correct-Laplace); the endpoint Laplace factor \(1/\mu=1/2\) is missing in (B.10).

#### Counterevidence and impact.

The directly cited publisher version of [bs], Theorem 1.3, physical p. 9, printed p. 174, already gives \(G_{01}\sim\Gamma(3/2)/\sqrt{PN}\) at criticality, agreeing with [eq:correct-Laplace] (#eq:correct-Laplace). Its Theorem 3.1, physical p. 21, retains the factor \(1/\mu\). The exact integral formulas are checked on physical pp. 24–25. The same formulas give \(G_{00}=1+O(N^{-1/2})\) in a fixed window: integrating its extra derivative term by parts leaves \(1-V_\nu'(0)+G_{01}-\int e^{-NV_\nu}V_\nu''\). Thus \(G_{00}\) does not change the leading susceptibility prefactor. The corrected constants are \(\lambda_1=P^{-1/2}\) and \(\lambda_2=bP^{-1/2}\). Proposition B.2 states the existence of positive constants, so the incorrect displayed identification does not disprove that proposition. This is a local normalization error.

# Coverage, independent checks and limitations

All 118 source pages, including definitions, proofs, appendices and bibliography, were read by the primary executor. Coverage included the boundary Laplacian and zero-mode decomposition; the admissible mass range; the scaling, moment and sequence restrictions; last-scale domination; the definition and composition of the RG map; norm weights and strict contraction margins; critical-point construction and its derivative hypotheses; and the profile identities and endpoint asymptotics.

In particular, positive and negative growing-window restrictions were kept distinct. The negative range is at most \((\log N)^{1/4}\) in dimension four and \(N^{1/4}\) above it; the positive range in Theorem 1.2 and the different mesoscopic range in Theorem 1.3 were not interchanged. Appendix A’s extension to negative values of \(n\) explicitly restricts \(s\) above its pole boundary; that is not a counterexample to an unrestricted claim.

Independent reviewer calculations include the exact rational quartic counterexample, the dimension-dependent exponent comparisons, Gaussian quadratic-form moments, the susceptibility ratio, and the endpoint Gaussian integral. Numerical integrations of the profile recurrence for \(n=1,2,3,8\) and \(s=-4,0,4\) had maximum relative floating-point residual \(3.70\times10^{-14}\). These ordinary floating-point checks corroborate the analytic calculations; they are neither interval enclosures nor simulations of an RG orbit. No author code was executed.

Actual native images were inspected for 62 source pages covering all substantive evidence locators. For [book], physical pages 82, 122–123, 129, 132–136 and 166–171 were read and viewed; the entire 267-page book was not read. All six pages of the downloaded corrections [errata], actually dated 22 October 2026, were read and viewed. An earlier cached search date was not treated as the current file’s date. The corrections identify issues in some older critical-point and norm arguments. The paper’s quantitative interval control and its placement of expectation before a norm were examined separately; old book errors are not automatically attributed to this paper. The replacement reference arguments are not all independently reproved here.

For [bs], physical pages 6–10, 21–25 and 27–28 were read and viewed. The acquired file is the publisher’s screen PDF, not an asserted copy of its separate arXiv version. The reference’s formulas and hypotheses used in Problem 6 were checked directly. Bounded primary-source version and correction searches were performed; failure to locate an additional target-paper correction is not proof that none exists.
