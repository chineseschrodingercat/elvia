Abstract: The assigned accepted author manuscript is reviewed through its lattice, planar-graph and four-manifold arguments. Five local correction groups are established: coordinate choices in the Hajós lemmas, graph statements with missing loop/bridge qualifications, two nonequivalent descriptions of an oriented cut, an incorrect reduced-diagram criterion, and a reversed positive-definite correction-term inequality. Each has an explicit small example and a bounded repair. The principal link classification is not refuted by these examples. The diagram-identification step in Proposition 5.1 and several imported topological inputs remain outside the completed verification. The assessment is Minor revision; scientific adjudication and human review remain pending.

The work is smooth. A \(\chi\)-slice link has nonzero determinant and bounds a properly embedded surface of Euler characteristic one with no closed components; orientability is not imposed. # Established problems

Problem 1 (Minor: Hajós coordinates and the last-column permutation).  Location. Lemma 2.4, pp. 9–10; Lemma 2.6 and its proof, p. 10. Compare Lemma 2.3 on p. 9 and the use in Proposition 4.1 on p. 20.

Lemma 2.4 asserts that the first nonzero entry of every vector of an arbitrary embedded 2-cube tiling lattice is even. This needs a specified Hajós coordinate order. The lattice \[\Lambda=\mathbb Z(1,2)+\mathbb Z(2,0)\subset\mathbb Z^2                 \tag{2.1}\] is the coordinate swap of the lattice with matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), hence is a 2-cube tiling lattice. Its vector \((1,2)\) has odd first entry. For a lattice already written \(\Lambda_H\), the intended parity proof is valid by lower triangularity.

There is a separate local error in the stated permutation in Lemma 2.6. Take \[H'=\begin{pmatrix}2&0&0\\0&2&0\\0&1&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad y=2e_1.\] The prescribed simultaneous transposition of the first and last rows and columns produces \[\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\0&2&0\\0&0&2\end{pmatrix},                 \tag{2.2}\] which is not a Hajós matrix. Instead, moving the first row and column to the end while preserving the relative order of the others gives \[\begin{pmatrix}2&0&0\\1&2&0\\0&0&2\end{pmatrix}.                 \tag{2.3}\]

Impact and repair boundary. Specify \(\Lambda=\Lambda_H\) in Lemma 2.4, or explicitly choose coordinates first. In Lemma 2.6 move the pure column \(2e_i\) to the end by the stable permutation just described. The remaining principal submatrix stays lower triangular, and the new last row has permitted entries. If a sign change is needed for \(y\), reduce that last row modulo two by column operations afterwards. These repairs establish the intended last-column assertion; the examples refute the printed coordinate claim and the proposed operation, not the existence theorem.

Problem 2 (Minor: loops and bridges are missing from the graph qualifications).  Location. Definition of blocks and Proposition 3.2(a), p. 14; Proposition 3.3(a), p. 15; Proposition 3.8, p. 19; Lemma 4.3 proof and Theorem 4.4, pp. 23–24; algorithm, p. 25.

On p. 14, a 2-connected graph is explicitly loopless, and blocks are maximal induced 2-connected subgraphs. Proposition 3.2(a) nevertheless describes the full flow lattice of an arbitrary finite graph using only nontrivial such blocks. A graph with one vertex and one loop has \(\operatorname{Flow}(G)=\langle1\rangle\), but has no block under that definition. The universal condition that its blocks belong to the recursive class \(\mathcal G\) is vacuous, whereas its rank-one lattice has determinant one rather than four. It therefore also contradicts the unrestricted formulation of Theorem 4.4. The failure already occurs in its claimed one-edge base case.

Bridges cause the dual omission. For a graph consisting of one ordinary edge, the only block is declared trivial, but \[\operatorname{Cut}(G)=\mathbb Ze\ne0.                                \tag{2.4}\] Thus Proposition 3.3(a) cannot restrict to nontrivial blocks.

The \(d\)-invariant rigidity statement needs a related qualification. Let \(G\) be a triangle with an additional loop. It is connected and has no bridge, so is 2-edge-connected in the usual edge-deletion sense. Its flow lattice is \[\operatorname{Flow}(G)=\langle3\rangle\oplus\mathbb Z.\] The Euclidean factor has trivial discriminant group and \(d\)-invariant zero: its unique characteristic coset has minimum square one and rank one. Hence \[d_{\langle3\rangle\oplus\mathbb Z}\simeq d_{\langle3\rangle},
 \tag{2.5}\] but \(\langle3\rangle\) cannot be a stabilization of the rank-two lattice \(\operatorname{Flow}(G)\). This is an explicit boundary failure of Proposition 3.8 if loops are allowed.

Impact and repair boundary. State the needed loopless hypothesis for the rigidity and graph classification, or adopt a block convention that accounts for loops and rejects their norm-one flow factors. Include bridge \(\mathbb Z\)-summands in the cut decomposition. The general graph algorithm must reject loops before applying the recursive test. In Lemma 4.3(2), the claim that both factors are 2-connected blocks is unnecessary for the unrestricted case: the flow direct sum follows from their meeting at one vertex, even if the factors themselves have cut vertices.

These are local scope/convention repairs. A reduced alternating diagram has a loopless, bridgeless Tait graph. More generally a 2-cube tiling lattice has no norm-one vector, so its flow graph cannot contain a loop. The examples therefore do not disprove the intended reduced-link applications, and the full block convention should be repaired before transporting the statements to arbitrary diagrams.

Problem 3 (Minor: cut-lattice membership does not imply one directed bipartition cut).  Location. The two claimed equivalent descriptions of an oriented cut, p. 13; Proposition 3.3(b) and Lemma 3.4, pp. 15–16; cut reversal in Proposition 3.7, p. 18.

Consider the 4-cycle with vertices \(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3\) in cyclic order. Orient its edges as the two directed paths \(v_0\to v_1\to v_2\) and \(v_0\to v_3\to v_2\). Set the vertex potential to \(z=(2,1,0,1)\), using the convention of p. 16 that \((\partial^*z)\cdot(v,w)=z_v-z_w\). Then \[x=\partial^*z=(1,1,1,1)\in\operatorname{Cut}(G)                 \tag{2.6}\] in these oriented edge coordinates. It is the class of the entire oriented subgraph, and it is simple: for any integral decomposition \(x=y+w\), each coordinate satisfies \(y_iw_i=y_i(1-y_i)\le0\).

However this orientation is not \(E(A,V\setminus A)\) directed entirely out of one subset \(A\). The middle vertex \(v_1\) would have to be outside \(A\) because it receives the edge from \(v_0\), and inside \(A\) because it sends the edge to \(v_2\). This is impossible. The example is loopless and 2-connected, so removing graph degeneracies does not fix it.

Impact and repair boundary. If the first description, \([H]\in\operatorname{Cut}(G)\), is adopted as the definition, Proposition 3.3(b) is valid but the subsequent bipartition description is not equivalent. If the bipartition description is intended, Proposition 3.3(b) and the corresponding form of Lemma 3.4 are false. Use a simple cut-lattice vector, equivalently a compatible edge-disjoint union of directed cuts, throughout the latter arguments.

The acyclic-orientation conclusion in Proposition 3.7 has a direct repair. If an acyclic orientation \(O\) is reversed along such an \(H\) with \([H]\in\operatorname{Cut}(G)\), any directed cycle \(C\) in the result would satisfy \[[H]\cdot[C]=-\,|E(H)\cap E(C)|.\] Orthogonality to \(\operatorname{Flow}(G)\) makes this zero, so \(C\) uses no reversed edge and was already a directed cycle in \(O\), a contradiction. Thus the needed preservation of acyclicity survives the correction; no failure of the unique-short-characteristic argument is established.

Problem 4 (Minor: zero stabilization does not characterize reducedness).  Location. Introduction, p. 2, the assertion \(\Lambda(D')\simeq\Lambda(D)\oplus\mathbb Z^n\) with \(n=0\) if and only if \(D'\) is reduced. Compare the nugatory-crossing discussion and graph–diagram correspondence on p. 24.

Take \(D\) to be the crossing-free unknot and \(D'\) the one-crossing unknot whose black Tait graph is a single ordinary edge. This is the bridge-type nugatory crossing included among the recursive base diagrams in the paper. Then \[\Lambda(D)=0,\qquad \Lambda(D')=\operatorname{Flow}(\text{one edge})=0,
 \tag{2.7}\] so \(n=0\), although \(D'\) is not reduced.

Impact and repair boundary. Keep the valid stabilization statement and the implication that a reduced diagram has \(n=0\), but remove the converse. Bridge-type nugatory crossings do not affect the flow lattice; loop-type ones contribute norm-one factors. Thus \(n\) measures the latter contribution, not every failure of reducedness. The normalized-determinant equality in Theorem 3 excludes the loop contribution while allowing bridges, consistently with its recursive base diagrams. This does not refute Theorem 3.

Problem 5 (Minor: the positive-definite correction-term inequality is reversed).  Location. Section 6.1, p. 26, equation (1) and the final comparison with \(d_{\Lambda_X}\). Compare the minimum convention for \(d_\Lambda\), p. 19.

For a positive-definite filling the text prints \[d(Y,\mathfrak t)\ge\frac{c_1(\mathfrak s)^2-b_2(X)}4
 \quad\text{for every extension }\mathfrak s.      \tag{2.8}\] Take \(X=\mathbb{CP}^2\setminus\operatorname{int}B^4\) and \(Y=S^3\). The characteristic class \(3H\) is the first Chern class of a legitimate \(\operatorname{Spin}^c\) structure, \(H^2=1\), and its boundary structure is the unique one on \(S^3\). Thus \[d(S^3)=0,\qquad \frac{(3H)^2-1}{4}=2,             \tag{2.9}\] contradicting (2.8).

Impact and repair boundary. In the positive-definite convention used here the bound has direction \(\le\); the last paragraph must correspondingly read \(d(Y,\mathfrak t)\le d_{\Lambda_X}(\varphi(\mathfrak t))\). The equality condition defining a sharp extension is unchanged. The later cubiquity proof uses a sharp extension and the equality \(c_1^2=b_2\), not the erroneous inequality direction. The explicit example disproves the displayed universal bound, while leaving that equality argument available.

# Positive verification and proof dependencies

## Lattice and planar-graph mechanism

Proposition 2.1 is verified directly by translating the unit cube and choosing a representative of the coset \(-x+\Lambda\). Its finite set of \(2^n\) vertices supplies every coset, which forces full rank and \[[\mathbb Z^n:\Lambda]\le2^n,\qquad
 \det\Lambda=[\mathbb Z^n:\Lambda]^2\le4^n.               \tag{3.1}\] Equality makes the representatives unique. The associated half-open side-two cubes then give the tiling. The rank-zero case is the zero lattice, corresponding to a tree on the flow side; rank one at equality is \(\langle4\rangle\), corresponding to a 4-cycle.

In the Hajós induction, \(2e_i\) is primitive in the tiling lattice, since otherwise \(e_i\) would violate uniqueness in a unit cube. Orthogonal projection has kernel \(2\mathbb Ze_i\), so the index halves and the projected determinant is \(4^{n-1}\). This validates the reduction in Lemma 2.3, conditional on the cited Hajós theorem. A rank-two check is \[H=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 H^tH=\begin{pmatrix}5&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \det(H^tH)=16.                                  \tag{3.2}\] A theta graph with path lengths \(2,2,3\) has these cycle pairings and \(2\cdot2+2\cdot3+2\cdot3=16\) spanning trees.

The irreducibility/rigidity calculation in Lemma 2.7 was checked in actual Hajós coordinates. The flow-lattice interpretation uses edge-disjoint decompositions of simple Eulerian subgraphs; sharing more than one vertex permits a new oriented cycle and prevents rigidity. In the planar argument, the pairwise-nesting and common-edge mechanism reduces an arbitrary 4-cycle to the decomposing configuration of Proposition 4.1. The overlap calculation gives inner product two with the last cycle; a conflicting overlap on both sides would produce an inner product at least three where the geometric intersection has only two edges. After correcting the coordinate permutation, no additional counterexample to this core step was established.

The composition deletes the two distinguished edges and introduces four new ones, so edge count rises by two. The flow rank is the sum of the factor ranks plus one. The lifted basis appends a last coordinate indicating whether a cycle used the distinguished edge, and the new cycle has square four. This is the block Hajós matrix construction in Lemma 4.3. The diagrammatic 0-handle/1-handle pair in Figure 3 has net Euler characteristic zero and supports the recursive \(\chi\)-ribbon construction. These are positive checks of the intended loopless classification, not an independent proof of the external Hajós or flyping theorems.

## Cobordism and rational-ball mechanism

For a cubiquitous embedding \(\Lambda_2\subset\Lambda_1\oplus\mathbb Z^n\) of index \(I\), determinant and rank give \[\underline{\det}(\Lambda_2)=\left(\frac I{2^n}\right)^2\underline{\det}(\Lambda_1).
 \tag{3.3}\] Equal normalized determinants imply \(I=2^n\), justifying the unique-point property \((\star)\) and the block-basis construction of Proposition 5.1.

The extension argument in Lemma 6.3 uses the actual quasi-ribbon surjectivity hypothesis: extend the incoming \(\operatorname{Spin}^c\) structure across the cobordism, choose a sharp outgoing extension, and glue. Rational homology cobordism preserves the appropriate correction term. The proof of Theorem 4 then uses an acyclic orientation to select a unique short characteristic vector in the graph factor. Within that characteristic coset, all short extensions differ only in the \(\{\pm1\}^n\) Euclidean coordinates, and translating by \((-1,\ldots,-1)\) and dividing by two gives the required \(\{0,1\}^n\) representatives. The corrected cut-reversal argument in Problem 3 preserves this step.

Lemma 7.2 also admits a direct homological check. Its stated handle decomposition has two 0-handles, \(n+1\) 1-handles and \(n\) 2-handles, giving \(\chi(W)=1\) and \(H_3(W;\mathbb Q)=0\). Since \(\partial W\) is a rational homology sphere, \[H_3(W;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H_3(W,\partial W;\mathbb Q)
 \longrightarrow H_2(\partial W;\mathbb Q)\] forces the middle group to vanish. Duality gives \(b_1(W)=0\), and Euler characteristic then gives \(b_2(W)=0\). Connectedness and the handle indices give the claimed ribbon rational ball. The inverse braid words and the two band moves in Figure 7 are consistent with the displayed three-component unlink. Mutation preserves the double cover; this argument does not assert that the original link itself is \(\chi\)-slice. Proposition 7.1 expressly provides counterevidence to that stronger inference.
