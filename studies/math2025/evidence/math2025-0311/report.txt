Abstract: We examine marginal preservation, the conditional logarithmic Sobolev argument, and Gaussian specializations in the specified preprint. A local quantitative discrepancy is established: the rate defined by the displayed formulas equals \(2\varepsilon(\alpha_U+\alpha_V)\) in the isotropic Gaussian case, whereas the subsequent first-order description gives one quarter of this coefficient. This is a Minor finding concerning the stated specialization, with no demonstrated damage to the principal convergence results. The review is claim-focused, not a verification of every proof.

# Assumptions and checks that survive

Theorem 1.1 assumes \(c,U,V\in C^2\), normalized densities \(e^{-U},e^{-V}\), subgaussian marginals, nonnegative \(c\) with bounded Hessian, Hessians of \(U,V\) bounded below, gradient moments of order \(p>2(d+1)\), and integrable \(U,V\). It quantifies over every initial coupling \(P_0\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). The initialization condition is essential; the authors separately identify nonstationary marginals as an open problem.

For a test function depending only on \(X\), the centered conditional force has zero expectation by the tower property. The resulting generator is \(\varepsilon(\Delta-\nabla U\cdot\nabla)\), preserving \(e^{-U}\); the same argument holds for \(Y\). This verifies the cancellation in (1.7) and Lemma 3.2, while the invoked Fokker–Planck uniqueness theorem remains an external dependency.

In the notation of Theorem 1.2, write \(A=\kappa^{X|Y}>0\), \(B=\kappa^{Y|X}>0\), and let \(\bar I\) denote the projected information. The conditional LSIs give \(I\ge(A+B)H\). The transport estimates used in (5.4) bound the squared conditional-mean discrepancies by \(4H/B\) and \(4H/A\), respectively. Consequently, \[\bar I\ge
\left(A+B-\frac4{\varepsilon^2}\left(\frac1A+\frac1B\right)\right)H
=\frac r\varepsilon H,
\quad
r=\varepsilon(A+B)\left(1-\frac4{\varepsilon^2AB}\right).\] Thus the coefficient in (1.19) agrees with the proof when \(\varepsilon\sqrt{AB}>2\). Combining this bound with the dissipation inequality (4.2) yields the stated exponential estimate. Sign reversals in conditional-mean expressions disappear under the squares and do not invalidate this argument. This check does not independently prove the external transport inequality or every weak-regularity passage.

For centered one-dimensional Gaussian marginals with variances \(u,v>0\), Itô’s formula gives \[\dot s=(u^{-1}+v^{-1})[uv-s(s+\varepsilon)].\] This agrees with the scalar specialization on p. 9. The negative root \(-\varepsilon/2-\sqrt{\varepsilon^2/4+uv}\) is outside the admissible covariance interval \([-\sqrt{uv},\sqrt{uv}]\) for \(\varepsilon>0\). The limiting case \(\varepsilon=0\) cannot be substituted into the positive-noise uniqueness assertion.

# A local quantitative finding

Problem 1 (Gaussian rate coefficient; Minor).  The paragraph following (1.24), p. 7, describes the first-order rate defined by (1.19) and (1.23) as \(\varepsilon(\alpha_U+\alpha_V)/2\). For the admissible isotropic Gaussian family this coefficient is incorrect.

## Hypotheses and decisive calculation

Equation reference: eq:correct. Take any \(a,b,\varepsilon>0\), any \(d\ge1\), \[\mu=N(0,a^{-1}I_d),\qquad \nu=N(0,b^{-1}I_d),\qquad
c(x,y)=\tfrac12|x-y|^2.\] The normalized potentials are quadratic with Hessians \(aI_d,bI_d\). All smoothness, subgaussian, lower-Hessian, gradient-moment and integrability hypotheses hold, and Corollary 1.3 allows \(\alpha_U=\beta_U=a\), \(\alpha_V=\beta_V=b\). Let \[q=\sqrt{1+\frac4{\varepsilon^2ab}}>1.\] The constants in (1.23) are exactly \(A=a(q+1)\) and \(B=b(q+1)\). Because \(q^2-1=4/(\varepsilon^2ab)\), \[\begin{aligned}
1-\frac4{\varepsilon^2AB}
 &=1-\frac{q^2-1}{(q+1)^2}=\frac2{q+1},\\
r&=\varepsilon(a+b)(q+1)\frac2{q+1}
  =2\varepsilon(a+b). \end{aligned}\] This is an identity for every \(\varepsilon>0\), not a numerical asymptotic fit. Also \(\varepsilon\sqrt{AB}
=\varepsilon\sqrt{ab}(q+1)>2\), verifying the strict assumption of Theorem 1.2. In particular \(a=b=1\) gives \(r=4\varepsilon\), while the prose expression gives \(\varepsilon\). Therefore \(\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}r/\varepsilon=2(a+b)\).

## Counterevidence, dependency, and correction boundary

The inspected source image confirms the factor \(1/2\); this is not a text-extraction artifact. If the sentence merely intended the order \(r=\Theta(\varepsilon)\), that order is correct. A rate four times smaller is also a valid conservative rate when the stronger estimate is valid. Neither interpretation makes it the first-order coefficient of the specifically defined \(r\).

The discrepancy lies in the explanatory specialization following the corollary. It is not used in the proof of the projected LSI, in the proof of the corollary, or in the qualitative convergence reduction inspected here. No core conclusion is shown to fail; hence Major is unsupported. The precise local repair is to replace that first-order coefficient by \(2\varepsilon(\alpha_U+\alpha_V)\) under the stated isotropic Gaussian equalities, where it is exact. No change to (1.19), (1.23), or their positivity condition is justified by this calculation. No claim of author adoption or first discovery is made.
