Abstract: This initial review identifies one supported Major issue. The equality of group classes in Lemma 6.2 is false, including within the countable, torsion-free range of Theorem 1.6. A self-contained counterexample and a precise class-theoretic repair are given below. The defect leaves a missing directed-union step in the stated proof of Theorems 1.6–1.7; it does not refute their Atiyah or localization conclusions. The extension of the complex finite-rank argument to arbitrary conjugation-closed coefficient fields remains a separate Needs Evidence item. Initial review is not independent acceptance, and the scientific decision remains pending.

# Identity, scope, and claims

The assigned identity is Pablo Sánchez-Peralta, Universal Localizations, Atiyah Conjectures and Graphs of Groups, DOI `10.1007/s00039-025-00710-4`. The reviewed file is [paper], arXiv:2409.12268v2, dated 23 March 2025, with 30 physical PDF pages. The original supplied manifest and the preserved source copy identify these exact bytes. All 30 pages were read. Page numbers in this report refer to that PDF, not to the journal pagination. The accompanying coverage ledger records the proof-level checks, original-reference passages, and actual page-image inspections. No byte-equivalence with the journal PDF is claimed.

Theorems 1.1–1.2 concern a countable fundamental group of a graph of groups, finite edge groups, a field \(K\subseteq\mathbb C\) closed under complex conjugation, strong Atiyah assumptions on every vertex group, and finite \(\operatorname{lcm}(G)\). Besides preservation of the dimension restriction, the paper asserts faithfulness of the induced rank on finitely generated projectives, universal localization at full maps of projectives, and the two indicated \(K_0\)-surjections. Full maps are defined by factorization through arbitrary finitely generated projectives; they are not restricted to square matrices between free modules. Corollaries 1.3–1.4 address the algebraic and center-valued Atiyah conjectures.

Definition 6.1 explicitly defines \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\) by finite groups, unrestricted directed unions, and the graph-of-groups operation when its output is virtually locally indicable. Theorem 1.6 asserts semisimplicity and the \(\Sigma(K,G)\)-universal localization property of \(\mathcal R_{K[G]}\), together with the algebraic Atiyah conjecture, for every countable \(G\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\) with finite \(\operatorname{lcm}(G)\). Here \(\Sigma(K,G)\) consists of matrices over \(K[G]\) invertible in \(\mathcal U(G)\). Theorem 1.7 uses this to compute weak \(K_1\) over a conjugation-closed ring \(\mathbb Z\subseteq R\subseteq\mathbb C\), with its fraction field \(F\). The broader conjectures in Section 7 are not treated as proved results.

# Problem 1: the class equality in Lemma 6.2 is false

Severity: Major. Location: Definition 6.1, the definition of \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\), and Lemma 6.2, p. 26; the first sentence of the proof of Theorem 1.6, p. 27; its subsequent use in Theorem 1.7, p. 28.

Every member of the paper’s class \(\mathcal Y=\bigcup_\alpha\mathcal Y_\alpha\) is virtually locally indicable: finite groups have the trivial subgroup of finite index, successor stages explicitly impose this property, and a limit stage is a union of classes, not a new operation of taking unions of groups. In contrast, the directed-union operation in Definition 6.1 has no such restriction. The claim that \(\mathcal Y\) is closed under directed unions therefore requires proof and is false.

The group \(\bigoplus_{n\geq1}C_2\) already separates the two classes, but has infinite \(\operatorname{lcm}\). The following construction avoids that limitation and meets the group and coefficient hypotheses of Theorem 1.6.

## A torsion-free, virtually abelian group which is not locally indicable

Write an affine map of \(\mathbb R^3\) as \((D,v)\), with product \((D,v)(E,w)=(DE,Dw+v)\). Let \[a=\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1),(1/2,1/2,0)\bigr),\qquad
 b=\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1),(0,1/2,1/2)\bigr)\] and put \(H=\langle a,b\rangle\), \(c=ab\). For translation by \(v\) write \(T_v\). Direct exact calculation gives \[\begin{aligned}
 c&=\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1),(1/2,0,-1/2)\bigr),\\
 x=a^2&=T_{(1,0,0)},&
 y=b^2&=T_{(0,1,0)},&
 z=c^{-2}&=T_{(0,0,1)},\\
 [a,b]&=xy^{-1}z^{-1},&
 bxb^{-1}&=x^{-1},&
 aya^{-1}&=y^{-1}.\end{aligned}\] The subgroup \(L=\langle x,y,z\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^3\) is normal: conjugation of a translation by \(a\) or \(b\) changes only coordinate signs. Modulo \(L\), the generators commute and have order two. Their four distinct linear parts show that \[H/L\cong C_2\times C_2,\qquad H=L\sqcup La\sqcup Lb\sqcup Lc.\] There are no additional translation cosets.

An element of \(L\setminus\{1\}\) has infinite order. For \(\ell=T_{(p,q,r)}\), \(p,q,r\in\mathbb Z\), the squares of \(\ell a,\ell b,\ell c\) have respectively a translation coordinate \(2p+1\), \(2q+1\), or \(2r-1\), so each is nontrivial. Thus \(H\) is torsion-free and virtually \(\mathbb Z^3\), hence virtually locally indicable.

Nevertheless, if \(f:H\to\mathbb Z\) is a homomorphism, conjugation of \(x\) to \(x^{-1}\) gives \(f(x)=0\), and \(x=a^2\) gives \(f(a)=0\). Similarly, \(aya^{-1}=y^{-1}\) and \(y=b^2\) give \(f(b)=0\). Therefore every such \(f\) is zero. The nontrivial, finitely generated group \(H\) is not locally indicable.

## Membership in the class actually defined in the paper

Equation reference: eq:amalgam. Projection to the first affine coordinate maps \(H\) onto the infinite dihedral group. The images of \(b\) and \(c\) are reflections about \(0\) and \(1/4\), and the kernel is \(L_0=\langle y,z\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\). The two vertex preimages are \[B=\langle b,z\mid bzb^{-1}=z^{-1}\rangle,\qquad
 C=\langle c,y\mid cyc^{-1}=y^{-1}\rangle.\] Both are Klein-bottle groups. In \(B\), \(y=b^2\); in \(C\), \(z=c^{-2}\). The subgroup \(L_0\) has index two in each, and \[
 H\cong B*_{L_0}C .\] For completeness, this map is surjective since \(a=cb^{-1}\). A reduced word alternating between the two vertex groups outside \(L_0\) maps to a nonempty reduced word in \(C_2*C_2\); a nontrivial word in \(L_0\) remains a nontrivial translation. This proves injectivity.

The group \(\mathbb Z\) is an HNN extension of the trivial group. The identity HNN extension of \(\mathbb Z\) gives \(\mathbb Z^2\), and its inversion HNN extension gives the Klein-bottle group. Each output is virtually free abelian. Equation [eq:amalgam] (#eq:amalgam) therefore proves \(H\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\).

The same argument gives \(H^n\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\) for every finite \(n\). Inductively put \(P=H^{n-1}\). Identity HNN extensions construct \(P\times\mathbb Z\) and \(P\times\mathbb Z^2\); an HNN extension with the identity on \(P\) and inversion on \(\mathbb Z\) constructs \(P\) times a Klein-bottle group. Finally, \[P\times H\cong
 (P\times B)*_{P\times L_0}(P\times C).\] All outputs in this finite construction are virtually free abelian, so the additional hypothesis on each graph operation is satisfied.

## The finite-\(\operatorname{lcm}\) counterexample

Set \[G=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}H=\bigcup_{n\geq1}H^n .\] Then \(G\) is countable, torsion-free, belongs to \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\), and has \(\operatorname{lcm}(G)=1\). Take \(K=\mathbb Q\), which is closed under complex conjugation.

We show that no finite-index subgroup \(J\leq G\) is locally indicable. Its normal core \(N\) has finite index, and the quotient \(Q=G/N\) is finite. There are only finitely many homomorphisms \(H\to Q\), because \(H\) has two generators. Infinitely many coordinate copies of \(H\) therefore have the same restriction \(\rho:H\to Q\) of the quotient map. Let \(m\geq1\) be the exponent of the finite group \(\rho(H)\). Choose \(m\) distinct such coordinates. The diagonal copy of \(H\) on these coordinates maps to \(\rho(h)^m=1\) for every \(h\), so it lies in \(N\subseteq J\). It is a nontrivial finitely generated subgroup without an epimorphism onto \(\mathbb Z\). Consequently \(J\) is not locally indicable.

Thus \(G\notin\mathcal Y\), although it satisfies the hypotheses \(G\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\), countability, and finite \(\operatorname{lcm}(G)\). This is a counterexample to Lemma 6.2 within the parameter range used by the main application. The affine identities were also checked independently with exact rational arithmetic. The infinite-group argument above is analytic; it is not inferred from a finite enumeration or from author code.

## Impact and limits

The proof of Theorem 1.6 explicitly reduces all \(G\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\) to the classes \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\) using Lemma 6.2. That reduction misses the group just constructed and, more generally, groups produced by unrestricted directed unions which cease to be virtually locally indicable. Theorem 1.7 inherits this gap through its use of Theorem 1.6. This is a supported failure in the derivation of a main stated scope, which warrants Major under the frozen review calibration.

The counterexample does not say that the Atiyah or localization conclusions fail for \(G\). It does not affect the finite-edge amalgam/HNN arguments merely by refuting the separate class lemma. A restriction of the statements to \(\mathcal Y\) would avoid this particular counterexample, but would narrow the class stated in Definition 6.1. The repair below can instead retain that definition.

# A precise repair and its verification boundary

Equation reference: eq:repair. Define \(\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal Y)\) to be the groups whose nontrivial finitely generated subgroups belong to \(\mathcal Y\). The correct class identity is \[
 \mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}=\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal Y).\] Here is a proof, independent of the incorrect directed-union claim.

First, each \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\) is subgroup-closed. This is immediate at stage zero. At a successor stage, a subgroup acts on the original Bass–Serre tree; its vertex and edge stabilizers are subgroups of conjugates of the original stabilizers, and remain locally in the previous class. A subgroup of a virtually locally indicable group is virtually locally indicable, by intersection with a locally indicable subgroup of finite index. Limit stages follow directly. Moreover, the stages are increasing: a member of \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\) is locally in \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\), and its one-vertex graph puts it in \(\mathcal Y_{\alpha+1}\).

Induction also gives \(\mathcal Y\subseteq\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\), because a group locally in \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\) is the directed union of its finitely generated subgroups. Hence \(\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal Y)\subseteq\mathcal T_{\mathrm{VLI}}\). Conversely, \(\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal Y)\) contains the finite groups and is closed under directed unions: a finite generating set lies in one member of the directed system. Suppose a virtually locally indicable group is the fundamental group of a graph with vertex and edge groups in \(\operatorname{Loc}(\mathcal Y)\). The finitely generated subgroups of all these vertex and edge groups form a set. Choose one ordinal bounding their \(\mathcal Y\)-ranks. Every vertex and edge group is then locally in that single stage, so the fundamental group belongs to the next stage of \(\mathcal Y\). Minimality in Definition 6.1 proves the other inclusion in [eq:repair] (#eq:repair).

Theorem 1.6 must then begin with a directed-union permanence step, before its induction on \(\mathcal Y_\alpha\). The required checks are concrete. If \(G=\bigcup_iG_i\) is directed, then \[K[G]=\bigcup_iK[G_i],\qquad
 \mathcal R_{K[G]}=\bigcup_i\mathcal R_{K[G_i]}.\] The second equality follows by the finite expression defining each element of the star-regular closure and uniqueness of relative inverses under the subgroup embedding. For a square matrix over \(K[G_i]\), von Neumann dimension induction gives invertibility in \(\mathcal U(G_i)\) exactly when it is invertible in \(\mathcal U(G)\). Consequently \(\Sigma(K,G)\) is the directed union of the corresponding finite-stage matrix sets, and their universal properties give the localization assertion at the limit.

Finite \(\operatorname{lcm}(G)\) ensures \(\operatorname{lcm}(G_i)\mid\operatorname{lcm}(G)\); the strong Atiyah dimension restriction therefore passes to the union and gives semisimplicity by the paper’s regular-closure criterion. For the algebraic assertion, a \(K_0\)-class over the union is represented by finite matrix data and descends to one stage. Surjectivity from finite subgroup rings passes to the union. These observations identify the missing argument without assuming that the union remains virtually locally indicable.

Equation [eq:repair] (#eq:repair) and the counterexample have been checked here. The proposed corrected proof still uses the localization, dimension, and subgroup functoriality results employed elsewhere in the paper. This initial report does not certify a revised manuscript or claim author adoption. In particular, the separate coefficient-field question below is not resolved by repairing the class identity.

# Other proof chains and boundary checks

## Finite-edge amalgams

The definition of a full map, the right-exact cokernel argument in Lemma 2.13, and the split kernel/cokernel argument in Lemma 2.14 were checked with the dimension normalizations on pp. 11–12. Epimorphy of the star-regular closure agrees with the original multiplication-isomorphism proof in [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~24--25]{basechange}].

For \(G=A*_CB\), the normal-form deletion operator in Lemma 3.1 commutes with the right \(B\)-action. Its right-\(A\) commutator is the finite-\(C\) projection expression printed on pp. 13–14. The trace calculation in Lemma 3.2 uses cyclicity with finite-rank operators and ultimately averages over \(C\): \[\sum_{c\in C}T\cdot c=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T=\frac1{|C|}\sum_{c\ne1}T\cdot(1-c).\] The division by \(|C|\) and the finite projection are substantive uses of the finite-edge hypothesis. If one vertex equals the edge group, the associated intersection identity reduces directly to the obvious equality.

The Mayer–Vietoris result [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~9--10, Theorem 6]{dicks}] requires lifting, for example flatness, over the vertex rings. The regular vertex rings in Proposition 3.3 satisfy this condition. The complex tensor identification with finite-rank operators then gives the stated Tor vanishing over \(\mathbb C\). Bergman’s actual induced-submodule theorem and projective decomposition [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~3--5, 21--23]{bergman}] have a semisimple amalgamating ring, as supplied here by \(\mathbb C[C]\). They support the hereditary and dimension arguments in Theorem 3.4.

One implicit scalar-extension step can be supplied. Write \(S=\mathcal R_{\mathbb C[G]}\) and \(R_\Gamma\) for the graph ring. Since \(R_\Gamma\to S\) is epic, \(S\otimes_{R_\Gamma}S\cong S\). Since \(S\) is regular, the inclusion \(S\subseteq\mathcal U(G)\) is pure as a right \(S\)-module map: its quotient is flat. Thus \(S\otimes_{R_\Gamma}M\) injects into \(\mathcal U(G)\otimes_{R_\Gamma}M\). Let \(\overline M\) be the image of \(M\) in these modules. The map \(S\otimes_{R_\Gamma}M\to S\otimes_{R_\Gamma}\overline M\) is surjective by right exactness. Its composite with the extended inclusion of \(\overline M\) is an isomorphism by epimorphy, so it is itself an isomorphism. Extending further to \(\mathcal U(G)\) justifies the replacement by \(\overline M\) on pp. 17–18. The induced vertex summands then give dimensions in \(\operatorname{lcm}(G)^{-1}\mathbb Z\), using the vertex denominator restrictions.

## HNN extensions, limits, and stable induction

The kernel of the stable-letter map \(G\to\mathbb Z\) is the tree amalgam of conjugates of the vertex group. The increasing finite amalgams, the skew Laurent description, and the Tor transfer on pp. 20–23 were checked. In Lemma 4.1, semisimplicity gives noetherianity, and the star-closed crossed product is semiprime. Goldie’s theorem alone does not prove that its regular elements are invertible in \(\mathcal U(G)\); the additional analytic input is indeed supplied by [Retained latex: \cite[physical pp.~68--71, Corollary 8.16 and
Proposition 8.17]{reich}]. Their constant-term and semisimple hypotheses match the application.

A \(K_0\)-surjection supplies stable induction, not in general an unstabilized decomposition. This suffices for Proposition 2.16. Explicitly, from \([Q]=[S\otimes P_1]-[S\otimes P_2]\), choose a projective complement \(P'_2\) with \(P_2\oplus P'_2\cong R^m\). Equality in \(K_0(S)\) gives a stable isomorphism; adding a complement to its stabilizing projective yields \[Q\oplus S^{m+n}\cong S\otimes_R(P_1\oplus P'_2\oplus R^n).\] Thus the shorthand on p. 22 need not create a separate defect.

Similarly, faithfulness of the rank on projectives is supported by the actual induced-projective decomposition and positivity at vertices, rather than by a \(K_0\)-surjection alone. Countability of the fundamental group need not force countability of an arbitrarily redundant underlying graph. The exhaustion on pp. 23–25 can be taken over the directed set of finite connected subgraphs. All idempotents, maps, and their relations involve finite data. This supplies the same limit argument without a countability assumption on the graph itself.

## The locally indicable and weak-\(K_1\) inputs

The original locally indicable theorem and the Hughes-free coefficient-field consequence are available in [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~2--3, 30--31, 43]{locally}]. The division ring is therefore the appropriate one for the locally indicable subgroup used in Section 6. The graph-of-division-rings result [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~7, 14, 24, Lemma 7.6]{fisher}] covers general graphs, rather than only a two-vertex amalgam. Its proof uses the semifir property, universality, and maximality of the Hughes-free rank.

For Theorem 1.7, the relevant general statement is [Retained latex: \cite[p.~3, Theorem 1.2]{weak}]: the localization map must be injective and the inverted maps must be endomorphisms of finitely generated projectives. Square matrices over \(R[G]\) meet the latter condition, and the claimed realization inside \(\mathcal U(G)\) supplies injectivity once Theorem 1.6 has been established over \(F\). Nonzero coefficients from \(R\) are among the weakly invertible one-by-one matrices, so the passage to \(F\) is compatible with this localization. The regular closure is rationally closed in \(\mathcal U(G)\). After semisimplicity, Morita invariance and the Dieudonné determinant give the stated product of abelianized division-ring unit groups. This check does not remove the dependence on the scope of Theorem 1.6.

# Needs Evidence and limits of verification

## The coefficient-field passage

The assertions preceding Proposition 3.3, p. 15, at the beginning of Section 4, p. 20, and their use in Section 5, p. 23, extend the proof to every conjugation-closed \(K\subseteq\mathbb C\). The displayed operator identification on pp. 16–17 is over \(\mathbb C\). Replacing its tensor symbol by \(\otimes_K\) is not an isomorphism to complex finite-rank operators.

For example, with \(K=\mathbb Q\), the tensor \[i\delta_e\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\delta_e-
 \delta_e\otimes_{\mathbb Q}i\delta_e\] is nonzero. On the rational span of \(\delta_e,i\delta_e\), take coordinate functionals \(f,g\) with \(f(\delta_e)=g(i\delta_e)=1\) and \(f(i\delta_e)=g(\delta_e)=0\), and extend them rationally. Their tensor product evaluates the displayed tensor to \(-1\). Its image under the complex-bilinear rank-one operator map is zero. The original operator argument in [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~9--11]{sylvester}] also uses the complex tensor product.

This proves only that a mechanical coefficient substitution is insufficient. It is not a counterexample to Tor vanishing over \(K\), or to the strong Atiyah conclusion over \(K\). A coefficient-specific argument, or a descent theorem with its hypotheses checked, would settle this item. No soficity assumption may silently be added. This remains Needs Evidence; it is not counted as a second supported Major Problem.
