Abstract: The annular-cover projection estimate used in the main modulus argument is false with its printed constant. An explicit flat-torus example satisfies the auxiliary estimate’s hypotheses. A corrected bound, with a factor two only on the discrete term, repairs both downstream uses and preserves the final contradiction. Two local corrections concern weak admissibility, squared norms, and an unused collar formula. No counterexample to the main nonexistence theorem is established. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# A false circular-projection estimate

Problem 1 (Major: one-Lipschitz projection does not give (14)).  In the proof of Lemma 5.4, pp. 16–17, the annular cover is projected orthogonally onto its closed geodesic axis \(\widehat\gamma\). Equation (14) asserts \[\ell(\widehat\gamma)\le
 \ell_{\widehat S}(\widehat\gamma')
 =
 \ell(\widehat\gamma'\cap\widehat S)
 +\sum_{\widehat D\cap\widehat\gamma'\ne\varnothing}
      \operatorname{diam}(\widehat D).\] The diameter is the ambient annular-cover diameter. A one-Lipschitz map controls diameter, but a connected subset of a circle can have length up to twice its circular diameter. Contractibility of the domain does not prevent its projection from covering the circle.

## A counterexample with all local hypotheses

Take the flat unit torus \(M=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2\) and the horizontal primitive geodesic \(\gamma\), of length one. Its annular cover is \((\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb R\). In the universal plane let \[K=\{(s,s/10):-1/100\le s\le101/100\},\qquad w=1/1000,\] and let \(D\) be the projection to \(M\) of the open \(w\)-neighborhood of \(K\).

The closed capsule is disjoint from every nontrivial integral translate. Its horizontal span is \(1.022<2\), and its vertical span is \[H=0.102+2w=13/125<1.\] Vertical translates are therefore disjoint. For the only possible nonzero horizontal translates, the containing parallel lines are separated by \(1/\sqrt{101}>2w\). Thus the projection is injective on the closed capsule, and \(\overline D\) is an embedded closed disk. At \(p=(1/2,1/20)\) the domain contains \(B(p,w)\) and lies in \(B(p,3/5)\): circular horizontal distance is at most \(1/2\) and vertical distance at most \(H/2\), so \[\frac14+(H/2)^2<(3/5)^2.\] It satisfies bounded geometry with constant \(600\). If an infinite collection is desired, add pairwise closure-disjoint round disks of shrinking radii in an open region disjoint from this domain and the curve below. Their geometry constant is one; the resulting complement \(S\) is compact. Density is not a hypothesis of Lemma 5.4.

Let \(\gamma'\) follow \((s,s/10)\) for \(0\le s\le1\) and then close by the vertical interval at \(x=0\) from \(y=1/10\) to \(0\) on the torus. It is a simple rectifiable curve in the horizontal primitive class. The slanted portion lies inside \(D\); the portion outside \(D\) has length at most \(1/10\). The complement of \(\overline D\) along the curve is one vertical open arc. Adding that arc to \(\overline D\) contains the essential horizontal loop, so there is exactly one essential intersection. No added round disk meets \(\gamma'\).

Equation reference: eq:counter. The domain lift \(\widehat D\) met by the lifted curve has vertical span \(H\) and circular horizontal distance at most \(1/2\). Hence \[\operatorname{diam}(\widehat D)\le
 \sqrt{\frac14+H^2}
 =\frac{\sqrt{16301}}{250}<\frac{511}{1000}.\] It is the only lift met by this curve in the annular cover. Consequently \[\ell_{\widehat S}(\widehat\gamma')
 \le\frac1{10}+\frac{\sqrt{16301}}{250}
 <\frac{611}{1000}<1=\ell(\widehat\gamma).
 \] This disproves (14) under the auxiliary estimate’s own assumptions. It does not construct a diffeomorphism satisfying the globally forbidden hypotheses of Theorem 1.4.

## Corrected estimate and modulus decay

Equation reference: eq:fixed. If \(A\) is connected in a geodesic circle, then \(\mathcal H^1(A)\le2\operatorname{diam}(A)\): an arc of length at most half the circumference has length equal to its diameter; a longer arc, or the whole circle, has diameter half the circumference. The projected domain lift is connected. Applying this fact and one-Lipschitz projection to the covering of the axis gives \[\ell(\widehat\gamma)\le
 \ell(\widehat\gamma'\cap\widehat S)
 +2\sum_{\widehat D\cap\widehat\gamma'\ne\varnothing}
       \operatorname{diam}(\widehat D).
 \] The continuous term needs no factor two.

Equation reference: eq:base. The source’s uniform lift-comparison constant \(c\in(0,1)\) and the at-most-\(N\) lift count now yield \[\ell(\gamma)\le
 \ell(\gamma'\cap S)+\frac{2N}{c}
     \sum_{D_k\cap\gamma'\ne\varnothing}\operatorname{diam}(D_k)
 \le\frac{2N}{c}\ell_S(\gamma').
 \] Let \(\rho\) be one on \(S\) and \(\rho(D_k)=\operatorname{diam}(D_k)\). Disjoint inner balls and bounded geometry imply \(\sum_k\operatorname{diam}(D_k)^2<\infty\), so \(\operatorname{mass}(\rho)<\infty\). The distribution \(2N\rho/(c\ell(\gamma))\) is admissible and gives \[\operatorname{mod}_S(\Gamma)\le
 \frac{4N^2\operatorname{mass}(\rho)}{c^2\ell(\gamma)^2}.\] This proves the stated conclusion of Lemma 5.4 with a changed constant. The constant remains independent of the iterated geodesic class. Pseudo-Anosov length growth therefore still forces the upper bound to zero, contradicting the fixed positive modulus given by conformality on \(S\).

An alternative repair retains diameters of universal-cover lifts throughout: their projection onto the axis line has length at most their diameter. The preceding paragraph of the original proof already provides the requisite uniform comparison. Either repair requires changing the printed reasoning.

## Why the same repair preserves the final variation

Equation reference: eq:qcompetitor. The second use is Proposition 7.2, pp. 20–21, in the quadratic differential metric. Its universal cover has cone angles at least \(2\pi\) and the usual nonpositive-curvature projection property. The same argument applies, with a uniform comparison constant \(c_q\) and axis length normalized to one. An admissible competitor can therefore be chosen as \[\rho_1|_S=|q|^{1/2},\qquad
 \rho_1(D_k)=\frac{2N}{c_q}\operatorname{diam}_q(D_k).
 \] Its discrete mass is finite because the \(q\) metric is bounded above by a constant times the smooth metric. The continuous part is unchanged. This matters: multiplying that part by two would not preserve the strict area comparison used in the proof. The corrected estimate [eq:fixed] (#eq:fixed) requires the factor only on the discrete weights and supplies exactly the needed competitor.

Thus the defect is an invalid central derivation with a verified local repair. The conclusions of Lemma 5.4, Proposition 7.2 and Theorem 1.4 have not been refuted.

# Norms, exceptional curves, and the final contradiction

Problem 2 (Minor: Lemma 7.3 needs squared norms and almost-everywhere admissibility).  Pages 20–21 identify \(L^2\) norms with mass or \(q\)-area, although the definition on p. 8 makes mass the squared norm. They also state the admissibility inequality for every horizontal trajectory, whereas the definition allows a family of classical 2-modulus zero.

The correct identities are \[\|\rho_1|_S\|_2^2=\operatorname{area}_q(S),\qquad
 \|\rho_0\|_2^2=\operatorname{mass}(\rho_0)\] when \(\rho_0\) is purely continuous. For example, a unit density on a set of area four has norm two and mass four. An \(L^2\) representative can be modified on one horizontal circle without changing its class; that circle has 2-modulus zero. Hence pointwise admissibility on every height does not follow from weak admissibility.

Here the required inequality has a full repair. Use the single-curve Jenkins–Strebel cylinder of circumference one and height \(b\); its complement is a critical graph of \(q\)-area zero. A family of horizontal circles indexed by heights in a measurable set \(E\) has 2-modulus \(|E|\), by circlewise Cauchy–Schwarz and the density equal to one on those circles. In particular a zero-modulus exceptional family has a null set of heights. The same conclusion for arbitrary exceptional height sets follows by enclosing them in the measurable superlevel sets of arbitrarily small-energy admissible densities.

Equation reference: eq:gap. Extend \(\rho_0\) by zero off \(S\), and express its density in the flat cylinder coordinates. For almost every height \(y\) its horizontal integral is at least one. Cauchy–Schwarz on the unit circle and Fubini give \[\operatorname{mass}(\rho_0)\ge b=\operatorname{area}_q(C_q)>\operatorname{area}_q(S)
   =\|\rho_1|_S\|_2^2 .
 \] The strict inequality uses a nonempty open complementary domain: \(q\) has only isolated zeros, so that domain has positive \(q\)-area. The full-area single-cylinder property is the specific Jenkins–Strebel input used here; it is stronger than merely having some closed trajectories.

Equation reference: eq:variation. Write \(\rho_0=(u,0)\) and \(\rho_1=(v,d)\) in \(L^2(S)\oplus\ell^2\). The exact variation is \[\begin{aligned}
 &\operatorname{mass}((1-t)\rho_0+t\rho_1)-\operatorname{mass}(\rho_0) \notag\\
 &\qquad=2t\bigl(\langle u,v\rangle-\|u\|^2\bigr)
  +t^2\bigl(\|v-u\|^2+\|d\|^2\bigr).
 \end{aligned}\] By [eq:gap] (#eq:gap), \(\langle u,v\rangle\le\|u\|\|v\|<\|u\|^2\). The quadratic coefficient is finite even after the discrete weights in [eq:qcompetitor] (#eq:qcompetitor) are doubled. For small positive \(t\) the variation is negative, contradicting extremality. Convex combinations remain admissible outside the union of their two zero-modulus exceptional families. This proves the needed contradiction with the corrected norm identities and quantifiers.

If \(S\) has area zero, a purely continuous distribution is already zero as a Hilbert-space element and contradicts positive transboundary modulus directly. No separate positive-area hypothesis is required.

# An unused numerical collar formula

Problem 3 (Minor: the displayed width can be nonreal).  On p. 15 the collar width is written \(d=\operatorname{arccosh}(\operatorname{coth}(\ell(\gamma))/2)\). For \(\ell(\gamma)=1\) the argument is less than one, so this is not a real positive width. More generally it is less than one whenever \(\ell(\gamma)>(\log3)/2\).

The proof needs no explicit width. A smooth simple closed geodesic has a sufficiently small embedded annular neighborhood. Choose one and then a relatively compact conformal subannulus, as the text already does. On that subannulus the required derivative bounds and uniform lengths hold. Deleting the incorrect numerical expression fully repairs this local use without changing Corollary 5.2.

# Other proof and boundary checks

## Distortion and invariant conformal structure

In Lemma 2.1 the rescaled limit \(\Omega\) contains a unit disk and lies in a disk of radius \(C\). Its image by \(A=D_pf^n\) contains a disk of radius \(r'\) and lies in a concentric disk of radius \(Cr'\). If \(a\ge b>0\) are the singular values of \(A\), comparison of widths gives \(a\le Cr'\) and \(r'\le Cb\), hence \(a/b\le C^2\). Taking the shrinking-domain limit separately for each fixed iterate is sufficient; the differentiability remainder need not be uniform over all iterates.

The Beltrami composition law acts by hyperbolic disk isometries. Countability of the group permits one common conull set, and the bounded orbit’s circumcenter transforms equivariantly. Measurability follows from finite-subset circumcenters and their limit. Extending the resulting form by zero and applying measurable uniformization produces conformality on the residual set. The quasiconformal conjugacy preserves bounded geometry through its quasisymmetry on the compact surfaces. An orientation-reversing map may first be replaced by its square; all wandering-domain conditions survive.

## Existence, uniqueness, and conformal invariance of modulus

The nonnegative cone in \(L^2\oplus\ell^2\) is weakly closed. Mazur combinations of a minimizing sequence converge strongly and remain weakly admissible after deleting a countable union of exceptional families. With error at most \(2^{-K}\), the tail densities \(\sum_{K\ge i}K|\rho_K-\rho|\) have norms tending to zero and control the bad limsup family in Proposition 3.1. That family contains no constant curve: for a point in a domain, the single coordinate error is eventually less than \(1/K\); at a point of \(S\) the length contribution is zero. Thus Corollary 3.6 applies with its nonconstant-curve hypothesis. Strict convexity yields uniqueness as a Hilbert-space element.

The transfer from classical to transboundary modulus in Lemma 3.4 uses the positive diameter lower bound as stated. Its disk enlargement estimate is applied to the disjoint inner balls, and compact-surface area bounds give the required squared-radius comparison. In Lemma 3.7 one removes the quasiconformal absolute-continuity exceptional family before pulling back line integrals. On \(S\), conformality gives \(|Df|^2=J_f\) almost everywhere, so change of variables preserves the continuous mass; the domain permutation preserves the discrete mass. Applying the same reasoning to the inverse proves equality of moduli.

## Topology and the two mapping-class branches

The essential-intersection count is controlled by a positive uniform neighborhood scale. Small domains use a common local contractibility scale; the finitely many large contractible closures use the minimum of their individual scales. Each essential excursion costs a fixed positive length. The cyclic lift may be based outside the domain, or counted directly in the annular cover, to avoid splitting a single block at the parameter endpoints. Homeomorphisms preserve the count, so the same \(N\) works for all iterates of the original collar family.

For the \(q\)-length-one trajectories in Section 7, apply that same compact-neighborhood/excursion proof directly in the \(q\) length metric. It induces the original topology and has only finitely many cone singularities. One must not infer a bound on smooth length by reversing the one-sided metric comparison on p. 18. This direct metric version supplies the required uniform \(N\).

The pseudo-Anosov branch uses the corrected modulus bound and the cited growth of shortest representatives. In the periodic or reducible branch, pass to an iterate fixing the selected curve class. The family \(\Gamma_N\) is invariant. Uniqueness of its extremal distribution and conformal pullback force each discrete weight to be constant along its domain orbit. Every such orbit is infinite, so square summability forces all discrete weights to vanish. The repaired variation [eq:variation] (#eq:variation) then rules out the remaining purely continuous extremal distribution.

The sphere is expressly excluded from Theorem 1.4. The dilation example does not contradict that theorem. The stated Denjoy-product boundary example uses the applicable \(C^{1+\alpha}\) range \(0<\alpha<1\) and loses bounded geometry; it is not a claim of a \(C^2\) circle Denjoy example. The review found no additional counterexample to the nonexistence conclusion.
