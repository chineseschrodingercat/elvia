Abstract: Three substantive but locally confined corrections are supported: the all-\(q\) gradient and pressure membership in Theorem 9.5 is false on general planar Lipschitz domains; a fixed-ball Poincare assertion in Remark 2.3 fails for large-slope graphs; and Theorem A.1 omits the necessary boundary-flux condition. An explicit corner construction verifies the first defect with bounded solenoidal forcing. Each finding is classified as Minor because its demonstrated effect is confined to the stated auxiliary or secondary assertion. The principal bounded-velocity resolvent estimates are not refuted. The report also supplies a finite-\(q\) completion of the exterior uniqueness argument.

# 0118-F1: excessive regularity in Theorem 9.5

## Exact claim and why the proof does not establish it

On PDF p. 39, Theorem 9.5 takes any bounded Lipschitz \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\), any \(1<q<\infty\), and \(F\in L^q_\sigma(\Omega)\). It asserts a solution in \(W^{1,q}_0(\Omega)\times L^q(\Omega)\), with mean-zero pressure, as well as the velocity estimate (9.13). Its proof interpolates the \(L^2\) and \(L^\infty\) velocity estimates and then uses duality. That reasoning does not supply the asserted gradient or pressure membership. The following construction shows that the extra membership is actually false.

## A homogeneous no-slip corner solution

Set \(\omega=3\pi/2\). There is a unique \(\nu\in(1/2,3/5)\) such that \[\sin(\omega\nu)=\nu.\] Indeed, the left side minus \(\nu\) is positive at \(1/2\), negative at \(3/5\), and strictly decreasing on this interval. Let \(c=\cos(\omega\nu)\), and for \(0<\vartheta<\omega\) set \[\begin{split}
 \Phi(\vartheta)
  ={}&(\nu-1)\sin((\nu+1)\vartheta)
      -(\nu+1)\sin((\nu-1)\vartheta)\\
     &+c\{\cos((\nu+1)\vartheta)-\cos((\nu-1)\vartheta)\},\\
 \psi_s(r,\vartheta)={}&r^{\nu+1}\Phi(\vartheta),
 \qquad J\nabla\psi=(\partial_y\psi,-\partial_x\psi).
\end{split}\] Direct substitution gives \(\Phi(0)=\Phi'(0)=\Phi(\omega)=\Phi'(\omega)=0\). At \(\omega\), these identities use \(\sin((\nu+1)\omega)=-c\), \(\sin((\nu-1)\omega)=c\), \(\cos((\nu+1)\omega)=\nu\), \(\cos((\nu-1)\omega)=-\nu\), and \(c^2+\nu^2=1\). The distinct angular frequencies show \(\Phi\not\equiv0\). Thus \(u_s=J\nabla\psi_s\) is divergence free and vanishes on both sector walls.

Writing \(h=\Delta\psi_s\), exact differentiation yields \[\begin{split}
 h={}&-4\nu r^{\nu-1}
 \{(\nu+1)\sin((\nu-1)\vartheta)
                 +c\cos((\nu-1)\vartheta)\},\\
 p_s={}&4\nu r^{\nu-1}
 \{c\sin((\nu-1)\vartheta)
                 -(\nu+1)\cos((\nu-1)\vartheta)\}.
\end{split}\] Both functions are harmonic, and \(\nabla p_s=J\nabla h=\Delta u_s\). Consequently \((u_s,p_s)\) solves the homogeneous stationary Stokes equations in the sector.

## Bounded domain and genuinely solenoidal forcing

Choose a bounded simply connected domain contained in the sector, agreeing with it near the vertex, with smooth boundary away from that vertex. The outer joins can be rounded. It is a Lipschitz domain with one reentrant corner.

Let a smooth radial cutoff \(\chi\) equal one near the vertex and have support inside the neighborhood where the boundary consists of the two exact sector rays. Set \(\psi_0=\chi\psi_s\). Both \(\psi_0\) and its normal derivative vanish on the entire boundary. The function \[b=(h-\Delta\psi_0)|_{\partial\Omega}\] is smooth and supported away from the vertex. In smooth boundary coordinates \((s,d)\), where \(d\) is normal distance, choose a collar correction with local form \(v(s,d)=\tfrac12d^2b(s)\), multiplied by suitable cutoffs. A partition of unity provides a smooth correction supported away from the corner with \[v=\partial_n v=0,\qquad \Delta v=b
 \quad\hbox{on }\partial\Omega.\] The last equality follows because the boundary Hessian has only the normal-normal entry \(b\); first-derivative curvature terms vanish there.

Put \(\psi=\psi_0+v\), \(u=J\nabla\psi\), and take \(\lambda=1\). Then \[F=-\Delta u+\nabla p_s+u
   =J\nabla(h-\Delta\psi+\psi).\] Near the vertex this is \(u_s=O(r^\nu)\); elsewhere all terms are smooth. Hence \(F\) is bounded. Its scalar stream function \(h-\Delta\psi+\psi\) has zero boundary trace. Therefore \(\operatorname{div}F=0\) and \(F\cdot n=0\). There is no hidden vertex flux: the flux across a small corner arc is \(O(r^{\nu+1})\). Equivalently, the force stream function belongs to \(W^{1,5}_0(\Omega)\), so smooth compactly supported approximations of it show \(F\in L^5_\sigma(\Omega)\). Thus the forcing meets the theorem’s solenoidal requirement, rather than merely being an arbitrary bounded vector field.

## Failure at \(q=5\), consequence, and repair

The solution has \[u\in W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)\cap L^\infty(\Omega),
 \qquad p_s\in L^2(\Omega).\] Near the corner, its nonzero angular factors give \(|\nabla u|\) and \(|p_s|\) of order \(r^{\nu-1}\) on an angular interval. Since \(\nu<3/5\), \[\int_0^\varepsilon r^{5(\nu-1)+1}\,dr=\infty.\] Hence \(u\notin W^{1,5}(\Omega)\) and \(p_s\notin L^5(\Omega)\). Subtracting the finite mean of \(p_s\) fixes the pressure gauge without changing either conclusion.

A hypothetical \(W^{1,5}_0\times L^5\) solution for this same \(F\) and \(\lambda=1\) would also belong to the energy class. Energy uniqueness forces its velocity to equal \(u\) and its mean-zero pressure to equal the normalized \(p_s\), a contradiction. This is a counterexample to the printed existence class.

Classification: Minor. The failure concerns a secondary regularity assertion. The example’s velocity is bounded and does not violate (9.13), Theorem 9.4, or Theorem 1.1. A correction should state the velocity resolvent realization separately and restrict gradient/pressure regularity to the domain-dependent range near \(2\), or strengthen the boundary regularity. Interpolating velocity bounds must not be presented as proving gradient bounds.

# 0118-F2: the arbitrary-graph Poincare assertion

Equation (2.13), PDF p. 11, and Remark 2.3, PDF p. 12, assert for arbitrary Lipschitz graphs that \[\|\eta-\beta\|_{L^{q'}(I_R)}
 \le CR^{1-1/q'}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q'}(D_{2R})},
 \qquad \beta=\frac1{|D_{2R}|}\int_{D_{2R}}\eta,\] where \(D_R=B(0,R)\cap\mathbb H_\psi\) and \(I_R=B(0,R)\cap\partial\mathbb H_\psi\). The fixed-ball intersection need not be connected.

Take \(d=2\), \(R=1\), and \[\psi(x)=\max\{0,\,3-12|x-\tfrac12|\}.\] It is globally Lipschitz with \(\psi(0)=0\). For \(0.45<x<0.55\), \(\psi(x)\ge2.4\), so this entire vertical strip misses \(D_2\). There are positive-area components of \(D_2\) on both sides. Choose smooth \(\chi\) equal to zero for \(x\le0.45\) and one for \(x\ge0.55\), changing only inside that strip. For \(\zeta\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R^2)\) equal to one near \(\overline{B_2}\), let \(\eta(x,y)=\chi(x)\zeta(x,y)\). It is an admissible homogeneous Sobolev test function, and \(\nabla\eta=0\) everywhere in \(D_2\). However, \(\beta\in(0,1)\), and \(I_1\) contains positive-length segments with \(\eta=0\) and with \(\eta=1\). The claimed inequality has positive left side and zero right side, for example for \(q'=3/2\).

Classification: Minor; explicit local repair. The example lies outside Theorem 2.2’s small-slope assumption. For a graph with Lipschitz constant \(M\), use instead the connected graph box \[P_R=\{(x',x_d):|x'|<4R,\quad
               \psi(x')<x_d<(4M+4)R\}.\] Its geometry is controlled by \(M\), and it contains the relevant \(D_{2R}\) and graph patches. Choose the same \(\beta\) as its average throughout the localization calculation. Poincare and trace inequalities on this box give the required bounds with \(C(M)\), the same powers of \(R\), and \(\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q'}(P_R)}\) on the right.

The dual test in Section 2 already satisfies \(\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q'}(\mathbb H_\psi)}\le1\). Changing its averaging box therefore requires no additional values of \(u\) or \(F\) outside their existing localization supports. The cancellation \(\int g=0\) permits subtracting this new constant. This repairs the arbitrary-slope use needed in the planar argument; it does not justify the false fixed-ball assertion itself.

# 0118-F3: missing flux compatibility in Theorem A.1

On PDF pp. 39–40, Theorem A.1 quantifies over every \(f\in W^{1,q}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb R^d)\), for \(q\) near \(2\), and claims a divergence-free \(v\) with boundary trace \(f\). It omits \[\int_{\partial\Omega} f\cdot n\,d\sigma=0.\] For the unit disk, \(q=2\), and \(f(x)=x\) on the unit circle, every printed geometry and regularity hypothesis holds, but \[0=\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}v
   =\int_{\partial\Omega}f\cdot n=2\pi\] would be required. Thus the theorem as stated is false.

Classification: Minor. Adding the displayed zero-flux hypothesis repairs this obstruction. The explicit Appendix A application has \(f=-w\), with \(\operatorname{div}w=0\), and is compatible. This example therefore does not invalidate that application or the main resolvent estimate.

# Checks of the main proof and the exterior solution class

## Localization, pressure scales, and absorption

The localized divergence is \(g=\operatorname{div}(u\varphi)\). Its compact support and zero trace imply \(\int g=0\), which is essential to the homogeneous negative Sobolev pairing. The boundary scaling \[(Nr)^{(d-1)/q}(Nr)^{-1/q'}=(Nr)^{d/q-1}\] matches the localization estimate. A common pressure constant can be subtracted from the interior and boundary terms.

For \(C^1\) charts one fixes \(q>d\), chooses a uniform small-slope radius, and then chooses the large parameter \(N\). The condition \(|\lambda|>16r_0^{-2}N^4\), with \(r=2N|\lambda|^{-1/2}\), ensures \(2Nr<r_0\). There is no circular choice of the frequency threshold. The complementary bounded-frequency range is handled by (3.25). For planar Lipschitz domains, Theorem 9.3 and the boundary regularity interval allow a common \(q>2\) sufficiently close to \(2\). This is enough to make \(2/q-1<0\) in (9.12). The main argument does not need Theorem 9.5’s false all-\(q\) gradient membership.

The truncated boundary quantity \(M_q(g)(t)\) takes suprema over radii at least \(t\). The pressure proofs use near patches at scale \(r\ge t\), larger dyadic annuli, and a fixed-scale far remainder. Their terms have summable factors \(2^{-j\sigma}\) or \(j2^{-j\sigma}\). They do not require gradient control at arbitrarily smaller radii. Away from the boundary, an occasional notation \(M_q(g)(\delta(x))\) beyond its defined upper cutoff is read through the fixed-scale/global boundary bound already supplied by the proof.

The tangential operator \(n_i\partial_j-n_j\partial_i\) has zero integral on the closed boundary. It supplies the Neumann compatibility and integration by parts in Sections 4–6. On interior approximating boundaries, the pressure data retain the necessary term \(h_\ell=-\lambda u\cdot n_\ell\); one cannot set it to zero before taking the limit. In an exterior domain, its total flux is exactly zero by applying the divergence theorem to the bounded collar. This removes the planar logarithmic obstruction. The exterior pressure mean is controlled through Lemma 5.3, rather than freely discarded after fixing its value at infinity.

## A finite-\(q\) completion of exterior uniqueness

Theorem 1.6, PDF p. 7, uses \[u\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline\Omega)\cap L^\infty,
 \qquad p\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline\Omega).\] The uniqueness paragraph on PDF p. 35 instead starts with \(u\in W^{1,\infty}_0\). A bounded gradient is not a consequence of the stated \(C^1\) boundary hypothesis. The connection can nevertheless be supplied with the finite-\(q\) estimates already used in the paper:

For a homogeneous bounded weak solution, rewrite the stationary forcing as \(-\lambda u\in L^\infty\). The local estimate underlying (3.12) gives \(W^{1,q}\) velocity and \(L^q\) pressure on boundary patches, with finite \(q>d\) for \(C^1\) domains or \(2<q<2+\varepsilon\) in the planar Lipschitz case. The potential decomposition in Lemma 3.5 and (3.13) give finite-\(q\) boundary gradient control. The trace argument (A.4)–(A.8) provides the needed nontangential traces and convergence on approximating boundaries.

Since \(q>d\), the zero velocity trace gives \(\sup_{\partial\Omega_\ell}|u|\to0\). A finite cover of the compact physical boundary suffices; no global \(W^{1,q}\) norm on the unbounded domain is assumed. The condition that \(p\) is bounded at infinity, together with harmonicity and Lemma 5.1, gives \(p\to\beta\). Subtract \(\beta\), retain the collar’s exact zero-flux condition, and pass to the finite-\(q\) exterior pressure estimates. The local bounds at \(t=|\lambda|^{-1/2}\) control the boundary gradient quantities by \(C|\lambda|^{1/2}\|u\|_\infty\). The same high-frequency absorption as in (8.1), now with \(F=0\), forces \(u=0\).

Thus there is a proof-class clarification to make, with a concrete completion using weaker estimates. It is not evidence of nonuniqueness and is not counted as an additional independent substantive defect.

## Semigroup scope and verification limits

The bounded-domain semigroup conclusion explicitly distinguishes the \(C_0\) semigroup on \(C_{0,\sigma}\) from the non-\(C_0\) analytic semigroup on \(L^\infty_\sigma\). On compactly supported solenoidal data, the resolvent identity \(\lambda R_\lambda F-F=R_\lambda\Delta F\) verifies the large-\(\lambda\) approximation used with density. For exterior domains, PDF p. 8 explicitly states that uniform semigroup boundedness has not been established from the available high-frequency estimates. This report does not extend that conclusion.
