Abstract: The chiral asymptotic formula is contradicted by a family of full-height columns whose weights remain of order one. The exact covering integral is also false: its printed measure gives a divergent integral, and the proposed infinite genus series diverges independently of its normalization. The generalized Frobenius probability family is not normalizable for base genus at least two. The tightness argument for the unrestricted limit has a core proof gap, repaired here by a uniform union bound. Three further local errors admit explicit repairs. These findings do not refute the finite-order expansion of the unrestricted unitary partition function; its main domination mechanism is checked separately.

# Identity, definitions, and scope

The reviewed paper is Thibaut Lemoine and Mylène Maïda, Gaussian measure on the dual of \(U(N)\), random partitions, and topological expansion of the partition function, DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1749. The exact source is the 26-page arXiv:2405.08393v2 PDF, dated 11 April 2025 [paper]. Source locators below use one-based PDF pages, equal to printed pages. The complete source, including proofs and references, was read. Journal-final textual equivalence is not established.

Equation reference: eq:chiral. For a partition \(\alpha\), let \(A=|\alpha|\), \(\ell(\alpha)\) be its number of rows, and \[K(\alpha)=\frac12\sum_i\alpha_i(\alpha_i+1-2i),\qquad
\varphi(q)=\prod_{j\geq1}(1-q^j),\qquad 0<q=e^{-t/2}<1.\] The \(q\)-uniform law assigns mass \(\varphi(q)q^{|\alpha|}\). The unitary Casimir is \[c_{2,N}(\lambda)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i(\lambda_i+N+1-2i).\] The unrestricted partition function sums over all nonincreasing integer \(N\)-tuples. In contrast, the chiral function is explicitly defined on pages 5 and 21 by \[
Z_N^{\mathrm{chir}}(1,t)=
\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\leq N}q^{|\alpha|+2K(\alpha)/N}.\] No fixed-degree restriction or half-height cutoff is imposed in this definition.

The review keeps \(t>0\) and the expansion order fixed when taking \(N\to\infty\). It does not assume uniformity as \(t\downarrow0\), or interchange the expansion order with that limit. A specifically cited supporting source, Novak’s arXiv:2401.00628v1 [novak], was acquired and its relevant definitions and convergence statements were read and visually checked. Other imported representation-theoretic results were followed as dependencies, not independently re-proved.

# The chiral asymptotic statement

Problem 1 (Major: Theorem 1.4 has the wrong leading term).  Theorem 1.4, equation (8), page 5, implies \(Z_N^{\mathrm{chir}}(1,t)\to\varphi(q)^{-1}\). This conclusion is false for the chiral function [eq:chiral] (#eq:chiral). The proof on page 21 transfers estimates whose essential half-height hypothesis is absent from this summation domain.

Proof. The full-height column \(\alpha^{(N)}=(1^N)\) is an admissible partition with \(\ell(\alpha^{(N)})=N\). Its content and Casimir are \[K(\alpha^{(N)})=-\frac{N(N-1)}2,\qquad
c_{2,N}(\alpha^{(N)})=N-(N-1)=1.\] It therefore contributes exactly \(q>0\), for every \(N\).

Equation reference: eq:liminf. This alone is not compared with the entire limiting partition sum; possible double counting must be excluded. Fix an integer \(M\). For \(N>M\), the column \((1^N)\) is distinct from every partition of size at most \(M\), all of which are admissible. Positivity gives \[Z_N^{\mathrm{chir}}(1,t)\geq
q+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq M}q^{|\alpha|+2K(\alpha)/N}.\] First let \(N\to\infty\) with \(M\) fixed, then let \(M\to\infty\). Euler’s partition identity yields \[
\liminf_{N\to\infty} Z_N^{\mathrm{chir}}(1,t)
\geq q+\varphi(q)^{-1}>\varphi(q)^{-1}.\] All hypotheses of Theorem 1.4 are met for every fixed \(t>0\). ◻

Core dependency and alternatives. Lemma 4.1, pages 13–14, uses \(\ell(\alpha),\ell(\beta)\leq N/2\). For a single column of height \(N\), its prospective bound \(c_{2,N}\geq|\alpha|/2\) fails once \(N>2\). Thus its domination cannot be transferred unchanged to [eq:chiral] (#eq:chiral). The proof on page 21 also drops the factor \(\varphi(q)^{-1}\) in its expectation expansion, but restoring that normalization does not fix [eq:liminf] (#eq:liminf).

Restricting the definition to a half-height sector or to fixed degree would exclude the example; either changes the observable or quantifiers. The paper’s repeated explicit domain \(\ell(\alpha)\leq N\) prevents treating such a restriction as implicit. For the stated domain, the leading term and subsequent expansion require a fresh derivation. This is a false final theorem for the chiral sector, not merely a missing proof detail. It does not contradict Theorems 1.1–1.3 for the unrestricted highest-weight model, which retains the central integer and both partitions.

# Infinite expansions and the covering integral

Problem 2 (Major: Corollary 6.2 is a divergent integral).  Corollary 6.2, equation (43), page 22, claims an exact finite covering integral. Its proof on page 23 begins by letting the order \(p\) tend to infinity in Theorem 1.4. That step is invalid, and the proposed infinite series diverges. Independently, the measure in Definition 6.1 makes the asserted integral infinite.

Divergence of the genus series. Equation (35) on page 19 gives \(H_1(m,2k)=\sum_{\alpha\vdash m}K(\alpha)^{2k}\). The single row \((m)\) has \(K=m(m-1)/2\), hence the nonnegative series used on page 23 is bounded below by \[\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m\geq1}q^m\sum_{k\geq0}
 \frac{t^{2k}}{(2k)!N^{2k}}\left(\frac{m(m-1)}2\right)^{2k}
&=\sum_{m\geq1}q^m\cosh\left(\frac{tm(m-1)}{2N}\right)\\
&\geq\frac12\sum_{m\geq1}
 \exp\left(-\frac{tm}{2}+\frac{tm(m-1)}{2N}\right)=\infty .\end{aligned}\] For every fixed finite \(N\) and \(t>0\), the summands on the right do not even tend to zero. Tonelli’s theorem justifies rearrangement to the value \(+\infty\); it cannot identify that series with a finite partition function. The latter is finite because completing the square in the Casimir gives a Gaussian lattice sum in fixed dimension \(N\).

The same lower bound applies to the full expansion in Theorem 4.5: retain only \(\beta=\varnothing\), the central integer \(n=0\), and \(\alpha=(m)\) in its nonnegative even-moment coefficients. Thus the phrase convergent asymptotic expansion in Remark 4.6 cannot mean a convergent infinite \(1/N\) series. Finite-order asymptotics remain a distinct, viable statement. ◻

Divergence under the printed covering measure. Fix the base torus \(X=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)\). For every \(m\geq1\), the inclusion of lattices \(m\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z\) gives a connected unbranched degree-\(m\) covering. It is compact, has Euler characteristic zero, and has exactly \(m\) deck transformations. All its ramifications are simple vacuously, so it belongs to the space in Definition 6.1.

That definition weights this covering by \(m!/|\operatorname{Aut}(Y)|=(m-1)!\). The even-conditioned Poisson branch process is empty with probability \[\mathbb P\{J=0\mid J\text{ even}\}=\frac1{\cosh t},
\qquad J\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(t).\] Consequently the proposed integral is at least \[\frac1{\cosh t}\sum_{m\geq1}q^m(m-1)!=\infty.\] This direct counterexample uses no interchange of an asymptotic expansion. ◻

Normalization checks. The weights \(m!/|\operatorname{Aut}(Y)|\) also disagree with equation (35). In degree two with no branch points, the four commuting pairs in \(S_2\) give four covering classes, each with automorphism group of order two. Standard weighted count is \(4/2=2=p(2)=H_1(2,0)\); the printed weights give four. An independent enumeration verifies these numbers. In addition, an even-conditioned Poisson process assigns branch-count probability \(t^{2k}/((2k)!\cosh t)\), whereas the proof uses the coefficient without \(\cosh t\). Neither correction alone resolves the divergent all-genus sum.

Counterevidence and repair limits. Novak’s Theorem 4.5 [novak, p. 22] proves absolute convergence for fixed degree \(d\) in the stable range \(1\leq d\leq N\), after interchanging a finite representation sum with an exponential series. His Definition 4.6 labels the degree-summed torus object formal, and page 17 explicitly separates the additional interchange needed for the full chiral partition function. This supports the fixed-degree result, not the stronger attribution on page 21 of the reviewed paper. A valid replacement should retain a formal or finite-order coefficient interpretation, or introduce and justify a distinct regularization. The exact positive-measure formula as printed cannot be repaired by an appeal to nonnegativity.

# The generalized probability family

Problem 3 (Major within the asserted family: nonnormalizable Frobenius weights).  Section 5.2, pages 19–20, introduces a probability family for every integer \(g\geq0\), \(g'\geq1\), and \(q\in(0,1)\), with weights \[w_{g,g'}(\alpha)=
\left(\frac{\chi_\alpha(1)}{|\alpha|!}\right)^{2-2g}
K(\alpha)^{2g'-2}q^{|\alpha|}.\] For \(g\geq2\) these weights have infinite total mass.

For the row \(\alpha=(m)\), \(m\geq2\), the symmetric-group representation is trivial, so \(\chi_\alpha(1)=1\). Its weight is \[(m!)^{2g-2}\left(\frac{m(m-1)}2\right)^{2g'-2}q^m.\] The ratio of successive terms tends to infinity for every \(q>0\) and \(g\geq2\). The terms therefore fail to tend to zero, already when \(g=2,g'=1\). The alleged probability law does not exist on that parameter range. Propositions 5.1–5.2, written conditionally on a random partition having this law, have no such instance there; the issue is the asserted existence and normalization, not a counterexample to a conditional-expectation identity for an existing probability.

The restriction matters for the proposed general family. It does not invalidate the torus specialization \(g=1\) used in Theorem 5.3 and Theorem 6.3. In that case polynomial content moments are summable under the exponentially decaying \(q\)-partition weights. For \(g=0\), the dimension-square identity provides factorial suppression. Restricting to a normalizable range, or conditioning on fixed degree with positive finite normalizing mass, gives possible repairs; it does not produce the originally asserted family for \(g\geq2\).

# Tightness and auxiliary identities

Problem 4 (Minor: the exponential rate in Lemma 4.4 is false).  Lemma 4.4, page 16, claims, for each fixed \(k\geq1\), \(\mathbb E[F^k\mathbf1_{\Lambda_N^c}]\leq C_{k,q}q^{N/2}\), where \[F=K(\alpha)+K(\beta)+n(|\alpha|-|\beta|),
\quad
\alpha,\beta\sim U(q),\quad n\sim G_1(q)\] independently. The assertion fails for \(k=2\).

Let \(N=2m\), \(m\geq2\), and retain the event \(\alpha=(1^m),\beta=\varnothing,n=0\). Since \(A_N=m-1\), this event lies in \(\Lambda_N^c\). Its probability is \(\varphi(q)^2q^m/\theta(q)\), and \(F=-m(m-1)/2\). Thus \[\frac{\mathbb E[F^2\mathbf1_{\Lambda_N^c}]}{q^{N/2}}
\geq \frac{\varphi(q)^2}{\theta(q)}
       \left(\frac{m(m-1)}2\right)^2\longrightarrow\infty.\] All parameters and distributions are exactly those of the lemma.

The proof’s Cauchy–Schwarz argument instead yields \(\mathbb E[|F|^k\mathbf1_{\Lambda_N^c}]=O(q^{N/4})\), using finite \(2k\)-th moments and the union bound for the two length tails. This still implies every polynomial rate in equation (30), and suffices for equation (32) in the fixed-order expansion. The false stronger exponent is therefore a local minor issue; it does not refute Theorem 4.5.

Problem 5 (Major proof gap: the tightness argument omits the required tails).  On page 15, the proof of Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 controls the complement of \(X_M=\{|\alpha|,|\beta|,|n|\leq M\}\) by an expression containing \(\mathbf1_{\{|\alpha|>M,\ |\beta|>M\}}\mathbf1_{\{|n|>M\}}\). The displayed left indicator also loses its complement. Neither expression represents \(X_M^c\).

For example, \(\alpha=\beta=\varnothing\), \(n=M+1\) belongs to \(\Lambda_N\cap X_M^c\) for every \(N\), with positive weight \(q^{(M+1)^2}\), but its printed intersection indicator is zero. For \(N=1\), only the empty partitions are admissible: the purported intersection bound is zero while the actual \(n\)-tail is positive. The equality is therefore false under both the literal printed indicator and its intended complemented reading.

A direct repair is available. Put \(r=\sqrt q\), \(Z_r=\sum_\alpha r^{|\alpha|}=\varphi(r)^{-1}\), \(S_M=\sum_{|\alpha|>M}r^{|\alpha|}\), and \(C_q=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{(n+x)^2}<\infty\). For the dominating exponent \(C_N\) of Lemma 4.1, the partition tails contribute at most \(2C_qZ_rS_M\). On the remaining set \(|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq M\) and \(N\geq2\), the shift \(\delta=(|\alpha|-|\beta|)/N\) has magnitude at most \(M/2\). For \(|n|>M\), \(|n+\delta|\geq|n|/2\). Therefore \[\sup_{N\geq1}\sum_{\Lambda_N\cap X_M^c}q^{C_N}
\leq2C_qZ_rS_M+
 Z_r^2\sum_{|n|>M}q^{n^2/4}\longrightarrow0.\] For \(N=1\), the shift is zero, so the same bound holds. Applying it to absolute values also handles signed bounded test functions. This supplies the required tightness and preserves the limiting conclusions. The invalid estimate is the sole displayed verification of the uniform-tail criterion used for Theorems 1.1 and 1.3, so it is a Major defect in that derivation. The bound above repairs the step without changing the theorem statements; no false decoupling theorem is established.

Problem 6 (Minor: sign in equation (40)).  The middle expression in equation (40), page 20, is \(D\log\varphi(q)\), with \(D=q\,d/dq\). It has the opposite sign from the mean partition size.

Differentiating the Euler product for \(0<q<1\) gives \[D\log\varphi(q)=-\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{jq^j}{1-q^j}<0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{U(q)}|\alpha|=-D\log\varphi(q)>0.\] Thus the printed equality is false for every allowed \(q\). The preceding general identity uses the correct normalizing function \(F_{1,1}=\varphi^{-1}\), and the Eisenstein-series expression on the right has the positive sign. Inserting a minus sign in the middle term is the local repair; the later differentiated \(F_{1,g}\) formulas need no change from this error alone.

Problem 7 (Minor: coordinate indexing in Proposition 3.3).  The proof on page 12 defines \(\ell_2\) as the first index below the middle coordinate, then uses it as the number of such coordinates; it also prints \(\beta_j=\lambda_k-\lambda_{N-j-1}\). These formulas do not give the claimed partition.

The paper’s own Figure 1, page 2, has \(\lambda=(4,3,3,2,1,-1,-2,-2)\), \(N=8\), \(k=4\), and \(n=2\). The first index below \(n\) is five, whereas the number of entries below \(n\) is four. Using the printed indexing gives \((3,1,0,-1,-1)\), not a partition; the figure correctly gives \(\beta=(4,4,3,1)\). Define instead \[\ell_2=\#\{i:\lambda_i<n\},\qquad
\beta_j=n-\lambda_{N+1-j},\quad 1\leq j\leq\ell_2.\] Together with the corresponding positive deviations above \(n\), this is the intended bijection. Equation (22) and Figure 1 support that interpretation. The existence of a bijection, the Casimir identity, and the later density formula are not disproved by these local indexing slips.

# Checks on the unrestricted finite-order expansion

The preceding distinctions are material: the unrestricted model does satisfy the half-height restriction. With \(A=|\alpha|\), \(B=|\beta|\), \(d=A-B\), the intended bijection gives \[c_{2,N}=A+B+n^2+\frac2N F.\] For a decreasing partition of length \(\ell\), \(\sum_i i\alpha_i\leq(\ell+1)A/2\), so \(\sum_i(1-2i)\alpha_i\geq-\ell A\). Combining this with \(A^2\leq\ell\sum_i\alpha_i^2\) and \(\ell\leq N/2\) proves the bounds used in the domination and remainder arguments: \[\begin{aligned}
A+B+n^2+\frac2N F
 &\geq \frac12(A+B)+\left(n+\frac dN\right)^2,\\
A+B+n^2+\frac3N F
 &\geq \frac14(A+B)+\left(n+\frac{3d}{2N}\right)^2.\end{aligned}\] The shifted Gaussian sum is uniformly bounded, and the partition factors are summable for fixed \(q<1\). All polynomial moments of \(F\) are finite. These facts justify the fixed-order Hölder remainder estimate on pages 17–18 after the local tail-rate repair.

The law is invariant under \((\alpha,\beta,n)\mapsto(\alpha',\beta',-n)\), which sends \(F\) to \(-F\); odd moments therefore vanish. The second coefficient provides a further check: \[a_2(t)=\frac{t^2\theta(q)}{\varphi(q)^2}
\left(\mathbb E[K(\alpha)^2]+\mathbb E[n^2]\operatorname{Var}(|\alpha|)\right),\] consistent with Remark 6.4. An independent exact enumeration of 1,286 highest weights, for \(1\leq N\leq8\) and coordinates in \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\), verifies the Casimir identity and both bounds with zero residual failures. This finite check corroborates, and does not replace, the analytic inequalities above.

No false final unrestricted finite-order theorem was established. Its coefficientwise topological interpretation can coexist with divergence of the infinite series. The confirmed false conclusions are the stated chiral leading limit and the exact covering integral; the generalized probability family also fails on its asserted \(g\geq2\) range.
