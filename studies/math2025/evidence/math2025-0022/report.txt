Abstract: The manifest-pinned author manuscript repeats an incorrect local Euler linearization and the corresponding quadratic wave system. The error is independently established and its dependence on the companion energy argument is traced. This supports Major for the invalid core derivation, without disproving the existence or uniqueness theorems. A separate incorrect Sobolev embedding is Minor: the immediately preceding stronger bound supplies a precise local repair. The relative-entropy sign mechanism and a potentially misleading domain-extent reading were also checked; they do not support additional findings in this inspection.

# Assumptions and dependencies

The equation of state is \(p=k_0\rho^\gamma\), \(k_0>0\), \(1<\gamma<3\). Space is \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\); the background is a nondegenerate two-rarefaction Riemann solution away from vacuum. Definition 1.1, pp. 7–8, requires smooth data on both closed half-tubes, sufficiently small high-order Sobolev perturbations of fixed constant states, and distributional irrotationality across the initial interface. The fixed positive separation between the two wave families is part of the smallness comparison. Theorem 1 constructs compatible rarefaction data at \(t=\delta\). Corollary 1, p. 19, expressly uses the companion paper’s a priori energy estimates. Section 4 then passes to \(\delta\downarrow0\) on compact sets bounded away from \(t=0\).

The local potential identities on p. 10 have a different scope from these global perturbation hypotheses: they are asserted for smooth irrotational solutions of the stated Euler system. We use that local scope in Problem 1 and do not claim a counterexample to the small-data existence theorem.

# Problem 1: an incorrect local wave equation in a core dependency

## Claim, location, and analytic verification

Equation reference: bad, good. On p. 10, equations (1.10)–(1.12), and source lines 638–677, the manuscript defines \[g=-c^2dt^2+\sum_{i=1}^2(dx^i-v^idt)^2,\qquad
\psi_\mu=\partial_\mu\phi,\qquad v=-\nabla\phi,\qquad \Omega=\rho/c\] and claims \[
\Box_g\psi=-\tfrac12Q_g(\log(\rho/c),\psi),\qquad
Q_g(f,k):=g^{\mu\nu}f_\mu k_\nu .\] Here \(h=\phi_t-\frac12|\nabla\phi|^2=c^2/(\gamma-1)\). The pressure Lagrangian has \(p_h=\rho\), \(p_{hh}=\rho/c^2\) and Hessian \(L_{\phi_\mu\phi_\nu}=-\rho g^{\mu\nu}\). Its variation therefore satisfies \[\partial_\mu(\rho g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\psi)=0.\] As \(\det g=-c^2\), division by \(\rho\) and the coordinate formula \(\Box_g=c^{-1}\partial_\mu(cg^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu)\) prove \[
\Box_g\psi=-Q_g(\log(\rho/c),\psi).\] Thus the coefficient in (1.10) is incorrect. In three space-time dimensions the corresponding conformal metric is \((\rho/c)^2g\); the density of its inverse metric is exactly \(\rho g^{\mu\nu}\).

Equation reference: badw, goodw. The paper defines \(w=c/(\gamma-1)+\psi_1/2\) and \(\underline{w}=c/(\gamma-1)-\psi_1/2\). Equation (1.12) gives the \(w\) equation \[
\Box_g w=-c^{-1}\left[
Q_g(w,w)+\frac{\gamma-3}{4}Q_g(w,\underline{w})
+\frac{\gamma+1}{4}Q_g(\underline{w},\underline{w})+\frac12Q_g(\psi_2,\psi_2)\right].\] The corrected local system is \[\begin{aligned}
\Box_g w&=-c^{-1}\left[
\frac{7-\gamma}{4}Q_g(w,w)+\frac{\gamma+1}{4}Q_g(\underline{w},\underline{w})
+\frac12Q_g(\psi_2,\psi_2)\right],\\
\Box_g\underline{w}&=-c^{-1}\left[
\frac{\gamma+1}{4}Q_g(w,w)+\frac{7-\gamma}{4}Q_g(\underline{w},\underline{w})
+\frac12Q_g(\psi_2,\psi_2)\right],\\
\Box_g\psi_2&=-\frac{3-\gamma}{2c}
\{Q_g(w,\psi_2)+Q_g(\underline{w},\psi_2)\}.\end{aligned}\] To verify these coefficients, set \(b=\gamma-1\) and use \(\log(\rho/c)=((3-\gamma)/b)\log c+\mathrm{constant}\). Applying [good] (#good) to \(\psi_\mu\) and differentiating \(c^2/b=\psi_0-(\psi_1^2+\psi_2^2)/2\) gives \[\Box_gc=-\frac{2}{bc}Q_g(c,c)
-\frac{b}{2c}\{Q_g(\psi_1,\psi_1)+Q_g(\psi_2,\psi_2)\}.\] Insertion of \(c=b(w+\underline{w})/2\) and \(\psi_1=w-\underline{w}\) gives the displayed correction. This verifies a local algebraic repair, not the full repaired existence proof.

## Exact test and all applicable conditions

On \(0<t<1\), \(x\in\mathbb R\), \(y\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\), write \(s=1+t\) and take \[\gamma=2,\quad k_0=\tfrac12,\quad
\rho=s^{-1},\quad c=s^{-1/2},\quad p=\tfrac12s^{-2},\quad
v=(x/s,0),\quad
\phi=-x^2/(2s)+\log s .\] These are smooth positive-density fields, periodic in \(y\), with \(v=-\nabla\phi\) and zero curl. The pressure and sound-speed laws hold. For \(B=\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla\), \[B\rho+\rho\operatorname{div}v=-s^{-2}+s^{-2}=0,\qquad
Bv+\rho^{-1}\nabla p=(0,0).\] Moreover \(\phi_t-\frac12|\nabla\phi|^2=1/s=c^2/(\gamma-1)\), and the nondivergence potential equation (1.9) vanishes. Time translation supplies an exact one-parameter family with variation \(\psi_0=\phi_t\).

Direct evaluation of the divergence-form wave operator gives \[\Box_g\psi_0=\frac1{2s^2},\qquad
Q_g(\log(\rho/c),\psi_0)=-\frac1{2s^2}.\] The predicted right side of [bad] (#bad) is \(1/(4s^2)\), leaving a nonzero residual \(1/(4s^2)\). The corrected residual is zero. Furthermore \[w=s^{-1/2}-x/(2s),\quad \underline{w}=s^{-1/2}+x/(2s),\quad \psi_2=0,\] \[Q_g(w,w)=Q_g(\underline{w},\underline{w})=0,\qquad
Q_g(w,\underline{w})=-1/(2s^2),\qquad
\Box_gw=0.\] Equation [badw] (#badw) predicts \(-1/(8s^{3/2})\); [goodw] (#goodw) predicts zero.

The affine velocity is not claimed to meet Definition 1.1’s global Sobolev smallness or the rarefaction data ansatz. The example refutes the asserted local identities only. It does not refute Theorems 1–3 or Proposition 2.12.

## Counterevidence, dependence, and severity

Constant \(\rho/c\) makes the differing term vanish, and \(\gamma=3\) would also do so, but the paper’s allowed case \(\gamma=2\) does not. Reinterpreting \(\Omega\) as its square contradicts its explicit definition on p. 10. The direct metric and source checks rule out a text-extraction ambiguity.

The same identities occur in the manifest-pinned companion paper, arXiv:2302.09714v2, equations (2.2) and (3.7), whose Sections 6 and 8 use them in the core energy proof. In this paper, Corollary 1 on p. 19 and the uniform estimates on p. 60 expressly invoke that companion result; those estimates feed the compactness construction in pp. 61–62. This establishes the core dependency.

The supported judgment is Major for the repeated invalid core derivation and affected proof dependency. It is one shared mathematical error across the two manuscripts, not two independently discovered mechanisms. The corrected sources remain quadratic null forms, which is significant counterevidence against claiming that the strategy or theorem must fail. A complete correction requires rechecking the energy argument and all transferred estimates. No such full repair is claimed here. The independent computation is exact symbolic verification, not an empirical experiment.

# Problem 2: the Sobolev embedding used in the limit passage

## Source and complete check

Section 4.3, p. 62, takes strong convergence in \(H^{N_{\rm top}}(K_k)\) and cites an embedding into \(C^{N_\infty}\) with \(N_\infty=N_{\rm top}-1\). But \(K_k\) is a three-dimensional space-time domain, not a two-dimensional spatial slice. In three dimensions, this claimed Sobolev embedding is false.

Here is a direct norm test. Let \(m=N_{\rm top}\), choose a nonzero smooth compactly supported bump \(\eta\) inside a Euclidean coordinate ball, with a nonzero derivative of order \(m-1\), and set \[f_\epsilon(z)=\epsilon^{m-3/2}\eta((z-z_0)/\epsilon).\] For \(0\leq j\leq m\), \[\|D^j f_\epsilon\|_{L^2}
=\epsilon^{m-j}\|D^j\eta\|_{L^2}.\] Hence the \(H^m\) norms stay bounded, whereas \[\|D^{m-1} f_\epsilon\|_{L^\infty}
=\epsilon^{-1/2}\|D^{m-1}\eta\|_{L^\infty}\longrightarrow\infty.\] A continuous embedding \(H^m\hookrightarrow C^{m-1}\) cannot satisfy this test. The example is a test of the function-space assertion, not a sequence of Euler solutions.

## Local repair and impact

There is already stronger input on the same page: a uniform \(H^{m+1}\) bound and weak \(H^{m+1}\) convergence on each fixed \(K_k\), from Proposition 4.2. Let \(f_j\to f\) strongly in \(H^m\) with that higher bound. The weak limit lies in \(H^{m+1}\), and for any \(1/2<\sigma<1\), interpolation gives \[\|f_j-f\|_{H^{m+\sigma}}
\leq C\|f_j-f\|_{H^m}^{1-\sigma}
      \|f_j-f\|_{H^{m+1}}^\sigma\longrightarrow0.\] Since \(m+\sigma>(m-1)+3/2\), Sobolev embedding yields strong \(C^{m-1}\) convergence on fixed regular compact subdomains. Alternatively, compactness can be extracted directly below the \(H^{m+1}\) regularity threshold. The argument is local away from \(t=0\) and uses the regular coordinate domains already considered; it makes no uniform-in-\(k\) assertion at the singular boundary.

The existence of a classical solution needs much less than \(C^{m-1}\) when \(m\) is large. The erroneous embedding is therefore Minor, with a supported local repair using the paper’s own displayed bound. This repair is conditional on that bound and does not resolve Problem 1 or certify all higher-order boundary statements. It is not a separate Major claim based merely on an inaccurate regularity sentence.

# Additional checks and rejected readings

The leading relative-entropy sign in (5.42), p. 84, was checked directly. For the conservative variables \((\rho,\rho v^1,\rho v^2)\), the entropy Hessian is \[H=\rho^{-1}
\begin{pmatrix}|v|^2+c^2&-v^1&-v^2\\-v^1&1&0\\-v^2&0&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Differentiation with respect to \(\underline{w}\) gives the column \(\big(\rho/c,\rho v^1/c+\rho,\rho v^2/c\big)^\mathsf T\). Its transpose times \(H\) equals \((c-v^1,1,0)\). As the first component of \(QF^1\) is zero, the contraction equals \[p(\rho)-p(\bar\rho)-p'(\bar\rho)(\rho-\bar\rho)
+\rho(v^1-\bar v^1)^2\geq0.\] The sign of \(\partial_1\bar{\underline{w}}>0\) therefore permits discarding the singular leading term as done in the proof. This is a verified algebraic mechanism, conditional on the prior bounds and the justified weak formulation; the entire relative-entropy treatment at \(t=0\) was not independently reconstructed.

A possible objection based on restricting a centered fan to a smaller \(u\)-interval was rejected after inspecting the actual p. 24 image and source line 1337. Although a region subscript contains an extra prime, the displayed union uses the same interval \(u'\in[0,u^*]\), and the conclusion compares regions at that common extent. A counterexample using unequal interval lengths would not test that reading of the proposition. No additional scientific finding is assigned to this notation.
