Abstract: The reviewed object is arXiv:2207.10554v3. Three auxiliary statements require correction: a Lorentz bound uses the boundary dimension in place of the ambient dimension, an averaging map cannot preserve norms of arbitrary signed sequences, and a Harnack lower estimate lacks a sign hypothesis. Explicit counterexamples are given below. Their identified uses in the main arguments admit more restricted formulations, so these are classified as Minor. A separate ambiguity concerning the admissibility of corona roots remains a request for evidence, not a verified counterexample to the main regularity theorem. The scientific disposition remains pending.

# Main statements, quantifiers, and dependency checks

Write \(d=n+1\geq3\), \(L=-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}A\nabla\), and \[2^*=\frac{2d}{d-2},\qquad 2_*=\frac{2d}{d+2}.\] The coefficient matrix is real, bounded, strongly elliptic, and need not be symmetric. The distinction between \(L\) and \(L^*\) is essential. Theorem 1.11 assumes a corkscrew domain with \(n\)-Ahlfors regular boundary, and also assumes either that \(\Omega\) is bounded or that \(\partial\Omega\) is unbounded. For \(p>1\), with \(p'\) its Hölder conjugate, solvability of \((D^L_{p'})\) yields the Poisson–Dirichlet estimate with \(\widetilde N_{2^*}w\), \(\mathcal C_{2_*}(\delta H)\), and \(\mathcal C_2(F)\). The initial construction uses bounded compactly supported interior data; the appendix supplies the density passage. The restriction on the domain is retained throughout this review.

Theorem 1.22 relates this Dirichlet problem to Poisson–Regularity estimates for the adjoint operator and to local landscape and Green function estimates. The open exponent ranges in those assertions are not replaced by endpoint claims. In particular, Remark 1.13 uses the conjugates \(q'\) and \(r'\) in the Carleson functionals; treating these as \(q\) and \(r\) would produce a spurious criticism.

The following checks support the main Poisson argument.

1. The near-pole estimate on pp. 24–25 uses Definition 2.12(vii), the energy mappings \(L^{-1}:L^{2_*}\to L^{2^*}\) and \(L^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}:L^2\to L^{2^*}\). Their exponents have \(1/2_*+1/2^*=1\) and \(d/2-d/2^*=1\), giving the required factor \(\delta\) for the divergence data. It does not require the erroneous quantitative Lorentz assertion identified in Problem 1.

2. The choice \(p_1>p\) in the reverse Hölder argument gives \(p_1'<p'\). Consequently \(p'/p_1'>1\), which is the range needed for the subsequent maximal-operator estimate. Far-field contributions carry a positive Hölder exponent and a summable geometric factor.

3. In the duality step on p. 31, \(L^*v=H\) and \(Lw=-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}F\) give \[\int_\Omega Hw=\int_\Omega A^T\nabla v\cdot\nabla w
            =\int_\Omega F\cdot\nabla v.\] The adjoint orientation is consistent. The analogous identity with vector test data and the Caccioppoli estimate account for the \(\delta^{-1}F\) term in Poisson–Regularity.

Theorem 1.33 concerns a bounded corkscrew domain with uniformly rectifiable boundary and a DKP matrix with an arbitrary finite global DKP constant. In the orientation of its statement, \((D^{L^*}_{p'})\) implies \((R^L_p)\). The reduction on pp. 33–34 passes through the conormal functional of Proposition 4.4. Section 5 constructs Lipschitz pieces with small local coefficient control; Theorem 6.3 then invokes a local regularity theorem. Section 7 estimates the almost elliptic extension and completes the conormal estimate. Retaining \(\mathcal C_2(F)\), including in the term \(T_{222}\), is substantive: a bound only with \(\mathcal C_{p'}(F)\) would not supply the same conclusion when \(p\leq2\).

The reading checked the stated hypotheses, the direction of these reductions, the scaling, signs, conjugate exponents, and the near/far decompositions. It is not a claim to have independently re-proved every external geometric or elliptic theorem used in Sections 5–7.

# Problem 1: the Lorentz estimate has the wrong dimension

## Statement and exact location

Definition 2.12(v), p. 21, asserts an \(L^\infty\) estimate for the Green representation with \[H\in L^{n/2,1}(\Omega),\qquad F\in L^{n,1}(\Omega),
 \qquad
 \|w\|_\infty\lesssim
 \|H\|_{L^{n/2,1}}+\|F\|_{L^{n,1}}.\] Here the ambient space is \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\). The same exponents occur in the acquired TeX, so this is not a text-extraction error.

## Counterexample within the geometric and elliptic assumptions

Take \(n=2\), \(\Omega=B_1(0)\subset\mathbb R^3\), \(A=I\), \(F=0\), and \(H_\varepsilon=\mathbf1_{B_\varepsilon(0)}\), where \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). The domain is smooth and bounded; the coefficient matrix is uniformly elliptic; and the data are bounded with compact support in the domain. The zero Dirichlet solution is radial: \[w_\varepsilon(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle \frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}
 -\frac{\varepsilon^3}{3}-\frac{r^2}{6},
       &0\leq r\leq\varepsilon,\\[4pt]
 \displaystyle \frac{\varepsilon^3}{3}\left(\frac1r-1\right),
       &\varepsilon\leq r\leq1.
 \end{cases}\] The value and radial derivative agree at \(r=\varepsilon\), so no interface measure is introduced by the distributional Laplacian. The solution is regular at the origin, vanishes at \(r=1\), and satisfies \(-\Delta w_\varepsilon=H_\varepsilon\). It belongs to \(W^{1,2}_0(B_1)\). Equivalently, integration against \(G(0,y)=(4\pi)^{-1}(|y|^{-1}-1)\) gives \[w_\varepsilon(0)=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}
                  -\frac{\varepsilon^3}{3}.\] Since \(L^{1,1}=L^1\) up to a fixed convention-dependent factor, \[\|H_\varepsilon\|_{L^{1,1}}
 =\frac{4\pi}{3}\varepsilon^3,\qquad
 \frac{w_\varepsilon(0)}{\|H_\varepsilon\|_{L^1}}
 =\frac{3}{8\pi\varepsilon}-\frac1{4\pi}\longrightarrow\infty.\] No constant depending only on this fixed domain and operator can make the displayed estimate true.

## Correction and impact

For the scalar data, the critical Lorentz exponent indicated by the Green kernel is \(d/2=(n+1)/2\). Indeed the upper bound \(G(x,y)\lesssim |x-y|^{2-d}\) and rearrangement give \[|L^{-1}H(x)|
 \lesssim \int_0^\infty s^{2/d-1}H^*(s)\,ds
 \lesssim \|H\|_{L^{d/2,1}}.\] This verifies the scalar \(L^\infty\) repair; it does not by itself prove every assertion about the vector data or the energy-space membership on unbounded domains. Those assertions should be stated with their own hypotheses.

The confirmed impact is Minor. The erroneous uniform bound is a preliminary assertion, while the main quantitative near-pole estimate uses the correctly scaled energy mappings in part (vii). Bounded compactly supported data still have a valid Green representation. The counterexample does not contradict Theorem 1.11.

# Problem 2: signed sequences invalidate Lemma A.9

## Statement and mechanism

On pp. 58–59 the passage from sequences to functions is \[F(u)(x)=\frac{\sum_Q u_Q(x)\mathbf1_{w^*(Q)}(x)}
                   {\sum_Q\mathbf1_{w^*(Q)}(x)}.\] Lemma A.9, (A.10)–(A.11), claims two-sided norm equivalences for every sequence. Its proof assumes \(u_Q\geq0\) without loss of generality. Such a reduction is valid for an upper estimate, but fails for a lower estimate of a signed averaging map.

## An admissible overlap and a nonzero kernel vector

Use \(\Omega=\mathbb R^3_+\). To respect the dyadic normalization on p. 17, at generation \(j\) partition its boundary into half-open squares of geometric side \(2^{-j-1}\) and assign them the manuscript’s scale \(\ell(Q)=2^{-j}\). Their diameter is \(2^{-j}/\sqrt2\leq\ell(Q)\), their area is \(\ell(Q)^2/4\), and the nested partitions satisfy the thin-boundary condition. These boundary scales differ from the ordinary geometric side lengths of the interior Whitney cubes. Use the following \(1/2\)-Whitney decomposition. For every \(a=2^m\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\), tile the slab \([64a,128a]\) by cubes of side \(a\) with vertical intervals \([ja,(j+1)a]\), \(j=64,\ldots,127\), and the standard horizontal grid. Interiors are disjoint and the slabs cover all positive heights. In the notation of p. 19, \(\widehat c=1/2\) gives \(k=3\), and each such cube satisfies \[32\,\operatorname{diam}I
 \leq\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(I,\partial\Omega)
 \leq256\,\operatorname{diam}I,\] because \(32\sqrt3<64\) and \(127<256\sqrt3\).

Let \[I=[0,1]^2\times[64,65],\quad
 Q=[0,\tfrac12)\times[0,\tfrac12),\quad
R=[\tfrac12,1)\times[0,\tfrac12).\] The boundary cubes are disjoint and have assigned scale \(\ell(Q)=\ell(R)=1=\ell(I)\), although their geometric sides are \(1/2\). Both have distance \(64\) from \(I\), hence satisfy (2.7), whose bound is \(512\operatorname{diam}I\). Thus \(I\subset w(Q)\cap w(R)\) and also \(I\subset w^*(Q)\cap w^*(R)\).

Set \(u_Q=\mathbf1_I\), \(u_R=-\mathbf1_I\), and all other coordinates equal to zero. Then \(F(u)=0\) everywhere, whereas the discrete non-tangential sequence functional is strictly positive on \(Q\cup R\). For \(p=q=2\), write \(V_Q=m(w^*(Q))\) and \(V_R=m(w^*(R))\). Both are finite and positive, and the squared non-tangential sequence norm is \(\tfrac14(V_Q^{-1}+V_R^{-1})>0\). The discrete Carleson sequence norm is also finite and nonzero. It is positive on \(Q\) and locally bounded by \(4(\sqrt{V_Q}+\sqrt{V_R})\). A dyadic ancestor containing \(Q\) or \(R\) and a distant point \(\xi\) has area bounded below by a constant times \(|\xi|^2\). The functional therefore decays as \(O(|\xi|^{-2})\), whose square is integrable on the two-dimensional boundary. Both claimed equivalences therefore fail for these signed data.

## Repair and actual downstream use

For arbitrary signed sequences, retain the bounds from the sequence norm to the norm of \(F(u)\). They follow from \(|F(u)|\leq F(|u|)\) and the positive estimates. The reverse inequalities can be stated for coordinatewise nonnegative sequences: bounded overlap then gives \(u_Q\leq C F(u)\) on \(w^*(Q)\), which is precisely the pointwise argument in the manuscript.

This correction has bounded impact. In Lemma A.18 on p. 61, the duality argument may first replace the function by its absolute value and choose nonnegative dual sequence coordinates. Replacing those coordinates by their absolute values preserves their sequence norm and improves their pairing with a nonnegative function. The corrected positive version of Lemma A.9 therefore suffices there.

In Appendix A.3 on p. 63, the difference \(S(H)-h^k\) need not be nonnegative. However, the needed convergence follows from the upper inequality \[\|\mathcal C_q^d F(S(H)-h^k)\|_{L^p}
 \lesssim\|\mathcal C_q^s(S(H)-h^k)\|_{L^p}\longrightarrow0.\] The displayed two-sided comparison is unnecessary for that conclusion. This is a Minor correction to an overbroad lemma, not a counterexample to Proposition 2.4 or the main PDE results.

# Problem 3: a sign condition is missing from the Harnack estimate

Lemma 2.10, p. 20, allows an arbitrary weak solution of \(Lu=0\) in \(B^*=(1+\theta)B\) and includes \[\left(\frac{1}{|B^*|}\int_{B^*}|u|^q\right)^{1/q}
 \lesssim\min_{\frac12B}|u|.\] There is no nonnegativity hypothesis. Choose \(\Omega=B_3(0)\subset\mathbb R^3\), \(B=B_1(0)\), \(\theta=1\), \(A=I\), \(q=2\), and \(u(x)=x_1\). This is a smooth harmonic function satisfying all the displayed assumptions, but \[\frac{1}{|B_2(0)|}\int_{B_2(0)}x_1^2\,dx=\frac45>0,
 \qquad \min_{B_{1/2}(0)}|x_1|=0.\] Thus the lower comparison is false.

A fixed-sign hypothesis is necessary for the Harnack part. The local boundedness and Hölder estimates do not require that extra sign restriction in the same way. On pp. 24–25, the Harnack comparison is applied to the positive Green function; the boundary arguments also explicitly use nonnegative solutions or elliptic measure. The counterexample therefore does not invalidate those applications. The identified impact is Minor. The review does not claim a new proof of the full rough-coefficient Harnack theorem.

# An unresolved root condition in the corona argument

On p. 36 the family \(\mathcal B(\varepsilon,\theta)\) contains cubes with either excessive bilateral flatness error or \(\lambda_\Omega(Q)>\theta\). The local construction begins with \(R\notin\mathcal B\). In contrast, the explicit inductive definition of \(\mathrm{Top}\) on p. 41 imposes only \(b\beta_\sigma(k_1R)\leq\varepsilon\) on the roots it selects.

The proof of Lemma 5.28 on p. 43 uses the assertion that a cube in the tree has either \(\lambda_\Omega(S')\leq\theta\) or a parent with that bound. At the root this requires particular care if the root itself were allowed to be coefficient-bad. The earlier condition \(R\notin\mathcal B\) may be intended to remain implicit when defining \(\mathrm{Top}\); that reading should be made explicit and checked against the packing and buffer estimates.

The requested evidence is a precise root admissibility rule and its propagation through Lemmas 5.22, 5.28, and Theorem 6.3. This review has not completed a counterexample satisfying all the geometric choices, nor a full repair of the corona construction. Accordingly this is recorded as Needs Evidence, with no confirmed Major or Critical finding attributed to it. Its position on the dependency chain to Theorem 1.33 warrants resolution even though the main theorem has not been disproved.
