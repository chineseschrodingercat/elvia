Abstract: The review examines the reduction argument and its simple-group applications in the 52-page preprint arXiv:2507.19618v1. A nonbasic spin character of \(\widetilde A_{10}\), labelled by the strict partition \((9,1)\), is rational valued and does not extend to \(\widetilde S_{10}\). It contradicts the exhaustive alternatives in Theorem 7.15(iv), leaving a missing case in the stated proof of the alternating-group application. This is a Major proof issue with a specific local repair. A separate local error concerns the split-class description and spin-character values in Lemma 7.3 and Theorem 7.5. Exact Clifford-matrix calculations and rational symmetric-function calculations support the findings. Neither finding is a counterexample to the Feit conjecture or to the main reduction theorem. Independent scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions, hypotheses, and the dependency chain

The paper uses \(\mathcal G=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\). For a nonabelian finite simple group \(S\) and its universal covering group \(X\), Definition 3.1 requires, for every \(\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(X)\), a proper subgroup \(U<X\) and \(\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(U)\) such that \(U\) is self-normalizing and intravariant in \(X\), the stabilizers of \(\chi\) and \(\eta\) in \(\Gamma\times\mathcal G\) agree, where \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Aut}(X)_U\), and the specified central Galois-triple relation holds. Theorem 3.2(a) supplies the latter relation when these conditions hold and \([\chi|_U,\eta]=1\).

Theorem A concerns primitive characters and assumes the inductive Feit condition for nonabelian composition factors. The subsequent passage to the Feit conjecture for all characters uses the stronger hypothesis in Corollary B that all relevant simple groups involved satisfy that condition. This distinction matters when induction passes to subgroups. Theorem C asserts the inductive condition for all alternating simple groups. Its concluding proof, Theorem 7.17 on page 37, explicitly combines Theorems 6.3, 7.15, 7.16, and Lemma 5.2. Thus the nonbasic spin-character case of Theorem 7.15 is part of a principal application.

Equation reference: eq:presentation. Write \(D(n)\) for strict partitions of \(n\), \(O(n)\) for partitions with odd parts, and \(\ell(\lambda)\) for the number of parts. The parity convention is that \(\lambda\in D_{\bar0}(n)\) when \(n-\ell(\lambda)\) is even. For a strict partition, \(z_\lambda=\prod_j\lambda_j\). The double cover used on page 33 has central element \(z\) and generators \(t_1,\ldots,t_{n-1}\) with \[
 z^2=1,\qquad t_j^2=z,\qquad (t_jt_{j+1})^3=z,\qquad
 t_jt_k=zt_kt_j\quad (|j-k|>1).\] A spin character has \(z\) acting as \(-1\). For \(\lambda\in D_{\bar0}(n)\) the self-associate irreducible character \(\varphi^\lambda\) of \(\widetilde S_n\) restricts as \(\psi^\lambda_++\psi^\lambda_-\) to \(\widetilde A_n\), with distinct irreducible constituents interchanged by odd conjugation. Basic spin characters correspond to \(\lambda=(n)\).

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (A rational nonextendible nonbasic spin character is omitted).  Theorem 7.15(iv), page 36, is false as an exhaustive alternative. The last paragraph of its even-partition proof incorrectly concludes that the character field always has degree two.

## Claim and complete input

For every nonbasic \(\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}^-(\widetilde A_n)\), Theorem 7.15 claims a suitable \(m\ne n/2\) and \(\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}^-(\widetilde A_{n-m,m})\) satisfying multiplicity one, equality of fields, and one of two alternatives. In paraphrase, these are: (a) \(\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}\) and both characters extend to the respective symmetric covers; or (b) \([\mathbb{Q}(\chi):\mathbb{Q}]=2\), both characters are nonextendible, and their Galois and outer-conjugation orbits coincide.

Equation reference: eq:input. Take \[
 n=10,\qquad \lambda=(9,1),\qquad
 \chi=\psi^\lambda_+\quad\hbox{or}\quad\psi^\lambda_- .\] This is a strict partition, is nonbasic, and satisfies \(n-\ell(\lambda)=8\). The group \(\widetilde A_{10}=2A_{10}\) is the universal cover in the range used by the alternating-group application; \(n\ne6\). The constituents are genuine irreducible spin characters of degree 64. No hypothesis requires a nonsquare discriminant.

## Exact calculation and contradiction

Equation reference: eq:field. Corollary 7.7(i), page 34, gives the field \[
 \mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}\!\left(\sqrt{(-1)^{(10-2)/2}z_{(9,1)}}\right)
        =\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt9)=\mathbb{Q}.\] This conclusion also follows from the corrected classical value formula, rather than depending on the faulty piecewise formula discussed below. The sum of the two constituents is \(\varphi^{(9,1)}|_{\widetilde A_{10}}\); their difference is supported on the exceptional split cycle type and has values \(\pm3\) there. The classification and difference formula are also recorded in [Retained latex: \cite[Theorems 4.0.7--4.0.8]{Livesey}]. The remaining values follow from rational Schur \(Q\)-function coefficients, so both constituents are rational valued.

Equation reference: eq:Q. For an exact finite check, the reviewer expanded \[
 q_r=\sum_{\mu\in O(r)}\frac{2^{\ell(\mu)}}{z_\mu}p_\mu,
 \qquad Q_{(9,1)}=q_9q_1-2q_{10}.\] The resulting constituent degree is 64; on cycle type \((9,1)\) the values are \(1\) and \(-2\), with the labels interchanged on the other alternating class. The complete odd-cycle-type calculation appears in Appendix 5 (#app:values). It gives squared norm one and mutual inner product zero, and all values are rational. These checks corroborate the analytic classification; a norm calculation by itself would not construct an arbitrary irreducible character.

Neither constituent extends to \(\widetilde S_{10}\). Indeed, \(\varphi^{(9,1)}\) restricts to two distinct constituents, which are interchanged by the nontrivial coset of \(\widetilde A_{10}\). A character extending to \(\widetilde S_{10}\) would be invariant under that conjugation. Consequently alternative (a) fails by nonextendibility and alternative (b) fails by [eq:field] (#eq:field). These failures concern \(\chi\) alone; no choice of \(m\) or \(\eta\) can make either alternative true.

## Alternative readings and bounded impact

The example is not a basic spin character, a small exceptional cover, or an excluded \(n=6\) case. Interpreting the quadratic-field notation as allowing \(\mathbb{Q}\) does not repair the explicit degree-two requirement. Nor does fixing the overlap in Theorem 7.5 change the square discriminant. The source itself treats square discriminants separately for basic spin characters in Theorem 7.16(b), page 37, but no corresponding case is provided in Theorem 7.15.

The displayed structural theorem is therefore false, and the printed argument for the nonbasic part of Theorem C does not cover this case. This establishes a Major issue in that core application. It does not prove Theorem C false, does not affect the logical content of the conditional reduction in Theorem A, and supplies no counterexample to the Feit conjecture.

## Precise repair and its checked range

For the usual universal double-cover range \(n\ge8\), add a third alternative to Theorem 7.15(iv): \[\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}(\eta)=\mathbb{Q},\qquad
 \chi\text{ does not extend to }\widetilde S_n,\quad
 \eta\text{ does not extend to }\widetilde S_{n-m,m}.\] It arises when the even-partition discriminant is a square. Choose \(\eta\) as in Corollary 7.13(ii). Multiplicity one remains valid, and Corollary 7.11 identifies the two fields. Odd conjugation interchanges the two constituents on each side, while inner conjugation fixes them; hence the stabilizers in the subgroup-preserving automorphism group agree. Both rational characters are fixed by all of \(\mathcal G\), so \((\Gamma\times\mathcal G)_\chi=(\Gamma\times\mathcal G)_\eta\). With the same proper intravariant parabolic subgroup, self-normalizing inside \(X=\widetilde A_n\), Lemma 3.3(i) and Theorem 3.2(a) complete this case. This repairs the omitted route to the inductive condition; it does not make the original two-case assertion true. Exceptional universal covers must still be handled separately as in Lemma 5.2. No author adoption or corrected journal version has been verified.

Problem 2 (Missing average term and missing split classes).  Lemma 7.3 and Theorem 7.5, page 33, omit the extra splitting and the rational average contribution when a strict even partition also has distinct odd parts.

## Claim and admissible boundary

## Independent matrices in the stated presentation

Let \[X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\] and let \[\gamma_1=X\otimes I,\quad \gamma_2=Y\otimes I,\quad
 \gamma_3=Z\otimes X,\quad \gamma_4=Z\otimes Y,\quad
 \gamma_5=Z\otimes Z.\] Set \(e_j=i\gamma_j\), \(t_j=(e_j-e_{j+1})/\sqrt2\), and \(z=-I_4\). The identities \(e_je_k+e_ke_j=-2\delta_{jk}I_4\) verify every relation in [eq:presentation] (#eq:presentation). Thus the computation uses the cover and generator normalization actually stated in the manuscript.

Equation reference: eq:matrix. Put \(E=(e_1+\cdots+e_5)/\sqrt5\) and \(P_\pm=(I_4\mp iE)/2\). Then \(E^2=-I_4\), \(E\) anticommutes with each \(t_j\), and \(P_\pm\) are rank-two projections commuting with even products. The full-generator commutant has dimension one, and the commutant of the even generators \(t_jt_{j+1}\) has dimension two, as verified by exact linear algebra. Complete reducibility therefore identifies the rank-two summands as distinct irreducible constituents. The irreducible self-associate four-dimensional representation is labelled by \((5)\), the only strict even partition of five. The retained irreducibility script records this check; its exact path and hash are included in the evidence inventory. For the manuscript’s canonical five-cycle lift \(w=t_1t_2t_3t_4\), exact multiplication yields \[
 \operatorname{tr}(w)=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(P_\pm w)=\frac{1\mp\sqrt5}{2}.\] The characteristic polynomial of \(w\) is \(x^4-x^3+x^2-x+1\). The script checks all defining relations symbolically and evaluates [eq:matrix] (#eq:matrix) exactly. Neither ordering of the two overlapping printed branches produces these values. In particular, \(1/2\) cannot be the value of a finite-group character, since a rational algebraic integer is an integer.

The class count also changes. The five-cycles of \(A_5\) already form two classes. In \(2A_5\), a lift has centralizer of order ten: it is the preimage of the cyclic centralizer of order five, and that preimage is abelian. Its class has size \(120/10=12\). There are 48 elements above all five-cycles, hence four classes, rather than the two claimed for \(\widetilde C_{(5)}\) in Lemma 7.3(ii).

## Correct formula, interpretation, and impact

Equation reference: eq:correct. Write \(\Delta_\lambda=\psi^\lambda_+-\psi^\lambda_-\). The correct relation on the even cover is \[
 \psi^\lambda_\pm(g)=
 \frac{\varphi^\lambda(g)\pm\Delta_\lambda(g)}{2},\] where \(\Delta_\lambda\) vanishes off the exceptional cycle type \(\lambda\) and, on the chosen exceptional lift, is \(i^{(n-\ell(\lambda))/2}\sqrt{z_\lambda}\) up to the constituent labels. When \(\lambda\in O(n)\), the average term need not vanish. Equation [eq:correct] (#eq:correct), with the extra alternating classes retained, gives [eq:matrix] (#eq:matrix); central multiplication changes the signs and odd conjugation interchanges the constituents. The difference formulation in [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 4.0.8(3)]{Livesey}] is consistent with this correction.

This is a numerical and class-theoretic error, not merely a missing citation. Giving priority to either printed branch does not fix it. The missing rational term does not change the quadratic field generated by the values, so Corollary 7.7’s field description can survive. No additional loss of the principal reduction has been established from this error alone; its severity is therefore Minor. Actual inspection of page 33 confirms that the overlapping branches and the two-class statement are present in the typeset source, rather than artifacts of text extraction.
