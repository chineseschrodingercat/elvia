# The local coalgebra in the Tate example

Equation reference: eq:local. Take \(k\) of characteristic zero and \(X=\mathbb A^1\), with its standard \(\mathbb G_m\)-action. The coherent dualizing complex is \(\omega_X=k[x]\,dx[1]\). Local cohomology at the origin is computed by the localization complex \[\bigl[k[x]\longrightarrow k[x,x^{-1}]\bigr]\otimes k\,dx[1].
 \] The unshifted complex has its nonzero cohomology in degree \(1\); after the dualizing shift, the quotient lies in degree \(0\). Hence it has a basis \[e_n=x^{-n-1}dx,\qquad n\geq0.\] With the usual induced action on functions, \(x\) and \(dx\) have weight \(-1\), so \(e_n\) has weight \(n\). This gives precisely the positive grading used in Lemma 6.2.2 and Corollary 6.2.4, pp. 499–500.

The residue pairing is \[\langle e_n,x^m\rangle=\operatorname{Res}_{x=0}(x^{m-n-1}dx)
 =\delta_{nm}.\] It is perfect in each graded component. One must retain this graded qualification: the unrestricted linear dual of an infinite direct sum is a product. The paper explicitly formulates perfection degree by degree.

Equation reference: eq:coproduct. Dualizing polynomial multiplication gives \[\Delta(e_n)=\sum_{a+b=n}e_a\otimes e_b,\qquad
 \epsilon(e_n)=\delta_{n0}.
 \] If \(v\) is the standard weight-one generator of the usual symmetric coalgebra, the identification is \(e_n\leftrightarrow v^n/n!\), because \[\Delta\!\left(\frac{v^n}{n!}\right)
 =\sum_{a+b=n}\frac{v^a}{a!}\otimes\frac{v^b}{b!}.\] Thus the occurrence of \(\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Std})\), rather than the symmetric algebra on the dual representation, is correct. The characteristic-zero hypothesis makes this divided-power normalization legitimate. The degree-zero term is the coaugmentation and must not be discarded when passing between augmented and non-unital notation.

This verifies the classical one-dimensional instance needed for Corollary 6.2.4. It does not independently establish the full statement of Lemma 6.2.2 for every perfect complex and every derived base, nor Proposition 4.5.2 relating local distributions to factorization homology.

# The functional equation and its shifts

Fix the spin line \(S=\Omega_C^{1/2}\) from Section 2.4.1. On a component of \(\operatorname{Bun}_1\) with \(\deg L=d\), the displayed definitions in Section 6.5 use \[E=R\Gamma(C,L\otimes S),\qquad
 E'=R\Gamma(C,L^{-1}\otimes S).\] The spin twist is necessary. The short fiber description in the first paragraph of Section 6.5.1 omits it, but the displayed definition and the proof on p. 508 include it; the calculation here uses those explicit formulas.

Equation reference: eq:rank, eq:serre. Riemann–Roch and \(\deg S=g-1\) imply \[\operatorname{rank}(E)=\chi(C,L\otimes S)=d,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(E')=-d.
 \] Serre duality gives \[E^\vee\simeq E'[1],\qquad E'\simeq E^\vee[-1].
 \] Consequently the derived self-intersection of the zero section in \(\operatorname{Tot}(E^\vee)\) is \(\operatorname{Tot}(E')\). The word derived is indispensable: replacing this self-intersection by its classical intersection would lose the cohomological information in [eq:serre] (#eq:serre).

Equation reference: eq:ft. Write \(\pi\) and \(\pi'\) for the corresponding projections. The functoriality in Section 3.1.5 and the constant/delta transform in Example 3.1.3 give, with the paper’s convention \(\langle n\rangle=\Pi^n[n](n/2)\), \[\pi_! k_{\operatorname{Tot}(E)}
 \simeq z_{E^\vee}^{*}\delta_{E^\vee}\langle-2d\rangle
 \simeq \pi'_! k_{\operatorname{Tot}(E')}\langle-2d\rangle.
 \] The first shift is the sum of the two rank contributions \(-d\); its Tate twist is \(-d\), and its parity change is even. The second isomorphism uses the derived Cartesian square and the stated base-change formalism. These Fourier and base-change results are inputs to this focused check, not results independently reproved here.

Now apply the normalization in (6.5.1): \[\begin{split}
 P_X^{\mathrm{norm}}
 &\simeq \pi_!k_{\operatorname{Tot}(E)}\langle d+g-1\rangle\\
 &\simeq \pi'_!k_{\operatorname{Tot}(E')}
       \langle-2d+d+g-1\rangle\\
 &=\pi'_!k_{\operatorname{Tot}(E')}
       \langle-d+g-1\rangle
 \simeq P_{X'}^{\mathrm{norm}}.
 \end{split}\] This agrees with (6.5.2) and Proposition 6.5.1. The argument permits positive, zero and negative \(d\); virtual rank is not required to be a nonnegative dimension.

As a boundary check, take \(C=\mathbb P^1\), \(S=\mathcal O(-1)\), and \(L=\mathcal O(d)\). Then \[h^0(L\otimes S)=\max(d,0),\qquad
 h^1(L\otimes S)=\max(-d,0).\] The classical truncations of the total spaces have these respective affine dimensions. Their compact-support shifts satisfy \[-2\max(d,0)=-2\max(-d,0)-2d,\] including \(d=0\), where both complexes vanish. This checks the sign in [eq:ft] (#eq:ft). Such fiber calculations alone do not prove a canonical global isomorphism; that role is played by the derived Fourier construction.
