Abstract: This review concerns the acquired 57-page arXiv version 2210.15047v2, dated 20 June 2024, by Francesco C. De Vecchi, Luca Fresta and Massimiliano Gubinelli. Two major, localized proof issues are established: the printed finite-dimensional stochastic-quantization identity has incompatible fermionic signs, and the local part of the graded flow need not satisfy the decay estimate used in its induction. Three further local defects concern Wick indexing, a cutoff definition and a claimed lower asymptotic. These findings do not establish that the final continuum existence or clustering theorems are false. The initial scientific decision remains pending independent review.

# Scope, conventions and assessment

The reviewed source is [paper], arXiv v2, not a byte-verified copy of the 61-page published article, Annals of Probability 53(3), 906–966 (2025), DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1714. The official arXiv history says that v2 corrected proof errors and typos in the earlier version. The present findings refer to v2 itself; no independent formal erratum was located in the bounded metadata search, and no assertion of exhaustive absence is made.

All source body and appendix pages 1–54 were read, and bibliography entries on pp. 54–57 were examined. The analysis traced the Grassmann probability space, conditional expectations, stochastic calculus, truncated flow, localization, fixed-point argument, cutoff limits and correlation estimates. The source’s ambient state is positive on a \(C^*\)-algebra; its Grassmann observable algebra need not be closed under adjoints. Thus failure of a classical positive-probability interpretation is not counted as an error.

Throughout this report, source page numbers are PDF page numbers. Independent exact computations use reviewer-written exterior-algebra and CAR matrix calculations; no author code was executed. The two Major findings below concern core derivations. Neither is a counterexample to the final continuum Theorems 1.1 and 1.3.

# Problem 1: incompatible signs in stochastic quantization (Major)

## Source and hypotheses

Relevant locations are Definition 2.5 (p. 11), Definitions 2.15–2.16 (p. 13), Definition 3.1 (pp. 14–15), Lemma 3.7 and (3.11) (pp. 16–17), Proposition 3.8 (p. 18), Proposition 3.10 and Remark 3.11 (p. 18), and Theorem 3.12, (3.18)–(3.19) (pp. 19–20). The latter identity is used in the passage to Gibbs functionals; compare (3.30), p. 23, Remark 4.26, p. 43, Lemma 4.29, p. 45, and the generating-functional calculation on p. 48.

Use \(\mathfrak h=\mathbb C^2\), coordinate conjugation \(\Theta\), and \[J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G_s=sJ,\quad 0\leq s\leq1,\qquad
 V=q e_1e_2,\quad q=\tfrac14.\] The left derivatives specified in the source give \(D_1V=q e_2,\ D_2V=-q e_1\). Its explicit row action (3.17) therefore gives \(JDV=-q(e_1,e_2)\). Choose a norm-compatible Grassmann Brownian motion with \(\omega(X_s^1X_t^2)=\min(s,t)\), zero initial field, and its usual past filtration.

For completeness these data are realizable in a positive ambient space. On the CAR Fock space over \(L^2([0,1];\mathbb C^2)\), set \(\Xi(h)=a^*(Jh)+a(\overline h)\) and \(X_s(f)=\Xi(\mathbf1_{[0,s]}f)\), with \(J\) acting componentwise. The vacuum covariance is the stated skew covariance, the fields jointly anticommute, and the vacuum conditional expectations onto the past CAR algebras are positive, unital bimodule maps with the tower property. Moreover \(\|\Xi(h)\|\leq2\|h\|\); this supplies norm compatibility and norm continuity. These are the actual hypotheses used here, not an assumption that an arbitrary classical Gaussian process is Grassmann-valued.

## A solution of the printed FBSDE with the wrong Gibbs law

Equation reference: eq:fbsde, eq:gibbs. Write \[a(s)=1-q(1-s),\qquad
 Y_s=\int_0^s a(r)^{-1}\,dX_r,\qquad
 \Psi_s=a(s)Y_s.\] Since \(a\geq3/4\), these are bounded, adapted, norm-continuous fields on the entire interval. \(Y\) is a martingale and \(\Psi_1=Y_1\), whence \(\mathbb E_s\Psi_1=Y_s\). Consequently \[d\Psi_s=qY_s\,ds+dX_s
       =-J\mathbb E_s[DV(\Psi_1)]\,ds+dX_s .\] This is exactly the sign of (3.18). Its terminal covariance is \[\omega(\Psi_1^1\Psi_1^2)
   =\int_0^1\frac{dr}{(1-q+qr)^2}
   =\frac1{1-q}=\frac43.                         \] On the other hand \(V^2=0\), \(\omega(X_1^1X_1^2)=1\), and the Gibbs functional asserted in (3.19), evaluated at \(P=e_1e_2\), is \[\frac{\omega(P(X_1)e^{V(X_1)})}{\omega(e^{V(X_1)})}
       =\frac1{1+q}=\frac45.                     \] The denominator is nonzero. Thus Theorem 3.12, with the printed conventions, fails already for an even quadratic potential in dimension two.

An exact four-dimensional CAR representation independently checks [eq:gibbs] (#eq:gibbs): take \(a_1=a\otimes I,\ a_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\otimes a\), where \(a=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and \(\psi_1=a_1-a_2^*,\ \psi_2=a_2+a_1^*\). Their squares and anticommutator vanish and their vacuum ordered pairing is one. This calculation verifies the terminal algebra; the continuous-time construction above supplies the stochastic hypotheses.

## Location of the sign conflict and a second check

For the same \(P=e_1e_2\), the printed operator is \(D_J^2P=\sum J_{ij}\partial_i\partial_jP=-2\). Lemma 3.7’s plus-\(\tfrac12D_J^2\) drift would give expectation \(-t\), whereas the defining covariance gives \(+t\). Moving the odd first derivative past the odd increment in (3.11) requires a minus sign. The order cannot be repaired by silently changing the meaning of derivative composition: the Berezin calculation on p. 9 fixes it.

There is also a direct check of Proposition 3.10. The conditional exponential is \[U_s(\varphi)=1+q(1-s)+q\varphi_1\varphi_2,
 \qquad D\log U_s=\frac{q}{1+q(1-s)}D(\varphi_1\varphi_2).\] Its printed negative-\(G\) drift yields \[\omega(\Psi_1^1\Psi_1^2)
       =\int_0^1[1+q(1-r)]^2\,dr=\frac{61}{48},\] again different from \(4/5\). All normalizing factors are positive real numbers in this example.

## Counterevidence, repair and impact

Reversing that drift gives the correct \(4/5\) in this example. The source itself uses a plus-\(G\) drift in the generating-functional calculation on p. 48, evidence of a plausible intended convention. One can consistently change covariance or derivative conventions, but that requires changing the affected formulas together; it does not validate the incompatible formulas as printed.

The severity is Major because the finite-dimensional identification underlies the claimed Gibbs interpretation. A repair must synchronize the Itô rule, backward equation, effective potential, FBSDE and subsequent flow identities. Absolute-value analytic estimates might survive such a repair, but that has not been proved by this review. The continuum construction and final clustering assertions are not disproved by [eq:fbsde] (#eq:fbsde)–[eq:gibbs] (#eq:gibbs).

# Problem 2: the local graded estimate need not decay (Major)

## Source, allowed data and decisive calculation

The relevant locations are Definitions 4.2 and 4.5 (pp. 30–31), (4.11)–(4.18) and Theorem 4.13 (pp. 34–35), the last inequality of (4.23) (p. 36), and (4.24) and Corollary 4.15 (p. 37). The norm estimate is subsequently used in Proposition 4.20 (pp. 39–40).

Theorem 4.13 allows a nonzero local linear boundary value \(K^{(1)}\); it does not impose the particular cubic initial condition of Corollary 4.15. Choose \[\begin{gathered}
 d=1,\quad \gamma=\tfrac1{10},\quad \beta=\tfrac3{20},\quad
 \alpha=\tfrac{23}{50},\quad \kappa=\tfrac1{50},\\
 n=24,\quad \varepsilon=0,\quad K^{(1)}=mI,\quad K^{(3)}=0,
 \quad m=\tfrac14 .
 \end{gathered}\] Here \(I\) denotes the local identity kernel on the internal components. It is translation and reflection invariant, satisfies the degree-one antisymmetry condition (which is vacuous), and has the required finite initial norm since \(\alpha-\beta>0\). The five margins in (4.14)–(4.16) are respectively \[\tfrac1{100},\quad\tfrac7{100},\quad\tfrac{23}{50},
 \quad\tfrac{177}{100},\quad\tfrac{54}{25}.\] Thus none of the strict parameter assumptions is being dropped.

At zero spatial Fourier frequency the source covariance has the form \(\widehat G_s(0)=g(s)U\), where \(g(0)=0,\ g(\infty)=1\) and \(U^2=I\). On each charge block, the \(\Theta\)-real orthonormal basis \[u_1=(1,1)/\sqrt2,\qquad u_2=(i,-i)/\sqrt2\] turns the original \(U=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) into \(B=iJ\); in particular \(B^2=I\). This uses the actual model block, rather than substituting a covariance with a different square.

Equation reference: eq:local. All degrees \(k\geq3\) remain zero. The Laplacian of a linear map vanishes. The derivative component \(\mathrm{Ren}\) vanishes on constant spatial fields; the zero-frequency coefficient is therefore exactly the local coefficient. If \(A_\ell(s)\) denotes that coefficient in one block, the printed triangular equation and local boundary conditions give \[\frac{d A_{\ell+1}}{dg}
       =-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}A_jBA_{\ell-j},\qquad
 A_0=mI,\qquad A_\ell(0)=0\quad(\ell\geq1).\] Induction, or differentiation, gives the exact solution \[A_\ell(s)=(-1)^\ell m^{\ell+1}g(s)^\ell B^\ell.   \] The reviewer checked the recurrence through order 24 with exact rational matrix algebra. For example \[A_{24}(\infty)=4^{-25}I\ne0,\qquad
 \alpha-\beta-24\kappa=-\tfrac{17}{100}.\] Already \(A_{16}(\infty)=4^{-17}I\ne0\) while \(\alpha-\beta-16\kappa=-1/100\).

The claimed (4.23)–(4.24) would bound these local coefficients by constants times \(2^{-17s/100}\) and \(2^{-s/100}\), respectively, tending to zero. The local operator norm dominates its action on constant fields. A derivative component cannot cancel that action. This contradicts [eq:local] (#eq:local) and proves failure of the uniform graded-norm induction for admissible boundary data.

## Counterevidence, repair and impact

The last integration in (4.23) runs from \(0\) to \(t\). When an integrable forcing has nonzero total integral, its primitive approaches a nonzero constant; it cannot be bounded by a purely decaying exponential. Bounds with a constant term, or a different boundary condition for high-order local coefficients, would avoid this particular mistake. Changing the overall contraction sign merely changes signs in [eq:local] (#eq:local), leaving the obstruction.

This example does not show nonexistence of the finite-order flow. Nor does it refute the special cubic initial data \(K^{(1)}=0,\ K^{(3)}=\lambda\psi^3\) in Corollary 4.15. It does refute the general estimate invoked to prove that corollary. A repair must treat the high-order local terms separately or establish the needed estimate for the actual cubic data by an additional argument, then revisit the norm input to the fixed-point proof. The Major rating concerns that central unsupported derivation, without asserting failure of the final continuum theorem.

# Three local defects

## Problem 3: Wick recursion omits the last variable (Minor)

Definition 2.16, p. 13, sums contractions over \(j=1,\ldots,n\) for a product of \(n+1\) variables. For \(n=1\) its only covariance is the self-covariance, which vanishes, so the displayed recursion gives \(:X_t^1X_t^2:=X_t^1X_t^2\). For the legitimate Brownian field above its expectation is \(t\), violating the martingale assertion of Proposition 2.17. The needed term is \(-G_t(e_1,e_2)\); with the printed sign convention the contraction index should range over \(j=2,\ldots,n+1\). This is a local indexing repair and is distinct from the calculus sign conflict in Problem 1. It does not independently refute a final theorem.

## Problem 4: the stated smooth cutoff cannot exist (Minor)

On p. 22 immediately before Definition 3.16, the source asks for a smooth nondecreasing function \(\min_\varepsilon(a)\) equal to \(a\) on \([0,A]\), \(A=\varepsilon^{-1}\), and equal to \(A\) on \([A+1,\infty)\). Monotonicity forces the function to be identically \(A\) on \([A,A+1]\). Its left derivative at \(A\) is one and its right derivative zero. Thus it is not even \(C^1\).

A smooth saturation can instead start before \(A\), or have a higher plateau. For example use derivative one up to \(A-1\), smoothly decrease the derivative to zero on \([A-1,A+1]\), with integral one on that interval, and keep the value \(A\) afterwards. A smooth symmetric transition realizes these conditions for \(A\geq1\). This changes the stated identity interval; the covariance and kernel bounds should be checked for the actual replacement. The defect is in the exact regularizer definition, not a demonstrated impossibility of the ultraviolet limit.

## Problem 5: the lower asymptotic needs a mass condition (Minor)

Theorem 1.3, p. 5, permits any \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\) with \(\|\chi\|_{L^1}=1\). Remark 1.4, p. 6, then uses the free two-point comparison \((\varepsilon\vee r)^{-2\gamma}\) to obtain a lower-order comparison for the interacting field. The stated test-function assumption does not imply nonzero integral.

Choose a nonconstant compactly supported smooth \(\varphi\) and \(\chi=\partial_1\varphi/\|\partial_1\varphi\|_{L^1}\). Then \(\int\chi=0\). Take a nonvanishing opposite-charge free covariance component \(K(r)\); this avoids the simpler zero pairing between equal charges. For \(0<2\gamma<d\), the source’s heat-kernel representation gives \(|\nabla^2K(z)|\leq C|z|^{-2\gamma-2}\) near zero. Double mean-zero cancellation and Taylor’s formula give, when \(\varepsilon\ll r\), \[\left|\int\chi(u)\chi(v)K(r+\varepsilon(v-u))\,du\,dv\right|
       \leq C\varepsilon^2 r^{-2\gamma-2}.\] With \(\varepsilon=r^2\) and \(r\downarrow0\), the ratio to \(r^{-2\gamma}\) tends to zero. Hence the free lower comparison used in the remark is false for its stated test-function class.

A nonnegative unit-mass mollifier and an explicit nonzero component pairing are plausible intended restrictions; the lower estimate and small-coupling constants must then be justified for that class. The upper difference bound in Theorem 1.3 is not contradicted by this cancellation example.

# Coverage and limits

Several structural checks support the intended framework. The covariance is \(\Theta\)-skew, the norm-compatible realization exists in a positive ambient CAR algebra, and the subcritical parameter constraints have nonempty interior when \(\gamma<\min(d/4,1)\). The additional \(\gamma<1\) restriction corresponds to the use of a mass counterterm without derivative counterterms. These checks do not repair Problems 1–2.

The review examined the kernel estimates, localization gain, weighted norms, finite-volume coupling, fixed-point bounds and claimed ultraviolet and infinite-volume passages. The external constructive-field-theory, functional-analysis and Besov-space results cited by the authors were not independently reproved, and no claim of exhaustive verification of every auxiliary estimate is made. The source’s restrictions on small coupling, regularity losses, cutoff order and the order of limits are retained. Neither missing code nor the absence of a classical probabilistic interpretation is treated as a defect.
