Abstract: The paper constructs local commuting bubbles that preserve the polynomial differential-form spaces individually. The stated shape-regularity-only stability estimate fails in dimension one for the actual averaging operator: a continuous piecewise quadratic on a two-interval mesh gives an unbounded norm ratio while the shape constant stays equal to one. This is a Major qualification of the uniform-stability claim. A boundary-link gauge requires a local convention correction, and several displayed formulas need minor corrections. The algebraic decomposition and the principal support and commutation mechanisms withstand the checks described below. A highest-degree cancellation also verifies the potentially delicate trimmed-space preservation step, conditional on correctly defined weights.

# P1. Uniform stability needs a dimension or local-size hypothesis (Major)

Proposition 8.1, p. 1260, asserts that the norms of \(W^k\) and the squared sum of bubble norms are bounded by a constant depending on the mesh only through \[c_{\mathcal T}=\max_{T\in\Delta_n(\mathcal T)}
\frac{\operatorname{diam}(T)}{\operatorname{diam}(\mathbb B_T)},\] with an additional dependence on the fixed dimension. Lemma 8.2, p. 1261, derives local diameter comparability from this constant. This implication fails in dimension one, and the failure propagates to the operator actually constructed.

Fix \(\Omega=(0,1)\) and the mesh \[T_0=[0,\varepsilon],\qquad T_1=[\varepsilon,1],
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12.\] Every interval is its own largest one-dimensional ball up to endpoints, so \(c_{\mathcal T}=1\). Equations (2.12)–(2.13), pp. 1231–1232, give normalized cell densities \(z_T=|T|^{-1}\mathrm dx\) and their arithmetic average at a vertex. In particular, \[z_0=\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_{T_0}\mathrm dx,\quad
z_\varepsilon=\tfrac12\bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}\chi_{T_0}
 +(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\chi_{T_1}\bigr)\mathrm dx,\quad
z_1=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\chi_{T_1}\mathrm dx .\] These are not volume-weighted averages over the vertex patch. The sign convention \(z_{v,\emptyset}=-z_v\) on p. 1233 and the definition following (2.23), p. 1235, therefore yield \[W^0u=\sum_{v\in\{0,\varepsilon,1\}}\lambda_v\int_\Omega u\,z_v .\]

Take \[u_\varepsilon(x)=
\begin{cases}
6(x/\varepsilon)(1-x/\varepsilon),&0\le x\le\varepsilon,\\
0,&\varepsilon\le x\le1.
\end{cases}\] This is a continuous piecewise quadratic with single-valued traces, and it vanishes at both physical endpoints. It belongs to the stated domain and to \(\mathcal P_2\Lambda^0(\mathcal T)\). Its average on the short interval is one, so the three averaging coefficients are \((1,\tfrac12,0)\). Consequently, \[(W^0u_\varepsilon)(x)=
\begin{cases}
1-x/(2\varepsilon),&0\le x\le\varepsilon,\\
(1-x)/(2(1-\varepsilon)),&\varepsilon\le x\le1.
\end{cases}\] Exact integration gives \[\|u_\varepsilon\|_2^2=\frac{6\varepsilon}{5},
\qquad
\|W^0u_\varepsilon\|_2^2=\frac{1+6\varepsilon}{12},
\qquad
\frac{\|W^0u_\varepsilon\|_2^2}{\|u_\varepsilon\|_2^2}
=\frac{5(1+6\varepsilon)}{72\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\infty .\] Thus Proposition 8.1 and Lemma 8.4 do not hold for this construction with the stated dependence of the constant. The uniform-stability conclusion attributed to the construction in Theorem 2.3 and Section 9 must be qualified. This is an operator counterexample, not merely a missing justification for Lemma 8.2.

The obstruction already occurs at fixed polynomial degree two and on a fixed domain. It also occurs in nested refinements: retain \([1/2,1]\), repeatedly bisect the adjacent left cell toward \(1/2\), and put the same zero-endpoint quadratic on the shrinking cell. Its average contributes \(1/2\) at the shared vertex and creates a fixed-size output on the retained interval.

The counterexample does not refute the algebraic decomposition, commutation, space preservation, or boundedness on each fixed mesh with a mesh-dependent constant. It also does not disprove the existence of some different stable decomposition. For \(n\ge2\), a shared facet contains an edge, and shape regularity compares adjacent element diameters; bounded star cardinality then gives the local comparability used in the proof. The statement can be restricted to \(n\ge2\), or dimension one can be treated with an explicit adjacent-size comparability assumption. Changing the averaging weights would require a new argument and is not offered here as a verified repair.

# P2. The terminal link gauge needs a boundary exception (Minor)

The complex (2.7), pp. 1229–1230, is completed by the oriented terminal functional (2.9). The text observes that the final arrow can be redundant for a boundary link. Nevertheless, Lemma 2.1 imposes \(\delta_{k+1}\widetilde c=0\), and Section 3 explicitly invokes (2.9) for \(\delta_1\) when the link is one-dimensional, immediately after (3.8), p. 1240. If that terminal functional is retained for boundary links, the asserted primitive with this gauge need not exist.

Let \(\Omega\) be a single triangle and let \(f\) be a boundary vertex. Its link is one oriented edge \(e=[a,b]\). In the ordered bases, \[\partial_1 e=(-1,1),\qquad \partial_0(-1,1)=0 .\] The unique primitive of \(c=(-1,1)\) is \(\widetilde c=e\), but (2.9) gives \(\delta_1e=\pm1\), not zero. The same contradiction occurs for the actual right-hand side in (3.8): \[\beta_a=\frac{\lambda_a-\lambda_b}{2},\quad
\beta_b=-\beta_a,\quad
\mu_e=\frac{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}{2}.\] The incidence equation forces this nonzero \(\mu_e\), whereas the terminal gauge would force it to vanish.

The natural correction is precise: on a boundary link, use the ordinary augmented cochain complex of a ball, ending in \(\mathcal C_q(f^*)\to0\), where \(q=\dim f^*\). Retain the terminal integral functional for a closed sphere link. A ball has no top cycle, so its top primitive is already unique; the terminal condition is unnecessary. Under this convention the displayed witness has a solution, and the subsequent identities in Sections 4–5 use \(\partial\mu=\beta\) without imposing the incompatible integral condition. This correction must be propagated through (3.8) and (4.6)–(4.7).

This is a local convention repair, consistent with the paper’s own remark about the final arrow, rather than evidence against a corrected bubble transform. The distinct terminal integral in the full-zero-trace differential-form complex on \(\Omega_f\) is a relative-cohomology condition and must not be deleted. The repair of the link convention and its immediate algebraic consequences were checked; this is not a claim to have independently certified every statement of a rewritten general theorem.

# P3. Local formula and index corrections (Minor)

The following corrections are directly determined by the surrounding definitions.

1. The displayed formula for the exterior derivative on p. 1224 omits the alternating sign. Its summand should be \((-1)^{j+1}\partial_{v_j}u_x(v_1,\ldots,\widehat v_j,\ldots,v_{k+1})\). For \(\alpha=x\,\mathrm dx\) and \(v_1=v_2=\partial_x\), the printed sum equals two, whereas an alternating two-form must vanish on repeated arguments. Later identities use the usual signed exterior calculus; this local omission is not a counterexample to those identities.

2. On p. 1228, the zero-dimensional link case is \(m=n\), not \(m=n-1\), because the preceding convention is \(f\in\Delta_{m-1}\) and \(\dim f^*=n-m\). Section 4.1 uses the correct case.

3. In the proof of Lemma 4.4, p. 1248, the condition \(0\le j<m-n\) must be \(0\le j<n-m\), as in the preceding induction and Corollary 4.2.

4. In (5.4), p. 1251, the target form degree must be \(k-j-1\), not \(k\): \(Q^k_{e,f}\) uses \(\Pi_{j+1}\) and lowers form degree by \(j+1\). The immediately preceding definition states this degree correctly.

# Positive checks and the preservation mechanism

Several potentially serious objections do not survive closer examination.

The affine averaging map stays in each source simplex containing \(f\). At \(b=0\) it restricts to the identity on \(f\), and \(\int z_f=1\) supplies the asserted trace. Averaged pullbacks commute with \(\mathrm d\). The alternating sums defining \(K_{m,f}\) have the required support: on \(\lambda_i=0\), terms indexed by \(g\) and \(g\cup\{i\}\) have equal restricted pullbacks and opposite signs. The top-cell residual in (2.26) legitimately has the larger dependence domain \(\Omega_f^E\); the paper does not claim dependence only on the top cell.

For the weights in Section 4, full vanishing tangential trace on \(\partial\Omega_f\) is explicitly distinguished from support on \(\Omega_f\). That distinction removes the boundary term in the Stokes calculation in Lemma 5.2. The identities involving \(\delta^+\) and the common-face restriction \(G_f(y,L_gx)=G_g(y,L_gx)\) support the sum rearrangements in Sections 5 and 7, subject to the corrected link convention. In Lemma 7.2, the reversible change \(f'=f\setminus\{i\}\), \(e=e_0\cup\{i\}\) accounts for both the index sets and the leading minus sign. Exact finite checks of this signed bijection on one- and two-simplex complexes in dimensions two through four agree. The two lemmas in Section 7 then reproduce \(C_{m-1}^k\) from (2.20), and the differences telescope.

On p. 1255 the parentheses in the alternative expression for \(K_{m,f,g}^k\) matter: the derivative applies to the entire wedge product. Each summand has the form \[\mathrm d\bigl(\eta\wedge T^ku\bigr)+\eta\wedge T^{k+1}\mathrm du\] up to a common sign. Here \(\eta\) is a Whitney-type \((q-1)\)-form and \(T^k\) is the normalized order-reduction operator with \(q\) physical-variable slots. Thus commutation follows from \(\mathrm d^2=0\).

The same expression also explains the trimmed-space property, which is less immediate than ordinary polynomial-degree preservation. On each simplex let \(X\) be the radial vector field, let \(u_r\) be the homogeneous degree-\(r\) part of a trimmed polynomial \(k\)-form, and let \(\eta_1\) be the degree-one part of \(\eta\). The trimmed-space criterion is \(\iota_Xu_r=0\). Removing \(b^q\) from the mixed pullback cancels its \(q\) physical-slot factors. Its remaining evaluation map is affine in \(x\), say \(A(y)x+c(y)\). Moving the radial argument past those \(q\) slots gives \[\iota_X(T^ku)_r=0,\qquad
\iota_X(T^{k+1}\mathrm du)_{r-1}
=(-1)^q(r+k)(T^ku)_r .\] Since \(\iota_X\eta_1=0\), the highest-degree term \(H_{r+1}=\eta_1\wedge(T^ku)_r\) satisfies \(\iota_XH_{r+1}=0\). Cartan’s identity and the wedge sign therefore give \[\iota_X\mathrm dH_{r+1}=(r+k)H_{r+1},\qquad
\iota_X\bigl(\eta_1\wedge(T^{k+1}\mathrm du)_{r-1}\bigr)
=-(r+k)H_{r+1}.\] The unwanted highest-degree contraction cancels. Ordinary degree is at most \(r\), so the result is in \(\mathcal P_r^-\Lambda^k\). The zero-map cases \(q>k\) are handled by the same identities, with \(T^ku=0\). This supplies a direct explanation of the preservation step without assuming a stronger, unstated degree-lowering property for \(R\) or \(Q\).

As a concrete check, triangulate the unit square by its diagonal from \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\), take \(f=(0,0)\), and use the corrected boundary convention. For \(u=x\,\mathrm dy-y\,\mathrm dx\in\mathcal P_1^-\Lambda^1\), exact rational integration and the weight equations give, on both triangles, \[K_{1,f}^1u=\frac{(1-y)\mathrm dx+(x-1)\mathrm dy}{6},
\qquad
\mathrm dK_{1,f}^1u=K_{1,f}^2\mathrm du
=\frac13\,\mathrm dx\wedge\mathrm dy .\] The separate terms have nonzero highest-degree contractions, which cancel in their sum. This finite calculation supports, but does not replace, the preceding analytic argument.

For the stability proof under local diameter comparability, the powers of the local scale cancel in every operator type. An \(R\) term has factors \(h^{-j}\), \(h^n\) and \(h^{j-n}\). A \(Q\) term can be rewritten using \[\rho_g^{j+1}\mathrm d\!\left(\frac{\phi_e}{\rho_g^{j+1}}\right)
=\mathrm d\phi_e-(j+1)\mathrm d\rho_g\wedge\frac{\phi_e}{\rho_g},\] whose size is \(O(h^{-j-1})\); the normalized averaging factor has size \(O(h^{j+1})\). The \(W\) coefficient and Whitney norm contribute inverse powers \(h^{k-n/2}\) and \(h^{n/2-k}\). Bounded local incidence counts give uniform finite overlap and uniformly bounded inverses for the finite coefficient systems with entries \(0,\pm1\). No increasing polynomial degree enters these constants. The full kernel estimate imported from [7] in Lemma 8.5 was not independently reconstructed; these are checks of its use, the geometric conditions, and the scaling, not a claim to have reread that external proof.

# Supporting checks

The supported assessment is Major, on account of P1: the published construction does not satisfy its asserted shape-only uniform bound in an allowed dimension. P2 and P3 are local corrections. The checked support, commutation, decomposition, and trimmed-preservation mechanisms give positive counterevidence against a broader claim that the construction is intrinsically invalid. A dimension qualification and a consistent boundary-link convention are the principal requirements for a revision.
