Abstract: The analytic reduction from exponential completeness to weak-star approximation is examined in the exact March 2025 author manuscript. One peripheral substantive claim is false: elliptic semigroups do not invariably fail the analogous eigenvector-completeness question. The dilation semigroup supplies an explicit counterexample. This does not contradict the main theorems, whose hypotheses are non-elliptic. This Minor finding is distinguished from notation-level concerns and from the geometric classification not independently re-proved here.

# Definitions and checks of the central framework

The source uses conformally invariant Hardy spaces: \(f\in H^p(\Omega)\) when \(|f|^p\) admits a harmonic majorant, and \(H^\infty(\Omega)\) consists of bounded holomorphic functions. For a non-elliptic semigroup its univalent Koenigs map satisfies \[h(\varphi_t(z))=h(z)+t,\qquad \Omega+t\subseteq\Omega\quad(t\geq0).\] Thus \(e^{\lambda h}\circ\varphi_t=e^{\lambda t}e^{\lambda h}\). The frequency set is \(\Lambda_p(\Omega)=\{\lambda:e^{\lambda z}\in H^p(\Omega)\}\), including \(\lambda=0\), and \(E_p\) is its linear span. Keeping the constant eigenfunction is essential.

Lemma 2.4 correctly uses weak-star continuity of the inclusion into reflexive \(H^p\), \(1<p<\infty\), followed by equality of weak and norm closures of a linear subspace. Density in \(H^1\) follows by the continuous dense inclusion from any such \(H^p\). Proposition 3.3’s rescaling is consistent: \(\Lambda_q=(p/q)\Lambda_p\), since \(|e^{(p/q)\lambda z}|^q=|e^{\lambda z}|^p\).

For \(S=\{|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi/2\}\), the key transform in Theorem 4.3 is \[\int_0^\infty e^{itz}e^{-\beta t}\,dt=(\beta-iz)^{-1},
 \qquad \operatorname{Re}\beta>\pi/2.\] Its tail is uniformly bounded by \(e^{-R(\operatorname{Re}\beta-\pi/2)}/(\operatorname{Re}\beta-\pi/2)\). Finite atomic approximations are uniformly bounded on \(S\) and converge pointwise, giving weak-star convergence by the criterion on page 10. The pole is \(-i\beta\); reversing the sign of \(t\) supplies the other side of the strip. This verifies the transform mechanism conditional on the cited rational-density theorem.

Theorems 5.5 and 5.8 distinguish complement connectivity in a half-plane from that in a strip. Their respective hypotheses remain distinct in the proof assembly on page 50. The no-half-plane case of Theorem 1.3 follows from \(|e^{\lambda z}|=e^{\operatorname{Re}(\lambda z)}\): a nonzero bounded frequency supplies a containing half-plane. This is a bounded-frequency argument and does not exclude the finite-\(p\) examples in Section 10.

# A peripheral substantive error

Problem 1 (Elliptic semigroups can have complete eigenvectors).  Section 1, page 3, in the paragraph beginning It is here worth emphasizing, asserts that the analogous completeness questions always have negative answers in the elliptic case because of a common fixed point. This universal assertion is false under the eigenvector-completeness formulation motivating the paper.

## Counterexample and verification of hypotheses

Set \[\varphi_t(z)=e^{-t}z,\qquad z\in\mathbb D,\quad t\geq0.\] Each map is a holomorphic disk self-map; \(\varphi_0\) is the identity; \(\varphi_t\circ\varphi_s=\varphi_{t+s}\); and dependence on \(t\) is uniform on compact subsets. The point \(0\) is an interior fixed point for every \(t\). This is a nontrivial elliptic semigroup under the source’s definition on page 8.

For \(f_n(z)=z^n\), including \(n=0\), \[C_{\varphi_t}f_n=e^{-nt}f_n,\qquad \Gamma f_n=-n f_n.\] The derivative is a limit of scalar multiples of a fixed vector, so exists in each \(H^p\) norm, \(1\leq p<\infty\). All these eigenvectors are bounded and belong to every finite-\(p\) Hardy space. Their span contains all analytic polynomials, which are norm-dense in \(H^p(\mathbb D)\). The source itself uses this polynomial density in Lemma 2.4 on page 11.

The weak-star assertion also has a direct bounded-approximation proof. Given \(f\in H^\infty(\mathbb D)\), choose \(r_j\uparrow1\). The Taylor series of \(f(r_jz)\) converges uniformly on the closed unit disk. A Taylor polynomial \(P_j\) can therefore satisfy \(\|P_j-f(r_j\cdot)\|_\infty\leq1/j\). Then \[\|P_j\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\infty+1,\qquad P_j(z)\longrightarrow f(z)
 \quad(z\in\mathbb D).\] The criterion on page 10 gives weak-star convergence. No norm-strong continuity on all of \(H^\infty\) is assumed.

## Alternative readings, impact, and repair

The additive Koenigs relation for non-elliptic semigroups must not be imposed on this elliptic example; elliptic linearization is multiplicative. If the disputed sentence refers only to the additive formulation, that formulation does not define an elliptic analogue and cannot justify a universal negative statement. A shared value does not obstruct density of a linear span, since coefficients are free and the constant eigenfunction is included.

The finding is Minor because its scope is peripheral: Theorems 1.1–1.4 explicitly assume non-ellipticity, and the checked proof assembly uses neither the elliptic assertion nor its consequences. This grading does not depend on ease of editing. A justified local replacement is: The additive Koenigs-domain formulation is restricted to non-elliptic semigroups; elliptic eigenvector completeness is a separate question and can hold, as for the dilation semigroup. Only this introductory replacement has been verified.

# Other checks and limitations

The arbitrary-measure approximation in Theorem 4.6, pages 18–19, needs attention to a possible atom at \(R\), since the displayed intervals are half-open. Choosing \(R\) strictly above compact support removes this issue before forming the approximation. The final bound should use finite total variation \(|\mu|([0,R])\), rather than the modulus of complex mass. These available choices do not supply a counterexample to the theorem and are not counted as additional central defects.

For Proposition 10.4, choosing the separating smooth function compactly supported away from \([0,M]\) supplies the required decay. In Proposition 10.6, a subsequence with almost-everywhere convergence suffices for the contradiction. Neither observation refutes the stated conclusion.

No separate supplement, released computational repository, or correction of the elliptic assertion was located in the bounded searches retained with the evidence. This is not proof of absence.
