Abstract: The retained 13 July 2022 author version of Hamel and Rossi’s Fisher–KPP symmetry paper is examined in full. The truncation, reflection, and directional propagation arguments have a coherent mathematical structure, and no counterexample to Theorems 2.1–2.4 is obtained. A quantitative statement accompanying the counterexamples nevertheless exceeds the stated \(C^1\) hypotheses: an admissible logarithmic reaction term has a different front delay. The general-data extension also needs an explicit thickness condition; its two displayed distance conditions alone admit a counterexample. Four local proof corrections and a degenerate case of the final conjecture are identified. The distinction between a false quantitative assertion, an ambiguous extension scope, and the unrefuted main symmetry results is maintained. The initial recommendation is revision; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Proof structure and affirmative checks

Proposition 3.1 separates invasion inside expanding neighborhoods of the thick core from extinction beyond speed \(c^*\) measured from the initial support. The retracting supersolution in Lemma 3.2 uses a finite sum of traveling fronts and the subadditivity of the zero-extended KPP reaction. The coefficient two can be implemented by repeating each front twice; it does not require \(2\varphi\) to be an exact solution.

Lemma 4.1 removes the distant part of the initial support while keeping a uniformly small \(C^1\) error on a half-cylinder. Its exterior-distance estimate has a speed strictly larger than \(c^*\) when the auxiliary interior speed is chosen sufficiently close to \(c^*\). This leaves room for fixed spatial constants and one extra time unit. The use of a later \(L^\infty\) bound to control an earlier gradient is compatible with the nonnegative difference equation: Harnack controls an earlier neighborhood, and interior estimates then control its gradient.

After rotation about a nearest point, the opening condition places the relevant support inside a sufficiently flat cone. Truncation puts it inside a half-cylinder. A line in a nonzero transverse gradient direction avoids that cylinder, so a separating reflection plane and the Hopf lemma yield a contradiction. A second reflection establishes monotonicity; an entire-time limit and the strong maximum principle give strictness. Problems 3–5 below concern local formulas in this chain.

Lemma 4.3’s stability under finite Hausdorff distance survives a direct comparison of the two nearest-point directions and chord directions. The needed last estimate is a nonpositive upper limit; an unnecessary intermediate nonnegativity need not be used. The Gaussian estimate for the shrinking closed balls in Proposition 4.6 is also valid. The balls in (4.36) really are closed.

In the converse part of Theorem 2.4, a hypothetical uniformly small directional gradient allows a fixed positive seed to advance by \(1/(4\varepsilon)\) every fixed recovery time. That time is independent of \(\varepsilon\), which contradicts the finite propagation-speed bound. The selected indices must diverge: otherwise all selected points lie in finitely many compact segments, where invasion and parabolic estimates force gradients to zero. This implicit step can be supplied.

The long- and short-chord estimates in §6 use the uniform increment hypothesis (2.6). The scale in (6.8)–(6.9) is \(\sqrt[3]R\), not \(3\sqrt R\). The localization in Theorem 7.2 is directional, and its full vertical-line gradient conclusion uses equality in (7.1) when the vertical coordinate tends to \(-\infty\). Finally, the Hessian conclusion follows from compactness and the rank at most one of every limiting Hessian. The review does not infer the converse.

# A quantitative claim beyond the stated regularity

Problem 1 (Minor: the universal logarithmic phase is false).  On p. 21, immediately after (4.34), the paper states that the phase of the Heaviside solution satisfies \[\zeta(t)=c^*t-\frac{3}{c^*}\log t+x_\infty+o(1)\] under the standing \(C^1\) KPP assumptions. There are reaction terms allowed by (1.2) for which this phase cannot satisfy (4.34).

Admissible example. Set \(f(0)=0\) and, for \(0<s\le1\), let \[f(s)=s\bigl[1-(1-\log s)^{-2}\bigr].\] Writing \(q=1-\log s\), direct differentiation gives \[f'(s)=1-q^{-2}-2q^{-3},\qquad
 \left(\frac{f(s)}s\right)'=-\frac{2}{s q^3}<0.\] Thus \(f\in C^1([0,1])\), \(f'(0)=1\), \(f(1)=0\), \(f>0\) on \((0,1)\), and \(c^*=2\).

The precise external input is Bouin–Henderson [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 1.2(ii), p.~3]{bh}], with \(r=3\), \(A=1\), and Heaviside initial data, which meet their (1.4). Let \[\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
 \alpha(\alpha-1)=1,\qquad
 X(t)=2t-\left(\alpha+\frac12\right)\log t.\] Their (1.9) gives \[\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}
                  v(t,X(t)+L)=0.\] The solution \(v\) is nonincreasing in space by comparison. For the phase printed in the reviewed paper, \[\zeta_{\rm print}(t)-X(t)
       =(\alpha-1)\log t+x_\infty+o(1)\longrightarrow+\infty.\] For each fixed \(L\), therefore, \(v(t,\zeta_{\rm print}(t))\le v(t,X(t)+L)\) eventually. Taking the time upper limit and then \(L\to\infty\) proves \[v(t,\zeta_{\rm print}(t))\longrightarrow0.\] But (4.34), evaluated at that same spatial point, would give \(v(t,\zeta_{\rm print}(t))\to\varphi(0)>0\). This is a contradiction to the joint printed assertion, not just an objection about a citation.

Impact and repair boundary. The quantitative regularity claim needs correction. The nonplanar construction of Proposition 4.4 uses convergence with some phase, and Proposition 4.6 needs only its leading speed \(\zeta(t)\sim c^*t\); neither uses this exact logarithmic coefficient. No counterexample to either proposition or the main symmetry theorems is asserted. Delete the unsupported quantitative parenthesis, or state and verify additional near-zero assumptions for that claim. The bounded-support, higher-dimensional sharp asymptotic mentioned on p. 17 also deserves a hypothesis check, but the one-dimensional Heaviside example is not presented as its direct counterexample.

The first five pages of [bh], including the hypotheses and the decisive theorem, were actually read and viewed. Its whole proof was not independently reconstructed. The contradiction above is an analytic consequence of that explicit external theorem. Exact symbolic calculations check the parameter match and derivatives, not the PDE theorem.

# The general-data extension needs an explicit scope

Problem 2 (Minor: clarify the retained thickness hypothesis).  On p. 31, the sentence extending Corollary 2.5 lists \[d_H(\{u_0\ge h\},\operatorname{supp}u_0)<\infty,
 \qquad d_H(U,\operatorname{supp}u_0)<\infty,\] where \(U\) obeys (2.5)–(2.6). These two conditions alone are insufficient. The second condition of (8.1), the thickness of a high-level set, must remain in force.

A counterexample to the two-condition reading. Take \(N=2\), \(f(u)=u(1-u)\), \(H=\{x_2\le0\}\), and \[S=\bigcup_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z^2\\k_2\le0}}
       \overline B_{\exp(-|k|^2)}(k),\qquad u_0=\mathbf1_S.\] The union is locally finite and closed, with positive-measure interior seeds. For any \(h\in(0,1]\) the high-level set and support both equal \(S\), and \(d_H(H,S)<\infty\). Thus both printed distances are finite, with the comparison subgraph \(H\) given by \(\gamma=0\). For each \(\delta>0\), however, \(S_\delta\) is bounded or empty: sufficiently remote balls are disjoint and have radii below \(\delta\). The omitted uniform thickness fails.

The same heat-kernel estimate used in Proposition 4.6 gives some finite \(A>0\) with \[u(1,x)\le A e^{-|x|^2/8},\qquad
 u(t,x)\le A e^{-|x|+2t}\quad(t\ge1).\] For completeness, the weighted initial mass is finite because \[\sum_{k_2\le0}
  |B_{\exp(-|k|^2)}|\,e^{(|k|+1)^2/4}<\infty.\] The first bound follows from \(|x-y|^2/4\ge |x|^2/8-|y|^2/4\). For any unit vector \(\xi\), the Gaussian is bounded by \(e^{-\xi\cdot x+2}\); comparison with the linearized equation and optimization over \(\xi\) give the second bound. Consequently \(u\) tends uniformly to zero beyond every speed \(c>2\) measured from the origin.

If every translated large-time limit depended only on \(x_2\), parabolic compactness would imply \(\|\partial_{x_1}u(t,\cdot)\|_\infty\to0\). Let \(K\ge1\) be a global large-time gradient bound. A value at least \(1/4\) gives a value at least \(1/8\) on a ball of radius \(1/(8K)\). KPP invasion then supplies a fixed recovery time \(\tau\) after which the center is at least \(1/2\). The fixed ball \(B_1(0)\subset S\) supplies the initial seed. Once \(|\partial_{x_1}u|\le\varepsilon\), each recovery permits a further displacement \(L=1/(4\varepsilon)\) along \(e_1\) while retaining a value at least \(1/4\). Choose \(\varepsilon<1/(8\tau)\). Iteration produces points moving at speed \(L/\tau>2\) whose values stay positive, contradicting the Gaussian propagation bound. Hence a nonvertical limiting profile exists.

Interpretive boundary and repair. The preceding paragraph of the article may intend to keep all of (8.1) implicit for this sentence. On that reading, the example is excluded and no counterexample to the intended extension is supplied. This is why the item is classified as a scope clarification rather than an unconditional refutation of the extension. Explicitly retain \(d_H(\{u_0\ge h\},\{u_0\ge h\}_\delta)<\infty\). The example demonstrates why it cannot simply be dropped, and does not use a null-set modification of the initial data.

# Local proof corrections

Problem 3 (Minor: the seed radius in Lemma 4.1).  On p. 11, the comparison solution is initialized by \(\mathbf1_{B_1}\), although the assumptions only provide \(B_\delta(x_0)\subset U\). For \(\delta=1/4\) and \(U=B_\delta(x_0)\), domination of the unit-ball initial value already fails in the intervening annulus. It also fails at suitable positive small times. Replace the initial datum by \(\mathbf1_{B_\delta}\). The waiting time already may depend on \(\delta\), so the lemma’s quantifiers and intended conclusion survive.

Problem 4 (Minor: an allowed zero denominator).  Theorem 4.2, Step 2, p. 15, ends its estimate with a constant containing \(|\beta'|/|\beta_N|\). Only \(\beta'\ne0\) is assumed, so \(\beta_N=0\) is allowed. Keep the constant supplied by direct substitution: \[\left(\frac{|\beta'|-\vartheta}{|\beta_N|+\vartheta}
                   +1\right)|\bar x|.\] For fixed \(\vartheta>0\) it is finite, and the coefficient of the large time variable remains positive after choosing \(\vartheta\) sufficiently small. This repairs the separation argument without changing its geometric conclusion.

Problem 5 (Minor: original and rotated coordinates are mixed).  In Step 6, p. 17, the limit is defined using \(u(t_n+t,x_n+M_nx)\), with \(M_ne_N=e_n\to e\). It follows that \[u_\infty(0,x)=\psi(Mx)=\Psi(x_N),\] whereas the printed text uses \(\Psi(x\cdot e)\) and then translates along \(e\) in the rotated coordinates. Use \(e_N\) consistently in this paragraph, or take the unrotated entire-time limit from its definition. The strong-maximum-principle argument then retains its strict-monotonicity conclusion.

Problem 6 (Minor: the support-to-level-set distance is missing).  In the general-data proof on p. 31, put \(A=\{u_0\ge h\}\) and \(S=\operatorname{supp}u_0\). The paper uses \(L=d_H(A,A_\delta)+1\), although the coordinate origin is a projection onto \(S\). The sufficient uniform choice is \[L>d_H(S,A)+d_H(A,A_\delta).\] Both terms are finite under the full (8.1). For a concrete failure of the printed choice, take two unit balls centered at \(0\) and \(100e_1\), assign values \(1\) and \(\varepsilon<h\) on them, and take \(\delta=1/4\). Then the printed \(L\) is \(5/4\). The projection \(100e_1+e_2\) of \(100e_1+2e_2\) onto \(S\) is at distance \(\sqrt{10001}-3/4\) from \(A_\delta\). Thus it cannot find the required core inside the stated \(L\)-ball. Adding the missing distance repairs this step.

# A boundary case of the final conjecture

Problem 7 (Minor: constant limits need not both occur).  Conjecture 8.1, p. 31, states without excluding degenerate supports that \[\Omega(u)=\{0,1,\varphi(x\cdot e+a):
                         e\in\mathcal E,\ a\in\mathbb R\}.\] Theorem 2.2 permits \(U=\mathbb R^N\). Here \(u\equiv1\), \(\mathcal E=\varnothing\), and \(\Omega(u)=\{1\}\), while the displayed right side contains zero as well. Likewise \(U=\varnothing\) gives only the zero function. More generally, nonempty relatively dense thick sets invade uniformly, so zero is not a limiting profile.

Restrict the conjecture to nonempty, non-relatively-dense supports, or include only the constant profiles that actually occur. This is an error in a conjecture’s boundary scope, not a counterexample to a proved main theorem. The bistable statements in the final section are treated according to their stated status as extensions or open questions, not as completed proofs.
