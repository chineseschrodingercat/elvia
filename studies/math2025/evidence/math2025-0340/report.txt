Abstract: The reviewed source is arXiv:2212.03124v2, submitted on 12 February 2023. Two quantitative statements are false under their printed hypotheses. A sequence of radial harmonic maps into the circle contradicts the finite-stage collar estimate when the limiting image length is zero. A compactly supported Jacobian concentrated near the outer boundary contradicts the weighted Wente inequality. Both affect analytic ingredients of the main proof and are assessed as Major. A corrected weighted inequality is proved here; the collar argument requires a finite-stage error which its printed estimate omits. Two further Minor findings concern nearest-point projection derivatives and the inhomogeneous function space in the annular extension lemma. The main index inequalities are not disproved. The source formulas have been checked against actual page images; the original exact computations are retained. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The claims and their dependencies

The functional is the Dirichlet energy with a pulled-back two-form on a closed oriented surface. The admissible variations are sections of the pullback tangent bundle. In the harmonic-map case, the quadratic form displayed on p. 11 is \[Q_u(w)=\int_\Sigma |dw|_h^2
 -\int_\Sigma\langle I_u(du,du)_h,I_u(w,w)\rangle,
 \qquad w(x)\in T_{u(x)}N.\] For the general functional, the additional first-order and zeroth-order terms involving the three-form tensor are retained. They cannot be discarded when discussing the full theorem. The counterexamples in Problems 1 and 3 use the allowed special case in which that tensor vanishes.

Theorem I.1, p. 4, asserts an upper bound for \(\operatorname{Ind}+\operatorname{Null}\) along a bubble-convergent sequence on a fixed closed surface, by the corresponding sum for the base limit and the bubbles. Theorem I.2, p. 6, addresses degenerating conformal structures under the specified finite collar decomposition, absence of collar bubbles, and a strict smallness condition on the limiting collar image lengths. Neither statement is an unrestricted assertion for arbitrary maps on arbitrary annuli.

The principal proof chain is as follows. Angular Lorentz control and the holomorphic Hopf differential give full \(L^{2,1}\) neck control on a fixed surface. The antisymmetric-potential gauge, a Hodge decomposition, and harmonic Fourier modes separate a logarithmic neck mode from the other modes. Weighted Wente and dyadic estimates then produce pointwise control. A weighted Poincaré inequality makes the Hessian positive on the neck. Finally, weighted nonpositive eigensections are transferred to the base and bubble limits; a linear combination vanishing in both limits would contradict neck positivity.

The small-potential hypotheses and gauge conclusion were checked against Theorem I.4 of the accessible primary preprint of Rivière [riviere]. The angular estimate was compared with Theorem 0.2, the Lorentz argument in its proof, and the annular weak-\(L^2\) estimate in the accessible Laurain–Rivière preprint [laurain]. These are interface checks. Equation numbering in that preprint differs from the journal reference used by the source; the source’s citation of an estimate numbered (56) is not equated with the preprint’s weak-\(L^2\) equation solely because the numbers match.

For \(T=\log(\eta^2/\delta)\), the radial change of variable \(s=\log(r\eta/\delta)\) gives the exact one-dimensional Dirichlet scale \(\pi^2/T^2\). This explains why an error multiplied by \(T^2\), and not just an unweighted error tending to zero, matters to the neck argument. The printed weights add boundary-decay terms to this logarithmic scale. The limiting singular weights belong to \(L^p\) for suitable \(p>1\), so the compact embedding used in Appendix B follows from Rellich compactness and Hölder’s inequality. A piecewise-defined weight need not be smooth across its matching circles for this quadratic-form argument.

The unprojected ambient eigen-equation on p. 31 should be read with care: pp. 33 and 35, equations (IV.48), (IV.67), and (IV.68), and Appendix B explicitly use the tangent projection. Those later formulas prevent treating the earlier omission as a demonstrated failure of the spectral proof. The argument with any fixed finite family of eigensections and their linear combinations supplies the required dimension comparison without assuming a uniform dimension bound in advance.

# The collar estimate and a vanishing limiting length

Equation reference: eq:printed-collar. Problem 1 (A limit is substituted for a nonzero finite-stage error).  Lemma V.1, p. 36, equations (V.1)–(V.3), assumes vanishing energy on individual doubling annuli and a finite limiting averaged radial length \(\Lambda\). It asserts constants \(C>0\), \(0<\beta<2\), independent of \(k\), such that \[
 r^2|\nabla u_k|^2
 \le C\left[\left(\frac r\eta\right)^\beta
       +\left(\frac{\delta_k}{\eta r}\right)^\beta\right]
       \int_{A(2\eta,\delta_k)}|du_k|^2
       +\frac{C\Lambda^2}{\log^2(\eta^2/\delta_k)}.\] This pointwise finite-stage estimate is false under the stated local hypotheses.

## An admissible sequence

Take the closed target \(N=S^1\subset\mathbb R^2\), zero two-form, and \[\delta_k=e^{-k},\qquad
 u_k(r,\theta)=
 \left(\cos\frac{\log r}{k^2},\,
       \sin\frac{\log r}{k^2}\right).\] The map is defined on the entire punctured plane, so its restrictions to both annuli occurring in [eq:printed-collar] (#eq:printed-collar) exist. The phase is harmonic. Direct differentiation gives the sphere harmonic-map equation \[\Delta u_k+|\nabla u_k|^2u_k=0,
 \qquad |\nabla u_k|^2=\frac{1}{k^4r^2}.\] Thus these are smooth critical points on the required annuli, with the flat metric in conformal coordinates. On every doubling annulus, \[\int_{B_{2\rho}\setminus B_\rho}|\nabla u_k|^2
 =\frac{2\pi\log2}{k^4}\longrightarrow0.\] The map is independent of \(\theta\), so either averaging the radial derivative first and then taking its norm, or averaging its norm, gives the same radial length. For fixed \(\eta\) and sufficiently large \(k\), \[\ell_{\eta,k}
 =\int_{\delta_k/\eta}^{\eta}|\partial_r u_k|\,dr
 =\frac{k+2\log\eta}{k^2}
 \longrightarrow0.\] Consequently the iterated limiting length in (V.2) is \(\Lambda=0\). The annular energies are bounded and in fact tend to zero. The construction satisfies every local hypothesis of Lemma V.1.

## Failure at the middle circle

Set \(r_k=\sqrt{\delta_k}=e^{-k/2}\), which lies in \(A(\eta,\delta_k)\) for every fixed \(\eta>0\) and sufficiently large \(k\). Then \[r_k^2|\nabla u_k(r_k)|^2=k^{-4},
 \qquad
 \int_{A(2\eta,\delta_k)}|\nabla u_k|^2
 =\frac{2\pi}{k^4}\bigl(k+2\log(2\eta)\bigr).\] Since the \(\Lambda\)-term is exactly zero, division of the proposed right side by the left side gives \[4\pi C\eta^{-\beta}e^{-\beta k/2}
       \bigl(k+2\log(2\eta)\bigr)\longrightarrow0.\] This contradicts [eq:printed-collar] (#eq:printed-collar) for any fixed \(C\) and any positive \(\beta\), even if \(C\) were allowed to depend on the fixed \(\eta\). The symbolic calculation verifies the harmonic-map residual, the energy expressions, and this limit exactly; no extrapolation from finite numerical samples is used.

## Impact and necessary repair

This is Major for the quantitative lemma used in Section V’s proof of Theorem I.2. The defect is the replacement of a finite-stage logarithmic mode by its limiting length with no residual error. A statement compatible with the example must retain an error \(R_{\eta,k}\) for which an appropriate assertion has the form \[\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}
 \log^2(\eta^2/\delta_k)\,R_{\eta,k}\le C\Lambda^2,\] or an eventual bound with \(\Lambda+\varepsilon\), with thresholds depending on \(\varepsilon\). When \(\Lambda=0\), this permits a nonzero error of smaller order than \(T^{-2}\); it does not set that error identically to zero. In the example, \(R_{\eta,k}=k^{-4}\) has precisely this permitted behavior.

A strict margin below the critical collar length could absorb a sufficiently controlled residual of this kind. The full general collar estimate with that residual is not proved here. This report therefore does not certify the complete repair of Theorem I.2’s argument. It also does not disprove Theorem I.2: no extension of the example to critical maps on the degenerating closed surfaces of that theorem is claimed. The fixed-surface global conservation and Hopf-differential information used in Section III are additional restrictions, absent from the local lemma.

# The weighted Wente inequality at the outer boundary

Equation reference: eq:printed-wente. Problem 2 (The displayed weight vanishes at an uncontrolled boundary).  Lemma E.1, p. 45, equations (E.1)–(E.3), states that a Dirichlet solution of \[-\Delta\varphi=J(a,b):=\partial_1a\,\partial_2b
                         -\partial_2a\,\partial_1b
 \quad\hbox{in }B_1,\qquad \varphi|_{\partial B_1}=0\] satisfies \[
 \int_{B_1}|x|^2|\nabla\varphi|^2
 \le C\left(\int_{B_1}|\nabla a|^2\right)
       \left(\int_{B_1}f(|x|)|\nabla b|^2\right),
 \quad
 f(r)=r^2\log^2(1+1/r)\log(1+\log(1/r)).\] The constant is asserted to be universal. The weight tends to zero as \(r\uparrow1\), and this makes the assertion false even for compactly supported data.

## An explicit Jacobian and its exact solution

Equation reference: eq:energies. Write \(s=|\xi|^2\). Define the following functions inside \(B_1\), extending them by zero outside: \[\begin{aligned}
 \psi(\xi)&=(1-s)^4,\\
 \varphi_0(\xi)&=\partial_2\psi=-8\xi_2(1-s)^3,\\
 b_0(\xi)&=-\Delta\psi=16(1-s)^2(1-4s).\end{aligned}\] Their zero extensions have respectively \(C^3\), \(C^2\), and \(C^1\) regularity. In particular, no boundary distribution is introduced in the identity below. Put \[a_0(\xi)=\xi_1\chi(s),\qquad
 \chi(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&s\le1,\\
 (s-4)^2(2s+1)/27,&1<s<4,\\
 0,&s\ge4.
 \end{cases}\] This is a compactly supported \(C^1\) function on \(B_2\), and \(\nabla a_0=e_1\) on the support of \(\nabla b_0\). It follows, including across the support boundary, that \[J(a_0,b_0)=\partial_2b_0=-\Delta\varphi_0.\] All three functions belong to \(W^{1,2}(\mathbb R^2)\). Their Dirichlet energies are positive and finite. Exact polynomial integration gives \[
 E_\varphi=\frac{384\pi}{35},\qquad
 E_b=\frac{3072\pi}{5},\qquad
 E_a=\frac{184\pi}{35}.\] For example, with \(g(s)=-8(1-s)^3\), \[E_\varphi=2\pi\int_0^1s^2g'(s)^2\,ds,
 \qquad
 E_b=4\pi\int_0^1s
       \left[\frac{d}{ds}\{16(1-s)^2(1-4s)\}\right]^2ds.\] The retained independent computation checks these integrals and the Jacobian identity exactly.

## Concentration near the outer boundary

For \(0<\varepsilon<1/10\), let \[c_\varepsilon=(1-3\varepsilon,0),\qquad
 (a_\varepsilon,b_\varepsilon,\varphi_\varepsilon)(x)
 =(a_0,b_0,\varphi_0)\left(\frac{x-c_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right).\] The support of \(a_\varepsilon\) is inside \(B_{2\varepsilon}(c_\varepsilon)\Subset B_1\), and the other two supports are inside \(B_\varepsilon(c_\varepsilon)\). Thus these are admissible Dirichlet data and an exact Dirichlet solution. The equation scales by \(\varepsilon^{-2}\) on both sides, and the three Dirichlet energies are unchanged in dimension two.

On the support of \(\varphi_\varepsilon\) and \(b_\varepsilon\), \[1-4\varepsilon\le |x|\le1-2\varepsilon.\] Consequently, \[\begin{aligned}
 \int_{B_1}|x|^2|\nabla\varphi_\varepsilon|^2
   &\ge(1-4\varepsilon)^2E_\varphi,\\
 \int_{B_1} f(|x|)|\nabla b_\varepsilon|^2
   &\le E_b\sup_{1-4\varepsilon\le r\le1-2\varepsilon}f(r).\end{aligned}\] The exact boundary limit is \[\lim_{r\uparrow1}\frac{f(r)}{1-r}=(\log2)^2.\] The first quantity stays bounded away from zero, whereas the proposed right side in [eq:printed-wente] (#eq:printed-wente) is \(O(\varepsilon)E_aE_b\). This disproves every possible universal constant.

For a fully elementary bound, on the relevant interval \(r>1/2\), \[\log(1+1/r)<2,\qquad
 \log(1+\log(1/r))\le\log(1/r)\le2(1-r)\le8\varepsilon.\] Hence \(f(r)\le32\varepsilon\). For any proposed \(C>0\), taking \[0<\varepsilon<
 \min\left\{\frac1{10},\,\frac{9E_\varphi}{800CE_aE_b}\right\}\] already gives a strict contradiction. The example satisfies more support and regularity conditions than the lemma requires.

## A verified replacement

Equation reference: eq:repair-wente. The following replacement retains the analytic property needed later: \[
 \int_{B_1}|x|^2|\nabla\varphi|^2
 \le C\left(\int_{B_1}|\nabla a|^2\right)
       \int_{B_1}|x|^2
       \left(1+\log\frac1{|x|}\right)^2|\nabla b|^2.\] Here is a proof, including the endpoint summation.

Use the decomposition of gradients in Lemma E.2, pp. 45–47, with \(\operatorname{supp}\nabla b_j\subset A_j\cup A_{j+1}\), \(A_j=B_{2^{-j}}\setminus B_{2^{-j-1}}\), and \[x_j=\|\nabla b_j\|_2,\qquad
 x_j^2\le C\int_{A_j\cup A_{j+1}}|\nabla b|^2,\qquad
 A=\|\nabla a\|_2.\] Let \(\varphi_j\) be the zero-boundary solution for \(J(a,b_j)\). The classical Wente estimate, together with the harmonic annular estimates underlying Lemma E.3, gives \[\|\nabla\varphi_j\|_{L^2(A_k)}
 \le CA\,2^{-|j-k|}x_j .\] A bounded shift of an index changes only \(C\). For completeness, outside the support one subtracts the outer constant of \(b_j\), so the total Jacobian integral is zero and there is no logarithmic harmonic mode. Inside the support’s inner circle the solution is harmonic, and the interior energy scales quadratically with radius. The neighboring annuli use the unweighted Wente bound. The first outer annulus is included in this last bound; no vanishing of \(b\) at the outer boundary is assumed.

Set \(B_j=2^{-j}x_j\). For any finite sum of the \(\varphi_j\), Minkowski’s inequality gives \[2^{-k}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^2(A_k)}
 \le CA\left[
       \sum_{j\le k}4^{-(k-j)}B_j+\sum_{j>k}B_j\right].\] The first sum is bounded on \(\ell^2\) by the summable geometric convolution kernel. For the second, put \(T_k=\sum_{j\ge k}B_j\) for a finite nonnegative sequence. Telescoping and Cauchy–Schwarz give \[\begin{aligned}
 \sum_kT_k^2
 &=\sum_k(k+1)(T_k^2-T_{k+1}^2)\\
 &\le2\sum_k(k+1)B_kT_k
 \le2\left(\sum_k(k+1)^2B_k^2\right)^{1/2}
       \left(\sum_kT_k^2\right)^{1/2}.\end{aligned}\] Therefore \(\sum_kT_k^2\le4\sum_k(k+1)^2B_k^2\). Summing over \(k\), using the local bound for \(x_j\), and comparing \(j+1\) with \(1+\log(1/r)\) on \(A_j\), proves [eq:repair-wente] (#eq:repair-wente). The gradient decomposition converges in \(L^2\); the unweighted Wente estimate gives convergence of the corresponding solution gradients, so the finite-sum argument passes to the general case.

The repaired weight is positive at \(r=1\), and it is \(o(r^\alpha)\) as \(r\downarrow0\) for every \(0<\alpha<2\). On the annuli bounded away from zero used in (F.7), p. 55, [eq:repair-wente] (#eq:repair-wente) bounds the unweighted solution energy. The harmonic splitting in Lemma F.1 and the rescaling and geometric summation in Lemma F.2 therefore retain their stated type of estimates with this replacement and changed constants.

## Impact

This is Major for the claimed weighted inequality and its explicit use in (F.7), which feeds the pointwise neck argument. Case 1 of the printed proof, p. 49, gives a weight \(r^2\); that bound cannot be absorbed uniformly into the displayed \(f\) near \(r=1\). The fact that a replacement is available does not make the printed core analytic assertion true. The replacement above repairs this specific interface. It is not a full independent certification of all remaining steps of either main theorem, and no counterexample to those main theorems is asserted.

# Projection derivatives in the second variation

Problem 3 (Nearest-point differentiation away from the target).  On p. 10, the text before (II.1) identifies the derivative of the nearest-point projection at \(u+t\phi\) with the tangent projection at its footpoint. Equations (II.1)–(II.2) then give an incorrect acceleration for general ambient \(\phi\). On p. 11, the first formula in (II.14) also has an incorrect sign.

Take the allowed harmonic constant map \(u=e_1\in S^1\), and an ambient constant variation \(\phi=ae_1+be_2\). The nearest-point projection is \[\pi(z)=\frac z{|z|},\qquad
 D\pi_z=|z|^{-1}P_{\pi(z)}.\] The missing scalar factor already refutes the asserted off-target identity. More decisively, \[\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\pi(e_1+t(ae_1+be_2))\right|_{t=0}
 =-b^2e_1-2ab e_2,
 \qquad
 D_{be_2}P_{e_1}(ae_1+be_2)=-b^2e_1-ab e_2.\] For \(a=b=1\), the actual acceleration is \((-1,-2)\), whereas the printed formula gives \((-1,-1)\). The independent symbolic derivative verifies the discrepancy exactly.

For a sphere, \[P_u=I-u\otimes u
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 dP_u=-du\otimes u-u\otimes du.\] The printed difference of tensors in (II.14) is skew-symmetric, whereas a derivative of a symmetric projection must be symmetric. Its contraction with \(du\) also has the wrong sign. The subsequent formula (II.15) nevertheless displays the correct value \(-u|du|^2\).

The necessary second-variation calculation is repaired without altering the final harmonic Hessian. Any curve in \(N\) with initial velocity \(w\) has normal acceleration \(I_u(w,w)\). The error in the explicit circle example is tangential. At a harmonic map, the first-variation vector is normal, so the pairing relevant to (II.8) is unchanged. Alternatively, choosing the ambient variation \(\phi=w\) tangent along \(u\) from the outset gives the correct normal acceleration directly. At a critical point of the full functional, the Hessian likewise depends only on the initial tangent velocity, because the term containing a change of acceleration is the first variation. Thus the corrected projection calculus supplies the needed path-independent Hessian.

The assessment is Minor. The pointwise identities are genuinely false, but their correct replacements recover the indicated second-variation interface. The final sphere quadratic form and the correctly projected spectral operator are not refuted.

# The annular extension space

Problem 4 (An inhomogeneous whole-plane extension cannot obey the stated bound).  Lemma C.1, p. 42, asserts an extension \(\widetilde a\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb C)\) with compactly supported gradient and a gradient estimate depending only on \(\nabla a\) in the original annulus. With the usual inhomogeneous meaning of \(W^{1,2}\), this is false.

Take \(r=1\), \(R=3\), and \(a\equiv1\) on \(B_R\setminus B_r\). The hypotheses \(0<2r<R\) and \(a\in W^{1,2}\) hold. Equation (C.1) forces \(\nabla\widetilde a=0\) almost everywhere. A function with zero weak gradient on the connected plane is constant; equality to \(a\) on the annulus forces that constant to be \(1\). It is not in \(L^2(\mathbb C)\), hence not in inhomogeneous \(W^{1,2}(\mathbb C)\). This is an exact counterexample, not a question of the unspecified universal constant.

## The correct extension and boundary matching

The appropriate assertion is \[\widetilde a\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb C),\qquad
 \nabla\widetilde a\in L^2(\mathbb C),
 \qquad
 \widetilde a-a_R\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb C),\] where \(a_R\) is the mean on \(B_R\setminus B_{R/2}\). Its homogeneous class is sufficient for the gradient and Jacobian operations in the proof.

The inner cutoff and inversion in the source provide the inner extension. For the outer extension, the displayed formula on p. 43 needs \(\chi_{R/2}\), not \(\chi_R\), where \(\chi_t(x)=\chi(|x|/t)\), \(\chi=1\) on \([0,1]\), and \(\chi=0\) on \([2,\infty)\). Define inside \(B_R\) \[\breve a=(1-\chi_{R/2})a+\chi_{R/2}a_R,\] interpreting it as the constant \(a_R\) on \(B_{R/2}\). This only uses values of \(a\) on its domain because \(R/2>r\). It agrees with \(a\) at radius \(R\), is constant at radius at most \(R/2\), and obeys \[\int_{B_R}|\nabla\breve a|^2
 \le C\int_{B_R\setminus B_{R/2}}|\nabla a|^2\] by the scaled Poincaré inequality. Invert it outside \(B_R\): \[\widetilde a(x)=\breve a\left(R^2x/|x|^2\right),\qquad |x|>R.\] The traces match, the extension is constant for \(|x|\ge2R\), and conformal invariance of the planar Dirichlet integral gives (C.2). The analogous inner construction gives (C.3); adding the original annulus gives (C.1). After subtracting \(a_R\), the extension has compact support and finite \(W^{1,2}\) norm.

By contrast, the printed \(\chi_R\) is identically one throughout \(B_R\), so that formula cannot generally match the original boundary trace. The inversion must also use the outer function \(\breve a\); the printed use of the inner-function symbol there is inconsistent with its following energy identity.

The assessment is Minor. The homogeneous formulation and corrected outer cutoff establish the extension required for the gradients in Sections III and V. If the original notation was intended to denote a homogeneous Sobolev class, the function-space issue becomes a necessary clarification; the explicit cutoff correction is still required. No main index conclusion is disproved by this issue.
