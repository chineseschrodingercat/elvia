Abstract: The reviewed author manuscript has a gap in its multi-factor torsion induction, a false general spectral lemma, and incompatibilities in the proposed equivariant site. A genus-two finite étale cover has torsion subgroup \((\mathbb Z/2)^5\) in its second integral cohomology, contradicting an unrestricted reading of the diagram-defined family in Proposition 6.5. Its occurrence among the intended coprime-degree motivic terms is not established. A two-term integral complex gives a counterexample to Lemma 6.18 even over a circle. The stack fixed-point construction and the arbitrary-gerbe scope also require substantive repair. Five local corrections are distinguished from these Major issues. None of these examples is presented as a counterexample to the final Higgs-moduli theorem. The findings concern a precisely identified earlier version; the scientific decision remains pending.

# A finite cover with integral torsion

Problem 1 (Major).  Under an unrestricted reading of the covers defined by diagram (21), p. 23, Proposition 6.5, p. 25, is false. The proposition does not separately specify the parameters produced by the preceding motivic construction with \((d,n)=1\). Independently, the reverse induction on p. 27 applies Lemma 6.7 to a generally singular boundary, whereas that lemma requires a smooth closed subvariety.

Let \(C\) be a smooth projective genus-two curve, \(c_0\) a Weierstrass point, and \(\tau\) its hyperelliptic involution. Write \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(C)\) and \(\operatorname{AJ}(x)=\mathcal O_C(x-c_0)\). Then \(\tau(c_0)=c_0\) and \(\operatorname{AJ}(\tau x)=-\operatorname{AJ}(x)\). The positive parameters in the diagram-defined family \[n=2,\qquad l=(1,1),\qquad k=(1,1)\] give the smooth finite étale cover \[Y=\{(x,y,L)\in C^2\times J:2L=\operatorname{AJ}(x)+\operatorname{AJ}(y)\}.
                                                        \tag{1}\] It has degree 16. All indicated \(l_i,k_i\) are positive. The proposition does not explicitly quantify over all parameters or identify the actual admissible motivic subclass. This example tests the unrestricted reading; its membership in the coprime-degree motivic terms has not been shown.

Put \(\Pi=\pi_1(C,c_0)\), \(A=H_1(C,\mathbb Z/2)=(\mathbb Z/2)^4\) and \(K=\ker(\Pi\to A)\). The cover’s monodromy is the surjection \[\Pi\times\Pi\longrightarrow A,\qquad(u,v)\longmapsto\bar u+\bar v.\] Consequently \(Y\) is connected and \(\pi_1Y=\Gamma=\ker(\Pi^2\to A)\). Projection to the first factor yields \(1\to K\to\Gamma\to\Pi\to1\). It splits by \(u\mapsto(u,\tau_*u)\), induced by the actual section \(x\mapsto(x,\tau x,\mathcal O_C)\) of (1). Since \(\tau_*\) is an automorphism and inner conjugation by \(K\) is trivial on its abelianization, \[H_1(Y,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^4\oplus H_1(K,\mathbb Z)_A.                       \tag{2}\]

The five-term group-homology sequence for \(1\to K\to\Pi\to A\to1\) is \[\mathbb Z=H_2(\Pi,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(A,\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow H_1(K,\mathbb Z)_A\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^4
 \longrightarrow A\longrightarrow0.\] By the integral Künneth formula, \(H_2(A,\mathbb Z)=\bigwedge^2 A=(\mathbb Z/2)^6\). With a symplectic basis \(a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2\), the surface fundamental class maps to \[\omega=\bar a_1\wedge\bar b_1+\bar a_2\wedge\bar b_2.\] This follows by factoring the classifying map through the abelianization \(\mathbb Z^4\); cup-product evaluation gives precisely the two symplectic pairs. The image has order 2. The kernel of \(\mathbb Z^4\to A\) is \(2\mathbb Z^4\). Hence \[0\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/2)^5\longrightarrow H_1(K,\mathbb Z)_A
 \longrightarrow2\mathbb Z^4\longrightarrow0\] splits as abelian groups because its quotient is free. Equation (2) and the integral universal coefficient theorem now give \[H_1(Y,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^8\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^5,\qquad
 \boxed{\operatorname{Tor}H^2(Y,\mathbb Z)=(\mathbb Z/2)^5.}                        \tag{3}\] In particular the torsion subgroup has order 32; the rational first Betti number is 8 whereas the mod-2 first Betti number is 13.

The proof defect is visible independently. In Step 2 on p. 27, the complement of the product of affine symmetric powers is a union of coordinate divisors. Near an intersection of two coordinate divisors, the boundary has local equation \(xy=0\). Finite étale pullback preserves this crossing. Thus the boundary to which Lemma 6.7 is applied is not smooth. This crossing geometry is independent of any coprimality imposed on the numerical parameters. If the open set is instead read as the complement of only the lower product, the product-open definition and affine calculation in (25) no longer describe the same object. Neither reading supplies the missing hypothesis. Simply discarding smoothness cannot establish the unrestricted statement in view of (3).

The counterexample does not prove torsion in the actual fixed-determinant Higgs moduli space. Here \(\sum k_i l_i=2\) is not coprime to \(n=2\). The motivic computation may use a narrower parameter class; its exact restrictions and the torsion argument applicable to it must be stated and justified. No claim that an additional gcd condition alone suffices is certified. The displayed multi-factor reverse induction has an unmet smooth-boundary hypothesis in the proof used for Theorem 6.3. This is a core proof gap, not a counterexample to the actual coprime-degree Higgs-moduli conclusion.

# Finite monodromy does not detect all fiber torsion

Problem 2 (Major).  Lemma 6.18, p. 31, is false for its stated class of spectra, already for a one-dimensional torus. It is an explicit input to Theorem 6.11 on p. 32.

Fix a prime \(p\). In homological degrees 1 and 0, let \[C_1=\mathbb Z\{u,v\},\qquad C_0=\mathbb Z\{a,b\},\qquad
 d(u)=pb,\quad d(v)=0.\] For the associated Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum \(E=H(C)\), \(\pi_0E=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/p\), \(\pi_1E=\mathbb Z\), and all other groups vanish. Define the chain map \[N_0(a)=b,\quad N_0(b)=0,\qquad N_1(u)=v,\quad N_1(v)=0.\] It satisfies \(N^2=0\), so \(T=1+N\) is a chain automorphism with inverse \(1-N\). The degree-one homotopy \(h(a)=u\), \(h(b)=v\) obeys \[dh+hd=pN,\qquad T^p=1+pN\simeq1.                       \tag{4}\] The locally constant spectrum on \(S^1\) with monodromy \(T\) therefore becomes trivial on the degree-\(p\) cover. This uses only the classification of local systems on a circle by an automorphism up to homotopy.

Its global sections are \(\operatorname{hofib}(N)\). Write subscripts \(s,t\) for source and target in the mapping cone. The only nonzero degree-one boundary images are \[a_s\longmapsto b_t,\qquad u_t\longmapsto pb_t,\] and the degree-two differential sends \[u_s\longmapsto-pb_s+v_t,\qquad v_s\longmapsto0.\] The degree-one kernel has basis \(b_s,u_t-pa_s,v_t\). The image is generated by the primitive vector \(-pb_s+v_t\). Consequently \[H_0(\operatorname{Cone}N)=\mathbb Z,\quad
 H_1(\operatorname{Cone}N)=\mathbb Z^2,\quad
 H_2(\operatorname{Cone}N)=\mathbb Z.\] After the fiber shift, the only nonzero section groups are \[\pi_{-1}\Gamma=\mathbb Z,\qquad\pi_0\Gamma=\mathbb Z^2,\qquad
 \pi_1\Gamma=\mathbb Z.                                       \tag{5}\] There is no \(p\)-torsion, although the fiber has it and (4) satisfies the precise finite-cover hypothesis.

The erroneous step on p. 31 says that an automorphism preserves a chosen complementary torsion-free summand. In this example its action on \(\pi_0E\) is \((z,\bar t)\mapsto(z,\bar t+\bar z)\), which mixes such summands. An invariant torsion subgroup does not give an invariant chosen complement. Nor does nontrivial invariant torsion necessarily survive the extension in the homotopy-fiber exact sequence.

A repair must establish an appropriate torsion-detection theorem using additional structure of the actual geometric local system, or replace this step of the PGL argument. The chain-complex example refutes the general lemma; it is not asserted to arise as the particular Higgs-moduli K-theory spectrum. A helper’s stronger KU-module variant was not needed or independently certified for the adopted finding.

# The orbit site and stack fixed points

Problem 3 (Major).  The partial-domain morphisms in Definition A.4, p. 34, and the covering rule in Definition A.6, p. 35, are incompatible with the stability axiom of a Grothendieck topology.

Take \(G=C_2\) acting on \(X=\mathbb C\) by \(z\mapsto-z\), so \(X^G=\{0\}\). The allowed generalized orbits \[P=\mathbb C^*\times(\{e\}\backslash G),\qquad
 E=\varnothing\times(G\backslash G)\] satisfy \(P\ne\varnothing\) and \(P|_{s(G)}=P\times_X X^G=\varnothing\). The unique map \(P|_{s(G)}\to E\) is continuous, equivariant and over \(X\). Since \(\{e\}\subset G\), Definition A.4(c) makes it a morphism \(P\to E\).

Definition A.6 explicitly makes the empty family cover \(E\). Pulling its empty covering sieve back along \(P\to E\) gives the empty sieve on \(P\). Stability requires that sieve to cover \(P\), contradicting the stated covering criterion, since its union of images is empty. The example is a finite-dimensional, locally compact Hausdorff complex analytic space with nonempty fixed locus. Empty opens are allowed in A.3; A.6–A.7 explicitly retain the empty object; Example A.5 confirms that the partial-domain convention is intentional.

Interpreting the rule as a generating family for a topology does not supply the asserted theory. For the free action \(X=G=C_2\), each nonempty generalized orbit maps to the empty \(G\)-type orbit in exactly this way. The generated topology forces an empty cover on every object, making the sheaf category terminal. Lemma A.10 instead requires \(\operatorname{Sh}(G;G)\simeq\operatorname{Space}\).

The site needs a compatible replacement, followed by new proofs of the orbit comparison, stalk formulas, functoriality and base change. Changing one exceptional empty-object clause is not a certified repair. This is foundational to the appendix used in Section 5, without disproving the final K-theory equivalence.

Problem 4 (Major in the stated general stack framework).  Definitions A.29(b) and A.31, p. 43, use coarse fixed points as if they supplied coherent fixed objects. The resulting orbit action map need not be defined independently of a coset representative.

Let \(\mathcal X=BC_3\) and let \(G=C_2\) act by inversion on \(C_3\). This is a strict action allowed by A.29(a). Its coarse space is a point, so A.29(b) gives \(\mathcal X^G=\mathcal X\). Take \(H=G\) and \(U=\mathcal X\) in A.31. The unique coset in \(G\backslash G\) can be represented by \(1\) or the nontrivial element \(s\). The formula \((x,Hg)\mapsto x\cdot g\) then gives the identity functor or the inversion functor on \(BC_3\).

These are not naturally isomorphic: a natural isomorphism would be an element \(k\in C_3\) satisfying \(kr=r^{-1}k\) for a generator \(r\). Since \(C_3\) is abelian, that would imply \(r=r^{-1}\). Thus passing to a 2-categorical interpretation does not resolve the failure. Coarse fixed points alone do not provide the required trivialization of the subgroup action.

One must restrict actions or retain coherent fixed-object data and verify the subsequent descent constructions. The actual map \(M'\to[\mathfrak{sl}_n/PGL_n]\) on p. 21 has a target described as carrying trivial \(\Gamma\)-action. The example above is not asserted to occur there. The general framework is overbroad even if that specific application can be constructed in a narrower framework.

# The gerbe quantifier

Problem 5 (Major for the arbitrary-Brauer-class argument).  Theorem 6.11, p. 27, allows an arbitrary Brauer class. Corollary 6.16 and its use in Corollary 6.17, p. 30, identify the corresponding character cover with a fixed geometric cover by using a property specific to the universal-family obstruction gerbe.

For the zero equivariant gerbe, the line bundles \(L_{\gamma'}\) of Sections 2.5 and 6.3 are trivial and their algebra is the ordinary group algebra. Therefore, for \(c=\langle\gamma\rangle\), \[C_c^0=M^\gamma\times c^* .                             \tag{6}\] Take \(n=2,d=1\), a curve of genus at least 2, and nonzero \(\gamma\in J[2]\). The associated connected étale double cover \(\widetilde C_\gamma\) has connected rank-one Higgs moduli \[\widetilde M_\gamma=T^*\operatorname{Pic}^1(\widetilde C_\gamma).\] The deck involution acts freely on \(\operatorname{Pic}^1\): an invariant line bundle can be linearized for the cyclic group over \(\mathbb C\) and descended, but a pullback from the base has even degree. Thus \(M^\gamma=\widetilde M_\gamma/\mu_2\) is connected, using the geometric description also stated in Proposition 6.15. The left side of Corollary 6.16 for \(\alpha=0\) has two connected components by (6), whereas \(\widetilde M_\gamma\) has one. They are not isomorphic.

If that corollary is interpreted as concerning only the canonical obstruction gerbe, this example does not refute the restricted interpretation. It does show that such a corollary cannot justify the arbitrary-\(\alpha\) reduction as written. Its proof invokes the obstruction property without a new restriction after the arbitrary gerbe assertions on pp. 27–29. The repair must state and track the canonical-gerbe hypothesis, and separately prove any claimed extension to arbitrary Brauer classes. This does not establish that Theorem 6.11 itself is false.

# Local corrections with bounded repairs

Problem 6 (Minor).  Lemma 3.3, p. 12, requires the support of every semisimple perverse summand to meet the open set. It must instead control every simple constituent.

On \(B=\mathbb A^1\), with \(B^\heartsuit=\mathbb G_m\), set \(T=\mathbb Q_B[1]\oplus\mathbb Q_{\{0\}}\), \(\mathcal F=T\) and \(\mathcal G=\mathbb Q_B[1]\). Let \(f\) be projection and \(g\) inclusion. The support of \(T\) is \(B\); \(gf\) restricts to the identity on the open set, but on global sections \(\mathbb Q[1]\oplus\mathbb Q\) it is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\). It is not unipotent and \(\Gamma(B,\mathcal F)\) is not a retract of \(\Gamma(B,\mathcal G)\). As usual zero summands are ignored; if desired, periodizing this example gives nonzero \(T_i=T\) in every integer degree with the same failure degree by degree.

Requiring each simple constituent’s support to meet the open set removes this example. Situation 3.8(b), p. 14, already explicitly describes that stronger middle-extension condition. The local lemma defect therefore does not by itself refute that application.

Problem 7 (Minor).  In the proof of Lemma 3.5, p. 13, the quotient \(\widetilde F^{b-2}/\ker(\bar d^{b-1}\widetilde d^{b-2})\) does not extend the original complex term.

On the original open set \(U\), the composite is zero, so its kernel is the whole term and the quotient restricts to zero. Even \(U=W\) and a nonzero three-term complex with all differentials zero exhibits the error. Replace the quotient by \(\ker(\bar d^{b-1}\widetilde d^{b-2})\) itself. Noetherian coherence is preserved; restriction to \(U\) is the original term; the composite vanishes. Repeating the construction gives the intended descending extension. This corrects the construction without refuting extension existence.

Problem 8 (Minor; the space-valued proof).  Claim A.22, p. 40, that internal \(\operatorname{Map}(H\backslash G,-)\) preserves arbitrary colimits is false.

Let \(G=C_2\), \(H=e\), and use the coproduct of two terminal objects in \(\operatorname{Space}_G\). Evaluate the proposed comparison at the free orbit \(G\). The left side has two points. The right side consists of \(G\)-maps \(G\times G\to\{0,1\}\), where the two-point target is trivial. There are two free orbits in \(G\times G\), hence four such maps. The comparison is not an equivalence.

A finite coproduct of representables gives a finite product of mapping values, which does not preserve arbitrary coproducts of spaces. This invalidates the indicated proof of Lemma A.21, not necessarily its target identity. A restriction–coinduction or slice-category proof in a corrected equivariant theory is needed. Finite products and coproducts agree for spectra, so this particular space-valued example is not automatically a counterexample to the stable analogue.

Problem 9 (Minor).  Lemma 6.8, p. 26, gives \(m\) copies after setting \(m=\gcd(k,n)\); the component count is \(m^{2g}\) for a genus-\(g\) Jacobian.

For an elliptic curve, \(l=1\) and \(k=n=2\), the pullback is \(\{(x,L):2L=2\operatorname{AJ}(x)\}=C\times J[2]\). It is four copies of \(C\), not two. In general the components of the isogeny pullback are indexed by \(J[m]\). The single-factor torsion-free reduction survives the corrected finite multiplicity. It does not resolve the two-factor obstruction in Problem 1.

Problem 10 (Minor).  Lemma 4.6, p. 18, gives the rank of \(R^q\pi_*\mathcal O\) for a \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)\)-torsor as \(\binom gq\). With the notation fixed on p. 16, the correct rank is \(\binom{g_C}q\).

Here \(g\) is the arithmetic genus of the spectral curve and \(g_C\) the genus of the base curve. For example, a smooth genus-one curve mapping finitely to \(\mathbb P^1\) has \(g=1,g_C=0\). The torsor has zero-dimensional trivial group, so \(\pi\) is an isomorphism and \(R^1\pi_*\mathcal O=0\), not rank one. The subsequent cohomological range already uses \(g_C\); this is a local correction, without a counterexample to the intended Prym equivalence.

# Counterevidence, dependencies and coverage limits

The ordinary unipotent argument in Theorem 3.10, p. 15, has a valid integral mechanism: on a torsion-free group, rational nilpotence implies integral nilpotence, and a finite geometric series in the nilpotent operator gives the inverse. Corollary 3.11 should cite the integral torsion-free-quotient conclusion of Theorem 3.10. Rational isomorphism and absence of torsion alone would be insufficient, as multiplication by 2 on \(\mathbb Z\) shows.

Several additional formulas and scope transitions merit explicit clarification. Equation (4), p. 12, should use twisted K-theory of \(\mathcal X\), rather than untwisted K-theory of \(I\mathcal X\) on its left. For \(\mathcal X=BC_2,\alpha=0\), the literal latter reading has rank 4, while the stated inertia-cohomology right side has rank 2. The expected Proposition 3.1 is not refuted by this notation issue.

Claim 2.26, p. 11, passes from a stabilizer stratum to Lemma 2.24, which assumes trivial action of the whole group. The reduction should handle the free quotient and choose a suitable stabilizer subgroup, rather than an arbitrary cyclic subgroup without explanation. The Heisenberg-twist test does not give a counterexample: the residual quotient acts nontrivially on the character cover, correcting a naive rank count. Construction 3.9 also needs to explain how the proved finite-abelian-quotient comparison covers its more generally stated finite-quotient framework; the Higgs group \(\Gamma\) is abelian. These are recorded as proof-interface limits, not additional confirmed false main theorems.

In the appendix, the source and target variables in A.39–A.40, p. 46, must be made consistent. The main application needs a smooth map to the target with trivial group action. Theorem A.35, p. 44, has explicit topological-space and hypercompleteness hypotheses; Corollary A.41, p. 47, needs a descent argument for any broader stack scope. No separate counterexample to the actual representable quotient-torsor application is established here.

A suspected error from the index \(i\in\mathbb N\) was withdrawn: if \(P_B=\mathbb Q_B[d]\) is perverse, then \(\mathbb Q_B=P_B[-d]\) has perverse cohomology in degree \(+d\), not \(-d\). It supplies no adopted finding. An additional twisted elliptic-surface proposal was not independently completed and is excluded. A periodic direct sum alone is also not an obstruction to the unipotence argument: finite-dimensional support gives uniformly bounded perverse-cohomology amplitude degree by degree.

Positive bounded checks include the Brauer-class torsion argument of Proposition 3.12, the finite-field Betti-number criterion via universal coefficients, the smooth affine-complement mechanism of Lemma 6.7 under its actual hypotheses, and the large-degree projective-bundle case for a single symmetric power. The Prym spectral-sequence strategy and determinant-of-cohomology descent were traced, but all external Cohen–Macaulay, relative duality and motivic inputs have not been independently reconstructed. Full source coverage does not mean complete independent certification of every external theorem.
