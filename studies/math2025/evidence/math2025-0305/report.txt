# Assessment and scope

One substantive incorrect conclusion is established in Remark 6.5: the specified action–angle transformation does not convexify the limiting zero-energy surface. This is a Major finding because it invalidates the asserted geometric conclusion, with an explicit family of boundary points witnessing nonconvexity. Its location in a remark does not reduce that mathematical impact. The finding does not refute the periodic-orbit theorems or the separate finite-parameter convexity classification in Theorem 2.11. A localized energy-rescaling error on page 12 is also documented.

# Hypotheses and proof coverage

The normalized Hamiltonian has \(\alpha,\varpi>0\), negative energy, and compact regular levels in \[1<2\varpi^2|h|<(1+4/\alpha)^2.\] After setting \(h=-1\), the parameters \(\beta=(1+4/\alpha)^{-1}\) and \(e=(1-2\varpi^2\beta^2)^{1/2}\) lie in \(D=\{(\beta,e):\beta,e>0,\ \beta^2+e^2<1\}\). The extended square used to study the Euler orbit is distinguished from this physical parameter domain.

The reduction, unique critical point of the effective potential, compactness thresholds, contact-type argument, and winding-based intersection estimates were followed. The positive rotation of \((p_z,z)\) on a compact level ensures repeated crossings of the disc bounded by the Euler orbit. The construction of a simple symmetric brake orbit and the disc and annular section arguments use imported tight-contact and global-section results; those external theorems were not independently reproved.

The large-\(\alpha\) scaling separates the limiting Hamiltonian into \[K_\infty=\frac{p_v^2}{2}+4v^2+K_2(p_u,u),\qquad
 K_2=\frac{p_u^2}{2}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{1/4+4u^2}}.\] The monotone quarter-period, the finite collection of transverse shooting intersections and their persistence were traced in Section 6. The reversible half-return map, its square, the symmetry/parity classification and the boundary/interior rotation comparison were read in Section 7.

The Euler variational equation reduces to \(x''+(1+7\beta/(1+e\cos\theta))x=0\). The periodic coexistence argument, antiperiodic recurrence, monotonicity in \(\beta\), negative hyperbolic bands, and the alternative Maslov-index treatment were examined. The exclusion of positive hyperbolicity uses coexistence and phase monotonicity; the two bands inside \(D\) are distinguished from the bands on the extended parameter square. Imported index and reversible-map theorems remain dependencies.

The curvature criterion and the critical-parameter classification in Section 8 and Appendix B were followed, including the boundary test, exclusion of competing stationary branches, and the monotonicity/concavity argument for \(e_{\rm conv}(\beta)\). An independent symbolic differentiation verifies the polynomial for the scaled curvature quantity in (8.7)–(8.9). This is a check of that identity, not exhaustive certification of every lengthy auxiliary polynomial inequality.

# Major finding P1: the claimed convexification fails

## Location and sign error

Remark 6.5, pages 28–29, equation (6.13) and the displays immediately following it, uses the action \(I\) with angle of period one. Writing \(E=h_u(I)\in(-4,0)\) and \[a(E)=\sqrt{E^{-2}-1/16},\qquad
 I(E)=4\int_0^{a(E)}
       \sqrt{2\left(E+\frac{2}{\sqrt{1/4+4u^2}}\right)}\,du ,\] gives \(I_E=T(E)>0\). Lemma 6.2 supplies \(T_a>0\), whereas \[a_E=-\frac{1}{E^3a}>0,\qquad
 h_u''(I)=-\frac{T_E}{T^3}
        =-\frac{4T_a}{T^3E^2\sqrt{16-E^2}}<0.\] The final sign in the paper’s expression for \(h_u''\) is positive. Equation (6.13) itself is the correct chain rule; substituting the incorrect sign is what makes the ensuing Hessian argument fail.

## A counterexample to convexity of the actual image

A negative \(h_u''\) alone would not establish nonconvexity of the energy surface, so the following argument concerns the surface itself. Under the claimed transformation \(I=\pi(x^2+y^2)\), let \[\widetilde K(p_v,v,x,y)=p_v^2/2+4v^2+h_u(\pi(x^2+y^2)).\] For every \(0<\varepsilon<4\), take two exact points on \(\widetilde K^{-1}(0)\) with \(v=y=0\): \[P_\varepsilon=\left(\sqrt{2\varepsilon},
              \sqrt{I(-\varepsilon)/\pi}\right),\qquad
 Q_\varepsilon=\left(\sqrt{2\varepsilon/16},
              \sqrt{I(-\varepsilon/16)/\pi}\right),\] where the displayed coordinates are \((p_v,x)\). Both corresponding one-dimensional energies lie in \((-4,0)\); there is no use of an unbound orbit or an excluded endpoint.

Substitution \(u=s/\varepsilon\) and dominated convergence give \[I(-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\varepsilon}
 \longrightarrow
 4\sqrt2\int_0^1\sqrt{s^{-1}-1}\,ds=2\pi\sqrt2 .\] The domination follows from the integrable bound \(s^{-1/2}\) after extending the integrand by zero beyond its turning point. At the midpoint, the kinetic term is \(25\varepsilon/64\) and its action is \[I_m=\frac{(\sqrt{I(-\varepsilon)}
                 +\sqrt{I(-\varepsilon/16)})^2}{4}.\] Consequently, \[\frac{I_m}{I(-\varepsilon)}\longrightarrow\frac94,\qquad
 \frac{I(-25\varepsilon/64)}{I(-\varepsilon)}
       \longrightarrow\frac85.\] Their limiting difference is \(13/20>0\). Thus, for every sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), \(I_m>I(-25\varepsilon/64)\). Since \(h_u\) is strictly increasing, \(\widetilde K((P_\varepsilon+
Q_\varepsilon)/2)>0\). A segment joining two genuine boundary points leaves the sublevel set \(\{\widetilde K\leq0\}\). This disproves convexity, and hence strict convexity, of the claimed image.

An independent 60-digit quadrature at \(\varepsilon=1/100\) gives \[I_m=196.1055521845\ldots,\qquad
 I(-25\varepsilon/64)=138.7821948443\ldots .\] Their difference is \(57.3233573402\ldots>0\). These floating-point values corroborate the analytic counterfamily; they are not represented as interval-certified estimates. The program and its actual output are retained.

## Impact and repair boundary

Changing the sign of the displayed derivative cannot rescue the convexification claim: the specified image is nonconvex. The claim should be withdrawn, or replaced by a different transformation with a new proof. This review does not exclude the existence of some other convex embedding. The argument does not invalidate the preceding dynamical-convexity estimate, nor the finite-\(\alpha\) classification in Section 8, which does not rely on this convexification. As a further normalization check, the small oscillation period is \(\pi/4\), so the Hessian in these action coordinates tends to \(8I_2\), rather than the printed \(32I_2\).

# Minor finding P2: the energy rescaling is inverted

On page 12, after (3.9), put \(c=m^{5/2}/\sqrt2>0\) and write the old Hamiltonian as \[H_{\rm old}=\frac{p_r^2+p_z^2}{2}
   +\frac{\varpi_{\rm old}^2}{2r^2}-cU_0(r,z).\] The stated normalization of angular momentum is \(\varpi_{\rm new}^2=\varpi_{\rm old}^2/c\). With the corresponding momentum/time rescaling, \(H_{\rm new}=H_{\rm old}/c\), so \(h_{\rm new}=h_{\rm old}/c\). The text instead states \(h_{\rm new}=c h_{\rm old}\).

For an exact example, set \(c=2\), \(\alpha=4\), \(\varpi_{\rm old}^2=2\), and \((p_r,p_z,r,z)=(0,0,1,0)\). Positive physical masses realizing \(c\) are obtained by taking \(m=(2\sqrt2)^{2/5}\) and \(m_3=m/4\). Then \(U_0=2\), \(H_{\rm old}=-3\), and \(H_{\rm new}=-3/2\). The printed rule assigns energy \(-6\) to the same normalized configuration. In fact, the normalized minimum is \(-2\), so that assigned level is empty. The old energy is strictly between its minimum \(-4\) and escape threshold \(-1\). The repair is the reciprocal energy factor, with the momentum and time rescaling stated explicitly. The theorems formulated directly for the normalized Hamiltonian are unaffected by this localized error.
