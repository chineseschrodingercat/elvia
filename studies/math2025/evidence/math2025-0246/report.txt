Abstract: The assessment is Major. The cumulative linking formula fails its finite-orbit compatibility, and the signed-measure Portmanteau lemma used in the continuity argument is false even under a uniform total-variation bound. Further invalid steps occur in the positive approximation used to establish the Reeb-like condition, the construction of regular return times, the pressure concentration estimate, and the spanning-chain calculation underlying the local linking identity. Their demonstrated roles in the main construction determine the severity. The rational-coefficient assertions and the empty-set convention have separately delimited consequences. Explicit corrections and their verification boundaries are given below. The additional positive-avoidance argument, full atlas compatibility construction, and full weak-topology continuity claim are not certified by the local repairs. No counterexample to the corrected main orbit-equivalence theorem has been established. Scientific decision and adjudication remain pending.

# Scope and structure of the argument

The primary reviewer read all 73 pages of the acquired source text and actually inspected 44 selected source-page images, including every page used for the confirmed issues. A second auxiliary check supplied only partial observations from pp. 1–22. The primary reviewer independently evaluated the reported arguments and performed the exact finite calculations. Roles and model self-reports are recorded separately from the controller’s UI observation.

The main construction obtains a suitable null-homologous Gibbs measure, changes the differentiable structure and time parametrization, and smooths the resulting flow while correcting its invariant volume class. The linking criterion converts the Reeb-like measure condition into a contact reparametrization. Consequently the construction and continuity of the linking map in Section 5 are substantive dependencies of the main result, not isolated introductory notation.

The review traced the separation and averaging proof of the contact criterion, the Gibbs concentration and degree argument, the local return-time construction, the cohomology correction, the Markov reduction and linking kernel, the finite-orbit extension, and the orientation double cover. It did not independently reprove the full external Asaoka, Markov partition, shadowing, structural-stability, equilibrium-state, Fried or Birkhoff-section inputs. Complete reading does not certify every external dependency. A bounded publisher/manuscript search yielded no additional supplement or code artifact used here; that limitation is not a mathematical defect.

# Major issues

## F1. The cumulative definition fails its finite-orbit compatibility (Major)

Location. Page 64, Definition 89 and Lemma 91; the reduction is on p. 48, homology preservation on p. 50, and the local kernel and linking identity on pp. 53–54.

Write \[N_k=N_{R_k}\circ\cdots\circ N_{R_1},\qquad N=N_p.\] The printed cumulative term is \[\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\int L_{R_k}\,d(N_k^*\nu)\otimes\mu.\] It omits the change from \(\nu\) to \(N_1^*\nu\), and the kernel for the next change should be \(L_{R_{k+1}}\). Thus the displayed telescope in Lemma 91 starts from \(N_1^*\nu\), not from \(\nu\).

An admissible nonzero test. In the oriented stable/unstable setting of Section 5, take an interior two-branch first-return subsystem in a Markov rectangle \(R\), with distinct return words \(a,b\) and no intermediate visit to \(R\). Such a subsystem is provided, for example, by a return horseshoe of an orientable hyperbolic toral suspension. The return branches may be selected/refined so that the finite cycles below avoid coding boundaries. Enumerate \(R\) as \(R_1\), and let \(\eta_w\) denote the periodic counting measure of the full return word \(w\). Put \[\nu=\eta_{ab}-\eta_a-\eta_b,\qquad
 \mu=\eta_{aababb}-\eta_{aaabbb}.\] Lemma 70 gives \[N_R^*\nu=0,\qquad
 N_R^*\mu=(3\eta_a+3\eta_b)-(3\eta_a+3\eta_b)=0.\] Proposition 74 therefore makes both inputs null-homologous. The five primitive return cycles are distinct up to cyclic rotation, so their physical realizations give disjoint supports for \(\nu\) and \(\mu\). The two length-six cycles also avoid \(\operatorname{MinLoop}(P)\), since they visit \(R\) repeatedly.

Order the vertical branches as \(a<b\). Binary periodic fractions can be used as labels for lexicographic order; no metric assumption about the actual strips is involved. The two phases of \(ab\) have labels \(1/3\) and \(2/3\). The relevant part of the kernel tests the interval \([1/3,2/3)\). Exact cyclic phase counts are: \[\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{return word}&\text{all numerators, denominator }63&
 \text{numerators in }[21,42)\\ \hline
aababb&11,22,44,25,50,37&22,25,37\\
aaabbb&7,14,28,56,49,35&28,35
\end{array}\] If the horizontal branch order is also \(a<b\), the contributing phase of \(ab\) has sign \(-1\) and the other phase contributes zero. Reversing the horizontal order gives the opposite sign. Constant \(a\) and \(b\) cycles in the first input contribute zero, since the return reduction does not change them. Hence \[\int L_R\,d\nu\otimes\mu=-(3-2)=-1
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad +(3-2)=1.\] The count has also been executed with exact rational arithmetic. Theorem 80 gives this nonzero value for the finite-orbit linking number. However, \(N_k^*\nu=0\) for every \(k\ge1\), so both terms in printed Definition 89 vanish. This contradicts the finite, disjoint-support compatibility asserted by Lemma 91.

Repair and impact. With \(N_0=\mathrm{id}\), the consistent formula is \[\operatorname{link}_\sigma(\nu,\mu)
 =\operatorname{link}_\phi^f(\Theta_PN^*\nu,\Theta_P\mu)
 +\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\int L_{R_{k+1}}\,d(N_k^*\nu)\otimes\mu.\] Indeed, the corresponding differences sum to \[\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(N_k^*\nu-N_{k+1}^*\nu)=\nu-N_p^*\nu.\] This repairs the algebraic cumulative step. It does not by itself repair the continuity argument in F2. The defect affects the definition feeding Lemmas 90–92, Theorem 15 and the Reeb-like criterion used in the main construction. Its local index correction does not make the original failure peripheral. The countercalculation is not a refutation of the main theorem with a corrected linking construction.

## F2. Lemma 67 is false for signed measures (Major)

Location. Pages 46–47, Lemma 67; applications include Proposition 73 on p. 50 and Proposition 81 and its proof, including Lemma 84, on pp. 54–59.

Take \[X=[-1,1],\quad Y=\{0\},\quad f=\mathbf1_{(0,1]},\qquad
 \mu_n=\delta_{1/n}-\delta_{-1/n},\quad \nu=0.\] The function is bounded and continuous outside \(Y\). For every continuous \(g\), \[\int g\,d\mu_n=g(1/n)-g(-1/n)\longrightarrow0,\] so \(\mu_n\) converges weakly to \(\nu\), with \(\|\mu_n\|_{\rm TV}=2\). Nevertheless, \[\int f\,d\mu_n=1,\qquad
 2|\nu|(Y)\|f\|_\infty=0.\] Thus the stated bound fails even with uniformly bounded variation. The proof on p. 46 uses monotonicity of integration against a signed measure: \(f\le h_t^+\) does not imply the corresponding inequality of integrals.

Counterevidence and restricted repair. This example does not refute Proposition 73. Its exceptional sets \(E_R^\pm\) are null for every invariant positive measure. Given a weakly convergent sequence of signed invariant measures, uniform boundedness gives bounded variation. Pass to a common subsequence of their Jordan parts: \[\mu_n^+\Rightarrow a,\qquad \mu_n^-\Rightarrow b.\] The limits are invariant positive measures, \(a-b=\nu\), and both give zero mass to \(E_R^\pm\). Positive-measure Portmanteau applies separately; subtraction proves the desired sequential convergence. Applying this to a further subsequence of every subsequence removes dependence on the chosen Jordan limits.

For the two-input kernel, first prove the positive-input version of the cutoff argument in Lemma 84. Take common Jordan limits in both variables, choose exceptional atom neighborhoods for those positive limits, and combine the four pairs by bilinearity. Choosing neighborhoods only from \(|\nu_\infty|\) is insufficient: positive and negative limiting atoms may cancel in the signed limit. The existing total-variation tail estimates can then be used.

Repair boundary. These arguments explain how to recover the bounded sequential statements needed in the indicated steps. They do not automatically prove joint continuity on the entire signed-measure weak topology, as stated in Theorem 15 and Proposition 81. No independent counterexample to that full topological claim for the actual Anosov linking form was completed. The confirmed issue is the false general lemma and its uncorrected central use; the report does not turn the remaining topology question into a proved false theorem.

# Further proof defects and scope corrections

## F3. The density statement does not justify the positive approximation used in Lemma 31 (Major)

Location. Page 11, Lemma 14, compared with Lemmas 77–78 on pp. 52–53.

Consider the unit-roof suspension of \[A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.\] For the period-one orbit \(\gamma\) over a fixed point, let \(\mu=\sqrt2\,\operatorname{Leb}_\gamma\). The closed base-circle form \(dt\) gives \[[\mu]_\phi\cdot[dt]=\sqrt2.\] Every periodic orbit has integer winding around that circle. Every rational-coefficient multi-orbit therefore pairs rationally with \(dt\) and cannot have exactly this homology class.

The probability enhancement fails independently. With constant roof \(\sqrt2\), all orbit periods are \(n\sqrt2\). Every rational multi-orbit has mass in \(\sqrt2\mathbb Q\), never equal to one, whereas normalized periodic probability measures exist.

Lemmas 77–78 naturally produce real coefficients and do not establish the extra rational assertion. Use real coefficients for arbitrary homology. For a finite null-homologous combination, the weights satisfy \(Ac=0\) for an integer homology matrix \(A\); rational vectors in \(\ker A\) are dense. On a fixed positive support, sufficiently close rational weights preserve positivity. Exact mass normalization may then require real coefficients.

Core dependency. Lemma 31 on p. 22 obtains its positive intersection lower bound for positive multi-orbits whose support avoids \(\partial S\), then invokes Lemma 14 to pass to an arbitrary null-homologous invariant probability. Lemma 14 supplies avoidance without the required positivity, and supplies its probability conclusion only for an empty excluded set. Lemma 77 supplies positivity, whereas Lemma 78 obtains avoidance by a signed subtraction. These statements do not justify the asserted limiting inequality. The resulting Reeb-like condition is used in Theorem 34 on p. 31, from which the proof of Theorem A starts on p. 37. This missing justification is a Major defect in the derivation. The suspension examples separately refute the rationality clauses; they do not refute the skewed main theorem.

Repair boundary. A repair must prove suitable positive approximation avoiding the prescribed finite orbit set, or justify another passage of the intersection estimate to the limit. A homological-fullness route requires proving the existence of positive avoiding periodic measures whose homology convex hull contains zero in its interior. Once that construction and positive avoiding approximations of homology \(h_n\to0\) are established, a positive correction of mass \(O(\|h_n\|)\) can cancel \(h_n\), followed by normalization. This report does not certify those missing inputs merely by naming homological fullness and shadowing. The real-coefficient and rational-kernel observations alone do not complete this step.

## F4. The local time-change formula is inverted (Major)

Location. The convention on p. 33 and the construction before Proposition 48 on p. 34.

The convention \(f\,\partial_t\psi^f=\partial_t\phi\) implies \[T^f(x)=\int_0^{T^1(x)} f(\phi_t x)\,dt.\] The printed constant-on-segment choice \(f=T^1g\) gives \((T^1)^2g\), not \(g\). Taking \(T^1=1/2\) and \(g=1\) yields \(f=1/2\) and \(T^f=1/4\), and also violates the intended range \(f\in[1,2]\).

Use \(f=g/T^1\) instead. This gives \(f=2\) and \(T^f=1\) in the same test. To retain \(f\in[1,2]\), the chosen target time must satisfy \(T^1\le g\le2T^1\), with room for compatible regularization. This verifies the corrected local identity.

Dependency and scope. On p. 34 the claimed equality \(T^f=g\) is the stated idea for the inductive construction of regular short return times. The printed choice gives \((T^1)^2g\), whose regularity does not follow from that of \(g\) when \(T^1\) is only continuous. Lemma 49 and Proposition 48 use regular return times to construct the required \(C^{1+H}\) atlas on p. 35. Theorem A on p. 37 uses that atlas and its regular invariant volume. The failed calculation is therefore a Major construction defect. The quotient repairs the local identity, but all global inductive compatibility and range choices have not been independently completed. No counterexample to atlas existence is asserted.

## F5. The pressure competitor requires probability normalization (Major)

Location. Page 29, proof of Lemma 38; the orbit-measure convention is on p. 9.

The paper defines \(\operatorname{Leb}_\gamma\) to have mass \(T_\gamma\). Hence \(p|_\gamma=D\) gives \[\int p\,d\operatorname{Leb}_\gamma=DT_\gamma,\] not \(D\). For \(T_\gamma=2\) and \(D=1\), the two values are two and one. The pressure variational principle also takes probability measures.

Replace the competitor by \(\operatorname{Leb}_\gamma/T_\gamma\), whose entropy is zero. The proof then reads \[D\le\int p\,d\mu_p+h_{\mu_p}
 \le C\mu_p(M\setminus U)+D\mu_p(U)+h_{\rm top},\] and yields exactly the stated concentration bound. This verifies the concentration bound with an admissible probability competitor.

Dependency and scope. The printed comparison uses a measure outside the probability class and drops its period factor. Lemma 39 on p. 30 invokes Lemma 38 to force concentration; Proposition 37 and Lemma 41 use Lemma 39. Theorem 34 on p. 31 uses these concentration and degree arguments to obtain the required Reeb-like Gibbs measure, from which Theorem A starts on p. 37. This invalid probability comparison is part of the core derivation and is assessed Major. The normalized calculation restores the same bound; neither the concentration conclusion nor the corrected main theorem is disproved.

## F6. The spanning chain has the opposite orientation (Major)

Location. Page 61, the definition of \(S\), Claim 1 and the intersection calculation; \(\Gamma\) is defined on p. 60.

The displayed boundaries give \[\partial e_i=a_i+b_i+\gamma_{s_i}-\operatorname{segment}_i(r),
 \qquad \partial C=\sum_i(a_i+b_i).\] Therefore \[\partial\Bigl(\sum_i e_i-C\Bigr)
   =\sum_i\gamma_{s_i}-\gamma_r=-\Gamma,\] not \(\Gamma\). Moreover, since the \(e_i\) avoid \(\Gamma'\), the printed chain intersects \(\Gamma'\) as \(-C\cdot\Gamma'\), while the proof uses the positive sign. Define \(S=C-\sum_i e_i\) instead: both \(\partial S=\Gamma\) and \(S\cdot\Gamma'=C\cdot\Gamma'\) are restored. The corrected orientation supports the intended local kernel sign used in F1.

Dependency and scope. The chain in Lemma 87 has the opposite boundary and intersection sign from those asserted. On p. 63, Theorem 80 explicitly invokes Lemmas 87–88 to identify the local kernel integral with finite-orbit linking, an identification required by the cumulative construction on p. 64. This invalid spanning-chain derivation is therefore Major. Reversing the chain restores both required identities; no opposite-sign counterexample to the corrected linking theorem is claimed.

## F7. The Reeb-like minimum needs an empty-set convention (Minor)

Location. Page 13, Definition 20; suspension cases are discussed on pp. 5 and 68.

For a unit-roof suspension, \(dt(X)=1\), so every invariant probability \(\rho\) satisfies \([\rho]_\phi\cdot[dt]=1\). Thus \(\mathcal M_p^0(\phi)\) is empty, and its ordinary real minimum is undefined. Specify an extended infimum with \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\), or an explicitly quantified convention. This does not create a false Reeb volume in the suspension: a positive invariant volume has nonzero homology and already fails the other defining condition.

# Positive checks and remaining qualifications

The ambient orientation is explicitly preserved by orbit equivalence on p. 6; an orientation-reversing-map objection would not meet the hypotheses. The contact criterion has the consistent sign \[\alpha\wedge\iota_XV=\alpha(X)V.\] The separation and time-averaging argument makes \(\alpha(X)\) uniformly positive while preserving \(d\alpha=\iota_XV\). The final smoothing construction subtracts the cohomology correction with the correct sign. In the orientation double cover on pp. 65–66, transfer doubles both projected measure and intersection count, so the factor \(1/2\) is consistent.

After F5 is corrected, the Gibbs concentration estimate supports the orthant degree argument. A fixed-orthant subsequence uses the appropriate signed homology basis; the zero-dimensional homology case should be handled directly rather than via a negative-dimensional sphere. The positive Birkhoff-section construction was traced, while its external geometric inputs remain outside an independent proof certification.

Two regularity/topology qualifications remain separate from confirmed theorem counterexamples. First, smooth functions are not dense in the full endpoint Hölder norm: for \(0<\alpha<1\), the seminorm distance from \(|r|^\alpha\) to any smooth function near zero is at least one. The smoothing step on p. 40 therefore needs an exponent strictly below available regularity. Since the exponent is chosen in the construction, this can be accommodated by retaining such slack; no failure of the main theorem follows from this observation. Second, transverse transfer under orbit equivalence does not generally preserve probability mass. Positive normalization is needed where probability compactness is used in Lemma 56; it preserves the relevant positivity and can be controlled for converging time changes.

The source image on p. 58 resolves an extraction ambiguity: the definition of the cutoff set uses intersection. No set-difference error is reported. The report also makes no unsupported claim that absence of an acquired supplement or executable code is a mathematical flaw.

# Disposition

The assessment remains Major. F1–F6 identify invalid definitions or proof steps with demonstrated roles in the linking construction, the Reeb-like Gibbs-measure argument, or the regular atlas needed by the main proof. Correct the cumulative indices and chain orientation, replace the signed-measure argument with justified positive-measure and Jordan-limit reasoning, supply the missing passage of the positive intersection estimate, and correct the time-change and probability normalization. F7 additionally needs its empty-set convention. The local repairs do not certify the positive-avoidance construction, all atlas compatibility choices, or full joint weak-topology continuity. No counterexample to the corrected orbit-equivalence theorem has been established, and that theorem has not been independently certified in its entirety.
