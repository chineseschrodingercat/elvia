Abstract: This scoped review identifies a missing finite-family restriction in Proposition 8.5 of Even-Zohar, Lakrec, and Tessler’s paper. An explicit countable collection of intervals satisfies the stated local stratification and boundary-pairing conditions but fails the asserted covering conclusion. The defect is local to the general formulation: the actual BCFW family is finite, so this counterexample does not invalidate the principal triangulation result. An independent exact \(k=1,n=6\) example verifies injectivity, separation, and coverage for a fixed positive matrix. The supported assessment is Minor. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# A missing restriction in Proposition 8.5

Problem 1 (The local boundary conditions do not control an infinite family).  Proposition 8.5 (printed pp. 1120–1121; PDF pp. 112–113) is stated for a collection \(\{S_a\}_{a\in A}\) without explicitly assuming that \(A\) is finite. Its conclusion \[f\left(\bigcup_{a\in A}\overline{S_a}\right)=K\] is false for a general infinite family under the stratified-frontier convention used in the cell application.

## Original hypotheses and exact counterexample

The proposition assumes a smooth submersion \(f:B\to N\) between manifolds without boundary, \(\dim N=n\), a connected open \(L\subset B\) with compact closure, and \(K=f(\overline L)\). The \(n\)-dimensional submanifolds \(S_a\subset\overline L\) have disjoint images, compact closures with finite boundary stratifications, shared codimension-one boundaries unless their images lie in \(\partial K\), and an injective restriction with continuous inverse on each cell together with its shared boundaries.

Equation reference: eq:example. Set \[
 \begin{aligned}
 &B=N=\mathbb R,\quad f=\mathrm{id},\quad n=1,\quad
 L=(0,1),\quad K=[0,1],\\
 &a_j=\frac12+\frac1{2j},\qquad S_j=(a_{j+1},a_j),\qquad j\ge1.
 \end{aligned}\] Every hypothesis can be checked directly.

First, \(f\) is a smooth submersion, \(L\) is connected and open, and \(\overline L\) is compact. Each \(S_j\) is a smooth embedded one-dimensional manifold without boundary contained in \(L\), and distinct \(S_j\) have disjoint images.

Second, \(\overline{S_j}=[a_{j+1},a_j]\) is compact. Its two boundary strata are the singleton sets \(\{a_{j+1}\}\) and \(\{a_j\}\), both smooth zero-dimensional manifolds. Each has empty frontier \(\overline{\{a\}}\setminus\{a\}\), so the lower-stratum requirement holds. Here the boundary of a stratum is understood as its frontier, as required in the proposition’s stratified-cell application; it is not the ambient boundary of a lower-dimensional closed subset.

Third, \(a_1=1\) belongs to \(\partial K\). Every \(a_j\), \(j\ge2\), lies in the interior of \(K\) and is the common boundary point of \(S_{j-1}\) and \(S_j\). Therefore every codimension-one boundary stratum not mapped to \(\partial K\) is shared, exactly as required.

Finally, the identity map is injective and has continuous inverse on each interval together with its shared endpoints. This checks the fourth condition without an unstated differentiability assumption on the inverse.

Equation reference: eq:failure. Nevertheless, \[
 f\left(\bigcup_{j\ge1}\overline{S_j}\right)
 =\left(\frac12,1\right]\ne[0,1]=K .\] In particular, the interior point \(1/4\) is missing. Even taking the closure of the entire union would not repair coverage of that point. The only nonshared boundary stratum is \(\{1\}\), so the proposition’s additional description of \(\partial K\) also misses \(0\). This is an exact analytic counterexample, not a finite numerical surrogate.

## Where finiteness enters the proof

Lemma 8.6, quoted immediately after the proposition, explicitly assumes a finite collection of lower-dimensional submanifolds. Applying it to the combined boundary strata requires a corresponding finiteness condition that is absent from the proposition’s statement. The next page (printed p. 1122) also treats the union of the compact images \(f(\overline{S_a})\) as closed. That inference is valid for finite unions, but fails in [eq:example] (#eq:example): the union accumulates at \(1/2\), which it omits.

These are specific failures in the stated level of generality, rather than objections to stratification terminology or to an untested computational implementation.

## Counterevidence, impact, and repair

The actual BCFW family is finite. Its explicit cardinality on printed page 1011 is \[\frac1{k+1}\binom{n-3}{k}\binom{n-4}{k}.\] The counterexample therefore does not belong to the main triangulation application. It establishes neither failure of Theorems 1.3–1.5 nor failure of a finite-family version of the assembly argument.

A narrow repair is to require \(A\) to be finite and nonempty, or to state the proposition only for the finite stratified family used in its application. This excludes [eq:example] (#eq:example), makes the union of the compact images closed, and makes the combined finite boundary stratifications eligible for Lemma 8.6. These two repairs are verified. The present check does not certify every other aspect of a revised abstract proposition.

The supported impact is Minor: a local overgeneralization with no demonstrated damage to the central triangulation theorem. If finiteness is explicitly adopted as a standing contextual restriction, the issue is instead resolved by scope clarification. The finding is not a claim of first discovery or of absence of a later erratum.

# Independent checks supporting the bounded conclusion

## Linear algebra and the child case

In Lemma 4.35 (printed pp. 1067–1068), let \(U=\operatorname{span}(Y_1,\ldots,Y_k)\) and \(V=\operatorname{span}(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{m+1})\) in \(\mathbb R^{k+m}\). The stated rank and nonzero mixed-minor assumptions give \(\dim(U+V)=k+m\) and therefore \(\dim(U\cap V)=1\). The alternating mixed-cofactor vector \[\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{j-1}
 \det(Y,Z_1,\ldots,\widehat Z_j,\ldots,Z_{m+1})\,Z_j\] belongs to that intersection and is nonzero by the independence of the \(Z_j\). This verifies the local reconstruction mechanism.

Positive ordered maximal minors imply that every subset of at most \(k+4\) rows of \(Z\) is independent. The six-row independence used for a nonsticky child in Proposition 5.1 is compatible with this bound because a child requires \(k\ge2\); it is not silently applied when \(k=1\). This check does not replace the full combinatorial nonvanishing proof.

## A complete \(k=1,n=6\) example

Take \(Z_i=(1,i,i^2,i^3,i^4)\) for \(1\le i\le6\). The six ordered maximal minors, in lexicographic row-subset order, are \[288,\ 1440,\ 2880,\ 2880,\ 1440,\ 288,\] so this matrix satisfies the required strict positivity. The three BCFW supports in this case are \[\{1,2,3,4,6\},\qquad\{1,2,4,5,6\},\qquad\{2,3,4,5,6\}.\] Normalize each nonnegative row \(c\) by \(\sum_i c_i=1\). The left kernel of \(Z\) is generated by \[r=(-1,5,-10,10,-5,1),\qquad rZ=0,\qquad\sum_i r_i=0.\] The independence of the five supported rows proves injectivity on each cell. If positive rows on two distinct supports, missing odd indices \(i\) and \(j\), had the same image, then \(c-d=t r\). The \(i\)th coordinate forces \(t>0\), whereas the \(j\)th forces \(t<0\), since both corresponding entries of \(r\) are negative. Thus the open images are disjoint.

For any nonnegative normalized \(c\), let \[t=\min\{c_1,c_3/10,c_5/5\},\qquad d=c+t r.\] Then \(d\ge0\), \(\sum_i d_i=1\), \(dZ=cZ\), and at least one odd coordinate vanishes. Hence the three closed simplices cover the polytope. Their open interiors have an open dense union because their omitted faces form a finite union of lower-dimensional sets. This proves the three triangulation properties for this fixed \(Z\).

The independent exact calculation also reproduces the relevant separator signs in the stored \(n=6\) output. For positive support coefficients \(a,b,c,d,e\), the paired expressions include \(1440c\) versus \(-2880d\), \(1440a\) versus \(-288d\), and \(-4147200a(c+2d)\) versus \(414720b(c+2d)\). No sampled positivity assumption or author-code execution is used. This verifies the bounded example, not all values of \(k,n\) or the complete Sage implementation.

# Conclusion

The finite-family omission in Proposition 8.5 has a complete counterexample and a precisely limited consequence. The actual BCFW family meets the missing finite restriction. The independent small example and local linear algebra provide positive checks, without being presented as a general proof of the paper.
