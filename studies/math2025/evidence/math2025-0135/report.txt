Abstract: The acquired publisher version is checked through the Hodge-filtration reconstruction, deformation and representation extension spaces, and local–global compatibility. Two local defects are established: the dimension calculation in the proof of Corollary 2.14 has the wrong sign, and the proof of Theorem 4.5 identifies the entire locally algebraic part with its simple socle even though a nonsplit smooth twist extension lies inside it. Corrected arguments recover the required conclusions. An apparent dimension conflict in Theorem 3.34 is resolved by the parabolic-image spaces supplied by its cited diagram; it is not presented as a counterexample. The assessment is Minor within the inspected scope. No failure of the main reconstruction or compatibility theorem is established. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Assumptions and notation that matter

Let \(K/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite, \(d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]\), and let the coefficient field \(E\) contain all embeddings of \(K\). The smooth characters \(\phi_i\) are generic: the ratio of any two distinct ordered parameters is neither \(1\) nor \(|\cdot|_K\). The Hodge–Tate weights are strictly decreasing at every embedding. Every refinement is noncritical; noncriticality of only one selected refinement would not support the argument.

The category \(\Phi\Gamma_{\mathrm{nc}}(\phi,\mathbf h)\) includes crystabelline \((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-modules that are not étale. An example in this category must not silently be called a Galois representation. The Galois applications separately assume that \(D=D_{\mathrm{rig}}(\rho)\) and impose their stated automorphic hypotheses.

On the Galois side, the bar has a precise meaning: \[\overline{\operatorname{Ext}}^1_?(D,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^1_?(D,D)/\operatorname{Ext}^1_0(D,D),\qquad
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_0(D,D)=\operatorname{Ker}\kappa_w .\] Lemma 2.11 makes the last kernel independent of \(w\). Writing \(c=n(n-1)/2\) and \(b=n(n+1)/2\), the dimension checks give \[\begin{aligned}
 \dim\operatorname{Ext}^1(D,D)&=1+n^2d,&
 \dim\operatorname{Ext}^1_0(D,D)&=1+cd-n,\\
 \dim\overline{\operatorname{Ext}}^1(D,D)&=bd+n,&
 \dim\overline{\operatorname{Ext}}^1_w(D,D)&=n(d+1).\end{aligned}\] For \(K\ne\mathbb Q_p\), the representation determines the flattened single-embedding objects \(D_\sigma\), not necessarily all relative filtrations of \(D\). The stronger full-\(D\) conclusion is correctly restricted to \(K=\mathbb Q_p\).

# Two confirmed local problems

Equation reference: eq:wrongdim. Problem 1 (Wrong sign in the quotient dimension).  In the proof of Corollary 2.14, p. 19, the dimension comparison used to prove that (2.19) is bijective prints \[
 \dim_E\overline{\operatorname{Ext}}^1_{\mathcal F_P}(D,D)
   =d\dim(B\cap L_P)-n
   =\sum_i\dim_E\overline{\operatorname{Ext}}^1(M_i,M_i).\] The middle expression must have \(+n\).

Equation reference: eq:rightdim. Direct calculation and hypotheses. Let \(L_P=\prod_{i=1}^r\mathrm{GL}_{n_i}\), with \(\sum_i n_i=n\), and set \(b_L=\sum_i n_i(n_i+1)/2\). The paper’s Proposition 2.13 and (2.14) give \[\begin{aligned}
 \dim_E\overline{\operatorname{Ext}}^1_{\mathcal F_P}(D,D)
 &= (1+d\dim P)-(1+dc-n)\notag\\
 &=d(\dim P-c)+n
 =db_L+n\notag\\
 &=\sum_i\left(d\,\frac{n_i(n_i+1)}2+n_i\right).
 \end{aligned}\] Here \(\dim P-c=b_L\), an identity for the block upper triangular parabolic. Equation [eq:rightdim] (#eq:rightdim) equals the target dimension by (2.16) applied to the \(M_i\).

For a concrete allowed case take \(n=2\), \(K=\mathbb Q_5\), \(P=B\), distinct Frobenius eigenvalues \(1,2\), and weights \(1>0\). A filtration whose nontrivial line is \(E(1,1)\) is transverse to both coordinate lines, hence every refinement is noncritical. The generic ratios avoid \(1\) and \(5^{\pm1}\). The filtered-module construction gives an object of the paper’s \((\varphi,\Gamma)\)-module category; no weak-admissibility assertion is needed. Then \(\dim\operatorname{Ext}^1_0=0\), the source dimension is \(4\), and the two rank-one target factors each have dimension \(2\). The printed middle value in [eq:wrongdim] (#eq:wrongdim) is \(2-2=0\).

Counterevidence, impact and correction. The minus sign is visible in the original PDF, so this is not an extraction error. The preceding formulas and the target calculation already force the corrected value. The error affects the explicit auxiliary numerical equality; it does not refute Corollary 2.14. The map is surjective as established before that equality, and [eq:rightdim] (#eq:rightdim) proves equal source and target dimensions. Thus its bijectivity follows with no changed hypotheses or conclusion. This is a Minor calculation defect with its complete local repair verified. An exact diagnostic checked [eq:rightdim] (#eq:rightdim) for all ordered block compositions of \(1\le n\le7\) and \(d=1,2,3\) (381 cases); the displayed algebra proves the general formula.

Problem 2 (The locally algebraic part is larger than the socle).  The proof of Theorem 4.5, p. 63, asserts \(\widetilde\pi^{\mathrm{lalg}}\simeq\pi_{\mathrm{alg}}(\phi,\mathbf h)\), citing Lemma 4.2(2). The cited lemma controls the multiplicity of the simple representation in a Hom space. It does not exclude nonsplit locally algebraic selfextensions, and such an extension occurs inside \(\widetilde\pi\).

Equation reference: eq:twist. A selfextension satisfying the operative conditions. Put \(A=\pi_{\mathrm{alg}}(\phi,\mathbf h)\), and normalize \(v_K(\varpi_K)=1\). Define an \(E\)-representation on \(V=A\otimes_E E[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) by \[
 g\cdot(a\otimes u)
   =(g\cdot a)\otimes
     \bigl(1+\epsilon\,v_K(\det g)\bigr)u .\] It fits into \(0\to A\to V\to A\to0\). The additional factor is smooth, since \(v_K\circ\det\) is locally constant. As \(A\) is smooth tensored with algebraic, every vector of \(V\) is locally algebraic.

This extension is nonsplit. The central element \(z=\varpi_K I_n\) acts on \(A\) by its nonzero central scalar \(\chi_A(z)\), whereas on \(V\) it acts by \[\chi_A(z)
 \begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\] in a suitable order of the two factors. The coefficient \(n\) is nonzero in the characteristic-zero field \(E\). A split sum of two copies of \(A\) would have scalar action at \(z\).

Why this extension is present in the actual object. Fix a refinement \(w\) and put \(B_w=\mathrm{PS}_1(w(\phi),\mathbf h)\). Its quotient by \(A\) has the nonalgebraic simple constituents listed in Section 3.1.2 and no copy of \(A\). Consequently \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{GL}_n(K)}(A,B_w/A)=0\), and the long exact sequence makes \[\operatorname{Ext}^1(A,A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^1(A,B_w)\] injective. Push out \(V\) along \(A\hookrightarrow B_w\). The resulting nonzero extension \(V_w\) contains \(V\) as a subrepresentation. Proposition 3.6(2) identifies this pushout with the smooth character direction used in the principal-series construction.

By Lemma 3.35, p. 58, \(I_{B^-(K)}^{\mathrm{GL}_n(K)}\widetilde\delta_w^{\mathrm{univ}}\) is the tautological universal extension of \(A\) by \(B_w\). The inverse image of the one-dimensional extension-class subspace spanned by \([V_w]\) is \(V_w\) itself. Lemma 4.3 embeds that universal extension in the patched representation, and its image is among the generators defining \(\widetilde\pi\). Thus \[V\hookrightarrow V_w\hookrightarrow
 I_{B^-(K)}^{\mathrm{GL}_n(K)}\widetilde\delta_w^{\mathrm{univ}}
 \hookrightarrow\widetilde\pi .\] This argument works in any instance satisfying Section 4.1’s hypotheses; it does not substitute an arbitrary unrelated representation for \(\widetilde\pi\), and it does not use Theorem 4.5’s asserted isomorphism to prove containment. Since \(V\) has length two and \(A\) is simple, \(\widetilde\pi^{\mathrm{lalg}}\) cannot equal \(A\).

Counterevidence and the conclusion actually needed. The disputed superscript is visibly \(\mathrm{lalg}\) in the publisher PDF. The surrounding argument excludes all nonalgebraic simple subrepresentations and needs only \(\operatorname{soc}_{\mathrm{GL}_n(K)}\widetilde\pi\simeq A\). Lemma 4.2(2) gives \[\dim_E\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{GL}_n(K)}
   \bigl(A,\Pi_\infty^{R_\infty\text{-an}}[\mathfrak a_x]\bigr)=1 .\] An existing copy of \(A\) lies in \(\widetilde\pi\). Together with the preceding exclusion of the other possible simple constituents, this proves exactly the required socle assertion. Replace the false equality by this Hom-dimension argument. The subsequent multiplicity and universal-extension steps then retain their stated inputs. The confirmed defect is the unnecessarily strong local assertion, not a demonstrated failure of Theorem 4.5 or its compatibility corollaries. Its assessment is Minor; the weaker sufficient statement is already proved by the cited lemma, and its use has been traced explicitly.

# Central proof checks and resolved candidates

## Higher intertwining and single-embedding recovery

The reconstruction in Section 2 distinguishes the dimension-\(d\) cup-product kernel for full \(D\) from the dimension-one kernel after flattening away from a single embedding. For \(K=\mathbb Q_p\), the extension class therefore spans the kernel; for general \(K\), this argument applies to \(D_\sigma\). The rank and Hom arguments use genericity and strict weight separation.

The last equivalence in Theorem 2.32 can be made explicit. After twisting to set \(\kappa_{\mathcal F,2}=0\), restriction \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(D,D_1)\to\operatorname{Ext}^1(D_1,D_1)\) has kernel dimension \((n-1)d-1\). By Proposition 2.29, the inverse image of the specified intertwining subspace has dimension \[[1+(n-1)(n-2)d]+[(n-1)d-1]=(n-1)^2d .\] It contains the injective image of \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(C_1,D_1)\), which has that same dimension. Hence the two spaces agree, supplying the converse rather than only the forward inclusion.

## Spanning in Theorem 3.21

Equation (3.43), p. 49, is written as a direct sum of character spaces of maximal-Levi centres \(Z_j\), together with the full centre \(Z\). Each \(Z_j\) has rank two, so that literal direct sum has dimension \((2n-1)(d+1)\), whereas \(\operatorname{Hom}(T(K),E)\) has dimension \(n(d+1)\). The proof’s next step requires only the spanning assertion, which holds.

Indeed, put \(H=\operatorname{Hom}(K^\times,E)\), so \(\operatorname{Hom}(T(K),E)=H^n\). Characters of the two blocks of \(L_j\), pulled back by their determinants, include \[(h,\ldots,h,0,\ldots,0),\qquad h\in H,\] with \(j\) initial entries. Together with the all-entries diagonal characters, these vectors span \(H^n\), by taking consecutive differences. They belong to the subspaces used in (2.21). The block Weyl element in the proof fixes the corresponding block characters. The two known endpoint cases therefore extend to every refinement exactly as required on p. 50. The overstated direct-sum notation is recorded as a clarification; the necessary spanning step has been verified and no dimension argument in the construction relies on directness.

## The spaces in the reconstruction diagram

Theorem 3.34, p. 56, uses barred automorphic Ext symbols in (3.53)–(3.54), while the explicitly cited (3.52) supplies parabolic-image spaces. Treating the bars as ordinary full Ext would give incorrect dimensions. The following contextual reconstruction makes the needed domain precise.

Equation reference: eq:correctdiagram. Write \(A=\pi_{\mathrm{alg}}(\phi,\mathbf h)\), \(B=\pi_{1,\sigma}(\phi,\mathbf h)\), and \[\mathcal E=\operatorname{Ext}^1_\sigma(A,B),\quad
 \mathcal E_{\mathcal F},\mathcal E_{\mathcal G}\subset\mathcal E,\quad
 \mathcal K=\operatorname{Ker}t_{D_\sigma},\quad
 \mathcal K_{\mathcal F}=\mathcal K\cap\mathcal E_{\mathcal F},
 \quad
 \mathcal K_{\mathcal G}=\mathcal K\cap\mathcal E_{\mathcal G}.\] The tautological connecting maps identify the parabolic images in \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_\sigma(A,\pi^-)\) and \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_\sigma(A,\pi^+)\) with \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}/\mathcal K_{\mathcal F}\) and \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal G}/\mathcal K_{\mathcal G}\). Proposition 3.20 gives the surjection \[
 \frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal F}}{\mathcal K_{\mathcal F}}
 \oplus
 \frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal G}}{\mathcal K_{\mathcal G}}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal E}{\mathcal K}.\] Its kernel is determined by the common representation data. Diagram (3.52) identifies its source with \(V(D_{1,\sigma},C_{1,\sigma})_\sigma\). After the induction hypothesis identifies \(D_{1,\sigma}\) and \(C_{1,\sigma}\), the middle map is unchanged for \(D\) and \(D'\). The same kernel therefore gives the same two Galois graph subspaces; Corollary 2.38 recovers \(D_\sigma\).

For \(n=3\), the two restricted source dimensions are \(7\) and \(7\), the target dimension is \(9\), and the kernel dimension is \(5\). Full Ext spaces would instead give \(20\) in the middle. The undefined extra bar admits the parabolic-image reading specified by (3.52), and [eq:correctdiagram] (#eq:correctdiagram) completes that argument. Accordingly the full-Ext dimension objection is not graded as a proved defect in the reconstruction theorem.

# Overall impact and limits

The main construction has kernel dimension \((2^n-n(n+1)/2-1)d\); its single-embedding kernels have dimension \(2^n-n(n+1)/2-1\) and form the direct sum in Corollary 3.31. These dimensions and the rank-one and rank-two boundary cases are consistent. In the global argument, the first-order algebra action, annihilator description of \(\pi_{\min}(D)\), and the multiplicity argument are distinct from the false local equality in Problem 2.

The unpatched application is conditional on Hypotheses 4.10 and 4.15 and the stated automorphic setup; it is not an unconditional local Langlands construction for all representations. The review checks the interfaces used to pass to the specified tangent subspace and the stated multiplicity, while retaining the external patching, adjunction, triangulation and Selmer-vanishing theorems as external inputs.
