Abstract: This review checks the stochastic-cycle argument in Lemma 9.5 and its place in the remainder estimates, with additional checks of the formulation and selected energy identities. The union in (9.17) makes the ensuing bound on the number of short flights false, even in a ball. An explicit family of admissible trajectories proves the discrepancy. The earlier reference uses an intersection, strongly supporting an intended local correction. An independent corrected counting argument recovers the stated exponential-tail scaling. The finding concerns an invalid step in a core derivation; neither the tail estimate nor the main ghost-effect theorem is disproved. This is a bounded initial review of arXiv:2301.09427v3; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and operative assumptions

Theorem 1.2, p. 7, assumes a bounded \(C^3\) domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3\), the small wall-temperature condition (1.14), and a fixed \(P>0\); it asserts the result for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\). The unit ball and constant wall temperature \(T_w=1\) are admissible. The latter makes the temperature-gradient smallness requirement automatic.

In the stochastic cycles, \(n(x)\) is the outward unit normal and \[V_j=\{v\in\mathbb R^3:v\cdot n(x_j)>0\},\qquad
 d\sigma_j=\mu_M(v_j)|v_j\cdot n(x_j)|\,dv_j,\] where \(\mu_M\) is a fixed positive isotropic Gaussian. Definitions 9.2–9.3, p. 59, use \[t_b(x,v)=\inf\{t>0:x-\varepsilon tv\notin\Omega\},
 \qquad t_{j+1}=t_j+t_b(x_j,v_j).\] The initial point may be interior, velocities are nonzero, and boundary hits under consideration are nongrazing. Lemma 9.5 claims an exponentially small diffuse measure of \(t_k<\mathcal T/\varepsilon\) for large \(\mathcal T\) and \(k\) of order \(\mathcal T^{5/4}\). This is a measure estimate, not the absence of individual short trajectories.

# Substantive finding

Problem 1 (The set in (9.17) does not imply the claimed flight count).  The deterministic counting assertion immediately following (9.17), p. 60, is false with the union printed in that equation.

## Original step and location

Equation reference: eq:union. Equation (9.17) defines \[
 V_j^\eta
 =\{v\in V_j:v\cdot n(x_j)>\eta\}
 \ \cup\ \{v\in V_j:|v|<\eta^{-1}\}.\] For small \(0<\eta<1\), the next sentence asserts that \(t_k<\mathcal T/\varepsilon\) permits at most \[M=\left\lfloor C_\Omega\mathcal T/\eta^3\right\rfloor+1\] indices with \(v_j\in V_j^\eta\). Equation (9.18) uses that count to reduce the integral to configurations with at most \(M\) such velocities. The rendered PDF confirms the union symbol; this is not a text-extraction ambiguity.

## Admissible counterexample

Equation reference: eq:delta, eq:velocity. Take \(\Omega=B(0,1)\), \(T_w=1\), any fixed \(P>0\), and any admissibly small \(\varepsilon>0\). For every proposed \(C_\Omega>0\), every \(\mathcal T>0\) and \(0<\eta<1\), define \(M\) as above, choose an integer \(k\ge M+2\), and set \[
 \delta=\min\{\eta/2,\mathcal T/(16k),1/8\}>0.\] At each boundary point \(x_j\), choose a unit tangent vector \(\tau_j\perp x_j\) and put \[
 v_j=\delta x_j+\sqrt{1/4-\delta^2}\,\tau_j.\] Then \(|v_j|=1/2\) and \(v_j\cdot n(x_j)=\delta>0\). Every velocity is nonzero and nongrazing, and belongs to [eq:union] (#eq:union) through the speed condition despite \(\delta<\eta\).

Equation reference: eq:flight. Proof. Starting at \(x\in\partial B(0,1)\) with such a velocity, the next sphere intersection solves \[|x-sv|^2-1=-2s\delta+s^2/4=0.\] The nonzero root is \(s=8\delta\), the intervening segment is inside the ball, and therefore \[
 t_b(x,v)=8\delta/\varepsilon,\qquad
 x_{\rm next}=x-8\delta v\in\partial\Omega.\] Choose \(x_1\in\partial\Omega\), \(v_1\) as above, and start at \(x_0=x_1-4\delta v_1\), \(v_0=-v_1\). Since \(|x_0|^2=1-4\delta^2<1\), the first backward exit is \(x_1\) at \(t_1=4\delta/\varepsilon\). Continue with [eq:velocity] (#eq:velocity) at every hit. It follows that \[t_k=(8k-4)\delta/\varepsilon<\mathcal T/\varepsilon,\] although all \(k-1\ge M+1\) sampled velocities belong to the printed \(V_j^\eta\). This contradicts the asserted count. The construction works for arbitrarily large \(\mathcal T\) and for the integer choices of \(k\) in the proof whenever \(k-1>M\). ◻

The inequalities have strict margins. For each finite \(k\), nearby velocities preserve the violation: transversal sphere hits depend smoothly on the input, and the Gaussian density is positive. Thus this is not an isolated zero-measure trajectory. This observation does not compare that neighborhood’s measure with the claimed exponential bound; it does not refute Lemma 9.5 itself.

The root-authored symbolic check in `verification/0431/problems/P01/` verifies the speed, intersection equation, interior initial point and exact rational examples. The argument above proves the universal counterexample family; finite diagnostics are supplementary.

## Verified local repair

Equation reference: eq:intersection. Replace [eq:union] (#eq:union) by \[
 G_j^\eta=\{v\in V_j:v\cdot n(x_j)>\eta,\ |v|<\eta^{-1}\}.\] A bounded \(C^3\) domain has a uniform interior tangent-ball radius \(r>0\). The segment through that ball gives \[t_b(x_j,v)\ge
 \frac{2r\,v\cdot n(x_j)}{\varepsilon |v|^2}
 >\frac{2r\eta^3}{\varepsilon}
 \quad(v\in G_j^\eta).\] Hence \(t_k<\mathcal T/\varepsilon\) permits at most \(M=\lfloor\mathcal T/(2r\eta^3)\rfloor+1\) such flights. Global convexity is unnecessary.

Equation reference: eq:binomial. The tail estimate can be recovered without assuming that \(d\sigma_j\) has unit mass. Set \(a=\max\{1,\sup_j\int_{V_j}d\sigma_j\}\) and \(b=\sup_j\int_{V_j\setminus G_j^\eta}d\sigma_j\). Gaussian integration gives \(b\le C\eta<1\) for small \(\eta\): small-normal velocities have mass \(O(\eta^2)\), and the large-speed part has a Gaussian tail. Uniform conditional bounds at successive hits give \[\begin{aligned}
 I_k&:=\int\mathbf1_{\{t_k<\mathcal T/\varepsilon\}}
                       \prod_{j=1}^{k-1}d\sigma_j\\
 &\le\sum_{m=0}^{M}\binom{k-1}{m}a^m b^{k-1-m}
 \le(M+1)(ka)^M b^{k-1-M}. \end{aligned}\] The sum includes \(m=0\); zero good flights cannot be discarded. For \(k-1\ge16(M+1)\) and \(k=O(\mathcal T/\eta^3)\), this is at most \[(M+1)\bigl(C'\mathcal T\eta^{12}\bigr)^M.\] Choose \(\eta=c\mathcal T^{-1/12}\) with \(C'c^{12}\le1/4\). Since \(M+1\le2^M\) for \(M\ge1\), \[I_k\le2^{-M}\le2^{-c'\mathcal T^{5/4}}.\] Here \(M\asymp\mathcal T^{5/4}\), and a sufficiently large fixed \(C_1\) in \(k=\lceil C_1\mathcal T^{5/4}\rceil\) satisfies the required bounds for large \(\mathcal T\). Constants may depend on the domain and fixed Gaussian parameters, but not on \(\varepsilon,\mathcal T\). This proves the required scaling with the corrected set and inclusive counting sum.

## Core dependence, severity and boundaries

Proposition 9.1, p. 59, identifies this stochastic-cycle modification as part of its weighted \(L^\infty\) proof. Its bound enters (10.4) and the combined estimate (10.6), p. 61. The iteration (12.3), p. 68, invokes that a priori estimate for the remainder in Theorem 1.2.

The assessment is Major for the displayed derivation: a demonstrated invalid count lies on this core proof chain. Neither the tail bound, the existence theorem nor the ghost-effect limit is thereby proved false. The corrected intersection and argument above restore this specific geometric-probabilistic step; they do not certify all later analytic estimates. The likely typographical origin and the valid earlier construction are part of this assessment.

# Additional checks and limits

The Gaussian coefficient in (5.15), p. 30, survives an independent exact check. For \(V\sim N(0,TI_3)\), \[\mathbb E[V_i^2(|V|^2-5T)^2]=10T^3.\] Multiplication by \(\rho=P/T\) and \(2/(4T^2)\) gives \(5P\), as required. This validates that coefficient, not the entire energy inequality.

The small-data closure and its \(L^\infty\) dependence were traced. Selected apparent difficulties in the positivity discussion were not promoted to findings: a collision lower bound there must be interpreted on the compact velocity range used by the auxiliary cutoff. No global lower bound outside that range is certified. Unresolved normalization questions elsewhere were not used to claim a false theorem.
