Abstract: The inspected manuscript reverses the two positive shear maps when identifying its discrete map with the inverse time-one flow. An explicit point separates the maps for every even amplitude. Coordinate exchange supplies an exact conjugacy, including diffusion and arbitrary kicks, so the stated decay conclusions survive a bounded repair. A ball-scale comparison has the wrong inequality direction, and a continuous-time correlation step requires a Lipschitz approximation argument. These are local issues with verified corrections; no counterexample to the optimal logarithmic dissipation timescale or exponential mixing conclusion was established. The highest supported impact is Minor. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The inverse flow and the analyzed map

Problem 1 (The shear composition is reversed).  Page 2 defines \(u_\alpha\) as the negative vertical shear first and negative horizontal shear second. Page 7 sets \(T=\phi_1^{-1}\). Page 22 defines positive vertical and horizontal shears \(T_1,T_2\) but asserts \(T=T_2\circ T_1\). These definitions are inconsistent.

Write, modulo \(\mathbb Z^2\), \[V_+(x,y)=(x,y+\alpha|x-\tfrac12|),\qquad
 H_+(x,y)=(x+\alpha|y-\tfrac12|,y),\] and let \(V_-,H_-\) denote their inverses. Each half-period has duration \(1/2\), while the velocity coefficient is \(2\alpha\). Therefore \[\phi_1=H_-\circ V_-,\quad
 T_{\rm actual}=V_+\circ H_+,\quad T_{\rm printed}=H_+\circ V_+.\] For every even \(\alpha\ge2\), set \(z=(1/(2\alpha^2),0)\). Then \[T_{\rm actual}z=\left(\frac1{2\alpha^2},1-\frac1{2\alpha}\right),
 \qquad
 T_{\rm printed}z=\left(\frac12+\frac1{2\alpha^2},1-\frac1{2\alpha}\right)
 \pmod1.\] Thus one cannot retain both definitions as identical. The matrices on pages 7 and 23 concern the printed map, with a nearly horizontal unstable cone, rather than the actual inverse map.

## Verified repair and impact

For \(S(x,y)=(y,x)\), one has \(T_{\rm actual}=S T_{\rm printed}S\). The identity extends to arbitrary kicked words: \[(T_{\rm actual})_\xi^n=S(T_{\rm printed})_{S\xi}^nS,
 \qquad S\xi=(S\xi_1,S\xi_2,\ldots).\] The map \(S\) preserves grids, Lebesgue measure and \(L^p\) norms. The pullback \(U_Sf=f\circ S\) commutes with heat, giving \[\Phi_{\nu,{\rm actual}}=U_S\Phi_{\nu,{\rm printed}}U_S.\] Thus Sections 3–4 apply to the printed map and their estimates transfer to the actual map before using Lemma 2.4.

Equivalently, the velocity \(w_\alpha(t,z)=S u_\alpha(t,Sz)\) has flow \(S\phi_tS\) and printed inverse time-one map. Its advection–diffusion evolution is unitarily conjugate to that of \(u_\alpha\), preserving diffusion, the Lipschitz bound, mean-zero data and decay constants. Correlations transfer in the same way. This is a complete bounded repair; the supported impact is Minor. The counterexample refutes the identification, not the main rates.

# Two local comparison issues

Problem 2 (Remark 2.3 reverses the ball-radius inequality).  Remark 2.3, page 19, claims that dyadic mixing at scale \(h=2^{-N}\) implies (39) for every \(0<\epsilon\le Ch\), with a fixed tolerance strictly below one. This is false.

Take \(f_m(x,y)=\sin(2\pi2^m x)\), \(\kappa'=1/2\), \(m\ge0\). At level \(N=m\), every dyadic square average is zero. At level \(m+1\), some averages have magnitude \(2/\pi>1/2\). The largest absolute dyadic conditional average cannot decrease under refinement, by averaging children to recover a parent. Hence \(\operatorname{mix}_{1/2}(f_m)=2^{-m}\) exactly. At a point where \(f_m=1\), continuity gives \[\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac1{m(B_\epsilon(z))}\int_{B_\epsilon(z)}f_m\,dm=1.\] For any \(\kappa<1\) and \(C>0\), a sufficiently small \(\epsilon\le C2^{-m}\) contradicts (39).

The correct comparison uses a radius larger than the grid scale by a fixed factor. If each \(h\)-square average is bounded by \(\kappa'\|f\|_\infty\), squares wholly inside a ball of radius \(r\ge4\sqrt2h\), below the injectivity radius, occupy at least half its area: their union contains \(B_{r-\sqrt2h}\). The remaining boundary strip contributes at most \(\|f\|_\infty\), so \[\left|\frac1{m(B_r)}\int_{B_r}f\,dm\right|\le\frac{1+\kappa'}2\|f\|_\infty.\] For coarser scales, the whole torus and zero mean provide the comparison after an absolute adjustment of the constant. This suffices to transfer exponential decay of the dyadic scale to the usual ball-based scale. Theorem 4’s dyadic statement and the dissipation proof do not require the false all-smaller-radii assertion. Impact: Minor.

Problem 3 (A Lipschitz flow need not preserve \(C^1\)).  Theorem 2’s proof, page 22, inserts \(g\circ\phi_s\) into a \(C^1\) correlation estimate using only a Lipschitz bound for \(\phi_s\). The composition need not be \(C^1\).

For \(0<s<1/2\), \(\phi_s(x,y)=(x,y-2\alpha s|x-1/2|)\). Taking \(g(x,y)=\sin(2\pi y)\), the one-sided \(x\)-derivatives of the composition at \((1/2,0)\) are opposite nonzero numbers.

To repair the step, mollify a Lipschitz observable periodically. The approximants converge uniformly, with \(C^1\) norms bounded by its supremum norm plus Lipschitz constant. Apply Lemma 2.7 and pass to the limit in the correlation integral. Its bound then holds for Lipschitz observables. Since \(\|g\circ\phi_s\|_{\rm Lip}\le C_\alpha\|g\|_{C^1}\) uniformly on \(0\le s\le1\), the stated continuous-time exponential estimate follows. Impact: Minor, with verified repair.

# Substantive estimates and boundary checks

## Minorization and energy comparison

The one-step density is \(G_\nu(z_0-T^{-1}z)\), with gradient bound \(C_\alpha\nu^{-3/2}\). A dyadic approximation of mesh at most \(\nu^2\) has error \(O(C_\alpha\sqrt\nu)\). Lemma 2.1 supplies cell mass at least \(\lambda/2\) times area after \(O(|\log\nu|)\) steps. The final heat step on a square of side \(2^{-N+1}\), \(2^{-N}\le\sqrt\nu<2^{-N+1}\), gives density at least \(\lambda/(32\pi e^2)\). To keep the approximation threshold independent of \(\alpha\), condition instead on the first Gaussian before applying \(T\); its gradient bound is amplitude-independent and the subsequent maps form an allowed kicked word.

Doeblin contraction gives \(L^\infty\) decay. The \(L^2\) step uses both kernel normalization and Lebesgue invariance: \[\|\Phi_\nu^nf\|_2^2\le
 2\|f\|_2^2\sup_z\int|p_n(z,z')-1|\,dz'.\] Reversibility is not needed. The comparison with continuous diffusion is \[\|F_d(2n)-f(n)\|_2^2\le
 C\epsilon\nu\int_0^{2n}\|\nabla F_d\|_2^2+
 C\epsilon^{-1}\nu\int_0^n\|\nabla f\|_2^2.\] Heat dissipation controls each subsequent transport interval, proving the global gradient integral of Lemma 2.6. Small actual dissipation and \(\epsilon=\delta\) would make the two solutions too close to permit the pulsed factor \(1/2\), a contradiction. For general \(L^2\) data, the first unit interval either dissipates a fixed fraction or ensures the scaled gradient condition for \(f(1)\). One iterates at intervals \(n_0+1\), using monotonicity between them. The omitted square on a gradient norm on page 18 and the occurrence of \(n_0\) for \(n_0+1\) on page 15 do not alter this argument.

## Geometry and Jacobians

Branch determinants are one. The forward/backward cone bounds prevent cancellation for arbitrary matrix products and translations. The never-long ancestry term is bounded using \(4^n/(\delta_1\alpha^2)^n\). The application to \(I_k\) on page 28 requires \(\alpha^{-3k/2}\), not \(\alpha^{-3(n-k)/2}\); the final finite sum is unchanged by reindexing. The final segment enlargement loses \(O(1/\alpha)\) of preimage length.

Equation (47) gives ordinary slopes \(dy/dx=\pm\alpha\). The \(\pm1/\alpha\) on page 24 are their reciprocal slopes \(dx/dy\). The actual singularity equations give the required at-most-four component bound for a short unstable segment. In the area formula, the application uses nonnegative indicators and an almost everywhere positive Jacobian.

Appendix A can be checked exactly. Write \[A=D T_\xi^n(T_\xi^{-n}z)=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad B=D T_{\theta^n\xi}^n(z)=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\g&h\end{pmatrix}.\] Since \(\det A=\det B=1\), \[J_F=|ae+cf|,\qquad J_uJ_s=\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\sqrt{e^2+f^2}.\] The cone bounds give \(|r|,|s|\le(\alpha\delta_1)^{-1}\) for \(r=c/a\), \(s=f/e\). Thus \[\frac{J_uJ_s}{J_F}
 =\frac{\sqrt{(1+r^2)(1+s^2)}}{1+rs}
 =\sqrt{1+\frac{(r-s)^2}{(1+rs)^2}}
 =1+O(\alpha^{-2})\] uniformly in \(n,\xi\). This strengthens (56) and avoids the unrestricted wording of (57), whose use requires \(|b/a-1|<1\). That restriction is available in its large-\(\alpha\) applications. The safe constant \(4/25\), from \(|W|,|V|\le5/2\), may replace \(4/20\) on page 31 without affecting any required positive lower bound.

For odd iterates, use half-lengths \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) and \(\lceil n/2\rceil\). The two complexity bounds and the same matrix calculation hold with unequal lengths. Uniformity in large \(\alpha\) is recovered by retaining the actual ancestry factor \[\frac{[4/(\delta_1\alpha^2)]^m}{8\alpha|W|}.\] It is enough that \([4/(\delta_1\alpha^2)]^m\le4|W|\). Subdividing an initially long segment into lengths comparable to \(\alpha^{-1}\) costs only boundedly many iterates; for length \(2^{-N}\), a uniform \(O(N)\) bound suffices. These completions address parity and amplitude dependence without claiming a counterexample to Lemma 2.1.

Remark 1.4 also needs the second-half time shift: use a smooth nonnegative profile on \([0,1/2]\), compactly supported in its interior, with integral \(\alpha/2\); place it in the vertical half-period and its translate in the horizontal half-period. Since \(H_1,V_1\) have coefficient \(2\), this preserves the original integrated shear. Integral \(\alpha\) instead yields effective amplitude \(2\alpha\), which still gives the asserted existence of a smooth-in-time example. The printed formula does not literally preserve the parameter without this normalization.

The continuous-time step for Theorem 4 also requires attention to order: \(\phi_{n+s}=\phi_s\circ\phi_1^n\), so the partial-period composition cannot in general be moved to the initial observable. Instead, for a dyadic square \(R\) of side \(L\), its preimage under \(\phi_s\) has area \(L^2\) and boundary length at most \(4L\operatorname{Lip}(\phi_s^{-1})\). The part not covered by complete \(h\)-grid cells has area at most \(C L\operatorname{Lip}(\phi_s^{-1})h+Ch^2\). Taking \(L\) a sufficiently large fixed multiple of \(h\), uniformly for \(s\in[0,1]\), leaves at least half the area in complete cells. The tolerance becomes \((1+\kappa)/2<1\), preserving exponential geometric mixing with adjusted constants. This supplies the omitted composition-order argument.

## Correlation decay and optimality

For a step function on a square grid, the geometric lower bound gives a doubly stochastic matrix with a fixed positive constant component. The remaining part is an \(L^2\) contraction, proving (44). Approximation yields ergodicity of every power.

For [DL], the required piecewise smooth branches and finite singularity curves are present; the images of the singularities are transverse to the stable cone. Expansion dominates the bounded one-step intersection count, with a sufficiently small exponent in condition (P1). Its Theorem 2.8(5) gives exponential correlations from total ergodicity. Lebesgue measure is invariant here. This verifies the use of that external theorem, not its entire proof.

The lower-bound scaling is also consistent: for fixed smooth data, \(\|\nabla f(t)\|_2^2\le e^{Ct}\|\nabla f(0)\|_2^2\). The energy identity then prevents a fixed fractional loss before a constant times \(|\log\nu|\). No faster uniform rate is inferred.
