Abstract: This review concerns the assigned 65-page author version. Six localized problems are established: an infinite-rank extension missing geometric divided-power convergence, a geometric face-map subscript, a cohomological normalization cluster, a finite-orbit definition, an arithmetic Higgs commutator sign, and a missing factor in a Sen derivative. Explicit examples distinguish these defects from the finite-rank principal correspondence. The finite-rank coefficient algebra, semilinear cocycle, Sen normalization and gluing mechanism have the positive checks recorded below. The assessment is Minor revision within the stated scope; this is neither a whole-proof certification nor an independent acceptance. Scientific adjudication and human review remain pending.

# Established problems

Problem 1 (Minor: the quasi-coherent extension lacks geometric convergence).  Location. Remark 4.6, pp. 29–30 (author TeX line 2254); required coefficient convergence on p. 35, (4.13) on p. 36, and (4.19)/Lemma 4.22 on p. 39.

The remark extends the target to \(p\)-complete modules with an integrable Higgs field and an arithmetic operator satisfying Definition 4.1(2), and asserts the same equivalence for quasi-coherent crystals. The finite-rank implication of geometric nilpotence is unavailable in this extension. We use the natural intended integral \(R^+\)-module meaning of the remark; its incidental notation \(R\)-modules is not the basis of this finding.

Set \(E(u)=u-p\), \(a=1\), \(R^+=\mathbb Z_p\langle T^{\pm1}\rangle\), and \[H=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{n\ge0}R^+e_n,\qquad
 \theta_0e_n=e_{n+1},\qquad \phi e_n=-ne_n,\qquad
 \theta=\theta_0\otimes\omega,\quad \omega=\frac{d\log T}{E(u)}.
 \tag{2.1}\] Thus \(H\) is the module of sequences tending \(p\)-adically to zero. It is separated, complete and \(p\)-torsion-free. Both operators are continuous, preserve \(p^rH\), and have norm at most one. Integrability is automatic in one variable, and \[[\phi,\theta_0]=-\theta_0,\qquad
 P_m(\phi)e_n=(-1)^m m!\binom nm e_n,\qquad
 P_m(\phi)H\subseteq p^{v_p(m!)}H.
 \tag{2.2}\] The arithmetic smallness holds uniformly as an operator. Nevertheless, \(\theta_0^m e_0=e_m\notin pH\) for every \(m\).

The coefficient recurrence of any putative PD stratification forces \(\phi_{0,m}=\theta_0^m\). After setting the arithmetic coordinate \(X=0\), it would give \[\varepsilon(e_0)=\sum_{m\ge0}e_mY^{[m]}.
 \tag{2.3}\] This is not an element of \(H\widehat\otimes_{R^+}R^+\{Y\}_{\mathrm{pd}}^\wedge\): modulo \(p\), that tensor product consists of finite sums in the basis \(e_nY^{[m]}\), whereas (2.3) has infinitely many coefficient-one terms. Thus the enlarged target contains an object that cannot arise from the required crystal.

Impact and repair boundary. The additional infinite-rank equivalence is false under the listed conditions. Remove or restrict the remark, or supply a topology-appropriate condition ensuring convergence of the full coefficients \(\theta^{\mathbf n}P_k(\phi)(x)\) as \(k+|\mathbf n|\to\infty\), together with the continuity needed for completed descent. No unrestricted quasi-coherent theorem is proved merely by proposing this necessary condition. This example does not refute the finite-rank main theorem: finite rank supplies the nilpotence used to reduce full convergence to (1.1). The limited supplementary scope supports the Minor classification.

Problem 2 (Minor: the geometric zeroth face has the wrong subscript).  Location. Proposition 4.13(1), p. 32, (4.2); the derivation on p. 33; author TeX lines 2326 and 2401. Compare the correct absolute formula (4.4), p. 33, and its use on p. 43.

The printed geometric formula is \(p_0(Y_{s,j})=Y_{s,j+1}-Y_{s,j}\). From the definition \[Y_{s,j}=\frac{T_{s,0}-T_{s,j}}{E(u_0)T_{s,0}}\] and the shift of tensor-factor indices under \(p_0\), the geometric reduction instead gives \[p_0(Y_{s,j})=Y_{s,j+1}-Y_{s,1}.                 \tag{2.4}\] There is a direct consistency test. Suppressing \(s\), the printed maps give \[p_0p_0(Y_1)=Y_3-2Y_2+Y_1,\qquad
 p_1p_0(Y_1)=Y_3-Y_2.
 \tag{2.5}\] Their difference \(Y_1-Y_2\) is nonzero in the free completed PD algebra, so the required cosimplicial identity fails.

Impact and repair boundary. Replace the final subscript \(j\) by \(1\) in (4.2) and the corresponding derivation. The adjacent ratio on p. 33 should be \(T_{s,0}/T_{s,1}=(1-E(u_0)Y_{s,1})^{-1}\); its reduction modulo \(E\) is still one. These are one local correction group. The correct absolute formula and subsequent calculations already use the reference coordinate \(Y_{s,1}\), providing counterevidence to a broader failure of the intended classification.

Problem 3 (Minor: incompatible normalizations in the cohomological construction).  Location. Construction 4.30, p. 41; its chain-map proof, p. 42; proof of Lemma 4.32, p. 43; compare Example 4.25, p. 40, and Construction 4.35, pp. 44–45. TeX anchors are 2780, 2787, 2813 and 2871–2872.

Differential base. The left-hand differential module is declared relative to \(\mathcal O_K\), but the displayed right-hand denominator is \(R^+\{X_i\}_{\mathrm{pd}}^\wedge\), which removes the base differentials. At \(m=n=0,q=1\) on the torus, the claimed identification becomes \[\widehat\Omega^1_{R^+/\mathcal O_K}\simeq\widehat\Omega^1_{R^+/R^+}=0,
 \tag{2.6}\] although the left side contains \(d\log T\). The base forms must be retained; the natural intended relative base on the right is \(\mathcal O_K\{X_i\}_{\mathrm{pd}}^\wedge\), with the compatible completed PD differential convention.

Breuil–Kisin basis. Construction 4.30 prints \(p_0^1(E(u))=(1-aX_1)^{-1}E(u)\), whereas the preceding example and following proof use the direct factor. The discrepancy breaks the asserted chain-map property, not merely a cosmetic convention. On the same torus with \(a=1\), take \[H=R^+e_0\oplus R^+e_1,\quad
 \phi=\operatorname{diag}(0,-1),\quad
 \theta_0e_0=e_1,\quad\theta_0e_1=0.
 \tag{2.7}\] All enhanced hypotheses hold: \(\theta_0^2=0\), \([\phi,\theta_0]=-\theta_0\), and \(P_2(\phi)=0\). Writing \(b=1-X_1\), the action \(b^{-\phi}\) fixes \(e_0\) and sends \(e_1\) to \(be_1\). The printed inverse action on \(E(u)\) sends \(\omega=d\log T/E(u)\) to \(b\omega\), so \[p_0^1d(e_0)=b^2e_1\omega,\qquad
 dp_0^1(e_0)=e_1\omega.                            \tag{2.8}\] The difference is nonzero. The corrected action \(E(u)\mapsto bE(u)\) sends \(\omega\mapsto b^{-1}\omega\) and restores equality.

Positive-index faces. In the \(k\ge1\) calculation on p. 43, the extra factor \(\exp(\sum_l\theta_lY_{l,1})\) conflicts with the identity action on \(H\) assigned to both positive-index faces. For (2.7), applying it to \(e_0\) gives \(e_0+Y_{1,1}e_1\), instead of \(e_0\). Remove it from both copied intermediate lines; the zeroth face legitimately retains it.

Impact and repair boundary. Align the differential base, direct \(E\)-factor and positive faces throughout this local construction. The surrounding proof already uses the corrected \(E\)-factor, so these tests do not establish that the cohomology comparison theorem is false. The cited PD homotopy and descent inputs still require their own hypotheses; this review does not claim to reconstruct those theories from the local corrections alone.

Problem 4 (Minor: finite orbit is not the Sen finite-vector condition).  Location. Theorem 7.4, its preceding prose and proof, and Theorem 7.5, p. 57; TeX lines 3462, 3468 and 3476.

The text defines \(W^{\mathrm{fin}}\) by requiring the orbit to be a finite set, then asserts \(W^{\mathrm{fin}}=W^{\mathrm{la}}\). Take the admissible rank-one semilinear representation at a point \[R=K=\mathbb Q_p,\quad W=\widehat K_{\mathrm{cyc}}e,\qquad
 g(ce)=g(c)\chi(g)e.
 \tag{2.9}\] The vector \(e\) is locally analytic because the cyclotomic character is analytic on a sufficiently small open subgroup. Its orbit \(\{\chi(g)e\}\) is infinite. Hence \(e\in W^{\mathrm{la}}\setminus W^{\mathrm{fin}}\) under the printed definition. No assertion that \(W^{\mathrm{fin}}\) is zero is needed.

Impact and repair boundary. The equality, the proof’s choice of a finite-vector basis, and the subsequent definition of \(D_{\mathrm{Sen},K_{\mathrm{cyc}}}\) are incompatible with the literal finite-set definition. Replace that definition consistently by the actual Sen decompleted module and justify its equality with locally analytic vectors. At a point the usual finiteness concerns the finite-dimensional \(K\)-span of an orbit. In the relative case the appropriate finite \(R\)-module span or finite-level decompletion must be stated precisely using Proposition 7.2. The example does not contradict the corrected decompletion theorem.

Problem 5 (Minor: the arithmetic Higgs commutator has the opposite sign).  Location. Definitions 7.15 and 7.16, p. 61; Construction 7.23 and Theorem 7.24, pp. 62–63; TeX 3644 versus 3647 and 3662 versus 3665. Compare Definition 6.2, pp. 48–49, and Theorem 7.13, p. 60.

Both arithmetic definitions print \([\Phi,\Theta]=-\Theta\), but their claimed equivalent first square says \[(\Phi-1)\Theta=\Theta\Phi,\quad\text{that is}\quad
 [\Phi,\Theta]=+\Theta.                           \tag{2.10}\] The explicit functor resolves which sign agrees with the rest of the paper. Set \(b=(\zeta_p-1)\lambda\). Construction 7.23 assigns \[\Phi=-\phi_H/a,\qquad \Theta=-b\theta_H.\] Using the enhanced relation \([\phi_H,\theta_H]=-a\theta_H\), one obtains \[[\Phi,\Theta]=(b/a)[\phi_H,\theta_H]
             =-b\theta_H=\Theta.                 \tag{2.11}\] The rank-two object (2.7) satisfies every enhanced condition. Its image has \(\Phi=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) and nonzero \(\Theta=-bE_{21}\omega\), so it fails the printed negative relation in characteristic zero.

Impact and repair boundary. Change both displayed commutator conditions to \(+\Theta\), preserving the existing \(\Phi-j\) diagrams and the functor normalization. This is also the Sen shift associated with the Tate \((-1)\) twist. As printed the functor does not land in its declared target; the explicit compatible repair is local, and the example does not refute the correctly signed correspondence.

Problem 6 (Minor: the derivative of the analytic coordinate omits a factor).  Location. Construction 7.7 and the proof of Proposition 7.9(2), p. 58; TeX lines 3502 and 3533. Compare the Lie operators on p. 59.

The actual cocycle is \(g(\alpha)=\chi(g)^{-1}(\alpha+c(g))\). For the cyclotomic direction fixing \(K_\infty\), \(c(g)=0\), whence \[\lim_{g\to1}\frac{g(\alpha)-\alpha}{\chi(g)-1}
 =\lim_{g\to1}-\frac{\alpha}{\chi(g)}=-\alpha.
 \tag{2.12}\] The printed value is \(-1\). Since \(\alpha_m\) is algebraic at finite level, its infinitesimal derivative is zero, so \(\nabla_\gamma(\alpha-\alpha_m)=-\alpha\) as well.

Impact and repair boundary. Correct the derivative and the following constant-coefficient explanation. On the local analytic coordinate expansion, \(\nabla_\gamma=-\alpha\nabla_\tau\), not \(-\nabla_\tau\). The scalar \(\alpha\) is nonzero: \(\tau(\alpha)=\alpha+1\) rules out zero. It is therefore invertible in the coefficient field and locally analytically invertible after restricting the analytic neighborhood. The two derivatives have the same kernel, and characteristic-zero differentiation still yields the required constant coefficients. This defect does not refute Proposition 7.9’s kernel conclusion.

# Positive checks, dependencies and limits

## Finite-rank local classification

For finite rank in characteristic zero, \([\phi,\theta_i^m]=-ma\theta_i^m\) implies \(\operatorname{tr}(\theta_i^m)=0\). Newton identities and Cayley–Hamilton, or the paper’s Lie-theoretic argument, give nilpotence. Localizing in fraction fields and using projectivity over the reduced smooth algebra descends the identity. Commuting nilpotents are jointly nilpotent. This identifies exactly the finite-rank step absent from Problem 1.

The recurrence on pp. 35–36 forces the ordered coefficients \[\phi_{k,\mathbf n}=\theta^{\mathbf n}P_k(\phi)
                 =P_k(\phi+|\mathbf n|a)\theta^{\mathbf n}.
 \tag{3.1}\] Only finitely many geometric multi-indices survive in finite rank, reducing full convergence to \(P_k(\phi)\to0\). Independently, put \(S(X)=(1-aX)^{-\phi/a}\), interpreted by its integral PD series, and \(U(Y)=\exp(\sum_i\theta_iY_i)\). Then \[S(X_1)\theta_iS(X_1)^{-1}=(1-aX_1)\theta_i,\qquad
 (1-aX_1)\left(1-a\frac{X_2-X_1}{1-aX_1}\right)=1-aX_2.\] These identities verify the closed-form cocycle \[U(Y_1)S(X_1)
 U\!\left(\frac{Y_2-Y_1}{1-aX_1}\right)
 S\!\left(\frac{X_2-X_1}{1-aX_1}\right)=U(Y_2)S(X_2).
 \tag{3.2}\] The geometric exponential is finite and the arithmetic series converges by the hypothesis. This checks the intended absolute stratification without claiming an independent line-by-line reconstruction of every printed coefficient elimination.

The enhanced Higgs complex correctly uses \(\phi+qa\) in degree \(q\). The notation \(F(X,Z)=((1-aX)^{-Z/a}-1)/Z\) in Construction 4.35 denotes a series with polynomial coefficients; it does not require \(Z\) invertible. Its shifted coefficients converge by the rising-factorial Vandermonde identity and \(P_m((q+1)a)=a^m m!\binom{q+m}{m}\to0\). At relative dimension zero the intended cohomology complex is \([H\xrightarrow{\phi}H]\).

## Arithmetic action, Sen normalization and gluing

With \(b=(\zeta_p-1)\lambda\), write \(q_g=1+\pi ab\,c(g)\). Equation (6.1) gives \(g(b)=\chi(g)b/q_g\). Since \(c(gh)=c(g)+\chi(g)c(h)\), direct multiplication yields \[q_g\,g(q_h)=q_{gh}.                              \tag{3.3}\] Conjugation by \(q_g^{-\phi/a}\) multiplies \(\theta\) by \(q_g\); the scalar transformation of \(b\) and the Tate \((-1)\) factor cancel this. Treating \(b\) as Galois-fixed would incorrectly suggest a defect. Differentiating the actual Kummer action gives \(\nabla_\tau|_H=-\pi b\phi_H\), in agreement with pp. 51 and 60.

The remaining constant in Theorem 7.13 can also be checked directly. Write \(\widetilde\lambda=E(u)P(u)/E(0)\) near \(u=\pi\), where \(P\) is a unit, and \(\mathfrak t=t/(p\widetilde\lambda)\). The logarithmic derivative of \(P\) contributes zero after applying \(\theta\), since \(\theta(t)=0\). Thus \[p\,\theta(u\widetilde\lambda'\mathfrak t)
 =\pi a\,\theta(t/E(u))
 =\pi a(\zeta_p-1)\lambda.                        \tag{3.4}\] Here \(\theta(t/\xi)=\zeta_p-1\) and \(\lambda=\theta(\xi/E(u))\). The normalized Sen operator is therefore \(-\phi_H/a\). This verifies the sign and scalar, while retaining the imported period-ring existence and analytic-vector results as dependencies.

Proposition 6.11 invokes the later Sen calculation, but the examined proof of Theorems 7.12–7.13 uses the earlier explicit action, rather than the desired full faithfulness. No circular use was found in this dependency check. Full faithfulness then uniquely lifts the identity of the common Galois–Higgs realization between two framed constructions. Uniqueness supplies the triple-overlap cocycle before the étale stack descent on p. 55.
