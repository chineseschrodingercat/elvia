Abstract: The reviewed accepted-author version contains an inverted Gaussian target in its Wasserstein theorem, with an admissible counterexample to the printed rate. Three separate proof problems concern missing centering, a block normalization, and a uniform dependence hypothesis that fails at allowed bandwidths. Three local issues concern the denominator definition, finite-window dependence, and a constant quantified uniformly over all \(q\). These findings have different repair boundaries. They do not establish that the intended, correctly rescaled central limit theorems are false. The assessment is Major; scientific adjudication and human review remain pending.

# Established problems

Problem 1 (Major: the linearized sums are not centered).  Location. Definitions (4.7)–(4.8), p. 18; (4.16)–(4.20), pp. 22–23; mean-zero hypothesis of Lemma 4.5, pp. 20–21; Proposition 4.10, p. 29; smoothed definitions and moments (4.46), (4.48)–(4.51), pp. 33–34.

Evidence. With the source’s initial-boundary convention, let \[B_{kb}=\sum_{h=1}^bX_{k-h},\quad
Y_k(x)=X_k-\frac{xX_k^2}{2\sqrt n\,\sigma_{nb}}
             -\frac{xX_kB_{kb}}{\sqrt n\,\sigma_{nb}},\quad
R_n(x)=\sum_{k=1}^nY_k(x).\] Here \(\sigma_{nb}^2=\mathbb E\widetilde\sigma_{nb}^{\,2}\); \(R_n(x)\) is the source’s \(S_n(x)\). Its mean is exactly \[\mathbb ER_n(x)=-\frac{x\sqrt n\,\sigma_{nb}}2 .\] Consequently the linearized statistic in (4.16) equals \[\frac{S_n}{\sqrt n\,\sigma_{nb}}
-\frac{x(\widetilde\sigma_{nb}^{\,2}-\sigma_{nb}^2)}{2\sigma_{nb}^2}
=\frac{R_n(x)-\mathbb ER_n(x)}{\sqrt n\,\sigma_{nb}},                    \tag{2.1}\] whereas the subsequent argument uses the uncentered \(R_n(x)\).

An exact all-assumptions check is \(X_k\) iid \(N(0,1)\), with any admissible growing bandwidth. Then \(\sigma_{nb}=1\). For \(W_b=(\mathbf1_{\{|i-j|\le b\}})_{i,j=1}^n\), \(R_n(x)=S_n-xX^\top W_bX/(2\sqrt n)\). Odd Gaussian moments vanish, \(\operatorname{tr}W_b=n\), and \(\operatorname{tr}W_b^2=n(2b+1)-b(b+1)\). Therefore \[\mathbb ER_n(x)^2
=n+\frac{x^2n}{4}+\frac{x^2(2b+1)}2-\frac{x^2b(b+1)}{2n}.          \tag{2.2}\] At \(x=1\), the extra \(n/4\) contradicts (4.18)’s \(O((1+x^2)\sqrt n)\) error. In fact \(X^\top W_bX/n\to1\) in \(L^2\), so \(R_n(1)/\sqrt n\Rightarrow N(-1/2,1)\). In (4.20) at \(x=1,\mu=0\), the probability tends to \(\Phi(3/2)\), whereas the displayed approximant tends to \(\Phi(2/\sqrt5)\). Their difference is nonzero.

Impact and repair. Mean-zero Lemma 4.5 is not applicable to the defined variables. The same omission enters the smoothed raw moments: a mean-zero coboundary does not remove this mean. Define centered variables and sums, replace raw moments by variances and central cumulants, and carry these changes through the event comparisons. Proposition 4.10 already estimates \(\mathbb E[(Y_k-\mathbb EY_k)(Y_0-\mathbb EY_0)]\), which supports a possible correction but cannot justify the present raw-moment calculation.

There is also an explicit boundary obligation: setting \(X_{k-h}=0\) for \(k-h\le0\) (p. 17) makes the defined \(Y_k\) nonstationary. Section 4.3 removes this convention only within that section. A repair must control the initial terms or use a suitable nonstationary approximation theorem. A subsidiary variance remainder immediately before (4.18) should be \(O(1)\), not generally \(O(n^{2-a})\): for the variance-one Gaussian AR(1) with covariance \(2^{-|h|}\), \(\mathbb ES_n^2=3n-4+4\,2^{-n}\). This smaller correction fits the subsequent \(O(\sqrt n)\) tolerance. These are failures of the written proof, not a counterexample to the intended centered CLT.

Problem 2 (Major: the concentration proof drops a block factor).  Location. Definition of \(W_k\) and Lemma 4.8, p. 26; blocks (4.30)–(4.31), p. 27; final paragraph of Lemma 4.9 and its use in Proposition 4.6, p. 29.

Evidence. The source sets \[W_k=2b^{-1/2}X_kB_{kb},\qquad
V_{\ell b}=\sum_{k\in I_{\ell b}}(b^{-1/2}X_k^2+W_k).\] On p. 29 it concludes from Lemma 4.8 and the block cardinality \(b\) that \(\left\|\sum_{k\in I_{\ell b}}(W_k-\mathbb EW_k)\right\|_q\lesssim1\). For iid standard Gaussian observations, choose the interior block \(I_{2b}=\{b+1,\ldots,2b\}\), so no initial zero boundary enters. These \(W_k\) are martingale differences, with \(\mathbb EW_k^2=4\). Thus \[\left\|\sum_{k\in I_{2b}}W_k\right\|_2=2\sqrt b,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(V_{2b})=2+4b.                                \tag{2.3}\] The second identity uses orthogonality of the diagonal and off-diagonal Gaussian quadratic monomials. Since \(L^q\) norms dominate \(L^2\) for \(q\ge2\), the claimed uniform block bound fails under the hypotheses used in the concentration proof.

Impact and repair boundary. This invalidates the written bounded-block step supplying the small-lag constants to the concentration argument. An additional normalization of the centered blocks by \(\sqrt b\), with consistent changes to the sum identity and tail thresholds, is a possible repair that must be carried out. The target \(\sqrt{b/n}\) fluctuation scale in Proposition 4.6 is not disproved by (2.3).

The coupling at the artificial sample origin must not be confused with a stationary block coupling. Under the literal zero-boundary convention, changing the innovation block \(\zeta_0\) does not affect iid positive-index blocks. We do not report a nonzero norm for that literal coupling. The decisive contradiction is the actually printed block-sum bound (2.3); restoring stationarity would require separate uniform relative-lag bounds.

Problem 3 (Major: the uniform Edgeworth hypothesis fails for allowed bandwidths).  Location. Lemma 4.2, pp. 19–20; Lemma 4.12(ii), p. 34; its application in the central region of the Wasserstein proof, p. 35; bandwidth assumption (A3), p. 6.

Evidence and quantifiers. Lemma 4.12 requires an exponent \(d>5/2\) and a constant independent of \(n\) bounding \[\sum_{k=1}^n k^d\sup_{\ell\ge k}\left\|Y_\ell-Y_\ell'\right\|_q .
                                                                    \tag{2.4}\] The assertion that \(a>4\) and Lemma 4.2 verify this misses the latter lemma’s explicit factor \(1+n^{-1/2}|x|b^{d+1}\).

This is a real obstruction, not just a loose upper estimate. Grant the favorable repairs of centering and stationarity from Problem 1, take iid standard Gaussian \(X_k=\varepsilon_k\), and fix \(x=1\). Replacing \(\varepsilon_0\) gives, for \(1\le\ell\le b\), \[Y_\ell^c-(Y_\ell^c)'=-n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_\ell
                        (\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon_0'),\] and gives zero for \(\ell>b\). Hence (2.4) equals a positive constant times \[n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^b k^d\asymp n^{-1/2}b^{d+1}.\] At \(b=\lfloor(n/\log^3n)^{1/4}\rfloor\), this diverges as \[n^{(d-1)/4}(\log n)^{-3(d+1)/4}\] for every \(d>5/2\). The original process satisfies the assumptions with, for example, \(p=8,a=5\). The independent smoothing coboundary only changes lag one; lags \(2,\ldots,b\) retain this obstruction. Under the literal initial zero convention the sequence is nonstationary, so that convention cannot instead validate the stationary lemma.

Impact and repair boundary. The claimed uniform Edgeworth input for Theorem 2.6 has not been established over the stated bandwidth range. It needs a theorem adapted to the actual triangular array or block structure, or a narrower bandwidth range with the bias tradeoff rechecked. This particular sufficient condition already requires \(b^{d+1}=O(\sqrt n)\) for fixed \(x\), substantially below the allowed \(n^{1/4}\) scale. Enlarging the original decay exponent \(a\) does not remove the iid obstruction. The calculation does not disprove the correctly targeted Wasserstein theorem. In contrast, the Kolmogorov lemma admits \(2/3<d<\min(1,a-3/2)\), so this obstruction should not be transferred to that weaker requirement.

Problem 4 (Major: the stated Wasserstein target has the reciprocal scale).  Location. Theorem 2.6, p. 9; the law notation and CDF identity in (4.53), p. 34; compare Theorem 2.3 and its variance explanation, p. 7.

Printed discrepancy. The named law is \(\mathcal L(G\sigma_b/\sigma)\), but the proof integrates against \(\Phi(x\sigma_b/\sigma)\), which is the CDF of \(\mathcal L(G\sigma/\sigma_b)\). The discrepancy can be much larger than \(n^{-1/2}\).

Admissible counterexample. Take the stationary variance-one Gaussian process \[X_k=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j}\varepsilon_{k-j},
\qquad \varepsilon_j\ {\rm iid}\ N(0,1).\] All moment and weighted dependence assumptions hold with \(p=8,a=5\); \[\gamma(h)=2^{-|h|},\quad \sigma^2=3,\quad
c_b=3-2\cdot2^{-b}.\] Choose \(b=\lfloor\log\log n\rfloor\) for sufficiently large \(n\), using natural logarithms. It satisfies (1.1). To give the statement the benefit of an explicit real denominator, adopt the favorable repair \[R_n=\sqrt{Q_n\vee\tau_n^{-2}},\qquad T_n=S_n/(\sqrt n\,R_n).\] The following argument is independent of the disputed proof. Write \(P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/n\), so \(Q_n=n^{-1}X^\top PW_bPX\). The covariance matrix \(\Gamma\) has \(\left\|\Gamma\right\|_{\rm op}\le3\), and the Gaussian quadratic-form formula gives \[\operatorname{Var}(Q_n)
=\frac2{n^2}\left\|\Gamma^{1/2}PW_bP\Gamma^{1/2}\right\|_F^2
\le\frac{18(2b+1)}n .\] The uncentered boundary bias is \(O(n^{-1})\); removing the sample mean changes the expectation by \(O(b/n)\), by the fixed-rank projection and \(\left\|W_b\right\|_{\rm op}\le2b+1\). Thus \(\left\|Q_n-c_b\right\|_2=O(\sqrt{b/n})\).

Let \(A_n=S_n/\sqrt n\). It is exactly Gaussian with \[\operatorname{Var}(A_n)=3-\frac4n(1-2^{-n}).\] The function \(g_n(u)=(u\vee\tau_n^{-2})^{-1/2}\) is globally Lipschitz with constant at most \(\tau_n^3/2\), including negative \(u\). Eventually \(g_n(c_b)=1/\sigma_b\), and Cauchy–Schwarz, without any independence assumption on \(A_n,Q_n\), gives \[\mathbb E|A_n(g_n(Q_n)-1/\sigma_b)|
\le\left\|A_n\right\|_2\frac{\tau_n^3}2\left\|Q_n-c_b\right\|_2
=O(\tau_n^3\sqrt{b/n}).\] It follows by coupling and the exact normal law of \(A_n\) that \[W_1(\mathcal L(T_n),\mathcal L(G\sigma/\sigma_b))
=O(\tau_n^3\sqrt{b/n}+n^{-1})=o(2^{-b}).\] On the other hand, for positive normal scales the distance is exactly the scale difference times \(\mathbb E|G|\), so \[W_1(\mathcal L(G\sigma/\sigma_b),\mathcal L(G\sigma_b/\sigma))
=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\frac{3-c_b}{\sqrt3\sqrt{c_b}}
\asymp2^{-b}\asymp(\log n)^{-\log2}.\] The triangle inequality therefore gives a lower bound \(c\,2^{-b}\gg n^{-1/2}\) against the printed target.

Impact and repair boundary. This refutes the actual quantitative target in Theorem 2.6, even after a natural denominator completion. Reverse the ratio in the named Gaussian laws; the CDF direction used in the proof is already correct. The Major classification reflects a false principal theorem statement at its asserted rate, despite a short textual correction. It neither disproves the intended reciprocal version nor repairs Problems 1–3.

Problem 5 (Minor: the random denominator needs a real-valued completion).  Location. Rectangular lag sum (1.6), p. 3; the choice \(\omega=1\) and (2.6), pp. 6–7; truncation (2.11), p. 9; good-event positivity (4.12)–(4.15), pp. 21–22.

Evidence. Let the observations be iid with probabilities \[\mathbb P(X=-2)=\tfrac14,\quad \mathbb P(X=0)=\tfrac12,\quad \mathbb P(X=2)=\tfrac14.\] They are centered, have \(\sigma^2=2\), all moments, and zero positive-lag dependence coefficients. For even \(n\) and odd \(b<n-1\), the two alternating samples \(0,2,0,2,\ldots\) and \(2,0,2,0,\ldots\) have total probability \(2^{1-3n/2}>0\). On this event \[\begin{aligned}
&\bar X_n=1,\quad S_n=n,\quad
\widehat\gamma_X(h)=(-1)^h(1-h/n),\\
&Q_n=-1+(b+1)/n<0.
\end{aligned}\] An odd sequence \(b=2\lfloor\log\log n/2\rfloor+1\) is admissible eventually. Thus the square root is not a real random variable on a positive-probability event at arbitrarily large admissible \(n\). Applying a maximum to the already undefined \(\widehat\sigma_{nb}\) does not supply a definition.

Impact and repair boundary. Specify the statistic on \(\{Q_n\le0\}\). For Wasserstein distance, write the squared-estimator floor explicitly, \(R_n=\sqrt{Q_n\vee\tau_n^{-2}}\). For the Kolmogorov result, an explicit measurable real completion has distributional cost at most the exceptional-event probability. The bounds asserted in (4.13) are of the appropriate order if their concentration proof is repaired. This is a local definition issue, not evidence that a reasonable completion destroys the rates. Arbitrarily large completions would not automatically preserve a Wasserstein bound.

Problem 6 (Minor: the finite-window coupling bound omits shifted coordinates).  Location. Proposition 3.1, (3.1)–(3.3), p. 10; single-innovation coupling (2.2), p. 5.

Evidence. Use \(\mathbb Y=\mathbb R\), a window of length two, and \(f(u,v)=\operatorname{clip}(v,-1,1)\). On all of \(\mathbb R^2\), this satisfies (3.1) with \(\alpha=1,\beta=0,C_f=2\), since \(|f(x)-f(y)|\le2\min(d(x,y),1)\). Take \(Y_k=\varepsilon_k\) iid Rademacher and \(p=q=6\). Then (3.2) gives \(X_k=\varepsilon_{k-1}\). At lag \(\ell=1\), \[\left\|X_k-X_k^{(1,\prime)}\right\|_6=2^{5/6}>0,\qquad
d(Y_k,Y_k^{(1,\prime)})=0.\] Thus the displayed right side of (3.3) vanishes while its left side is positive. The notation (3.2) explicitly lists the window coordinates; it has not redefined \(Y_k\) to be the whole window.

Impact and repair boundary. Replace that right side by a bound on the coupled window or a finite sum of shifted dependence coefficients, including lag zero where needed. A fixed finite window preserves weighted summability after these shifts, so the example does not itself refute the later examples or main CLT. It disproves the stated pointwise auxiliary inequality.

Problem 7 (Minor: the constant in Corollary 2.7 cannot be uniform in the written form).  Location. Corollary 2.7, p. 9: one constant depending only on \(\Theta_{a,p},p,\sigma^2\), followed by all \(q\ge1\), in \(\int|F_n-\Phi(\,\cdot\,\sigma_b/\sigma)|^q\le Cn^{-q/2}\).

Evidence under those quantifiers. Take iid \(X_k=B_k-\pi_0\), where \(B_k\) is Bernoulli with \(\pi_0=1/100\), and \(p=8,a=5\). The assumptions hold and \(\sigma_b=\sigma\). Grant the positive finite denominator completion in Problem 5. For \(100\mid n\), \(T_n=0\) if and only if \(S_n=0\), so its distribution has an atom \[\alpha_n=\mathbb P\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,\pi_0)=n\pi_0\}
\sim[2\pi n\pi_0(1-\pi_0)]^{-1/2}.\] Any continuous comparison CDF therefore has \(\operatorname*{ess\,sup}|F_n-\Phi|\ge\alpha_n/2\). This follows from the two one-sided limits at the jump and right continuity, not from a measure-zero value. Consequently \[\liminf_{\substack{n\to\infty\\100\mid n}}
\sqrt n\,\left\|F_n-\Phi\right\|_\infty
\ge\frac1{2\sqrt{2\pi(0.01)(0.99)}}>2.\] For fixed \(n\), the positively truncated statistic is bounded, so \(F_n-\Phi\in L^1\cap L^\infty\) and its \(L^q\) norm tends to the essential supremum. Taking \(q\)-th roots of the printed bound and sending \(q\to\infty\) would instead force \(\left\|F_n-\Phi\right\|_\infty\le n^{-1/2}\), a contradiction for arbitrarily large eligible \(n\).

Impact and repair boundary. This is a defect in the literal common-constant, common-\(n\)-range formulation. If a \(q\)-dependent threshold \(N(q)\) was intended, that is a different quantification requiring specification. Natural repairs are \(C^q n^{-q/2}\), a \(q\)-dependent constant, or the norm statement \(\left\|F_n-\Phi\right\|_q\le Cn^{-1/2}\). Indeed interpolation gives \(\left\|f\right\|_q^q\le\left\|f\right\|_\infty^{q-1}\left\|f\right\|_1\), naturally producing \(C^q\). No fixed-\(q\) convergence rate is disproved here.

# Positive checks, assessment, and limits

The square-root linearization (4.3)/(4.16) has the correct sign and remainder on its positive-denominator event; the error occurs in identifying its centered linear part with the defined sum. The ordinary dependence-exponent interval \(2/3<d<a-3/2\) is nonempty precisely when \(a>13/6\), and one can additionally choose \(d<1\). The displacement bound \[\sup_{|x|\le C\sqrt{\log n}}\frac{|x|b^2\log n}{n}
=O(n^{-1/2})\] is consistent with (A3). The deterministic \(c_b\) is eventually positive by covariance summability and \(\sigma^2>0\); this does not imply positivity of the random rectangular estimator. The smoothing perturbation telescopes to \(H_n-H_0\), and its sinc-power density has compact Fourier support. These checks support particular intended steps without establishing the missing uniform inputs.

Assessment: Major (four Major and three Minor Problems). The target-scale counterexample concerns a principal printed assertion. The centering, concentration, and Edgeworth issues affect indispensable proof inputs and have separate correction requirements. The remaining three issues have explicit local repair boundaries. Repairability does not make the first four inconsequential, and none warrants the broader claim that every intended theorem or application is false.
