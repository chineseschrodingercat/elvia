\section*{Puncturing, the multiplier, and the change of variables}
\subsection*{Act I: pp. 471–476}
Lemma 3.1 combines a zero on each relevant circle with the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy–Schwarz. Radial segments extend the circle bound across the surrounding annulus. A cover of bounded overlap then bounds the full \(L^2\) norm by the gradient norm. Scaling the geometry by \(\varepsilon\) gives a Poincaré constant \(P\le C\varepsilon^2\). The construction removes holes near both \(0\) and \(z_{\max}\); this only changes the net constant. The printed \(z_{\max}\) lies in the closed half-radius ball (p. 474); a lost overline in extracted text does not give a failure of maximum attainment.\par
For a domain with \(P=k^2\), the Dirichlet Poisson estimate used in Lemma 3.2 is \(\|w\|_\infty\le CP\|v\|_\infty\). Iteration with right-hand side \(-V\) times the preceding term is a contraction when \(CP\|V\|_\infty<1/2\). Its sum gives \(\varphi-1\in W^{1,2}_0\), \(\Delta\varphi+V\varphi=0\), and \(\eta:=\|\varphi-1\|_\infty=O(\varepsilon^2)\). The energy norms are summable as well. Positivity follows by choosing \(\eta<1/3\). The general Poisson construction rests on Lemmas A.6 and A.10; the later circle-zero consequence A.14 is not a premise of that construction.\par
\subsection*{Act II: pp. 476–481}
The Sobolev product and reciprocal rules give, almost everywhere, \[\varphi^2\nabla(u/\varphi)=\varphi\nabla u-u\nabla\varphi.\] Subtracting the two weak equations cancels the potential term. Across the nodal set \(F_0\), use a cutoff whose gradient lies in the shrinking exterior shell around \(F_0\). On the compact support of a test function, \(|u|\le C\,\operatorname{dist}(\,\cdot\,,F_0)\), so the cutoff derivative is bounded after multiplication by \(u\). Cauchy–Schwarz and the shrinking shell make its integral against \(\nabla\varphi\) tend to zero. The shell shrinks outside \(F_0\); the argument does not assume that \(F_0\) has measure zero. Thus \(\operatorname{div}(\varphi^2\nabla f)=0\), \(f=u/\varphi\), throughout the punctured domain, including across its nodal interfaces.\par
On a simply connected local disk let \(\widetilde f_y=\varphi^2 f_x\), \(\widetilde f_x=-\varphi^2 f_y\). For \(w=f+i\widetilde f\), \[w_z=\tfrac12(1+\varphi^2)(f_x-if_y),\qquad
 w_{\bar z}=\tfrac12(1-\varphi^2)(f_x+if_y).\] Consequently the measurable coefficient \(w_{\bar z}/w_z\), set to zero where \(\nabla f=0\), is independent of the additive conjugate constant. Its norm is below one, and \[K\le \max\{(1+\eta)^2,(1-\eta)^{-2}\}\le1+6\eta
 \quad (0\le\eta\le1/3).\] One global Beltrami map therefore works for the local factorizations. Their real parts give a globally defined harmonic \(h=f\circ g^{-1}\). A global single-valued conjugate on the multiply connected punctured domain is unnecessary. The smooth disk precursor in [NPS], Lemma 3.4, supports this interface; the weak and punctured-domain steps were checked in the present article.\par
Choose \(\varepsilon=c/\sqrt{\log R}\) with the constants in their stated order. Mori distortion then keeps the chosen holes and their collars uniformly separated at the relevant scales. The sign of \(u\) survives division by the positive \(\varphi\) and composition with \(g\). These sign collars are an essential hypothesis of Act III.\par
\section*{The harmonic estimate and recovery of the main theorems}
\subsection*{Act III: pp. 481–487}
Write \(m_j=\min_{\partial3D_j}|h|\). Harnack on each sign-preserving annulus and an interior gradient estimate give \(\max_{\partial3D_j}(|h|+|\nabla h|)\le A m_j\), after enlarging \(A\). The local theorem normalizes the supremum in the smaller large ball to one and takes \(k=\lfloor C(R+N)\rfloor\). Assuming \(\sup_{B_r\setminus\cup3D_j}|h|\le(r/R)^{3k}\) yields inner-boundary bounds \((r/R)^{2k}\) and outer-boundary bounds \(Ae^N\).\par
In the punctured annulus the function \[F(z)=\frac{h_x-ih_y}{z^k}\] is single-valued and holomorphic. A curve of length at most \(4R\) from the inner boundary to a point witnessing the normalized supremum gives \(\sup|\nabla h|\ge1/(8R)\). Separated disks permit the necessary detours. The estimates \[2^{-k}<\frac1{8R},\qquad
 Ae^N(126/127)^k<\frac1{8R}\] exclude both annular boundary components from the maximum of \(|F|\). Thus an interior hole carries it; if there is no such hole the contradiction is already complete.\par
Choose the hole maximizing \(m_j/|z_j|^k\), where \(z_j\) is its closest point to zero. The gradient bound along the inward radius gives loss at most \(ARm_j/(k+1)\le m_j/2\). Arrival at the inner boundary conflicts with \[\frac{m_j}{2}\ge\frac{(r/(2R))^k}{16AR}>(r/R)^{2k}.\] Arrival at another hole conflicts with maximality, since the intervening distance is at least 96 and \((1-96/R)^k<1/(2A)\). All these conditions hold with one sufficiently large \(C\): the exponential losses are controlled by \(k\asymp C(R+N)\), whereas the competing factors have logarithms \(N+O(\log(AR))\). The independent check uses \(C=512(A+1)\) on a finite grid as a sanity check, in addition to these monotonic estimates. The outward-ray proof of the global toy theorem has the same structure.\par
\subsection*{Normalization and scaling: p. 481}
Set \(U=\sup_{B_R}|u|\) and retain the protected point \(z_{\max}\). The exact harmonic doubling parameter may be taken as \[N_*=N+\log\frac{1+\eta}{1-\eta}\le N+1.\] Also \(|h(g(z_{\max}))|\ge e^{-N}U/(1+\eta)\). Scaling by \(g/(32\varepsilon)\) gives \(R'=R/(32\varepsilon)\). Even the weaker lower bound obtained by applying upper Mori to the inverse map gives an available radius with \(r'/R'\ge (r/R)^2/256\) when \(K\le2\). For \(L=\log(R/r)\ge\log4\), \[\log(R'/r')\le\log256+2L\le6L.\] The harmonic estimate, the factor \((1-\eta)/(1+\eta)\), and the extra \(e^{-N}\) therefore combine into \[\sup_{B_r}|u|\ge(r/R)^{C(R\sqrt{\log R}+N)}U.\] This avoids assuming that the supremum inside an excluded hole equals the exterior supremum.\par
Theorem 1.2 follows by centering the local theorem at \(x_0\), using the ball of radius \(3R/2\) inside the original \(B(0,2R)\), and taking \(N=0\). The unit radius then contributes the extra \(\log R\). For Theorem 1.1 a nonzero globally decaying solution has a global maximum. A large ball centered a distance \(R\) from that maximum, of radius \(4R\), has \(N=0\); its ball of radius \(R/2\) lies far away. The resulting \(\exp(-CR\sqrt{\log R})\) lower bound contradicts the asserted decay with a sufficiently large absolute decay constant.\par
\section*{Appendix checks and independent calculations}
\subsection*{Regularity, variational construction, and iteration}
The potential-convolution argument in A.2 starts with \(u\in L^1_{\rm loc}\), uses the planar logarithmic kernel to obtain every finite local \(L^p\), and then takes \(p>2\) for the first derivatives. Weyl’s lemma handles the harmonic remainder. The sign of that remainder must follow \(\Delta u=-Vu\); an isolated printed sign does not change the estimate.\par
The variational functional \(\Phi(u)=\int|\nabla u|^2+\int vu\) has Euler equation \(\Delta u=v/2\). The localized weighted Poincaré estimate controls approximate minimizers on unit balls. A.8 and A.9 then make the Poincaré constant small on superlevel sets. In the smooth surgery, the lower part is constant on the support of any competitor in the upper domain, so the cross gradient term really vanishes.\par
Before the stopping index, the recurrence gives \(k_j^2\le k_{j-1}^3\). For \(k_0\le1/4\) it implies \[k_j\le4^{-(3/2)^j},\qquad
 \sqrt{k_j}\le2^{-1-j/2},\] because \((3/2)^j\ge1+j/2\). Thus the thresholds are summable; the weaker intermediate geometric estimate alone need not be used. If \(A=4\int|v|\), the freely chosen surgery errors can be taken as \(\delta_j=\epsilon/[16(A+1)2^j]\). This controls both the added variational error and the total pointwise shift.\par
The actual PDF on p. 499 correctly displays \[\widetilde\Omega=(k_0/k)\Omega,\quad
 \widetilde u(x)=\frac{k_0^2}{k^2}u\!\left(\frac{k}{k_0}x\right),\quad
 \widetilde\Phi(\widetilde u)=\frac{k_0^4}{k^4}\Phi(u).\] These formulas preserve the Poisson right-hand side and give \(\|u\|_\infty\le Ck^2\|v\|_\infty\) when the Poincaré constant is \(P=k^2\). A numerator reversal in extracted text was rejected after viewing the original. A disk torsion solution \(u=(|x|^2-a^2)/8\), \(\Delta u=1/2\), confirms the dimensional scaling.\par

\section*{Scope clarifications, counterchecks, and limits}
\subsection*{Connectedness in Corollary 2.5}
The statement on p. 471 describes a closed two-dimensional Riemannian manifold and compares a local supremum with \(\sup_M|u|\). The chart-chain argument on pp. 506–507 uses connectedness. If disconnected manifolds are permitted by the convention, let \(M=M_1\sqcup M_2\) be two closed connected surfaces, \(V=0\), and let \(u\) equal one on \(M_1\) and zero on \(M_2\). This is a smooth solution, with \(|V|\le\lambda\) for \(\lambda=3\). For \(x\in M_2\) and small \(r\), the left-hand local supremum is zero while the claimed positive multiple of \(\sup_M|u|=1\) is positive.\par
This is a complete counterexample to the disconnected extension, not to the planar theorem or to the connected reading of the corollary. State connected explicitly, or replace the global supremum by the supremum on the component containing \(x\). The latter correction is exactly what the propagation proof supplies. Because the standard connected-manifold reading resolves the scope, this item is recorded as a clarification rather than a confirmed substantive proof defect.\par
\subsection*{Bookkeeping distinguished from substantive findings}
On p. 481 use \(N_*\) from Section 3 and \(R'=R/(32\varepsilon)\); the displayed rescaling map and the neighboring asymptotic force this radius. The intended comparison there is a lower bound, as stated and proved in Theorem 5.3. In A.10 write the Poincaré constant as \(P=k^2\) consistently with Lemma 3.2 and the preceding derivation. These deterministic notation and constant corrections preserve the exponents and assumptions. Typographical corrections alone are outside the substantive-finding scope of this review.\par
