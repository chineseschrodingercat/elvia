Abstract: The reviewed version contains a one-step Milnor–Witt weight error in the proof of Lemma 4.0.7. The corrected weight gives the same stabilization conclusion by a direct divisibility argument; this is a Minor issue. A separate cohomology-kernel comparison in Lemma 4.0.8 is made explicit below under the main theorem’s hypothesis that \(d!\) is invertible. Its broader characteristic range remains a limitation of this review, not a demonstrated counterexample. The determinant twist, shifted obstruction actions, factorial bounds, and dimension-three argument were checked. No counterexample to the cancellation theorem is established. # Proof architecture and hypothesis checks

## Determinants and change of base point

The representability identification is an external input: \[[X_+,B\mathrm{GL}_n]_{\mathbb A^1}\simeq\mathcal V_n(X).\] For a prescribed bundle, the motivic Postnikov tower is based at its actual stable class. The translation in \(K_0\) changes the loop base point, and the resulting obstruction maps retain its Chern classes. Replacing that class by the trivial bundle would prove only a stably free special case; the source does not make that replacement.

The relative Eilenberg–Mac Lane construction in Lemma 2.2.2 uses a fixed torsor and maps over that torsor. Thus the relevant fiber is not mistakenly enlarged by taking an additional quotient by all torsor automorphisms. In Lemma 2.2.3 (pp. 12–13), squares in \(k^\times\) identify the last Rost–Schmid terms canonically. One must also identify the images of their incoming differentials. The source does so by normalizing each codimension-\((d-1)\) curve, using the 2-divisibility of its Picard group to make the pulled-back line bundle a square, and comparing finite pushforwards. This proves the top-cohomology untwisting needed later, not a global untwisting of all sheaves.

The first homotopy sheaf of \(\mathbb A^d\setminus0\) is \(\pi^{\mathbb A^1}_{d-1}(\mathbb A^d\setminus0)=\mathbf K_d^{MW}\). For the fiber \(F_{d-1}\) of \(B\mathrm{GL}_{d-1}\to B\mathrm{GL}\), the first sheaf is Milnor–Witt in odd \(d\) and Milnor in even \(d\). The vanishing of \(I^m(L)|_X\) for \(m\geq d+1\), in the stated characteristic, makes their restrictions agree with \(\mathbf K_d^M|_X\). This is the legitimate point at which determinant dependence disappears from this coefficient sheaf.

## The two obstruction actions

Equation reference: eq:chern. Writing \(\xi\) for the fixed stable class, equation (3.0.9) on p. 25 gives \[
 \Delta(M)=c_d(M)+\sum_{r=1}^{d-1}c_r(M)c_{d-r}(\xi).\] For the Suslin map \(S_d\), the lower Chern classes vanish and the top one is \((d-1)!\kappa_d\). Surjectivity of \(\kappa_d\), together with prime-to-characteristic divisibility of \(H^{d-1}(X,\mathbf K_d^M)\), makes the first action surjective. This works for every \(\xi\). In dimension three, only surjectivity as a map of pointed sets is needed at this step; one need not impose an unjustified stable cohomotopy group law.

For the next action (pp. 26–28), the target identifies with \(H^d(X,\mathbf K_{d+1}^M)\). It is a quotient of \(\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}k^\times\), hence is \(d!\)-divisible. Given \(\alpha\), choose \(\beta\) with \(d!\beta=\alpha\) and a preimage of \(\beta\) under \(\kappa_{d+1}\). The chosen \(S_{d+1}\)-loop lifts through the preceding stage: all its Chern classes of degrees at most \(d\) vanish, so [eq:chern] (#eq:chern) is zero. Its upper obstruction action is therefore \(\alpha\). Arbitrary stable loops are not asserted to lift. This verifies the quantifier needed for the second transitivity argument.

Only stages \(d-1\) and \(d\) can contribute on a smooth affine \(d\)-fold. For disconnected smooth schemes, the argument applies componentwise: on lower-dimensional components, rank \(d-1\) is already at least their dimension and the older cancellation theorem suffices.

# Divisibility, dimensions, and annihilation

All cohomology below is Nisnevich unless specified otherwise. The proof of Lemma 4.0.3 uses the coniveau spectral sequence with nonzero region \(0\leq a\leq b\leq d\). No differential can enter \(E_2^{d-1,d}\) or \(E_2^{d-2,d}\), because its source would have second index greater than \(d\). No differential can leave them, because its target would lie below \(a=b\). Their total degrees exceed the affine cohomological dimension. The only possible incoming differential at \((d-1,d-1)\) is \[d_2:E_2^{d-3,d}\longrightarrow E_2^{d-1,d-1};\] it is onto for the same total-degree reason.

Norm residue, the cited torsion description, and the cited compatibility with the coniveau differential turn these facts into the dotted-map surjectivity in (4.0.4). The final term of the Gersten complex for \({}_\ell\mathbf K_d^M\) is zero, since \({}_\ell K_0^M(k)={}_\ell\mathbb Z=0\). The diagram chase gives unique \(\ell\)-divisibility of \(H^{d-1}(X,\mathbf K_d^M)\), for \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\).

For Lemma 4.0.5, first lift a class along the surjective vertical map in the p. 34 diagram, then subtract a preimage from the upper dotted map to kill its horizontal obstruction. This does not change its image under multiplication by \(\ell\). It gives divisibility of \(H^{d-2}(X,\mathbf K_d^M)\). If \(0\to H\to G\to M\to0\), \(G\) is \(\ell\)-divisible and \(M\) has no \(\ell\)-torsion, an \(\ell\)-root in \(G\) of an element of \(H\) still lies in \(H\). This checks Corollary 4.0.4 and, with the preceding result, the extension argument in Proposition 4.0.6.

The Suslin first-row calculation has the following exact ranges. Here \(n\) is the dimension of the punctured affine space and \(m\) the homotopy degree; Lemma 2.4.1 requires \(2\leq m\leq2n-4\).

The affine quadric projection is locally an affine-space bundle: on \(x_i\ne0\), solve for \(y_i\). Thus its use as a model for \(\mathbb A^n\setminus0\) is justified more specifically than by merely having affine fibers.

The Hermitian \(K\)-theory factorization in Proposition 4.0.9 and its cited top-cohomology vanishing make the stabilized Suslin image zero. The stabilization kernel bound and first-row composite then annihilate \(H^d(X,\pi^{\mathbb A^1}_d(\mathbb A^d\setminus0))\) by \(d!(d-1)!\). Its prime divisors are those of \(d!\); divisibility under the main theorem’s assumptions therefore forces this group to vanish.

# Minor correction: the weight in Lemma 4.0.7

Equation reference: eq:weight. On p. 35, the displayed identification following the two exact cohomology sequences uses \(\mathbf K_{d+1}^{MW}\), and then \(\mathbf K_{d+1}^M\), for the first nonzero homotopy sheaf of \(\mathbb A^{d+2}\setminus0\). The correct identification is \[
 \pi^{\mathbb A^1}_{d+1}(\mathbb A^{d+2}\setminus0)=\mathbf K_{d+2}^{MW}.\] It follows directly from the general formula on p. 14 with \(d\) replaced by \(d+2\); the printed index is one too small.

Equation reference: eq:symbol. This correction does not destroy the lemma. By Corollary 3.0.2, its restriction to \(X\) is \(\mathbf K_{d+2}^M\). The last Gersten term computing \(H^d(X,\mathbf K_{d+2}^M)\) is \(\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}K_2^M(k)\). For every positive integer \(N\) and symbol \(\{a,b\}\), algebraic closedness supplies \(c^N=a\), so \[
 \{a,b\}=N\{c,b\}.\] Thus this last term, and its cohomology quotient, are divisible. The argument uses divisibility, not unique divisibility. It is valid even if the characteristic divides \(N\).

If \(C\) is the first cokernel sheaf in the proof of Lemma 4.0.7, right exactness of top cohomology identifies \(H^d(X,C)\) with the relevant cokernel. Lemma 2.4.1 kills it by \((d+1)!\). It is also divisible as a quotient of the group just described, so it is zero. The second short exact sequence now gives the desired stabilization isomorphism. No new exclusion of characteristic \(d+1\) is needed.

This is a local index correction with a complete replacement for the affected sentence; the main vanishing and cancellation conclusions survive. Author adoption is unknown.

# Explicit completion of the kernel comparison

## The inference that needs explanation

Lemma 4.0.8 ends on p. 35 by passing from an exact sheaf segment \(B\to D\to E\) to a claim about the kernel of \(H^d(D)\to H^d(E)\). Such a segment alone does not make that cohomology kernel the image of \(H^d(B)\). One must compare the image sheaf with \(E\). This review supplies the comparison under the main theorem’s hypothesis \(N=d!\in k^\times\). This is a proof-completeness observation; no counterexample to the lemma is claimed.

For this comparison, work on a connected component of dimension \(d\geq4\), as in the source’s running convention. Lower-dimensional components are handled separately as above. Set, on the small Nisnevich site of \(X\), \[D=\pi^{\mathbb A^1}_d(\mathrm{GL}_d)|_X,\qquad
 E=\pi^{\mathbb A^1}_d(\mathrm{GL}_{d+1})|_X,\qquad
 A=\mathbf K_{d+1}^M|_X.\] The long homotopy sequence continues as \(D\to E\xrightarrow{r}A\). The Suslin map gives \(t:A\to E\) with \(rt=N\,\mathrm{id}\). Indeed the punctured affine space now has dimension \(d+1\); the first-row degree is \(d!\), and the homotopy degree \(d\) is in the permitted range.

## Divisibility needed for the comparison

For each prime \(\ell\mid N\), the generic field of an étale scheme over \(X\) has \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension at most \(d\). Norm residue gives \(K_{d+1}^M(F)/\ell=0\). Unramifiedness of the strictly \(\mathbb A^1\)-invariant quotient therefore gives \(A/\ell=0\) on this site.

We also need \(H^d(X,{}_\ell A)=0\). Contraction commutes with the kernel defining the torsion sheaf. Its final Rost–Schmid term is \(\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mu_\ell(k)\). The Milnor coefficient has trivial orientation action, so this computation has no residual line-bundle twist. Choose a primitive root \(\tau\). For each codimension-\((d-1)\) curve, the torsion symbol \(\{\tau,f\}\) has boundary \(\tau^{\operatorname{ord}(f)}\), with a consistent choice of residue sign. The resulting boundaries contain all rational-equivalence relations modulo \(\ell\). Hence \[\mathrm{CH}_0(X)/\ell\twoheadrightarrow H^d(X,{}_\ell A).\]

The group \(\mathrm{CH}_0(X)\) is divisible in this affine situation. Each closed point lies on an integral affine curve. Normalize that curve; the normalization is a smooth affine curve, finite over its image, with a \(k\)-point above the chosen point. Its Picard group is divisible: compactify, use a boundary \(k\)-point to remove the degree part, then use divisibility of the Jacobian’s \(k\)-points. Pushing forward an \(\ell\)-root of the point class gives an \(\ell\)-root in \(\mathrm{CH}_0(X)\). Closed points generate that group. Consequently the displayed target vanishes.

The exact sequence \(0\to{}_\ell A\to A\xrightarrow{\ell}A\to0\) then makes \(H^{d-1}(X,A)\) \(\ell\)-divisible. Iterating over the prime factors of \(N\) proves \(N\)-divisibility of both \(A\) and \(H^{d-1}(X,A)\). The relation \(rt=N\) now proves that both \(r\) and \(H^{d-1}(r)\) are onto.

## Recovering the desired kernel

Equation reference: eq:kernel. Put \(J=\ker r=\operatorname{im}(D\to E)\). From \(0\to J\to E\to A\to0\) and the preceding surjectivity, \(H^d(J)\to H^d(E)\) is injective. From \(0\to B\to D\to J\to0\) and cohomological dimension at most \(d\), \[H^d(B)\longrightarrow H^d(D)\longrightarrow H^d(J)
 \longrightarrow0\] is exact. Thus \[
 \ker[H^d(D)\to H^d(E)]
   =\operatorname{im}[H^d(B)\to H^d(D)].\] The preceding homotopy segment and Lemma 2.4.1 make \(H^d(B)\) \(d!\)-torsion, exactly as the source intended. This completes that use in the main proof.

Lemma 4.0.8 and Proposition 4.0.9 are placed under the wider running assumption \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\). The completion above does not establish their full range when \(\operatorname{char}k\mid d!\). Additional characteristic-\(p\) input or a different comparison would be needed there. That uncompleted extension is recorded as Needs Evidence, not as a demonstrated false statement or a refutation of the main theorem.

# The dimension-three boundary and external dependencies

The inequality \(d\leq2d-4\) excludes \(d=3\) from the first-row annihilation proof. The source instead uses Proposition 4.0.10 (pp. 36–37): \[T_5\longrightarrow\pi^{\mathbb A^1}_3(\mathbb A^3\setminus0)
       \longrightarrow GW_4^3\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 0\longrightarrow I^6\longrightarrow T_5
       \longrightarrow\mathbf K_5^M/24\longrightarrow0.\] The \(I^6\) restriction vanishes. The final term for \(H^3(X,\mathbf K_5^M/24)\) is a sum of \(K_2^M(k)/24=0\), by [eq:symbol] (#eq:symbol). The cited \(H^3(X,GW_4^3)=0\) then gives the required vanishing. This argument does not silently reuse the invalid dimension-three stable range. Characteristic three is already excluded from the cancellation theorem by \(3!\), although the auxiliary statement is formulated more broadly.
