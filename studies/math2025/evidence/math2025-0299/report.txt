Abstract: This review concerns the exact manuscript arXiv:2310.03677v3. The obstruction to embedding the product of matrix algebras into a uniform Roe algebra, the expander construction in the quasi-local algebra and the quantitative property A argument were traced. Two finite-index defects are confirmed: the small-coordinate requirement at the first index is impossible, and the radii chosen in the nonembedding proof are undefined at the initial indices. Explicit repairs preserve the stated theorem ranges. The random subspace argument and the uniform block estimate are supplied with quantitative details. The impossible choice is a Major defect in a core construction; its repair does not erase that proof failure. The radius issue is Minor because only an unused finite prefix fails. No counterexample to the principal theorems is established, and scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions, quantifiers and principal dependencies

Throughout, \(X\) is a uniformly locally finite metric space: \(N_X(R)=\sup_x|\operatorname{Ball}(x,R)|<\infty\) for each finite \(R>0\). The algebra \(C_{\rm u}^*[X]\) is the norm closure of the finite-propagation operators on \(\ell_2X\). Quasi-locality requires finite \(\varepsilon\)-propagation for every \(\varepsilon>0\), with the radius allowed to depend on \(\varepsilon\).

Theorem A excludes even nonunital embeddings of \(\prod_{n\ge1}\mathbb M_n\) into \(C_{\rm u}^*[X]\) for every such \(X\). Theorem B assumes an expander sequence in \(X\), and embeds that product into \(C_{\rm ql}^*[X]\). These imply proper inclusion in Corollary C for spaces containing expanders. The discussion of all spaces without property A is an expectation, not another theorem proved here. Theorem 5 gives equality under property A, together with \(\operatorname{dist}(u,C_{\rm u}^*[X])<18\varepsilon^{1/4}\) for contractions with finite positive \(\varepsilon\)-propagation.

## The nonembedding mechanism

Lemma 2 partitions the controlled relation into \(2N_X(R)\) graphs of partial translations. A graph multiplier is a complete contraction: after its associated partial isometry it is a diagonal compression. Lemma 3 uses Schur orthogonality for an irreducible \(n\)-dimensional unitary representation of a finite group. In particular, \[\sup_{\|\alpha\|_\infty\le1}
 \left\|\frac1{|\Gamma|}\sum_g\alpha_g\pi(g)\right\|
 \le n^{-1/2}.\] The conjugate representation in the tensor calculation is present in the source image; it was not inferred from potentially lossy text extraction.

For Lemma 1 the approximation \(\pi(g)+b_g\) has norm at most one, since its two terms occupy orthogonal corners. Averaging with \(\overline{\pi(g)}\) and compressing to the first corner gives an invariant vector, and hence the lower bound \(1-\varepsilon\). The graph partition and the coefficient estimate then give \[\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2N_X(R)}
 < \frac{1+\varepsilon}{\sqrt n}.\] If \(\varepsilon<1\), the hypothesis \(N_X(R)<\sqrt n/8\) implies \(4(1-\varepsilon)<1+\varepsilon\), or \(\varepsilon>3/5\). The case \(\varepsilon\ge1\) already satisfies that conclusion. Subtracting the added \(1/10\) yields the required distance bound. This checks the constants and the role of the nonunital complementary corner.

Once valid radii tending to infinity have been selected as below, the product argument needs no normality of the alleged embedding. For the product element \(a=(a_n)_n\), the element obtained by setting its \(n\)-th coordinate to zero has norm at most one and maps into the complementary corner of the \(n\)-th matrix unit. Lemma 1 applies directly to this algebraic difference. No identification of the image with a strongly convergent diagonal sum is necessary.

# Confirmed findings and repairs

Problem 1 (Major: the first random-subspace requirement is impossible).  In the proof of Theorem B, p. 5, the choices \[\delta_k=\frac1k,\qquad
 \varepsilon_k=100\sqrt{\delta_k\log(1/\delta_k)}\] are followed by the requirement \[\max_{\substack{E\subset[d(n)]\\ |E|/d(n)\le\delta_k}}
       \|P_{V(n)}|_{\ell_2E}\|<\varepsilon_k
 \quad\hbox{for every }k=1,\ldots,n.\] At \(k=1\), one has \(\delta_1=1\) and \(\varepsilon_1=0\). The permitted set \(E=[d(n)]\) gives the norm of the orthogonal projection onto the nonzero \(n\)-dimensional space \(V(n)\), namely one. The requirement is therefore \(1<0\), for every possible choice of \(V(n)\). This is an exact contradiction under all the stated dimension and coordinate-set conditions, not a numerical failure of a particular random sample. In addition, Lemma 4 assumes \(0<\delta<1/10\), which excludes the first ten listed parameters.

A complete parameter repair is \[\widehat\delta_k=\frac1{k+10},\qquad
 \widehat\varepsilon_k
 =100\sqrt{\widehat\delta_k\log(1/\widehat\delta_k)}.\] Every \(\widehat\delta_k\) lies in the lemma’s domain, and \(\widehat\varepsilon_k\to0\). For each fixed \(n\), Lemma 4 applies to the finitely many parameters \(k\le n\). A union bound, followed by an increase of the ambient dimension, gives a positive probability of satisfying all these requirements simultaneously. Thus there is such a subspace. The ambient dimensions can be chosen from a sufficiently large subsequence of the expander sizes.

The downstream argument requires only this vanishing sequence and a finite family of requirements at each stage. The uniform estimate, including the early blocks, is checked in Section 4. Nevertheless, the printed requirement is impossible for every stage, so the subspace selection feeding Theorem B and Corollary C fails as stated. This is a Major core construction error. The explicit repair retains the theorem range and supplies counterevidence to theorem falsity; it does not reduce the severity of the invalid printed construction.

Problem 2 (Minor: the displayed radii are not defined at the initial indices).  The proof of Theorem A, p. 3, states that Lemma 1 supplies an element for each \(n\), with \[R_n=\sup\{R>0:N_X(R)<\sqrt n/8\}-1.\] For every nonempty \(X\), \(N_X(R)\ge1\). When \(1\le n\le64\), the set in this expression is empty. Consequently \(R_n\) is not a positive admissible radius for Lemma 1. For instance \(X=\mathbb Z\) with its usual metric satisfies all the metric hypotheses and has \(N_X(R)=2\lfloor R\rfloor+1\); the same defect is immediate there. This is a defect in the chosen auxiliary sequence, not a counterexample to the nonembedding theorem.

One may instead form the finite set \[J_n=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:64N_X(j)^2<n\}.\] When \(J_n\ne\varnothing\), take \(r_n=\max J_n\) and use Lemma 1. When it is empty, take \(a_n=0\) and impose no radius requirement. For every fixed integer \(j\), uniform local finiteness implies that \(j\in J_n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Hence only finitely many sets are empty, and \(r_n\to\infty\) along the remaining indices. All chosen radii are finite and satisfy exactly the strict hypothesis of Lemma 1. This also avoids an infinite supremum for a finite space. Finite \(X\) in any case cannot admit arbitrarily large matrix corners.

For any prescribed propagation radius, a sufficiently late coordinate now gives distance at least \(1/2\), as required in the proof of Theorem A. No finite initial coordinate affects that argument. The scope and constant of Theorem A are preserved.

# Checks of the random-subspace and block construction

The central concentration estimate was checked beyond the parameter error. Write \(e=25\sqrt{\delta\log(1/\delta)}\). If \(e>1/4\), the claimed strict bound \(4e\) exceeds one and is automatic. For \(e\le1/4\), the single-sphere concentration estimate and entropy bound in the manuscript give, for all sufficiently large \(d\), \[{\mathbb P}\left\{\max_{|E|\le\delta d}\|1_E x\|>2e\right\}
 <2\exp(-e^2d/4).\] The median bound becomes less than \(e\) after increasing \(d\). For nonintegral \(\delta d\), use \(\lfloor\delta d\rfloor\) and extend smaller coordinate sets to that size. The entropy inequality is valid because \(H(\delta)<2\delta\log(1/\delta)<e^2/4\) for \(0<\delta<1/10\).

Here is a direct uniformization of the final net step on pp. 5–6. The span of the independent uniform sphere vectors has the invariant Grassmann distribution. Choose a Haar orthonormal frame \(Q:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) for that subspace. For every fixed unit \(\alpha\), \(Q\alpha\) is uniform on the sphere. An \(e\)-net of the unit sphere of \(\mathbb R^n\) has at most \((1+2/e)^n\) elements. The union bound therefore makes all its coordinate restrictions at most \(2e\), except on a set of probability at most \[2(1+2/e)^n\exp(-e^2d/4).\] Since \(\|1_EQ\|\le1\), net approximation raises the bound only to \(3e<4e\). For fixed \(n,\delta\), increasing \(d\) absorbs the prefactor into \(\exp(-e^2d/8)\). Finally \(\|P_V|_{\ell_2E}\|=\|1_E|_V\|\). Thus the stated conclusion follows with positive constants of the required quantifier dependence. This argument supplies the quantitative uniformity implicit in the manuscript’s almost-orthonormal discussion. It relies on the cited sphere concentration input; it is not a new numerical validation of that external theorem.

The diagonal notation also requires orthogonal blocks. If the expander subsets originally overlap, choose them successively large enough and remove the finite union of preceding blocks. One obtains disjoint finite sets \(Y_n\) retaining at least half of each selected expander, with arbitrarily large sizes. The inherited separation estimate becomes \[\min\!\left(\frac{|A|}{|Y_n|},\frac{|B|}{|Y_n|}\right)
 \le 2\kappa^{-\operatorname{dist}(A,B)/(2R_0)}
 \qquad(A,B\subset Y_n).\] This uniform decay, rather than a new one-step expansion assertion for \(Y_n\), is all the proof uses. The extra factor two is harmless when selecting the separation radius. This supplies disjoint blocks without requiring the original theorem to assume coarse separation of the subsets.

Apply the corrected subspace construction on these blocks. Given \(\varepsilon>0\), select \(k\) with \(\widehat\varepsilon_k<\varepsilon\), and choose a uniform separation radius for the blocks \(n\ge k\). For each such block, \[\|1_Au_n1_B\|
 \le \min\{\|1_AP_{V(n)}\|,\|P_{V(n)}1_B\|\}
 <\widehat\varepsilon_k\] when \(\|u_n\|\le1\) and the sets are sufficiently separated. Enlarge the radius also beyond the diameters of the finitely many blocks \(n<k\). Their contribution then vanishes. Cross-block entries are zero, and the operator norm of the diagonal sum is the supremum of the block norms. This proves uniform quasi-locality for every bounded product element, which is the conclusion needed for the possibly nonunital embedding.

# Property A estimate and assessment

For Theorem 5 the chosen property A families have successive variation bounds at \(\delta=\sqrt\varepsilon\). With \(f_z(x)=\sqrt{\nu_x(z)}\), the map \(\Phi_\nu(u)=\sum_z f_zuf_z\) is unital completely positive and has propagation at most \(2T\). Uniform local finiteness bounds the random function \(f_\omega=\sum_z\omega_zf_z\), and its moments are \[\mathbb E f_\omega(x)^2=1,\qquad
 \mathbb E f_\omega(x)^4
 =3-2\sum_z f_z(x)^4\le3.\] The exact fourth-moment expansion was independently checked. Clipping at \(C=\delta^{-1/2}\) and averaging with the first property A family give \(g,h\) with \[\mathbb E|f-g|^2\le3C^{-2},\quad
 \mathbb E|g-h|^2<\delta,\quad
 |h(x)-h(y)|\le C\delta\quad(\operatorname{dist}(x,y)\le R).\] Apply Lemma 6 to \((h+C)/(2C)\), whose variation parameter is \(\delta/2\). Its commutator estimate is \[\|[h,u]\|
 \le 2C(2\delta+4\varepsilon/\delta)=12\sqrt\delta.\] The operator Cauchy–Schwarz estimate for replacing \(f\) by \(h\) costs at most \(2(\sqrt3+1)\sqrt\delta\). Consequently the actual constant is bounded by \(14+2\sqrt3<18\), which supplies the stated strict inequality. The band decomposition in Lemma 6 uses at most two neighboring diagonals for its \(2\delta\|u\|\) contribution, and the \(\varepsilon\)-propagation bound for the separated diagonals. No zero-\(\varepsilon\) extension of the strict estimate is assumed.
