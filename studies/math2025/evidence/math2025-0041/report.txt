Abstract: This review checks selected definitions, the annulus calculation used for the Tate twist, and specified steps of the cancellation argument in arXiv:2412.03382v3. One Major finding concerns a false root-location criterion used to identify the coefficient fibers in Proposition 5.19, whose annulus model and compactness enter the cancellation proof. An exact counterexample and a complete local correction are given. The corrected coefficient domain remains contractible, so the finding does not refute Proposition 5.19 or the cancellation theorem. No additional substantive issue was established in the inspected scope. The journal version was not compared with this author manuscript.

# Definitions, quantifiers, and checked implications

An effective motive is a finitary, ball-invariant arc-hypersheaf of complexes. The finitary requirement in Definition 1.2 is tested on filtered colimits of strictly totally disconnected Banach rings; it is not asserted for arbitrary filtered colimits of every Banach ring. Definition 5.1 requires \[M(A)\longrightarrow M(A\langle T\rangle_1)\] to be an equivalence for every Banach ring over the base. Corollary 5.4 reduces this test to nondiscrete algebraically closed Banach fields using arc-descent and the preservation of finitary sheaves in Theorem 4.20. That theorem explicitly assumes a proper, zero-truncated map of finite cohomological dimension. Omitting its finiteness assumption would change the argument.

For Proposition 5.6 (pp. 27–28), the checked local implication is the passage from equality of endpoint pullbacks to ball-invariance. Let \(p:\mathbb B_A\to A\) and let \(i:A\to\mathbb B_A\) be the zero section. Then \(p i=\mathrm{id}\), while multiplication \(H(s,t)=st\) joins \(i p\) to \(\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb B_A}\). Consequently, once the stated endpoint homotopy for the localized presheaf is available, \[i^*p^*=\mathrm{id},\qquad p^*i^*\simeq\mathrm{id}.\] Thus \(p^*\) is an equivalence. This verifies that specific implication; it is not a replacement proof of all coherence and descent statements needed to construct the localization.

The relative rigidity statement retains its hypotheses: Theorem 1.12 (p. 6) and Proposition 10.3 (p. 54) require an analytic Banach base of finite cohomological dimension in the affine case. The more general version on p. 54 adds qcqs and the specified quasi-pro-étale geometric-point condition. No unconditional rigidity assertion for an arbitrary small arc-stack is inferred.

# Problem 1: the annulus coefficient criterion is false

## Exact claim and location

Equation reference: eq:printed. In the proof of Proposition 5.19, pp. 34–35, fix \(\mathbb T(r_1,r_2)=\{z:r_1<|z|<r_2\}\), with \(0<r_1<1<r_2\), and a monic polynomial \[f(X)=X^n+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1X+a_0,
\qquad r_1^n<|a_0|<r_2^n.\] The first display on p. 35 characterizes all roots lying in \(\mathbb T\) by \[
|a_i|<\max\{r_2^{\,n-i},|a_0|r_1^{-i}\},
\qquad 1\leq i<n.\] The same \(\max\) is present in the acquired TeX at line 1267. This is not a PDF-extraction ambiguity.

## Exact counterexample and all assumptions

Take \(C=\mathbb C_3\), the completed algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_3\), with \(|3|=1/3\). It is a nondiscrete algebraically closed nonarchimedean Banach field, so its geometric-point base meets the reduction used in the proof. Choose \[n=2,\quad r_1=1/27,\quad r_2=3,\quad
f(X)=(X-9)(X-1/9)=X^2-\frac{82}{9}X+1.\] Both radii belong to the value group, \(0<r_1<1<r_2\), and \[r_1^2=\frac1{729}<|a_0|=1<9=r_2^2.\] Since \(82\) is not divisible by \(3\), \[|a_1|=\left|-\frac{82}{9}\right|_3=9
 <\max\{3,27\}=27.\] Thus every inequality in [eq:printed] (#eq:printed) holds. Nevertheless the exact roots have norms \[|9|_3=\frac19,\qquad |1/9|_3=9.\] The second root lies outside the upper boundary \(3\). The printed criterion is therefore false even in degree two and within the stated nonarchimedean setting. Exact rational arithmetic independently verified both roots and every displayed comparison; no floating-point root solver was used.

## Correct criterion and proof

Equation reference: eq:correct. The correct criterion is \[
|a_i|<\min\{r_2^{\,n-i},|a_0|r_1^{-i}\},
\qquad 1\leq i<n.\] Here and below the assumed bounds on \(a_0\) remain in force.

Necessity. If the roots are \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\), all with \(r_1<|\alpha_j|<r_2\), the ultrametric inequality applied to the elementary symmetric functions gives \[|a_i|<r_2^{\,n-i}.\] Apply the same argument to the monic polynomial \(a_0^{-1}X^n f(1/X)\), whose roots are \(\alpha_j^{-1}\). Its coefficient corresponding to \(a_i\) yields \[|a_i/a_0|<r_1^{-i}.\] Both bounds must hold simultaneously, which gives the minimum.

Sufficiency. Suppose [eq:correct] (#eq:correct) holds. If a root \(\alpha\) satisfied \(|\alpha|\geq r_2\), then for each \(i<n\), \[|a_i\alpha^i|<|\alpha|^n,\] including \(i=0\) by the bound on \(a_0\). The leading term would strictly dominate every other term, impossible in a vanishing nonarchimedean sum. If instead \(|\alpha|\leq r_1\), the bounds give \[|a_i\alpha^i|<|a_0|\quad(1\leq i<n),
\qquad |\alpha|^n\leq r_1^n<|a_0|.\] The constant term would strictly dominate, again impossible. Every root is therefore in the required annulus.

For fixed \(a_0\), the corrected coefficient domain is the product of the open balls \[|a_i|<R_i(a_0),\qquad
R_i(a_0)=\min\{r_2^{\,n-i},|a_0|r_1^{-i}\}>0.\] It contains the origin. The homotopy \[(s,(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}))
 \longmapsto (s a_1,\ldots,s a_{n-1}),\qquad |s|\leq1,\] preserves every strict inequality and contracts the fiber onto the polynomial \(X^n+a_0\). Its roots have norm \(|a_0|^{1/n}\), which lies strictly between \(r_1\) and \(r_2\). This proves the specific fiber-contractibility assertion needed at that step. For \(n=1\), there are no coefficient inequalities and the fiber is a point, consistently with the corrected statement.

## Counterevidence, dependency, and classification

The defect is in the coefficient description, not in the conclusion that the fibers are contractible. Proposition 5.19 supplies the annulus model and compactness of the Tate twist used on p. 41 in Theorem 7.1, so the dependency has been checked explicitly. The proof above establishes the same needed local conclusion with the corrected domain. It does not refute either Proposition 5.19 or the cancellation theorem.

The finding is Major: the false equivalence is the calculation used to identify the fibers as open balls in Proposition 5.19, and that proposition supplies the annulus model and compactness explicitly used in the core cancellation proof. It is therefore an invalid core proof step, even though replacing maximum by minimum restores the needed local conclusion. The ease of this repair and survival of the stated theorems do not remove that dependency. The proved repair is restricted to the coefficient criterion and fiber contraction; it does not independently reconstruct every descent argument in Proposition 5.19 or Theorem 7.1. The author’s intent has not been established, and no author contact or adoption request has occurred.

# Cancellation: additional checks and boundaries

Theorem 7.1 states full faithfulness for every small arc-stack. The inspected argument reduces the adjunction unit to strictly totally disconnected bases and then geometric points, using proper pushforward and the annulus model. The rational free-sheaf expression on p. 41 invokes Theorem 6.1, whose coefficient inversion and rationalization qualifications are explicit on p. 35; those qualifications were retained.

Three algebraic aspects of the remaining argument were checked.

First, the splitting on p. 42 compares multiplicity \(N\) with multiplicity \(N-1\), so its composite is multiplication by \(1\). In residue characteristic \(p\), counting distinct roots would be incorrect. The manuscript explicitly includes multiplicities for the \(N-1\) term. Indeed, writing \(m=p^a u\), \(p\nmid u\), gives \[T^m-1=(T^u-1)^{p^a}\] over a characteristic-\(p\) field; the total multiplicity remains \(m\). This checks the arithmetic of the asserted splitting, not the entire geometric construction of \(Y_2\).

Second, for the basepoint contribution the equality is \(p_2=1\). Both loci are then defined by \(p_1^N=1\); their difference is zero. Thus the checked construction annihilates the indicated basepoint summand and is compatible with the pointed quotient at this level. The overline on \(\overline{\mathbb G_m}=\mathbb G_m/(1+\mathcal O_{<1})\) was confirmed in both the page images and the TeX, rather than erased by plain-text extraction.

Third, Lemma 7.3 on p. 43 uses the degree-zero condition \(\sum_i n_i=0\) to show that translation by \(-1\) changes \(\prod_i\overline{x}_i^{\,n_i}\) by \((-1)^{\sum_i n_i}=1\). The later swap on \(\overline{\mathbb G_m}\otimes\overline{\mathbb G_m}\) has the sign coming from the shift in \(\mathbb Z(1)=\overline{\mathbb G_m}[-1]\). The proof uses that swap twice, producing sign \(+1\). The text distinguishes elementwise triviality from homotopy-coherent triviality and does not use the latter as an unproved premise to finish cancellation.

No additional substantive defect was established in these checked steps. Uniform construction over the base, the eventual-sequence formalism, and all transfer/descent inputs remain outside an independently completed proof reconstruction.
