Abstract: The exact CVGMT manuscript dated 19 September 2025 is assessed as Major because two displayed proof steps fail: the auxiliary exhaustion function has incompatible prescribed values, and a decisive scaled Ricci determinant estimate is false in the specified metric family. The latter step admits a local repair by a corrected Hessian calculation and a Schur complement. Two additional scope errors concern circle-action freeness and exact volume exponents. These findings do not constitute a counterexample to the asserted Ricci-nonnegative manifold. Source and report image inspection remains blocked.\par

\section*{P1: incompatible data in the exhaustion construction}
\subsection*{Exact obstruction}
The proof of Lemma 3.3 on page 15 places the centres \(x_a\) on the sphere of radius \(r_{j+1}/10\), in a rotation orbit, with disjoint annuli \(A_{r_j,2r_j}(x_a)\) and \(r_{j+1}\gg r_j\gg1\). It asserts a function \(\eta\) on the complement of the inner balls with, among other properties, \[\begin{aligned}
\eta(x)&=|x-x_0|^2 &&\text{on }A_{r_j,2r_j}(x_0),\\
\eta(x)&=|x|^2 &&\text{outside }B_{100r_j}(0).\end{aligned}\] The subscript in the latter radius is \(j\) in both layout and raw extraction of the bound page.\par
Take \[r_j=100,\quad r_{j+1}=10^6,\quad k_{j+1}=2,\quad
x_0=(10^5,0,0),\quad x_1=(-10^5,0,0).\] The centres form the required rotation orbit. Their distance is \(200000>4r_j\), so the annuli are disjoint; neither contains the origin. The point \[x=(100150,0,0)\] is in \(A_{100,200}(x_0)\) and outside \(B_{10000}(0)\). The two conditions therefore force \[\eta(x)=22500,\qquad \eta(x)=10030022500,\] which is impossible. These integer calculations were executed independently. The obstruction is parametric: choose the point \(x=x_0+\tfrac32 r_j e_1\) and any ratio \(r_{j+1}/r_j>1000\). Thus it persists under arbitrarily strong scale separation, rather than relying on a borderline interpretation of \(\gg\). No smoothness or critical-point issue needs to be decided before this contradiction.\par
\subsection*{Impact and repair boundary}
This finding is assessed as Major. Lemma 3.3 is used on page 14 to assemble a proper Morse–Bott function on the limiting cover, without critical points at positive levels. That is the stated route to Proposition 3.1 and the main universal-cover diffeomorphism \(\widetilde M\simeq S^3\times\mathbb R^3\). The actual induction as written cannot use the asserted \(\eta\).\par
The obstruction is to this proof, not an example of a different topology for the constructed cover. It does not refute the independent claim that a six-dimensional Ricci-nonnegative counterexample to finite generation exists. An outer matching radius enclosing all the gluing annuli, for example one depending on \(r_{j+1}\), removes the displayed overlap. A completed correction must still construct the function with affine matching at the other holes, no critical points, compatibility with preceding stages, and the properness estimate. Those requirements have not been supplied merely by changing the index of a radius.\par
\section*{P2: the central Ricci estimate and its local repair}
\subsection*{An admissible endpoint contradicts the determinant bound}
Proposition 6.7 deforms the fibre length and connection while keeping the base metric \((N,h^\alpha)\) fixed; see pages 29–30 and equations (97)–(99). The last paragraph on page 33 asserts that the determinant and trace of \(\alpha^2\operatorname{Ric}\) on \(\operatorname{span}\{U_\beta,F_2^{\alpha,\beta}\}\) are both greater than \(1/2\) for every \(\beta\in[0,1]\) and sufficiently small \(\alpha\).\par
Use the permitted even value \(k=2\), the permitted endpoint \(\beta=1\), and the regular point \(\psi=\pi/2\). The fibre length is then constant one. Equations (60)–(62), (90), (95), and (104) give the actual orthonormal two-dimensional block \[R=\begin{pmatrix}
2 & \displaystyle\frac{2\alpha k}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^2k^2}}\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha k}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^2k^2}}&
\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{-2}+2\alpha^2k^2}{1+\alpha^2k^2}
\end{pmatrix}.\] Here the horizontal contraction of the curvature form in the \(F_2\) direction vanishes, as does the Hessian of the constant fibre length. Direct calculation yields \[\det(\alpha^2R)=\frac{2\alpha^2}{1+\alpha^2k^2}.\] For \(k=2\), \[\frac12-\det(\alpha^2R)=\frac{1}{2(1+4\alpha^2)}>0.\] The assertion therefore fails for every positive \(\alpha\), including arbitrarily small values below any proposed threshold. At \(\alpha=1/10\), the determinant is exactly \(1/52\). This uses the paper’s metric family and all of the relevant hypotheses, not an unrelated matrix with a similar asymptotic form.\par
\subsection*{The base cannot be changed when differentiating the fibre length}
There is a related exact-formula error in (106)–(107), page 31. Write \[c=(1-\beta)k,\qquad
f=\sqrt{1+\alpha^2k^2\sin^2\psi},\qquad
u=\sqrt{1+\alpha^2c^2\sin^2\psi}.\] The base still uses \(k\), even though the circle-fibre length is now \(u\). The metric on a two-sphere fibre in the base has meridian length element \(\alpha\,d\psi\) and rotational profile \[a(\psi)=\frac{\alpha\sin\psi}{f}.\] Its fibres are totally geodesic. For a function of \(\psi\), direct differentiation gives \[\begin{aligned}
\frac{\operatorname{Hess}u(F_1,F_1)}{u}
 &=\frac{c^2\cos^2\psi}{u^4}-\frac{c^2\sin^2\psi}{u^2},\\
\frac{\operatorname{Hess}u(F_2,F_2)}{u}
 &=\frac{1}{\alpha^2u}\frac{a'}a\,u'
 =\frac{c^2\cos^2\psi}{u^2f^2}.\end{aligned}\] The second coefficient in (107) instead has denominator \(u^4\). Their difference, actual minus printed, is \[-\frac{\alpha^2c^2(k^2-c^2)\sin^2\psi\cos^2\psi}{u^4f^2}.\] For \(0<\beta<1\), nonzero \(k\), and an interior latitude this is generally nonzero. For example, with \(k=2,\beta=1/2,\psi=\pi/4,\alpha=1/10\), the actual and printed coefficients are respectively \(5000/10251\) and \(20000/40401\). Consequently the corresponding Laplacian identity (106) must also be corrected. These derivative identities were independently checked symbolically. The endpoint determinant counterexample above is unaffected by this additional error, since \(u=1\) there.\par
\subsection*{What can be repaired}
This is assessed as Major: the proof of the central equivariant twisting proposition ends with a false decisive estimate and uses inaccurate exact derivatives. Its assessment concerns the written proof and its role in the main construction; ease of a possible repair does not make the estimate true. Positive Ricci curvature itself is not disproved.\par
Indeed, a local repair is available. Let \(A,B,C\) be the corrected entries of the two-dimensional block and put \(q=(1-\beta)k\sin\psi\). The stated connection formulas, with the corrected derivatives, give \[A=2+q^2+O_k(\alpha^2),\qquad
\alpha B=-q+O_k(\alpha^2),\qquad
\alpha^2C=1+O_k(\alpha^2).\] The estimates are uniform in \(\beta\in[0,1]\) and \(\psi\in[0,\pi]\): after removing the indicated negative powers of \(\alpha\), the formulas extend smoothly to \(\alpha=0\), with denominators involving only \(f,u\geq1\). The symbolic check verifies the exact limiting entries.\par
For the matrix \(R-I\), \(C-1>0\) at small \(\alpha\), and its Schur complement is \[A-1-\frac{B^2}{C-1}
=A-1-\frac{(\alpha B)^2}{\alpha^2C-\alpha^2}
=1+O_k(\alpha^2).\] It is uniformly positive for sufficiently small \(\alpha\). This proves the required positivity of this block by a valid argument. The separate \(Z_2,Z_3\) and \(F_1\) directions retain the lower bounds used on page 32; their small-\(\alpha\) limits or dominant \(\alpha^{-2}\) terms are consistent with the corrected calculation.\par
The repair verifies this local step from the specified base and connection expressions. It does not independently certify all subsequent bundle deformations or the inherited metric gluing. No author adoption is claimed.\par
\section*{Two local scope corrections}
\subsection*{P3: free circle actions}
Proposition 2.1(4), page 8, asserts freeness for coprime integers \((a,b)\) with \(a\ne0\). In the stipulated product end, choose \((a,b)=(2,1)\) and a pole of the \(S^2\) factor. The nonidentity circle element \(\theta=\pi\) acts on \(S^3\) by the Hopf phase \(e^{2i\pi}=1\) and fixes the pole of \(S^2\). It therefore fixes every point with that pole and has nontrivial stabilizer. Coprimality and \(a\ne0\) both hold.\par
This is Minor in its actual use: the induction uses weights \((1,k)\) and \((1,0)\), whose first weight is one, so their freeness survives. Restricting this assertion to \(a=\pm1\) is sufficient by the globally free first Hopf action, and necessary on the stated product end. No obstruction to the actions actually required in the induction is shown.\par
\subsection*{P4: exact volume exponents}
Proposition 4.4, page 18, states for every \(\eta>0\) that there are unbounded radii \(t_i\) with \[\operatorname{Vol}(B_{t_i}(p))=t_i^{3+\eta}.\] Take \(\eta=4\). In dimension six, completeness and \(\operatorname{Ric}\geq0\) imply \[\operatorname{Vol}(B_t(p))\leq\omega_6t^6,\qquad \omega_6=\frac{\pi^3}{6}.\] For completeness, the radial shape-operator trace \(m\) satisfies \(m'+m^2/5\leq0\) along minimizing radial geodesics, with \(m\sim5/r\) at the centre. Hence \(m\leq5/r\), and the polar Jacobian is at most \(r^5\) before the cut locus. Integrating over directions and the minimizing radial interval gives the displayed bound. Thus equality with \(t^7\) is impossible for \(t>\omega_6\), and cannot hold along an unbounded sequence. An exact check using \(\pi<22/7\) also verifies the contradiction already at \(t=10\).\par
The assessment is Minor because the main theorem requires weaker inequalities, not these exact equalities for all positive exponents. Restricting the construction proposition to small \(\eta\), for example \(0<\eta<1\), is sufficient for its use: choose \(\eta_0<\min\{\eta,1\}\) and apply monotonicity of powers for radii at least one. This fixes the logical range needed for the theorem; existence of the small-\(\eta\) sequences still depends on the construction.\par
\section*{Further checks, outcome, and limitations}
The nested cyclic-group scheme, exact isometric-core embeddings, and scale choices are the operative route to a complete smooth limit and its action. The topological extension requires the more precise fibrewise orthogonal representative of the twisting diffeomorphism supplied in Section 6.7; an arbitrary diffeomorphism of \(S^3\times S^2\) would not be sufficient. The gauge-isotopy argument uses simple connectedness to lift a circle-valued gauge function. These requirements were retained in tracing the proof.\par
Locally uniform noncollapse does not imply a positive asymptotic volume ratio. Oscillation between the stated large-scale exponents does not alone contradict volume comparison: the coefficients and relevant scales vary. Likewise, an arbitrary nonmetric flat Ehresmann connection is not a counterexample to the existential choice of compatible orthogonal bundle data. These possible objections were not promoted to findings.\par
The supported outcome is Major for the proof defects P1 and P2, with separate Minor findings P3 and P4. The main manifold has not been disproved. P2 has the local repair above; P1 still needs a full replacement exhaustion construction.
