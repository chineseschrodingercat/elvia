Abstract: The change of measure stated in Theorem 1.12 has an incorrect constant: for one particle it gives a deterministic density below one, although the two chains have identical unit jump rates. The proof supplies the corrected constant. The unrestricted parameter range of the stronger asymptotic statement, Theorem 4.1, also fails: the finite-n sine prefactor cannot be replaced uniformly when p = k/n is unbounded. This does not refute the fixed-p limit of Theorem 1.4. A further factor one half is missing from an intermediate mixed determinantal identity; it cancels in the normalized correlation ratio. Exact algebra and finite diagnostics support the stated boundaries of these findings. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Identity, definitions and scope

The inspected source is Samuel G. G. Johnston, Continuous Kasteleyn theory for the bead model, arXiv:2207.13538v6, 18 September 2025, 49 PDF pages [source]. The journal identifier is 10.4171/jems/1728 (https://doi.org/10.4171/jems/1728). All locations below refer to the retained preprint. Equation reference: eq:delta. A bead configuration on \(\mathbb T_n=[0,1)\times\mathbb Z_n\) has \(k\) beads on each string, neighboring strings interlace, and its occupation number is \(\ell\). The volume \(\operatorname{Vol}^{(n)}_{k,\ell}\) measures the corresponding ordered configurations. The empty case has separately defined weights \(\operatorname{Vol}^{(n)}_{0,\ell}=\binom n\ell\). The infinite-horizontal process has \(1\leq\ell\leq n-1\) particles, state \(E\subset\mathbb Z_n\), and \[
 \Delta(E)=\prod_{1\leq j<r\leq\ell}
 \left|e^{2\pi i h_r/n}-e^{2\pi i h_j/n}\right|,
 \qquad E=\{h_1,\ldots,h_\ell\}.\] An allowed jump replaces \(h\in E\) by \(h+1\notin E\), with addition modulo \(n\).

The free-probability discussion in Section 2 and its external references were not independently re-proved. # The probability density in Theorem 1.12

Equation reference: eq:printedRN. Problem 1 (A per-string factor in a global change of measure).  Theorem 1.12, page 9, states \[
 \left.\frac{d\mathbb P^{n,\ell,\mathrm{TASEP}}}{d\mathbb P^{n,\ell}}
 \right|_{\mathcal F_t}
 =\frac{\Delta(X_0)}{\Delta(X_t)}
 \exp\left\{\int_0^t
       \bigl(\operatorname{Traffic}(X_s)-c_{n,\ell}\bigr)\,ds\right\},\] with \[c_{n,\ell}=\ell-\frac1n\mu_{n,\ell},
 \qquad
 \mu_{n,\ell}=\frac{\sin(\pi\ell/n)}{\sin(\pi/n)}.\] For the rates actually specified in the paper, the correct constant is \(c_{n,\ell}=\ell-\mu_{n,\ell}\).

## A complete admissible counterexample

Take any \(n\geq2\), \(\ell=1\), and the same fixed initial state for both chains. This is permitted by Definition 1.9. Every state is a singleton, so the empty product in [eq:delta] (#eq:delta) is \(1\), and \(\operatorname{Traffic}(E)=\#\{h\in E:h+1\in E\}=0\). Theorem 1.11, page 8, gives the rate \(\Delta(E')/\Delta(E)=1\). The TASEP rate is explicitly \(1\) in (1.24), page 9, and in the generator (6.22), page 45. Both chains therefore have generator \[Gf(h)=f(h+1)-f(h)\] and identical laws. Their Radon–Nikodym derivative is identically \(1\).

In contrast, \(\mu_{n,1}=1\), so the right side of [eq:printedRN] (#eq:printedRN) is the deterministic quantity \[\exp\{-(1-1/n)t\}<1\qquad(t>0).\] It has total mass below one and cannot be the claimed probability density or unit-mean martingale. For \(n=2\) it is \(e^{-t/2}\); at \(t=1\) the retained calculation gives approximately \(0.60653066\). The contradiction is exact and does not depend on that numerical approximation. Starting both chains from their common uniform one-particle distribution gives the same contradiction.

## Correction and counterevidence

Equation reference: eq:totalrate. The source proof already provides the needed normalization. Lemma 6.7 and (6.26)–(6.27), page 46, give \[\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{E':\,E\to E'}\frac{\Delta(E')}{\Delta(E)}
 &=\mu_{n,\ell},\\
 G_{\mathrm{TASEP}}\Delta(E)
 &=\bigl(\mu_{n,\ell}-\ell+\operatorname{Traffic}(E)\bigr)\Delta(E).\end{aligned}\] For identical initial laws, the product of the jump-rate ratios along a path is \(\Delta(X_0)/\Delta(X_t)\). The difference of total holding rates is \(\mu_{n,\ell}-(\ell-\operatorname{Traffic}(E))\). The finite-state path likelihood ratio is therefore exactly [eq:printedRN] (#eq:printedRN) with \(c_{n,\ell}=\ell-\mu_{n,\ell}\). This agrees with the reciprocal change of measure at the end of the proof.

The factor \(1/n\) does occur legitimately in the per-string bead density in Theorem 1.10, page 8. It does not occur in either global generator. Actual source images confirm the distinction, so neither an extraction error nor a justified common time rescaling removes the counterexample.

This is Major: a stated principal theorem gives an invalid probability-density normalization. The proof supports a short, fully checked correction. The other determinant formulas and the construction of the process are not refuted by this example. Author adoption of the correction is unknown.

# The unrestricted range in Theorem 4.1

Equation reference: eq:uniform. Problem 2 (The sine-prefactor approximation is not uniform in \(p\)).  Theorem 4.1, page 23, puts \(p=k/n\), \(\tau=\ell/n\) and asserts \[
 \left(\frac{e\sin(\pi\ell/n)}{\pi}\right)^{-nk}
 k^{nk}\operatorname{Vol}^{(n)}_{k,\ell}
 =
 \frac{e^{p\pi^2/6}}{\sqrt{2\pi p}}
 \left[
 \mathcal P(e^{-pq_\tau^-})\mathcal P(e^{-pq_\tau^+})
 +O(e^{-cp\tau\log n})
 \right]\] whenever \(n^{-1/4}\leq\tau\leq1-n^{-1/4}\) and \(p\geq n^{-1/4}\). Here \(q_\tau^\pm=2\pi^2\pm2\pi^2i\cot(\pi\tau)\) and \(\mathcal P\) is the integer-partition generating function. There is no upper bound on \(p\), and the ensuing text specifies universal constants. The claimed range is incompatible with the exact volume formula.

## An admissible family and the exact dominant contribution

Equation reference: eq:volume. Take even \(n\geq16\), \(\ell=n/2\), and positive integer \(k\). Then \(\tau=1/2\) satisfies both stated bounds, and \(k\) may be arbitrarily large. Set \(N=nk\) and \[M_n=\frac1{\sin(\pi/n)}.\] Corollary 1.3, page 5, gives \[
 \operatorname{Vol}^{(n)}_{k,\ell}
 =\frac{(-1)^{k(\ell+1)}}{N!}
 \sum_{\substack{S\subset\{z:z^n=1\}\\|S|=\ell}}
       \left(\sum_{z\in S}z\right)^N .\] The coefficient extraction, parity and complement signs leading to this formula on pages 22–23 were checked.

For \(\ell=n/2\), the \(n\) consecutive blocks of roots maximize the modulus of the inner sum, with value \(M_n\). This can also be seen by projecting any root sum onto its own direction: the \(\ell\) largest projections select consecutive roots. Equality at the maximum gives a consecutive block. Formula (4.14), page 23, shows that the \(N\)-th powers of all these blocks have the same sign, which is canceled by the prefactor in [eq:volume] (#eq:volume). Every other block has strictly smaller modulus.

Equation reference: eq:dominance. Thus, for each such \(n\), there are \(0\leq\rho_n<1\) and finite \[B_n=\frac{\binom n{n/2}-n}{n}\] such that the exact formula implies \[
 \operatorname{Vol}^{(n)}_{k,n/2}
 =\frac{nM_n^N}{N!}(1+\varepsilon_{n,k}),
 \qquad
 |\varepsilon_{n,k}|\leq B_n\rho_n^N.\] This bound controls possible complex cancellations in the nonmaximal terms; it does not assume that each term is positive.

## Contradiction with the claimed uniform estimate

Equation reference: eq:ratio. Write the Stirling remainder as \[N!=\sqrt{2\pi N}(N/e)^N e^{r_N},
 \qquad 0<r_N<\frac1{12N}.\] Using [eq:dominance] (#eq:dominance), the ratio of the left side of [eq:uniform] (#eq:uniform) to \(e^{p\pi^2/6}/\sqrt{2\pi p}\) is \[
 (1+\varepsilon_{n,k})\exp\{pA_n-r_N\},
 \qquad
 A_n=n^2\log\frac{\pi/n}{\sin(\pi/n)}-\frac{\pi^2}{6}.\] For \(n\geq16\), the convergent expansion of \(-\log(\sin x/x)\) gives \[A_n=\frac{\pi^4}{180n^2}+O(n^{-4})>0.\] For every even \(n\), choose an integer \(k=k_n\) sufficiently large that \[B_n\rho_n^{nk_n}\leq\frac1n,\qquad
 \frac{k_n}{n}A_n\geq n,\qquad k_n\geq n^3.\] Such integers exist, and the resulting family satisfies every printed parameter condition. By [eq:ratio] (#eq:ratio), its actual ratio tends to infinity.

However, at \(\tau=1/2\) the bracket asserted on the right of [eq:uniform] (#eq:uniform) is \[\mathcal P(e^{-2\pi^2p})^2
 +O(e^{-cp\log n/2})\longrightarrow1.\] This contradicts the unrestricted estimate for every universal \(c>0\). The counterexample concerns the parameter range of the theorem, not a floating-point instability. Retained high-precision calculations of the sine prefactor are corroborating diagnostics only.

## Scope of the failure

The last step of the proof, page 29, uses \[\left(\frac{\sin(\pi/n)}{\pi/n}\right)^{pn^2}
 =(1+o(1))e^{-p\pi^2/6}.\] Its logarithmic error begins at order \(p/n^2\), so this replacement is not uniform over all lower-bounded \(p\). Retaining the exact finite-\(n\) sine and factorial prefactor avoids this particular loss; it does not by itself prove a corrected uniform bound for the remaining terms.

In contrast, Theorem 1.4, page 5, fixes \(p>0\) and \(\tau\in(0,1)\) before sending \(n\) to infinity. The present obstruction then vanishes. Neither that fixed-parameter conclusion nor its free-energy corollary is disproved by this argument, and this report does not certify their other analytic steps. The stronger uniform guarantee in Theorem 4.1 is Major within the advertised fine-volume asymptotics. A complete repaired parameter range and remainder have not been verified.

# A local factor in the mixed determinant

Problem 3 (Theorem 5.2 omits \(1/2\)).  The mixed determinant formula in Theorem 5.2, page 31, and Lemma 5.4, page 34, omit the factor \(1/2\) present in the defining density (5.11), page 30, and in the unmixed formula (3.12), page 17.

Take \(n=2\), \(\theta=(1,1)\), \(\lambda=\log2\), and one occupied observation \(w=(o,t,0)\), with no beads. Thus \(N=1\), \(k=0\), \(\ell_o=1\), \(\ell_u=0\). A single time satisfies the distinct-times condition, and all kernel denominators are nonzero. Let \(\beta=\lambda+\pi i\); since \([0]_o=1\), (5.13) gives \[C^{\beta,1}(w,w)
 =-\frac{e^{-\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}}
 =\frac13.\] The left side printed in Theorem 5.2 equals \[(1+\tfrac12)^2\cdot\tfrac13=\frac34,\] while the density in (5.11) equals \[g_1^{\lambda,\theta}(w)
 =\frac12\cdot\frac12(1+\tfrac12)=\frac38.\] This is a nonempty determinant, so the mismatch cannot be explained by a convention for \(0\times0\) determinants. Exact rational arithmetic confirms the discrepancy.

The left sides of Theorem 5.2 and Lemma 5.4 need the prefactor \(1/2\); the associated prefactors in (5.29)–(5.30), page 38, require the same correction. The constant then cancels between numerator and denominator in the normalized ratio (5.31). Consequently this check does not show that Theorem 1.7’s normalized correlation kernel is false. Its bounded assessment is Minor: a local intermediate normalization error with the cancellation explicitly verified.

# Supporting checks and overall impact

The elementary interlacing determinant in Lemma 3.3 was checked by row operations, including the cyclic alternative. Independent exact arithmetic also checked 580 small cases with weak coincidences and \(\zeta\in\{0,1,2/3,2\}\). The three-versus-one sign identity and (3.23) agree algebraically; 840 finite parity cases and seven empty-configuration partition identities passed.

For \(k=1\), every strict ordering of the \(n\) bead times gives a simplex of volume \(1/n!\). Counting its cyclic descents independently determines \(\ell\). For \(n=2,\ldots,7\), these exact counts agree with numerical evaluations of [eq:volume] (#eq:volume) for every \(\ell\), including the zero-volume endpoints, to absolute error below \(6\cdot10^{-16}\). For \(n=2,\ldots,8\), all 494 nontrivial occupancy states give [eq:totalrate] (#eq:totalrate) within \(1.8\cdot10^{-15}\); the stationary weights \(\Delta(E)^2/n^\ell\) sum to one within \(9\cdot10^{-16}\). These finite diagnostics corroborate the specific calculations. They do not prove a universal theorem.

The first finding concerns a false normalization in a principal probability theorem, with a correction supplied by its own proof. The second concerns the stronger asymptotic range, while preserving the distinction from a fixed-\(p\) limit. The third is local and cancels in the normalized kernel formula. The highest supported assessment is Major; it is not a claim that every result of the paper fails.
