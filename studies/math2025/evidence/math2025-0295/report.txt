Abstract: This report reviews Jan Nienhaus and Matthias Wink’s exact author manuscript arXiv:2303.04832v3, associated with DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1668. The proof’s coordinate changes, cone linearization, angle barrier and intersection mechanism were examined. Two local defects are confirmed: a missing compactness restriction in the preliminary geometric discussion, and an unjustified assertion about repeated entry into a disk in the rotation lemma. Explicit examples establish both defects. A first-arrival truncation repairs the latter argument without changing the intersection bound used in the main theorem. The supported assessment is Minor; no counterexample to the three-metric existence theorem was found. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Main argument and independent checks

Theorem A on p. 1 asserts at least three pairwise non-isometric, non-round positive-scalar-curvature Einstein metrics on \(S^{10}\). The construction uses \[g=dt^2+f_1(t)^2g_{S^{d_1}}+f_2(t)^2g_{S^{d_2}},
 \qquad (d_1,d_2)\in\{(2,7),(3,6),(4,5)\}.\] The smooth endpoint conditions (5)–(6), p. 7, collapse different sphere factors at the two ends. The cited singular initial-value result supplies the one-parameter family with \(f_1(0)=0\), \(\dot f_1(0)=1\), \(f_2(0)>0\), \(\dot f_2(0)=0\). Endpoint smoothness is substantive, not merely positivity of the interior warping functions.

For \(n=d_1+d_2\), the rescaled variables reduce the equations to the compact invariant region \(\mathcal S\). Writing \(\Delta=X_1-X_2\), equation (9), p. 10, gives \[\begin{aligned}
 Z'&=\frac{2\Delta}{n}
 \left(d_1d_2 Z\Delta H+\frac{d_1d_2}{n}\Delta^2
       -\frac{n-1}{n}+(d_1-d_2)Z\right),\\
 \Delta'&=\frac{\Delta H}{n}
             \bigl(d_1d_2\Delta^2-(n-1)\bigr)+Z,\\
 H'&=-\frac{1-H^2}{n}(d_1d_2\Delta^2+1).\end{aligned}\] Thus \(H\) is strictly decreasing in the interior. A trajectory from \(p_1^+\) to \(p_2^-\) crosses \(H=0\) exactly once; the reflection \((Z,\Delta,H)\mapsto(Z,-\Delta,-H)\) gives the intersection description in Lemma 2.12.

Independent symbolic calculations reproduced the change from the \(Y_i\) equations to \(Z'\), the weighted-square constraint, and \[\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathcal F=2d_1d_2\Delta^2H\] in the interior. At \(p_1^+\) the computed eigenvalues are \(2/d_1\) twice and \(-(d_1-1)/d_1\), for each of the three pairs. At the positive cone base point with \(n=9\), the transverse matrix is \[\begin{pmatrix}0&-16/81\\1&-8/9\end{pmatrix},\] with double eigenvalue \(-4/9\) and eigenvector \((4,9)\). This agrees with the direction used in Corollary 2.17.

The angle equation is \[\varphi'=\cos^2\varphi+\frac{16}{81}\sin^2\varphi
              -\frac89 h\sin\varphi\cos\varphi,\qquad
 h(s)=-\tanh(s/9).\] Its completed-square form in (12) was independently verified. The four comparisons in Proposition 4.2 and the uniform perturbation in Lemma 4.3 were read with attention to reversing \(s\) versus \(h\). The comparison point is \[h_*=\tanh\bigl(\operatorname{artanh}(1/2)+\pi/8\bigr).\] Its decimal evaluation, and that of \(h_*+1/4\), are retained as diagnostics; the proof uses the explicit inequalities, not plot resolution.

For the helicoidal barrier in Proposition 5.1, direct symbolic linearization of the normal component gives \[\langle N,E\rangle
       =\frac{1-h^2}{9}\bigl(\delta r+O(r^2)\bigr).\] The positive leading coefficient was checked for all three pairs. The invariant \(\mathcal F\)-tube then keeps nearby trajectories on the required side. The resulting strict angle bounds exceed \(2\pi\) on each unreflected curve; after reflection the difference exceeds \(4\pi\). The rotation argument yields at least two intersections, one round and at least one non-round. Remark 5.2 distinguishes the three non-round metrics by the topology of their principal orbits and the cited classification of homogeneous Einstein metrics. No claim of exactly three such metrics is proved; the stronger numerical uniqueness discussion on pp. 25–27 remains conjectural.

# Confirmed local findings

Problem 1 (Missing restriction in the preliminary discussion).  Section 1.1, p. 6, introduces an arbitrary Riemannian manifold with an isometric cohomogeneity-one action, then asserts that an Einstein metric is flat or has positive scalar curvature. Compactness is not included in those stated hypotheses. The assertion is false at that level of generality.

## Decisive example and all hypotheses

For any \(n\ge2\), take hyperbolic space \[M=\mathbb H^{n+1},\qquad
 g=dr^2+\sinh^2(r)\,g_{S^n}.\] The polar expression extends smoothly across \(r=0\), since \(\sinh r=r+O(r^3)\), and gives a complete manifold. The group \(\mathrm{SO}(n+1)\) acts isometrically by rotations, with principal orbits \(S^n\), one singular center and one-dimensional orbit space \([0,\infty)\). For \(r>0\), the radial and tangential sectional curvatures are \[-\frac{(\sinh r)''}{\sinh r}=-1,\qquad
 \frac{1-((\sinh r)')^2}{\sinh^2r}=-1.\] They extend across the center. Hence \(\operatorname{Ric}=-ng\), so this is neither flat nor of positive scalar curvature. The curvature identities were also checked exactly by the independent script.

## Impact and correction boundary

The counterexample satisfies the written preliminary hypotheses, even completeness and compactness of the acting group. It is noncompact, and therefore does not satisfy the later sphere setting explicitly imposed in Section 1.2, p. 7. The narrow correction is to state the compact setting at the start of Section 1.1, or to restrict its curvature conclusion to the closed spheres under consideration. Completeness must accompany any general invocation of Myers’ theorem.

For the actual \(S^{10}\) setting, the positivity restriction does not rely on the erroneous noncompact generalization. A nonzero Killing field on a closed Einstein manifold satisfies the integrated Bochner identity \(\int|\nabla X|^2=\int\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)\), excluding negative Einstein constant. If the constant were zero, every Killing field would be parallel; a nonzero parallel field cannot exist on \(S^{10}\), whose Euler characteristic is 2. The group action supplies nonzero Killing fields. The issue is therefore Minor: its demonstrated effect is on the scope of the preliminary statement, not on the positive-curvature construction on the compact sphere. No adoption of this correction by the authors has been established.

Problem 2 (The disk-entry assertion in Lemma 3.4’s proof).  In the first paragraph of the proof of Lemma 3.4, p. 16, continuity is used to conclude that both simple curves enter each sufficiently small disk just once and remain there. Continuity and injectivity do not imply that assertion.

## An example within the lemma’s curve hypotheses

Identify \(\mathbb R^2\) with \(\mathbb C\). For \(0\le t<1\), put \(u=1-t\), \(R=2+\sin1\), and define \[\alpha(t)=u\bigl(2+\sin(1/u)\bigr)e^{iu},
 \qquad
 \beta(t)=Ru\,e^{i(2-u)}.\] Both curves are nonzero and continuous, have the same initial point \(Re^{i}\), and extend to zero at \(t=1\). Each is injective because its angle is strictly monotone through an interval of length less than \(2\pi\). Their interiors are disjoint: their respective angular ranges are \((0,1)\) and \((1,2)\). Taking \(\gamma_1=\beta\), \(\gamma_2=\alpha\), their truncated angles are \(1-u\) and \(u-1\). For all sufficiently small \(u>0\) these are at least \(1/2\) and at most \(-1/2\), respectively. Thus the eventual inequalities in Definition 3.3 hold with \(\theta_0=1\). Their limits \(1\) and \(-1\) alone would not justify the endpoint choice \(\theta_0=2\). The example does not contradict the lemma’s intersection bound; it contradicts the asserted truncation property.

Indeed, let \(0<\rho<1/(2\pi)\). Choose an integer \(k\) such that \[x=2\pi k+\pi/2\in[2/\rho,\,2/\rho+2\pi).\] At phases \(1/u=x-\pi,x,x+\pi\), the radii of \(\alpha\) are \[\frac1{x-\pi}<\rho,\qquad
 \frac3x>\rho,\qquad
 \frac1{x+\pi}<\rho.\] The inequalities follow from \(x-\pi>1/\rho\) and \(x<3/\rho\). The initial radius is larger than \(\rho\). As \(t\) increases, the curve therefore enters, exits, and enters this disk again. This holds for every such \(\rho\), not merely for isolated radii. All of the curve assumptions, including finite mutual intersection, have been retained.

## Verified replacement of the truncation step

The proof can instead use the first arrival at the radius. For each curve and sufficiently small \(r>0\), set \[t_i(r)=\min\{t\in[a_i,b_i):|\gamma_i(t)|=r\}.\] This time exists by continuity and convergence to zero, and \(|\gamma_i(t)|>r\) for \(a_i\le t<t_i(r)\). Moreover \(t_i(r)\to b_i\) as \(r\downarrow0\): for every \(\eta>0\), the compact initial segment \(\gamma_i([a_i,b_i-\eta])\) has a strictly positive minimum distance from the origin. Choosing \(r\) below that minimum forces the first arrival to occur after \(b_i-\eta\).

Retain only these initial segments, irrespective of later exits. Consequently the eventual angle bounds of Definition 3.3 still hold at the truncation times. In particular this applies to the two separate strict bounds used on pp. 22–24. The retained prefixes are strictly outside the circle except at their endpoints. A positively oriented circular arc joining those endpoints adds a nonnegative angle and introduces no intersection with the prefixes. For small \(r\), the endpoints are distinct since the mutual intersections are finite and away from zero. Thus the closed-curve argument has exactly the same allowed source of self-intersections and the same winding lower bound as required in the original proof.

This replacement removes the false assertion without adding radial monotonicity, endpoint differentiability, or a stronger hypothesis on the Einstein trajectories. In the main application, the strict difference remains greater than \(4\pi\), hence the integer winding number is at least 3 and the required intersection count remains at least 2. This is why the issue is assessed as Minor: a locally verified correction preserves the actual core count. No false rotation-lemma conclusion or false metric-existence conclusion is asserted. Author adoption is unknown.
