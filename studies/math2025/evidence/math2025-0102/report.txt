Abstract: This report checks labeled-shape definitions, stabilizer quotients, normalization, and selected geometric inputs in the publisher version of Arana–Herrera and Calderon’s paper. An exact finite ribbon graph gives a genus-three witness against the full-stabilizer invariance asserted for arbitrary labeled-shape weights. The defect enters the construction and unfolding used for the principal equidistribution statement. The later oriented formulation identifies consistent repairs, whose definitions and counting identities are verified here. A second explicit hyperbolic family refutes the general center-stability assertion in Lemma 4.8; a verified estimate with both inradii bounded repairs its compact-surface application. Separately, hyperbolic pairs of pants contradict the ratio-one asymptotic in Lemma 2.5. The findings have different demonstrated impacts; they do not establish that a consistently oriented or unlabeled equidistribution theorem is false. Scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Definitions and essential dependencies

In Section 1.1, curves are unoriented free homotopy classes, and \(s(X,\gamma,L)\) counts their mapping-class orbit up to length \(L\). In Section 2.6, \(\mathcal{MRG}_{h,b}\) has distinctly labeled boundary components. Cutting a nonseparating curve in genus \(g\) gives type \((g-1,2)\). Write \[Z=\mathcal{MRG}_{g-1,2}(1,1),\qquad \tau:Z\longrightarrow Z\] for the involution exchanging its boundary labels. An orientation of the curve distinguishes its sides. The full stabilizer \(\operatorname{Stab}(\gamma)\) contains orientation-reversing-on-the-curve mapping classes, whereas \(\operatorname{Stab}_0(\gamma)\) preserves that orientation.

The realization of positive ribbon graphs uses the fixed-boundary spine homeomorphism stated in Theorem 2.4 (p. 582). The measure argument uses Theorem 2.7, the polynomial total-volume asymptotic, and earthquake-flow ergodicity. The finite calculation below does not replace these background results. Necessary external source statements and their applicable hypotheses are separately bound in the dependency checks.

# A labeled shape does not descend through the full stabilizer

Problem 1 (Core definition and descent defect; Major).  For arbitrary nonnegative \(h\in C_c(Z)\), the measure in (5.1) is asserted to be \(\operatorname{Stab}(\gamma)\)-invariant and is then pushed locally to \(\mathcal T_g/\operatorname{Stab}(\gamma)\). This is false under the paper’s explicit labeled-boundary convention.

## Source and core dependency

The relevant statements are the unoriented convention on p. 573; the single Dirac mass \(\delta_{\operatorname{RSC}_\alpha(X)}\) and Theorem 1.1 on p. 574; the labeled target on p. 578; and the invariance assertion immediately after (5.1) on p. 597. Proposition 6.6 (pp. 610–611) uses the same quotient when replacing the count by a coset sum and unfolding. The issue therefore affects a required step of the main proof, rather than only an auxiliary description.

## An explicit positive metric graph

Take the cube graph with the following edge order: \[(01),(02),(04),(13),(15),(23),(26),(37),(45),(46),(57),(67).\] Specify its ribbon structure by these cyclic neighbor orders: \[\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
v&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\ \hline
\text{order}&124&035&036&127&056&147&742&653 .
\end{array}\] For example, \(124\) means \((1,2,4)\). The boundary permutation sends \((u,v)\) to \((v,w)\), where \(w\) follows \(u\) at \(v\). Its two cycles, listed by successive vertices and then closed, are \[\begin{split}
 F_1&=(0,1,3,2,6,7,5,1,0,2,3,7,6,4),\\
 F_2&=(2,0,4,5,7,3,1,5,4,6).
\end{split}\] They exhaust all 24 directed edges. Since \(V=8\), \(E=12\), and \(b=2\), the thickened graph satisfies \(2-2h-b=V-E=-4\), so \(h=2\).

In the stated edge order assign \[w=(1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,2081,512,1024,2048).\] All lengths are positive. With \(C=2+4+8+16+64+128+512+1024=1758\), the two perimeters are \[P_1=C+2(1+32+2048)=5920,\qquad
 P_2=C+2(2081)=5920.\] Division by 5920 gives \(G\in\mathcal{MRG}_{2,2}(1,1)\). Any ribbon-graph automorphism preserves the number of directed edges in a boundary cycle, but here the counts are 14 and 10. Hence \(G\ne\tau G\). Independently, the distinct lengths force every metric automorphism to fix all edges, and then all trivalent vertices.

## Hypotheses and failure of invariance

Set the ambient genus to \(g=3\). The fixed-length spine homeomorphism realizes \(G\) as a compact hyperbolic \(Y\) of genus two with two geodesic boundaries of length one. This application was checked in Luo’s published Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.4 [Luo]: all fundamental-cycle sums are positive because every coordinate is positive, and each boundary-cycle sum is one with multiplicity. The coordinate formula and canonical-spine interpretation in Mondello’s Definition 2.8 and Theorem 2.14 [Mondello] confirm that these are the full boundary-projection widths, with no factor of two. Gluing them by an orientation-reversing isometry gives a closed oriented genus-three hyperbolic surface \(X\). The glued geodesic \(\gamma\) is essential, simple, has length one, and is nonseparating because cutting it recovers connected \(Y\).

Choose an orientation of the reference curve to define a labeled cut on Teichmüller space. A mapping class \(\phi\in\operatorname{Stab}(\gamma)\setminus\operatorname{Stab}_0(\gamma)\) reverses that orientation and exchanges its sides. Thus \[\operatorname{RSC}_\gamma(\phi X)=\tau\operatorname{RSC}_\gamma(X).\] Choose a relatively compact neighborhood \(U\) of \(G\) in its trivalent cell, with \(U\cap\tau U=\varnothing\), and a nonnegative \(h\in C_c(U)\) equal to one near \(G\). Then \[h(G)=1,\qquad h(\tau G)=0,\qquad
 \int_Zh\,d\eta_{\operatorname{Kon}}>0.\] The last assertion uses the positive volume form on the cell. For cutoff \(L=2\), the length indicators in (5.1) at \(X\) and \(\phi X\) both equal one, whereas the weighted densities equal one and zero. This difference persists on an open Teichmüller neighborhood of positive Weil–Petersson measure, by continuity of length and the labeled spine. Since the Weil–Petersson measure and length cutoff are \(\phi\)-invariant, the weighted measure is not. The claimed full-stabilizer descent fails for an allowed \(h\).

An arbitrary single-label choice per unoriented curve does not repair Theorem 1.1. For every curve choose a labeling outside \(U\); such a choice always exists because \(U\cap\tau U=\varnothing\). The resulting \(h\)-weighted count is zero for every \(L\), while the claimed limiting integral is positive. This tests a continuous compactly supported function. It does not infer failure of vague convergence from total mass alone.

## Counterevidence and verified repair scope

Section 7.3 (p. 614) explicitly identifies the orientation obstruction and introduces ordered oriented curves and \(\operatorname{Stab}_0\). Remark 7.6 also expressly discusses the larger oriented orbit. These passages provide substantial evidence for the intended repair.

For one nonseparating curve, ordering has no effect and \[s_{\rm or}(X,\vec\gamma,L)=2s_{\rm un}(X,\gamma,L).\] For \(g\ge3\), Definition 7.3 and the scaling degree imply \[m_{\vec\gamma}=\frac{c_g}{6g-6}
       =2\,\frac{c_g}{12g-12}=2c(\gamma).\] The literal unoriented definition of \(s\) in Section 7.6 must therefore be reconciled with the oriented interpretation of Theorem 7.5 and the oriented numerator in Theorem 7.7. This linked convention correction is not counted as a separate Major.

Three definitions remove the demonstrated obstruction:

1. Use \(Z/\langle\tau\rangle\) for the unoriented cut, with limiting probability \(q_*(\eta_{\operatorname{Kon}}/c_g)\).

2. Assign \(\tfrac12(\delta_{G_\alpha}+\delta_{\tau G_\alpha})\) to each unoriented curve in the labeled target. This measure is independent of the initial labeling and has total mass \(s_{\rm un}\).

3. Count oriented curves, use \(\operatorname{Stab}_0\) throughout, and normalize by \(s_{\rm or}\). For a general multicurve use its actual stabilizer index, rather than assuming a universal \(2^k k!\).

The second formulation tests \(h_{\rm av}=(h+h\circ\tau)/2\). For arbitrary labeled \(h\), the oriented horoball pushforward equals twice the unoriented pushforward weighted by \(h_{\rm av}\); the unaveraged formula in Remark 7.9 requires this qualification.

These repairs have been verified for definitions, descent, and exact counting identities. A full reproof of the corrected convergence theorem is not claimed. The Major classification rests on the demonstrated false invariance and its direct use in the core construction and unfolding, independently of the survival of the intended geometric phenomenon.

# The general center-stability bound is false

Problem 2 (Uniform geometric lemma used in the core proof; Major).  Lemma 4.8 (p. 593) asserts that an \(e^\varepsilon\)-bi-Lipschitz map moves the center of three pairwise disjoint, mutually nonseparating geodesics a distance bounded by \(D(r,\varepsilon)\) from the center of the straightened image triple, where \(r\) is the input inradius. This bound fails without a bound on the target inradius.

The proof bounds the distance of \(f(u)\) from all three image geodesics and then asserts the same bound for their center \(u'\). An equidistant center need not minimize the maximum distance to the three lines. The following counterfamily addresses all the stated geometric and mapping hypotheses.

## A triple whose center escapes

Use the hyperboloid model with form \(\langle x,y\rangle=-x_0y_0+x_1y_1+x_2y_2\). Fix \(a=2\), \(S=\sinh a\), and \(h_*=\log S\). For \(h\in(h_*,a]\), take geodesics with spacelike unit normals \[n_1=(S,\cosh a,0),\quad n_2=(S,-\cosh a,0),\quad
 n_3(h)=(\sinh h,0,\cosh h),\] and central halfplanes \(\langle x,n_i\rangle<0\). The normal products are \[\langle n_1,n_2\rangle=-\cosh(2a)<-1,\qquad
 \langle n_j,n_3(h)\rangle=-S\sinh h<-1.\] Indeed the least limiting product magnitude is \((S^2-1)/2>1\). The lines are ultraparallel. Each line’s two companions lie in its central halfplane, so none separates the others.

Their central equidistant point and inradius are \[u_h=(\cosh t_h,0,-\sinh t_h),\qquad
 t_h=\operatorname{artanh}\frac{S-\sinh h}{\cosh h},
 \qquad r_h=h+t_h .\] The fraction belongs to \([0,1)\). Direct substitution gives \[d(u_h,g_1)=d(u_h,g_2)
   =\operatorname{arsinh}(S\cosh t_h)=h+t_h=d(u_h,g_3(h)).\] All central sign conditions hold, so the closed ball of radius \(r_h\) is contained in the central region and tangent to all three lines. In particular, these are genuine finite inscribed circles. Since \(e^{h_*}=S\), one has \(t_h\to\infty\) as \(h\downarrow h_*\).

## Uniformly bi-Lipschitz maps, including arbitrarily small distortion

In the Poincaré disk, a line at distance \(d\) from the origin, in direction \(\beta\), has ideal endpoints \(\beta\pm\theta_d\), where \(\theta_d=\arccos(\tanh d)\). Here \(\theta_{h_*}=2\theta_a\) and \(3\theta_a<\pi/2\).

First fix \(h_0\in(h_*,a]\). The input triple has third parameter \(h_0\), input center \(u_{h_0}\), and fixed finite radius \(r_{h_0}\). Let \(h_n\downarrow h_*\) with \(h_n<h_0\). Define an increasing piecewise linear circle homeomorphism \(\phi_n\) which fixes the east and west line endpoints, sends \(\pi/2\pm\theta_{h_0}\) to \(\pi/2\pm\theta_{h_n}\), is linear on the three intervening northern intervals, and is the identity on the remaining circle. Its nonunit slopes are \[m_n=\frac{\pi/2-\theta_{h_n}-\theta_a}
           {\pi/2-\theta_{h_0}-\theta_a},\qquad
 M_n=\frac{\theta_{h_n}}{\theta_{h_0}}.\] They obey fixed positive bounds \[0<m_*=\frac{\pi/2-2\theta_a-\theta_a}
                 {\pi/2-\theta_{h_0}-\theta_a}
 \le m_n\le1,\qquad
 1\le M_n\le M_*=\frac{2\theta_a}{\theta_{h_0}}.\] The radial extension \[F_n(re^{i\theta})=re^{i\phi_n(\theta)},\qquad F_n(0)=0,\] has radial and angular length factors \(1\) and \(\phi_n'\). The hyperbolic conformal factor is unchanged because the radius is unchanged. Integrating the length bounds across the finitely many break rays, and applying them also to the inverse, proves a global bi-Lipschitz bound \(\max(M_*,m_*^{-1})\), including at the origin.

As \(h_0\downarrow h_*\), both \(m_*,M_*\to1\). Thus for any prescribed \(\varepsilon>0\) one can fix \(h_0\) so that all \(F_n\) are \(e^\varepsilon\)-bi-Lipschitz. The radius \(r_{h_0}\) may depend on \(\varepsilon\), but is fixed independently of \(n\), as the lemma requires. The south-pointing ray is fixed pointwise, so \[F_n(u_{h_0})=u_{h_0},\qquad
 d(F_n(u_{h_0}),u_{h_n})=t_{h_n}-t_{h_0}\longrightarrow\infty.\] Straightening the images gives exactly the specified target triple. This contradicts \(D(r_{h_0},\varepsilon)<\infty\), even for an arbitrarily small prescribed positive \(\varepsilon\).

The saved reviewer program checks the basic family \(h_0=a\), the normal products, angular slope bounds, and equal-radius identity. For \(h_n=h_*+(a-h_*)/n\), its center distances at \(n=10^2,10^4,10^6,10^8\) are approximately \(2.50729,4.81188,7.11449,9.41707\), with bi-Lipschitz bound below two. The divergence and the small-distortion strengthening are analytic arguments, rather than conclusions inferred from this numerical table.

## A verified bound for the actual application

This defect matters because Proposition 4.2 invokes Lemma 4.8 on pp. 594–595 to control corresponding spine vertices and hence edge weights. The principal averaging argument subsequently uses that estimate. The Major classification concerns this false general lemma and the missing hypothesis in the invoked proof; it does not assert a counterexample to Proposition 4.2 itself.

A sufficient replacement is \[d(f(u),u')\le
 2\max(e^\varepsilon r+\delta(\varepsilon),r'),\] where \(r'\) is the target inradius and \(\delta\) is the two-sided Morse bound. To prove it, both points lie in the intersection of the \(C\)-neighborhoods of the target lines, for the displayed maximum \(C\). That intersection has diameter at most \(2C\). If points \(x,y\) in it were more than \(2C\) apart, each of the three lines would meet their disjoint \(C\)-balls and hence cross the perpendicular bisector of \([x,y]\). The middle of three disjoint lines crossing a common transversal separates the other two, contradicting the hypothesis. This proves the required form of Claim 4.9 directly.

For the compact-surface application, fix \(\varepsilon_0\) and \(s_0=e^{-\varepsilon_0}s\). Compactness of the \(s_0\)-thick part in fixed genus gives a finite diameter bound \(R\). Every point is within \(R\) of the nonempty multicurve, so both source and target spine inradii are at most \(R\). Choose a common Morse bound \(\delta_0\) for \(0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\), and put \[C_0=e^{\varepsilon_0}R+\delta_0,\qquad
 E_0=(e^{\varepsilon_0}+1)R+2C_0 .\] Matched boundary basepoints are within \(E_0\) after applying \(f\). The two endpoint errors and the bi-Lipschitz inequalities give \[e^{-\varepsilon}|e|_X-2E_0
 \le |e|_{X'}\le e^\varepsilon|e|_X+2E_0 .\] Taking \(K_2>\!K_1\) and \(K_2\ge2E_0\) restores the required uniform estimate. Lemma 4.1’s short-arc matching is justified by the separate coarse bounds below. The counterfamily’s target inradii diverge, so it is consistent with this corrected estimate.

# A ratio-one short-arc asymptotic fails

Problem 3 (Quantitative preliminary; Minor).  Lemma 2.5 (p. 583) asserts \[|e|_Y\sim\log(1/\ell_Y(a)),\] and explicitly defines \(\sim\) as ratio tending to one. A symmetric pair of pants gives ratio two.

Equation reference: eq:seam. For \(T>0\), take the hyperbolic pair of pants \(Y_T\) with all three boundary lengths \(2T\). Its spine is a theta graph with every edge weight \(T\), as follows by solving its three perimeter equations. The dual perpendicular seam has length \(a_T\). The right-angled hexagon identity gives \[\cosh a_T
 =\frac{\cosh T+\cosh^2T}{\sinh^2T}
 =\frac{\cosh T}{\cosh T-1},\] or, equivalently, \[
 a_T=2\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\frac{1}{2\sinh(T/2)}\right)
     \sim2e^{-T/2}.\] All boundary lengths and spine weights are positive; \(a_T\) is the shortest orthogeodesic in its class. Both limiting regimes in the lemma hold. Nevertheless, \[\log(1/a_T)=T/2-\log2+o(1),\qquad
 \frac{|e|_{Y_T}}{\log(1/a_T)}\longrightarrow2.\] This is an analytic counterexample, supplemented by the independently executed calculations in `checks/geometry/`. No universal corrected ratio is inferred from this one family.

## Downstream dependence and a bounded correction

The sharp ratio is stronger than what the ensuing coarse estimates need. The actual published Lemma 6.6 of Calderon–Farre, inspected in the geometry check, supplies for spine weight \(c\) and dual length \(a\) \[\min\!\left\{\log3,\,
 2\operatorname{artanh}
 \frac{\tanh(\log\sqrt3)}{\cosh(c/2)}\right\}
 \le a\le\frac{2\pi}{c}.\] In particular, for small \(a\), \[c\ge2\operatorname{arcosh}
       \left(\frac{1/2}{\tanh(a/2)}\right)
     =2\log(1/a)+O(1).\] This is enough for the logarithmic lower bound required in Section 3. For Lemma 4.1, replacing the incorrect exponential comparison by \[2\delta(\varepsilon)+\frac{2\pi e^\varepsilon}{K_1}
       <\log\sqrt3\] provides the needed uniform short-arc threshold by first choosing \(\varepsilon\) small and then \(K_1\) large. These inequalities, rather than a globally asserted ratio two, are the verified substitute.

The demonstrated defect is a false sharp preliminary, but the required coarse downstream bounds can be supplied from the cited dependency. No failure of Proposition 3.1, Lemma 4.1, or Proposition 4.2 is established. The Minor assessment follows this checked dependence, not simply the ease of repair.

# Other checked arguments and resolved technical details

Write \(d=6g-6>0\). The complementary fixed-boundary slice has dimension \(d-2\). Conditional on a correctly defined invariant weight, Proposition 5.4 has twist factor \(\ell\) and symmetry factor \(1/2\): \[\frac12\int_0^L\ell^{d-1}\,d\ell=\frac{L^d}{2d}.\] The \(\sqrt L\) split controls the short part by \(O(L^{d/2})\), and convergence of the normalized integrand controls the long part uniformly in its tail. This yields \(1/(12g-12)\). The paper’s separate total-volume polynomial argument justifies the constant-weight case and the comparison in Corollary 5.8 without extending \(C_c\) convergence to the constant function without justification.

The measure check traces invariance, absolute continuity, and nonescape through the earthquake-flow endgame. Finite approximation of the open cover avoids interchanging an infinite sum with a \(\limsup\). The hypotheses of the necessary horosphere dependency were actually checked for \(n=0,k=1,a_1=1,f=\chi_{[0,1]}\); that source explicitly allows the bounded Borel length cutoff. Its whole proof was not independently reproduced.

For Proposition 6.3, inversion and division of the geometric comparison, using \(\ell_X\ge\sqrt L\), \(0<\varepsilon<1\), and \(e^\varepsilon<3\), give exactly \[e^{-2\varepsilon}\frac{w_Y}{\ell_Y}-\frac{3K}{\sqrt L}
 \le\frac{w_X}{\ell_X}\le
 e^{2\varepsilon}\frac{w_Y}{\ell_Y}+\frac{3K}{\sqrt L}.\] This verifies (6.8)–(6.11) under the stated geometric hypotheses. The shrinking Morse bound was checked in its actual application \(\mathbb H^2\), with a parametrized compactness argument and the inverse-map step for two-sided Hausdorff distance.

The same-facet envelopes used in Section 6 need not be declared continuous to close the averaging argument. On the marked locally finite filling-arc structure, extend the positive edge coordinates by zero to all arc classes, use their sup norm, and take the infimum over mapping classes to define a quotient metric. It is nondegenerate: if the distance between two fixed orbits were zero, sufficiently close representatives would have identical supports, leaving only finitely many permutations of the fixed positive weights. A filling support has finitely many completions, so this metric induces the stated topology and is locally compact. Since the fixed perimeters imply \(\sum_e|e|=1\), the neighborhood \(N_{2\varepsilon,\delta}(x)\) lies in a metric ball of radius \(\rho=e^{2\varepsilon}-1+\delta\). For a nonnegative \(f\in C_c(Z)\), choose a compact neighborhood of its support and a continuous compactly supported cutoff \(\chi\) equal to one on it. For sufficiently small \(\rho\), if \(\omega_f(\rho)\) is a modulus of continuity, the continuous functions \[H^+=(f+\omega_f(\rho)\chi),\qquad
 H^-=(f-\omega_f(\rho))_+\] bound the same-facet supremum and infimum. Their integrals converge to \(\int f\,d\eta_{\operatorname{Kon}}\). Substituting these continuous bounds legitimately applies the stated \(C_c\) results. For fixed \(\varepsilon,\delta\), first let \(L\to\infty\); only then let \(\varepsilon,\delta\to0\), so that each weight is fixed when applying equidistribution. The full support and metric details are retained in the bounded envelope check; no RSC Borel-weight extension is assumed.

Local transcription inconsistencies are recorded without creating additional substantive Problems: the missing \(b\) in the exterior degree in Section 2.7, the reciprocal mass ratio in Lemma 5.9’s proof, and missing \(h\) subscripts in the final Section 5 endgame. Explicit comparison calculations and the correctly stated neighboring results resolve their intended use.
