# Definitions and quantifiers

The main proof fixes the finite elementary abelian group \(G=\mathbb F_2^n\). Entropies use natural logarithms and the continuous convention \(0\log 0=0\). The distance is \[\mathrm d[X;Y]=\mathrm H[X'-Y']-\tfrac12\mathrm H[X']-\tfrac12\mathrm H[Y'],\] where the primed variables are independent with the prescribed marginals (page 4, (1.1)). Thus neither a given coupling of \(X,Y\) nor equality of their underlying sample spaces is an assumption. The conditional distance in (A.14), page 27, averages over product marginal weights. The single common-fibre average in Lemma A.2 is a different expression; the manuscript explicitly distinguishes it. Fibres of zero probability are omitted, so no conditional distribution at an impossible event is needed.

All minima below are over pairs of probability distributions on a fixed finite group. The product of the two closed simplices is compact, and entropy, convolution, and the functional \(\tau\) are continuous up to the boundary. The combinatorial conversion uses a nonempty set \(A\), as required to form \(U_A\); then the doubling assumption implies \(K\ge1\). This is the natural nonempty-set domain of that formulation, also explicit in the inspected Lean theorem. The empty set is not used as a purported counterexample. Point masses, \(n=0\), \(K=1\), and \(k=0\) cause no division by zero in the proof.

# Checked proof chain

## Compactness and the constants 11 and 6

For \(\eta=1/9\), write \[\tau[X_1;X_2]=\mathrm d[X_1;X_2]
       +\eta\mathrm d[X_1^0;X_1]+\eta\mathrm d[X_2^0;X_2].\] Proposition 2.1 forces a minimizer to have \(k=\mathrm d[X_1;X_2]=0\). The zero-distance conclusion of Lemma 2.2 is consistent with the equality case of entropy concavity: \(H(X-X')=H(X)\) forces the law of \(X\) to be invariant under all differences of its support. Its support is a coset of the subgroup generated by those differences, and its law is uniform there. Applying the distance-zero equalities to the two marginals gives the same subgroup. Translation invariance then gives \(U_H\).

The comparison in the proof on page 7 is with the swapped reference pair \((X_2^0,X_1^0)\). With \(d_0=\mathrm d[X_1^0;X_2^0]\), this gives \[\eta\{\mathrm d[X_1^0;U_H]+\mathrm d[X_2^0;U_H]\}
 \le(1+2\eta)d_0,\] hence the sum bound \(11d_0\). The Ruzsa triangle inequality bounds the difference of the two terms by \(d_0\), giving each term at most \(6d_0\). For completeness, that triangle inequality follows by taking independent \(X,Y,Z\), observing \(\mathrm H[X-Y\mid X-Z]\ge\mathrm H[Y]\), and then using \(\mathrm H[X-Y,Y-Z]\le\mathrm H[X-Y]+\mathrm H[Y-Z]\). This checks the needed inequality without executing a proof assistant.

## Fibring and the two estimates

For independent \(Z_1,Z_2\) and a homomorphism \(\pi\), the chain rule gives exactly the nonnegative defect in Proposition 4.1 (page 15): \[\begin{aligned}
 &\mathrm d[Z_1;Z_2]-\mathrm d[\pi Z_1;\pi Z_2]
      -\mathrm d[Z_1\mid\pi Z_1;Z_2\mid\pi Z_2]\notag\\
 &=\mathrm I[Z_1-Z_2:(\pi Z_1,\pi Z_2)\mid\pi(Z_1-Z_2)].\end{aligned}\] The independence assumptions and the coordinate change used for Corollary 4.2 were checked. Product-fibre averaging of the universal minimality inequality gives (3.15); it does not presume that two original conditioning variables are independent.

For Lemma 5.2, the first inequality follows from the entropy-increment inequality of Lemma A.1, and the second from conditioning reducing entropy: \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathrm d[X;Y-Z]-\mathrm d[X;Y]&\le\tfrac12(\mathrm H[Y-Z]-\mathrm H[Y]),\\
 \mathrm d[X;Y\mid Y-Z]-\mathrm d[X;Y]&\le\tfrac12(\mathrm H[Y-Z]-\mathrm H[Z]).\end{aligned}\] Here \(Y,Z\) are independent and \(X\) may be replaced by an independent copy. These formulas reproduce the signs in the bound v2 text. The four reference-distance penalties in the first estimate have cancelling \(\tfrac14(\mathrm H[X_1]-\mathrm H[X_2])\) terms. Thus (3.13) indeed gives \(I_1\le2\eta k\). The second estimate, with \(D=\mathrm d[X_1;X_1]+\mathrm d[X_2;X_2]\), gives \[D\le2k+\frac{2(2\eta k-I_1)}{1-\eta}.\] The denominator is positive. Exchanging \(X_1\) and its independent identically distributed copy preserves the total sum and proves \(I_2=I_3\); equality is not inferred merely from similar notation.

## Endgame and exact numerical margin

Let \(R=2\eta k-I_1\ge0\), and use the variables \(U,V,W,S\) of Section 7. The relation \(U+V+W=0\) uses characteristic two essentially. The checked bounds on pages 21–25 are \[\widetilde\delta\le6\eta k-\frac{1-5\eta}{1-\eta}R,\qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^2\sum_{T\in\{U,V,W\}}
  \bigl(\mathrm d[X_i^0;T\mid S]-\mathrm d[X_i^0;X_i]\bigr)
 \le(6-3\eta)k+3R.\] For the last two costs, \(W'=W+S\); at fixed \(S=s\) this is a fixed translation, so the conditional distance is unchanged. Lemma 7.2 uses the common-fibre BSG bound, then averages the six permutations of the triple. A positive-probability fibre and a permutation no larger than their averages exist. This justifies the existence step without an unwarranted simultaneous choice of unrelated fibres.

Equation reference: margin. Substitution gives \[k\le(8\eta+\eta^2)k-
 \left(\frac{1-5\eta}{1-\eta}(1+\eta/3)-\eta\right)R.\] At \(\eta=1/9\), independent exact rational calculation gives \[
 \frac8{81}k+\frac{11}{27}R\le0.\] Nonnegativity forces \(k=R=0\). This verifies the contradiction required for Proposition 2.1, including its boundary case.

## From entropy to covering

Appendix B uses \(\mathrm d[U_A;U_A]\le\log K\) and the equal-reference case of Theorem 1.8 to get \(\mathrm d[U_A;U_H]\le(11/2)\log K\). The entropy difference bound gives \(K^{-11}|A|\le|H|\le K^{11}|A|\). The largest-atom entropy bound yields a translate with \[|B|:=|A\cap(H+x_0)|\ge K^{-11/2}|A|^{1/2}|H|^{1/2}.\] A maximal disjoint collection of translates \(a+B\), \(a\in A\), has at most \(|A+B|/|B|\) members and covers \(A\) after enlargement by \(B-B\). Since \(B-B\subset H\), this gives at most \(K^{13/2}\sqrt{|A|/|H|}\) cosets. If \(|H|\le|A|\), the bound is \(K^{12}\). Otherwise a subspace \(H'\le H\) with \(|A|/2<|H'|\le|A|\) exists by the powers-of-two subgroup sizes. Refining the cover multiplies its size by less than \(2|H|/|A|\), giving at most \(2K^{12}\). No integrality or endpoint obstruction was found.

# Excluded observations and counterevidence

These observations are not substantive findings under the requested exclusion of typography alone.

On page 16, the first mutual-information expression in the proof of Corollary 4.2 conditions on \(\pi(Z_1)+\pi(Z_2)\); the preceding proposition and the next line require \(\pi(Z_1)-\pi(Z_2)\). Source TeX line 616 contains the plus sign. Replacing that sign gives exactly the stated identity by substitution; in the main characteristic-two application the two forms already coincide. No changed hypothesis or result is needed.

On page 29, (A.22) prints \(\mathrm H[A_2,B_1]\le\mathrm H[A]-\mathrm H[B]\). The correct subadditivity bound is \[\mathrm H[A_2,B_1]\le\mathrm H[A_2]+\mathrm H[B_1]=\mathrm H[A]+\mathrm H[B].\] Both the source TeX label `bsg-25` and the image confirm the minus. To test the literal printed line, take independent uniform bits \(A,B\) in \(\mathbb F_2\), \(Z=A+B\), and construct conditionally independent trials by choosing \(Z,A_1,A_2\) independent uniform bits and setting \(B_i=Z-A_i\). Each \((A_i,B_i)\) has the required original law, and the two pairs are independent conditional on \(Z\). Then \((A_2,B_1)\) is uniform on four points, so its entropy is \(2\log2\), whereas the printed right side is zero. The corrected right side is \(2\log2\).

The correction suffices in full: (A.20) gives \(2\mathrm H[A,B]-\mathrm H[Z]\) for the middle joint entropy in (A.19); the corrected (A.22) and (A.21) each give \(\mathrm H[A]+\mathrm H[B]\). Consequently \(\mathrm H[A_1-B_2]\le2\mathrm I[A:B]+\mathrm H[Z]\). Conditioning and \(\mathrm H[A_1\mid Z]=\mathrm H[B_2\mid Z]=\mathrm H[A,B]-\mathrm H[Z]\) give precisely (A.16), with its original constants. For the uniform-bit example, both sides of (A.16) are zero. The manuscript’s very next displayed bound uses this plus-sign calculation. This is decisive counterevidence against interpreting the misprint as a failed BSG lemma or a new substantive gap in the core result.
