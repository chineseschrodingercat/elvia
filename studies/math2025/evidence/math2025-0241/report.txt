Abstract: This claim-focused review of Horbez and Huang’s arXiv:2206.00884v3 identifies a false adjacency criterion in Lemma 3.9. The unrestricted auxiliary vertices permit a degenerate witness for every eligible adjacent pair. An explicit example in \(\operatorname{BS}(1,2)\) verifies all hypotheses. The criterion is used directly in Proposition 3.1 and hence in the combinatorial rigidity argument. Requiring four distinct vertices restores the lemma; a general proof of this local correction is given. The supported assessment is Major for the affected core proof step. No counterexample to the main measure equivalence theorem is established. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and operative assumptions

For nonzero integers \(m,n\), write \[\operatorname{BS}(m,n)=\langle a,t\mid ta^mt^{-1}=a^n\rangle.\] The source assumes \(|m|<|n|\) in Section 3. A standard \(a\)-line or \(t\)-line is a left coset of \(\langle a\rangle\) or \(\langle t\rangle\), respectively. The bipartite intersection graph \(\Lambda\) has these lines as vertices, with an edge when two lines intersect. Its \(a\)-type vertices correspond to vertices of the Bass–Serre tree \(T\). Positive \(t\)-edges define the orientation and partial order used below [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~16--17]{HH}]. Set \[\Lambda_{ij}=\operatorname{lk}_\Lambda(u_i)\cap\operatorname{lk}_\Lambda(u_j).\] Lemma 3.9 further requires \(m\mid n\), and \(u_1,u_2\) must be strictly comparable \(a\)-type vertices.

Equation reference: eq:nesting. Corollary 3.7 states that if \(v_a<v_b<v_c\) and \(\Lambda_{ac}\ne\varnothing\), then, in this divisible case, \[
 \Lambda_{ac}\subsetneq\Lambda_{ab},\qquad
 \Lambda_{ac}=\Lambda_{bc}.\] The distinction between strict containment and equality is essential. Lemma 3.6 uses \(h=\gcd(|m|,|n|)\), \(p=|m|/h\), and \(q=|n|/h\). For separation by \(d\) tree edges the relevant spacings are \(hp^d\) and \(hq^d\). When \(m\mid n\), \(p=1\) and \(q>1\), giving [eq:nesting] (#eq:nesting). The arithmetic argument on p. 19 was checked: the endpoint divisibility conditions imply \(h\mid s q^{d-1}\) and \(h\mid s p^{d-1}\); coprimality of \(p,q\) therefore implies \(h\mid s\). Negative parameter signs do not change these spacings.

# Substantive finding

Equation reference: eq:w1, eq:w2. Problem 1 (Unrestricted witnesses invalidate Lemma 3.9).  Under the preceding hypotheses, Lemma 3.9 on p. 21 characterizes adjacency of \(\bar u_1,\bar u_2\) in \(T\) by \(\Lambda_{12}\ne\varnothing\) and the nonexistence of vertices \(u_3,u_4\in\Lambda^{(0)}\) satisfying both \[\begin{aligned}
 \Lambda_{12}&\subseteq\Lambda_{34}, \\
 \Lambda_{34}&=\Lambda_{3i}\quad\text{for some }i\in\{1,2\}. \end{aligned}\] As written, this characterization is false.

## Exact counterexample and evaluation

Take \(m=1,n=2\). These parameters are nonzero, \(|m|<|n|\), and \(m\mid n\). Let \(u_2\) correspond to \(\langle a\rangle\) and \(u_1\) to \(t\langle a\rangle\). The lines are distinct: the height homomorphism sending \(a\) to \(0\) and \(t\) to \(1\) separates them. The \(t\)-edge from \(1\) to \(t\) projects to an oriented edge of \(T\), so \[\bar u_2<\bar u_1,\qquad d_T(\bar u_2,\bar u_1)=1.\] They satisfy the type and comparability hypotheses and are genuinely adjacent. The \(t\)-line \(\langle t\rangle\) meets both \(a\)-lines, so \(A:=\Lambda_{12}\ne\varnothing\).

Now choose \[u_3=u_1,\qquad u_4=u_2,\qquad i=2.\] These choices belong to the stated quantifier domains and give \[\Lambda_{34}=A=\Lambda_{32},\qquad A\subseteq A.\] Both [eq:w1] (#eq:w1) and [eq:w2] (#eq:w2) hold. The claimed right-hand condition, that no such witnesses exist, is false, whereas actual adjacency is true. This is an exact counterexample, not a finite sampling diagnostic.

An explicit set realization is also available. In \(\operatorname{BS}(1,2)=\mathbb Z[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb Z\) use \((r,k)(s,\ell)=(r+2^k s,k+\ell)\), \(a=(1,0)\), and \(t=(0,1)\). The \(t\)-lines are \(V_r=\{(r,j):j\in\mathbb Z\}\). The two \(a\)-lines are \(\{(z,0):z\in\mathbb Z\}\) and \(\{(2z,1):z\in\mathbb Z\}\), giving \(A=\{V_{2z}:z\in\mathbb Z\}\). The counterexample already follows from the coset definitions and does not depend on this additional realization.

## Alternative readings and source checks

The rendered source p. 21 was inspected: the first relation is \(\subseteq\), and the domain of \(u_3,u_4\) contains no exclusion of \(\{u_1,u_2\}\). This is not an OCR substitution for strict inclusion. No convention making all indexed vertices automatically distinct was found in the checked discussion or the targeted full-text search. In contrast, Lemma 3.8 on p. 20 explicitly excludes \(u_1,u_2\) from its auxiliary vertex domain.

The proof of Lemma 3.9 assumes, for adjacent \(u_2<u_1\), that the remaining positions for \(u_3\) are \(u_2<u_1<u_3\) or \(u_3<u_2<u_1\). It omits the endpoint coincidences allowed by its statement. This indicates the intended distinct-vertex interpretation, but does not make the literal quantifiers valid. The failure is systematic: the degenerate witness works for every pair with nonempty \(\Lambda_{12}\).

# A verified local correction

Proposition 1.  Retain all assumptions and both relations of Lemma 3.9. Replace the witness domain by \[u_3,u_4\in\Lambda^{(0)}\setminus\{u_1,u_2\},
 \qquad u_3\ne u_4.\] With this restriction the asserted adjacency equivalence is valid.

Proof. Assume \(u_2<u_1\). First let the corresponding tree vertices be adjacent, and suppose distinct witnesses satisfy \(\varnothing\ne A=\Lambda_{12}\subseteq\Lambda_{34}\). An element of \(A\) supplies a single \(t\)-line meeting all four \(a\)-lines, so their projections lie in a single oriented chain.

If \(u_j>u_1\), for \(j=3\) or \(4\), then [eq:nesting] (#eq:nesting) applied to \(u_2<u_1<u_j\) gives \(\Lambda_{2j}\subsetneq A\), whereas the witness inclusion gives \(A\subseteq\Lambda_{2j}\), a contradiction. No tree vertex lies strictly between adjacent endpoints, and endpoint coincidences have been excluded. Thus both \(u_3,u_4\) lie below \(u_2\).

If \(u_4<u_3<u_2<u_1\), apply [eq:nesting] (#eq:nesting) to \(u_4<u_3<u_i\), for \(i=1,2\), obtaining \(\Lambda_{3i}=\Lambda_{4i}\subsetneq\Lambda_{43}\). If \(u_3<u_4<u_2<u_1\), apply it to \(u_3<u_4<u_i\), obtaining \(\Lambda_{3i}\subsetneq\Lambda_{34}\) directly. Both orders contradict [eq:w2] (#eq:w2).

Conversely, suppose the endpoints are not adjacent. If \(A=\varnothing\), the proposed adjacency condition already fails. Otherwise choose a common \(t\)-line and vertices along it with \(u_4<u_2<u_3<u_1\). An intermediate \(u_3\) exists by non-adjacency; a lower \(u_4\) exists because the \(t\)-line extends in both directions. The four vertices are distinct. Formula [eq:nesting] (#eq:nesting) gives \[A\subsetneq\Lambda_{23}=\Lambda_{34}=\Lambda_{32}.\] These are permitted witnesses with \(i=2\), so the nonexistence condition fails as required. ◻

This is a sufficient correction throughout the original parameter domain of Lemma 3.9. It is not asserted to be minimal and does not constitute a reproof of every step in Proposition 3.1 or the main theorem.

# Dependency, impact, and conclusion

The first paragraph of the proof of Proposition 3.1 on p. 22 explicitly uses Lemma 3.9 to obtain preservation of tree adjacency when \(m\mid n\). Proposition 4.3 uses Proposition 3.1 to propagate order preservation across the interlocking Baumslag–Solitar subgroups [HH, p. 25]. Theorem 4.2 identifies the automorphism group of the intersection graph; the proof of Theorem 1.5 on p. 31 uses that identification on the route to Theorem 1.1. The affected branch includes the classical parameters \((1,2)\) and is not an unused peripheral lemma.

The supported impact is Major for the invalid core-dependent criterion as stated. The short repair does not change that dependency. The counterexample does not refute the line-preserving rigidity theorem or the main measure equivalence conclusion, and Proposition Proposition 1 (#prop:repair) restores the identified lemma. No empirical rerun is implicated. The remaining groupoid and operator-algebra arguments require their own checks before whole-proof validation could be asserted.

The consulted arXiv record still lists v3 as its latest version. A bounded search of that record and the publisher record did not locate a correction to this lemma. This is neither a first-discovery claim nor proof that no correction exists elsewhere. The finding is not attributed to the uninspected publisher full text.
