Abstract: The review checks the hypotheses, normalization and principal theta-lift dependencies in the acquired author manuscript of Herrero, Imamoğlu, von Pippich and Schwagenscheidt. An order-three discriminant-coset counterexample refutes the component trace identities of Theorems 4.2–4.3. A separate zero-versus-nonzero counterexample refutes the first identity of Theorem 5.15 and affects the printed derivation of the odd partially twisted trace formula. A separate class-number convention in Remark 3.7 gives a factor-two error under the ordinary field-class-number interpretation. The two core identity failures are classified Major; the bounded class-number convention is Minor. These conclusions concern this exact acquired manuscript. They do not establish failure of every algebraicity assertion. Scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Source identity and review scope

The reviewed paper is Special values of Green’s functions on hyperbolic 3-space, by S. Herrero, Ö. Imamoğlu, A.-M. von Pippich and M. Schwagenscheidt [paper], associated with DOI https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2025-0019. The exact source is the undated, 60-page ETH-hosted author PDF retrieved on 26 October 2026. Its production metadata is not treated as a submission date or version designation. The separately acquired 52-page arXiv:2405.01219v1, submitted 2 May 2024, and its TeX source are distinct artifacts. The checks cover the main hypotheses, the Eisenstein coefficient normalizations, the trace/theta-lift and splitting arguments, and the twisted formulas. Native AI helpers performed bounded proof and source-page checks; their exact coverage and limitations are retained separately. # Hypotheses and checks that survive

Theorem 1.1 retains the prime-discriminant assumption, the vanishing plus cusp-space obstruction, even spectral parameter \(2n\), \(n\geq1\), and the nonsquare condition on \(mm'\). Theorem 1.5 has distinct even/odd and twisted/untwisted hypotheses; its partially twisted normalization is \(\pi(\sqrt{mm'})^{1+n}\), with only the square-root factor raised to \(1+n\). Replacing these hypotheses by a general claim about individual Green values would exceed the theorems.

For \(\rho>0\), the radial kernel in (1.2), page 3, is \[\varphi_s(\cosh\rho)=\frac{e^{-s\rho}}{\sinh\rho}.\] Direct differentiation gives \(f''+2\coth\rho\,f'=(s^2-1)f\), consistent with the stated nonnegative-Laplacian eigenvalue \(1-s^2\). The \(1/\rho\) singularity explains the distinct-orbit restriction.

If two positive forms of determinants \(m,m'\) define the same special point up to \(\Gamma\), conjugation reduces their relation to \(X=tY\). Their positive integral diagonal entries force \(t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), whence \(mm'=(tm')^2\). Thus the nonsquare hypotheses exclude singular pairs. For the primitive outer trace in Theorem 4.12 the more precise coefficient condition excludes all positive integer multiples of the fixed primitive form. In the determinant-\(4\) example, \(2I\) is excluded by primitivity; no unsupported diagonal subtraction is needed.

An independent finite calculation also confirms the stabilizers \(4\) and \(12\) in Example 1.3, page 5. For \[I,\qquad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}1&(1+i)/2\\(1-i)/2&1\end{pmatrix},\] the corresponding \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z[i])\) stabilizers have orders \(8\) and \(24\). For \(2Y\), each row of a stabilizing matrix has squared Euclidean norm at most \(2/(2-\sqrt2)<4\), so an exhaustive search of integer real and imaginary entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) suffices. Quotienting by \(\{\pm I\}\) gives the stated orders and the factor \(48\) in the trace normalization. This does not purport to numerically evaluate the infinite Green sum.

# Individual cosets in the untwisted theta lift

Problem 1 (The positive trace loses its residue class under a sign change).  Theorems 4.2 and 4.3, pages 26–27, identify a component Poincaré theta lift with twice one positive-coset Green trace. The unfolded vector sum contains both signs. Replacing a negative vector by its negative changes the coset from \(\mu\) to \(-\mu\); the two positive traces need not coincide.

## Explicit data and an off-diagonal contradiction

Let \[D=-3,\qquad b=\frac1{\sqrt{-3}}=-\frac{i}{\sqrt3},
 \qquad \mu=b+\mathcal O_D,\qquad m=\frac23,\qquad n=1,\] and set \[X_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\\bar b&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 X_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-b\\-\bar b&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Both matrices are primitive, positive, and of determinant \(2/3\); their eigenvalues are \(1\pm1/\sqrt3\). The class \(\mu\) has order \(3\), so \(\mu\ne-\mu\). The source’s definition on page 25 uses positive definite matrices in the specified coset, not an average of the two cosets.

Page 24 states that \(\Gamma\) acts trivially on \(L'/L\). If a positive norm-\(m\) matrix in one coset defined the same point up to \(\Gamma\) as a matrix in the other, their positive rays and equal determinants would force equality after conjugation. This would imply \(\mu=-\mu\), a contradiction. Thus the two trace cycles have disjoint supports.

Put \(T_\pm(P)=\operatorname{tr}_{m,\pm\mu}(G_2(\,\cdot\,,P))\). The full-vector sum and gamma-duplication formula actually displayed on page 27 give, at \(n=1\), \(s=3/2\), \[\Phi(P)=4\sqrt m\,[T_+(P)+T_-(P)].\] The printed Theorem 4.3 instead gives \(\Phi(P)=8\sqrt m\,T_+(P)\). To exhibit failure away from every diagonal, take \[P(t)=\frac{i}{\sqrt3}+\sqrt{\frac23}e^t j,\qquad t>0.\] Its distance from the point of \(X_-\) is exactly \(t\). Every fixed-determinant positive form point has height \(\sqrt m/c\leq\sqrt m\), with \(c\in\mathbb N\), whereas \(P(t)\) has larger height. Thus \(P(t)\) is off both cycles for every \(t>0\). The opposite-coset trace is locally bounded near the limiting point because the cycles are disjoint and locally finite. Using \(\varphi_2(\cosh t)=t^{-1}+O(1)\) and cancellation of the stabilizer count against the trace weight, \[T_-(P(t))=\frac{\pi}{t}+O(1),\qquad T_+(P(t))=O(1).\] The actual value minus the printed right side is consequently \[4\pi\sqrt{\frac23}\,\frac1t+O(1),\] which is nonzero for all sufficiently small positive \(t\). This is an off-diagonal counterexample, not an evaluation at an undefined singular point.

## Scope, contrary evidence and supported correction

The central Weil action independently confirms the same obstruction: the relevant Poincaré series has principal part \(q^{-m}(e_\mu+e_{-\mu})\), and \(F_{1-2n,m,\mu}=F_{1-2n,m,-\mu}\). Its lift cannot equal an unsymmetrized trace with a different pole set.

The supported replacement in both component identities is \[2\operatorname{tr}_{m,\mu}\quad\longmapsto\quad
 \operatorname{tr}_{m,\mu}+\operatorname{tr}_{m,-\mu}.\] Equivalently, one may use the average of the two positive traces. Restricting to \(2\mu=0\) is another, narrower repair. The Gaussian examples with two-torsion discriminant group do not detect this error.

This is a Major defect in the claimed core component lift-to-trace identity, explicitly described as a main result of Section 4. Its impact is bounded by an important cancellation: the relevant modular coefficients satisfy \(a_\mu=a_{-\mu}\), so \[\sum_\mu a_\mu(T_\mu+T_{-\mu})
       =2\sum_\mu a_\mu T_\mu .\] The corrected component formula therefore gives the same fully summed expression used in Theorem 4.12. This counterexample does not refute that symmetric theorem or the introductory full-trace result. The subsequent individual-coset simplification in Remark 4.13(4), page 35, requires a fresh symmetry check; no separate counterexample to that specialization is claimed here.

# A false twisted splitting identity

Problem 2 (An exterior character forces an incorrect vanishing).  The first identity of Theorem 5.15, page 51, includes an exterior factor \(\chi_D(X_0)\) in the untwisted primitive outer trace of the twisted modified theta kernel. Under the stated hypotheses, that factor can vanish while the left side is nonzero.

## An admissible counterexample

Use \(D=-4\), \(m=1\), \(\mu=0\), and \(X_0=I\). The vector \(I\) is positive and primitive in \(L'\), and belongs to the specified determinant-\(1\), zero-coset set. Section 5.1, page 40, explicitly extends \(\chi_D\) by zero outside \[L_D=\{X\in L':Q(X)\in D\mathbb Z\}.\] Since \(Q(I)=1\notin4\mathbb Z\), the printed right side of the first identity is identically zero. The first identity does not assume \(\chi_D(X_0)\ne0\) or \(m\in |D|\mathbb Z\).

There is one class of positive integral binary Hermitian forms of determinant \(1\) over \(\mathbb Z[i]\). Here is a short verification. Choose a primitive shortest vector and complete it to an \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z[i])\) basis. The leading coefficient \(a\) is the minimum positive value, so \(c\geq a\). Reducing both components of \(b\) modulo \(a\) gives \(|b|^2\leq a^2/2\). Hence \(1=ac-|b|^2\geq a^2/2\), and the positive integer \(a\) equals \(1\). Now \(b\) can be reduced to zero and \(c=1\). Since \(|\Gamma_I|=4\), the claimed left side is \(\Theta^*_{L,\chi_{-4}}(\tau,j)/4\).

## A nonzero Fourier coefficient

Write \(\tau=u+iv\), \(X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\\bar b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and \(t=a+c\). The source’s \(v(j)=I/\sqrt2\) gives \[(X,v(j))=\frac{t}{\sqrt2},\qquad Q(X_v)=\frac{t^2}{4}.\] By Definition 5.3, page 42, the coefficient of frequency \(1\) in \(u\) of \(\Theta^*_{L,\chi_{-4}}(\tau,j)\) is \[A_1(v)=\frac{v^{3/2}}{4\sqrt2}
   \sum_{\substack{a,c\in\mathbb Z,\ b\in\mathbb Z[i]\\ac-|b|^2=4}}
   \chi_{-4}(X)t\,
   \exp\!\left[-2\pi v\left(\frac{t^2}{8}-1\right)\right].\] Necessarily \(|t|\geq4\). At \(t=\pm4\), only \(X=\pm2I\) occurs and its character is zero. At \(t=5\), the complete list is \[\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(a,c)&b&\text{number}&\chi_{-4}(X)\\ \hline
(1,4),(4,1)&0&2&1\\
(2,3),(3,2)&\pm1\pm i&8&-1
\end{array}\] and therefore \(\sum_{t=5}\chi_{-4}(X)t=-30\). Replacing \(X\) by \(-X\) reverses both the character and \(t\), so \(t=-5\) contributes another \(-30\). The remaining counts grow at most polynomially in \(|t|\), whereas the exponential is Gaussian in \(t\). Consequently \[\frac14 A_1(v)=
 -\frac{15}{4\sqrt2}v^{3/2}e^{-17\pi v/4}
 +O\!\left(v^{3/2}e^{-7\pi v}\right)
 \quad(v\longrightarrow\infty).\] This is nonzero for all sufficiently large \(v\), contradicting the identically zero right side. The finite enumeration was independently implemented using integer arithmetic; the proof of exhaustiveness and the tail bound, rather than a numerical truncation, establish the result.

## Impact, counterevidence and repair boundary

The example satisfies the prime-discriminant and primitive-vector conditions. It concerns a smooth theta identity, so a Green-function diagonal exclusion does not remove it. No choice of a nonzero global normalizing constant repairs zero versus nonzero.

The covariance at the bottom of page 51 yields for the untwisted outer orbit sum \[\operatorname{tr}^0_{m,\mu}(\Theta^*)=
 \frac1{\operatorname{vol}(K)}
 \int_{H(\mathbb Q)\backslash H(\mathbb A_f)}
 \chi_D(\nu(h))\Theta^*(\tau,P_0,h)\,dh .\] It supplies no exterior \(\chi_D(X_0)\). Such a factor does arise when the outer trace is itself twisted. The second identity of Theorem 5.15 is therefore not refuted by this example, and removing its character would not be justified.

The first identity is a central splitting step in the odd partially twisted evaluation. Theorem 5.20, page 53, repeats the exterior factor, and its proof on page 54 explicitly invokes Theorem 5.15. This is a Major issue in the exact identity and the printed derivation. The present counterexample does not independently refute Theorem 5.20 for an admissible weakly holomorphic input, nor the weaker rational-multiple-of-\(\pi\) conclusions of Theorem 5.21 and Theorem 1.5(2). Removing the exterior factor from the first identity is the natural repair suggested by the orbit integral; all constants, descent maps and the downstream formula still require rechecking. Its local form does not reduce the impact of the currently false principal identity.

# A class-number normalization

Equation reference: eq:classnumber. Problem 3 (Ordinary and narrow class numbers).  Remark 3.7(1), page 20, writes \[
 L(\chi_\Delta,1)=
 \frac{h(\Delta)\log\varepsilon_\Delta}{\sqrt\Delta}
 \qquad(\Delta>0).\] defines \(h(\Delta)\) as the class number of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt\Delta)\), and defines \(\varepsilon_\Delta\) as the smallest totally positive unit greater than \(1\). Under the ordinary ideal-class-number meaning, [eq:classnumber] (#eq:classnumber) is false.

Take \(\Delta=12\). The field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) has ordinary class number \(1\): the Minkowski bound \(\sqrt{12}/2<2\) leaves a norm-\(1\) ideal in each class. Its smallest totally positive unit is \(2+\sqrt3\). Directly from the four nonzero residue classes of \(\chi_{12}\), \[\begin{aligned}
 L(\chi_{12},1)
 &=\int_0^1\frac{1-x^4-x^6+x^{10}}{1-x^{12}}\,dx\\
 &=\int_0^1\frac{1-x^2}{1-x^2+x^4}\,dx
   =\frac{\log(2+\sqrt3)}{\sqrt3}.\end{aligned}\] The value in [eq:classnumber] (#eq:classnumber) is half of this. The integral can be checked by differentiating \[\frac1{2\sqrt3}
 \log\frac{x^2+\sqrt3x+1}{x^2-\sqrt3x+1}.\]

The distinction can affect an actual nonzero coefficient: for the positive definite rank-three lattice \((\mathbb Z^3,Q=x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2)\), \(\gamma=0\), \(n=3\), the data in Theorem 3.6 give \(N_{0,3}(8)=96\) and \(c^+(3,0)=-6L(\chi_{12},1)/\pi\). Substitution of [eq:classnumber] (#eq:classnumber) with ordinary \(h(12)=1\) halves that coefficient. The representation count is retained as an exact finite calculation.

There is material counterevidence against a broader claim of damage. Theorem 3.6 itself retains the \(L\)-value. The worked coefficient on page 57 correctly uses \[-\frac{2L(\chi_{12},1)}{\pi}
 =-\frac{2\log(7+2\sqrt{12})}{\sqrt{12}\,\pi},\] and \(7+2\sqrt{12}=(2+\sqrt3)^2\). Thus that worked value is not refuted, and the rational logarithmic span is unchanged. The displayed real quadratic formula becomes correct if \(h\) means the narrow class number; the acquired text does not specify that convention. This is a Minor normalization/definition issue on the declared ordinary-field interpretation, with an explicit factor-two calculation and a bounded effect. Clarifying the narrow convention, or including the norm-dependent factor with ordinary class numbers, is the required repair.

# Assessment and evidence limits

The component trace and twisted splitting identities require correction. The latter problem must be resolved before the printed odd partially twisted evaluation can be accepted on the given derivation. The ordinary/narrow class-number convention also requires clarification. None of these findings is a claim that all Green-value or algebraicity results fail.

The nonzero cusp-space sign in the introduction’s proof sketch is contradicted by the correctly stated zero obstruction in the main argument and is not a separate substantive finding. The missing stabilizer weight in one display of the classical Siegel–Weil formula is bracketed by correctly weighted expressions. Other isolated indices were not converted into additional Problems without a demonstrated effect on a conclusion.

The zero-index justification in Remark 3.7(2) also needs a more careful argument: for \(\gamma=n=0\), the zero vector solves every congruence, so the representation counts cannot eventually vanish. For \(Q=x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2\) at \(p=2\), direct reduction modulo \(4\) gives \(N_0=1\), \(N_1=4\), and \(N_r=8N_{r-2}\) for \(r\geq2\). The local factor is nevertheless \[(1-4X)\sum_{r\geq0}N_rX^r
 =\frac{1-16X^2}{1-8X^2},\] and vanishes at \(X=1/4\), as the coefficient calculation requires. The retained note supplies the general anisotropic compactness estimate for this mechanism. This check corrects the stated justification without furnishing a counterexample to the zero-index coefficient or adding another core-theorem failure.
