Abstract: This review concerns the pinned author manuscript arXiv:2008.13656v7. A nonadditive proposed filtration obstructs the stated Rees construction. A separate example contradicts the identification of the constructed open set with the inverse image of the smooth locus of its moment polytope. Three local defects concern an illustrative toric equation, a completeness hypothesis, and a zero-weight exception. These conclusions do not disprove the existence of collective integrable systems asserted in the paper. The scientific disposition remains pending.

# The construction and its dependencies

Theorem 1.1 constructs a collective map for a compact connected Lie group, with a torus action on a dense connected open subset of each connected Hamiltonian manifold. Its compact-case assertions include connected fibers, a convex polytope, and a description of that open subset using the smooth locus of the polytope. Section 3 supplies a stratified gradient-flow construction under (GH1)–(GH6). Section 4 uses a good valuation to produce the required degeneration, and Section 5 applies it to the base affine space. Section 6 transfers the construction to Hamiltonian manifolds.

The valuation convention is the maximum convention: \(v(f+g)\leq\max\{v(f),v(g)\}\), together with multiplicativity and invariance under nonzero scalar multiplication. Conditions (v1)–(v5) control an ordered lattice \(L\), a grading map \(w\), finite grading fibers, and finite refinements by integral linear functionals. A refinement for finitely many specified comparisons is not automatically an order-preserving function on all of \(L\). This distinction is essential to Problem 1.

# Problem 1: the proposed filtration need not be additive

## Claim and location

Equation reference: eq:filtration. On p. 20, following (15), the paper defines \[
 A_{\leq k}=\{f\in A:e(v(f))\leq k\text{ or }f=0\}\] and calls these sets subspaces forming a filtration. The Rees algebra (16) and Proposition 4.5 then use that assertion. The choice of \(e\) is constrained by nonnegativity on the value semigroup and the finitely many comparisons associated with the chosen relation generators.

## A complete counterexample to additivity

Take \(A=\mathbb C[x,y]\), \(L=\mathbb Z^2\), and the lexicographic order on pairs \((a,b)\), with \(a\) compared first. For a nonzero polynomial define \(v(f)\) to be its largest monomial exponent in this order. The value semigroup is \(S=\mathbb N^2\), which is finitely generated and saturated. Leading coefficients show that the valuation has one-dimensional leaves: two polynomials with the same leading exponent have a unique scalar multiple canceling that leading term. Multiplication adds leading exponents, and addition satisfies the required maximum inequality.

Let \(H=(\mathbb C^*)^2\), with the standard multigrading, and set \(w=\mathrm{id}_L\). Then (v1) holds for the induced lexicographic grading order; \(0\) is the least element of \(w(S)\), as required by (v2); every fiber in (v3) is a singleton; and each homogeneous polynomial is a scalar monomial, so (v4) holds. For (v5), there are no unequal elements with the same \(w\)-value. Every requested finite set of strict comparisons is therefore empty, and a nonnegative integral functional suffices.

Use the identity embedding \(\mathbb C^2\hookrightarrow E=\mathbb C^2\) and the homogeneous Khovanskii basis \(\{x,y\}\). The associated graded ring is \(\mathbb C[x,y]\), so its presentation has no ideal generators: \(J=0\). Consequently (15) imposes no strict inequalities. The choice \(e(a,b)=a+b\) satisfies all the stated restrictions. But, for \[f=x,\qquad g=-x+y^2,\] one has \[v(f)=v(g)=(1,0),\quad e(v(f))=e(v(g))=1,
 \qquad v(f+g)=(0,2),\quad e(v(f+g))=2.\] Thus \(f,g\in A_{\leq1}\), while \(f+g\notin A_{\leq1}\). The set in [eq:filtration] (#eq:filtration) is not a vector subspace. In particular, the displayed direct sum in (16) is not even closed under addition with these allowed data.

## An additional obstruction in the preceding displayed formulas

Lemma 4.4 and (14)–(15), p. 20, use the evaluated quantities \(v(\bar g_j(f_1,\ldots,f_n))\) and \(v(p_j(f_1,\ldots,f_n))\). If \(g_j=\bar g_j+p_j\) lies in the kernel of evaluation and both evaluated terms are nonzero, these values are equal. If both terms vanish, the displayed valuation is undefined. The intended formal degree \(s_j\) of \(\bar g_j\) is different from that evaluated value.

For a nonzero concrete case, keep \(A=\mathbb C[x,y]\) but order exponents lexicographically by \((a+2b,a)\), and let \(w(a,b)=a+2b\). Use the homogeneous Khovanskii basis \[f_1=x,\qquad f_2=y,\qquad f_3=x^2+y .\] It gives a closed equivariant embedding in \(\mathbb C^3\). Here \(S=\mathbb N^2\), the leaves are one-dimensional, \(w(S)=\mathbb N\) has least element zero, and each \(w\)-fiber is finite. The grading condition holds. Moreover, for a fixed value \(\lambda=a+2b\), the functional \(e(a,b)=2a+2b=\lambda+a\) strictly respects every comparison in that fiber and is nonnegative. Thus (v1)–(v5) hold.

The graded relation is \(\bar g=z_3-z_1^2\), of formal degree \(s=(2,0)\). Its lift is \(g=z_3-z_1^2-z_2\), with \(p=-z_2\). However, \[\bar g(f_1,f_2,f_3)=y,\qquad p(f_1,f_2,f_3)=-y,\] so both evaluated values are \((0,1)\). No \(e\) can satisfy the strict inequality in (15) for this single correction monomial. The acquired TeX source contains the same expressions; this is not a PDF extraction error.

## Alternative interpretation, impact, and repair status

Replacing the evaluated degree by \(s_j\) addresses the latter local notation error. In this particular example it gives the valid equation \(z_3-z_1^2-t^2z_2=0\), since substitution of \((z_1,z_2,z_3)=(t^2x,t^2y,t^4(x^2+y))\) makes it vanish. That local repair does not address the preceding nonadditivity counterexample. A construction using an appropriate homogeneous basis or weight filtration needs a new argument; none is asserted here as a complete repair of the paper.

This is a Major defect in the stated construction, rather than a classification based on a typographical error alone. Section 4.3.1 explicitly applies Section 4.1.2 in Proposition 4.13 (p. 24), which supplies Theorem 4.16 (p. 27); Theorem 5.5 (p. 33) and the Section 6 construction depend on it. The example disproves the claimed general filtration step. It does not prove that no valid degeneration or collective system can be constructed by another argument. Evidence: source pp. 19–24, 27, 33; calculation record `recovery_checks_output.json`.

# Problem 2: the constructed open set need not map to the smooth locus

## Claim and assumptions

Proposition 6.10(iii), p. 39, identifies the image of the constructed open piece with the smooth locus of the corresponding moment image. The proof on p. 40 treats \(\Delta_Y\cap\sigma\) as smooth. No multiplicity-free hypothesis is imposed on \(Y\). The proof of Theorem 1.1 on p. 41 invokes Proposition 6.10 for its first and third assertions.

## Counterexample and the construction in the abelian case

Let \(K=\mathbb T^3\) and \(Y=\mathbb {CP}^6\), with its standard invariant Fubini–Study form. Act diagonally with weights \[w_0=0,\quad w_1=e_1,\quad w_2=-e_1,\quad
 w_3=e_2,\quad w_4=-e_2,\quad w_5=e_3,\quad w_6=-e_3 .\] This is a compact connected smooth Hamiltonian \(K\)-manifold. The action is effective because the coordinate of weight zero and the three positive coordinate weights force a projectively trivial torus element to be the identity. With a harmless choice of moment-map normalization, \[\mu([z_0:\cdots:z_6])
   =\frac{\sum_{j=0}^6 |z_j|^2w_j}{\sum_{j=0}^6|z_j|^2},
 \qquad
 \Delta_Y=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}
          =\{p:|p_1|+|p_2|+|p_3|\leq1\}.\] Every convex combination is realized by choosing squared coordinate moduli, so this is an equality of images, not merely an inclusion.

For a torus the semisimple factor in Section 6.2 is trivial. There is only one Weyl-chamber stratum, \(\sigma=\mathfrak t^*\); the universal space is \(T^*\mathbb T^3\), with \(c=F=\mathrm{id}\) on moment coordinates. Symplectic implosion leaves \(Y\) unchanged. The reduction \((Y\times T^*\mathbb T^3)//\mathbb T^3\) is canonically \(Y\): the moment equation determines the cotangent coordinate, and the free translation factor supplies a unique representative with torus coordinate one. This also identifies the reduced symplectic form with that of \(Y\). All the universal space is smooth. Consequently the constructed open piece \(U\), and its inverse image \(D\), are the whole of \(Y\).

At \(p=e_1\), four distinct facets of the octahedron are active: their outward primitive normals are \((1,\pm1,\pm1)\). Each supporting hyperplane is indeed a facet, since it contains three affinely independent signed coordinate vertices. A three-dimensional smooth rational polytope is simple at a smooth vertex; here four facets meet. Equivalently the tangent cone is not a simplicial unimodular cone. Thus \(e_1\) is not in the smooth locus in the toric, lattice sense used by the paper. Yet \(\mu(U)=\mu(Y)=\Delta_Y\) contains \(e_1\). This contradicts the asserted identification for the constructed \(U\).

## Scope and alternatives

The example has positive complexity: the complex dimension is six and the effective torus dimension is three. Smoothness of a Hamiltonian manifold does not force its moment polytope to be smooth in this setting. Results about smooth toric manifolds therefore do not remove the example.

The defect is Major because the main proof explicitly uses this identification. It does not refute convexity, connected fibers, or the existential form of Theorem 1.1 by itself. In the example one can restrict the existing torus action to the inverse image of the polytope’s smooth locus; this demonstrates why a false claim about the specific constructed set is different from a proof of nonexistence. A general corrected construction and its connectedness assertions have not been verified here. Evidence: source pp. 3, 36–37, 39–41; exact facet calculation in the independent-check output.

# Problem 3: the displayed equations for an illustrative toric variety

Example 4.17, (21), p. 28, describes the toric variety using only \[y_3y_6=y_4y_5,\qquad y_1y_5=y_2y_3 .\] Its six displayed semigroup generators are \[\begin{aligned}
 v_1&=(0,1,0,0),&v_2&=(0,1,0,1),&v_3&=(1,0,0,0),\\
 v_4&=(1,0,1,0),&v_5&=(1,0,0,1),&v_6&=(1,0,1,1).
\end{aligned}\] The equality \(v_1+v_6=v_2+v_4\) requires the toric relation \(y_1y_6-y_2y_4=0\): under the semigroup-algebra map both monomials map to the same basis element. The point \(q=(1,0,0,0,0,1)\) satisfies both printed equations, but the required binomial has value one. Hence the printed zero set strictly contains the toric variety. Saturating on a torus chart could remove extra points, but the displayed equality is an affine zero-set description, not such a saturation.

This is Minor. The text on p. 27 explicitly states that the example is not used in subsequent results. No claim is made that appending this one missing equation suffices scheme-theoretically. As counterevidence against a broader criticism of the example, its rectangle/trapezoid lattice counts agree: \[\sum_{j=0}^{a}(2a+b-2j+1)=(a+1)(a+b+1)
 \quad(a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}),\] and the corresponding areas are both \(a(a+b)\). The symbolic identity and 49 finite parameter checks were reproduced; they do not independently prove the claimed symplectic statement. Evidence: source pp. 27–29 and the exact binomial residual calculation.

# Problem 4: properness onto the image does not imply complete flow

Lemma A.1, p. 44, assumes that \((\pi,\psi)\) is proper as a map onto its image, and concludes existence of the gradient Hamiltonian flow starting in \(U_0\) for every real time. Take the smooth affine variety \[X=\mathbb C\setminus\{-1\},\qquad \pi(z)=z ,\] with the restricted Euclidean Kähler form. Let the connected group \(K=S^1\) act trivially and set \(\psi=0\). The action is Hamiltonian, preserves the metric and every fiber, and its moment map is continuous. There are no critical or singular points, so \(Z=\varnothing\subset X_0\), and \(U_0=\{0\}\) is nonempty. The map \(z\mapsto(z,0)\) is a homeomorphism onto its image, hence proper onto that image.

The normalized negative gradient field of \(\operatorname{Re}\pi\) is \(-\partial_{\operatorname{Re}z}\). Its unique trajectory from zero is \(\varphi_t(0)=-t\), which leaves \(X\) at time \(t=1\). The all-real-time conclusion is false with every listed assumption satisfied. A compactness argument over a segment in the full target fails because its intersection with the image need not be compact in that image.

This is Minor with the present dependency evidence. Condition (GH4)II on p. 12 instead requires properness to the full target. The example fails that stronger assumption, and therefore does not contradict Theorem 3.7. Full-target properness excludes this escape mechanism; a replacement proof of the entire lemma under modified hypotheses is not claimed. Evidence: source pp. 12, 44–45 and the explicit trajectory.

# Problem 5: the pointed-cone criterion needs a zero-weight exclusion

Lemma 2.2(ii), p. 7, ends with an equivalence between properness of a linear unitary torus moment map and strong convexity of its image. Take the \(S^1\)-representation \(E=\mathbb C_0\oplus\mathbb C_1\). Its standard moment map is \[\mu(z_0,z_1)=\tfrac12|z_1|^2 .\] The image \([0,\infty)\) contains no line and is strongly convex, but the fiber of zero contains \((n,0)\) for every positive integer \(n\); it is not compact. Thus the moment map is not proper.

The earlier criterion in the same lemma, excluding a nontrivial nonnegative combination of the weights equal to zero, correctly detects this example. The final equivalence loses the possibility of zero weights. For a finite set of weights the corrected criterion is: its cone is pointed and every weight is nonzero. Indeed, a pointed finitely generated cone admits a linear functional strictly positive on all of its nonzero generators. If its values on the weights are at least \(c>0\), then \(\ell(\mu(z))\geq c\|z\|^2/2\); inverse images of compact sets are closed and bounded, hence compact. Conversely, a zero weight gives the unbounded fiber above; a nontrivial nonnegative zero relation similarly gives a ray of preimages of zero by taking squared moduli proportional to that relation.

This is Minor: Lemma 4.10, p. 23, explicitly excludes zero weights in the relevant later application. Its properness use is therefore not refuted by this example. Evidence: source pp. 7, 23.
