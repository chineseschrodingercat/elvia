Abstract: The printed degree convention makes central filtered-module assertions false in rank one. A bounded admissible-lattice choice in Proposition 5.23 is also false, even for an acyclic rank-two pair satisfying all its bounds. Both have complete local repairs: a corrected degree and Harder–Narasimhan argument, and a direct use of the coherent-sheaf/BC equivalence, respectively. Nine further groups of local statement or formula corrections are documented. These findings concern the reviewed arXiv version; they do not provide a counterexample to the intended geometric comparison theorems. Scientific adjudication is pending.

# Degree convention and the central \(K\)-side comparison

Problem 1 (Major: pp. 32–35, Definition 5.1, Lemmas 5.3–5.4, and Theorem 5.8).  The printed degree \(t_N-t_H\) conflicts with the HN convention and with the claimed relation between acyclicity and admissibility.

## A rank-one witness

Equation reference: eq:rankone. Page 14 defines semistability by \(\mu(E')\leq\mu(E)\) for strict subobjects. Page 32 defines degree as \(t_N-t_H\) and calls an object acyclic when its HN slopes are nonnegative. Take \(K=F=\mathbb Q_p\) and \[
 M=Fe,\qquad \varphi(e)=pe,\qquad N=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Fil}^iM_K=
 \begin{cases}M_K&i\leq0,\\0&i\geq1.\end{cases}\] For the version with \(G_K\)-action, extend scalars to \(F^{\mathrm{nr}}\) and use the coefficient action. This is a legitimate rank-one filtered module, of printed degree \(1\), hence acyclic under Definition 5.1.

A filtration contained in this one has its unique jump \(h\leq0\). Weak admissibility in rank one requires \(h=t_N=1\). Thus Lemma 5.3(b) is impossible. This also contradicts Lemma 5.4 with \(r=1\): the Frobenius slope is in \([0,1]\), \(\operatorname{Fil}^0=M_K\), and \(\operatorname{Fil}^2=0\). There is no omitted endpoint hypothesis that excludes the example.

The consequence for Theorem 5.8 is substantive. The actual associated bundle is \(\mathcal O(-1)\); by (3.10), p. 15, its \(H^0\) is zero. Equivalently, an element contributing to \(V_{\mathrm{st}}\) is \(x=t^{-1}y\), where \(y\in B_e\cap tB_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\). Since \(B_e\cap B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+=\mathbb Q_p\), this intersection is zero. Thus \(V_{\mathrm{st}}=0\), whereas the asserted recovered module \(M^*\) is nonzero. This refutes the printed algebraic comparison assertion under its printed definition. It does not produce a geometric \(X\) violating a main comparison theorem.

## Complete local correction

Equation reference: eq:correct-degree. Use degree \[
 \delta(M)=t_H(M)-t_N(M)\] with the existing HN convention. The proof of Lemma 5.3 also needs its HN orientation repaired; changing only the displayed sign does not justify its choice of a largest slope-zero subobject.

Here is a complete replacement. If \(M\) has nonnegative HN slopes and positive degree, let \(P=M^{>0}\) be its positive HN subobject and \(Q=M/P\) its slope-zero quotient. Every nonzero quotient of \(P\) has positive integral degree, hence degree at least one. Choose a nonzero graded piece of the filtration on \(P_K\), and replace that filtration step by a hyperplane containing the next step. On every induced quotient filtration the Hodge degree falls by either zero or one; its Newton degree is unchanged. Every quotient therefore still has nonnegative degree. By the quotient criterion for HN slopes, the new \(P\) is acyclic.

Choose a splitting of the underlying filtered \(K\)-vector-space sequence \(0\to P_K\to M_K\to Q_K\to0\), replace only the filtration on \(P_K\), and keep that on \(Q_K\). This gives a contained filtration on \(M_K\), an extension of two nonnegative objects, and lowers \(\delta(M)\) by exactly one. Repeating finitely many times gives a nonnegative object of degree zero, hence a weakly admissible one. Conversely, enlarging a filtration increases the Hodge degree of every quotient, so a filtration containing a weakly admissible one is acyclic. This proves both directions.

For Lemma 5.4, the Frobenius slopes of \(P\) remain in \([0,r]\), so \(t_N(P)\geq0\). Since \(\delta(P)>0\), one has \(t_H(P)>0\) and hence \(\operatorname{Fil}^1P_K\ne0\). Cut the largest nonzero step \(i_0\geq1\). This preserves both \(\operatorname{Fil}^0=M_K\) and \(\operatorname{Fil}^{r+1}=0\) throughout the induction.

With this repair, choose a contained admissible filtration and write the exact sequence used in Theorem 5.8 as \(0\to V_1\to V\to W\to0\). Here \(V_1\) is finite-dimensional and \(W\) is a finite torsion \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\)-module. Proposition 2.14 gives the required Galois-equivariant period-Hom vanishing for \(W\); the injections and dimension comparison in the source then give the desired isomorphisms. The filtration check uses the correctly printed dual rule \(\operatorname{Fil}^iM_K^*=(\operatorname{Fil}^{1-i}M_K)^\perp\). This repairs the local argument, subject to the cited weak-admissibility/admissibility and HN inputs.

# A false bounded lattice choice, and a direct repair

Problem 2 (Major: pp. 38–39, proof of Proposition 5.23).  The proof chooses a weakly admissible lattice \(N\) with \(L_0\subset N\subset L\), where \(L_0=M\otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\) and \(L=M_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\). Such a lattice need not exist under the proposition’s hypotheses.

## Counterexample satisfying the stated bounds

Equation reference: eq:lattices. Put \(R=B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\), with uniformizer \(t\), and start with \(E=\mathcal O(1)\oplus\mathcal O\) on the Fargues–Fontaine curve. At \(\infty\), choose local bases \(e,f\) and let \[
 L=Re+Rf,\qquad L_0=R(e+tf)+R(t^2f).\] Let \(E_0\) agree with \(E\) away from \(\infty\) and have lattice \(L_0\) at \(\infty\). Its intersection with the first summand is \(\mathcal O(1)(-\infty)=\mathcal O\), and its projection onto the second summand is \(\mathcal O(-1)\). The resulting extension splits because \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathcal O(-1),\mathcal O)=H^1(\mathcal O(1))=0\). Hence \(E_0\simeq\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(-1)\). Choose a \((\varphi,N)\)-module \(M\) with Frobenius slopes \(0,1\), \(N=0\), and standard bundle \(E_0\). No Galois-descent condition is imposed on these \(C\)-side pairs. The pair \((M,L)\) has bundle \(E\), whose slopes \(1,0\) are nonnegative, so it is acyclic. For \(r=2\), \[t^2L_0\subset t^2L\subset L_0,
 \qquad t^2e=t^2(e+tf)-t(t^2f).\] All hypotheses of Proposition 5.23 hold, with any \(k\geq4\).

But \(L/L_0\simeq R/(t^2)\), so it has a unique submodule of length one. Since \(\deg E_0=-1\), a degree-zero intermediate bundle would have to arise from that submodule. Its lattice is \(N=Re+Rtf\), and its bundle is \(\mathcal O(1)\oplus\mathcal O(-1)\), which is not semistable of slope zero. Thus there is no weakly admissible intermediate lattice. The acyclicity assumption applies to \(E\), not to \(E_0\); the example does not confuse these two bundles.

## The estimate itself survives, with a stronger bound

Equation reference: eq:full-faithfulness. The source already supplies enough theory to replace the invalid choice. Let \(L_r=t^rL\) and \(E_r=E(M\{-r\},L_r)\). Multiplication by \(t^r\) identifies \(E_r\) with the original nonnegative bundle. Remark 5.16 identifies \(V_{\mathrm{st}}^r\) with \(H^0(E_r)\). Theorem 3.12, p. 15, and the full embedding of BCs into VSs give \[\begin{aligned}

 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{VS}}(V_{\mathrm{st}}^r,B_k)
 &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Coh}_X}(E_r,i_{\infty,*}B_k)\\
 &\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_R(L_r,B_k),\qquad B_k=R/(t^k).\notag\end{aligned}\] Both objects are in the nonnegative part of the tilted category, so this is an ordinary sheaf Hom, not an incorrectly substituted derived Ext.

Equation reference: eq:restriction. The natural map in the proposition is now simply restriction \[
 \operatorname{Hom}_R(L_0,B_k)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_R(L_r,B_k).\] The inclusions \(t^rL_0\subset L_r\subset L_0\) give elementary divisors \(t^{a_j}\), \(0\leq a_j\leq r\). In suitable bases the restriction is multiplication by \(t^{a_j}\) on each \(R/(t^k)\) summand. Its kernel and cokernel are killed by \(t^{a_j}\), hence by \(t^r\). This proves the claimed \(t^{2r}\)-bound and in fact improves it. The proof works for all \(k\geq0\); \(k=0\) gives zero modules, and \(r=0\) gives an isomorphism.

For [eq:lattices] (#eq:lattices), the matrix of \(L_r\hookrightarrow L_0\) at \(r=2\) is \[\begin{pmatrix}t^2&0\\-t&1\end{pmatrix};\] restriction has the transpose matrix and elementary divisors \(1,t^2\). Exact finite-jet checks at \(k=4,5,8\) corroborate the kernel and cokernel calculation. The example refutes a proof step, while satisfying the repaired conclusion. Major severity reflects the invalid central auxiliary derivation, not a claim that Proposition 5.23 or the geometric theorems are false.

# Further local corrections

The following nine groups are Minor. Each has a direct local correction; none supplies an additional counterexample to a main geometric theorem.

## HN extension bounds: p. 14

The last bullet of §3.2.2 assumes only slopes of \(E_1\geq\lambda_1\) and slopes of \(E_2\leq\lambda_2\), yet concludes that an extension has slopes in \([\lambda_1,\lambda_2]\). The split extension \(\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(-1)\), with \(\lambda_1=0,\lambda_2=1\), disproves this. Require both factors to have slopes in the interval. The nonnegative-slope extension closure used in the corrected argument above is valid.

## Slope inequality and multiplicities: p. 17

At the end of Lemma 3.16 the conclusion must be \(h>d\), as the proof initially states, rather than \(h<d\). An injection \(U_{h,d}\to\mathbb V^h\) has cokernel of Dimension \((h-d,-h)\). Its first coordinate is nonnegative; if zero, its height cannot be negative. Hence \(h>d\).

In §3.2.6(2), if \(\lambda_j=d_j/h_j\) and \(\lambda_1+\lambda_2=d/h\) are reduced, the tensor multiplicity is \(h_1h_2/h\), forced by rank, rather than the printed \((h_1/h_2)h\). The displayed Hom formula must likewise include the multiplicity for \(-\lambda_1,\lambda_2\). For \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2=1/2\), the endomorphism algebra has \(\mathbb Q_p\)-dimension four, whereas the printed Hom formula gives \(H^0(\mathcal O)=\mathbb Q_p\). The categorical equivalence itself is unaffected by correcting these recalled formulas.

## The binomial choice in Lemma 4.13: p. 24

For \(k=p^r\), the proposed \(i=p^r\) is excluded by \(1\leq i<k\). For \(k\geq2\), use \[i=\begin{cases}
 1,&p\nmid k,\\
 p^r,&k=p^rm,\ r\geq1,\ m>1,\ p\nmid m,\\
 p^{r-1},&k=p^r,\ r\geq1.
 \end{cases}\] Then \(v_p\binom{k}{i}=0,0,1\), respectively. The middle case follows from the lowest nonzero base-\(p\) digit; in the last case use \(\binom{k}{i}=(k/i)\binom{k-1}{i-1}\), whose second factor is a \(p\)-adic unit. Consequently \(a_k=\binom{k}{i}^{-1}b_{i,k-i}\) has the required uniform \(p^{-1}\) bound and tends to zero with \(k\). The later index \(b_{i,k-1}\) must also be \(b_{i,k-i}\). The freely chosen linear term can be zero. The analytic conclusion is preserved; 635 exact integer cases merely corroborate this general argument.

## The Ext statement of Lemma 4.24(i): p. 28

The second displayed isomorphism should read \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathrm{TVS}}(U_d,W)=0\), not \(W\). The proof explicitly derives this vanishing and part (ii) uses it. For example take \(d=1\), \(W=B_1\ne0\); applying Hom to \(0\to\mathbb Q_pt\to U_1\to B_1\to0\), the connecting map onto \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(B_1,B_1)=B_1\) is surjective, giving zero for \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(U_1,B_1)\). Keep the first formula \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{TVS}}(U_d,W)\simeq W\).

## The specialization in Example 5.11: p. 35

The first formula omits the twist \(\{r\}\) already present in Corollary 5.10. Take rank-one \(M\) with \(\varphi e=pe\), \(\operatorname{Fil}^1M_K=M_K\), and \(r=1\). It is admissible and \(X_{\mathrm{st}}^1(M)=\mathbb Q_pe\). Its smooth Galois-equivariant Hom into \(B_{\mathrm{st}}\) has Frobenius slope zero, whereas \(M^*\) has slope \(-1\). The correct answer is \(M^*\{1\}\). In the second formula the natural answer is \(M_K^*\), not \(M_K\), for \(j\leq0\); for \(j\geq1\) it remains zero. Remark 9.2 retains the necessary twist in the actual final comparison.

## The positive-period kernel and lattice direction: p. 38

In Proposition 5.20, a map from full \(B_{\mathrm{st}}\)-invariants to a quotient of \(M\otimes B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\) is not defined by the displayed natural period inclusion. Use \(B_{\mathrm{st}}^+\) there, or use full \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}/t^rL\) as target. The immediately following exact sequence and Remark 5.16 specify the intended object.

For the second Hom isomorphism, the admissible lattice must be contained in \(L\), rather than containing \(L\). Remark 5.14(iii) supplies precisely the contained lattice. Then its finite-dimensional period space \(V\) satisfies \(t^rV\subset V_{\mathrm{st}}^r(M,L)\); its period-ring span is all of \(M\otimes B_{\mathrm{st}}\), yielding the stated Hom conclusion by the argument of Proposition 5.18. When using that proposition, choose the admissible lattice inside \(t^{-r}L_0\) to obtain the required bounded positive periods; the nonnegative bundle at that maximal lattice allows this choice. No additional lower bound \(L_0\subset N\) is asserted here. That stronger, false demand is the separate Major problem above.

## The bicartesian sequence: (6.17), p. 43

Both arrows have plus signs, so their composite is twice the common period map. Already for a point and \(r=0\), the diagonal element \(1\) maps to \(2\ne0\). Replace the last map by \((x,y)\mapsto\iota_{\mathrm{HK}}(x)-\operatorname{can}(y)\), or put the minus sign in the first arrow. The bicartesian square (6.16) then gives its usual exact sequence.

## Finite type versus finite length: p. 51

In the proof of Theorem 8.1(c), the group \[H^i(F^rR\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X^\times/B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+))\] need not have finite length over \(R\). For the proper point, \(i=0\), it equals \(t^rR\), a nonzero free rank-one module. Finite type is the appropriate property. The diagram’s surjectivity deduction uses exactness of the upper row and the de Rham identifications, not the false finite-length claim, so this correction leaves that deduction intact.

## Which Künneth factor is perfect: p. 54

Lemma 8.13’s proof calls \(R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(T,R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y,\mathbb Z_p))\) perfect. The hypothesis makes the proper-factor complex \(R\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y,\mathbb Z_p)\) perfect, not necessarily this global-section complex. With \(Y\) a point and \(T\) a countable disjoint union of points, the latter already has an infinite-dimensional rational \(H^0\). Such \(T\) is smooth, separated, taut, and countable at infinity.

Replace the claim by perfectness of the proper-factor complex. A bounded finite-projective representative commutes with taking derived global sections on \(T\), giving the displayed projection formula and the same Künneth result. Proper base change and proper finiteness remain the cited inputs. No Künneth formula for two arbitrary nonproper factors is inferred.

# Proof coverage, boundary checks, and limits

The full review covered §§1–9, Appendix A, and the references, with the following decisive dependency checks.

In §2, the almost-\(C\)-representation review assumes finite \(K/\mathbb Q_p\), while the alternative argument in §2.3 addresses the more general base field used later. Period-Hom vanishing requires the stated continuity and Galois equivariance. Density is in the specified natural topology, not an assertion that \(K\) is dense for the \(t\)-adic topology.

In §§3–4, curvature and numerical height were kept distinct: torsion supported away from \(\infty\) can have positive curvature and height zero. The generating-image hypothesis in Lemma 3.23, the separability and sympathetic-algebra conditions, and the same-type hypotheses of Theorem 4.30 were retained. The cocycle estimate, Mittag–Leffler and Baire steps, finite-length dévissage, and density arguments were checked in the categories actually specified; no unrestricted higher-Ext assertion was substituted.

In §5, monodromy is removed by its finite nilpotent exponential. On a Frobenius block of slope \(a/h\leq r\), the resulting equation \(\varphi^h x=p^{rh-a}x\), the independent unramified embeddings, and the finite quotient needed by Nakayama give the generation used in Lemma 5.17. The zero-slope endpoint and the positive-period restrictions were checked separately.

For the proper comparison in §6 the auxiliary integer must satisfy \(s>r\), so that degree \(r+1\) lies within the comparison range; the argument does not presuppose the degeneration it proves. The Stein result retains the Fréchet/LB distinction, strictness, and the quotient by \(\ker d\); at \(r=0\), negative-degree forms are zero.

In §7, the snake-lemma kernels and cokernels are the adjacent \(H^1\)’s of the modified bundles. Each nonzero such \(H^1\) has strictly negative height, by (3.10), so the two error terms cannot cancel in the height identity. The group in degree \((r,r+1)\) on p. 48 is syntomic, as its footnote states; it was not replaced by pro-étale cohomology outside the valid truncation range. The noncanonical splitting of §7.3 is explicitly unused later.

For §8, the product argument preserves nonnegative vector-bundle slopes, with the remaining coherent torsion supported at infinity. The resolution must be an isomorphism over the complement being studied. The almost-proper comparison quoted from the bibliography is an external input, not independently proved here. Tube hypotheses and radii, the countable exhaustion, Artinian-BC Mittag–Leffler arguments, and the finite proper factor in the product were retained.

In §9, the key assertion is density of appropriate cycles, proved using the Banach basis and finite partial sums, rather than a general claim that completion commutes with cohomology. Continuity then gives the period-Hom vanishing. The \(K\)-side twist and the finite-length density step on the \(C\)-side were checked. Appendix A uses strictness in the quasi-abelian setting; splitting requires the stated spherical-completeness or separability condition.

The full external proofs of the curve classification, Le Bras’s equivalence, the equivalence between weak admissibility and admissibility, previous syntomic comparison theorems, proper base change, resolution, and the almost-proper input remain outside this independent reconstruction. These limitations are not classified as errors. No later arXiv revision or journal version was substituted.
