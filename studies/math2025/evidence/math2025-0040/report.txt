# Definitions, quantifiers, and checked dependency structure

Stickiness controls packing dimension of the family of lines, not merely its Hausdorff dimension. The near-sticky formulation has the order for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there exists \(\eta>0\). Section 2 uses the bounded line chart \(\mathcal L_n\), a fixed dimensional tube thickness, and shadings that are unions of \(\delta\)-grid cubes. Covering is defined through the line metric.

Definition 2.1 requires essential distinctness, a directional multiplicity bound for covers at every intermediate scale, and a lower bound for total shading mass. A leaf-scale condition alone is insufficient. \(M(s,t,\delta)\) is an infimum of union volume subject to these conditions. \(N(s,t)\) uses a limsup of logarithmic ratios; \(\sigma_n\) is obtained in the small-parameter regime. Extremal families are obtained along a sequence, not necessarily at every small scale. In the checked argument of Proposition 2.1, packing dimension supplies a countable upper-box decomposition, followed by a positive-direction piece and scale pigeonholing. The sign reversal on dividing by \(\log\delta<0\) and the limsup choice are essential.

The central chain is \[\begin{gathered}
\text{Lemma 3.3}\to\text{Proposition 3.2}\to\text{Proposition 4.1}
\to\text{Proposition 5.1}\\
\to\text{Proposition 6.1}\to\text{Corollary 6.5}.
\end{gathered}\] Proposition 7.1 is compared with Corollary 6.5 to force \(\sigma=0\); Proposition 2.1 then returns the main conclusions. Theorem 5.2, the sum-product input, is proved using Section 8. The thin-tube discretization and bootstrap lie on that route. The Section 4 AD-set definition gives upper covering bounds; it does not assert matching lower Ahlfors bounds. Section 8 normalized Frostman and thin-tube inequalities require their own explicitly stated conversions.

# F1 — Minor: an unnecessary rounded-volume equivalence fails

## Location and precise claim

Definition 3.2, p. 11, defines rescaled shading as the union of \((\delta/\rho)\)-cubes meeting the affine image of the shading. In Lemma 3.3, pp. 12–13, equation (3.5) asserts, for \(n=3\), \[\left|\bigcup_{T\in\mathbb T}Y(T)\right|
 \sim \rho^2
 \left|\bigcup_{\widehat T\in\widehat{\mathbb T}}
              \widehat Y(\widehat T)\right|.
 \tag{F1.1}\] Its justification (3.4) is a Jacobian identity for raw images, not for their cubical enlargement. Relevant TeX lines are 420–425, 456–494, and 516–526. The final average-multiplicity identity on p. 13 also needs a separate justification after enlargement.

## An admissible family

Fix \(\varepsilon=1/4\). For any \(\eta>0\) selected by the lemma, put \[a=\min(\eta/2,1/8),\quad \rho=\delta^{1/2},\quad
 L=\lceil\delta^{-a}\rceil,\quad h=\delta/\rho.\] As \(\delta\to0\), we have \(\rho\in[\delta^{1-\varepsilon},1]\), \(L\rho\to0\), and \(a<\eta\). Let \(c=c(3)>0\) be the fixed constant in Definition 3.2. Choose a fixed \(K\ge100/c\), enlarged by a dimensional constant if needed. For \[u\in(K\delta\mathbb Z)^2\cap[-\rho/1000,\rho/1000]^2\] take the tube \(T_u\) with axis \(\ell_u=\{(uz,z):z\in\mathbb R\}\). There are \(J\asymp_K(\rho/\delta)^2\) such tubes. Their lines pass through the origin, are in \(\mathcal L_3\), and have third unit-direction coordinate at least \(1/2\). Direction separation is at least a fixed multiple of \(K\delta\), so the tubes are essentially distinct. The vertical \(\rho\)-tube covers them all.

For every \(\tau\in[\delta,1]\), choose a maximal angular net in this cap at a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(\tau\), with the same base point. The corresponding \(\tau\)-tubes cover the family. Sphere packing gives only \(O(1)\) members essentially parallel to any common \(\tau\)-tube, hence at most \(\delta^{-\eta}\) for sufficiently small \(\delta\). This verifies the full every-scale hypothesis.

For integers \(m\) with \(mL\delta\in[1/2,3/4-\delta]\), choose the grid cube \(Q_{u,m}\) containing \[(u(mL\delta+\delta/2),\,mL\delta+\delta/2).\] Use a fixed half-open grid convention; boundaries have no volume. Every cube lies within \(\sqrt3\delta\) of the axis and inside the ambient cube, so it is a legitimate shading cube. Let \(Y(T_u)=\bigcup_m Q_{u,m}\). At these heights, distinct slopes are separated by a fixed multiple of \(K\delta\), and different layers are disjoint. All chosen cubes are disjoint, so the shading multiplicity equals one. With \(M\asymp(L\delta)^{-1}\) layers, \[|E|=\sum_u|Y(T_u)|=JM\delta^3\asymp_K\rho^2/L
 \ge\delta^\eta\rho^2\] for sufficiently small \(\delta\), since \(a<\eta\). Thus all hypotheses of Lemma 3.3 hold. That lemma does not assume extremality of its input.

## Failure after every permitted refinement

The rescaling here is \[\phi(x,y,z)=(cx/\rho,cy/\rho,cz),\qquad
 |\det\phi|=c^3\rho^{-2}.\] The rescaled axes remain essentially distinct by the choice of \(K\). Any permitted refinement retains a polylogarithmic fraction of shading mass. Its shadings remain unions of the original disjoint fine cubes; denote their union by \(E'\).

An \(h\)-grid cube can meet images of at most \(C/(L\rho)\) retained cubes from one tube: its vertical preimage has length at most \(h/c+O(\delta)\), whereas layers have period \(L\delta\). It cannot meet images from two distinct tubes. At the chosen heights their rescaled horizontal separation is at least a fixed multiple of \(cKh\). Variation within a coarse vertical cell and the widths of images are only \(O(h)\), and the fixed choice of \(K\) separates them.

Assign each retained fine cube to any coarse cube meeting its image. If \(N'\) fine cubes are retained, at least \(c_0N'L\rho\) distinct coarse cubes are assigned. The rounded union satisfies \[|\widehat E'|\ge c_0N'L\rho h^3
             =c_0L\rho^{-2}|E'|.\] A permitted refinement has \(|E'|\ge(\log(1/\delta))^{-C_1}|E|\) for a fixed \(C_1\). It follows that \[\frac{\rho^2|\widehat E'|}{|E|}
 \ge c_0L(\log(1/\delta))^{-C_1}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{F1.2}\] The power growth beats every fixed polylogarithmic loss. Thus (3.5) fails even after the allowed refinement and even if its comparability notation tolerates polylogarithms.

## Counterevidence and the exact error boundary

The Jacobian identity for \(\phi(E')\) is correct. Definition 3.2 and Figure 1 explicitly include rounding, so \(\widehat Y\) cannot simply be read as a raw image. A constant-multiplicity refinement does not fix the issue: this example already has multiplicity one, and (F1.2) holds for every allowed mass-retaining refinement.

This does not disprove the conclusion of Lemma 3.3. Its original multiplicity is one and satisfies that conclusion. The example is not claimed to be extremal for Proposition 3.2. It disproves the precise intermediate volume comparison under the hypotheses of its proof. The final multiplicity identity needs a separate justification, but this multiplicity-one example alone does not disprove that separate identity.

## A sufficient multiplicity inequality and verified local repair

Set \(h=\delta/\rho\), and retain the constant-multiplicity refinement \((\mathbb T',Y')_\delta\) constructed in the proof. Write its multiplicity as \(\mu\), and let \((\widehat{\mathbb T},\widehat Y)_h\) be its unit rescaling. Every \(h\)-cube \(Q\) in \(\widehat E=\bigcup_{\widehat T}\widehat Y(\widehat T)\) meets \(\phi(E_{\mathbb T'})\). Choose \(q=\phi(p)\) in that intersection. Since \(p\) belongs to exactly \(\mu\) retained original shadings, \(Q\) belongs to at least \(\mu\) rounded shadings. These correspond to distinct tubes: \(\phi\) is invertible, and the preceding refinement makes the rescaled coaxial lines essentially distinct. Thus, integrating over the half-open grid cubes, \[\mu|\widehat E|
 \le \sum_{\widehat T\in\widehat{\mathbb T}}|\widehat Y(\widehat T)|.
 \tag{F1.3}\] Repeated intersections of a cube with one tube do not enter this count. No rounded-volume equivalence is used.

It suffices to consider \(0<\varepsilon\le1\). Choose \(s,t>0\) such that \[N(s,t)\le\sigma_n+\varepsilon/8,\qquad s,t\le\varepsilon/4,\] and then take \(\eta\) sufficiently small relative to \(\varepsilon,s,t\). The existing mass and covering transfers make the rescaled family admissible for \(M(s,t,h)\). In detail, if the refinement retains a fraction \((\log(1/\delta))^{-A}\), the raw-image Jacobian and inclusion of the raw images in the rounded shadings give \[\sum_{\widehat T}|\widehat Y(\widehat T)|
 \ge c_n\delta^\eta(\log(1/\delta))^{-A}
 \ge\delta^{2\eta}\ge h^{2\eta/\varepsilon}\ge h^s\] for sufficiently small \(\delta\), on choosing \(2\eta/\varepsilon\le s\). The original covering transfer has multiplicity bounded by a fixed constant times \(h^{-2\eta/\varepsilon}\); choose \(2\eta/\varepsilon<t\) to absorb that constant into \(h^{-t}\). These are the original parameter requirements, with explicit slack.

By the definition of the limsup in \(N(s,t)\), for all sufficiently small \(h\), \[|\widehat E|\ge M(s,t,h)\ge h^{\sigma_n+\varepsilon/4}.\] The negative sign of \(\log h\) gives the displayed lower-bound direction. The covering condition and essential distinctness give \[\#\widehat{\mathbb T}\le C h^{1-n-t},\qquad
 \sum_{\widehat T}|\widehat Y(\widehat T)|\le C h^{-t}.\] One may use the cover at scale \(2h\) to avoid any endpoint convention: each such covering tube contains only \(O_n(1)\) essentially distinct \(h\)-tubes, and an \(h\)-tube has volume \(O_n(h^{n-1})\). Combining these estimates with (F1.3) yields \[\mu\le C h^{-\sigma_n-\varepsilon/4-t}
 \le C h^{-\sigma_n-\varepsilon/2}
 \le h^{-\sigma_n-\varepsilon}\] after decreasing the scale threshold. The range \(\rho\in[\delta^{1-\varepsilon},1]\) gives \(\delta\le h\le\delta^\varepsilon\), so every required small-\(h\) threshold is uniform in \(\rho\). For \(\varepsilon>1\) that range is empty when \(\delta<1\).

This proves the precise multiplicity conclusion of Lemma 3.3 used in Proposition 3.2, Step 2, within the existing parameter hierarchy. Delete (3.5), and replace the final multiplicity equality by (F1.3). The demonstrated error concerns an unnecessary stronger volume equivalence; the inspected downstream argument needs only the valid one-sided inequality. Its classification is therefore Minor on the basis of actual dependence, rather than the length or ease of repair. The false volume equivalence is not restored, and this local repair does not certify the remaining steps of Proposition 3.2 or the main theorem.

# F2 — Major: Proposition 7.1 omits intercept control

## Location and uniform quantifiers

Proposition 7.1 and (7.1), p. 47, assert \[\left\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb T}\chi_{\pi_f(T)}\right\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^2)}
 \le\delta^{-1-\varepsilon},\qquad
 \pi_f(x,y,z)=(x+f(z)y,z),
 \tag{F2.1}\] for direction-separated tubes and \(1\le|f'|\le2\), \(|f''|\le1/100\). The threshold \(\delta_0\) is chosen from \(\varepsilon\) before \(f\). There is no condition on \(f(0)\) or \(\|f\|_\infty\). Lemma 7.3 on p. 47 does assume \(f(0)=0\). Its proof and the proof of Proposition 7.1 on p. 48 use the resulting bound on \(|f|\). Relevant TeX lines are 2027–2034, 2068–2077, and 2109–2125.

## Complete counterexample

Let \(\varepsilon=1/4\). Given any proposed \(\delta_0>0\), choose \(0<\delta<\min(\delta_0,1/10)\) and set \[M=\delta^{-3},\qquad f(z)=M+z.\] Both derivative hypotheses hold exactly. Take the single vertical \(\delta\)-tube through the origin. Its axis is in \(\mathcal L_3\) and direction separation is vacuous. It contains \([-\delta,\delta]^2\times[-1,1]\). For fixed \(z\), the projected interval has length \(2\delta(1+M+z)\) because \(M+z>0\). Thus \[|\pi_f(T)|\ge4\delta(M+1),\qquad
 \|\chi_{\pi_f(T)}\|_{3/2}
 \ge[4\delta(M+1)]^{2/3}
 >4^{2/3}\delta^{-4/3}>\delta^{-5/4}.\] This contradicts (F2.1). The uniform quantifiers allow \(f\) to depend on \(\delta\); allowing \(\delta_0\) to depend on \(f\) would change the proposition. For the actual exact-arithmetic test \(\delta=1/256\), \(M=16777216\), the cube of the norm is at least \(281475010265089/4096\), exceeding the cube \(1073741824\) of the claimed bound \(1024\). The general proof, not this single test, defeats every threshold.

## A normalized local replacement

Adding \(f(0)=0\) gives \(|f|\le2\) on \([-1,1]\). Conditional on the cited Pramanik–Yang–Zahl theorem, the following verifies the intended local projection argument. For \(g=a+bf+dtf\) the coefficient-to-jet matrix is \[A(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&f&tf\\0&f'&f+tf'\\0&f''&2f'+tf''
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det A(t)=2(f')^2-ff''\ge1.98.\] Entries and inverse are uniformly bounded. The coefficient norm, the pointwise jet norm, and the induced \(C^2\) norm have the required uniform comparisons. This gives cinematic separation and diameter bounds. Three-dimensional coefficient space gives a uniform doubling constant. The two halves of \([-1,1]\) are treated using \(f(t)\) and \(f(-t)\).

Partition directions into fixed charts and \(O(\delta^{-1})\) bins for the first slope. In each bin the second slopes are separated up to bounded multiplicity. Norm equivalence bounds the number of curves in any \(C^2\) ball of radius \(r\ge\delta\) by \(O(r/\delta)\). Bounded \(f\) puts every projected tube inside a fixed \(C\delta\)-neighborhood of its graph. The literal \(2\delta\) constant is unnecessary and must accommodate the manuscript’s dimensional tube thickness. Apply the cinematic estimate with a smaller auxiliary loss and sum the bins; after reducing \(\delta_0\), the bound is \(\delta^{-1-\varepsilon}\).

The external text actually consulted was Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.7, and its whole-plane consequence in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02259v1. Its Frostman scale range is \(r\ge\delta\) and its loss parameter can be chosen below the target loss. Its proof was not independently reconstructed. The statement was first read as retrieved text. After an unsuccessful browser screenshot request, the exact v1 PDF was acquired, and its original p. 3 was rendered and actually inspected. The saved external PDF, page image, acquisition hash, and inspection record are in the evidence directory. This does not constitute verification of the external theorem’s proof.

## Counterevidence and the unresolved preceding interface

The introductory outline on p. 5 has a bounded \(C^2\) norm, and Lemma 7.3 explicitly has the normalization. These favor a missing-hypothesis interpretation, but do not insert the condition into Proposition 7.1. Proposition 6.1, p. 46, uses \(\widetilde f(z)=100m^{-2}f(mz/100)\), which can enlarge an unchecked intercept. After translating a height \(z_0\) to zero, the bounded shear \(x'=x+f(z_0)y\) subtracts the intercept before derivative rescaling. The preceding bounded \(C^2\) class makes this shear controlled. Its effects on the tube chart, lengths, separation, shadings, and all exponent losses still need full verification. That interface is not completed here. The final uniform estimate is false as stated on the main dependency chain, so the classification is Major. The normalized replacement is compatible with the intended main conclusion; no main-theorem counterexample is established.

# F3 — Minor: the specified threshold does not retain \(1-c\) mass

## Location and admissible test

Lemma 8.3, p. 53, starts with a Borel set \(E\) of product probability at least \(1-c\). It forms \(E'\) from the whole product grid cells whose relative \(E\)-density is at least \(c\), then asserts that the discarded mass is less than \(c\). Relevant source lines are 2347–2361; the thin-tube estimate continues on p. 54.

Let \(c=1/10\), let \(G_1\) be one \(\delta\)-square, and let \(G_2\) consist of 200 disjoint \(\delta\)-squares. For sufficiently small \(\delta\) they fit inside the unit square. Each product cell has probability \(1/200\). Let \(E\) fill 179 cells and occupy a measurable coordinate slice of relative volume \(1/20\) in each of the other 21. Then \[(\nu_1\times\nu_2)(E)
 =\frac{179+21/20}{200}
 =\frac{3601}{4000}>\frac9{10}.\] Set \(\beta=0\) and \(K=1\). For every base point, every line through it, and every \(r>0\), the thin-tube slice has \(\nu_2\)-measure at most \(1=Kr^0\). All continuous thin-tube hypotheses therefore hold.

The prescribed threshold discards the 21 partly filled cells. It gives \[|E'|/(|G_1||G_2|)=179/200<9/10.\] This disproves the mass assertion for that construction, not existence of some other discretized thin-tube set. For this \(\beta=0\) example, the whole product already works.

## A verified weaker replacement and its limits

If the discarded cells have total probability \(q\), their missing \(E\)-density is greater than \(1-c\). Thus \[(1-c)q\le1-(\nu_1\times\nu_2)(E)\le c,\qquad
 q\le c/(1-c)\le2c\quad(0<c<1/2).\] The construction retains at least \(1-2c\). It also gives thin-tube constant \(5^\beta K/c\). Indeed, any \(\delta\)-square meeting \(N_r(\ell)\) is inside \(N_{(1+\sqrt2)r}(\ell)\) when \(r\ge\delta\). Moving the line parallel to a different point in the base square adds at most \(\sqrt2r\), so radius \(5r\) suffices. Integrate the original bound over the base square and use the density threshold \(c\) to obtain the factor \(K/c\). This proves a replacement with retained mass \(1-2c\). The endpoint \(c=0\) is excluded, consistently with division by \(c\) in the asserted constant.

A continuous loss \(3c\) followed by this discretization is bounded by \(6c\). After \(N\) fixed bootstrap steps, a fixed multiple of \(6^Nc\) replaces one of \(3^Nc\). In the application \(c\) is a positive power of \(\delta\), whereas \(N\) depends only on fixed parameters. For example, \(2\cdot6^N\delta^{\alpha'}\le\delta^{\alpha'/2}\) holds after reducing the scale threshold. This changes constants and thresholds, not the powers in that particular bookkeeping step. Thin-tube constants must also be updated through the recurrence. The other steps of Lemma 8.8 and the external bootstrap are not certified by this calculation.

This is Minor: the specified construction is invalid but a weaker bound has the required finite-iteration type of loss. Exact retention \(1-c\) in the original lemma has not been recovered, nor has the whole Section 8 proof been repaired.
