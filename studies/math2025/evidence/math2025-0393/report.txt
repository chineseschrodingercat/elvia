Abstract: The paper develops a coupling criterion for random dynamical systems with stationary, possibly correlated noise. The selected v3 repairs the earlier coupling marginal error. Two remaining Major problems concern the transfer of noise regularity to the abstract hypotheses and a common continuous-time invariant set asserted under a sliding-window energy bound. Explicit examples invalidate these steps without disproving the conditional abstract mixing theorem. Two further Minor problems concern time-boundedness of the proposed noise and an unjustified strengthening from dual-Lipschitz to total-variation type mixing. Each finding below includes its hypotheses, decisive proof, actual scope and repair limits.

# Scope, contribution, and assessment

All 56 pages of the selected source were read, including the assumptions, proofs, applications, appendix and references. The principal chain examined was the Markov lift, the local change-of-noise coupling, recurrence and squeezing, the approximate right inverse in Lemma 3.5, and the use of Theorem 3.4 in the noise constructions and ODE/PDE applications. The coupling construction in v3, pp. 22–23, now glues measures with the same middle marginal \[\Psi(U,U',\cdot)_*\mathcal P(U;\cdot).\] Equation (2.38) also includes the required variation distance between \(\mathcal P(U;\cdot)\) and \(\mathcal P(U';\cdot)\). These are material corrections to the corresponding v2 argument (Proposition 1.4 there); they are credited as current repairs, not repeated as findings.

The measurable gluing hypothesis was checked against Bogachev–Malofeev, Corollary 3.7, in the exact retained preprint arXiv:1904.03642v2, pp. 10–12. The spaces here are Polish and the parameterized pair laws are measurable, so its common-marginal construction fits the corrected step. This is a check of that dependency, not a review of the entire reference. Other cited monographs and PDE papers were not all independently audited.

The recurrence/squeezing mechanism was checked directly: once a pair is within distance \(\theta\), the sum of its subsequent failure probabilities is at most \(N\theta/(1-q)\); the probability of a first failure after \(r\) successful contractions is \(O(q^r)\). Uniform positive-probability access to a smaller neighborhood yields geometric returns and, with \(\theta\) small, exponential control of repeated attempts. Thus the intended dual-Lipschitz coupling conclusion is consistent with the stated local and global criteria. The problems below concern additional assertions and hypothesis verification. The overall first-review assessment is Major, with two Major and two Minor findings. No counterexample to Theorem 3.4 with all its written hypotheses is asserted.

# Noise regularity and the infinite-dimensional applications

Problem 1 (Major: unsupported transfers of noise regularity).  The infinite-dimensional part of \((\eta1)\) on p. 5 does not imply the total-variation Lipschitz condition (SF) on p. 16, contrary to p. 29. Moreover, the spatial product construction (4.16) need not preserve even total-variation continuity. The literal support requirement on the density in (DLP\('\)), p. 28, is itself impossible for a nonzero continuous density on an infinite-dimensional ambient space with compact \(K\).

## A. A literal counterexample to the claimed implication

Set \[f(x)=\tfrac34(1-x^2)\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}(x),\qquad
 a_l=2^{-l},\qquad r_l=4^{-l}.\] The function \(f\) is a globally Lipschitz probability density, \(\int xf(x)\,dx=0\), and \(\int|x|f(x)\,dx=3/8\). For every \(l\), let \((X_{k,l})_{k\in\mathbb Z}\) be a stationary Markov chain with invariant density \(f\) and transition density \[p_l(x,y)=f(y)(1+a_lxy),\qquad -1\leq x,y\leq1.\] Invariance follows by integrating in \(x\), and \(p_l(x,y)\geq(1-a_l)f(y)\geq f(y)/2\). Take these chains independently over \(l\) and put \(\eta_k=\sum_l r_lX_{k,l}e_l\) in \(E=\ell^2\). The coordinate box \(K=\prod_l[-r_l,r_l]\) is compact in \(\ell^2\); positive finite-dimensional cylinder probabilities give full support \(K^{\mathbb Z_-}\) for the past. The conditional kernel is thus defined on every past required by \((\eta1)\), not just a specially chosen null set.

The density of the \(l\)th scaled coordinate is \[p^l_\xi(z)=r_l^{-1}f(z/r_l)
       \left(1+a_l\frac{\xi_{0,l}}{r_l}\frac z{r_l}\right).\] For each fixed \(l\) this is jointly Lipschitz in \((\xi,z)\) for the prescribed history metric; no uniform constant in \(l\) is claimed or required there. Also \[\|p^l_\xi-p^l_{\xi'}\|_{L^1}
 =\frac{3a_l}{8}
       \left|\frac{\xi_{0,l}-\xi'_{0,l}}{r_l}\right|.\] Since \(\sum_l a_l<\infty\), dominated convergence proves exactly the summed \(L^1\) continuity in \((\eta1)\). The coefficients \(r_l\) are positive and summable, and the scalar stationary coordinates are bounded by one.

Equation reference: eq:badSF. Choose pasts which differ only at time zero in coordinate \(l\), with values \(+r_l\) and \(-r_l\). Their distance is \(2r_l\), independent of the choice of the history weight \(\iota>1\). The event that the next \(l\)th coordinate is positive has a probability difference \[2a_l\int_0^1 yf(y)\,dy=\frac{3a_l}{8}.\] Consequently \[
 \frac{\|Q(\xi;\cdot)-Q(\xi';\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{var}}}{d(\xi,\xi')}
 \ \geq\ \frac{3a_l}{16r_l}
 =\frac3{16}\,2^l\ \longrightarrow\infty .\] This event lower bound is valid for either usual normalization of variation.

The recurrence assumption \((\eta2)\) also holds. Given a neighborhood of zero, choose finitely many coordinates so that the remaining deterministic \(\ell^2\) tail is smaller than half its radius. In the finitely many selected coordinates, the minorization by \(f/2\) gives a strictly positive lower bound for any prescribed finite block to stay near zero, uniformly over the entire past. Taking the product gives the required uniform block probability. Thus recurrence does not repair [eq:badSF] (#eq:badSF). If a dynamics satisfying the introductory smoothing and controllability assumptions is desired, take \(H=V=\mathbb R\) and \(S(u,\eta)=\langle\eta,e_1\rangle\). Its free map is zero and its noise derivative is onto. This dynamics actually mixes; the example refutes the claimed implication used in the proof, not that mixing conclusion.

## B. Infinite independent spatial copies can destroy continuity

The failure of Proposition 4.10, p. 43, is stronger than a missing constant in a product estimate. Choose a bounded trigonometric orthonormal basis \((\varphi_l)\) of \(L^2(0,1)\) with \(\varphi_1=1\), and positive coefficients \(c_l\) so small that \(\sum_lc_l\|\varphi_l\|_\infty\leq1\). Let \(\nu\) be the law of \(\sum_l c_l Z_l\varphi_l\), where \(Z_l\) have density \(f\) independently, and write \(c_1>0\) for its first radius. Set \[\chi(y)=\max(-1,\min(1,\langle y,\varphi_1\rangle/c_1)),\qquad
 g(\xi,y)=1+\alpha\chi(\xi_0)\chi(y),\quad 0<\alpha<1.\] This is globally Lipschitz on the domain of Proposition 4.7, \(g\geq1-\alpha\), and its normalizing factor \(m(\xi)\) is one by symmetry. The resulting scalar block process has a first coefficient following the Markov kernel \(f(y)(1+\alpha xy)\); its other coefficients can be independent between blocks. It is stationary, has full past support, satisfies scalar (SF), has regular finite-dimensional conditional densities, and satisfies the uniform recurrence condition by the same minorization argument. Every time trajectory is bounded by one.

Take independent identical spatial copies of this scalar process and coefficients \(b_j=2^{-j}\) as in (4.16). Change only the first temporal coefficient in spatial coordinate \(j\) of the most recent past block between \(\pm c_1b_j\). The history distance is \(2c_1b_j\to0\). Nevertheless the event that the same coefficient in the next block is positive has probability difference \(3\alpha/8\), independent of \(j\). Both sequences of pasts approach the zero past; their conditional laws cannot both converge to its law in variation. Hence (SF) fails even at the level of continuity.

Rescaling independent coordinates makes them small in the Hilbert norm, but does not make distinguishable scalar probability laws close in variation. Faster spatial decay of \(b_j\) alone cannot fix this example. The scalar construction has bounded paths, so the additional scalar boundedness assumed in the Navier–Stokes application does not exclude it.

## C. The literal compact-support requirement in DLP-prime

There is a separate formal obstruction that must not be concealed in claiming an example satisfies the printed hypotheses. For fixed \(\xi\), the written (DLP\('\)) requires a Lipschitz function \(\rho_n(\xi,\cdot)\) on all of \(E\), supported in compact \(K\). If \(E\) is infinite dimensional, \(K\) has empty interior. A continuous function nonzero at a point is nonzero on a neighborhood of that point, which here would force an open ball into \(K\). Thus \(\rho_n(\xi,\cdot)\equiv0\), incompatible with a normalized conditional density. For \(g\equiv1\), formula (4.14) itself gives a nonzero product of the first \(n\) coordinate densities independent of the tail; it is not supported in compact \(K\) as a function on all of \(E\).

Accordingly, example B is a failure of the stated construction and of its proposed (SF) transfer after the natural finite-fiber interpretation of (DLP\('\)). It is not presented as satisfying the impossible literal infinite-dimensional support clause. Example A independently satisfies all the actual clauses of \((\eta1)\) and \((\eta2)\) and refutes the explicit implication on p. 29.

## Impact and repair limits

These failures occur in the passage from the advertised noise classes to the hypotheses of the central mixing criterion. In particular, Theorem 5.4’s proof, p. 49, invokes Theorem 3.4 and Proposition 4.10 without establishing the required (SF) for general correlated spatial copies. This is a core application gap, hence Major; it is not merely an omitted factor in an estimate. The abstract theorem with (SF) explicitly assumed is not refuted, nor is mixing of every affected PDE asserted false.

Possible repairs include imposing (SF) directly and proving it for a narrower noise class, or adding quantitative summability of the scaled coordinate variation-Lipschitz constants. Mere summed continuity is insufficient. For identical nontrivial spatial memory, a different noise model or a coupling argument using a weaker metric is needed; the existing product assertion cannot be justified by amplitude decay alone. The density support clause should be formulated in finite fibers (for example, bounded support in the \(F_n\) variable on relevant tail fibers) while the conditional measure remains supported in \(K\). That textual repair removes the empty-interior contradiction but leaves both variation-distance failures intact. A full revised application theorem is not supplied here.

# The common invariant set in the ODE application

Problem 2 (Major: Proposition 5.1’s invariant set is false under (5.3)).  The common compact set asserted on pp. 44–45 for all continuous times does not follow from (BAS), even for a globally exponentially stable linear oscillator system. The proof incorrectly treats concatenated controls as retaining the same sliding-window energy bound.

Use two oscillators, \(n=2\), with \[V(q_1,q_2)=\tfrac12(q_1^2+q_2^2),\qquad \gamma_1=\gamma_2=1.\] Both endpoints are damped, and \(q_i'=p_i,\ p_i'=-q_i-p_i+\zeta_i\). This is precisely a smooth coercive potential in (5.1). For each oscillator let \[A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P=\begin{pmatrix}3/2&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A^{T}P+PA=-I.\] The eigenvalues of \(A\) have real part \(-1/2\) and \(P>0\). Writing \(L=\sum_{i=1}^2(q_i,p_i)P(q_i,p_i)^T\) and \(c=1/(2\lambda_{\max}(P))>0\), Young’s inequality gives \[L'(t)\leq-cL(t)+\tfrac52\bigl(|\zeta_1(t)|^2+|\zeta_2(t)|^2\bigr).\] If (5.3) holds with any \(M>0\), splitting the convolution into intervals of length one yields \[L(t)\leq e^{-ct}L(0)+\frac{5M}{2(1-e^{-c})}.\] This proves (BAS), with a small additional margin for finite-time absorption, uniformly over every initial bounded ball. The unforced system converges to zero uniformly on every bounded set. It therefore satisfies the global stability property intended by (SE), including its displayed uniform convergence on any proposed compact \(X\).

Now fix \(M=1\) and suppose a nonempty compact \(X\) were invariant for every control satisfying (5.3). Choose a point \((q^*,p^*)\in X\) at which \(p_1\) is maximal, and set \[A_0=1+|p_1^*|+|q_1^*|,\qquad
 \delta=\frac1{2A_0^2},\qquad
 \zeta_1(t)=A_0\mathbf1_{[0,\delta]}(t),\quad \zeta_2(t)=0.\] Every sliding unit window has total energy at most \(A_0^2\delta=1/2<1\). At time zero, however, \[p_1'(0+)=-q_1^*-p_1^*+A_0>0.\] For sufficiently small positive \(t<\delta\) the trajectory has \(p_1(t)>\max_Xp_1\), so it leaves \(X\). Thus no such nonempty compact invariant set exists. The absorption assertion rules out using an empty \(X\). This counterexample does not depend on a numerical time step or an unproved stability criterion.

The stronger pointwise bound (1.12) would exclude arbitrarily high pulses. It is not a hypothesis of Proposition 5.1: p. 44 expressly treats it as a stronger sufficient condition, whereas (5.3) and (BAS) are the hypotheses under discussion. Similarly, invariance only at integer times is different from the displayed assertion for every \(t\geq0\).

The set built on p. 45 contains states reachable under controls of energy budget \(M\). Extending an arbitrary such state by another admissible control may put pieces of two controls into one sliding window, with energy as large as \(2M\). The asserted invariance therefore does not follow from its definition. It is subsequently used in the reduction of Theorem 5.2 to a compact invariant state space and in formulating (LC), so the false lemma is a Major proof dependency.

An integer-time repair is plausible and more appropriate to the stated mixing conclusion: concatenate aligned unit blocks, use the fact that their arbitrary sliding-window energy is at most \(2M\), and construct an invariant set for the block map using (BAS) at budget \(2M\). Compactness and the domain of (LC) must then be rechecked for that set. Alternatively one can impose a suitable uniform pointwise forcing bound and prove a genuine continuous-time invariant set. Neither option is a proof that the present statement of Proposition 5.1 is valid.

There is also a separate proof-level qualification on p. 46: convergence in (SE) on a compact set does not, by itself, give the global norm contraction on all of \(H\) required by (GD). Uniform attraction on the repaired compact set can instead be used directly for the global accessibility condition of Theorem 2.3. The printed argument needs this change; no counterexample to the final integer-time mixing conclusion is asserted.

# Two local excess claims

Problem 3 (Minor: \(L^2\) coefficient bounds do not give bounded paths).  Propositions 4.9 and 4.10, pp. 42–43, claim almost surely bounded time trajectories from the construction on p. 41. An arbitrary orthonormal basis and \(\sum_l c_l^2<\infty\) do not imply this assertion.

Partition \((0,1)\) into \(I_j=(2^{-j},2^{1-j})\), of lengths \(2^{-j}\). Choose an orthonormal basis whose odd members are \(\varphi_{2j-1}=2^{j/2}\mathbf1_{I_j}\); enumerate, at the even indices, orthonormal bases of the zero-mean subspaces of all the \(L^2(I_j)\). These together form a complete orthonormal basis. Give the odd coefficients radii \(c_{2j-1}=2^{-j/4}\) and the even ones radii \(c_{2j}=2^{-j}\), and take independent coefficients \(\zeta_l=c_lZ_l\) with density \(f\) for \(Z_l\). The prescribed square summability holds, each coefficient density is Lipschitz and compactly supported, and the resulting Hilbert box is compact in \(L^2\). Choose \(g\equiv1\) in the construction.

All even basis functions have integral zero over each \(I_j\). Hence the average of the \(L^2\) random function on \(I_j\) equals \(\zeta_{2j-1}2^{j/2}\). Independently for every \(j\), \[\mathbb P\{|\zeta_{2j-1}|\geq c_{2j-1}/2\}
 =2\int_{1/2}^1f(x)\,dx=\frac5{16}.\] The second Borel–Cantelli lemma implies that infinitely many averages have absolute value at least \(\tfrac12\,2^{j/4}\). No essentially bounded representative can have these averages. The failure already occurs within one time block, despite compact support and uniform boundedness in \(L^2\).

This example satisfies the coefficient and basis assumptions of the construction; it does not purport to satisfy the impossible literal support clause discussed in Problem Problem 1 (#p:noise). A sufficient repair is \(\sum_l c_l\|\varphi_l\|_\infty<\infty\). The Navier–Stokes section separately assumes scalar paths bounded by one, which excludes this example. Thus this demonstrated time-regularity overstatement is Minor; it does not independently refute that application.

Problem 4 (Minor: dual-Lipschitz convergence does not imply (4.11)).  The sentence on p. 39 deducing strong feeble mixing of a stationary weak solution from Proposition 3.7 is false in the stated generality. The supremum in (4.11) has no bound on the Lipschitz constants of its continuous test functions.

This time all hypotheses of Theorem 3.4 can be met literally with finite-dimensional noise. Let \(H=\ell^2\), \(E=\mathbb R\), \(a=1/2\), and \(R\) be the right shift. Take iid noise \(\eta_k\) of density \(f\) and \[S(u,\eta)=\eta e_1+aRu,\qquad
 X=\{u: |u_j|\leq a^{j-1}\}.\] The set \(X\) is compact and invariant. The map is smooth and bounded with its derivatives on bounded sets. Condition (GD) holds in one step. For \(G=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}\), \(\|(I-P_G)D_uS\|=a<1\), and \(D_\eta S\) has image \(G\), so (ALC) holds on the whole ambient space. The iid law has (SF) with constant zero. For (DLP\('\)) take \(F_n=E=\mathbb R\), \(F_n^\dagger=\{0\}\) and \(\rho_n=f\); its compact support is now legitimate. Positivity of \(f\) near zero gives (SRZ).

The stationary solution is \[u_k=(\eta_k,a\eta_{k-1},a^2\eta_{k-2},\ldots).\] Given its past through time zero, \((u_k)_{k+1}=a^k(u_0)_1\) is deterministic. Under the stationary unconditional law, this coordinate is continuously distributed. For a fixed past with \((u_0)_1=z\), take the continuous test function \[g_{k,\varepsilon}(x)=
 \max\left(1-\frac{|x_{k+1}-a^kz|}{\varepsilon a^k},\,0\right).\] Its sup norm is at most one. The conditional expectation is one, whereas its stationary expectation is at most \(3\varepsilon/2\), since \(f\leq3/4\). Thus the supremum in (4.11), already for \(m=1\), is at least \(1-3\varepsilon/2\) for every \(k\), and hence at least one after letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). It does not tend to zero.

The solution process also need not satisfy the noise-style (SF) that precedes the definition on p. 39. One cannot transfer that property from the driving noise to this state process. The example still converges exponentially in dual-Lipschitz distance by synchronous coupling, with its unforced tail shrinking as \(a^k\); it therefore does not challenge Proposition 3.7’s actual weak convergence. Retain the weaker feeble-mixing conclusion, or impose and prove additional total-variation regularization of the state process. The isolated strengthening is Minor relative to the main dual-Lipschitz results.

# Other proof checks and limits

The approximate inverse argument of Lemma 3.5 needs a small local justification: distance to \(A(y)F_n\) need not be continuous when its rank drops. It is upper semicontinuous, since it is the infimum of continuous residual norms over fixed coefficients. Pointwise approximation, monotonicity in \(n\), and a finite cover of the compact parameter–target set give the uniform estimate (3.25). The regularized inverse and its spectral estimate then give the intended conclusion. This was not promoted to another confirmed theorem defect.

Likewise, a Lipschitz extension into finite-dimensional \(F\) from a general Banach domain may cost a finite constant, rather than the Hilbert-space constant one of Kirszbraun’s theorem. Coordinatewise scalar extensions suffice for the appendix after reducing the smallness threshold. The finite-dimensional change-of-variables bound retains order \(O(\kappa)\). No independent counterexample to the appendix’s needed perturbative conclusion was found. The final choice of \(l\) in the proof of Proposition 2.5 should keep the earlier contraction waiting time \(k_*\) rather than replace it by one; the earlier estimates supply the required finite \(l\). These local repairs do not resolve Problems 1 or 2.

The Navier–Stokes energy dissipation and full-noise linear control mechanism, and the declared scope of the complex Ginzburg–Landau application, were read and traced. Theorem 5.5 explicitly assumes (SF); its conditional form is not refuted by Problem 1. Its proposed continuous-noise extensions require the same careful hypothesis check. The literature inputs for PDE regularity were not re-proved in full. No simulation or numerical evidence in this review is used as a proof of infinite-dimensional mixing.
