Abstract: The acquired preprint constructs special Lagrangian pairs of pants by inductive Dirichlet problems and identifies their ends with lower-dimensional cylinders. One substantive compatibility defect is established in the explicit prescription for a general positive definite ambient matrix. The printed face potential uses a principal submatrix, whereas the special Lagrangian phase of the asserted cylinder depends on its Schur complement. An admissible three-dimensional example has a nonzero exact phase residual, so the printed prescription and the claimed smooth cylindrical asymptote cannot both hold. A local replacement restores the cylinder’s phase condition; the full construction after that replacement is not verified here. The supported impact is Major, with scientific adjudication pending.

# The prescribed geometry

Equation reference: eq:forms. Write the ambient matrix of source §§1.2 and 2.1 (pp. 3 and 6) as \(G=(g_{ij})\), to distinguish it from its inverse. The ambient forms are \[
 \omega=\sum_{j=1}^n dx_j\wedge dy_j,\qquad
 \Omega_G=\bigwedge_{j=1}^n\left(dx_j+\mathrm i\sum_k g_{jk}dy_k\right),
 \qquad G=G^{T}>0.\] A phase-\(\theta\) special Lagrangian must satisfy \(\omega|_L=0\) and \(\operatorname{Im}(e^{-\mathrm i\theta}\Omega_G)|_L=0\). A positive real normalization of the holomorphic volume form does not affect the latter equation.

Equation reference: eq:block, eq:H. For \(n=3\), partition the allowed matrix as \[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}A&b\\ b^{T}&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A\in\mathbb R^{2\times2},\quad c>0,\quad
 S=A-\frac{bb^{T}}c>0.\] The relative interior of the face \(x_3=0\) is \[\Delta_2^\circ=\{x_1>0,\ x_2>0,\ x_1+x_2<\pi\}.\] With \(L'(t)=\log\sin t\), put \[\Phi(x_1,x_2)=L(x_1)+L(x_2)-L(x_1+x_2).\] The identity \(L(t)+L(\pi-t)=L(\pi)\) makes this the numerator of source equation (6), p. 8. Its Hessian is \[
 H_\Phi=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \cot x_1-\cot(x_1+x_2)&-\cot(x_1+x_2)\\
 -\cot(x_1+x_2)&\cot x_2-\cot(x_1+x_2)
 \end{pmatrix}>0,\qquad \det H_\Phi=1.\] These are source Lemma 2.4, p. 9, and also follow directly by differentiation and the cotangent addition identity.

Equation reference: eq:printed. The first displayed prescription in source §2.3, p. 10, sets the face value to \[
 u_A(x_1,x_2)=\frac{\Phi(x_1,x_2)}{\sqrt{g_{11}g_{22}-g_{12}^{\,2}}}
            =\frac{\Phi(x_1,x_2)}{\sqrt{\det A}}.\] Thus its Hessian \(H=D^2u_A\) satisfies \(\det H=1/\det A\). This is a legitimate two-dimensional solution for the matrix \(A\). The issue is its cylindrical embedding in the specified three-dimensional ambient geometry.

# Problem 1: the face potential and its claimed cylinder are incompatible

Problem 1 (General ambient matrices and the inductive asymptote).  For the literal face prescription [eq:printed] (#eq:printed), the product \(L_2\times\mathbb R_{y_3}\) in Theorem 1.3(2), p. 4, and Lemma 3.8, p. 29, need not be phase-\(\pi\) special Lagrangian in [eq:forms] (#eq:forms). It therefore cannot be the asserted \(C^{k,\alpha}\), in particular \(C^1\), asymptotic limit of a phase-\(\pi\) special Lagrangian.

## Complete local calculation

Equation reference: eq:cyl. Parameterize the portion of the proposed cylinder over \(\Delta_2^\circ\) by \[
 F(x_1,x_2,t)=
 (x_1,x_2,0;\ \partial_1u_A,\partial_2u_A,t),\qquad t\in\mathbb R.\] The pullback of \(\omega\) is zero: the \(x_1,x_2\) contribution is the antisymmetric part of the Hessian, and \(dx_3=0\). Thus this is indeed a Lagrangian cylinder, and failure below concerns the special phase rather than the Lagrangian condition.

Equation reference: eq:factor, eq:residual, eq:generaldefect. Proposition 1.  For any real symmetric Hessian \(H\) in [eq:cyl] (#eq:cyl), \[\begin{aligned}
 \Omega_G(F_{x_1},F_{x_2},F_t)
 &=\mathrm ic\det(I_2+\mathrm iSH),\\
 \operatorname{Im}\!\left(e^{-\mathrm i\pi}
 \Omega_G(F_{x_1},F_{x_2},F_t)\right)
 &=c\{\det S\det H-1\}.\end{aligned}\] For the printed potential [eq:printed] (#eq:printed), this residual equals \[
 -b^{T}A^{-1}b.\] Consequently it is nonzero whenever \(b\ne0\).

Proof. Evaluating the three complex one-forms in [eq:forms] (#eq:forms) on the tangent basis gives the matrix \[Z=\begin{pmatrix}I_2+\mathrm iAH&\mathrm ib\\
                  \mathrm ib^{T}H&\mathrm ic\end{pmatrix}.\] Taking its Schur complement with respect to the nonzero entry \(\mathrm ic\) gives [eq:factor] (#eq:factor). For any real \(2\times2\) matrix \(SH\), \[\det(I_2+\mathrm iSH)=1-\det(SH)+\mathrm i\operatorname{tr}(SH).\] Since \(e^{-\mathrm i\pi}=-1\), this proves [eq:residual] (#eq:residual). For [eq:printed] (#eq:printed), \(\det H=1/\det A\). The matrix determinant lemma gives \[\det S=\det A\left(1-\frac{b^{T}A^{-1}b}{c}\right),\] so substitution gives [eq:generaldefect] (#eq:generaldefect). Positive definiteness of \(A\) makes \(b^{T}A^{-1}b>0\) for nonzero \(b\). ◻

Equation reference: eq:example, eq:exact. Here is a fully explicit admissible example: \[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1/2\\0&1&0\\1/2&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A=I_2,\quad c=1,\quad b=\binom{1/2}{0},
 \quad S=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.\] The eigenvalues of \(G\) are \(1/2,1,3/2\), so it obeys the source’s full ambient positive-definiteness condition. Take \(x_1=x_2=\pi/3\), an interior point of the face, and arbitrary \(t\). The prescribed potential is exactly \(\Phi\), its two first derivatives vanish at this point, and \[H=\frac1{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det H=1.\] Direct evaluation on the tangent basis gives \[
 \Omega_G(F_{x_1},F_{x_2},F_t)
   =-\frac{7}{2\sqrt3}+\frac{\mathrm i}{4},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Im}(e^{-\mathrm i\pi}\Omega_G)=-\frac14.\] The cylinder is smooth, embedded locally and Lagrangian; the dimension, domain, potential, ambient matrix and claimed phase all agree with the source prescription. The nonzero residual is independent of \(t\), so moving sufficiently far into the claimed end cannot remove it.

## Why this contradicts the stated asymptotic identification

Assume the phase-\(\pi\) submanifold supplied by the construction has the normal-graph asymptote in Theorem 1.3(2) with this prescribed \(L_2\). Translate its \(y_3\to-\infty\) end to a fixed neighborhood of the point in [eq:example] (#eq:example). The ambient forms [eq:forms] (#eq:forms) are invariant under these translations. The stated exponential \(C^{k,\alpha}\) estimate implies \(C^1\) convergence to the cylinder [eq:cyl] (#eq:cyl). Pullback of a fixed smooth three-form is continuous under \(C^1\) convergence of the local parameterizations. Therefore the limiting cylinder must satisfy \(\operatorname{Im}(e^{-\mathrm i\pi}\Omega_G)=0\), contrary to [eq:exact] (#eq:exact). This argument does not require constructing a hypothetical \(u_3\) numerically or deciding whether the printed Dirichlet problem itself has a solution: it proves that the explicit prescription and the stated special-Lagrangian asymptote cannot hold together.

The same incompatibility occurs in the comparison step before that conclusion. Proposition 2.30, p. 23, treats the lower-dimensional function, extended independently of the new momentum variable, as a solution of the same transformed special Lagrangian equation. Lemma 3.8, p. 29, uses this identification to write a homogeneous elliptic equation for the difference of the two potentials. In the admissible example the proposed background cylinder fails the ambient phase equation, so this justification does not apply to the printed face data.

## Alternative readings and valid cases

This calculation uses the lower-index matrix in the actual printed \(\Omega_G\), not an OCR interpretation of \(G^{-1}\). Source p. 6 separately writes the position-space inner product using \(g^{ij}\). Both the ambient form and the explicit denominator on p. 10 were checked in the page images.

There is a natural intended interpretation under which the construction might be repaired: use the metric induced on the face in the position-space metric. Indeed, block inversion yields \[(G^{-1})_{\{1,2\},\{1,2\}}=S^{-1}.\] Its inverse, which is the matrix appearing in the reduced holomorphic volume form, is \(S\). Under this interpretation the denominator should be \(\sqrt{\det S}\), not the explicit ambient principal minor \(\sqrt{g_{11}g_{22}-g_{12}^{\,2}}\) in [eq:printed] (#eq:printed). Silently changing the meaning of those displayed coefficients does not validate the formula as written. A linear change that makes the ambient metric standard also changes the simplex coordinates and the face data; it cannot be used while retaining the unchanged printed prescription.

For an orthogonal splitting \(b=0\), \(S=A\), and this obstruction disappears. The two-dimensional formula itself remains valid. Reversing orientation, using the phase modulo \(\pi\), multiplying \(\Omega_G\) by a nonzero positive normalizing constant, or adding an affine function to \(u_A\) cannot make a nonzero phase residual vanish. No conclusion about failure of every standard-metric construction is drawn from this example.

# A verified local replacement and its limit

Equation reference: eq:repair. The exact compatible replacement for this cylinder is \[
 u_S(x_1,x_2)=\frac{\Phi(x_1,x_2)}{\sqrt{\det S}}.\] It is smooth and strictly convex on \(\Delta_2^\circ\), with \(\det D^2u_S=1/\det S\). Proposition Proposition 1 (#prop:phase) proves that the resulting cylinder is phase-\(\pi\) special Lagrangian. Its orientation may be chosen positively because \[e^{-\mathrm i\pi}\Omega_G(F_{x_1},F_{x_2},F_t)
     =c\,\operatorname{tr}(SD^2u_S)>0.\] For [eq:example] (#eq:example), this replaces \(u_A\) by \((2/\sqrt3)u_A\). At the displayed point the corrected Hessian is \[D^2u_S=\begin{pmatrix}4/3&2/3\\2/3&4/3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \Omega_G(F_{x_1},F_{x_2},F_t)=-7/3,\] and the phase residual is exactly zero.

This proves a local statement about the cylinder and identifies the appropriate reduced matrix. It does not supply a complete repaired theorem: the all-face inductive prescription, its compatibility at intersections, barrier estimates, transformed PDEs and subsequent geometric argument must be reconciled with the revised metric choices. No author adoption of this replacement is known from the inspected materials.

# Impact and conclusion

The supported assessment is Major. The defect concerns a background solution and an asymptotic model on the proof path \[\text{explicit face data}
 \ \longrightarrow\ \text{Proposition 2.30 and Lemma 3.8}
 \ \longrightarrow\ \text{Theorem 1.3(2)}.\] It affects the claimed construction for general allowed ambient matrices, rather than a peripheral estimate. The direct obstruction and the conditional limit contradiction are fully local and analytic. They do not disprove existence of special Lagrangian pairs of pants with a corrected prescription, nor independently disprove the smoothness or topology conclusions of Theorem 1.2.
