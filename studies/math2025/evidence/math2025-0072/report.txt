Title: Formal initial review: M25-0072
Orbit equivalences of pseudo-Anosov flows

# Scope and outcome

The inspected object is the 52-page author manuscript and its bound author-source tree. The DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01332-1 identifies the associated journal publication; it does not establish that this manuscript equals the 2025 version of record. None of the findings below is attributed to an uninspected journal text.

The manuscript develops rigidity of Anosov-like actions on bifoliated planes, applies it to orbit equivalence of pseudo-Anosov flows, and studies the necessity of transitivity. The full extracted text was read, with direct image inspection of 26 selected source pages. The review concentrates on quantified hypotheses, the continuity extension of the conjugacy, the construction in Section 7, and the proposed axioms in Section 8. Long geometric arguments and external classification results have not all been independently reproved.

Initial outcome: Major. Two demonstrated defects occur in arguments tied to central claims: a convergence criterion used in extending the conjugacy, and the stated obstruction to reversing the auxiliary flow in the sharpness construction. Two additional defects affect examples or a proposed definition. This is a judgment about the inspected derivations, not a counterexample to the main classification theorem and not scientific acceptance or rejection of the paper.

# Problem 1. The common-leaf case is missing from the convergence argument

Locations. Definition 4.1, p. 22; Observation 5.6, pp. 31–32; Corollary 5.8, p. 33; Lemma 5.21 and Remark 5.22, p. 38; Corollary 5.24, p. 39; Proposition 5.44, p. 46, and completion of Theorem 1.4, p. 47.

Claim and quantifiers. Corollary 5.8 takes a pairwise totally linked sequence \(z_n\to z\in P\cup\partial P\) and a positive NC fixed point \(w\) of \(g\), totally linked with every \(z_n\). It asserts that condition \((*)\) holds if and only if \(z\in\mathcal F^-(w)\). Its pertinent first clause says that for every sufficiently large \(i\), there exists \(K\) such that for every \(k>K\), \[g^{-k}z_i \text{ is not totally linked with }z_j
                    \quad\text{for every }j\leq i.\] The second clause requires eventual total linking with the tail of the sequence. Observation 5.6 explicitly assumes \(z_n\notin\mathcal F^-(w)\) and \(z\ne w\). The corollary omits these restrictions.

Counterexample with all displayed corollary hypotheses. Fix such a \(w\). Choose an embedded compact arc \(\iota:[1,2]\to\mathcal F^-(w)\setminus\{w\}\), and set \[z=\iota(1),\qquad z_n=\iota(1+1/n).\] These points are distinct, converge in \(P\), and their limit differs from \(w\). They, and \(w\), are pairwise totally linked: whenever \(a,b\) lie on the same negative leaf, \(\mathcal F^+(a)\) meets that leaf at \(a\), and \(\mathcal F^+(b)\) meets it at \(b\). Since \(g\) fixes \(w\) and preserves the foliations, it preserves \(\mathcal F^-(w)\). Thus \[g^{-k}z_i\mathrel{\mathrm{TL}}z_j
                    \quad\text{for every }i,j,k .\] The first clause of \((*)\) fails for every possible threshold. Nevertheless \(z\in\mathcal F^-(w)\). This disproves the corollary as stated without a constant sequence, an ideal boundary point, or a singular limit.

Core dependency and counterevidence. Lemma 5.21 invokes Observation 5.6 to produce \((*)\), transports it through total-linkedness preservation, and invokes Corollary 5.8 to confine accumulation points to a negative leaf. For distinct NC fixed points, the common-leaf obstruction is restricted: the earlier fixed-point structure prevents a different NC fixed point on the same invariant leaf. This is material counterevidence against treating the example as a refutation of that restricted lemma.

However, Remark 5.22 explicitly removes the fixed-point restriction and asserts that the same proof works in any subsequently enlarged domain of \(H\). After extension to intersections of NC leaves (Corollary 5.24), sequences on \(\mathcal F^-(w)\) are allowed, while the displayed invocation of Observation 5.6 cannot apply. Proposition 5.44 uses Lemma 5.21 in the enlarged domain to control accumulation before proving continuity. The claimed uniform extension of this proof thus has an unhandled case on the route to Theorem 1.4. This demonstrated dependency, rather than the false sentence alone, motivates Major.

Repair and limits. An off-leaf restriction removes the explicit counterexample from Corollary 5.8. It does not by itself prove Remark 5.22. A repair must handle common-leaf, constant, and mixed subsequences at each extension stage, or replace the criterion. Order preservation on a leaf may help, but the required global argument has not been completed here. No failure of the ultimate conjugacy or classification statement has been proved.

# Problem 2. The auxiliary flow cannot have the stated attractor–repeller asymmetry

Locations. Theorem 7.1 and its first proof paragraph, p. 49; the construction of \(N'\) on that page; the final contradiction, p. 50. Compare Proposition 2.38, p. 17.

Suspect step. The proof requires the auxiliary Anosov flow \(\phi_{N'}\) to have no direction-reversing self-orbit equivalence. It says this is ensured by giving it an attracting set but no repelling set. The construction removes a neighborhood of one periodic orbit from each of two closed manifolds and glues their torus boundaries. Thus \(N'\) is closed, rather than an open plug with an unglued escape boundary. The final paragraph uses the asserted impossibility of direction reversal to conclude non-equivalence of the two flows.

Compact-flow obstruction. For a complete continuous flow on a compact space, a proper attractor with a proper forward-trapping neighborhood \(U\) has a nonempty dual repeller. Choose \(U\) proper and a trapping time \(T>0\), so that its basin is \[B(A)=\bigcup_{n\geq0}\phi_{-nT}(U),\] an increasing union of open sets. If \(B(A)\) were the whole compact manifold, a finite subcover and the largest index would give \(\phi_{-NT}(U)=N'\). A time map is a bijection, so this forces \(U=N'\), a contradiction. Therefore \[R=N'\setminus B(A)\ne\varnothing .\] This is the compact invariant dual repeller: the complement of a trapping neighborhood is backward trapping, and its maximal backward-time invariant subset yields \(R\). The usual attractor/repeller meanings for closed Anosov flows thus rule out the stated premise.

This also agrees with the manuscript’s use of an attractor and a repeller for a nontransitive closed Anosov flow in Proposition 2.38. The argument does not forbid an attracting-only plug; it applies to the closed \(N'\) constructed here.

Impact. The premise offered to rule out a reversing self-equivalence is impossible in the stated setting. The later claim that this impossibility has already been established lacks the supplied justification. That obstruction is needed for the asserted non-orbit-equivalence in Theorem 7.1, which supplies the advertised necessity of transitivity. This is a Major defect of that proof. The classification theorem does not depend on this sharpness construction, so the finding is not propagated backwards to every main result.

Counterevidence and repair limits. Having both an attractor and a repeller does not imply that a reversing equivalence exists. A flow may forbid reversal through a verified asymmetry of basic pieces, their topology, or incidence. Neither Theorem 7.1 itself nor the existence of nonreversible Anosov flows is disproved. A correction needs an actual choice of auxiliary flow or plugs, and a proof that any self-orbit equivalence preserves enough structure to rule out reversal. Deleting the impossible premise alone leaves the step unproved. A substitute construction and its compatibility with the later gluings have not been independently established here.

# Problem 3. The displayed affine action violates its defining axiom

Locations. Axiom (A1), p. 5; Example 1, p. 6; related faithfulness and normal-form assertions in Remark 2.8, p. 7.

The example generates an action on the trivially bifoliated plane using \[\tau_1(x,y)=(x+1,y),\quad \tau_2(x,y)=(x,y+1),\quad
 D_\lambda(x,y)=(\lambda x,\lambda^{-1}y).\] Axiom (A1) requires that a nonidentity element preserving a leaf have a fixed point on that leaf, with the stipulated transverse hyperbolic behavior. But \(\tau_1\) preserves every horizontal leaf and has no fixed point anywhere. The vertical translation similarly violates the axiom. This holds for every choice of the dilation parameters. The first-coordinate action is also not faithful, since \(\tau_2\) acts trivially there.

Counterevidence. Generic or algebraically independent dilation parameters cannot remove the explicit translations from the generated group. The phrase about algebraic independence of a list containing \(1\) may be a clerical error, but correcting it does not resolve the axiom violation. The defect is therefore not merely typographical.

Impact and severity. This is not an Anosov-like action under the paper’s definition, so it cannot establish the asserted example or associated failure of rigidity in the trivial-plane class. The principal rigidity theorem excludes trivial planes. No dependency of its proof on this invalid example has been established. The severity is Minor.

Validated substitute with a different generating set. A coupled translation repairs the example’s main role. This is a reviewer-supplied replacement, not a construction attributed to the authors. Choose \(a,b,c>1\) algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\). For the pair \((a,b)\), use \[\tau(x,y)=(x+1,y+1),\quad D_a,\quad D_b .\] Every group element has the form \[(x,y)\longmapsto
 \left(qx+p(a,b),\ q^{-1}y+p(a^{-1},b^{-1})\right),
 \quad q=a^m b^n,\quad p\in\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1},s^{\pm1}].                 \tag{R}\] Composition gives the semidirect product of the additive Laurent-polynomial group by \(\mathbb Z^2\). Algebraic independence makes this normal form faithful. We have \(q=1\) only for \(m=n=0\); a nonzero \(p\) is nonzero at both parameter pairs in (R). Every nontrivial pure translation therefore moves both leaf spaces and preserves no leaf. When \(q\ne1\), the element has a unique fixed point, expands in one coordinate and contracts in the other; each preserved leaf contains that point. This proves (A1).

Density. The translation subgroup contains \(v_k=(a^k,a^{-k})\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\). If their additive subgroup had proper closure in \(\mathbb R^2\), the elementary structure of closed subgroups would give a nonzero character \((u,v)\) with \(w_k=u a^k+v a^{-k}\in\mathbb Z\) for all \(k\). But \[w_{k+1}+w_{k-1}=(a+a^{-1})w_k .\] Since \(a+a^{-1}\) is irrational, all \(w_k\) must vanish. The equations at \(k=0,1\) force \(u=v=0\), a contradiction. Thus the translation subgroup is dense.

# Completion of the substitute in Problem 3

Dense translations give dense point orbits, proving (A2). The conjugates \(T_vD_aT_v^{-1}\) fix the dense translation vectors \(v\), proving (A3). There are no singular leaves, making (A4) vacuous; both leaf spaces are Hausdorff, making (A5) vacuous. Every horizontal leaf intersects every vertical leaf, so there are no perfect fits or forbidden totally ideal quadrilaterals, establishing (A6).

The pairs \((a,b)\) and \((a,c)\) realize the same abstract semidirect-product group. In either action a nonidentity element has a fixed point precisely when its dilation exponent pair \((m,n)\) is nonzero. Their fixed-element sets agree. Any equivariant homeomorphism must commute with the common \(\tau,D_a\), hence with every \(v_k\)-translation, and by density with all translations. It is therefore \(h(z)=z+d\). Commutation with \(D_a\) forces \(d=0\). The identity cannot conjugate \(D_b\) to \(D_c\), since \(b\ne c\). This proves failure of equivariant rigidity for the repaired trivial-plane actions.

Reviewer-written SymPy checks verify affine composition, the fixed point of \(T_vD_aT_v^{-1}\), and the recurrence. These symbolic identities supplement the analytic arguments; they do not prove density by finite sampling.

# Problem 4. The proposed everywhere-hyperbolic class is empty as written

Locations. Definition 8.1, p. 50, conditions (2) and (3); motivation and Remark 8.2, pp. 50–51.

Condition (2) considers any pairwise distinct \(g_n\) with \(g_nx\to y\), any foliated box \(V\) about \(y\), and any compact sets \(K^\pm\subset\mathcal F^\pm(x)\). It requires eventually exactly one image \(g_nK^\pm\) to be contained in \(V\). Choose the permitted sets \(K^+=K^-=\{x\}\). Then both images equal \(\{g_nx\}\subset V\) eventually. Neither alternative holds. Thus (2) prohibits every convergent distinct-element orbit sequence.

This is not vacuous. Condition (3) supplies \(g_n^+\) such that \(g_n^+x\to y^+\) and every compact subset of \(\mathcal F^+(x)\) is eventually mapped into every neighborhood of \(y^+\). Take \(u\ne x\) on this leaf and the compact set \(\{x,u\}\). Both \(g_n^+x\) and \(g_n^+u\) converge to \(y^+\). The sequence must contain infinitely many distinct elements: otherwise some \(h\) recurs infinitely, forcing \(h(x)=h(u)=y^+\), contrary to injectivity. A pairwise distinct subsequence still has a convergent orbit at \(x\), contradicting (2) with the singleton compact sets. Conditions (2) and (3) are therefore incompatible.

Impact, counterevidence, repair. This invalidates the proposed class as a source of examples; it does not invalidate (A1)–(A6) or the main theorem. Severity is Minor. A necessary repair excludes degenerate compact sets and specifies suitable nondegenerate leaf segments. Whether a revised condition captures the intended dynamics remains unproved. That restriction is a necessary change, not a verified characterization theorem.

# Positive checks, exclusions, and coverage

The NC fixed-point assignment is consistent conditional on the earlier fixed-point and common-spectrum results: stabilizers at one NC point commute after the appropriate leaf-preserving passage, and their images must share their unique NC fixed point. Final equivariance follows on the dense fixed-point set from conjugation of stabilizers and extends if continuity is established. These checks isolate Problem 1 to the continuity justification.

Section 6 distinguishes the dynamics on the ideal boundary, rather than relying only on a count of four fixed points. Free elements with four boundary fixed points therefore do not alone refute that argument. Signs are assigned to the relevant orbits of scalloped trees, not independently to arbitrary lozenges.
