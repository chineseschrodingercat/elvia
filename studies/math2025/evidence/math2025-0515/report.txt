Abstract: The acquired arXiv version has an incorrect finite-time flat-current formula: its right-hand side drops the initial offset, although exact small-ring probabilities depend on that offset. This is a Major finding about the stated finite-time proposition, with a local repair suggested by the paper’s own derivation. Four further bounded findings concern inconsistent step-limit kernels, a missing deformation factor in the collision rule, the phase of the product of Bethe roots, and an unsupported extension by particle–hole duality. The appendix already gives the correct collision factor, and the later step-limit proof gives the kernel expected from the cited reference. These observations limit the impact of the findings. No claim is made that the large-system fluctuation theorems are false, or that the acquired preprint is identical to the journal version.

The state is an ordered configuration \(X=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\) with \(0\leq x_1<\cdots<x_N<L\). Each particle attempts a right exclusion jump at rate \(p\), and a left push at rate \(q\), where \(p,q\geq0\) and \(p+q=1\). A left push moves the consecutive occupied block to the first hole. The total current \(Q\) counts all individual displacements; the local current \(Q_j\) counts net crossings of the edge \(j\to j+1\). The generator (2.8), page 9, rather than an alternative convention about attempted jumps, is used for every calculation in this report.

Equation reference: eq:conservation, eq:gauge. The deformation is \[g(Y,X;\zeta;t)=\mathbb E_Y[\zeta^{Q(t)}\mathbf1_{\{X(t)=X\}}],
 \qquad |\zeta|=1.\] An independently checked conservation law is \[
 Q(t)=\sum_i(x_i-y_i)+LQ_{L-1}(t).\] It holds at each allowed transition, including a push across the origin, and hence along every path. This verifies (3.25) and the gauge relation (3.30), pages 23–24. In particular, \[
 \zeta^L=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 g(Y,X;\zeta;t)=\zeta^{\sum_i(x_i-y_i)}
 \mathbb P_Y(X(t)=X).\] Exact rational enumeration also checked this transition identity and uniform stationarity on the rings \((L,N)=(6,2),(4,3),(3,1)\), comprising 76 allowed transitions at \(p=q=1/2\). These are finite-system checks, not a proof of all contour or asymptotic estimates.

The contour conditions on page 15 require \[\frac{1}{1-\rho}<\beta_1<\frac1\rho,\qquad
 \frac1\rho<\beta_2<\frac{L}{N-1},\qquad \rho=N/L,\] and thus \(2N<L\). Remark 4.4, page 39, supplies modified conditions at \(2N=L\). The generic spectral expansion explicitly excludes finitely many \(\zeta\)’s. Corollary 3.5, page 20, additionally assumes simple coupling roots at \(\zeta=1\); this review does not silently remove that assumption. Remark 4.7, page 41, distinguishes a fixed small contour from its limit as its radius tends to zero, resolving a possible apparent discontinuity in \(u_0\).

# Problem 1: the finite flat-current formula loses the offset

Problem 1 (Major).  Proposition 5.4, page 59, equation (5.37), with the definitions (5.34)–(5.36) on page 58, cannot hold for every initial offset allowed in (5.30).

#### Assertion and hypotheses.

Fix integers \(d\geq2\), \(L=dN\), and \[Y_\delta=(\delta,d+\delta,\ldots,(N-1)d+\delta),\qquad 0\leq\delta<d.\] For an integer threshold \(a\), Proposition 5.4 represents \(\mathbb P_{Y_\delta}(Q_{L-1}(t)\geq a)\) by a contour integral of \(C_N^{(\mathrm{flat})}(z)\det(I+K_z^{(\mathrm{flat})})\). As printed, neither factor defined in (5.34)–(5.36) contains \(\delta\). The root sets, contour radius condition, energy and the map \(V\) are also independent of \(\delta\).

Claim 1.  For \(L=6,N=2,d=3,p=1,q=0,a=1\), the two admissible initial offsets \(\delta=0\) and \(\delta=1\) give \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P_{(0,3)}(Q_5(t)\geq1)&=\tfrac18t^4+O(t^5),\\
 \mathbb P_{(1,4)}(Q_5(t)\geq1)&=\tfrac12t^2+O(t^3).\end{aligned}\]

Proof. From \((1,4)\), the rightmost particle can make the two jumps \(4\to5\to0\). This is the unique two-transition path with a positive crossing of edge 5, so its contribution is \(t^2/2\). From \((0,3)\), that particle needs three jumps \(3\to4\to5\to0\), and the particle at 0 must first vacate 0. At least four transitions are required. In the shortest paths, \(0\to1\) can occur in any of the three positions preceding the final crossing. The three ordered paths each contribute \(t^4/4!\), giving \(t^4/8\). Terms with extra transitions or holding-time corrections have higher order. The finite jump rates justify these Taylor expansions. ◻

An independent exact rational computation of the generator on configuration/current pairs reproduced these coefficients through order six. At strictly positive rates \(p=q=1/2\), it gives \[\mathbb P_{(0,3)}(Q_5(t)\geq1)=\tfrac1{128}t^4+O(t^5),\qquad
 \mathbb P_{(1,4)}(Q_5(t)\geq1)=\tfrac18t^2+O(t^3).\] Thus excluding the one-way boundary of the rate parameter would not remove the discrepancy. The choices satisfy \(2N<L\), so this is not a high-density or contour-endpoint objection. A single expression independent of \(\delta\) cannot equal both probabilities.

#### Counterevidence and repair.

Equation reference: eq:offset-repair. The derivation itself retains \(\delta\): equation (5.38), page 60, contains \(w^{1+\delta}\); equation (5.39) contains \(v_j^{-N+\delta+2}\); and page 61 retains \(v^{L-N-Q+\delta}\). Consequently the necessary offset factors are \[
 C_{N,\delta}^{(\mathrm{flat})}
 =C_{N,0}^{(\mathrm{flat})}\prod_{v\in Q_1(z)}v^\delta,
 \qquad
 f_\delta^{(\mathrm{flat})}(w)=w^\delta f_0^{(\mathrm{flat})}(w).\] These factors follow directly from the displayed derivation; this is not a claim to have reverified every other determinant normalization in the proposition.

Spatial translation does not make the finite-time current at a fixed edge independent of the initial phase. It translates the observed edge as well. Conversely, conservation between two edges at distance \(\delta\) bounds the difference of their currents by \(\delta\), because it is a change in the particle count of the intervening arc. For fixed \(d\), this is bounded uniformly in time. It vanishes after the \(\sqrt L\) fluctuation normalization in Theorem 3.13. Thus the counterexample disproves the stated exact finite-time proposition, but does not disprove the asymptotic offset independence asserted on page 27. The core dependency is Proposition 5.4 \(\to\) the flat asymptotic calculation in Section 5.2; the omitted factors must be restored before that derivation is used.

# Problem 2: two different step-limit kernels

Problem 2 (Minor, with an essential formula correction).  Equation (3.41), page 27, defines a different kernel from the one identified in the proof of Theorem 3.14 on page 70.

#### Exact discrepancy.

Equation reference: eq:printed-kernel, eq:proof-kernel. For \(|z|<1\), \(z\ne0\), let \(S_-(z)=\{\xi:e^{-\xi^2/2}=z,\ \Re\xi<0\}\), and put \[H(\xi)=-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
   \int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\operatorname{Li}_{1/2}(e^{-\omega^2/2})\,d\omega.\] For \(x\in\mathbb R,\tau>0,\gamma\in\mathbb R\), the functions on pages 26–27 are \[\Psi(\xi)=-\tau\xi^3/3+x\xi+H(\xi),\qquad
 \Phi(\xi)=-\tau\xi^3/3+x\xi+2H(\xi).\] Suppressing common parameters, (3.41) prints \[
 K_{\rm def}(\xi_1,\xi_2)
 =\sum_{\eta\in S_-(z)}
 \frac{\exp\{\Psi(\xi_1)+\Psi(\xi_2)
              +\gamma(\xi_1^2-\eta^2)/2\}}
 {\xi_1\eta(\xi_1+\eta)(\xi_2+\eta)}.\] Page 70 instead uses \[
 K_{\rm proof}(\xi_1,\xi_2)
 =\sum_{\eta\in S_-(z)}
 \frac{\exp\{\Phi(\xi_1)+\Phi(\eta)
              +\gamma(\xi_1^2-\eta^2)/2\}}
 {\xi_1\eta(\xi_1+\eta)(\xi_2+\eta)}.\] Both the function and the second argument differ.

#### Decisive comparison.

This is not just a renaming of the summation variable. Take \(z=e^{-1/2}\), \(\tau=1\), \(\gamma=0\), \(\xi_1=-1\), and \(\xi_2=-\sqrt{1+4\pi i}\), using the square root with positive real part. Then \(\Re\xi_2<-1\). In [eq:printed-kernel] (#eq:printed-kernel), all \(x\)-dependence factors out as \(e^{x(\xi_1+\xi_2)}\), and its remaining series converges absolutely, since its summands have order \(|\eta|^{-3}\). Therefore \(e^{2x}K_{\rm def}(-1,\xi_2)\to0\) as \(x\to+\infty\). In [eq:proof-kernel] (#eq:proof-kernel), the unique root with maximal real part is \(\eta=-1\). Its nonzero contribution gives \[e^{2x}K_{\rm proof}(-1,\xi_2)
 \longrightarrow
 \frac{\exp\{2/3+4H(-1)\}}{-2(\xi_2-1)}\ne0.\] The remaining terms vanish: along the other roots, \(\Re\eta\leq\Re\xi_2<-1\), while \(\Re\eta^3\) grows positively of order \(|\eta|^3\). Also \(H(\eta)=O(|\eta|^{-1})\). The latter bound follows by integrating horizontally from the left, where \(|\operatorname{Li}_{1/2}(e^{-w^2/2})|
 \leq C_z e^{-\Re(w^2)/2}\). These bounds supply an absolute summable majorant. The two displayed kernels are consequently unequal as functions of \(x\).

#### Counterevidence, dependency and repair.

The cited Baik–Liu manuscript [BL], Section 4.2, equations (4.11)–(4.12), physical page 12, uses precisely the \(\Phi(\xi_1)+\Phi(\eta)\) form. The acquired paper’s own page 70 therefore supplies strong evidence of the intended correction. Replace (3.41) by [eq:proof-kernel] (#eq:proof-kernel), retaining the doubled integral coefficient in (3.42). The definition of \(F_2\) in (3.40) feeds Theorem 3.14 and the stated distributional properties, so this correction is necessary for that identification. The proof above establishes different kernels; it does not by itself establish different final contour-integrated distribution functions, nor a counterexample to the corrected fluctuation theorem. The local nature of the repair and its presence in the proof support the Minor classification.

# Problem 3: the collision rule omits a deformation factor

Problem 3 (Minor).  The exclusion/push rule (2.21), page 11, is incompatible with the claimed free-generator reduction for general \(\zeta\).

#### Hypotheses and direct check.

The extension is asserted for \(|\zeta|=1\), every admissible ordered configuration, and the generating function of total current. For two adjacent coordinates, it prints \(g(Y,(a,a);\zeta;t)=g(Y,(a,a+1);\zeta;t)\). Take \(L=5,N=2,p=1,q=0,Y=X=(0,1)\), and \(t=0\). There is exactly one allowed right jump from \(Y\), namely \(1\to2\). The actual diagonal forward derivative is therefore \(-1\). Even interpreting the free sum (2.22) as intended, over the two particle coordinates, its value under the printed rule is \[\zeta g(Y,(-1,1);\zeta;0)
 +\zeta g(Y,(0,0);\zeta;0)-2g(Y,(0,1);\zeta;0)
 =\zeta-2.\] The first incoming term is zero after the periodic identification; the collision term is one under (2.21). At \(\zeta=i\) the discrepancy is \(i-1\ne0\). The parameters satisfy the low-density range, and this calculation uses the actual generator and initial condition, not a choice of Bethe roots.

Equation reference: eq:collision. The correct local condition is \[
 g(Y,(a,a);\zeta;t)
 =\zeta^{-1}g(Y,(a,a+1);\zeta;t).\] It cancels the spurious incoming term against the loss for a blocked jump. The amplitudes (2.25) independently confirm the same factor. With \(a_j=1-(\zeta w_j)^{-1}\), the two-particle Bethe function at these two configurations, for \(a=0\), is \[u_{\rm coll}=1-\frac{a_2}{a_1},\qquad
 u_{\rm adj}=\frac1{w_2}-\frac{a_2}{a_1w_1}.\] For nonzero \(w_1,w_2,\zeta\) with \(a_1\ne0\), direct simplification gives \(u_{\rm adj}=\zeta u_{\rm coll}\). The symbolic calculation saved with this review checks this identity; the sample values \(w_1=2,w_2=3,\zeta=i\) are algebraic checks only, not asserted solutions of the periodic Bethe equations.

#### Counterevidence, repair and impact.

Lemma B.1, pages 80–81, explicitly proves the factor \(\zeta^{-1}\) in [eq:collision] (#eq:collision). Thus the appendix supplies the correct condition. At \(\zeta=1\) the discrepancy disappears. The chain (2.21) \(\to\) (2.22)–(2.23) \(\to\) Proposition 2.3 is invalid as printed, but correcting (2.21) consistently with Lemma B.1 removes this demonstrated local obstruction. This is not a counterexample to the final eigenfunction identity. The available internal repair supports a Minor classification.

# Problem 4: the product of Bethe roots has a missing phase

Problem 4 (Minor).  Item 1 of Lemma D.1, page 85, incorrectly asserts that the product of the post-change roots is an \(L\)-th root of unity for every unit-modulus \(\zeta\).

Equation reference: eq:root-product. The hypotheses are positive integers \(L\geq N\geq1\), \(\zeta\in\mathbb U\), and a solution of (dBE1)–(dBE2) with pairwise distinct roots. Multiplying the deformed Bethe equations (2.31), page 13, gives, wherever their ratios are defined, \[
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^N w_j\right)^L=\zeta^{LN}.\] Indeed, the ratios telescope in the product over \(j\), and the sign is \((-1)^{N(N-1)}=1\). A counterexample within the stated hypotheses is \[L=3,\qquad N=1,\qquad \zeta=i,\qquad w_1=i,\qquad z^3=1-i.\] Here \(z\ne0\), \(w_1\ne0,1\), and distinctness is automatic. The decoupled equations reduce to \(z^3=w_1^3(1-w_1^{-1})\) and \(z^3=\zeta^3(1-w_1^{-1})\); both equal \(1-i\). Nevertheless \(w_1^3=-i\ne1\).

The correct conclusion is that \(\zeta^{-N}\prod_jw_j\) is an \(L\)-th root of unity. The modulus identity \(\prod_j|w_j|=1\) survives, because \(|\zeta|=1\). This is the part used in the subsequent root-size estimate and small-\(z\) exclusion argument on pages 85–86. Those arguments feed the permissible residue contours; this finding does not disprove their final modulus or isolation conclusions. It also does not apply to the pre-change variables, whose product has the unshifted phase. The paper explicitly states after (2.31) that it drops the \(\zeta\) subscript, so interpreting Lemma D.1 as a statement about the old variables would require changing its specified decoupled equations. Correcting the phase is a local repair.

# Problem 5: the stated particle–hole extension needs a proof

Problem 5 (Minor, confined to the claimed extension of range).  Remark 2.5, page 16, does not establish its extension from the contour range \(2N<L\) to all densities by ordinary particle–hole duality for PushASEP with \(q>0\).

The contour inequalities in Section 1 of this report have a nonempty interval for \(\beta_1\) exactly when \(2N<L\). The modified contour conditions on page 39 and in Appendix A cover equality \(2N=L\). A further argument is required for larger densities. Under ordinary complementation (with or without reflection), left pushes of particle blocks become long jumps of holes, not nearest-neighbour pushes of single holes.

For an explicit generator check, take \(L=4,N=3,p=0,q=1\) with occupied sites \(\{1,2,3\}\) and a hole at 0. The clocks at 1, 2 and 3 move that hole respectively to 1, 2 and 3, each at rate one: they shift blocks of lengths one, two and three into 0. In particular, the hole jumps to the opposite site 2 at positive rate. A one-particle PushASEP on the four-cycle, at any choice of the two nearest-neighbour rates, cannot make that jump. Reflection does not change this obstruction. The opposite jump persists at positive rate for every \(q>0\); it is not a singularity specific to \(p=0\).

For \(q=0\), ordinary TASEP particle–hole symmetry does give a nearest-neighbour process, which is relevant counterevidence to any overly broad assertion that particle–hole reasoning always fails. Nor does this example rule out a different algebraic duality, a transformation to another process with a separate formula, or a direct extension of the contour proof. The point is that the unqualified ordinary particle–hole argument does not supply any of these.

The scope of the contour and delta-basis derivation should remain explicit unless the transformed generator, current observable, and resulting formulas are proved. The fully occupied case also needs separate treatment: the configuration generator (2.8) has no allowed move, whereas expressions involving \(1-\rho\) become singular. This is a gap in the claimed range extension, not a counterexample to the low-density contour formula or its fluctuation limits.

# Coverage, independent checks and version limits

All 91 physical pages of the acquired preprint were read, including the six appendices and the reference list. The definitions, hypotheses, quantifiers and immediate proof dependencies of all five findings were checked directly. Thirty-eight source pages containing the model, statements, decisive formulas, derivations, and counterevidence were also inspected as rendered images. The relevant Baik–Liu reference text on physical pages 11–12 was read; its title page and pages 11–12 were viewed. This does not constitute a complete independent review of that reference.

An independently written exact-arithmetic calculation used configuration/current states, the generator (2.8), and matrix powers through order six. It verified the finite flat examples, the transition-by-transition current conservation law, uniform stationarity in the stated small rings, the collision identity, and the one-hole transition obstruction. The source, actual process output, software versions and result payload are saved under this paper’s verification tree. No author code was run. The analytic kernel comparison in Problem 2 and the product identity in Problem 4 are proved above; neither is presented as a numerical experiment.

The full steepest-descent estimates, all uniform error bounds, all external Fredholm determinant results and every cited theorem were not independently reproved. Generic-\(\zeta\) qualifications, the simple-root hypothesis in Corollary 3.5, and exceptional spectral parameters remain part of the theorem range. In particular, the root-product finding is not used to claim that the generic spectral theorem fails at a parameter it excludes. The zero Bethe functions noted in Remark 2.4 are already disclosed by the authors and are not an additional finding. The limiting contour interpretation in Remark 4.7 was taken into account.

The arXiv record and the author’s publication list were checked, together with searches for corrections to this title. No specific author correction was found in those checks; that bounded negative result is not proof that none exists. A supplement request reached a security-check HTML response, and an ordinary request for the official journal issue likewise returned an anti-bot HTML page rather than a PDF. Both actual responses are preserved. The embedded appendices remain available and were read. Unavailable supplementary or journal material is an access limit, not a mathematical defect, and no preprint finding is attributed to an uninspected journal revision.
