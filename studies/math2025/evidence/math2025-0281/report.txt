Abstract: The reviewed source is arXiv:2307.13235v1. Reading covers its 26 pages, with detailed checks of weighted densities, the semisimplicity-to-splitting dependency, and the Ricci-flat appendix argument. The last inference in the proof of Proposition 7.1 confuses a zero induced Killing field with a zero element of a quotient Lie algebra. An effective hyperbolic-space action disproves that inference under the stated geometric assumptions. This is assessed as Major because it occurs in the central orbit-symmetry argument; a precise replacement of this inference is supplied. Three further local defects concern the explicit Gram–Schmidt calculation, the sign in an automorphism transformation law, and a global group-lifting assertion. These findings do not constitute counterexamples to the geometric splitting conclusions. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Assumptions and the main dependency

The negative Einstein setting uses a complete Riemannian manifold, a connected unimodular Lie group acting properly and cocompactly, and the normalization \(\operatorname{ric}=-g\). Section 2 and Appendix A explicitly pass to effective actions; Section 5 again discusses quotienting the ineffective kernel. These conventions matter: adjoining a trivially acting compact circle is not a counterexample to the effectively interpreted argument. The report treats this as a scope clarification, rather than presenting an ineffective action as a new defect.

The principal route is weighted-density control and Theorem 4.1, followed by polarity (Proposition 5.4), transitivity of a minimal parabolic on each orbit (Proposition 6.1), symmetry of the orbits (Proposition 7.1), trivial polar group (Corollary 7.4), and the final splitting argument in Section 8. Problem 1 concerns the step from Proposition 6.1 to Proposition 7.1. The defects in Problems 2–4 have the more limited dependencies stated individually.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (A zero induced field need not be a zero quotient-algebra element).  Assessment: Major; invalid inference in a central proof.

## Source claim and suspect step

On pp. 17–18, the proof of Proposition 7.1 sets \(\Phi:\mathfrak q\to\mathfrak q/\mathfrak n\) and \(\check Z=\Phi(Z)\) for \(Z\in\mathfrak m\). Its Bochner calculation gives \(\check Z^{*}=0\) as a Killing field on \(N\backslash M\). It then concludes \(\check Z=0\) and \(Z=0\), invoking injectivity of \(\Phi|_{\mathfrak m\oplus\mathfrak a}\). The action of \(Q/N\) on \(N\backslash M\) has not been shown effective. Injectivity of \(\Phi\) on the indicated subspace does not make the map from \(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak n\) to Killing fields injective.

## An example satisfying the geometric hypotheses

Take the upper half-space \[M=\{(x,y,z):z>0\},\qquad
 g=\frac{2}{z^2}(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2),\qquad L=\mathrm{SO}_0(3,1).\] This metric is complete hyperbolic three-space of sectional curvature \(-1/2\), so \(\operatorname{ric}=-g\). The group \(L\) is connected, semisimple without compact simple factors, and has finite center. Its usual action is effective, isometric, proper (the stabilizer is the compact group \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\)), and transitive, hence cocompact. These are Assumption 5.2, not weakened substitutes for it.

Choose \(N\) to be horizontal translations, \(A\) positive dilations, and the connected compact centralizer \(M_0=\mathrm{SO}(2)\) to be rotations in the \((x,y)\) plane. Then \(N\backslash M\) is parameterized by the height \(z\). The nonzero generator \[Z^{*}=-y\partial_x+x\partial_y\] belongs to \(\mathfrak m\). It induces the zero field on \(N\backslash M\), since \(Z^{*}z=0\), but it is not a zero Killing field on \(M\): at \((1,0,1)\) it equals \(\partial_y\). Moreover, \(\Phi(Z)\ne0\) because \(\mathfrak m\cap\mathfrak n=0\). Consequently the implication \[\check Z^{*}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \Phi(Z)=0\] is false even in an effective action satisfying all the geometric assumptions. The independent symbolic check verifies the displayed field identities and \([Z^{*},x\partial_x+y\partial_y+z\partial_z]=0\); the geometric hypothesis checks above are analytic.

## Counterevidence, impact and a supported replacement

The example has symmetric orbits, so it is not a counterexample to Proposition 7.1 itself. Writing \(\check Z=0\) informally for a zero field cannot justify the subsequent appeal to injectivity of the algebra map. Likewise, the assertion on p. 17 that \(M\leq H\) is equivalent to \(Q/N=G/N\) cannot be read as equality of the abstract groups in this example; the former concerns isotropy and the latter would eliminate the nontrivial compact centralizer.

There is a direct replacement for the final inference. Retain the established conclusion \(\check Z^{*}=0\). At a point \(p\) satisfying (11), Corollary 6.6 makes \(Z_p^{*}\) \(N\)-horizontal, while Lemma 7.2 makes it \(N\)-vertical because its projection vanishes. Hence \(Z_p^{*}=0\) and \(Z\in\mathfrak h\). This yields \(\mathfrak m\subseteq\mathfrak h\) and therefore \(M_0\subseteq H\). With the preceding relation \(K=M_0H\) in (14), it follows that \(K=H\), as required. This checks the replacement of this inference, conditional on the preceding lemmas; it is not a fresh verification of all imported curvature results.

The invalid inference sits in the proof establishing orbit symmetry, which is used by Corollary 7.4 and the proof of Theorem A. That core dependency supports Major under the assigned calibration. The short replacement and the survival of the geometric conclusion do not reduce the grading of the false derivation.

Problem 2 (The explicit non-diagonal density in Example 3.10 is incorrect).  Assessment: Minor; an erroneous illustrative calculation.

On p. 8, for the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra \([e_1,e_2]=e_3\), the example states for an arbitrary positive-definite inner product \(h\) that backwards Gram–Schmidt uses \[u_2=e_2-\frac{h_{23}}{h_{33}}e_3,\qquad
 u_1=e_1-\frac{h_{12}}{h_{22}}e_2-\frac{h_{13}}{h_{33}}e_3,\] and then gives \[v_{\beta^+}(h)^3=h(u_1,u_1)\,h(u_2,u_2)\,h_{33}^{\,2}.\] Set \[H=(h_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\1&2&1\\1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.\] Its leading principal minors are \(2,3,4\), so it satisfies the stipulated positive-definiteness assumption. The stated vector \(u_1=e_1-\tfrac12e_2-\tfrac12e_3\) has inner product \(-1/2\) with each of \(e_2,e_3\) and is not the required orthogonal residual. The published expression gives \(v_{\beta^+}^3=9\).

The correct backwards orthogonalization is \[u_1^{\rm corr}
 =e_1-\frac{h(e_1,u_2)}{h(u_2,u_2)}u_2-\frac{h_{13}}{h_{33}}e_3
 =e_1-\tfrac13e_2-\tfrac13e_3.\] Its squared norm is \(4/3\), whereas \(h(u_2,u_2)=3/2\) and \(h_{33}=2\), giving \(v_{\beta^+}^3=8\) and \(v_{\beta^+}=2\). More generally the corrected expression for this Heisenberg example is \[v_{\beta^+}(h)=(\det(H)\,h_{33})^{1/3}.\] This follows by the backwards Gram determinant identity and the weights \((2/3,2/3,4/3)\). Exact SymPy calculations reproduce \(9\ne8\), verify the corrected orthogonality, and are retained with inputs and outputs.

The diagonal specialization in the paper is correct. No orthogonality of \(e_2,e_3\) is assumed for the general formula, so that specialization does not rescue it. Definitions 3.7 and Proposition 3.8 define the functional without this explicit example; no downstream use of the incorrect non-diagonal formula was identified. The demonstrated impact is therefore local.

Problem 3 (The transformation law needs an absolute determinant).  Assessment: Minor; a sign error outside the determinant-one application.

Lemma 3.11 (p. 8) permits any automorphism \(\phi\) that is an isometry from \((\mathfrak n,h_1)\) to \((\mathfrak n,h_2)\), but states \(v_{\beta^+}(h_2)=\det(\phi)^{-1}v_{\beta^+}(h_1)\).

Use the nonabelian nilpotent algebra \(\mathfrak n=\mathfrak h_3\oplus\mathbb R\), with only \([e_1,e_2]=e_3\) nonzero, and the standard correctly gauged orthonormal metric \(h_1=h_2\). The map \(\phi=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1)\) preserves every bracket and this metric but has determinant \(-1\). The weighted density of the background metric is \(1\) by its definition. The stated equality thus gives \(1=-1\). All 16 basis-pair bracket checks, orthogonality and determinant were verified exactly.

The consistent factor is \(|\det\phi|^{-1}\), in agreement with the absolute determinants used in Proposition 3.4. An orientation-preserving restriction would also remove this counterexample, but it is not present in Lemma 3.11. The main use in Lemma 4.2 is for determinant-one transformations; this sign defect does not invalidate that use. The report does not infer a failure of the semisimplicity argument from this local extension error.

Problem 4 (An effective group action need not lift as the same group).  Assessment: Minor; the additional global group assertion in Theorem 9.2.

Theorem 9.2 (p. 19; also the statement after Theorem B on p. 2) adds, for an effective action, \(G^0\subseteq\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb R^k)\). Its proof says that the action of \(G\) lifts to the universal cover as a proper isometric action.

Let \(M=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\) with its flat metric and let \(G=S^1\) act by translations. The manifold is complete and Ricci-flat; the action is effective, proper and transitive, and its orbit space is compact. Its universal cover is \(\mathbb R\), so \(k=1\). But \(S^1\) cannot be a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb R)\): its connected image must lie in the additive translation group \(\mathbb R\), which has no nontrivial compact subgroup. The infinitesimal action does lift, and its connected covering group acts by translations, but the lifted translation by \(2\pi\) is nontrivial on \(\mathbb R\) although its projection is the identity on \(M\).

Theorem 9.2 does not impose noncompactness on \(M\). Even if such a restriction were intended, the flat cylinder with the transitive action \(S^1\times\mathbb R\) exhibits the same descent obstruction: a circle subgroup of the Euclidean plane is conjugate to rotations, and its continuous centralizer has no independent commuting translation subgroup.

The safe conclusion concerns lifted Killing fields or a covering group of \(G^0\), with the kernel specified; it is not an embedding of \(G^0\) itself. No assertion of a faithful lifted universal covering group is needed here. The geometric splitting of the universal cover remains true in these examples, and the cocompactness argument can use the group of all lifts together with deck transformations. This defect is assessed as local to the additional group-level assertion, rather than as a counterexample to the splitting theorem.

# Other checks and boundaries

The positivity of the weights in Proposition 3.8 is essential to vanishing at degenerate limits. Its determinant argument is compatible with bounded metric coefficients and divergent inverse triangular scale; the faulty explicit example is not needed there. The maximum-principle contradiction in Theorem 4.1 has the expected sign: at a maximum, \(\Delta\log v\leq0\), while strict negative Ricci curvature paired with positive \(\beta^+\) is negative, contradicting Lemma 4.3.

The section-wise scaling factors in (16)–(18) should be understood pointwise; the maximum-principle use can be read through the homothetic irreducible-factor metrics.
