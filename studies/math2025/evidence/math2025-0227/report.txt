Abstract: The specified principal vector-field family omits the regularized two-factor base family. Consequently, its stated nested spanning conclusion is impossible at an explicit four-dimensional symplectic example. The accompanying unregularized base definition also has genuine poles. This is a Major issue in the proof of stratified ellipticity, without a counterexample to the final factorization theorem. The finite-enlargement construction also fails at a core spanning-to-spray step and is classified as Major. Four further Minor findings concern flow order, the approximation hypothesis, block elimination, and the parity of critical values. Explicit counterexamples, counterevidence, and bounded repairs are given. Scientific decision remains pending, with no human reviews.

# The missing global base family

Problem 1 (Major).  Definition 3.19, p. 20, starts the union defining \(\mathcal V_K\) at \(L=3\). Together with Definition 3.35, p. 30, this makes the nested conclusion \(A_2\subset A_3\subset\mathcal Q_3=\Gamma(\mathcal V_3)\) in Theorem 3.36, p. 31, impossible. The base definition on p. 30 also needs regularization. The affected interfaces include Lemma 3.39, p. 32, and Lemma 3.42, pp. 33–34.

Use the transposed convention explicitly adopted on p. 11. For \(n=2\), set \[Z_1=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),\qquad Z_2=\operatorname{diag}(0,-1),\qquad Z_3=0.\] Then \[\Phi_1=(e_2,e_2),\qquad
 \Phi_2=(e_2,0),\qquad \Phi_3=(e_2,0).\] The two-factor fiber is generic, whereas the three-factor fiber is non-generic. Lemma 3.18, pp. 18–20, shows that every \(\varphi^3\) and \(\gamma^3\) vanishes where \(\widetilde P^2=P_s^2=0\). These are all the fields in \(\mathcal V_3\) as defined. Their complete flows fix each such point, and every pullback by the group they generate still vanishes there. Thus every element of \(\mathcal Q_3\) vanishes at the displayed point.

The generic two-factor fiber has dimension \(n(n-1)=2\). More concretely, Lemma 3.40, pp. 32–33, with \(j^*=2\), includes \[(P^1_2)^2\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1,11}}
 =\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1,11}}\] at this point. A family spanning that fiber cannot be a subset of a family all of whose values vanish. This is a contradiction to the claimed nesting, independently of any interpretation of a generic spanning claim away from this non-generic three-factor fiber.

Simply inserting the unmodified \(\mathcal V_2\) from p. 30 is insufficient. Write \(a=Z_1e_2=(a_1,a_2)\) and use \(t=z_{2,22}\) as a free coordinate in the chart \(a_1\ne0\) of \(Z_2a=b-e_2\). The coordinate pushforward is \[(\psi_1)_*\partial_t
 =\partial_{z_{2,22}}-\frac{a_2}{a_1}\partial_{z_{2,12}}
   +\frac{a_2^2}{a_1^2}\partial_{z_{2,11}}.                 \tag{1}\] It has genuine poles along \(a_1=0\), \(a_2\ne0\). Hence it is not a complete holomorphic field on the full affine ambient space required in Definition 3.28. Multiplication by \(a_1^2\) gives the polynomial field \[a_1^2\partial_{z_{2,22}}-a_1a_2\partial_{z_{2,12}}
       +a_2^2\partial_{z_{2,11}},                         \tag{2}\] which is complete: \(Z_1\) stays fixed and \(Z_2\) moves by translation. The analogous regularized fields over all charts, including the free \(Z_1\) coordinates, are finite and span each generic two-factor fiber.

A repair should define a regularized global base family first and include its coordinate lifts in every \(\mathcal V_K\). Its fields preserve \(\Phi_2\) and therefore all later \(\Phi_K\). This removes the explicit contradiction and supplies the missing old directions. The restrictions of generated fields and their flows in Lemmas 3.38–3.39 must then be reconciled with the revised family; it is not enough to assert that every newly listed conjugate vanishes on the non-generic stratum. The elementary repair ingredients are checked here, but a complete rewritten compatibility proof for the entire induction is not certified. This substantive dependency in the ellipticity argument motivates the Major assessment. No non-factorizable null-homotopic holomorphic map is established.

# Finite enlargement needs iterated pullbacks

Problem 2 (Major: invalid core spanning construction).  The construction in Lemma 3.30, pp. 28–29, takes only single-flow pullbacks of the original fields. Its resulting finite set need not span the claimed saturation. Lemma 3.31 inherits this proof step.

Take \(M=\mathbb C^3\), \(A=\{X,Y,Z\}\) with \[X=\partial_x,\qquad Y=x\partial_y,\qquad Z=y\partial_z,
 \qquad U=\{xy\ne0\}.\] All three fields are complete and span on \(U\). Every point can be moved into \(U\) by first translating \(x\) and then moving \(y\), so \(\mathcal C_A(U)=\mathbb C^3\). Under the usual pullback convention, \[(\alpha_s^X)^*Y=(x+s)\partial_y,\quad
 (\alpha_s^Y)^*Z=(y+sx)\partial_z,\quad
 (\alpha_s^Z)^*Y=x\partial_y-sx\partial_z.\] The other six single pullbacks are immediate. At the origin all nine types, for every time, lie in \(\operatorname{span}\{\partial_x,\partial_y\}\). Thus any collection of the form actually prescribed on p. 29 misses \(\partial_z\) there. In contrast, for \(\beta=\alpha_1^Y\circ\alpha_1^X\), \[\beta^*Z=(y+x+1)\partial_z\] is nonzero in that direction at the origin. A convention reversing pullbacks changes signs but not the single-step obstruction.

Here is a finite enlargement repair. Work on \(\Omega=\mathcal C_A(U)\), put \(B_0=A\), and let \(N_r\) be the analytic locus where \(B_r\) fails to span. For each irreducible component of \(N_r\), some original \(A\)-flow must leave \(N_r\) at a point of that component. Otherwise all these fields would be tangent to that component at its generic smooth points, their flows would preserve it, and its points could never reach \(U\). Choose one such point and field for each component. There are at most countably many components. For a fixed field, each chosen point has a discrete set of times returning to \(N_r\). A common time avoiding their countable union can therefore be chosen. Set \[B_{r+1}=B_r\cup
 \{(\alpha^V_{t_{r,V}})^*W:V\in A,\ W\in B_r\}.            \tag{3}\] Every irreducible component of \(N_r\) loses a point, and \(N_{r+1}\) is a proper analytic subset on every component. Its dimension decreases. After finitely many rounds, \(N_r\) is empty. Each \(B_r\) is finite and lies in \(\Gamma(A)\). This also applies to the vertical tangent bundle on the submersion saturation used in Lemma 3.31.

The two changes are essential: pull back the current family, allowing compositions, and use irreducible components in the dimension argument. This repairs the finite-enlargement lemma; the example is not a counterexample to its existence conclusion.

The dependency is part of the principal proof. Lemma 3.31, p. 29, imports this finite spanning construction. The paragraph following Theorem 3.36, p. 31, uses it to produce the generic spray, and Lemma 3.39, p. 32, uses the corresponding mechanism over the non-generic stratum. Corollary 3.32 and Remark 3.43, p. 34, transmit the step into the induction proving stratified ellipticity and the Oka lifting applications of Theorems 3.3 and 2.10, Corollary 2.11, and Theorem 2.12. The displayed construction therefore fails inside the core proof, which warrants Major even though an iterated repair is available. Neither the existence conclusion of Lemma 3.30 nor the final factorization theorem is disproved.

# Four further local corrections

Problem 3 (Minor: flow order).  Lemma 3.34, p. 30, gives the wrong order for its specified composition of flows.

Let \(f=y\) on \(\mathbb C^2\), \(N=\{y=0\}\), \(V_1=x\partial_y\) and \(V_2=\partial_x\). Then \(V_2V_1f=1\) everywhere on \(N\), but \[\alpha^{V_2}_{t_2}\circ\alpha^{V_1}_{t_1}(0,0)=(t_2,0)\] never leaves \(N\). The reversed composition gives \((t_2,t_1t_2)\) and does leave. Generally the mixed derivative of \(f(\alpha^{V_1}_{t_1}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha^{V_n}_{t_n}(x))\) at zero is \(V_n\cdots V_1f(x)\). Thus reversing the displayed flow order provides the intended assertion. The later saturation argument only needs a word in the generated group, so its escape mechanism survives.

Problem 4 (Minor: the approximation set).  Definition 2.7 and Theorem 2.8, p. 6, omit the requirement that \(K\) be an \(\mathcal O(X)\)-convex compact set.

Take the elliptic projection \(\mathbb C\to\{\mathrm{pt}\}\), \(X=\mathbb C\), one parameter, no interpolation subset, and \(K=\{|z|=1\}\). Define \[F_0(z)=
 \begin{cases}4\bar z,&|z|\le1/2,\\1/z,&|z|\ge1/2.\end{cases}\] This is continuous and holomorphic near \(K\). No entire function \(g\) can approximate it on \(K\) with error less than \(1\), since \[2\pi=\left|\int_{|z|=1}(1/z-g(z))\,dz\right|
 \le2\pi\sup_K|1/z-g|<2\pi\] would follow. Add holomorphic convexity and compactness, and the usual arbitrary accuracy quantifier. The lifting applications in Corollary 2.11 and Theorem 2.12 may take \(K=\varnothing\), so this omission does not refute those applications.

Problem 5 (Minor: block elimination).  The upper elementary factor in the elimination display on p. 10 has an extra minus sign.

In the notation of that display, write the remaining matrix as \[M=\begin{pmatrix}
 I_{n-1}&0&0&b\\
 -d^T&1&b^T&c\\
 0&0&I_{n-1}&d\\
 0&0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-b\\-b^T&-c-d^Tb\end{pmatrix}.\] The necessary right factor is \(U(B)\), where \(U(B)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&B\\0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), whereas the text uses \(U(-B)\). With \(b=d=0,c=1\) the printed product is \(U(2E_{nn})\), although its claimed right-hand side is \(I\). Using \(U(B)\) gives the required elimination. The Whitehead identities on p. 9 and the null-homotopy of the smaller symplectic block survive this correction; the main existence assertion is not disproved.

Problem 6 (Minor: parity of critical values).  Lemma 3.16, p. 17, and its repetition in Remark 3.37, p. 31, omit the even/odd distinction.

For \(K\ge2\), the critical-value set following from Theorem 3.4 is \[\Phi_K(S_K)=
 \begin{cases}
 \{e_{2n}\},&K\ \text{even},\\
 \{(a,e_n):a\in\mathbb C^n\},&K\ \text{odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{4}\] For even \(K\), every point of \(W_K^c\) has all the relevant odd blocks annihilating \(e_n\), so the recursion gives \(\Phi_K=e_{2n}\). For odd \(K\), only the last odd block can contribute, giving \((a,e_n)\); choosing the earlier even blocks zero realizes the critical values.

An explicit counterexample to the printed uniform condition is \(n=1,K=4,y=(1,1)\). Here \(S_4=\{z_1=z_2=z_3=0\}\) has image \((0,1)\). The fiber over \((1,1)\) is nonempty and smooth: after eliminating \(z_4\), its equation is \[z_1+z_3+z_1z_2z_3=1,\] whose gradient \((1+z_2z_3,z_1z_3,1+z_1z_2)\) never vanishes. Yet \(\pi_1(y)=1\). The sufficient-condition proof incorrectly infers vanishing of the odd blocks from the target’s second half alone. The even branch of Theorem 3.25 on p. 26 already uses the correct unique singular value. Smooth fibers still lie in \(W_K\), and the generic/non-generic partition by \(\pi_K\) remains valid.

# Positive checks, algebraic clarifications and limits

The insertion \((I_n,0,G_1-I_n,G_2,\ldots)\) on p. 8 preserves the complete product and places the continuous lift in \(W_L\). The Oka homotopy preserves the last row; the smaller symplectic block in pp. 9–10 starts at the identity, retaining the required null-homotopy. The uniform factor bound can be tracked by the existing uniform \(L(n,d)\) and the fixed Whitehead factor count. The final disjoint-union argument with varying parameter spaces is not independently certified as written, and the parametric \(n=1\) input remains an external boundary.

For every nonzero complex vector \(a\), \(Z\mapsto Za\) from symmetric matrices is surjective, without requiring \(a^Ta\ne0\). This verifies the three-step preimage construction in pp. 15–16, including isotropic vectors. The Jacobian recursion and low-order critical sets agree. The non-generic product decomposition and the connected-fiber projection argument were traced. Removing a positive-codimension subset from a connected singular space is not by itself sufficient to prove connectedness; the smooth-part argument on p. 26 should make explicit its projection over the connected complement of the exceptional affine subspace, with connected fibers and local openness.

Theorem 3.21’s completeness mechanism is valid. Its derivative columns are \(c_j+f_jv\); any cofactor containing two copies of \(v\) vanishes, so the vector field is affine in its active variables. The other coordinates remain fixed parameters. The eight sufficient field types and the forward complete-lifting mechanism of Lemma 3.18 were checked. This is not a proof of every global if and only if completeness formulation. The incomplete Riccati example is a different class and does not invalidate the listed sufficient types.

The complementary-bases assertion in pp. 38–40 can be checked directly. If a row \(u\) annihilates \(A_I\) and the basis columns \(B_J\), then \(uB=0\), while \(uA\) is supported in \(J\). From \(AB^T=BA^T\), \((uA)B^T=0\). Independence of \(B_J\) gives \(uA=0\); full rank of \([A\ B]\) then gives \(u=0\). Thus \([A_I\ B_J]\) is invertible.

The \(K=3,4,\ge5\) rank and escape calculations were read with their nonzero-component assumptions. Two displayed simplifications deserve explicit clarification. In Lemma 3.49, p. 45, put \(u=\widetilde P^{K-4}\), \(v=\widetilde P^{K-3}\). The actual determinant is \[\det B=\pm u_{i^*}^{\,n-k}v_{j^*}^{\,k}
 \det[(I+Z_{K-1}Z_{K-2})e_I\mid Z_{K-1}e_J].              \tag{5}\] The missing first factor is nonzero under the preceding reduction on p. 44, so the required nonvanishing survives. In the \(C_k\) steps on pp. 41 and 46, the exhibited motion in the previously zero \(i^*\)-diagonal direction raises the rank to \(k+1\); it can be routed directly to \(B_{k+1}\) instead of the stated intermediate \(B_k\setminus C_k\). Neither observation is a counterexample to the final rank-increase conclusion.

These checks do not certify a complete replacement of all interfaces after Problem 1. No failure of the final factorization theorem has been established. Absence of such a counterexample is not independent acceptance.
