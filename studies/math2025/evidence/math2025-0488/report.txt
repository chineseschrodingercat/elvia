Title: Scientific review of
A determinantal point process approach to scaling and local limits of random Young tableaux

# Scope and assessment

The examined source is the author-hosted journal-form PDF of J. Borga, C. Boutillier, V. Féray and P.-L. Méliot, Annals of Probability 53 (2025), 299–354, DOI `10.1214/24-AOP1706`. Page references below use the printed journal pagination. All 56 physical pages were read; this does not constitute independent verification of every contour estimate or of the cited external theorems.

The principal finding concerns the treatment of the time endpoints in the local limit theorem and in the proof of the height formula. An exact square tableau calculation contradicts the empty-process conclusion at these endpoints. It also disproves the uniform kernel convergence used to exchange limit and integral in the proof of Theorem 5. This is a Major issue in a derivation of a central result. The calculation does not disprove the height formula or the bulk local limit. Two separate Minor issues concern Proposition 22 and the parameter boundary in Proposition 11.

# Problem 1: endpoint behavior and the proof of Theorem 5

Theorem 16, p. 312, allows \(t_0\in[0,1]\) and asserts convergence to the empty process whenever \((x_0,t_0)\) is outside the liquid region. Propositions 37 and 40, pp. 345–346, explicitly retain the time endpoints. The omitted cases described there include precisely \(x_0=0,t_0=0\) and \(x_0=\eta b_i,t_0=1\).

## An admissible counterexample

Take \(\lambda^0=(1)\). Its \(n\)-fold dilation is an \(n\times n\) square with \(N=n^2\), \(\eta=1\), and interlacing coordinates \(a_0=-1,b_1=0,a_1=1\). Put \(x_0=0,t_0=1\); the arithmetic condition \(x_0\sqrt{|\lambda^0|}\in\mathbb Z\) is satisfied. Equation (8) becomes \[U(x_0+U)=(1-t_0)((x_0+U)^2-1),\qquad U^2=0.\] Thus this point is frozen according to Definition 4, p. 304.

Equation reference: eq:max, eq:nonempty. Let \(V_N\) be the value in the bottom-right box. This is the largest of all \(N\) entries and its Russian horizontal coordinate is zero. The uniform Poissonized law is normalized Lebesgue measure on the order polytope, by the definition on p. 302. Dilating every coordinate by \(s\) maps this polytope onto its subset with all entries at most \(s\). Consequently, exactly, \[
 \mathbb P(V_N\le s)=s^{N},\qquad 0\le s\le1.\] Its bead in the process (22) is at \((0,n(V_N-1))\). For the bounded set \(B=\{0\}\times[-1,0]\), \[
 \mathbb P\bigl(\widetilde M_{\lambda^N}^{(0,1)}(B)\ge1\bigr)
 \ \ge\ 1-(1-1/n)^{n^2}\ \longrightarrow\ 1 .\] Every bounded Borel set is a continuity set for the zero measure. Hence [eq:nonempty] (#eq:nonempty) contradicts convergence in probability to the empty process in the topology of Section 2.2. Equivalently, a continuous function equal to one near \((0,0)\), with bounded support, detects this bead with probability tending to one. There is no endpoint-of-the-test-set ambiguity.

The minimum entry \(W_N\), in the top-left box on thread zero, satisfies \(\mathbb P(W_N>s)=(1-s)^{n^2}\). Thus \(nW_N\to0\) in probability and gives the same contradiction at \((x_0,t_0)=(0,0)\). These examples use the specified Poissonized law, rather than replacing it with deterministic standard-tableau values.

## Failure of the asserted uniform convergence

Equation reference: eq:gap. The issue also affects a specific step in the proof of the central height formula. On p. 349 the proof claims kernel convergence on the whole closed parameter rectangle and then uniformity on compact sets, immediately interchanging a limit and an integral from zero. For the square, define \[f_N(s)=n^{-1}K_{\lambda^N}((0,s),(0,s)).\] The critical polynomial has discriminant \(x^2-4t(1-t)\), so at \(x=0\) the proposed density is \[f(s)=\frac{\alpha(0,s)}{\pi}
     =\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{s(1-s)}},\qquad 0<s<1.\] The one-point density includes the nonnegative contribution of the maximum entry. Differentiating [eq:max] (#eq:max) therefore gives \(f_N(s)\ge n s^{n^2-1}\). At the interior points \(s_n=1-n^{-2}\), \[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{f_N(s_n)-f(s_n)}{n}
 \ \ge\ e^{-1}-\pi^{-1}>0 .\] This disproves uniform convergence near the upper endpoint without relying on how a density is defined at the endpoint itself. The lower endpoint behaves similarly. In particular, uniformity on compact subsets strictly inside a regular regime cannot justify the stated integration across all exceptional parameters.

## Correction and limits of the finding

Restricting the local-limit statement to \(0<t_0<1\) removes the two counterexamples above; any endpoint theorem needs a separate analysis. For example, [eq:max] (#eq:max) gives \(N(1-V_N)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Exp}(1)\), which differs from the paper’s \(\sqrt N\) local scale. This observation identifies one extreme entry, not an entire endpoint point-process limit.

The height formula requires a justified endpoint and critical-time argument in place of the uniformity assertion. A suitable route is to work first away from exceptional times and then control their contribution through the limiting cumulative height or a uniform integrability estimate. The general version of that replacement is not proved here.

There is substantial counterevidence against interpreting the calculation as failure of the height formula: the maximum bead contributes only \(1/n\) to normalized total height, and the proposed square density is integrable, with \[\int_0^t f(s)\,ds=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\sqrt t,\qquad
 \int_0^1 f(s)\,ds=1.\] The error is nevertheless more than an isolated boundary value in a statement: the false uniformity premise is used in deriving Theorem 5, one of the paper’s stated main contributions (pp. 301, 304). The assessment Major concerns that verified invalid derivation. It does not assert failure of a corrected theorem, of the liquid-region analysis, or of the continuity criterion.

# Problem 2: a countable-event extension of weak convergence

Proposition 22, p. 320, extends convergence of probabilities from cylinder events based on bounded continuity sets to arbitrary countable unions, intersections and complements of such events. This extension is false even for deterministic finite counting measures.

On \(\mathcal X=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R\), let \[\nu_n=\delta_{(0,1/n)},\qquad \nu=\delta_{(0,0)},\qquad
 B_k=\{0\}\times(1/k,2),\quad k\ge1 .\] For every bounded continuous test function, its values at \((0,1/n)\) converge to its value at \((0,0)\); hence \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in the topology defined immediately above the proposition. Each \(B_k\) is bounded and \(\nu(\partial B_k)=0\). However, for \[A=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\{\mu:\mu(B_k)=1\},\] one has \(\mathbb P(\nu_n\in A)=1\) for every \(n\), by choosing \(k>n\), whereas \(\mathbb P(\nu\in A)=0\).

The finite-dimensional assertions (36)–(37) do not imply setwise convergence on their generated sigma-algebra. A correct replacement uses finite Boolean combinations of the stated cylinder events, or events whose boundary has probability zero under the limiting law. This is Minor: the claim itself is disproved, but no necessary use of its false countable extension was found in the main argument. In particular, the proof of Corollary 19 on pp. 352–353 uses a Skorohod coupling, a finite random height cutoff, and eventual agreement of finite restrictions. That argument is not refuted by this example.

# Problem 3: degenerate shapes in Proposition 11

The displayed domain on p. 309 includes \[r>0,\quad -1<p<r,\quad |p|<q\le\min(p+2,2r-p).\] Yet (20) asserts strict interlacing for \[a_0=-1,\quad b_1=(p+q-2)/2,\quad a_1=p,\quad
 b_2=(p-q+2r)/2,\quad a_2=r.\] Choose the admitted rational triple \((p,q,r)=(1/2,3/2,1)\). It yields \[(a_0,b_1,a_1,b_2,a_2)=(-1,0,1/2,1/2,1),\] so the purported strict inequality \(a_1<b_2\) fails. If the degenerate corner is cancelled, the shape is the square: its transition transform reduces exactly to \[\frac{U(U-1/2)}{(U+1)(U-1/2)(U-1)}
 =\frac{U}{U^2-1}.\] The square has continuous limiting surface, as also noted for rectangles on p. 306. But Proposition 11 for \(r=1\) requires \(p=0\) or \(q=2-\sqrt{2-p^2}\); neither holds for this triple. The proof on p. 350 also divides by \(a_1-b_2=0\).

Thus either the stated domain includes an object for which its proposed strict-coordinate definition fails, or, with the natural degenerate-shape interpretation, the stated continuity characterization is false there. The local correction is to impose \(q<\min(p+2,2r-p)\) for genuine \(L\)-shapes and handle the rectangular boundary separately. This is Minor; it does not challenge the criterion for strictly interlacing nondegenerate shapes.

# Other checks and remaining scope

The diagonal of the bead kernel (21), p. 311, is \(\alpha/\pi\), consistent with the factor in Theorem 5. The terminology intensity \(\alpha\) does not establish a missing factor of \(\pi\). For a direct check of Proposition 20, write the critical equation as \(1-t=U G(x+U)\). Implicit differentiation gives \[U_t=-\frac1{G+UG'},\qquad U_x=-\frac{UG'}{G+UG'},
 \qquad U_t+\frac{U(U_x+1)}{1-t}=0,\] at the simple liquid root. This agrees with the stated equation and its proof on p. 331.

The later height PDE is expressly presented with an informal ingredient on p. 314; it has not been treated as a fully proved theorem. The review follows the contour argument and its dependence on the critical roots, but does not certify all uniform estimates or reprove the external existence and limit-shape inputs. None of the findings depends on absent code, numerical simulation, or an assumption that the acquired journal PDF is identical to an arXiv revision.
