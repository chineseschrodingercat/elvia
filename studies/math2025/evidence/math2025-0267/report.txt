Abstract: The assessment of the exact acquired author version is Major. The construction supplying the uniform quantitative input for Theorem B necessarily contains too many elements: a base-expansion argument disproves its asserted size bound for every proposed universal constant. This is a failure of an essential proof input, not a counterexample to the existential small-set lemma or to Theorem B itself. Theorem A’s separate small-set input can be repaired despite false bounds at small primes. Two auxiliary orthogonality propositions confuse coordinate lines with hyperplanes; their corrected versions and their actual dependence in the main argument are identified. Normalization clarifications and a substantive factorial-division correction are also supplied. All 41 physical source pages were read; the finite appendix constructions were checked element by element. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The quantitative construction for Theorem B

Problem 1 (Major: the constructed set in Lemma 2 exceeds its asserted bound).  The last sentence of the proof of Lemma 2, pp. 7–8, asserts that its explicitly constructed union \(A\) has \(|A|\le p^{1/(2k+2)}\) whenever \(p>k^{Kk}\) for a suitable universal \(K\ge32\). A compulsory branch of the printed construction disproves this assertion for every such constant.

The argument uses only the unambiguous negative interval in \(B_1\) and the negative endpoint blocks in \(A_i,B_i\). It is independent of the conflicting formulas for \(C_i\). Suppose first that \(K\ge32\) is an integer, and set \(k=K\) and \(N=k^{Kk}\). Let \(p\) be the least prime greater than \(N\). Bertrand’s postulate gives a genuine prime satisfying \[N<p<2N.\] Thus all prime and parameter hypotheses are met. Put \[m=4k+4,\qquad i=m-1,\qquad
 y=p^{1/m},\qquad x=\lceil y\rceil.\] Since \(k/(k+1)\ge1/2\), \[y>k^{K/8}\ge k^4>k.\] Furthermore, \[\frac{x^i}{p}
 \le \frac{(1+1/y)^i}{y}
 \le \frac{\exp(i/y)}{y}
 <\frac2y<1,\] because \(i/y<(4k+3)/k^4<1/2\) for \(k\ge32\).

The definition of \(B_1\) contains all \(-kx+\delta_0\), \(0\le\delta_0\le k\). At step \(r\ge2\), choose \(j=2kx-\delta_{r-1}\), where \(0\le\delta_{r-1}\le k\), in its negative endpoint block. This is a permitted \(j\), and the same point belongs to the negative block of \(A_r\). Induction therefore shows that \(A_i\) contains every residue \[b_i(\delta)=
 -kx-2kx\sum_{\ell=1}^{i-1}x^\ell
       +\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-1}\delta_\ell x^\ell,
 \qquad 0\le\delta_\ell\le k.\] There are \((k+1)^i\) such digit vectors. They give distinct integers because \(x>k\). Their maximum difference is \[k\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-1}x^\ell<x^i<p,\] so they remain distinct modulo \(p\). Since the final \(A\) contains \(A_i\), this proves \[|A|\ge(k+1)^{4k+3}.\] On the other hand, \[p^{1/(2k+2)}
 <(2k^{Kk})^{1/(2k+2)}
 <2k^{K/2}
 <(k+1)^{4k+3}.\] The claimed bound is false. If an arbitrary real universal constant worked, increasing it to an integer would still give a valid threshold; the same construction contradicts that integer choice. The failure is therefore not repaired by making the fixed universal constant larger.

This is an analytic counterexample family, with the prime specified by a well-defined minimum and its existence and range proved. No enormous set or uncertified numerical prime is being substituted for the proof.

## Actual impact and repair boundary

Lemma 2 supplies the \(S_k(p)\) estimate used in Corollary 3(ii), p. 10, and then in Theorem 1(iii), pp. 26–30, which is the quantitative Theorem B. The displayed construction does not provide that essential input. The Major assessment reflects this direct loss in the stated proof of the uniform threshold.

The counterexample concerns the cardinality of the constructed union. It does not show that every \(S_k\)-type set is large, nor that Lemma 2’s existence statement or Theorem B is false. A different construction, or a separately proved changed threshold, is needed. A threshold on the scale \(k^{O(k^2)}\) might absorb this particular branch count, but neither the full \(S_k\) property nor such a repaired theorem is established in this review.

There are also literal inconsistencies to correct before any revised construction can be checked: \(A_1\) is printed as \(\{kx,kx\}\), and the separate \(C_2\) formula on p. 7 uses the negative predecessor map \(g\), whereas the general \(C_i\) formula on p. 8 uses \(f\) and interchanges the relevant \(B\) and \(C\) endpoint terms. The cardinality obstruction above survives every interpretation that retains the printed negative \(B_i\) blocks. It is not merely an objection to these typographical inconsistencies.

# Small-set bounds for Theorem A

Problem 2 (Minor: Lemma 1 is false at small primes).  Lemma 1, pp. 5–6, asserts \(S_1(p)\le2\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor\) for every prime \(p\ge3\). With the paper’s intended nonempty-set convention, \(S_1(3)=3>2\) and \(S_1(7)=5>4\).

For \(p=3\), a point and the two distinct endpoints of its nontrivial three-term progression already exhaust the field. For \(p=7\), suppose a set of at most four elements had the required midpoint property. After translation and scaling it contains \(-1,0,1\). With only one additional element \(t\), the midpoint condition for \(1\) requires \(t\in\{2,3\}\), whereas that for \(-1\) requires \(t\in\{4,5\}\). This is impossible. The set \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) does satisfy the condition, proving the stated minimum.

The last logarithmic equality on p. 6 fails when \(p+1\) is a power of two. The halving construction does, however, prove the uniform bound \[S_1(p)\le2\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor+2;\] away from that power-of-two boundary its original sharper bound is valid. This suffices to repair Corollary 1. For \(p\ge512\), writing \(h=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor\ge9\) gives \[(2h+2)^2<2^h-1\le p-1.\] The first inequality holds at \(h=9\) and persists by induction. For \(199\le p<512\), the possible boundary values \(255\) and \(511\) are composite; the original bound works except at \(257\), which is explicitly covered by the table. All remaining required primes are covered by the Appendix, including \(127\).

Every one of the Appendix’s 27 listed sets, containing 251 listed elements in total, was checked with exact modular arithmetic. For each element, a nonzero common difference and both endpoints were verified; all size inequalities \(|A|^2<p-1\) passed. The required prime list has no omissions. The auxiliary witnesses were independently rechecked by the primary reviewer. The claimed optimality through \(p=151\) and the necessity of the excluded-prime range were not exhaustively reproduced; they are not needed for this sufficient-condition repair. This issue therefore does not invalidate the checked route to Theorem A.

# The auxiliary orthogonality characterizations

Problem 3 (Minor: Propositions 2 and 3 use the wrong summation sets).  Proposition 2 on p. 16 and Proposition 3 on p. 17 impose zero sums on hyperplanes obtained by fixing one coordinate. Their proofs require zero sums along coordinate lines. The printed equivalences are false.

Take \(p=5\), \(n=1\) and the nonsingular matrix \(M=[1]\). Each set \(\{v:v_1=x\}\) is a singleton, so the printed hypotheses force \(f=g=0\). The universal pairing condition is consequently true over both \(\mathbb C\) and \(\mathbb F_5\). But \(x=1\) is a nowhere-zero vector with \(Mx=1\), so property \((\mathrm{P}'1)\) is false, and \[(1-g)^2=1-2g+g^2\] is nonzero in both \(\mathbb Z[\mathbb F_5]\) and \(\mathbb F_5[\mathbb F_5]\). Thus the relevant group-ring properties are also false. All matrix, dimension and prime hypotheses are satisfied.

The mismatch also occurs in dimension three: \(f(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_1^4\) on \(\mathbb F_5^3\) has all the printed hyperplane sums zero, but its sum on the first coordinate line through zero is \(-1\).

## Correct statements and verification

Replace the conditions by \[\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}f(v+te_j)=0,\qquad
 \sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}g(v+ta_j)=0
 \quad\hbox{for every }v,j.\] For the unnormalized Fourier transform, these conditions make the respective transforms vanish on the frequency hyperplanes \(u_j=0\) and \(\langle a_j,u\rangle=0\). The correct bilinear Parseval identity is \[\sum_vf(v)g(v)=p^{-n}\sum_u\widehat f(u)\widehat g(-u).\] The frequency hyperplanes are stable under negation. Hence the covering property implies orthogonality. Conversely, a nowhere-zero admissible frequency gives opposite characters with pairing \(p^n\ne0\). This proves the corrected complex version.

Over \(\mathbb F_p\), let \(T_bf(v)=f(v+b)\), \(D=\prod_j(I-T_{e_j})\) and \(D'=\prod_j(I-T_{a_j})\). The regular translation representation is faithful, so the group-ring identity is equivalent to \(DD'=0\). Triangular reduction of monomials shows that \(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}D\) is spanned by the monomials with every exponent at most \(p-2\); its dimension is \((p-1)^n\). Since \(\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}t^{p-1}=-1\), this is precisely the corrected line-sum space. The analogous assertion holds for \(D'\) after a basis change. Finally, \[D^*=(-1)^nT_{-\sum_j e_j}D,\qquad
 (\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}D)^\perp=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}D.\] Thus the corrected orthogonality condition is equivalent to \(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}D'\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}D\), or \(DD'=0\).

The source’s Lemma 6 already states the correct line-sum condition. The main proof in Section 6 actually invokes Lemma 7’s explicit finite-support binomial functions, whose independent coefficient proof is valid. It does not use the false hyperplane hypotheses of Propositions 2–3. The local classification follows that checked dependence, not merely the availability of a repair.

# Normalization and characteristic-p qualifications

## Normalization clarification (original Problem 4)

Several equalities in Section 5 have missing nonzero normalization or sign factors. The zero criteria used in the checked arguments survive these corrections. No necessary zero or nonzero test is invalidated by these unit factors; this item is not counted as a substantive Minor finding.

With \(\widehat f(u)=\sum_vf(v)\omega^{\langle u,v\rangle}\) and the source’s normalized convolution \(f*g=p^{-n}\sum_wf(w)g(v-w)\), the p. 13 formula should be \[\widehat{f*g}=p^{-n}\widehat f\,\widehat g.\] For \(p=5,n=1,f=g=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}\), its left side is the constant \(1/5\), whereas the printed right side is \(1\). The separate formula for the transform of a pointwise product is consistent with the normalized convolution.

For \(\delta_j=I-T_{e_j}\) on p. 18, the leading coefficient of a product of differences is \((-1)^n\prod_jb_j\), not \(\prod_jb_j\); already \(\delta x=-1\). The p. 20 line sum of \(x^{p-1}\) equals \(-1\), not \(1\). The point-indicator polynomial used on p. 23 takes value \((-1)^n\) at its support, and the top-degree coefficient/pairing identity on p. 24 has the same sign. These statements follow directly from \((X^p-X)'=-1\) and the finite-field power sums. None of the required nonvanishing tests changes. The phrase \((p-1)^n\) functions on p. 18 should refer to the dimension, not the cardinality of the function space.

Problem 4 (Minor: the proof of Lemma 9 divides by a possibly zero factorial).  On pp. 24–25 the proof divides by \(Q!\), although \(Q=\sum r_i=\sum s_j\) need not be less than \(p\). The final coefficient-duality conclusion is nevertheless true.

For \(p=5\), \(M=I_2\), \(r=s=(4,1)\) and \(Q=5\), the target coefficient is \(1\). The intermediate coefficient in \((x_1y_1+x_2y_2)^5\) is \(5=0\) in \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(Q!=0\). The displayed division is therefore undefined in the stated field, under fully permitted exponents.

Here is a proof avoiding that division. Sum over matrices \(L=(\ell_{ij})\) with nonnegative entries, row sums \(r_i\) and column sums \(s_j\), and put \[H=\sum_L\frac{\prod_{i,j}a_{ij}^{\ell_{ij}}}
                    {\prod_{i,j}\ell_{ij}!}.\] Every factorial in this expression is invertible, since \(\ell_{ij}\le r_i\le p-1\). If \(C\) is the coefficient for \(M\) and \(E\) the transposed coefficient, then \[C=\Bigl(\prod_i r_i!\Bigr)H,\qquad
 E=\Bigl(\prod_j s_j!\Bigr)H.\] This proves the claimed zero equivalence. The reduction bars do not affect these top-degree coefficients. Alternatively, the weighted coefficient identity can first be proved over the integers and then reduced. Exact checks covered all 480 matrices in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_5)\) and all 85 equal-total-degree exponent pairs, giving 40,800 successful comparisons, including 14,400 with \(Q\ge5\).

# Positive proof checks and remaining editorial corrections

Lemma 3’s probabilistic partition estimate was checked, including the ceiling in the number of colors and the integer greedy choice of disjoint progressions. With \(u=p^{1/(2k+1)}\) and \(p>k^{32k}\), the estimates \(\lceil u\rceil^{2k}<2u^{2k}\) and \(u/(8k^2)>2\log p\) suffice for the displayed union bound. Lemma 4’s random dilation argument and Corollary 2 then supply the separation sets whenever \(N_k(p)S_k(p)<p-1\). This valid conditional route is distinct from the failed uniform construction in Problem 1.

Lemma 7’s pairing is exactly a coefficient of the rescaled group-ring product. The tested shifts cover its support. Nonzero rescaling multiplies each factor by a unit whose augmentation is nonzero; the endpoints \(t_i=0,p-1\) cause no exception. Independent exact one-dimensional checks covered 1,600 parameter choices and 14,400 coefficient comparisons, alongside the general argument.

In Lemma 10, an inclusion-maximal support set \(I_u\) and a coordinate \(j\notin I_u\) force every remaining pairing term with \(\ell'_j<k\) to vanish. The surviving \(\ell'_j=k\) term removes the corresponding row factor. The subsequent truncated group-algebra coefficient extracts the lower-dimensional identity. On p. 26 the relevant general reference is Corollary 2. On pp. 29–30 the minor must delete row \(j\) and column \(\ell\), namely \(M_{j,\ell}\); the transposed and later inconsistent subscripts should be corrected. The associated products and quantifiers must omit \(j\) only in the row family, retaining every coordinate index in the other family. These changes restore the checked conditional Theorem 1(i) argument.

Theorem C’s larger-vector-space construction in Lemma 11 was reconstructed. Its two displayed systems are bases; projection supplies the extra row factor, the enlarged product vanishes modulo \(p\), and a Fourier witness with independently scaled blocks makes the integer product nonzero. The equality of the kernels of multiplication by \((1-g^{e_i})^2\) and \((1-g^{e_i})\) holds over the integer group ring by its embedding into the complex semisimple group algebra. In the final derivative calculation, the apparently omitted group elements are eliminated using the original product identity; this is not an additional error. Invertibility then permits the required row-factor removal and dimension reduction.

In Proposition 4’s converse, pp. 36–37, the repeated upper bound \(n+1\) should be \(n\), and the ambient ring should be that of the declared extended space. After scaling to a common affine hyperplane, the map \(v\mapsto v+L(v)w\) is injective and gives the stated lifting argument. The final coefficient basis is triangular in \(1-g^w\). Use \(\sigma_j=0\) for negative \(j\) to cover the small-dimension endpoint. Theorem B’s introductory statement should explicitly quantify a nonsingular \(M\) and put \(X_i,Y_i\subset\mathbb F_p\), as its intended coordinate formulation requires.
