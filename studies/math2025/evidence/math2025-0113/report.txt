Title: Initial mathematical review:
Kähler compactification of \(\mathbb C^n\) and Reeb dynamics

Authors: Chi Li and Zhengyi Zhou. Version: supplied publisher PDF, published online 7 October 2025, DOI `10.1007/s00222-025-01377-2`, 28 pages, printed pp. 181–208. Below, PDF page \(j\) means printed page \(180+j\).

# Outcome and scope

A concrete error was found in the isotropy-group indexing preceding Proposition 3.3. The asserted unique predecessor need not exist, and choosing one arbitrarily can produce a false Morse–Bott family in the first page of the spectral sequence. This is classified as a Major proof-level correction because that first page is an input to the general orbifold argument. A direct period-and-component replacement is given below, and the lowest-degree survival argument can be checked with this replacement. No counterexample to a main theorem is claimed. In particular the regular, free-circle-action case used for smooth-divisor compactification does not exhibit this obstruction.

All 28 PDF pages were read as text. The principal hypotheses, grading shifts, local cyclic-orbit formula, spectral survival argument and final index calculation were audited. Imported analytic compactification, Floer foundations, algebraic discrepancy results and metric rigidity theorems are dependencies, not independently reproved results of this review. Fifteen evidence pages were also inspected visually.

# Hypotheses and normalization checks

Theorem 1.1 assumes the compact complex manifold and smooth Kähler complement divisor setup from the introduction; it is not a statement for arbitrary noncompact \(X\). Theorem 1.3(2) retains the smooth-link assumption and, in general dimension, Shokurov’s conjecture. The unconditional dimensional conclusion in the paper is \(n\leq3\). The homological formula in Theorem 1.4 additionally requires a Liouville filling with \(c_1^\mathbb Q(W)=0\); its first, orbit-theoretic formula does not require that filling (PDF pp. 2–3).

Equation reference: eq:shift, eq:principal. The paper’s \(\operatorname{md}\) is discrepancy, so its value at a smooth \(n\)-dimensional point is \(n-1\), not the log-discrepancy value \(n\). Its lower SFT degree is \[
 \operatorname{lSFT}(\gamma)=\mu^\mathbb Q_{\rm LCZ}(\gamma)+n-3,\qquad
 \mu_{\rm LCZ}=\mu_{\rm RS}-\tfrac12\dim\ker(\phi(1)-I).\] For a principal orbit of the regular action, \(R(M)=r\), and for its \(\ell\)-fold cover (PDF pp. 17–18) \[
 \mu_{\rm RS}=2\ell r,\quad
 \mu_{\rm LCZ}=2\ell r-(n-1),\quad
 \operatorname{lSFT}=2\ell r-2.\] For the Hopf sphere, \(r=n\); the simple-orbit values are \(n+1\) and \(2n-2\), respectively. These agree with the first nonzero equivariant positive symplectic-homology degree \(n+1\) in Proposition 3.6 and with \(2\operatorname{md}(0,\mathbb C^n)=2n-2\). Thus the two relevant shifts pass this normalization test.

Equation reference: eq:local. The local calculation in Proposition 3.4 (PDF pp. 19–20), for a cyclic chart of order \(m\), yields \[
 \operatorname{lSFT}(\gamma)
 =\frac2m\left(rw_1+\sum_{j=2}^{n}w_j\right)-2.\] Indeed the local trivialization has \(\operatorname{lSFT}^\tau(\gamma)=-2\), including the zero normal weights through the nullity term. On the \(m\)-fold iterate the local value is \(-2\sum_{j=2}^nw_j-2\), while the global value is \(2w_1r-2\). Dividing their difference by \(m\) gives [eq:local] (#eq:local). This matches the weights and the final minus one in Proposition 2.16’s discrepancy formula. This check accepts that algebraic formula as a dependency rather than validating every valuation argument in its proof.

# Problem 1: the isotropy poset need not have unique predecessors

Major proof-level indexing defect. Source: PDF p. 17 (printed p. 197), the paragraph defining \(G^-\) and Proposition 3.3(1); the dependent first-page formula is on PDF p. 21 (printed p. 201).

The text considers the finite set \(S\) of isotropy subgroups, ordered by inclusion, and assigns to each nonminimal \(G\) a unique maximal smaller member \(G^-\). Such uniqueness does not follow from the subgroups being cyclic.

Equation reference: eq:action. Take the effective weighted circle action on \(S^5\subset\mathbb C^3\) \[
 t\cdot(z_1,z_2,z_3)
 =(e^{2\pi i2t}z_1,e^{2\pi i3t}z_2,e^{2\pi i6t}z_3),
 \qquad t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.\] This is a positive quasi-regular Reeb action in the standard Sasaki cone, with smooth link and \(c_1(\xi)=0\). Concretely, if the standard contact form is normalized by \(\alpha_0(R_a)=H>0\), where \(R_a\) generates [eq:action] (#eq:action) and \(H=2|z_1|^2+3|z_2|^2+6|z_3|^2\), then \(\alpha_0/H\) has Reeb field \(R_a\), up to the common period normalization. The identities \(R_a(H)=0\) and \(\iota_{R_a}d\alpha_0=-dH\) verify the Reeb equations directly.

The stabilizer at a point is the group of order equal to the greatest common divisor of the weights of its nonzero coordinates. Hence \[S=\{\{e\},\mathbb Z_2,\mathbb Z_3,\mathbb Z_6\}.\] Both \(\mathbb Z_2\) and \(\mathbb Z_3\) are maximal proper members below \(\mathbb Z_6\), and neither contains the other. The proposed \((\mathbb Z_6)^-\) is undefined.

This is not just a choice of notation. At the indicated times the full fixed-point sets are \[\begin{array}{c|c|c}
T&N_T=\operatorname{Fix}(\phi_T)&\text{topology}\\ \hline
1/6&\{z_1=z_2=0\}\cap S^5&S^1\\
1/3&\{z_1=0\}\cap S^5&S^3\\
1/2&\{z_2=0\}\cap S^5&S^3\\
1&S^5&S^5
\end{array}\] If one selects \(G^-=\mathbb Z_3\) for \(G=\mathbb Z_6\), the paper’s set \(G\setminus G^-\) includes \(k=3\). It then labels the period-\(1/2\) family by \(M^{\mathbb Z_6}=S^1\), although the actual family is \(N_{1/2}=S^3\); the latter is also counted under \(\mathbb Z_2\). Choosing \(G^-=\mathbb Z_2\) creates the analogous error at period \(1/3\). The smaller circle is not an independent Morse–Bott family at those periods: the kernel of the return map includes the additional fixed coordinate direction. Thus family dimension, nullity correction and multiplicity of the written first-page terms can be wrong.

## A direct correction and its mathematical extent

Equation reference: eq:page. Index by each positive period \(T\) and each connected component \(N_{T,i}\) of the full fixed-point set \(\operatorname{Fix}(\phi_T)\). Put \(B_{T,i}=N_{T,i}/S^1\). Count a component once at that period, including orbits that are iterates of shorter orbits; do not add a second copy for a proper fixed submanifold. In this notation the intended first page has the form \[
 E^1_{p,q}=
 \bigoplus_{\substack{T=p/N\\i}}
 H_{p+q-\mu_{\rm LCZ}(T,i)}(B_{T,i};\mathbb Q),\] with the same common period denominator \(N\) and the orientation conventions of the paper’s cited Morse–Bott construction.

The clean fixed-point property follows from the compact circle action: an invariant metric linearizes it normally, and \(\ker(d\phi_T-I)=T N_{T,i}\). The contact nullity is \(2\dim_\mathbb CB_{T,i}\), giving the stated lower-index shift. In the normal complement the nontrivial complex eigenvalues give positive real determinant for \(I-d\phi_T\); the mod-two lower index is therefore still \(n-1\). Thus the parity input to Proposition 3.8 is retained under this corrected indexing.

In the explicit example, one may equivalently remove the union of both proper subgroups when listing the \(\mathbb Z_6\) terms; only residues 1 and 5 remain. The period/component formulation avoids assuming a globally tree-like isotropy poset. This is a verified correction of the enumeration and of the abstract survival argument below; it is not a complete new construction of equivariant Floer theory. Author adoption and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Lowest-degree survival and final deductions

The proof on PDF p. 23 chooses a bottom \(H_0(B_{T,i};\mathbb Q)\) class \(\alpha\) with minimum rational degree \(a\), then maximum period among those classes. Since \(R(M)>0\), adding a full principal period raises degree by \(2R(M)>0\); finitely many period types suffice, so that maximum exists.

An outgoing differential would have total degree \(a-1\), which is absent. If an incoming differential kills \(\alpha\), some source \(\beta\) at a strictly larger period has degree \(a+1\) and the opposite mod-two degree. Every bottom class has parity \(n-1\); consequently \(\beta\) lies in an odd, positive homology degree \(j\geq1\) of its block. The bottom class of that block then has degree \(a+1-j\leq a\), contradicting either the minimal degree or the maximal-period choice. This checks the algebraic survival argument when the blocks are the correctly enumerated families in [eq:page] (#eq:page).

For the compactification filling, the source supplies a homology ball and a Liouville cobordism to the standard sphere (PDF pp. 8,11). The unital transfer and exact/Gysin sequences give nonzero degrees \(n+1+2m\), \(m\geq0\) (PDF pp. 20–21). Combining the lowest degree with [eq:shift] (#eq:shift) gives \[2\operatorname{md}=(n+1)+(n-3)=2n-2.\] In the smooth divisor case the local quotient terms disappear, so \(\operatorname{md}=r-1\), hence \(r=n\). This is the required maximal Fano index for a manifold of dimension \(n-1\), consistent with the invoked Kobayashi–Ochiai criterion. The weighted example does not contradict this regular-case deduction.

For the metric conclusion, the stated conditionality on Shokurov and the smooth-link condition must be retained. The volume functional on positive weights obeys \[\frac{(w_1+\cdots+w_n)^n}{w_1\cdots w_n}\geq n^n\] by arithmetic–geometric mean, with equality only for equal weights. The external cone classification and metric rigidity inputs in PDF p. 24 are not independently reproved.

# Supporting checks

The report requests correction of Proposition 3.3’s indexing and the dependent first-page formula. The demonstrated defect is in a core general-orbifold proof input, even though its replacement is concrete. No main theorem is shown false; the regular-case normalization and final index deduction pass the checks above. Remark 3.9’s extension using linearized contact homology is explicitly accompanied by a foundation caveat in the source; it is not treated here as an established substitute for the filling hypothesis of Theorem 1.4.

No released author code or separate supplement was identified in the manifest; neither absence is a defect for this theoretical paper. This review does not certify all analytic estimates, all quotient valuation constructions, the full Floer transversality/orientation machinery, or every cited external theorem.
