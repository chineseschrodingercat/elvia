Title: Initial mathematical review: M25-0158
Twisted generating functions and the nearby Lagrangian conjecture

Authors: Mohammed Abouzaid, Sylvain Courte, Stéphane Guillermou, and Thomas Kragh. Publication identity: Duke Mathematical Journal 174 (2025), no. 5, 949–1011; DOI 10.1215/00127094-2024-0052 (https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2024-0052).

Reviewed source: arXiv:2011.13178v3, 29 July 2024, 53 pages. Printed and PDF page numbers agree. The journal body was not acquired or compared.

Initial assessment: Major. The displayed derived local system in the proof of Theorem 3.26 has an incorrect shift, already for the zero section of \(T^*S^1\). It therefore does not satisfy the hypothesis of the lemma used to obtain the fiber homology needed for tube generation. This is a demonstrated invalid derivation in the core argument, with a cohomological repair described below. Four further bounded defects concern strict cocycles, global indexing, and a missing properness hypothesis in auxiliary constructions. No counterexample to the main geometric conclusions is established. The scientific decision remains `pending`, with `human_reviews=[]`; this package is an initial review, not independent acceptance.

# Scope and proof structure

The main claims concern closed exact embedded Lagrangians \(L\subset T^*M\) with \(M\) closed. Theorem A gives vanishing of the induced maps on all homotopy groups of the stable Gauss map. It does not assert nullhomotopy for every \(M\). Theorem E obtains nullhomotopy for a homotopy sphere. Theorem C characterizes the existence of twisted generating functions for Legendrian immersions by factorization of the stable Gauss map through \(\pi:L\to M\). Theorem D supplies the additional tube-type control for nearby Lagrangians.

The central route is: the external homotopy equivalence \(\pi\); the twisted Giroux–Latour construction; a doubled generating function linear at infinity; contact-isotopy continuation; a difference-function homology calculation; stable recognition of tubes; and the tube-space lift of the Gauss class. The integral conclusion in the last step uses both Bökstedt’s rational result and the injectivity of the \(J\)-homomorphism in the two torsion congruence classes. Omitting the latter would misrepresent the paper.

The entire assigned manuscript, including Appendix A and Figure 1, was read as text. The checks below are exact algebraic, geometric, and derived-category tests. Foundational Floer, sheaf-theoretic, and algebraic \(K\)-theory results were checked at selected primary statements, not reconstructed in full.

The monoid \(\mathcal Q\) consists of actual quadratic forms with eigenvalues \(\pm1\), with ordered direct sum. It is not a commutative monoid. A \(\mathcal Q\)-twisted map to \(\mathbb N\) or \(\mathbb Z\) requires, on every relevant intersection, \[n_j=n_i+\dim q_{ij},\qquad q_{ij}\oplus q_{jk}=q_{ik}\] as strict equalities. Equality up to an unspecified permutation or homotopy does not satisfy the given definition.

# Problem 1: the derived local system has the wrong shift

Location. Theorem 3.26, proof on pp. 37–38, especially the displayed formula for \(G\) and its immediate application of Lemma 3.27. The latter lemma is on p. 36; \(D\) and its actual critical values appear on pp. 28–29 and in Figure 1.

The asserted construction. Writing \(k=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) and \(\pi\) for projection to \(M\), the paper specifies \[G=\operatorname{RHom}\!\left(R\pi_*
 k_{\{-A<F(x,-)\le-4s_1\}},\,k_M[\dim M+d]\right).
 \tag{1}\] Here \(d\) has already been chosen by the Morse–Bott relation \(H_{*+d}(F\le-4s_1,F\le-\infty)\simeq H_*(M)\). The proof then applies Lemma 3.27 to conclude \(G\simeq k_M\). That lemma requires \(R\Gamma(M;G)\simeq R\Gamma(M;k_M)\), as well as constant cohomology sheaves.

An admitted test case. Take \(M=S^1\), take \(L\) to be its zero section, and choose the Legendrian lift with \(z=0\). All closedness, exactness, and embedding hypotheses hold; \(\pi:L\to M\) is the identity. Take the untwisted, base-independent generating function \(f(w)=D(w)\) used by the paper, and set \(s_1=2/3\). Since \(D(w)=w^3-3w\) near its critical points, its critical values are \(-2\) and \(2\), exactly \(\pm3s_1\). Its difference function is \[F(w,w')=D(w)-D(w').\] Below the threshold \(-4s_1=-8/3\), the only critical point in each fiber is \((1,-1)\), with value \(-4\) and Hessian \[\operatorname{Hess}_{(1,-1)}F=
 \begin{pmatrix}6&0\\0&6\end{pmatrix}.\] The associated global critical manifold is \(S^1\), with negative normal index \(d=0\). It is orientable. No covering or grading convention for a nonzero index is involved.

For \(A>4\), the fiber sublevel pair acquires a single index-zero handle in this band. Equivalently, its relative cohomology is \(k\) in degree zero. The family is a product over \(S^1\), so, with the extension-by-zero convention for the displayed locally closed band, \[H:=R\pi_*k_{\{-A<F\le-8/3\}}\simeq k_{S^1}.\] The unbounded part contributes the unchanged lower sublevel pair; the difference-function gradient control gives the usual relative Morse calculation. Formula (1) now gives \[G=\operatorname{RHom}(k_{S^1},k_{S^1}[1])\simeq k_{S^1}[1],
 \tag{2}\] rather than \(k_{S^1}\). In standard cohomological grading, \(R\Gamma(S^1;G)\) has nonzero groups in degrees \(-1,0\), whereas \(R\Gamma(S^1;k)\) has them in degrees \(0,1\). Thus the hypothesis of Lemma 3.27 fails. This discrepancy does not depend on the sign convention for \(d\), since \(d=0\).

A cohomological repair. Keep the relative-cohomology local system \[H=R\pi_*k_{\{-A<F\le-4s_1\}},\qquad \widetilde G=H[d],
 \tag{3}\] instead of the dual in (1). The controlled sublevel family gives locally constant finite-dimensional cohomology. After making the negative normal bundle orientable, the Thom identification gives \(R\Gamma(M;H)\simeq R\Gamma(L;k)[-d]\). The projection homotopy equivalence therefore gives \(R\Gamma(M;\widetilde G)\simeq R\Gamma(M;k)\). One may also pass to an orientation cover of \(M\) and to a finite cover trivializing the finitely many cohomology local systems: their monodromy groups lie in finite general linear groups over \(k\). Lemma 3.27 then applies to \(\widetilde G\).

This establishes the required rank-one fiber cohomology, hence the corresponding homology over the field. The argument can be repeated for each prime; the finite cover need not be uniform in \(p\). The finitely generated integral Morse complexes then permit the tensor-product argument in Lemmas 3.22 and 3.28. This repair uses the same controlled-sublevel and Thom inputs; it does not validate formula (1).

Impact. The faulty application is the step that supplies the fiber homology for Proposition 3.25, hence Theorem 3.26 and Theorem D. Tube generation then supplies the lift used for Theorems A and E. Under contract 0.7.1 this demonstrated invalid core derivation is Major, even though (3) repairs its normalization. The example does not refute tube generation or the Gauss-map conclusions: the zero section plainly satisfies them.

# Problem 2: the reordering cylinder requires negative dimensions

Location. Lemma 3.7, pp. 26–27, specifically its final sketch of equivalence of the underlying \(\mathcal Q\)-twisted maps. The application is Theorem 3.9, pp. 27–28.

Valid input and the failed construction. Let the base be a point, with three identical open sets, ordered \(1<2<3\). Take \[(n_1,n_2,n_3)=(0,1,1),\qquad
 q_{12}=q_{13}=u^2,\quad q_{23}=0.\] Use \(f_1=D\), \(f_2=D\oplus_b u^2\), and \(f_3=f_2\), with \(b\) a sufficiently large common bound. These are valid functions linear at infinity and generate the same two-point Legendrian immersion. The cocycle and dimension conditions hold.

For the adjacent swap \(i=1\), the proof sets \(m=2(n_2-n_1)=2\) and obtains \[(n'_1,n'_2,n'_3)=(2,1,3).\] The prescribed cylinder order on p. 27 is \[1<2<2'<1'<3<3'.\] At a middle parameter value all six cylinder charts intersect. The associated dimensions in this order are \(0,1,1,2,1,3\). Thus the edge \(1'\to3\) would require a quadratic form of dimension \(1-2=-1\). No element of \(\mathcal Q\) has that dimension. Independently, the prescription \(q_{3,3'}=0\) has dimension zero, whereas this edge requires dimension two. Filling in unspecified transition forms or permuting their coordinates cannot fix either obstruction while keeping these data.

Counterevidence and bounded impact. The preceding formulas (3.4)–(3.5) for the reordered functions and their cocycle pass the dimension and gluing tests; they still generate the same immersion. The defect is in the asserted cylinder proving equivalence of twisting classes. A general replacement cylinder was not completed in this review, so full repair of that subclaim is not asserted.

The existence part needed for the nearby-Lagrangian tube construction is not invalidated by this test. Moreover, for a nearby Lagrangian, \(\pi\) is a homotopy equivalence and the Gauss-map factorization can independently identify the twisting class of the generated component. The review therefore records a Minor defect in this auxiliary class-preservation proof, with its general correction outstanding, rather than using it to claim failure of Theorem D. This boundary is distinct from Problem 1’s actual homological application.

# Problem 3: the translation homotopy is not a strict cocycle

Location. Lemma 2.23 and its proof, p. 17. Definitions 2.7 and 2.9 are on pp. 7–8; strict bundle and twisted-map conditions are in Appendix A, pp. 48–49. The v3 version explicitly records that this proof was changed after an omission in an earlier version; the test here concerns the actual v3 construction.

Cocycle failure. The proposed doubled cover keeps the old forms within each copy and defines cross terms by \(q_{i'j''}=q\oplus q_{ij}\) for a fixed \(q\in\mathcal Q_1\). On the triple \(i'<j'<j''\), with \(q_{jj}=0\), the cocycle condition would require \[q_{ij}\oplus q=q\oplus q_{ij}.\] Take \(q=u^2\) and \(q_{ij}=-u^2\). The two matrices are \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\); at \((1,0)\) their values are \(-1\) and \(1\). Both forms satisfy the normalized eigenvalue condition. They are isometric, but they are not equal as required. The additional rule declaring every first-copy index less than every second-copy index also asks for \(q_{ij}\) when \(j<i\), and can demand a negative dimension.

This is not cured by choosing a special representative of the universal identity map. If \(q=[\varepsilon]\), \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), the necessary identity \(A\oplus[\varepsilon]=[\varepsilon]\oplus A\) forces \(A=\varepsilon I\) by comparison of matrix entries. All transitions would then lie in the definite-form submonoid, isomorphic to \(\mathbb N\), whose space \(|B(\mathbb N,\mathbb N)|\) is contractible. Such data cannot represent the identity of \(X=|B(\mathbb N,\mathcal Q)|\): the two edges \(0\to1\) given by \(u^2\) and \(-u^2\) form a loop detected nontrivially by the additive index map to \(B\mathbb Z\). Thus \(X\) is noncontractible independently of Lemma 2.23.

What is and is not damaged. The printed proof does not establish either its full translation argument or the asserted inclusion equivalence by that argument. No counterexample to those conclusions is supplied. A coherent block-interchange construction would require actual compatibility data; merely saying that direct sum is homotopy commutative is not a completed repair.

The stronger claim is not essential in the places initially suggesting a main-theorem dependency. In diagrams (2.50)–(2.51), the map from \(B(\mathbb N,\mathcal Q)\) to \(B(\mathbb Z,\mathcal Q)\) need not itself be an equivalence to deduce the Gauss-map factorization. The later explicit translation use in Theorem 3.9 is by \(2m\), and even translations on the full stabilized model follow from left multiplication by the stabilizing hyperbolic form and Theorem 2.21. Freely chosen auxiliary dimensions in the existence construction can be chosen even. This prevents rating an unneeded full \(+1\) argument as an established main-theorem obstruction. The issue is Minor; the complete broader proof remains to be supplied.

# Problem 4: the refinement relation can contain a cycle

Location. Definitions A.2–A.3, p. 43, and the proposed order on \(I\sqcup J\) in the proof of Lemma A.10, p. 47.

Counterexample within the stated hypotheses. Let \(X=\{0,1\}\) be discrete. Let \(I=\{a<b\}\), \(U_a=\{0\}\), \(U_b=\{1\}\); let \(J=\{c<d\}\), \(V_c=\{1\}\), \(V_d=\{0\}\); and set \(\sigma(c)=b\), \(\sigma(d)=a\). These are directed covers. The map \(\sigma\) is an allowed refinement because its restriction to each \(J_x\) is a map from a singleton and hence is nondecreasing. Global monotonicity is not required by Definition A.3.

The stated cross rules yield \(a<c\) because \(a<\sigma(c)=b\), and \(d<a\) because \(\sigma(d)=a\le a\). Together with \(c<d\) they give \[a<c<d<a .\] Consequently the displayed relation is not a partial order and cannot index the claimed directed cylinder cover.

Verified order repair and impact. First retain in \(J\) only comparisons supported by nonempty intersections \(V_j\cap V_{j'}\), then take their transitive closure. This remains a suborder of the old order and makes \(\sigma\) globally nondecreasing. Every local order on \(J_x\) and every nonempty MV simplex is unchanged. Next take the transitive closure of the indicated cross rules together with the orders on \(I\) and the modified \(J\).

This generated relation is acyclic. Along a cross path the image in \(I\) is nondecreasing, and every \(I\to J\) edge makes a strict increase. A cycle crossing between the two parts is therefore impossible; cycles within a part are already excluded. New comparisons within \(J\) either concern disjoint sets or agree with the existing local total order. Thus the endpoints, nonempty MV simplices, cocycle data, and resulting classifying maps are unchanged. One may identify the identical MV blow-ups when applying the definition of equivalence.

This is a Minor defect in the global indexing description, with a complete correction of that description. No additional failure of the classifying maps or of the main geometric calculation is established. The grading rests on the unchanged mathematical data, not simply on the length of the correction.

# Problem 5: compactly supported lifting needs proper critical data

Location. Theorem 3.6, pp. 25–26, read with the unrestricted generating-function definition on pp. 5–6. The statement permits a manifold without boundary and a family of Legendrian immersions, but gives no compactness or properness condition on their critical preimages.

Take \(M\) to be a point and the constant family \(f^t(v)=\sin v\) on \(\mathbb R\). Its critical set is the countable discrete zero-manifold \(L=\{\pi/2+k\pi:k\in\mathbb Z\}\), and \(f'\) vanishes transversely there. It generates a Legendrian immersion into \(J^1(\mathrm{pt})=\mathbb R\), with values \(1\) and \(-1\). Thus it is allowed by the printed unrestricted definition, although \(L\) is not compact.

Choose a compactly supported contact isotopy of \(\mathbb R\) which translates neighborhoods of both critical values by \(1/4\) at its final time. Such an increasing diffeomorphism is obtained from a compactly supported vector field equal to \(1/4\) along the two short trajectories. The target critical values are then \(5/4\) and \(-3/4\).

For every finite stabilization and compactly supported correction \(\eta\), the function \[\sin v+x_1y_1+\cdots+x_my_m+\eta(v,x,y)\] still has critical points \((\pi/2+k\pi,0,0)\) for sufficiently large \(|k|\). They lie outside the support of \(\eta\) and retain values \(1\) and \(-1\). It therefore cannot generate the stated translated immersion. This directly refutes the unrestricted lifting assertion, without executing author code.

The actual application in Theorem 3.9 has functions linear at infinity and a proper critical locus over the compact base support of the contact isotopy. It excludes the example. A properness condition over that support, together with the parametric control used by the generating-family lifting theorem, is needed in Theorem 3.6. No extension to arbitrary nonproper families is justified here. This is a Minor statement-scope defect; it does not refute the compact application or Theorem D.

# Checks supporting the remaining argument and limitations

The right-\(\mathcal Q\) equivariance in Lemma 2.17’s quadratic generating-family construction was checked against the formulas, including the linear change of variables. The codimension estimate underlying Lemma 2.20 gives at least \(m+1\) for the nontransversality locus. The stable Gauss-map construction retains both compactness and the difference from the vertical Lagrangian distribution. In Definition 2.9 one must use the actual local singular-set cover when some charts omit branches; the stronger displayed equality with every full inverse-image chart does not follow from one-sided domain inclusion. Using that local cover as a refinement avoids treating this observation as a further theorem counterexample.

The cutoff gradient estimate in Lemma 3.4 uses \(\|\nabla q(u)\|=2\|u\|\), which explains the normalized eigenvalues in v3. For the difference operation in Definition 3.16, consistency with \(F=\cdots+\epsilon-\epsilon'\) and the unit action forces a minus sign on the second cutoff term. With that normalization, the separate gradient-error bounds combine in squared norm and the claimed control is valid for general normalized \(Q\). This uniquely determined formula correction is not counted as another independent Problem.

The difference-function tensor-product calculation uses finite free Morse complexes. Lemma 3.28 should be understood with finitely generated cohomology, as in its actual application. Mere finite rational rank would admit, for example, \(A=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Q\) and \(B=\mathbb Z\), whose derived reductions modulo every prime are rank one although \(A\) is not a shift of \(\mathbb Z\). This scope qualification does not invalidate the finite Morse-complex use. The contact-isotopy lifting statement is likewise used with a compact/proper critical locus; a claim without such control for arbitrary noncompact immersed critical sets is not inferred.

Proposition 3.25’s stabilization allows both simple connectivity and the dimension range for disk embeddings and \(h\)-cobordism. Those hypotheses were tracked. The finite-field local-system argument, once normalized as in (3), legitimately uses finite covers that may depend on the prime.

For Section 4, the bar construction requires the homotopy-fiber conclusion supplied by a quasifibration; the primary Hatcher statement is sufficient for its exact sequence. The rational equivalence of the rigid-tube map implies injectivity on the free homotopy groups of \(BO\). In degrees \(1,2\bmod8\), the factorization through \(BG\) and the cited injective \(J\)-map handle the \(\mathbb Z/2\) groups. The separate index component handles \(\pi_0\) of the relevant fiber. Hence Corollary 4.7 is not an unjustified conversion of rational vanishing into integral vanishing. Nullhomotopy for a homotopy sphere follows from the homotopy-group statement and \(\pi:L\to M\); no blanket nullhomotopy for an arbitrary manifold is claimed.

The primary Bökstedt preview states the rational equivalence in its \(G/O\) and Whitehead-space formulation. The full Waldhausen comparison with tubes and the full proof were not rederived. The same limit applies to the full Floer/sheaf proof that \(\pi\) is a homotopy equivalence, and to all foundations of generating families and stable spherical fibrations. Reading the whole manuscript is not an assertion of exhaustive proof certification.
