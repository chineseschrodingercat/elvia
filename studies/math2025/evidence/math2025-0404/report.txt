Abstract: The retained March 2025 preprint of Nersesyan and Rissel is examined at the passage from localized transport controls to Navier–Stokes controls. Three central statements or derivations require correction: the displayed final control coefficients omit the normalization needed for zero mean; the scaling lemma allows forcing regularity and bounded-set uniformity for which exact shear solutions contradict its conclusion; and the small-time theorem places a single fixed control before an exact-limit quantifier that its approximate-control argument cannot supply. A separate sufficient condition for observability admits removable point discontinuities and is false as stated. Complete bounded arguments are given below. These findings do not constitute a counterexample to the main approximate-controllability conclusion. Repairs are stated only to the extent proved here.

# A normalization lost in the final control formula

Problem 1.  Equations (5.4), (5.14), and (5.15), on pp. 27 and 30, do not reproduce the mean-free control (3.9) on p. 18. For admissible coefficient functions, the displayed final formula can have nonzero spatial mean.

Equation reference: eq:c, eq:correctmean. Let \(K\) denote the number of covering squares, distinct from the Fourier index set \(\mathcal K\). Put \[c=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\chi\,dx,\qquad m(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\widehat\eta(x,t)\,dx,\qquad
 A(t)=\int_0^t m(s)\,ds .\] Since the \(K\) translates of \(\chi\) form a partition of unity in (3.3)–(3.5), integration gives \[Kc=1,\qquad c=K^{-1}.                                      \] The earlier control (3.9) is \[\widetilde\eta
 =\widehat\eta-c^{-1}A(t)\bar y(t)\cdot\nabla\chi
                  -c^{-1}m(t)\chi .                        \] Its spatial integral is zero, because \(\int_{\mathbb T^2}\bar y(t)\cdot\nabla\chi\,dx=0\).

Equation reference: eq:badmean. The profiles in (5.4), however, are printed as \(\bar y\cdot\nabla\chi\) and \(\chi\), and the coefficients in (5.15) are \(-A(t)\) and \(-m(t)\), with no factors \(c^{-1}\). In reference time, even granting that displayed time dependence, their combination is therefore \[\eta_{\rm printed}
 =\widehat\eta-A(t)\bar y(t)\cdot\nabla\chi-m(t)\chi,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb T^2}\eta_{\rm printed}\,dx=(1-K^{-1})m(t).                     \] The additional factor \(\sigma\) in (5.14) scales this nonzero mean; it does not remove it.

## An allowed choice with nonzero \(m\)

This discrepancy is not merely conditional on an impossible mean. Take the covering parameter \(K=36\), so its square side is \(l_K=2\pi/5<\pi/2\), and take \(\mathcal K=\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\), as in Example 5.4. Choose an admissible \(\omega\) containing the reference square and an admissible \(\Omega\) containing the prescribed cuts. The cutoff \(\chi\) is nonnegative and has positive integral \(1/36\).

During the first pause interval, \(\bar y=0\) and the return flow moves \(O_1=(0,l_K)^2\) to the reference square. At the end of that interval the replayed time \(\tau_1(t)\) tends to \(1\). By (3.11), \[\Xi(x,t)\longrightarrow Y(x,t,0)=x-p_\omega
 \quad\hbox{on }\operatorname{supp}\chi.\] Consequently \(\cos(\Xi_1(x,t))\) is strictly positive on that support for \(t\) sufficiently close to the pause endpoint: at the endpoint it is at least \(\cos(l_K)>0\), and the flow is continuous. Choose only the \((1,0)\) cosine coefficient nonzero, with a nonnegative, nonzero smooth time cutoff supported sufficiently close to replayed time \(1\). These are permissible \(L^2\) coefficient functions. Equation (3.10) then gives \(m(t)>0\) on a nonempty part of the first pause. On that part, \[\int_{\mathbb T^2}\eta_{\rm printed}\,dx=\frac{35}{36}m(t)>0.\] All drift, localization, and coefficient requirements used in this calculation are those of the actual construction.

## Impact and the bounded correction

The vorticity space is mean-zero, and the integral of the curl of a periodic vector field is zero. A nonzero-mean force cannot be the curl of the periodic velocity control asserted in (5.18). The failure occurs in the explicit construction used by Theorem 5.2 and the proof of Theorem 1.1, and is therefore assessed as Major under the core-derivation criterion.

Multiplying both correction coefficients by \(K\), or dividing both correction profiles by \(c\), restores exactly [eq:correctmean] (#eq:correctmean) and its mean-zero identity. This is an algebraic repair of this discrepancy, not a verification of the entire advertised profile representation. In particular, the source also lists the \(N+1\) profile as spatially fixed while writing it as \(\bar y(t)\cdot\nabla\chi\). The return drift uses horizontal and vertical translations. A treatment of that time dependence is still required to certify the exact claimed list of fixed profiles. No independent additional severity is assigned to that unresolved representation issue.

An implicit normalization \(\int_{\mathbb T^2}\chi\,dx=1\) would remove the numerical difference but conflicts with the actual partition-of-unity construction [eq:c] (#eq:c). Absorbing \(K\) into a profile is a valid correction, rather than an identity of the printed formulas.

# The scaling lemma exceeds its forcing assumptions

Problem 2.  Lemma 4.2, p. 23, assumes \(g,h\in L^2((0,1);H^{m-1})\) but asserts convergence in \(H^m\), uniformly over bounded sets of those forces. Both the regularity scope and the stated uniformity fail.

Its proof first asserts that the transported solution \(v\) stays bounded in \(C([0,1];H^{m+1})\) from precisely those assumptions [p. 24, after (4.7)]. Transport does not supply that gain. The following solutions also show failure of the conclusion, not just a missing justification.

## A common exact shear reduction

Set \(\nu=1\), \(m=1\), \(w_0=0\), and \(\widetilde U=0\). These meet all initial-state, viscosity, and mean-path requirements. For a mean-zero function of \(x_1\) alone, the zero-mean Biot–Savart velocity has only a second component. It is perpendicular to the vorticity gradient. Its nonlinear self-advection is thus identically zero. Translations by \(a(t)\) preserve this property.

Equation reference: eq:heat. For \(g(t,x)=f(x_1-a_1(t))\) and \(h=0\), the inviscid transport solution is \[v(t,x)=t f(x_1-a_1(t)),\qquad v(1,x)=f(x_1).\] At physical time \(\delta\), the Navier–Stokes vorticity in the moving coordinates solves a one-dimensional forced heat equation. If \(f(x_1)=\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\sin(nx_1)\), its exact endpoint is \[w_\delta(\delta,x)=\sum_{n\ge1}b_n F(\delta n^2)\sin(nx_1),
 \qquad F(z)=\frac{1-e^{-z}}{z}.                             \] Indeed, after putting \(s=t/\delta\) and removing the translation, each coefficient satisfies \(q_n'(s)+\delta n^2q_n(s)=b_n\), \(q_n(0)=0\). The return property removes the terminal translation. The construction works for the actual return drift; it does not replace that drift by zero.

## Failure even for a single fixed allowed \(g\)

Take \(b_n=1/n\). Then \(f\) is mean-zero and in \(H^0=L^2\), because \(\sum n^{-2}<\infty\), but \(f\notin H^1\), because \(\sum n^2n^{-2}=\infty\). Translations preserve its \(L^2\) norm, so \(g\in L^2((0,1);H^0)\) as required. The linear transport solution has \(v(1)=f\notin H^1\).

For every fixed \(\delta>0\), [eq:heat] (#eq:heat) has coefficients bounded by \(1/(\delta n^3)\), hence \(w_\delta(\delta)\in H^1\). Moreover, \[\|\partial_1(w_\delta(\delta)-f)\|_{L^2}^2
 =\frac12\sum_{n\ge1}\bigl(1-F(\delta n^2)\bigr)^2=\infty ,\] since \(F(\delta n^2)\to0\) as \(n\to\infty\). Thus the claimed \(H^1\) difference is not finite for any \(\delta>0\).

All source assumptions hold: \(w_0=0\in H^2\), \(g\in L^2H^0\), \(h=0\in L^2H^0\), and the mean path is zero apart from the prescribed return drift. The vorticity is a genuine shear solution, so no omitted nonlinear term repairs this example.

## Failure of bounded-set uniformity with smooth spatial data

The uniform assertion also fails within spatially smooth examples. Take \(g_n(t,x)=\sin(n(x_1-a_1(t)))\), \(h=0\), and \(\delta_n=n^{-2}\). The \(L^2_tH^0\) norms of \(g_n\) are all \(1/\sqrt2\), whereas \[\|\partial_1(w_{\delta_n}(\delta_n)-v_n(1))\|_{L^2}
 =\frac{n}{\sqrt2}\bigl(1-F(1)\bigr)
 =\frac{n}{e\sqrt2}.\] The error diverges along a bounded set of allowed forces.

Even restricting \(g\) to zero does not validate the asserted uniformity in \(h\). On \((0,1)\) define \[h_n(t,x)=
 \begin{cases}
 n\sin\!\bigl(n(x_1-a_1(t/\delta_n))\bigr),&0<t<\delta_n,\\
 0,&\delta_n\le t<1 .
 \end{cases}\] These are mean-free, spatially smooth forces with \(\|h_n\|_{L^2_tH^0}^2=1/2\). With \(g=0\) and \(w_0=0\), the inviscid comparison is \(v=0\), while the exact shear endpoint is \[w_{\delta_n}(\delta_n,x)=\frac{1-e^{-1}}{n}\sin(nx_1),
 \qquad
 \|\partial_1w_{\delta_n}(\delta_n)\|_{L^2}
 =\frac{1-e^{-1}}{\sqrt2}>0.\] Boundedness in \(L^2\) alone does not make integrals over shrinking time intervals uniformly small. This is the distinct obstruction in the estimate following source (4.12).

## What a valid restricted argument needs

The examples leave intact the possibility of a scaling statement for the particular smooth controls constructed earlier. A sufficient restricted hypothesis for the standard remainder argument is a uniform bound for \(v\) in \(C H^{m+1}\) together with \[\sup_{h\in\mathcal B}
 \int_0^\delta\|h(t)\|_{m-1}^2\,dt\longrightarrow0 .\] For example, fixed \(h\) has this absolute-continuity property. A bounded set in \(L^2_tH^{m-1}\) need not have it, as the \(h_n\) example demonstrates. The specific time-translated tails of one fixed prescribed force in Theorem 5.2 warrant their own estimate; (5.10) provides useful information of that kind. A complete corrected uniform theorem is not asserted here.

Lemma 4.2 is explicitly the scaling step used in Theorem 5.1, whose uniformity is then invoked in Theorem 5.2. Its false guarantee and unsupported regularity inference are assessed as Major. This does not show that the main theorem, under its stronger actual data and with appropriately quantified controls, is false.

# Fixed controls cannot have the asserted arbitrary exact limit

Problem 3.  Theorem 5.1, pp. 27–28, asserts that one collection \((\zeta_l)_{l=1}^N\subset L^2(0,1)\) can be fixed so that \(w_\delta(\delta)\to w_1\) in \(H^k\) as \(\delta\to0\) for arbitrary \(w_0,w_1\in H^{k+1}\). This places the control choice before the exact-limit quantifier. The proof instead chooses the controls after fixing an approximation tolerance.

## An admissible nonsmooth target

Equation reference: eq:target. Choose \(k=2\), \(w_0=0\), \(h=0\), \(\nu=1\), and \(U=0\), and let \[w_1(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-4}\sin(nx_1).                        \] This target has zero mean, \(\sum n^6n^{-8}<\infty\), and \(\sum n^8n^{-8}=\infty\). It belongs to \(H^3\) but not \(H^4\), so it meets the theorem’s target hypothesis and is not smooth. Zero velocity means are consistent with the mean-free initial and target vorticities.

For any single fixed collection of the asserted controls, put \(g=\widetilde\eta_{\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_N}\), using the correctly normalized earlier formula (3.9). Every spatial profile in (3.9)–(3.12) is smooth, with bounded derivatives of each order on the compact reference time interval. The generating drift is a finite Fourier sum with continuous time coefficients, so its flow and inverse have those spatial derivative bounds. Time replay, multiplication by \(\chi\), and the time-integrated scalar mean correction preserve this property. Therefore \[g\in L^2((0,1);H^q)\quad\hbox{for every integer }q\ge0.\] The constants may depend on the fixed controls, which is sufficient here. The corresponding transported endpoint \[v(1,x)=\int_0^1g(Y(x,1,s),s)\,ds\] is consequently in every \(H^q\), and is smooth.

## The actual limit for fixed smooth controls

Equation reference: eq:remainder, eq:bootstrap. The conclusion just used does not rely on the overbroad version of Lemma 4.2. For these fixed controls its relevant special case can be proved directly. Set \(W_\delta(s,x)=w_\delta(\delta s,x)\), let \(v\) solve \(\partial_sv+\bar y\cdot\nabla v=g\), \(v(0)=0\), and put \(r=W_\delta-v\), \(R=\Upsilon(r,0)\), \(V=\Upsilon(v,0)\). Then \[\begin{split}
 \partial_s r+\bar y\cdot\nabla r
 +\delta(V+R)\cdot\nabla r+\delta R\cdot\nabla v
 -\delta\Delta r
 &=\delta(\Delta v-V\cdot\nabla v),\\
 r(0)&=0 .
 \end{split}                                               \] The spatially constant translation term makes zero contribution to each differentiated \(L^2\) energy. The div–curl estimate gives \(\|R\|_{H^3}\le C\|r\|_{H^2}\). The differentiated product estimates in two dimensions and Young’s inequality thus give, for \(E=\|r\|_{H^2}^2\), \[E'(s)+\delta\|\nabla r\|_{H^2}^2
 \le C\delta(1+E(s)+E(s)^2),\qquad 0<s<1,                    \] where \(C\) depends on fixed Sobolev bounds of \(v\) and not on \(\delta\). For clarity, the transport commutator is bounded by \(C\delta\|V+R\|_{H^3}\|r\|_{H^2}^2\); the term \(R\cdot\nabla v\) is bounded using \(\|v\|_{H^3}\); and \(\delta\Delta v\) pairs with \(r\) through one integration by parts and half the dissipative term. These bounds imply [eq:bootstrap] (#eq:bootstrap). Smooth approximations justify the same energy inequality for the \(L^2\) time coefficients.

On any interval where \(E\le1\), [eq:bootstrap] (#eq:bootstrap) implies \(E(s)\le3C\delta\). Choosing \(\delta<(6C)^{-1}\) and using continuity closes this bound on \([0,1]\). Hence \[w_\delta(\delta)\longrightarrow v(1)\quad\hbox{in }H^2.\] If Theorem 5.1 held for [eq:target] (#eq:target) with a single fixed control, uniqueness of the \(H^2\) limit would force \(w_1=v(1)\). This is impossible because \(v(1)\) is smooth and \(w_1\notin H^4\).

## Quantifiers and impact

The source proof fixes an arbitrary \(\varepsilon>0\), uses Theorem 3.3 to choose a transport control whose terminal error is below \(\varepsilon\), and then takes \(\delta\) small. It establishes a tolerance-dependent control choice. It does not establish one control valid for every tolerance. Allowing a family \(\zeta^\varepsilon\) or \(\zeta^\delta\) changes the asserted quantifiers and avoids the smooth-endpoint contradiction.

A precise consequence for a fixed smooth transport control is \[\lim_{\delta\to0}
 \|w_\delta(\delta)-w_1\|_{H^2}
 =\|v(1)-w_1\|_{H^2}.\] This limit can be made arbitrarily small by a new approximate-control choice; it need not be zero for one fixed choice. No complete uniform replacement over the source’s full force class is claimed, especially in view of Problem 2.

The false exact-limit assertion is a central intermediate guarantee, subsequently used to select the activation time in Theorem 5.2. Its supported severity is Major. The main theorem asks for approximation at a given tolerance, which is weaker; the present counterexample does not contradict that conclusion.

# Point discontinuities do not imply observability

Problem 4.  The sufficient construction in Remark 2.5, p. 9, allows functions that are zero as \(L^2\) elements. Mere discontinuity at a dense countable set, even with left and right limits everywhere, does not imply Definition 2.4.

Take the allowed case \(T=1\), \(n=1\). Enumerate a countable dense subset \(D=\{d_1,d_2,\ldots\}\subset(0,1)\) without repetitions and define \[\phi(t)=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}{\bf1}_{\{d_j\}}(t).\] This is a bounded measurable function. At any point, its left and right limits are both zero: given an error bound, exclude the finitely many points whose weights exceed it, and bound the remaining weights. It is continuous at every point outside \(D\) and discontinuous at every \(d_j\), since its value there is \(2^{-j}\) but its two one-sided limits are zero. All literal requirements of Remark 2.5 are met; the disjointness requirement is vacuous for one set.

Nevertheless \(\phi=0\) almost everywhere. On any subinterval take \(b=0\) and \(a_1=1\). Then \(b+a_1\phi=0\) in \(L^2\) although \(a_1\) does not vanish. This contradicts Definition 2.4. For any larger finite family, disjoint dense countable sets give the same example component by component.

## A verified stronger sufficient condition

Replace the point spikes by \[\psi_j(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}{\bf1}_{\{t>d_{j,n}\}},\] where the sets \(D_j=\{d_{j,n}\}_n\) are pairwise disjoint and dense. The series converges uniformly, is bounded, has left and right limits, and has a nonzero jump at each point of \(D_j\); every other \(\psi_i\) is continuous there. If \(b+\sum_j a_j\psi_j=0\) almost everywhere, its one-sided limits are zero. Taking their difference at \(d_{j,n}\) gives \(a_j(d_{j,n})2^{-n}=0\). Density and continuity imply \(a_j=0\) everywhere, and then \(b=0\). This proves observability for the strengthened construction.

The distinction is between a nonzero jump and a removable point discontinuity; it is not a choice of an \(L^2\) representative that can be ignored in a sufficient pointwise hypothesis. The main generating-drift theorem separately assumes observability, and the stronger construction supplies it. This local defect is therefore assessed as Minor; no failure of approximate controllability is inferred from this example.

# Dependency, surviving scope, and outcome

The supported highest impact is Major. Problem 1 affects the explicit mean-free force used in the transition to a velocity control. Problems 2 and 3 affect the scaling and limiting statements used in Theorems 5.1 and 5.2. Problem 4 concerns a local sufficient condition that can be strengthened directly.

The analysis also checks several safeguards against overstatement. The first control profiles are allowed to be time-dependent; the source itself distinguishes its conclusion from time-independent-mode controllability. The nonlinear term vanishes exactly in the shear counterexamples, rather than being neglected. The nonsmooth target in Problem 3 has exactly the required Sobolev regularity. Higher spatial regularity of the actual constructed controls is relevant counterevidence to treating every use of the scaling idea as invalid. A corrected proof of the main result would need to reconcile the explicit normalization and profile representation, the required uniform scaling class, and the tolerance-dependent control choice. That complete reconstruction and any author adoption are unverified.

Appendix A was read, but a complete general topological audit was not performed. In particular, if a periodic curl-free field is changed only inside \(\mathbb T\times I\), with \(I\) a proper horizontal interval, the spatial mean of its first component cannot change: \(\int_{\mathbb T}\Lambda_1(x_1,x_2)\,dx_1\) is independent of \(x_2\) and can be evaluated outside \(I\). This constrains the claimed change of that component by the local cut perturbation on p. 38. It is retained as an unresolved scope point, not an additional graded finding or a counterexample to Theorem A.1.
