Abstract: This review concerns only arXiv:2408.12987v2. Two false proof inferences affect the central relative-barrier iteration and the lower Green-function argument. A separately asserted extension of the source space admits an explicit unbounded energy solution. These are Major issues in their stated scopes; no counterexample to the original weighted-source boundary regularity theorem or its final Green-function estimate is established. Five further issues concern first oscillations, an integer regularity endpoint, a kernel example, a weak-solution sign, and the Poisson normalization. Each is accompanied by an admissible example or an exact calculation. The initial review read all 58 pages as text and inspected its recorded source pages as images. The present independent acceptance focuses on the reported Problems and corrects the average convention used in Problem 4. The scientific decision remains pending.

# Identity, scope, and dependencies

The reviewed paper is by Minhyun Kim and Marvin Weidner [KW], with frozen annual identity M25-0368. The exact source is the 58-page preprint arXiv:2408.12987v2, dated 17 September 2025. The associated identifier is https://doi.org/10.4171/jems/1760; the publisher records online publication on 11 December 2025. Its subscription journal text was not compared with this preprint, and equivalence is not presumed. Page references below are one-based PDF pages, which agree with the printed numbering.

The global assumptions include \(n>2s\), \(0<s<1\), symmetry and ellipticity \[\lambda |x-y|^{-n-2s}\le K(x,y)=K(y,x)\le\Lambda|x-y|^{-n-2s},
 \qquad Lu(x)=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\mathbb R^n}(u(x)-u(y))K(x,y)\,dy.\] The coefficient condition (1.3) is Hölder continuity under simultaneous translation of \(x,y\), with exponent \(0<\sigma<s\), on the stated set \(A\). It is not angular smoothness or homogeneity. Theorem 1.6 assumes a \(C^{1,\alpha}\) domain, \(0<\alpha<s\), \(d_\Omega^{s-p}f\in L^q\), \(0<p\le s\), and \(q>n/p\). Its Hopf assertion additionally assumes nonnegative global \(u\), nonnegative bounded \(f\). Theorem 1.8 requires every completely frozen kernel to be homogeneous. Corollary 1.2 assumes \(s>1/2\).

The proof passes through weighted source estimates, freezing comparisons, a first Morrey iteration, locally frozen barriers, the relative Campanato estimate in Lemma 6.6, and Lemma 6.7. Theorem 6.8 then yields the normalized boundary quotient used in Theorem 6.1 and hence Theorem 1.6. The Hopf argument and the Green-function construction follow separately, with Section 9 invoking those boundary estimates. These dependencies matter: a defect in Lemma 6.6’s proof is a defect in a central derivation even without disproving its eventual conclusion.

# Major findings

Problem 1 (Subtracting a barrier changes the equation).  The first paragraph of the proof of Lemma 6.6, page 32, applies Proposition 6.2(iii) to \(v-c_0\psi\) as a homogeneous \(L\)-solution. The barrier \(\psi\) was defined for a different operator.

Equation reference: eq:forcing. Exact hypotheses and counterevidence. On pages 25–26, \(L\) is translation invariant, whereas \(\widetilde L\) need only coincide with \(L\) for jumps shorter than \(1/2\). Equation (6.1) gives \(\widetilde L\psi=0\) in \(D\); equation (6.2) explicitly retains \[L\psi=(L-\widetilde L)\psi .\] Proposition 6.2(iii), however, assumes \(Lv=0\). Consequently \[
 L(v-c_0\psi)=-c_0(L-\widetilde L)\psi ,\] which need not vanish.

Equation reference: eq:perturb. Here is a smooth admissible family making it nonzero. Work in dimension two, for example with \(s=1/2\), and a smooth domain with a boundary point at the origin. Choose \(D\) as in (6.1), with \(\Omega_1\subset D\subset\Omega_2\). Put \(k_0(h)=c|h|^{-n-2s}\). Let \(a,b\) be nonnegative smooth compactly supported functions bounded by one, with \(a\) supported inside \(D\), \(b\) supported outside \(B_3\), and the supports more than \(1/2\) apart. For \(0<\delta<1\), set \[
 \widetilde K(x,y)=k_0(x-y)
 \bigl[1+\delta(a(x)b(y)+a(y)b(x))\bigr].\] This kernel is symmetric, uniformly elliptic, satisfies simultaneous-translation Hölder bounds, and equals \(k_0\) for short jumps. Choose the allowed exterior datum \(g\), \(0\le g\le1\), to equal one on \(\operatorname{supp}b\). The variational Dirichlet solutions \(\psi\) for \(\widetilde L\) and \(v\) for \(L_0\), both with exterior datum \(g\), exist, are positive in \(D\), and satisfy \(0<\psi<1\). The strict upper inequality follows from the positive kernel and the exterior region where \(g=0\).

Equation reference: eq:nonzero. For \(x\in D\) with \(a(x)>0\), the exact operator identity is \[
 L_0\psi(x)=2\delta a(x)(1-\psi(x))
             \int_{\mathbb R^n}b(y)k_0(x-y)\,dy>0 .\] Take a small ball allowed in Lemma 6.6 meeting this support. The actual choice \(c_0=\mathop{\mathrlap{\mkern 3mu-}\int}\nolimits_{\Omega_R}(v/\psi)\) is positive, so [eq:forcing] (#eq:forcing) is nonzero even for the coefficient selected in the proof, not merely for an irrelevant arbitrary constant. One can arrange \(a(x_0)=b(x_0)=0\) at the freezing point while \(a\) is positive elsewhere in that ball. Then (1.9) freezes \(\widetilde K\) to \(k_0\) exactly, and (6.12) gives back \(\widetilde K\). Thus this obstruction also occurs in the actual locally frozen setup of Lemma 6.7.

Impact and repair boundary. This is a false application of a homogeneous estimate, not just an omitted citation. Lemma 6.7 invokes Lemma 6.6 on page 33, and Theorem 6.8 invokes the resulting iteration on page 36. The issue is therefore Major for the proof of the main regularity result and its dependent applications. The example does not disprove the full inequality of Lemma 6.6 or the final \(C^s\) conclusion. A repair must prove an inhomogeneous relative-barrier estimate with the term in [eq:forcing] (#eq:forcing), and show that its scale contribution is absorbed in the iteration. Alternatively, barriers and replacements could be made harmonic for the same operator, but the locally frozen closeness argument would then have to be reconsidered. Neither full replacement proof is asserted here.

Problem 2 (The printed source-space extension admits unbounded solutions).  Remark 3.2, pages 14–15, proposes replacing the weighted \(L^q\) source hypothesis in the main theorems by a space \(X\). The displayed support exponent has the wrong sign for that purpose.

Write \(a=1/2+p/n-1/q>0\). The displayed norm multiplies \(\int fw/[w]_{H^s}\) by \(|\{w\ne0\}\cap B_R|^{a}\). On the bounded testing region \(\Omega_1\), every energy-dual datum has finite such norm: \[\left|\int fw\right|\le C_f[w]_{H^s},
 \qquad
 |\{w\ne0\}\cap B_R|^a
       \frac{\left|\int fw\right|}{[w]_{H^s}}
 \le |\Omega_1|^a C_f .\] The supremum over both signs of \(w\) makes the lack of an absolute value in the numerator immaterial.

For a concrete counterexample, take \(n=2\), \(s=p=1/2\), \(q=\infty\), \(\alpha=\sigma=1/4\), and \(\Omega=B_2\). Choose \(0\le\eta\le1\), \(\eta\in C_c^\infty(B_3)\), equal to one on \(B_2\), and set \[u(x)=\eta(x)|x|^{-1/4},\qquad f=(-\Delta)^{1/2}u
       \quad\hbox{in }B_1 .\] Use the constant kernel normalized so that (1.1) equals this fractional Laplacian. All coefficient and domain assumptions hold. The local zero condition in \(B_1\setminus\Omega\) is vacuous, which is allowed by the localized Theorem 1.6. The singularity has finite energy: its dyadic \(H^s\) contributions are bounded by a convergent geometric series with scale exponent \[n-2(1/4)-2s=1/2>0 .\] Away from zero the cutoff is smooth; the same splitting controls the interactions between separated annuli. Thus \(u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb R^2)\), and the required weighted \(L^1\) tail is finite. The weak datum \(f\) is an energy-dual functional by Cauchy–Schwarz in the Dirichlet form, so it belongs to the printed \(X\).

There is also no sign or distributional loophole. The fractional-power identity gives, before cutoff, \[(-\Delta)^{1/2}|x|^{-1/4}
 =c_*|x|^{-5/4},\qquad
 c_*=\frac{2\Gamma(5/8)\Gamma(7/8)}
             {\Gamma(1/8)\Gamma(3/8)}>0 .\] It follows directly by multiplying the Fourier transform of the Riesz power by \(|\xi|\). Cutting off only outside \(B_2\) adds a nonnegative smooth term at points of \(B_1\). Hence \(f\) is a nonnegative locally integrable function there, and \(Lu=f\) holds in the usual weak sense. Nevertheless \(u\) is unbounded at the origin, contradicting the proposed \(C^s\) conclusion.

This is Major for the explicit extension of the main regularity statement. It is not a counterexample to the original weighted-\(L^q\) theorem: this \(f\) is not bounded. To encode (3.1), the support factor must instead have exponent \(-a\). A formulation based solely on (3.2) must specify the corresponding scale-dependent dual bound and justify each subsequent source estimate. The positive-exponent assertion cannot be retained.

Problem 3 (A lower-bound Harnack chain leaves the harmonic domain).  In Case 2 of the lower-bound proof of Theorem 1.1, pages 52–53, the auxiliary function is prescribed to vanish at some points at which the proof claims it is bounded below by a positive constant.

The proof sets \(R=2|x-y|\) and defines \(u\) by \[Lu=0\text{ in }\Omega_{R/4}(x_0),\quad
 u=1\text{ on }B_{\kappa R}(z),\quad
 u=0\text{ outside }\Omega_{R/4}(x_0)\cup B_{\kappa R}(z).\] After (9.5), the branch \(x\notin\Omega_{R/8}(x_0)\) invokes a Harnack chain to claim \(u(x)\ge c_1>0\).

Take a constant fractional kernel, \(n=2\), \(s=1/4\), \(\alpha=\sigma=1/8\), and \[\Omega=B_{10}((0,10)),\quad x_0=(0,0),\quad
 x=(0,3/4),\quad y=(0,7/4),\quad z=(0,4),\quad\kappa=1/8 .\] Then \(|x-y|=1\), \(R=2\), and \[d_\Omega(x)=3/4<1\le d_\Omega(y)=7/4,\quad
 2\le|y-z|=9/4\le4,\quad 2<|x_0-z|=4\le4.\] The ball \(B_{\kappa R}(z)=B_{1/4}(z)\) is inside \(\Omega\setminus B_R(x_0)\). All specified Case 2 geometric requirements hold. But \(x\) is outside \(\Omega_{R/4}(x_0)=\Omega\cap B_{1/2}(0)\) and outside \(B_{1/4}(z)\). Consequently the defining data give \(u(x)=0\) exactly. A Harnack chain for this \(u\) cannot reach \(x\) through a region where it is harmonic.

The issue is Major for the displayed proof of a principal lower Green-function bound; Case 3 reuses the same mechanism. It does not refute that final bound. A plausible repair is to propagate the lower bound for \(G(\cdot,y)\) itself through a chain avoiding its pole, or to change the comparison domain and radii. Such a repair must cover the bounded-domain large-scale geometry as well; it is not supplied merely by asserting a chain for the present \(u\).

# Further bounded findings

Problem 4 (First oscillations cannot have a superlinear decay exponent).  Lemma 5.5, page 23, asserts decay of the mean oscillation after subtracting only a constant with exponent \(\beta=\min\{2s-n/q,1+\sigma-\varepsilon\}\).

Take \(n=2\), \(s=3/4\), \(\sigma=1/4\), \(\varepsilon=1/8\), \(q=\infty\), \(u(x)=x_1\), \(R=1\), \(x_0=0\), and a constant fractional kernel. The affine function is locally in the required energy space and belongs to \(L^1_{2s}\), since its tail integral is proportional to \(\int_1^\infty r^{-2s}\,dr<\infty\). Its principal-value operator is zero by oddness. All hypotheses hold with \(f=0\). The source defines \((u)_{B_\rho}\) as the average of \(|u|\) (page 10). Reflection symmetry and \(|a-m|+|-a-m|\ge 2|a|\) imply, for every \(m\in\mathbb R\), \[\mathop{\mathrlap{\mkern 3mu-}\int}\nolimits_{B_\rho}|x_1-m|\,dx
 \ge \mathop{\mathrlap{\mkern 3mu-}\int}\nolimits_{B_\rho}|x_1|\,dx
 =\frac{4\rho}{3\pi}.\] Taking \(m=(u)_{B_\rho}\), we therefore obtain \[\mathop{\mathrlap{\mkern 3mu-}\int}\nolimits_{B_\rho}|u-(u)_{B_\rho}|\,dx
 \ge \frac{4\rho}{3\pi},
 \qquad \beta=\frac98.\] The right side of the proposed estimate is a fixed finite multiple of \(\rho^{9/8}\), an impossibility as \(\rho\downarrow0\). The proof’s step from a \(C^\beta\) seminorm to a pointwise difference of order \(|x-y|^\beta\) is invalid when \(\beta>1\).

This issue is Minor in its established impact. The subsequent uses for Theorem 1.7 and barrier closeness target exponents below \(s<1\), for which a suitable weaker estimate can suffice. For noninteger regularity exponents above one, cap the first-oscillation exponent at one; higher Campanato exponents require affine approximants. The critical integer endpoint also requires the restriction described in Problem 5. No additional failure of a principal conclusion is inferred from this example.

Problem 5 (The critical interior endpoint is not \(C^1\)).  Proposition 3.5, page 16, includes the integer endpoint \(2s-n/q=1\) without an exception.

Take \(n=2\), \(s=3/4\), \(q=4\), \(\sigma=1/4\), and \(\varepsilon=1/8\). With polar coordinates \(y=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\), put \[f(y)=(1+\cos\theta)\,r^{-1/2}
              [\log(e/r)]^{-1/2}\,\mathbf1_{\{r<1/4\}},
 \qquad u=I_{3/2}f .\] The Riesz-potential constant is positive and chosen so that \((-\Delta)^{3/4}u=f\). This \(f\) is nonnegative and lies in \(L^4\), because \[\int_0^{1/4}\frac{dr}{r[\log(e/r)]^2}
       =\frac1{1+\log4}<\infty .\] It is also compactly supported and in \(L^1\cap L^2\). The Fourier energy \(\int|\xi|^{-3/2}|\widehat f(\xi)|^2\,d\xi\) is finite, both at zero (\(\int_0^1r^{-1/2}\,dr<\infty\)) and at infinity by Plancherel. Thus \(u\) has finite homogeneous \(H^{3/4}\) energy, finite local \(L^2\), and the required nonlocal tail. These are sufficient for the weak-solution class on an interior ball; global \(L^2\) is not claimed.

To see the failure of Lipschitz continuity, split \(f\) into its radial and odd angular parts. For the odd part, pair \(y\) and \(-y\) on \(y_1>0\). The quotient of \[|te_1-y|^{-1/2}-|te_1+y|^{-1/2}\] by \(t>0\) is nonnegative. Its pointwise limit, multiplied by the odd datum and integrated angularly, is a positive multiple of \[\int_0^{1/4}\frac{dr}{r\sqrt{\log(e/r)}}=\infty .\] Fatou’s lemma makes the odd-part difference quotient tend to \(+\infty\). The radial part contributes a bounded quotient: split at \(r=2t\); the inner contribution is \(O(t)\) by scaling, and angular cancellation of the first derivative in the outer part leaves \(O(t^2\int_{2t}^{1/4}r^{-2}\,dr)=O(t)\). Therefore \((u(te_1)-u(0))/t\to+\infty\). The claimed exponent is exactly one, so both the Lipschitz and the continuously differentiable interpretations of \(C^1\) fail.

This is a Minor auxiliary endpoint issue in the established dependency analysis. The principal boundary targets are below one. The gradient corollary uses a homogeneous equation with \(s>1/2\), avoiding this particular endpoint. Exclude the integer endpoint, weaken the exponent, or supply a correctly stated endpoint space and estimate.

Problem 6 (The advertised kernel class need not freeze homogeneously).  The example immediately after Theorem 1.8, page 9, does not satisfy that theorem’s extra homogeneity assumption in general.

For \(0<\delta<1\), take the even angular density \(a_x(\theta)=2+\delta\cos x_1\), independent of \(\theta\), and the printed kernel \[K(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-2s}
          \bigl(a_x((x-y)/|x-y|)+a_y((x-y)/|x-y|)\bigr).\] It is symmetric, elliptic, and smooth under simultaneous translations. But (1.9) at the origin gives \[|h|^{n+2s}K_0(h)=4+\delta(1+\cos h_1),\] which varies along the ray \(h=re_1\). Hence \(K_0\) is not homogeneous. This Minor issue invalidates the claimed example, not Theorem 1.8 under its actual hypothesis. An \(x\)-independent even angular density is a valid restricted example.

Problem 7 (The supersolution definition reverses only one sign).  Definition 2.1, page 11, says to replace \(u\) by \(-u\) in (2.2), without also replacing \(f\) by \(-f\).

For \(f=1\) and \(u=0\), both printed inequalities hold for all nonnegative tests, so the resulting literal definition calls \(u\) a solution, although \(Lu=0\ne1\). The correction is \(\mathcal E^K(u,\varphi)\ge\langle f,\varphi\rangle\), equivalently reversing both \(u\) and \(f\). This is Minor: the proofs otherwise use the intended equation. The counterexamples in Problems 2 and 5 have nonnegative \(f\) and satisfy exact weak equality, so they also satisfy both printed inequalities; they do not depend on exploiting this sign error.

Problem 8 (The Poisson formula misses the operator’s factor two).  Equation (9.6), page 53, is inconsistent with (1.1) and the inverse Green-function normalization in Definition 9.1, page 50.

Let \(g\) be a smooth exterior datum supported away from \(\overline\Omega\), and let \(u\) be its \(L\)-harmonic extension. The zero exterior extension \(v=u\mathbf1_\Omega\) satisfies \[Lv(x)=2\int_{\Omega^c}g(z)K(x,z)\,dz .\] Applying the inverse Green operator and interchanging the nonnegative integrals yields \[P(x,z)=2\int_\Omega G(x,y)K(y,z)\,dy .\] The printed formula lacks this factor. Equivalently, exterior data \(g=1\) have harmonic extension \(u=1\); the printed kernel has total mass \(1/2\) instead of one. This follows by approximation with bounded exterior data supported away from the boundary, or directly from \(L\mathbf1_\Omega=2\int_{\Omega^c}K(x,z)\,dz\) in \(\Omega\). The exact representation needs correction, while the two-sided estimates up to unspecified multiplicative constants can absorb the factor. The established impact is Minor.
