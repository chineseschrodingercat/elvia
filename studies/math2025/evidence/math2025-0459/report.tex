Abstract: The isotropy-group reduction and the principal summability and telescoper criteria withstand the checks described here. A fully specified input nevertheless exposes an incorrect negative decision in the literal pseudocode of Algorithm 6.13: the operator used in its annihilator test has the opposite last spatial shift from the generator selected immediately before it. The input has the explicit telescoper \(S_t-1\). This is a Major finding about the stated constructive decision algorithm, with a verified local correction. It does not refute Theorem 6.7 or establish a failure of the released Maple code. Exact checks also validate several nontrivial rational certificates. Scientific adjudication remains pending.\par
\section*{Source, scope and conventions}
The source is S. Chen, L. Du and H. Fang, Symbolic Summation of Multivariate Rational Functions, arXiv:2204.06968v3, submitted 17 March 2025; its title page is dated 18 March. The acquired PDF has 55 pages; physical and printed page numbers coincide. The assigned DOI is https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-025-09710-0. The authors’ university publication record lists online publication on 24 April 2025 and volume 26(3) (2026), pp. 1635–1697. The acquired preprint is the mathematical object reviewed here; the 63-page journal text was not compared.\par
The base field has characteristic zero. The shifts are \(\sigma_{x_i}h(\mathbf x)=h(\mathbf x+\mathbf e_i)\) and \(\Delta_{x_i}=\sigma_{x_i}-1\). Summability means an identity in a rational-function field, \(f=\sum_i\Delta_{x_i}g_i\). A telescoper is a nonzero \(L\in K(t)\langle S_t\rangle\) for which \(L(f)\) is spatially summable. No analytic convergence, infinite-sum boundary condition or minimal-order assertion is included in this definition. Passive variables remain in the coefficient field during recursive reduction.\par
\section*{Problem 1: a wrong decision in the printed algorithm}
\subsection*{Location and dependency}
Algorithm 6.13, p. 42, lines 11–14, chooses \[\tau_0=\sigma_t^{k_0}\sigma_{x_1}^{-k_1}
                   \cdots\sigma_{x_n}^{-k_n},\qquad k_0>0,\] but its next line prints the operator with last factor \(S_{x_n}^{+k_n}\). The annihilator test in line 14 then uses this differently directed operator. Theorem 6.7, p. 38, uses the consistent negative sign. The mismatch affects the yes/no decision, not only the display of a certificate.\par
\subsection*{An input satisfying the algorithm’s hypotheses}
Work over \(\mathbb Q(t,x,y,z)\), with all three spatial variables summed, and put \[d=(t-3y+x)^2(t+y)(t+z)+1,\qquad
 a=\frac1{t+z},\qquad f=\frac{a}{d}.\] This is a proper simple fraction in \(x\), with \(\deg_x a=0<2=\deg_x d\). For completeness, set \(u=t-3y+x,\ v=t+y,\ w=t+z\). Together with \(t\), these are invertible rational linear coordinates. The polynomial \(u^2vw+1\) is primitive and linear in \(v\) over \(\mathbb Q[t,u,w]\), with coprime leading and constant coefficients, hence is irreducible. The rational input has no numerator–denominator cancellation.\par
A constant translation preserving \(d\) must preserve \(u,v,w\). Indeed its directional derivative is \(2\alpha uvw+\beta u^2w+\gamma u^2v\), whose coefficients force \(\alpha=\beta=\gamma=0\); characteristic zero makes invariance under one nonzero integer step equivalent to invariance along that polynomial translation direction. In original coordinates the integer solutions are \[(k_t,k_x,k_y,k_z)=q(1,-4,-1,-1),\qquad q\in\mathbb Z.\] Thus \(G_{t,d}=\langle\tau_0\rangle\), with \(\tau_0=\sigma_t\sigma_x^{-4}\sigma_y^{-1}\sigma_z^{-1}\); \(G_d=\{1\}\), and the positive time period is \(k_0=1\). The input reaches the trivial-spatial-stabilizer branch. In particular it is not already spatially summable, since its nonzero reduced numerator and trivial stabilizer satisfy the non-summability case of Theorem 5.9.\par
\subsection*{The two decisions and an explicit certificate}
The correct generator fixes both \(d\) and \(a\). Consequently \(T_0-1\) annihilates \(a\), and \(L=S_t-1\) is a telescoper. This conclusion does not depend solely on the paper’s criterion: the following rational certificates prove it: \[U=\sum_{\ell=0}^{3}\sigma_x^\ell\sigma_y\sigma_z(f),
 \qquad V=\sigma_z(f),\qquad W=f.\] Directly, \[\sigma_t(f)=\sigma_x^4\sigma_y\sigma_z(f),\qquad
 (S_t-1)f=\Delta_xU+\Delta_yV+\Delta_zW.\] These identities also specify the certificates without expanding large denominators.\par
The printed line 12 instead gives \(T'_0=S_tS_x^{-4}S_y^{-1}S_z\). Its successive actions on the numerator are \[(T'_0)^j(a)=\frac1{t+z+2j}\quad(j\ge0).\] They are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q(t)\): in any finite relation their distinct simple poles in \(z\) force every coefficient to vanish. Hence no nonzero operator in \(\mathbb Q(t)\langle T'_0\rangle\) annihilates \(a\), and the literal line-14 test returns false. This contradicts the explicit positive certificate above. No sampled numerical behavior or unverified conjecture is used in this argument.\par
\subsection*{Alternative reading, correction and calibration}
Reading line 12 as the operator representation of line 11 removes the discrepancy; this is strongly supported by Theorem 6.7 and the worked examples. The exact correction is \[T_{i,0}=S_t^{k_{i,0}}
           S_{x_1}^{-k_{i,1}}\cdots S_{x_n}^{-k_{i,n}}.\] It restores the required invariance and the correct positive answer for the displayed input. The reduction in (6.9)–(6.10) then uses the same generator throughout.\par
The released `RationalWZ.mm` was inspected statically: `IsTelescoperable` passes the stored isotropy vector to `TransitionMatrix2`; its annihilator test is applied after that coordinate change. `Transformation2` also uses the whole spatial vector coherently. These inspected routines do not reproduce the isolated last-coordinate sign change in the printed pseudocode. No author program was run, and no general implementation certificate is claimed.\par
The supported severity is Major for the literal Algorithm 6.13 correctness claim: a principal constructive decision procedure demonstrably returns the wrong answer. The severity rests on this dependency and explicit failure, rather than the typographical appearance of the cause or the difficulty of repair. The intended corrected algorithm and the mathematical existence theorem are not refuted. If the algorithm is defined by importing Theorem 6.7’s generator convention instead of line 12 literally, this particular failure disappears.\par
\section*{Checks of the mathematical reductions}
For shift equivalence, the coefficient/Taylor linearization argument in §3 was checked, including the role of an admissible cover in freezing higher homogeneous parts. In Lemma 3.16’s induction, one should use the actual earlier cover block containing \(c_\beta\); the general admissibility definition does not require that block to have the displayed index \(\ell-k+1\). Choosing its actual index \(j<\ell\) supplies the induction hypothesis and the same conclusion. For Theorem 3.20, the total-degree bound directly ensures coverage; the asserted containment of translated support in the union of the two original supports is unnecessary. These local proof clarifications were supplied explicitly; no failure of the SET criterion was established. Example 3.4 has the unique shift \((-1,2)\), verified by exact coefficient equations.\par
For summability, irreducible proper partial fractions give the direct orbital decomposition, and each component is shift-invariant. The positive and negative shift versions of (5.3) telescope to the same reduced fraction. For Theorem 5.9, the rank-zero induction excludes cancellation between endpoint orbits. In the rank-one case, the least positive period separates residue classes, and the chain of numerator congruences gives \(a\equiv\Delta_{\tau_r}\lambda_0\pmod W\). The space \(W\) is stable under the commuting shifts. Dividing by the invariant denominator gives sufficiency. The rational linear coordinate change in Proposition 5.12 need not be unimodular over the integers; invertibility over \(\mathbb Q\) suffices for the rational-function field. The recursive number of active variables strictly decreases.\par
For telescopers, the rank-zero proof in Lemma 6.4 uses the nonzero lowest time coefficient and successive distinct spatial orbits. Its coefficient range must include all \(I+\rho+1\) terms of the displayed expansion. In the rank-one case, exponent separation reduces to the residue-class operator matrix in Lemma 6.6. The invoked Ore elimination result from reference [22] was treated as an external dependency; its complete independent proof was not undertaken. Theorem 6.7’s reduction was checked with a consistent generator, and Proposition 6.8’s necessity follows from an extreme denominator factor in an infinite shift orbit: its pole cannot cancel against earlier shifts with coefficients depending only on \(t\). Sufficiency is finite-dimensional linear dependence over \(K(t)\) after removing an invariant denominator. Proposition 6.12’s coordinate map preserves \(K(t)\), including the inverse time rescaling \(t/k_0\).\par
\section*{Exact certificates and local corrections}
All three component identities in Example 5.15 were independently verified by exact rational simplification. The final combined certificate on p. 34 sums only \(i=1,2\), omitting the third component. Its omitted term is \[f_3-r_3=
 \frac{y+z/(y^2+z-1)-(1+z)/(y^2+z)}
      {(x+2y+z)^2},\] which is \(27/896\) at \((x,y,z)=(1,2,3)\), away from its poles. Replacing each upper limit by 3 restores the identity. Because the preceding individual identities are correct and the correction is simply their stated addition, this worked-example transcription is recorded without an additional scientific Problem or severity count.\par
The certificates for the two remainders in Example 6.14(3) were checked exactly. With \(L_1=S_t^3-t/(t+3)\) and \(L_2=S_t^3-1\), noncommutative multiplication verifies \[\left(S_t^3-\frac{t+3}{t+6}\right)L_1
 =\left(S_t^3-\frac{t}{t+6}\right)L_2
 =S_t^6-\frac{2(t+3)}{t+6}S_t^3+\frac{t}{t+6}.\] Coefficients were shifted during multiplication; ordinary commutative polynomial multiplication would not suffice.\par
For Example 6.15, write \[h_t(q)=\frac{t-2q}{(q+t+1)(2t-q+1)},\qquad
 Q_t(q)=\frac{8t^2-2tq-q^2+19t-2q+11}
 {(q+t+1)(2t-q+2)(2t-q+3)}.\] Exact simplification gives \(\Delta_q Q_t=h_{t+1}-h_t\). The displayed three-variable certificates are the resulting product telescoping identity. The first face term \(g_1\) vanishes exactly. Independently, \(h_t(t-q)=-h_t(q)\), so the finite cube sum is zero for every nonnegative integer \(t\); all its denominators are nonzero on that domain. Exact values for \(t=0,\ldots,6\) also agree. No conclusion about arbitrary infinite sums is inferred.\par
