# Assumptions, quantifiers and endpoint scope

Write \(v=\underline u\) for the second null coordinate. The spacetime has \(M=(-1,1)\times(-1,0)\times\mathbb S^2\), a \(C^2\) Lorentzian metric in the double-null gauge (2.1), and the stipulated continuous nondegenerate extension to \(v=0\). Proposition 3.6 bounds the indicated coefficients \(\underline\omega,\eta,\underline\eta,\underline\chi\), while the supremum of \(|\chi|\) is integrable in \(v\). Condition (3.15) likewise gives an \(L^1_v\) bound on the supremum of angular derivatives of the shift. These hypotheses do not say that all connection coefficients stay bounded.

Equation reference: eq:blowup. The curvature hypothesis is local at every boundary point and every open neighbourhood \(W\). Inside \(W\), it requires a boundary point \(q\), a compact neighbourhood \(\overline V\) with the stated manifold-with-corners cutoffs, continuous fields \(X_1,\ldots,X_4\), and \(\epsilon_0>0\), such that every continuous quadruple within \(\epsilon_0\) satisfies \[
 \left|\int_{\overline V\cap\{v<v_k\}}
 R(\widehat X_1,\widehat X_2,\widehat X_3,\widehat X_4)\,\operatorname{vol}\right|
 \longrightarrow\infty\] along a sequence \(v_k\to0\). The sequence may depend on the quadruple. The absolute value encloses the signed integral.

The conclusion excludes a locally Lipschitz extension admitting a limit endpoint of an affinely parameterized, future-directed and future-inextendible timelike geodesic with \(v\to0\) and limiting \(u<1\). It does not by itself exclude every extension through other regions or establish global strong cosmic censorship. Remark 3.20 makes this distinction explicitly (PDF p. 15).

# Verification of the proof mechanism

## Energy estimate and boundary flow

For \(E=2\Omega^2\dot v+2\dot u\), timelikeness controls the angular frame velocities. Equations (3.10)–(3.13) give \[E'\le C(E^2+E\dot v\,|\chi|).\] Dividing by \(E>0\) gives \[(\log E)'\le C E+C\dot v\,\sup_{u,\theta}|\chi(u,v,\theta)|.\] The integral of \(E\) is bounded by finite changes in the null coordinates, using the bounds on \(\Omega^2\). Changing variables from affine parameter to \(v\) in the other term invokes exactly the \(L^1_v\) hypothesis. Thus the bounded-energy conclusion uses the stated integrability with the correct coefficient (PDF pp. 12–13), not an uncontrolled global Riccati estimate.

In Remark 3.22 (PDF pp. 15–16), the difference of two coordinate flow curves satisfies the underlying ODE estimate \[|(|\delta|)'|\le L(v)|\delta|,\quad L\in L^1,\qquad
 |\delta(v)|\ge|\delta(v_0)|\exp\!\left(-\int_{v_0}^{v}L(s)\,ds\right).\] Distinct curves cannot acquire the same endpoint. The printed one-sided inequality is an abbreviated presentation of this ODE argument, not an established defect in injectivity.

## Connecting curves and identification of extensions

The connectors in (3.23) deliberately move backward and forward in \(v\), with slopes of absolute value one (PDF pp. 17–18). The integrable coefficient is therefore integrated over a shrinking \(v\)-interval. A connector constrained to a fixed \(v\)-slice would not justify this bound; the paper does not use such a connector.

In Step 2 (PDF pp. 19–23), uniformly integrable connection matrices along the space-filling family and bounded parallel frames give \[\sup_z|d(s_2,z)-d(s_1,z)|
 \le C\int_{s_1}^{s_2}\sup_z|A(s,z)|\,ds .\] This uniform Cauchy estimate supports continuous extension of the frames. Their inner products and the nondegenerate limiting metric preserve invertibility. The subsequent \(C^1\) identification invokes Proposition 5.11 of the paper’s reference [34]; Step 1 invokes Proposition 5.1 there. These external results are retained as proof dependencies. The parameter shifts in (3.30)–(3.31) do not establish a substantive defect after consistent translation of the affine parameters.

## Smoothing and the signed Stokes estimate

Step 3 (PDF pp. 23–25) pushes the continuous \(X_i\) through the \(C^1\) identification, smooths them on a compact neighbourhood in the hypothetical extension, and pulls them back. This produces one fixed admissible quadruple. The universal quadruple quantifier in [eq:blowup] (#eq:blowup) supplies its diverging sequence; a sequence uniform over all quadruples is unnecessary.

The fixed smooth fields and their derivatives are bounded on the compact set. The locally Lipschitz extension metric bounds its Christoffel symbols in the smooth interior, so the lower-order terms in (3.36) are bounded. Stokes’ theorem is applied to compact interior cutoffs. The fixed side boundary has finite piecewise \(C^1\) measure, and the moving face pulls back to the density \[\sqrt{-\det\widetilde g}\,|\widehat X_i^v|\,du\,d\theta^1\,d\theta^2.\] Its integrand and coordinate domain are uniformly bounded. This contradicts [eq:blowup] (#eq:blowup). The estimate bounds the absolute value of the signed curvature integral; it is not an \(L^1\) bound on absolute curvature.

# Examples and the volume factor

For Example 4.1, \[g=-2(du\otimes dv+dv\otimes du)+r(v)^2g_{\mathbb S^2},\qquad
 |\operatorname{vol}|=2r^2\sin\theta\,du\,dv\,d\theta\,d\phi.\] The convention in (4.2)–(4.4) gives \(R(\partial_v,\partial_\theta,\partial_\theta,\partial_v)=rr''\) and \(|\chi|=\sqrt2|r'|/r\).

In Example 4.5 (PDF pp. 27–28), set \(L=1-\log|v|\) and \(r=1+L^{-(p-1)}\), \(p>1\). Differentiation gives \[r'=\frac{p-1}{vL^p},\qquad
 r''=\frac{p-1}{v^2}(-L^{-p}+pL^{-p-1}).\] For \(L\ge2p\), \(r''\) is negative and comparable to \(-1/(v^2L^p)\). The first derivative is integrable, whereas the negative second-derivative integral diverges. Positive volume factors on the compact angular patch are bounded above and below; the leading sign survives sufficiently small allowed perturbations. This is consistent with the theorem.

Example 4.7 (PDF pp. 28–29) takes \(r''=\sin(1/v)/v\). Oscillatory integration bounds \(r'\), permitting a Lipschitz extension, although \(\int |r''|\,dv=\int^\infty|\sin x|\,dx/x\) diverges. The actual signed-integral hypothesis is stronger and is not contradicted.

Equation reference: eq:ibp. Example 4.8 (PDF p. 29) has \(r'=-\cos(-1/v)\), \(r\ge1\), and \(r''=\sin(-1/v)/v^2\). The fields in (4.9) give \(R=r''(1-v\sin(1/v))\). The volume factor must be retained. The first relevant term is bounded: \[
 \int r^2r''\,dv=r^2r'-2\int r(r')^2\,dv .\] Here \(r,r'\) are bounded on a finite interval. With \(x=-1/v\), the other term is, up to a fixed positive angular factor, \[-\int^\infty r(-1/x)^2\frac{\sin^2x}{x}\,dx\longrightarrow-\infty .\] Thus the divergence survives the volume weight. Replacing the first and fourth fields near the boundary by \(\partial_v\), with a cutoff away from the boundary, is an arbitrarily small continuous perturbation and leaves only the bounded expression [eq:ibp] (#eq:ibp). The example correctly demonstrates why one unperturbed quadruple is insufficient. This is a consistency clarification, not a defect or a proposed repair.
