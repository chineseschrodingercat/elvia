Abstract: This report examines the acquired 73-page arXiv version 2108.11832v2. Explicit examples disprove a normal-cone estimate in the proof of Theorem 3.11, the \(C^1\) form of Theorem 3.9(1), and a subgradient-selection assertion in the proof of Theorem 3.25. A replacement argument for Theorem 3.11 is supplied. For Theorem 6.2, uniform marginal noise is distinguished from the conditional uniformity used in its proof: a measurable generalized iteration satisfying the displayed marginal assumptions converges almost surely to strict saddles. A separate example identifies the missing regularity premise in its vector-field construction. An invariant-interval example also identifies a localization premise missing from the standalone nonconvergence theorem. These findings do not establish failure of the intended algorithms with fresh independent noise and sufficiently smooth active restrictions. Assessment: Major, with the precise boundaries below. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Conventions and operative hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:strong-a. The source uses limiting normal cones \(N_X(x)\), which need not be linear spaces. Its directed gap is \[\Delta(U,V)=\sup_{\substack{u\in U\\\left\|u\right\|=1}}\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(u,V).\] Strong \((a)\) regularity requires, uniformly for all nearby \(x\in X\) and \(y\in Y\), \[
 \Delta(N_X(x),N_Y(y))\le C\left\|x-y\right\|.\] The projected condition restricts \(y\) to a specified retraction \(\pi(x)\). A retraction in Definition 3.7 must take values in the target set and satisfy \(\left\|z-\pi(z)\right\|\le K\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(z,Y)\) locally. Function regularity is regularity of \(\mathop{\mathrm{epi}}f\) along \(\mathop{\mathrm{gph}}(f|_{\mathcal M})\) (Definitions 3.2–3.3, p. 12). For a singleton target its normal space is the whole ambient space, so strong \((a)\) is automatic. This elementary case is permitted by the stated theorems.

For the stochastic results, the standing Assumption E (p. 38) requires a measurable generalized map, \(\alpha_k\asymp k^{-\gamma}\), martingale-difference noise relative to \(\mathcal F_k=\sigma(x_j:j\le k;\nu_j:j<k)\), a conditional fourth-moment bound locally bounded in \(x_k\), and iterates in the domain. Assumption A (p. 33) requires local boundedness, a tangential approximation estimate, and proximal aiming, uniformly over every \(\alpha>0\) and every noise vector. It asks for \(f|_\mathcal M\) to be \(C^2\). Theorem 6.2 (p. 42) raises the smoothness of \(\mathcal M\) to \(C^4\), uses \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), and requires a negative eigenvalue of \(\nabla_\mathcal M^2 f\) at each point of the target set.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (One-sided normals invalidate equation (3.29)).  The proof of Theorem 3.11, p. 20, contains a false estimate central to its KL derivation. Severity: Major, confined to the submitted proof.

## Claim and exact hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:false-kl. Theorem 3.11 assumes definable sets \(X,Y\) in a Euclidean space, a point \(\bar x\in Y\), a locally closed \(X\), and a \(C^2\) manifold \(Y\) near \(\bar x\). It claims strong \((a)\Rightarrow(b_=)\). The proof defines \[g(x,v)=|\left\langle v,x-P_Y(x)\right\rangle|+\delta_{\mathop{\mathrm{cl}}X}(x)\] and, for unit \(v\in N_X(x)\), asserts \[
 \mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(0,\partial g(x,v))
 \le \left\|DP_Y(x)v\right\|+\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,Y).\] This is the source’s equation (3.29).

## Counterexample and hypothesis verification

Equation reference: eq:kl-counter. Take \(X=\{(u,w):w\ge u^2\}\), \(Y=\{(0,0)\}\), and \(\bar x=0\). Both sets are semialgebraic; \(X\) is closed and convex, and \(Y\) is a \(C^\infty\) manifold. Since \(N_Y(0)=\mathbb R^2\), strong \((a)\) holds with zero gap. For any \(t>0\) set \[x_t=(t,t^2),\qquad
 v_t=\frac{(2t,-1)}{\sqrt{1+4t^2}}.\] The outward normal cone is \(\mathbb R_+v_t\), and \(\left\langle v_t,x_t\right\rangle=t^2/\sqrt{1+4t^2}>0\). Thus the absolute value is on its positive smooth branch; no choice of the derivative at zero is involved. As \(P_Y=0\), \[\partial g(x_t,v_t)
 =\{((1+\lambda)v_t,x_t):\lambda\ge0\}.\] Consequently, \[
 \mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(0,\partial g(x_t,v_t))=\sqrt{1+t^2+t^4}
 >\sqrt{t^2+t^4}
 =\left\|DP_Y(x_t)v_t\right\|+\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x_t,Y).\] The difference of the squared sides is exactly \(1\), for every \(t>0\). Their ratio diverges as \(t\downarrow0\), so inserting an unspecified fixed multiplicative constant does not repair the bound.

The invalid cancellation would require \(-v_t\in N_X(x_t)\). A convex normal cone is a cone, not generally a subspace. The omission of the absolute-value sign in the preceding derivative formula is a further reason to handle branches carefully, but it is not the basis of this counterexample.

## Counterevidence, dependency, and repair

This example does not disprove Theorem 3.11. Indeed, \[\frac{|\left\langle v_t,x_t\right\rangle|}{\left\|x_t\right\|}
 =\frac{t}{\sqrt{1+4t^2}\sqrt{1+t^2}}\longrightarrow0.\] The zero normals in the interior and the multiples of these boundary normals also satisfy \((b_=)\) at the singleton.

The affected argument is the paper’s stated derivation of the implication in its regularity diagram (3.20). The generic function stratification Theorem 3.29 (p. 31) asserts both strong \((a)\) and \((b_=)\); its displayed proof explicitly establishes the former, with this implication supplying the latter for locally closed epigraphs. This is a substantive proof dependency, not merely a misplaced sign without mathematical consequences. Appendix 6 (#app:kl-repair) gives a checked replacement using the signed function \(-\left\langle v,x-P_Y(x)\right\rangle+\delta_{\mathop{\mathrm{cl}}X}(x)\) and the nonsmooth, two-sided KL inequality. It preserves the theorem’s stated hypotheses. No author adoption of that replacement is established.

Problem 2 (The \(C^1\) form of Theorem 3.9(1) is false).  Theorem 3.9(1), p. 17, and estimate (3.22), p. 18, require more than the stated \(C^1\) smoothness of the target manifold. Severity: Minor, a local theorem-range defect.

## Claim, example, and all hypotheses

The theorem allows arbitrary sets \(X,Y\), a point \(\bar x\in Y\), a \(C^1\) retraction onto \(Y\), and a target \(Y\) that is only a \(C^1\) manifold. It asserts equivalence of strong \((a)\) and strong \((a^\pi)\). Let \[X=Y=\{(u,|u|^{3/2}):u\in\mathbb R\},\quad \bar x=0,\quad
 \pi(u,w)=(u,|u|^{3/2}).\] This is a \(C^1\) embedded manifold and a \(C^1\) retraction. To check the quantitative retraction requirement, let \(L\) bound the Lipschitz constant of \(q(u)=|u|^{3/2}\) on a small interval. A nearest graph point to a sufficiently small \(z=(u,w)\) lies in that interval, since the graph contains the origin. For every such candidate \((a,q(a))\), \[|w-q(u)|\le |w-q(a)|+L|u-a|
 \le\sqrt{1+L^2}\left\|z-(a,q(a))\right\|.\] Taking the infimum proves the required distance bound.

Equation reference: eq:c1-ratio. For \(x\in X\), \(\pi(x)=x\), so the projected normal gap is zero. For \(x_t=(t,t^{3/2})\) and \(y=0\), however, the unit normal is \[n_t=\frac{(-3\sqrt t/2,1)}{\sqrt{1+9t/4}},\qquad t>0.\] Therefore \[
 \frac{\Delta(N_X(x_t),N_Y(0))}{\left\|x_t\right\|}
 =\frac{3}{2\sqrt t\sqrt{1+9t/4}\sqrt{1+t}}
 \longrightarrow+\infty .\] No uniform constant in [eq:strong-a] (#eq:strong-a) exists.

## Alternative interpretation and bounded correction

The proof explicitly invokes linear variation of the normal spaces in (3.22); \(C^1\) only supplies continuity. Replacing the target hypothesis by \(C^2\) is sufficient: in a local \(C^2\) graph chart, the normal projector is \(C^1\) and has bounded derivative on a smaller compact neighborhood, hence is locally Lipschitz. The triangle estimate (3.21)–(3.22) then works. A suitable \(C^{1,1}\) normal-variation assumption would also suffice, but \(C^2\) is the correction directly checked here.

The main cited uses already have higher smoothness: Proposition 3.10 uses \(C^3\) (p. 19), Theorem 3.11 uses \(C^2\), and Proposition 3.21 and Theorem 3.25 use \(C^2\) or higher (pp. 27, 29). Thus this example refutes the displayed \(C^1\) theorem, but does not demonstrate failure of those downstream applications. That limited dependency impact motivates the Minor grade.

Problem 3 (Strong \((a)\) does not imply the asserted subgradient selection).  Theorem 3.25’s proof, at the top of p. 30 following its statement on p. 29, uses a false selection estimate. Severity: Major for this spectral transfer derivation; no false final spectral theorem is established.

## Claim and decisive example

Equation reference: eq:false-selection. The hypotheses are a symmetric function \(f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}\), a symmetric \(C^2\) manifold \(\mathcal M\) containing \(\bar x\), local Lipschitz continuity of \(f\), and \(C^2\) smoothness of \(f|_\mathcal M\). For \(F=f\circ\lambda\), the strong \((a)\) part of the proof asserts that, for \(Y=P_{\lambda^{-1}(\mathcal M)}(X)\) and each \(Z\in\partial F(X)\), one can find \(W\in\partial F(Y)\) with \[
 \left\|Z-W\right\|_F\le C\left\|X-Y\right\|_F\] uniformly near the base matrix.

The theorem includes \(1\times1\) real symmetric matrices. Set \(f(u)=|u|^{3/2}\), \(\mathcal M=\{0\}\), and \(\bar x=0\). Symmetry under permutations of one coordinate is automatic; the manifold is \(C^\infty\), the restriction is constant, and \(f\) is locally Lipschitz. Its epigraph is strongly \((a)\) regular along the singleton \(\mathop{\mathrm{gph}}(f|_\mathcal M)\) because the latter’s normal space is \(\mathbb R^2\). All stated hypotheses hold. Here \(F=f\) and, for \(X=t>0\), \[Y=0,\qquad Z=\tfrac32\sqrt t,\qquad \partial F(0)=\{0\}.\] The only possible selection is \(W=0\), giving \[\frac{\left\|Z-W\right\|_F}{\left\|X-Y\right\|_F}
 =\frac{3}{2\sqrt t}\longrightarrow+\infty.\] Thus the claim is false even before any higher-dimensional eigenvalue multiplicity issue arises.

## Interpretation, dependency, and repair boundary

Strong \((a)\) controls the distance to the normal space of the restriction graph. That space is generally larger than the normals corresponding to actual subgradients of \(F\) at \(Y\). The set-normal argument in Theorem 3.20, p. 26, therefore does not justify [eq:false-selection] (#eq:false-selection) with the stated membership \(W\in\partial F(Y)\).

The proof uses this estimate to establish projected strong \((a)\) and then appeals to Theorem 3.9. It is its explicit justification of the strong \((a)\) spectral-transfer assertion, a central claim of this calculus subsection. A replacement must lift the appropriate restriction-graph normals and control the resulting tangential component; merely choosing a different element of \(\partial F(0)\) cannot fix the example. A complete general spectral replacement proof is not certified in this report. Conversely, the theorem’s conclusion holds in the example because its target is a singleton. Neither this example nor the reported proof failure establishes that the spectral-transfer theorem, or the later algorithmic conclusions, are false.

Problem 4 (Uniform marginals do not supply conditional excitation).  Assumption F and Theorem 6.2 on p. 42 omit the conditional interpretation used in the proof on p. 58. Severity: Major under the literal marginal-only assumptions, with an explicit interpretation boundary.

## The distinction and the operative range

Assumption F writes \(\nu_k\sim\mathop{\mathrm{Unif}}(B_r(0))\) for each \(k\). Together with the standing martingale-difference and fourth-moment requirements, this does not imply \(\mathcal L(\nu_k\mid\mathcal F_k)=\mathop{\mathrm{Unif}}(B_r(0))\). The latter is sufficient for the conditional lower bound in Theorem 6.1(2c). The proof on p. 58 explicitly invokes independence from the iterate history, which is strong evidence for the intended fresh-noise reading. The counterexample below concerns the displayed weaker assumptions of the generalized iteration, not an assertion that the intended independently perturbed subgradient method fails.

## A construction satisfying the displayed assumptions

Equation reference: eq:circle-hessian. Let \(S=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:\left\|x\right\|=1\}\), let \(J\) be rotation by \(\pi/2\), and set \[f(x)=-(\left\|x\right\|^2-1)^2,\qquad \mathcal M=\mathbb R^2.\] This is a closed \(C^\infty\) function. For every \(p\in S\), \(\nabla f(p)=0\) and \[
 \nabla^2 f(p)=-8pp^{\mathsf T},\] so the radial eigenvalue is \(-8\) and the other eigenvalue is zero. The manifold is \(C^\infty\) at every point of \(S\).

Equation reference: eq:circle-map. Define the measurable map \(G_\alpha\) as follows. At \(x=0\), put \(G_\alpha(0,\nu)=-R(\nu)/\alpha\), where \(R(\nu)=\nu/\left\|\nu\right\|\) for \(\nu\ne0\), and \(R(0)=(1,0)\). At \(x\in S\) with \(\left\langle x,\nu\right\rangle=0\), put \[
 G_\alpha(x,\nu)=
 \frac{x-(x-\alpha\nu)/\left\|x-\alpha\nu\right\|}{\alpha}.\] At all other points put \(G_\alpha(x,\nu)=\nabla f(x)+\nu\). The branches are Borel measurable; the exceptional origin is outside a small neighborhood of every \(p\in S\).

Equation reference: eq:circle-error. For the special branch let \(s=\sqrt{1+\alpha^2\left\|\nu\right\|^2}\). Direct calculation gives, for every \(\alpha>0\) and noise vector tangent at \(x\), \[
 \left\|G_\alpha-\nu\right\|=\frac{s-1}{\alpha}
 \le \frac{\alpha\left\|\nu\right\|^2}{2},\qquad
 \left\|G_\alpha\right\|\le\left\|\nu \right\|.\] For example, the latter follows from \(\alpha^2\left\|G_\alpha\right\|^2=2(1-1/s)\le s^2-1\). Since \(\nabla f=0\) on \(S\), these inequalities verify (A1) and (A2); off this branch their approximation error is zero and \(\nabla f\) is locally bounded. Since \(P_\mathcal M\) is the identity, \(\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,\mathcal M)=0\) and (A3) is automatic, with any positive aiming constant and zero little-\(o\) term. Thus Assumption A holds uniformly in all \(\alpha,\nu\) near every \(p\in S\).

Start at the deterministic \(x_0=0\), take \(\alpha_0=1\) and a first \(\nu_0\) uniform on the unit disk. Then \(x_1=R(\nu_0)\) is uniform on the unit circle. For \(k\ge1\), let \(\alpha_k=k^{-3/4}\) and take new independent variables \(\epsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}\) with equal probabilities and \(R_k\in(0,1)\) with density \(2r\), independent of the past. Set \[\nu_k=\epsilon_kR_kJx_k .\] Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_k\), its mean is zero and \(\mathbb E[\left\|\nu_k\right\|^4\mid\mathcal F_k]=1/3<2\). One may therefore use the constant \(q(x)=2\) in (E3). The iterates remain in the domain \(\mathbb R^2\); \(\gamma=3/4\in(1/2,1)\) satisfies the exact step range. Because the updates are rotationally equivariant, \(x_k\) remains uniform on \(S\), independently of the new radius and sign. Hence each marginal \(\nu_k\) is uniform on the unit disk: its angle is uniform and its radial density is \(2r\). Assumption F as displayed holds at every step.

## Almost-sure convergence and impact

Equation reference: eq:noise-contradiction. Equation [eq:circle-map] (#eq:circle-map) keeps every subsequent iterate on \(S\). Writing an unwrapped angle \(\theta_k\), \[\theta_{k+1}-\theta_k=-\arctan(\alpha_k\epsilon_kR_k).\] These are mean-zero independent increments with variance at most \(\alpha_k^2\mathbb ER_k^2=\alpha_k^2/2\). Their total variance is finite, so the angle martingale is bounded in \(L^2\) and converges almost surely. Therefore \[
 \mathbb P(\lim_{k\to\infty}x_k\in S)=1,\] contrary to (6.3) under the marginal-only interpretation. Theorem 6.2 explicitly allows arbitrary, nonisolated target sets \(S\); no isolated-saddle premise excludes this construction.

A precise repair to the noise assumption is to require \(\mathcal L(\nu_k\mid\mathcal F_k)=\mathop{\mathrm{Unif}}(B_r(0))\) almost surely. Then for deterministic unit \(w\), or an \(\mathcal F_k\)-measurable direction, \(\mathbb E[|\left\langle \nu_k,w\right\rangle|\mid\mathcal F_k]=rc_d\) with \(c_d>0\) by rotational symmetry, and the projection-continuity argument on p. 58 gives the needed positive lower bound. This repairs the identified noise implication; it is not by itself a certification of every other part of Theorem 6.2. The construction uses an admissible generalized map with an \(O(\alpha)\) correction. It does not establish a counterexample to the special subgradient, projected, or proximal algorithms under the intended independent-noise assumptions. Under that intended reading this finding is a necessary statement clarification, rather than a refutation of the intended result.

Problem 5 (The vector field need not be \(C^2\) under Assumption A).  The opening construction on p. 57 does not follow from the smoothness premises of Theorem 6.2. Severity: Minor, a regularity gap in the abstract application.

## Claim, counterexample, and scope

Theorem 6.1 (p. 41) requires a \(C^2\) vector field. On p. 57 the proof sets \(F_p=-\nabla(f\circ P_\mathcal M)\) and asserts \(f\circ P_\mathcal M\) is \(C^3\) because \(\mathcal M\) is \(C^4\). But (A2) requires only \(f|_\mathcal M\in C^2\). Take \(\mathcal M=\mathbb R\), \(p=0\), and \[f(t)=-t^2+|t|^{5/2},\qquad G_\alpha(t,\nu)=f'(t)+\nu .\] The function is closed and \(C^2\), \(f'(0)=0\), and \(f''(0)=-2\). The manifold is \(C^\infty\). Assumption A holds: \(f'\) is locally bounded, (A2) has zero error, and (A3) is vacuous because the projection is the identity. Fresh independent uniform noise and \(\alpha_k=k^{-3/4}\) satisfy E and the stronger intended reading of F. Yet for \(t>0\), \[F_p(t)=2t-\tfrac52t^{3/2},
 \qquad F_p''(t)=-\frac{15}{8\sqrt t},\] so \(F_p\) is not \(C^2\) near zero. This is an example to the claimed regularity implication, not to stochastic nonconvergence.

Requiring \(f|_\mathcal M\in C^3\) in this application is sufficient for this step: a \(C^4\) embedded manifold has a \(C^3\) local nearest-point projection, and composition in manifold charts makes \(f\circ P_\mathcal M\) \(C^3\). The explicit \(C^4\) active-saddle assumptions in Corollaries 6.3–6.4 already require \(f|_\mathcal M\in C^4\) by Definition 2.1 (p. 9). Thus those applications meet this repair. An alternative would be a separate nonconvergence result with weaker vector-field smoothness, which is not supplied or certified here.

Problem 6 (The auxiliary process is not localized under shrinking neighborhoods).  Theorem 6.1, p. 41, does not impose the localization of \(Y_k\) used in the first proof paragraph on p. 50. Severity: Minor in demonstrated application impact, although the unrestricted abstract statement is false.

## A complete example within the displayed hypotheses

Take \(d=1\), \(S=\{0\}\), \(X_k=0\) for all \(k\), \(\epsilon_0=4\), and \(F_0(y)=y-y^2\). The field is \(C^\infty\), \(F_0(0)=0\), and \(F_0'(0)=1>0\); thus its unstable space is \(\mathbb R\). Let \(\alpha_k=\frac14 k^{-3/4}\), \(Y_1=1\), \(E_k=0\), and let \(\xi_k\) be independent equiprobable signs of magnitude \(1/8\). Define \[Y_{k+1}=Y_k+\alpha_k(Y_k-Y_k^2+\xi_k).\] For \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), both maps \(T_\pm(y)=y+\alpha(y-y^2\pm1/8)\) are increasing on \([1/2,3/2]\): their derivatives are at least \(1-2\alpha\ge1/2\). Their extreme endpoint values satisfy \[T_-(1/2)=1/2+\alpha/8\ge1/2,\qquad
 T_+(3/2)=3/2-5\alpha/8\le3/2.\] Hence the entire interval is invariant for either sign and \(Y_k\in[1/2,3/2]\subset B_{\epsilon_0/2}(0)=B_2(0)\) at every step.

For every \(k_0\) and every positive stopping radius \(\delta<4\), \(\tau_{k_0,\delta}(0)=\infty\), since \(X_k=0\). The same bounded process \(Y_k\) restricted to \(k\ge k_0\) supplies the required local iteration. Relative to the stated filtration generated by the \(X\) and \(Y\) histories, the next sign is independent, \(\mathbb E[\xi_k\mid\mathcal F_k]=0\), its fourth moment is \(1/4096\), and \(\mathbb E[|\xi_k w|\mid\mathcal F_k]=1/8\) for either unit vector \(w\) in \(\mathbb R\). The two error conditions are identically zero. The steps are square summable and not summable. All constants can be fixed numerically, independently of \(\delta\). Nevertheless, \[\mathbb P(\lim X_k\in S)=1.\]

## Intended interpretation, dependency, and correction

The proof on p. 50 chooses \(\epsilon_p\) small enough for Proposition 8.1 and subsequent Lyapunov estimates. Existence of a suitable auxiliary process in one originally supplied ball does not guarantee one in that smaller ball. In this example the admissible process stays away from the unstable equilibrium; no condition in the displayed statement links its location to the size of the stopping neighborhood for \(X\).

One precise way to remove this obstruction is to require the local-process hypotheses for every sufficiently small outer radius \(\epsilon_p\), with the indicated noise constants uniform under that shrinking. The proof can then select its Lyapunov neighborhood before invoking the process hypotheses. Alternatively, a quantitative bound such as \(Y_k\in B_{C\delta_p}(p)\), with fixed \(C\), supplies the needed localization when the stopping radius shrinks.

This latter structure is actually present in Proposition 5.2 (pp. 39–40) and equation (8.8) (p. 57): the constructed shadows belong to \(B_{4\delta}(p)\). Thus the counterexample concerns the standalone abstract theorem’s range, not the existence of a flaw in that particular shadow localization. The standard interpretation that all neighborhoods may be made sufficiently small is consistent with the intended proof; the missing stability of the process hypothesis under that operation should be stated. The repair checked here removes this localization obstruction only, and is not a separate certification of the entire nonconvergence theorem.

# A replacement KL argument for Theorem 3.11

Write \(r(x)=x-P_Y(x)\) and use \[h(x,v)=-\left\langle v,r(x)\right\rangle+\delta_{\mathop{\mathrm{cl}}X}(x).\] Work inside a definable projection neighborhood and, if needed, restrict to a smaller closed neighborhood before extending by \(+\infty\). This makes a lower-semicontinuous definable function to which the local argument applies; all considered \(x\) are in its interior neighborhood. Local closedness ensures \(N_{\mathop{\mathrm{cl}}X}(x)=N_X(x)\) at the considered points \(x\in X\). If \(\bar x\notin\mathop{\mathrm{cl}}X\) the required local assertion is vacuous.

Equation reference: eq:signed-bound. The projection is \(C^1\) for \(C^2\) \(Y\) and its derivative is symmetric: \(P_Y\) is the gradient of \(\frac12\left\|x\right\|^2-\frac12\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,Y)^2\). Its range lies in \(T_Y(P_Y(x))\), so it annihilates \(N_Y(P_Y(x))\). For unit \(v\in N_X(x)\) the smooth-plus-indicator rule gives \[\partial h(x,v)
 =\bigl(-(I-DP_Y(x))^{\mathsf T}v+N_X(x)\bigr)\times\{-r(x)\}.\] Choosing the positive normal \(v\) in the first factor is legitimate. Strong \((a)\) and boundedness of \(DP_Y\) therefore give \[
 \mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(0,\partial h(x,v))
 \le \sqrt{\left\|DP_Y(x)v\right\|^2+\left\|r(x)\right\|^2}
 \le K\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,Y).\] This works for either sign of \(\left\langle v,r(x)\right\rangle\).

Theorem 14 and Remark 8 of [BDLS], printed pp. 568–570, yield a continuous definable desingularizing function \(\varphi\), with \(\varphi(0)=0\), positive derivative on a sufficiently small positive interval, and \[1\le\varphi'(|h(x,v)|)\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(0,\partial h(x,v))\] when \(h\ne0\) is sufficiently small. The reference’s positive continuous radius function has a positive minimum on the compact range under consideration, so this choice is uniform for nearby \(x\) and unit \(v\).

For completeness, \(s\varphi'(s)\to0\) as \(s\downarrow0\). Definability makes \(\varphi'\) eventually monotone. If it is increasing as a function of \(s\), it is bounded above on a smaller interval. If it is decreasing, then \[\frac{s}{2}\varphi'(s)
 \le\int_{s/2}^{s}\varphi'(u)\,du
 =\varphi(s)-\varphi(s/2)\longrightarrow0 .\] Combining this with [eq:signed-bound] (#eq:signed-bound), for \(d=\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(x,Y)>0\), \[\frac{|\left\langle v,x-P_Y(x)\right\rangle|}{d}
 \le K\,|h(x,v)|\varphi'(|h(x,v)|)\longrightarrow0\] uniformly, since \(|h(x,v)|\le d\). The zero-value case is immediate. This is projected \((b_=)\). Strong \((a)\) implies \((a)\), and the \((a)\) plus projected-\((b)\) part of Theorem 3.9 uses only \(C^1\) Taylor remainder control, not the false Lipschitz estimate in Problem Problem 2 (#pr:c1). It consequently gives the full \((b_=)\) conclusion. The original theorem therefore admits this replacement proof under its stated hypotheses, despite the invalid estimate in its submitted derivation.
