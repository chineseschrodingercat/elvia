Abstract: The manuscript constructs a generic Hecke-module model intended to connect metaplectic Whittaker spaces with representations of quantum groups. Five substantive defects are established in the acquired 94-page author manuscript: incompatible coefficient-ring specializations, omitted affine-wall orbits, an invalid universal restricted-weight decomposition, an incorrect even-order quantum Frobenius prescription, and a nonunital spherical convolution formula. The counterexamples are explicit rank-one computations. Three groups of local formula corrections are also documented. The affected derivations are specified separately from the intended conclusions: neither the standard quantum Frobenius theorem nor every corrected metaplectic correspondence is disproved. The publisher version was not acquired, and no equivalence with it is assumed. Scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Source, hypotheses, and proof architecture

This report concerns Valentin Buciumas and Manish M. Patnaik, Metaplectic covers of \(p\)-adic groups and quantum groups at roots of unity [paper], associated with DOI https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2025-0022. The exact primary is the undated, 94-page University of Alberta author-hosted PDF acquired on 26 October 2026. Its PDF metadata reports 24 November 2023; that field is not an authenticated author-stated revision date. The separately retained arXiv:2211.03724v1, dated 7 November 2022, has 63 pages, and its TeX is not the source of this primary. Journal metadata describes 115 pages, 3317–3431 in volume 174(16). The publisher access route returned a challenge. The findings below apply only to the actual 94-page snapshot.

All page numbers are one-based physical PDF pages, agreeing with the printed numbers on the inspected pages. The contents table is stale: Sections 2–8 begin on pages 20, 25, 50, 61, 68, 74, and 83. The full primary text, including references, was covered. The associated source manifests and files were checked against their actual bytes and hashes. No corresponding supplement or paper-specific computational repository was identified in the available metadata, source tree, PDF scan and saved searches; the inaccessible publisher supplement interface remains a limitation. The absence of code is not a mathematical defect.

The intended construction has three stages. First, affine parabolic Hecke modules and canonical bases are assembled into a model over a generic Gauss-parameter ring (Sections 3–4). Second, its \(p\)-adic specialization is identified with Iwahori and spherical Whittaker spaces (Sections 5–6). Third, quantum Frobenius and tensor-product theorems give an enriched Grothendieck-module interpretation (Section 7), from which Section 8 derives correspondences and applications.

The hypotheses require particular care. In Definition 3.2.1, page 27, simply-connected means that \(Y\) is freely generated by the simple coroots. The full coweight lattice is separately defined on page 28. The introduction and Section 8.1.3 require both \(\mathfrak D\) and \(\mathfrak D_{(Q,n)}\) to be simply-connected. Theorem 8.1.4, page 84, instead prints that \(\mathfrak D\) and the dual \((\mathfrak D_{(Q,l)})^\vee\) are simply-connected. These conditions are not identified in this review. In particular, the odd-degree \(A_1\) examples below satisfy the former conditions, but not the latter dual condition. The final theorem needs a coherent reconciliation with its preparatory constructions.

The tilting-basis identification in (7.32) and Section 8.1.6 is explicitly conditional/conjectural. It is not reviewed as a proved claim. Similarly, the prospective connections to metaplectic Macdonald polynomials and the principal-module coefficients in Sections 8.3.2 and 8.3.4 are not treated as established theorems.

# The generic parameters do not have the asserted specializations

Problem 1 (Coefficient-ring and Gauss-sum compatibility).  On page 21, (2.3)–(2.4) impose periodicity \(g_{k+n}=g_k\), \(g_0=-1\), and \(g_kg_{-k}=\tau^2\) for \(k\ne0\). The same page gives the \(p\)-adic Gauss sums using an unshifted character. These definitions do not support the specializations in Sections 2.3.2–2.3.3 as written.

For the literal coefficient ring, put \(k=n\ne0\). Periodicity gives \[1=g_ng_{-n}=\tau^2.\] A homomorphism sending \(\tau\) to an indeterminate cannot exist. Neither can a \(p\)-adic specialization sending \(\tau^2\) to \(q>1\). The intended exception is presumably \(k\not\equiv0\pmod n\), exactly the condition printed in the preceding numerical identity (2.2). This is strong evidence of the intended repair, but it is absent from the ring relation.

Even with that correction, the asserted free Laurent presentation in (2.5), with all \(g_1,\ldots,g_{n-1}\) independent, is false. For \(n=3\), the relation \(g_1g_2=\tau^2\) eliminates \(g_2\). For \(n=2\), the corrected ring has \[(g_1-\tau)(g_1+\tau)=0.\] Each factor is nonzero: evaluation at \(g_1=-\tau\) or at \(g_1=\tau\), respectively, proves this over \(\mathbb Z[\tau^{\pm1}]\). It is therefore not a Laurent polynomial domain. An odd-degree presentation must use one variable per inverse pair; an even degree retains the self-inverse residue relation, unless an additional quotient is explicitly chosen.

There is a separate, directly computable discrepancy in Section 2.2.4, on the same page. The measure has \(\operatorname{vol}(\mathcal O^\times)=q-1\), and \(\psi\) has conductor zero, so \(\psi|_{\mathcal O^\times}=1\). The text introduces \(\tau(u)=\psi(-\pi^{-1}u)\), but then defines \(g_k=g(\sigma,\psi)\), rather than \(g(\sigma,\tau)\). Take \(F=\mathbb Q_7\), \(n=3\), and the nontrivial cubic residue characters. These satisfy \(q\equiv1\pmod{2n}\). The literal definition gives \[(g_0,g_1,g_2)=(6,0,0),\] whereas (2.2) requires \(g_0=-1\) and \(g_1g_2=7\). The vanishing follows by summing a nontrivial multiplicative character over \(\mathbf F_7^\times\).

The intended shifted character restores those identities. For an exact check, choose a primitive twenty-first root \(z\), put \(\chi(3)=z^7\) on \(\mathbf F_7^\times\), and sum \[G_k=\sum_{u=1}^6\chi(u)^{-k}z^{-3u}.\] Reduction modulo \(\Phi_{21}(z)=z^{12}-z^{11}+z^9-z^8+z^6-z^4+z^3-z+1\) gives \(G_0=-1\) and \(G_1G_2=7\). The preserved calculation uses exact polynomial arithmetic.

This is a Major construction-level issue: the generic object and its two specialization maps are the central bridge between the three parts. The finding does not assert that the intended bridge is impossible. Section 3 also has constructions over the separate ring \(\mathbb Z_\tau\), which are not invalidated merely by this malformed presentation. A repair must state the actual coefficient ring, its units and relations, the shifted Gauss integral, and then recheck every specialization and bar operation over that ring.

# An affine-wall orbit is missing from the assembled modules

Problem 2 (The half-open negative alcove is not a fundamental domain).  The negative upper closure in (3.65)–(3.66), page 37, and its twisted dot version (3.74)–(3.75), page 38, omit the lower affine wall. The resulting all-weight decompositions in Sections 3.6, 4.2 and 6.2 are false.

Already without a shift, the rank-one affine Weyl group acts on the root coordinate \(z\) by \(z\mapsto\pm z+2k\). The orbit of \(z=1\), consisting of odd integers, has no representative in \((-1,0]\). Thus the strict lower inequality cannot be justified by removing duplicate representatives.

For an example satisfying the printed simply-connected lattice conditions, take \[Y=\mathbb Za^\vee,\qquad X=\tfrac12\mathbb Za,\qquad
 \langle a^\vee,a\rangle=2,\qquad Q(ma^\vee)=m^2,\qquad n=3.\] Then \(B(ma^\vee,ka^\vee)=2mk\), and \(\widetilde Y=3\mathbb Za^\vee\), with twisted simple coroot \(3a^\vee\). Both data are simply-connected under Definition 3.2.1. Use the intended usual \(\rho^\vee=a^\vee/2\), as specified by the pairing-one assertion after (3.15) and the introductory positive half-sum. The displayed half-sum in (3.15) itself omits the positive-root subscript; the unshifted argument above does not depend on repairing that typo.

The proposed integral representatives \(\eta=ma^\vee\) satisfy \[-3<2m+1\le0.\] Only \(m=-1\) is allowed. Its finite dot orbit is \(\{-a^\vee,0\}\), and translation by \(\widetilde Y\) covers only residues \(2,0\pmod3\). The dominant weight \(a^\vee\) is missing. Allowing real representatives does not recover it: the affine action preserves \(Y\), so any representative of an integral weight would itself be integral.

Put \(x=Y^{a^\vee}\). Over a consistent parameter specialization, for example \(\tau=1\), \(g_1=\tfrac12\), \(g_2=2\), the full polynomial module is \(R[x^{\pm1}]\), of rank three over \(R[x^{\pm3}]\). The proposed sum spans only \[x^{-1}R[x^{\pm3}]\oplus R[x^{\pm3}],\] of rank two. The monomial \(x\) is an explicit absent vector. This is the defect in the all-\(Y\) basis assertion of Section 3.6.5, page 44, and (4.38), page 56. The assertion in Section 6.2.4 that every weight has a corresponding finite-orbit-plus-translation expression fails for the same weight. Equations (6.24) and (6.28), page 73, explicitly inherit that decomposition.

The issue is Major: an entire component is absent from the modules and their claimed bases. It is not simply an incorrect name for a fundamental region. An earlier possible test using \(\eta=-2a^\vee\) was rejected: it lies on the excluded wall and cannot refute a statement quantified over admitted indices. The valid counterexample is the missing orbit.

A corrected closed-alcove construction must include the affine wall and its singular stabilizer, and align the negative alcove with the chosen affine simple system. Simply adding the missing label while retaining the old finite-stabilizer rank formula would not suffice. For the related local Shimura construction in Section 8.2.3, page 86, even the label \(-\rho^\vee=-a^\vee/2\) does not belong to \(Y\), so \(Y_{-\rho^\vee}\) is not a monomial of \(\mathbb C[Y]\). A shifted-coset or extended-lattice construction needs to be specified. In contrast, \(\widetilde\rho^\vee-\rho^\vee=(n-1)a^\vee/2\) is integral for odd \(n\); the spherical Steinberg formula (8.12) is therefore not disproved merely by the missing intermediate label.

# The restricted box does not cover the stated lattice

Problem 3 (Universal decomposition and its use in the tensor argument).  Equations (3.82)–(3.83), page 39, and the sentence following (7.17), page 79, assert that every dominant weight is uniquely a restricted weight plus a dominant modified-lattice weight. Existence already fails under the printed hypotheses.

Use the preceding \(A_1\) datum, now with \(n=5\). Then \[\widetilde Y=5\mathbb Za^\vee,\qquad
 \square=\{ma^\vee:0\le2m<5\}=\{0,a^\vee,2a^\vee\}.\] The dominant weight \(3a^\vee\) cannot equal \(\lambda_0+\eta\) with \(\lambda_0\in\square\) and \(\eta\in\widetilde Y_+\), since \(3\not\equiv0,1,2\pmod5\). This remains impossible if \(\eta\) is allowed in the whole modified lattice. The twisted fundamental coweight is \(5a^\vee/2\), outside \(\widetilde Y\), contrary to the integrality assertion in the proof and in (3.100). Simply-connectedness as defined in this manuscript does not supply all fundamental coweights.

This is a Major defect in the universal reduction. Proposition 3.9.1, page 48, and the quantum action calculation (7.25), page 81, use precisely this decomposition. Read as a conditional identity for a pair that actually exists, the Steinberg–Lusztig formula (7.18) is not refuted. The problem is the extension to every dominant weight and hence the proposed proof of an intertwiner on the entire module.

There is an additional bounded failure in the printed tensor-product proof. Section 3.9.4, page 49, uses the crystal for the twisted root datum but asserts in (3.146) that its nonhighest paths are paired by the original dot shift. For highest weight \(5a^\vee\), the rank-one crystal has weights \[5a^\vee,\quad0,\quad-5a^\vee.\] After deleting the highest path, the path of weight zero cannot be sent to a path of weight \(0\mathbin{\bullet}s=-a^\vee\), which is not in this crystal or even in \(\widetilde Y\). Using the twisted half-sum \(5a^\vee/2\) pairs zero with \(-5a^\vee\), as intended.

The same mismatch is visible in (3.154), page 50. For \(\lambda_0=0\), \(\lambda_0^\dagger=4a^\vee\), and \(\zeta=5a^\vee\), one has \(\mu=9a^\vee\), \(\langle\mu+\rho^\vee,a\rangle=19\), and residue \(4\pmod5\). The straightened weight \(\mu^{(1)}=5a^\vee\) has dot reflection \(-6a^\vee\). The printed terminal expression \(4a^\vee-5a^\vee-a^\vee\) is \(-2a^\vee\). Replacing the final subtraction by \(5a^\vee\) restores this particular calculation. These are proof-step counterexamples, not a disproof of the repaired conditional tensor identity.

A global correction must provide complete lattice representatives, or enlarge the lattice and then descend with the appropriate central-character conditions. Enlarging to the full coweight lattice changes the affine group and can require an extended affine Hecke algebra. Correcting the crystal shift alone does not repair the missing cosets; correcting the cosets alone does not supply the cancelled-path proof.

# The even-order Frobenius prescription is not multiplicative

Problem 4 (Root order versus divided-power divisor).  Section 7.3.1, page 77, permits \(\ell=5\), \(l=10\) and a primitive \(l\)-th root \(\zeta\). Sections 7.3.4–7.3.5, page 78, use \(l_i=l/\gcd(l,d_i)\) from (3.23), page 30, in the Frobenius formula (7.14). For \(A_1\), this gives \(l_i=10\), and the asserted algebra homomorphism fails.

In the rank-one integral divided-power algebra, \[E^{(a)}E^{(b)}=
 \begin{bmatrix}a+b\\a\end{bmatrix}_{u}E^{(a+b)}.\] At a primitive tenth root, the symmetric quantum binomial satisfies \[\begin{bmatrix}10\\5\end{bmatrix}_{\zeta}=-2.\] For a direct verification, write its factorial ratio as \[\frac{[6][7][8][9][10]}{[1][2][3][4][5]}.\] The first four corresponding ratios specialize to \(-1\); the last quotient is the Laurent polynomial \([10]/[5]=u^5+u^{-5}\), with value \(-2\). Equivalently, the saved exact integer calculation constructs \(u^{-25}{10\brack5}_{u^2}^{\rm Gaussian}\) and reduces it modulo \(\Phi_{10}(u)=u^4-u^3+u^2-u+1\), obtaining \(-2\).

The obstruction also respects the dotted algebra. For the adjoint dual \(A_1\) datum, write its root as \(\alpha\) and its character lattice as \(\mathbb Z\alpha\). Set \[x=E^{(5)}1_{5\alpha},\qquad y=E^{(5)}1_0.\] The idempotents match for multiplication, and \[xy=-2E^{(10)}1_0.\] The printed rule sends both \(x\) and \(y\) to zero, but sends \(E^{(10)}1_0\) to \(E'^{(1)}1_0\). This target element is nonzero: it acts nontrivially on the weight-zero vector in the three-dimensional rank-one quasi-classical representation. Consequently \[\operatorname{Fr}(x)\operatorname{Fr}(y)=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Fr}(xy)=-2E'^{(1)}1_0\ne0.\]

This is Major for the quantum construction: the exact embedding (7.15)–(7.16), the tensor action (7.23), and the intertwining derivation (7.28)–(7.30) use this map. The elementary algebra obstruction precedes any Kazhdan–Lusztig or Coxeter-number restrictions.

The standard quantum Frobenius is not disproved. Its divisor in this example is the order of \(\zeta^2\), namely \(5\), not \(10\). Odd-order choices \(l=\ell\) are not contradicted by this test. Repair requires changing the divisor and reconciling the modified root datum, its quasi-classical group and the metaplectic degree. In this example the two modified character lattices happen to coincide, but their roots, \(5\alpha\) and \(10\alpha\), do not. Changing only the exponent in (7.14) is therefore insufficient. Alternatively, a consistently restricted odd-order theorem would require the surrounding range and applications to be rewritten and verified.

# The displayed spherical action fails the identity test

Problem 5 (Normalization of (6.1)–(6.2)).  Section 6.1.2, page 68, defines \(\widetilde J_\mu\) to take value one at \(\pi^\mu\). Section 5.3.3, page 66, normalizes spherical convolution by \(\operatorname{vol}(K)=1\). Nevertheless (6.1)–(6.2), page 69, multiply the output coefficient by \(q^{\langle\rho,\zeta\rangle}\).

Take the trivial cover of \(SL_2(\mathbb Q_7)\), \(n=1\), \(K=SL_2(\mathbb Z_7)\), and \(\mu=a^\vee\). These satisfy the stated Part II assumptions. The spherical identity is \(h_0=1_K\), and \(\langle\rho,\mu\rangle=1\). With \(\lambda=0\), the multiplication map \[U\pi^\mu K\times^K K\longrightarrow U\pi^\mu K\] is an isomorphism. Its fiber at \(\pi^\mu\) is one point, represented by \((\pi^\mu,1)\), with character value one. Thus (6.1) gives \[\widetilde J_\mu\star_K h_0=7\widetilde J_\mu.\] The same result follows from (6.2), since \[\int_U\psi(u)1_K(u)\,du
 =\int_{U(\mathbb Z_7)}1\,du=1.\] Actual convolution by \(1_K\) gives \(\widetilde J_\mu\). Even without assuming a unital-module convention, the displayed operation fails associativity with the idempotent \(h_0\): two applications yield \(49\widetilde J_\mu\), whereas \(h_0\star h_0=h_0\) requires \(7\widetilde J_\mu\).

This is a Major normalization defect in the claimed spherical module action. The right-hand side of (6.1) drops a tilde from \(J_\zeta\), but no differently normalized \(J\)-basis is defined there; the surrounding paragraph and Proposition 6.3.1, page 73, use the same value-one basis. A diagonal change of basis of a genuine module cannot make its identity act by the nonidempotent scalar \(7\).

There is useful contrary evidence: the averaging map (6.3)–(6.5), page 70, legitimately contains modular factors and explicitly rescales its Iwahori input by \(q^{-\langle\rho,\lambda\rangle}\). A normalization may have been transferred into the wrong formula. Removing the extra factor for value-one convolution, or supplying a fully consistent input/output identification, is a plausible repair. All dependent basis identifications must then be checked. This does not disprove the underlying correctly normalized Casselman–Shalika correspondence.

# Additional bounded formula corrections

The following three groups are Minor in this report. Their classification rests on nearby correct definitions and the limited role demonstrated by the counterexamples, rather than on a rule that every locally repairable error is Minor.

Problem 6 (Hecke and Satake normalization displays).  Several displayed identities contradict the defining rank-one Hecke algebra, while adjacent formulas identify the intended conventions.

On page 32, with \(t=\tau^{-2}\), \(T^2=t^{-1}+(t^{-1}-1)T\) gives \[T^{-1}=tT+(t-1).\] The printed expression with \(t^{-1}\) in both coefficients is false. At \(\tau=2\), \(t=1/4\), and scalar \(T=4\), it gives \(19\), whereas \(T^{-1}=1/4\). The factored normalized relation is \((H-\tau)(H+\tau^{-1})=0\); the second factor in (4.2), page 51, has the wrong sign. At \(\tau=1\), \(H=-1\), its printed polynomial is \(4\). Equation (4.36), page 55, correctly gives \(T^2=(\tau^2-1)T+\tau^2\), so the actual operator is not refuted by the erroneous equivalence.

For the antisymmetrizer on page 33, rank one gives \(\epsilon^-=H-\tau\) and \[(\epsilon^-)^2=-(\tau+\tau^{-1})\epsilon^-.\] Formula (3.41) instead produces the coefficient \(-(\tau-\tau^{-1})\). At \(\tau=1\), it predicts zero, whereas \((s-1)^2=-2(s-1)\) in \(\mathbb Z[W]\). The neighboring eigenrelation (3.40) also needs \(\epsilon^-\), not \(\epsilon^+\), on its right-hand side.

Finally, (3.91), page 40, at \(\mu=0\) in rank one gives \[S(h_0)=\frac{1+\tau^{-2}}{1+\tau^2}=\tau^{-2}.\] The numerator follows by adding \((1-\tau^{-2}x)/(1-x)\) and \((1-\tau^{-2}/x)/(1-1/x)\). But \(h_0=\epsilon^2=\epsilon\), and the defining Satake map sends \(\epsilon\) to one. This is a false displayed normalization, not a refutation of the abstract Satake isomorphism. These corrections require consistent tracking of \(T,H,t,\tau\), symmetrizers and measures; the nearby correct quadratic, inverse/bar relation and abstract maps prevent an inference that the corresponding Hecke modules cannot exist.

Problem 7 (The product rule and the \(\kappa\) inversion set).  Equations (4.12), page 52, and (4.40), page 56, fail under a parameter point satisfying even the literal ring relations.

Use \(A_1\), \(n=3\), \(\tau=1\), \(g_0=-1\), \(g_1=1/2\), \(g_2=2\), and \(x=Y^{a^\vee}\). Let \(f=x^{-1}\) and \(h=x^3\). The actual operator (4.10) gives \[(fh)H=2x^{-3},\qquad fH=2.\] Because \(\tau-\tau^{-1}=0\), the printed (4.12) identifies these two expressions, an explicit contradiction. Semilinearity (4.6) instead yields the consistent rule \[(fh)H=(fH)h^s+
 (\tau-\tau^{-1})f\,\frac{h-h^s}{1-Y^{-\widetilde a^\vee}}.\] Thus the intended operator can satisfy the proper Bernstein relation; the displayed product rule, and its later uses, need correction.

For the same parameters, take \(\eta=-a^\vee\). Definition (4.39) gives \(v_\eta=Y^\eta=x^{-1}\). But the positive-sign condition \(a\sigma^{-1}>0\) in the product (4.40), at \(\sigma=1\), gives \(\kappa(\eta)=g_2=2\). It would assert \(x^{-1}=2x^{-1}\). At \(\sigma=s\), the actual operator gives \(v_0=2\), while the printed empty product gives \(\kappa(0)=1\). An inversion-set condition with \(<0\) restores both values in this test.

The definition of \(v_\eta\), the first sentence of the proof and its induction supply contrary evidence to the printed product. Ratios of \(\kappa\)’s in the later basis and Littlewood–Richardson formulas must use the corrected values. The rank-one product \(\kappa(\lambda)\kappa(\lambda\bullet w_0)\) is unchanged by exchanging the two factors, so this example alone does not refute the bar formula or the abstract generic canonical-basis construction.

Problem 8 (A simple module replaces a tilting module in (7.19)).  The right-hand side of (7.19), page 79, prints \(L_{\lambda_0^\dagger}\) where the tilting tensor formula requires \(T_{\lambda_0^\dagger}\).

Take odd order \(l=\ell=5\) in \(A_1\) and \(\lambda_0=\eta=0\). Then \(\lambda_0^\dagger=4a^\vee\), ordinary \(SL_2\) highest weight \(8\). The printed formula asserts \(T_8=L_8\).

The Weyl module \(\Delta_8\) has dimension nine, with standard vectors \(v_0,\ldots,v_8\). For \(1\le r\le4\), both \(E^{(r)}v_4\) and \(F^{(r)}v_4\) have coefficient \({4+r\brack r}_\zeta=0\): the numerator contains \([5]_\zeta\), while the denominator contains no zero factor. For \(r\ge5\), the target index is outside \(0,\ldots,8\). Thus \(v_4\) spans a proper trivial submodule, and the simple highest-weight quotient has \(\dim L_8\le8\). A tilting module of highest weight eight has a Weyl filtration containing \(\Delta_8\), so \(\dim T_8\ge9\). Hence \(T_8\ne L_8\). The four vanishing coefficients were also checked by exact reduction modulo \(\Phi_5\).

Equation (7.26), page 81, already uses \(T\), giving the appropriate local repair. The main simple-basis matching uses the other tensor identity, and the later tilting identification is explicitly conditional. No failure of that conjectural identification is inferred from this local \(L/T\) error.

# Assessment, coverage, and limits

The five Major findings concern specific central dependencies: existence of specialization maps, completeness of module indices, coverage of the all-weight tensor reduction, multiplicativity of quantum Frobenius, and associativity of the displayed spherical action. Each has an exact admissible calculation and a bounded repair discussion. The findings do not require the entire paper to collapse. They also do not establish that the intended final results, after all definitions and hypotheses are consistently repaired, are false.

The elementary twist lattice and Weyl-invariance identities were checked; the rank-one twisted lattice agrees with (3.18)–(3.21). The admitted parabolic Hecke multiplication, the usual triangular/bar construction on such a module, and the dominance bound for \(\lambda_0^\dagger\) provide no additional demonstrated defect. They cannot recover an orbit that was omitted from the indexing set. The current proof does not justify replacing its two different simply-connected assumptions by one another.
