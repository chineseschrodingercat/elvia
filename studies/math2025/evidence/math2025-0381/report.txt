# Identity, scope, and assumptions

The assigned journal identity is Rate Distortion Dimension of Random Brody Curves, Masaki Tsukamoto, DOI `10.1007/s00039-025-00709-x`. The reviewed PDF is the explicitly bound preprint [paper], not a verified byte-equivalent copy of the journal version. Page references below mean one-based physical PDF pages; the source’s printed Arabic page numbers agree with them. All 60 pages were read, with claim-focused checking of the main proof chains and selected original dependencies. The accompanying coverage ledger records the exact pages visually inspected and the limits of external-reference checking.

Fix the positive integer \(N\). The compact space \(\mathcal B^N\) consists of holomorphic maps \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb {CP}^N\) with \(|df|\leq1\), for the Fubini–Study normalization in which a projective line has area one. The action is \(T^af(z)=f(z+a)\), and the compatible metric is \[d(f,g)=\max_{z\in[0,1]^2}d_{\mathrm{FS}}(f(z),g(z)).\] Uniqueness of holomorphic continuation makes this a metric; compactness and Cauchy estimates provide the topology and continuity needed below. Put \(\psi(f)=2(N+1)|df|^2(0)\) and let \(\rho(f)\) be the supremal large-disc energy density defined in the source. Write \(\rho_*=\sup_{f\in\mathcal B^N}\rho(f)\). For invariant Borel probability measures, the source defines lower and upper rate distortion dimensions using bits per unit area and \(\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\).

Equation reference: eq:upper. Theorem 2.4, p. 7, asserts \[
 \overline{\operatorname{rdim}}(\mathcal B^N,T,d,\mu)\leq\int_{\mathcal B^N}\psi\,d\mu
 \qquad\text{for every invariant }\mu .\] Theorem 2.5 asserts existence of a measure with both dimensions equal to \(\int\psi\,d\mu=c\) for each \(0\leq c<2(N+1)\rho_*\). Neither ergodicity nor attainment of the right endpoint is asserted.

# Checked reasoning and decisive checks

## The universal upper bound

The proof in Sections 5–6 uses potential \(-\psi\). Proposition 6.2 (p. 24) supplies a local covering estimate about an \(R\)-nondegenerate curve: its leading exponent is \(2(N+1)\int_S|df|^2\), with a boundary contribution of order \(L\) for a square of side \(L\). The radius and leading constant may depend on \(R\), but are independent of the covering scale. The statement and relevant proof of the cited covering result [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~10--15, 24--26]{cover}] were checked. Changing the square by a fixed-width collar changes the energy by \(O(L)\); it does not change the area-normalized leading coefficient.

Proposition 6.3, p. 25, and its proof on pp. 29–34 replace a possibly degenerate curve by a nondegenerate one with derivative at most \(1+\eta\). The local metric distortion is controlled by universal constants, and the square-energy error is less than \(\eta L^2\). The rational perturbation has order \(|z-p|^{-3}\); this tail is summable on a two-dimensional lattice. The argument separates the sparse near-center discs from the uniformly small far-field perturbation. These facts support the compact convergence, derivative bound, and energy estimate used in the covering argument.

On p. 27 the square grows from \([0,L]^2\) to \([-3,L+3]^2\). Its extra area is exactly \(12L+36\). Since the original curve has \(|df|^2\leq1\), this contributes \(O(L^{-1})\) after division by \(L^2\). For fixed \(\eta\) and \(\varepsilon\), the \(C_2(\varepsilon)L\) contribution also vanishes. The order is \(L\to\infty\), then \(\varepsilon\to0\), followed by the small-neighborhood and \(\eta\to0\) limits. In particular, the proof does not need uniformity of the boundary constant as \(\varepsilon\to0\).

The local formula with continuous potential was checked against [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~48--55]{potential}]. Its proof removes a nonnegativity restriction by subtracting the minimum of the potential; using \(-\psi\) is therefore legitimate. The entropy-versus-covering inequality in [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~27--28]{potential}] gives \[\overline{\operatorname{rdim}}(\mathcal B^N,T,d,\mu)-\int\psi\,d\mu
 \leq\overline{\operatorname{mdim}}_{M}(\mathcal B^N,T,d,-\psi)=0 .\] This proves precisely [eq:upper] (#eq:upper); it does not give equality for arbitrary invariant measures.

## The lower bound and the extremal measures

The passage from small-set volume to information is developed in Section 8. The Kawabata–Dembo estimate was checked in [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~18--19]{potential}], including its small-distortion condition \(2\varepsilon\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\leq\delta\), its exponent, and its error \(K(s+1)\). In Proposition 8.4 the relevant exponent is \(sL_n^k\). After normalization, the error is \(K(s+L_n^{-k})\), so it survives the block limit only as a constant independent of the distortion scale and disappears in the final dimension quotient.

The finite-alphabet approximation, averaged block code, boundary volume estimate, and mutual-information inequalities on pp. 40–48 were checked. Convexity in the conditional law and concavity in the input law are used in their respective correct directions. Proposition 8.7 (pp. 48–50) additionally requires tame covering growth of the averaged metric and a shifted comparison with a discrete supremum metric. Lemma 9.1 and Proposition 9.2 supply these: the comparison has constant \(4\), and the sampling construction gives logarithmic covering growth \(O((\log(1/\varepsilon))^3)\). The former hypothesis cannot simply be omitted for a general action.

Equation reference: eq:inverse. For a fixed nondegenerate \(f\) with \(\left\lVert df\right\rVert_\infty<1\), Propositions 9.6–9.7, pp. 53–54, use the deformation results [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~7, 13--19]{deform}]. The strict derivative margin is present, the deformation radius may depend on \(f\), and the loss in the complex dimension is \(C_fL\), uniformly over translated squares. For \(w=u-v\) in the chosen finite-dimensional space, \[\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb C}\leq2\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\infty,S_n},
 \qquad
 \left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\infty,S_n}\leq
 d_{L_n}(T^{a_n}F(u),T^{a_n}F(v))
       +\tfrac18\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb C}.\] Thus \[
 \left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathbb C}
 \leq\tfrac83\,d_{L_n}(T^{a_n}F(u),T^{a_n}F(v)).\] Normalized Lebesgue volume in complex dimension \(m_n\) has real scaling exponent \(2m_n\), not \(m_n\). Combining this with [eq:inverse] (#eq:inverse) gives exactly the small-set exponent on p. 55, \[2m_n\geq 2(N+1)\int_{S_n}|df|^2-2C_fL_n .\] The factor two in the leading coefficient is therefore accounted for.

The averaged measures have invariant subsequential limits: for each fixed translation, the symmetric difference of the averaging squares has relative area tending to zero, uniformly in the initial probability measure. Lemma 7.1, p. 35, compares the energies of two uniformly close Brody curves by a homotopy fixed outside the square. The Kähler form is closed, and the remaining collar has area \(O(L_n)\) and uniformly bounded derivatives. Consequently the limit measure has \[\int\psi\,d\mu=2(N+1)\rho(f).\] The lower bound from Proposition 8.7 and [eq:upper] (#eq:upper) identify both dimensions with this value, as claimed in Theorem 9.8.

Finally, for \(0<c<2(N+1)\rho_*\), Corollary 6.9 provides a nondegenerate \(g\) with \(2(N+1)\rho(g)>c\). Taking \[\lambda=\sqrt{\frac{c}{2(N+1)\rho(g)}},\qquad f(z)=g(\lambda z)\] gives \(0<\lambda<1\), the required strict derivative margin, and \(\rho(f)=\lambda^2\rho(g)\) by change of variables in the disc-energy definition. The case \(c=0\) is supplied by a point mass at a constant curve. These checks explain both the range and the excluded endpoint.

# Counterevidence, limits, and outcome

The periodic elliptic-curve example in Section 2 is counterevidence to interpreting [eq:upper] (#eq:upper) as an equality for every invariant measure: a compact finite-dimensional orbit can be encoded with zero information per unit area while carrying positive energy. The final discussion, pp. 57–58, also explicitly leaves ergodicity open. An ergodic decomposition does not identify the lower and upper rate distortion dimensions of each component at a prescribed \(c\).

No substantive Problem section is warranted by the checks performed.

The notation on p. 39 was checked directly. Let \(M(\tau)\) be the number of sets in the cover. For sufficiently small \(\tau\), tameness gives \[\tau^\eta\log_2 M(\tau)<1.\] The counting argument then yields \[4^{L^k}(2C\tau^{1-\eta})^{sL^k},\] which gives exactly the claimed exponent loss. The printed use of \(\log\#\) for that cardinality is a local notation inconsistency with this immediate reading. It supplies no evidence of a damaged main conclusion.
