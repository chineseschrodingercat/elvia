# Checks of the proof structure

## Integrated equations and uniqueness

Integration of the vortex equation gives \[\int_\Sigma |\phi|_h^2\omega=\tau V-4\pi N>0.\] Integrating the scalar equation then yields \[c=\frac{2\pi(\chi(\Sigma)-2\alpha\tau N)}{V},\] consistent with (1.2). The strict volume bound and nonzero section are essential; the zero-section boundary case is not an admissible counterexample to the results under review.

The variation formula in Proposition 2.8 contains nonnegative squared norms for \(\alpha>0\). On the graph of the vortex solution the first residual vanishes, and on a smooth geodesic the geodesic residual vanishes. The weak-geodesic argument subsequently needs the uniform estimates and limiting results of Sections 3–4. The inspection checked this dependency and the sign structure, without certifying every intermediate estimate or extracting an error from ambiguous PDF notation.

For uniqueness, Lemma 5.2 uses the absence of infinitesimal automorphisms to obtain local branches of critical points of \(K_\alpha+\lambda J_\xi\). The proof of Theorem 5.3 compares two such branches along a weak geodesic. The endpoint sub-slope inequalities and convexity imply \[0\geq \lambda\bigl(J_\xi'(1)-J_\xi'(0)\bigr)
   \geq \lambda C\,d(\omega_{\varphi_0^\lambda},
                     \omega_{\varphi_1^\lambda})^2\] for small positive \(\lambda\). The two metrics coincide and remain equal as \(\lambda\to0^+\). This does not assert strict convexity of the unperturbed functional without the perturbation argument. The cited elliptic invertibility and weak-geodesic distance inequality remain external inputs.

## Polystability and the historical correction

Theorem 5.7 separates the already treated divisor supported on at most two points from the case with at least three distinct zeroes. In the latter case Theorem 5.6 supplies properness on normalized potentials. Along the specified projective one-parameter subgroup, the proof obtains the limiting slope \[\lim_{t\to+\infty}K_\alpha'(t)
   =2\alpha\tau(N-2n_1)\left(V-\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}\right).\] The normalized geodesic has distance growing linearly in \(t\). Properness therefore forces a strictly positive slope, giving \(2n_1<N\). The chosen point can be any point of the divisor, so this supplies the strict multiplicity inequalities needed for stability in this case. Replacing properness by a bare lower bound would not justify that strict conclusion; the manuscript does use the stronger input.

The authors explicitly state on page 3 that an earlier paper’s proof of the obstruction was flawed and that the present argument rectifies it. That statement is preserved as attributed historical context. This review did not reproduce the original flaw and does not classify it as a current defect in the bound manuscript. The uniform convergence of the normalized vortex solutions along the ray and the cited Futaki calculation are dependencies, not new computations performed here.

## Independent check of the existence condition

Equation reference: eq:coefficient. The main numerical implication of (5.24)–(5.26), pages 43–44, can be checked directly. Put \[B=\frac{4\pi N}{\tau},\qquad
C_0=-c=\frac{2\pi(2g-2+2\alpha\tau N)}{V}>0,\qquad
A=-\frac{1}{V}\int_\Sigma f\,\omega_0,
\quad X=\int_\Sigma i\partial f\wedge\bar\partial f.\] Absorbing bounded constants, (5.24) implies \(X\leq 2\pi N A+C\). Equation (5.25) then implies \[\left[\alpha\tau(V-B)-\frac{2\pi\alpha^2\tau^2N}{C_0}\right]A\leq C.\] The coefficient has the exact form \[\frac{\alpha\tau}{2g-2+2\alpha\tau N}
\left[(2g-2)(V-B)+\alpha\tau N(V-2B)\right].
\] All factors used in multiplying or dividing are positive. Positivity of this coefficient is precisely \[\alpha\tau\left(\frac{8\pi N}{\tau}-V\right)
  <\frac{2g-2}{N}\left(V-\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}\right),\] the manuscript’s condition (5.26). The already available upper bound on the average of \(f\) and this lower bound control that average, and (5.24) then controls the gradient terms.

This algebra was independently executed in a small reviewer-written SymPy script. The difference between the original coefficient and [eq:coefficient] (#eq:coefficient) simplified exactly to zero, with process exit code 0. For the admissible consistency example \(g=N=\tau=m=1\), \(V=12\pi\), and \(\alpha=1/4\), the coefficient is \(\pi/2\) and \(2\alpha\tau m=1/2<1\). This example is a check of the formula, not an existence simulation or counterexample.

The multiplicity threshold also has the expected local integrability role. Near a zero of order \(m_j\leq m\), \(|\phi|\asymp r^{m_j}\); the density used on page 43 is bounded in magnitude by a constant times \[|\log r|\,r^{-(2-\epsilon)m_j/m}.\] With the area factor \(r\,dr\), it is integrable for \(\epsilon>0\). The strict condition \(2\alpha\tau m<1\) permits the required positive \(\epsilon<2(1-2\alpha\tau m)\). No claim at the excluded endpoint is inferred. The subsequent bootstrap relies on the stated Green-function and Moser–Trudinger estimates; it was checked as a dependency chain, not re-proved in all analytic details.
