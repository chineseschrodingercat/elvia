Abstract: The acquired first preprint has an incorrect shared-noise sign in the lower comparison used to pass from positive auxiliary solutions to the main theorem. An admissible additive-noise example gives a positive-probability failure of the asserted comparison. The all-time uniform version of the convolution estimate also fails because of the periodic zero mode, although its short-time form suffices for the later argument. The assessment is Major for the core comparison derivation. Explicit local corrections are described; no counterexample to global existence itself is established.

Theorem 1.1 concerns the one-dimensional periodic stochastic heat equation on \(D=[-\pi,\pi]\), with bounded continuous initial data and a locally Lipschitz coefficient satisfying \[|\sigma(z)|\le C(1+|z|^{3/2}).\] It asserts nonexplosion of the local mild solution. # The lower comparison has the wrong noise sign

Problem 1 (Equations (3.3)–(3.4) and Proposition 3.1, pp. 8–10).  The auxiliary lower-envelope process is defined by \[\partial_t v_-=\Delta v_-+v_-^{-\alpha}
                   +\sigma(-v_-)\dot W,\qquad
 v_-(0,x)=\max\{-u_0(x),1\},\quad\alpha>3.\] Proposition 3.1 then asserts \(-v_-\le u\) almost surely up to the stated positivity and explosion stopping times, using the same \(W\) as in the equation for \(u\).

If \(w=-v_-\), its noise coefficient is \(-\sigma(w)\), whereas that of \(u\) is \(+\sigma(u)\). Consequently the same-noise comparison principle invoked in the proof does not have its required matching diffusion coefficient. Stopping in a compact state interval only makes the coefficients Lipschitz; it does not change this sign.

## An admissible counterexample to the comparison

Take \(\sigma\equiv1\), \(u_0\equiv0\), and \(\alpha=4\). These satisfy the paper’s hypotheses. Let \(Z\) be the additive stochastic convolution. Then \(u=Z\), and both auxiliary equations as printed have the same initial condition \(1\), coefficient \(1\), and singular positive drift. Local pathwise uniqueness gives \(v_-=v\).

Equation reference: eq:failure. Use the spatially constant Cameron–Martin forcing \(h(t,x)=-a\), where \[a=4096,\qquad T=\frac{3}{4a}=\frac{3}{16384}.\] Its convolution is \(Z_h(t,x)=-at\). The corresponding controlled solutions are spatially constant: \[u_h(t)=-at,\qquad v_h'(t)=v_h(t)^{-4}-a,\qquad v_h(0)=1.\] On \([0,T]\), positivity of the drift term gives \(v_h(t)\ge1-at\ge1/4\). Thus \[\int_0^T v_h(s)^{-4}\,ds\le256T=\frac{3}{64},
 \qquad
 v_h(T)\le\frac14+\frac{3}{64}=\frac{19}{64}.\] It follows that \[
 u_h(T)+v_h(T)\le-\frac34+\frac{19}{64}
                    =-\frac{29}{64}<0.\] Both auxiliary solutions remain strictly positive and finite on this interval.

This deterministic control gives a stochastic counterexample, rather than merely an exceptional path of probability zero. The additive convolution is a continuous centered Gaussian random field on the compact time–space cylinder. The constant forcing \(h\) has finite \(L^2\) norm; hence \(Z_h\) is a Cameron–Martin direction. Every uniform neighborhood of \(Z_h\) has positive probability, by the Gaussian support property (Hairer, Theorem 4.44 and Exercise 4.46, pp. 31–32). On a neighborhood where \(v\ge1/8\), the drift \(v^{-4}\) is Lipschitz. The mass-one semigroup and Gronwall’s inequality give continuous dependence of the mild solution on the additive convolution, with a stopped bootstrap maintaining that lower bound. A sufficiently small neighborhood therefore preserves positivity and the strict inequality in [eq:failure] (#eq:failure). On an event of positive probability, \(u(T)<-v_-(T)\) before any of the stopping boundaries relevant to Proposition 3.1.

## Correction, counterevidence, and impact

Replace the noise in the auxiliary equation by \[-\sigma(-v_-)\dot W,\] or equivalently drive the printed lower auxiliary equation by \(-W\). Then \(w=-v_-\) has coefficient \(+\sigma(w)\) and drift \(-f(-w)\le0\), as required for the localized lower comparison. The transformed coefficient \(z\mapsto-\sigma(-z)\) remains locally Lipschitz with exactly the same growth bound, and the subsequent estimates involving its square or absolute value are unchanged.

The paper explicitly uses Proposition 3.1 to deduce Theorem 1.1 from Theorem 3.3 on p. 10. The printed comparison is false for admissible data, and this is a verified defect in a core derivation, assessed as Major. The sign correction is substantial counterevidence to a stronger claim that global existence is false. This report verifies the local comparison repair, conditional on the standard localized comparison theorem, and does not claim a full repaired proof of every external prerequisite.

# The all-time convolution bound is too strong

Problem 2 (Theorem 1.2, equation (1.17), p. 5).  For \(p>6\), the theorem gives a constant \(C_p\) independent of every \(T>0\) such that \[\mathbb E\sup_{t\le T,x\in D}|Z^\varphi(t,x)|^p
 \le C_pT^{p/4-3/2}
       \mathbb E\int_0^T\int_D|\varphi(s,y)|^p\,dy\,ds .\] This cannot hold uniformly for large \(T\) on a periodic domain.

Take the deterministic adapted field \(\varphi\equiv1\). Its required integral is \(2\pi T<\infty\). With the mass-one kernel, \[\overline Z(T):=\frac1{2\pi}\int_D Z(T,x)\,dx
       =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^T\int_D W(ds,dy),
 \qquad \mathbb E\overline Z(T)^2=\frac{T}{2\pi}.\] Writing \(m_p=\mathbb E|N(0,1)|^p>0\), spatial averaging gives \[\mathbb E\sup_{t\le T,x}|Z(t,x)|^p
 \ge m_p\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{p/2},
 \qquad
 \text{claimed upper bound}=2\pi C_pT^{p/4-1/2}.\] The ratio grows as \(T^{p/4+1/2}\). The printed rescaling of the heat kernel changes only constants and leaves this contradiction intact. Correspondingly, the all-time bound \(G(t,x)\le Ct^{-1/2}\) on p. 7 misses the nondecaying zero mode.

A correct short-time statement restricts \(0<T\le1\), or fixes a finite horizon and permits its constant to depend on that horizon. The heat-kernel bound then has the appropriate form \(C(1+t^{-1/2})\) for unrestricted \(t\). The factorization proof on pp. 13–14 can choose \[\frac{3}{2p}<\beta<\frac14\] precisely when \(p>6\), and its two powers multiply to \(T^{\beta p-3/2}T^{p/4-p\beta}=T^{p/4-3/2}\) on the required short interval.

In Section 6 the actual horizons are \(T_m=C^2M^2\,2^{-2m+6}\), tending to zero for each fixed \(M\). Enlarging \(m_0\) also ensures \(T_m\le1\). Thus the false extra uniformity is unnecessary for the inspected doubling argument. This finding is assessed as Minor, with an explicit restriction that preserves that use.

# Surviving calculations and limits

For the positive auxiliary process, let \(I=\int_Dv\). Its mass equation has nonnegative drift \(\int_Dv^{-\alpha}\) and martingale quadratic variation \(\int\sigma(v)^2\). Stopping before the mass exceeds \(M\) and before the infimum falls below \(\epsilon\), Itô’s formula for \(I^2\) gives the bound by \(M^2\) used on pp. 11–12, after the usual finite-time localization.

The critical cancellation in the doubling estimate (pp. 16–17) is correct: \[2^{-pm}\,2^{(3/2)m(p-2)}
                (2^{-2m})^{p/4-3/2}=1.\] The remaining coefficient is proportional to \(M^{p/2-3}\), with \(M\) fixed. Summing over disjoint successive stopping intervals is then bounded by the stopped quadratic variation. The first Borel–Cantelli lemma needs no independence. On a finite explosion event, continuous paths would require infinitely many high-level upcrossings, contrary to that summability. This finite-time formulation avoids interpreting a strict inequality between two infinite stopping times. The subsequent removal of the lower cutoff and the mass cutoff uses the externally cited singular-positivity result; its full proof is outside this review.

Equation (2.4), p. 7, prints \[G(t,x)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
       +\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-k^2t}\cos(kx)
       =\sqrt{2\pi}\,K(t,x).\] Its integral is \(\sqrt{2\pi}\), rather than \(1\). The intended convolution kernel is \(K=(2\pi)^{-1}+\pi^{-1}\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-k^2t}\cos(kx)\). The mass calculation on p. 11 explicitly uses integral \(1\), and the contraction argument on p. 17 uses the corresponding semigroup. Correcting this displayed normalization is therefore necessary for literal consistency, but it is recorded as a clarification. The two substantive counterexamples above concern the intended normalized equation and are unaffected by this correction.
