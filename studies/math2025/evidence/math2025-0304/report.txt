Title: Initial scientific review
Three-point correlation functions in the \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) Toda theory II: the Fateev–Litvinov formula

Binding. Baptiste Cerclé, arXiv:2208.12085v3, 17 December 2024, 73 pages; frozen identifier M25-0304; journal DOI mapping 10.4171/JEMS/1677. Only this acquired preprint is assessed; equality with the publisher version is not assumed.

# Assessment and scope

Major corrections are required in the argument as written. The principal concerns are the passage from real to complex uniform bounds, the reciprocal normalization of the hypergeometric coefficients, and a divergent integral used to bound an OPE remainder. The first and third identify failures of specific proof steps; they do not disprove the Fateev–Litvinov formula. The second concerns an explicit intermediate formula and its monodromy.

All 73 pages were read as extracted text. The root normalization \(\langle e_i,e_i\rangle=2\), the domain \(\mathcal A_N\), reflection counterterms, exceptional pole loci and restrictions on degenerate insertions were retained. Theorem 1.2 concerns a semi-degenerate insertion \(\alpha_1=\kappa\omega_2\) and \(\gamma\in[1,\sqrt2)\); no formula for arbitrary three weights, other Lie algebras or all \(0<\gamma<1\) is inferred.

The findings have analytic evidence and independently written calculation checks. Selected equations were corroborated against the version-matched mathematical HTML. The necessary bound source images and every report page were actually inspected in the same-chat continuation. The original image-return failure and incomplete delivery state remain preserved. Scientific decision and independent adjudication remain pending.

# Major comment P1: complex domination is not implied

Location. Page 34, Section 2.4.7, first paragraph; the closing proof of Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 2.2. For fixed \(c\), the manuscript establishes holomorphy in the weights; for real weights in \(\mathcal O\) it establishes uniform exponential decay in \(c\). It then infers a uniform bound in a complex neighborhood from these facts and continuity in \(c\).

That inference is false. Fix an interior real parameter \(a\) and write \(z\) for one coordinate of \(\alpha-a\). For \(\xi>0\), put \[E_z(c_1,c_2)=
 \exp\{-2\xi\sqrt{c_1^2+c_2^2}\}\exp\{-c_1^2z^2\}.\] The family is entire on a common parameter neighborhood and continuous in \(c\). For every real \(z\), \(|E_z(c)|e^{\xi|c|}\leq e^{-\xi|c|}\leq1\). Every complex neighborhood of \(a\) contains \(z=i\varepsilon\) with real \(\varepsilon\ne0\). Along \(c=(t,0)\), \[|E_{i\varepsilon}(t,0)|e^{\xi|t|}
 =e^{\varepsilon^2t^2-\xi|t|}\longrightarrow\infty.\] The positive integral of \(E_{i\varepsilon}\) also diverges on \(|c_2|\leq1\). This counterfamily satisfies all three analytic properties invoked in the inference, including a stronger uniform real bound.

It is a counterexample to the stated inference, not a claim that the actual Toda integrand equals this family. Complex domination is needed for interchanging contour and \(c\) integration and applying Morera’s theorem. Theorem 2.2 is then used in the BPZ continuation on pages 36–38 and the OPE/shift continuations on pages 55, 59 and 65–70. The fixed-\(c\) regularization argument in Section 2.4.2 and the real-weight estimates of Lemma 2.4 do not, as stated, supply the missing uniform complex estimate.

Repair boundary. Prove a locally uniform integrable complex-weight majorant for the actual subtracted integrand, including reflection terms, away from the specified poles. Decay remaining positive under small imaginary perturbations would suffice. This review has not supplied that estimate; it does not assert that such an estimate is impossible.

# Major comment P2: reciprocal hypergeometric coefficients

Location. Equations (3.4)–(3.7), page 35; Proposition 3.2 and (3.12), page 38; pages 40–41. Write the coefficient printed in (3.7) as \[D_i=
 \frac{\prod_{j=1}^3l(A_j)l(B_i-A_j)}{l(B_1)l(B_2)}
 \frac{l(1+B_1+B_2-2B_i)}{l(B_i-1)},\qquad
 l(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(1-x)}.\] For precisely the unscaled functions \(H_i\) in (3.2), (3.4) and (3.5), single-valuedness requires \(D_i^{-1}\), whereas (3.6), (3.7) and (3.12) use \(D_i\).

Use the infinity basis with leading term \((-z)^{-A_i}\). The real connection coefficients of \(H_0\) are \[K_{0i}=
 \frac{\Gamma(B_1)\Gamma(B_2)\prod_{j\ne i}\Gamma(A_j-A_i)}
 {\prod_{j\ne i}\Gamma(A_j)\Gamma(B_1-A_i)\Gamma(B_2-A_i)}.\] For row \(k=1,2\), substitute \(A_j\mapsto1-B_k+A_j\) and \((B_1,B_2)\mapsto(2-B_k,1-B_k+B_{3-k})\). The branch phase is common to each row and cancels in \(|H_k|^2\). The cross coefficients of \(\sum_{k=0}^2\lambda_k|H_k|^2\) at infinity are \(\sum_k\lambda_kK_{ki}K_{kj}\), \(i\ne j\). Euler’s reflection formula reduces their cancellation to \[\sin x\sin(b_1-b_2)-\sin(b_1-x)\sin b_2
 +\sin(b_2-x)\sin b_1=0,
 \qquad \lambda_iD_i=\lambda_0.\] This agrees with the manuscript’s own definition \(\widetilde\lambda_i=\lambda_iD_i\) and conclusion \(\lambda_0=\widetilde\lambda_1=\widetilde\lambda_2\) at the end of the proof.

For a concrete nonresonant check take \[(A_1,A_2,A_3)=(1/100,1/4,1/3),\qquad
 (B_1,B_2)=(1/2,3/4).\] All six numbers in Proposition 3.2 are distinct modulo integers; \(B_i<1\), so the corrected expression has value \(1\) at zero. The retained 60-digit computation evaluates the connection matrix: printed weights leave nonzero cross coefficients, while reciprocal weights cancel them to the working precision. These floating-point results are corroboration, not certified interval enclosures. Analytically, keep the other four parameters fixed and let \(A_1=\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Then \(D_1\sim C/\varepsilon\) with \(C>0\), whereas the required coefficient tends to zero. For all sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), these are different expressions under every hypothesis of the proposition. The reciprocal expression is real, solves (3.3), and is invariant around zero and infinity, hence also around one. It provides a counterfamily to (3.12).

The defect changes the explicit BPZ solution and the coefficient identification underlying (5.7). Every ensuing \(A/B\) ratio must be reconciled with the independent \(\Upsilon\) calculation; correcting one inverse is not a full amended proof. The final Fateev–Litvinov expression has not been disproved.

There is also a local sign error in (3.13), page 40: \(G_i\) has prefactor \((-z)^{A_i}\). Substitution of \(z^\lambda\) in (3.3) gives the infinity indicial condition \(\prod_j(\lambda+A_j)=0\), requiring \((-z)^{-A_i}\). The corrected basis is the one used in the independent connection calculation above.

# Major comment P3: the OPE diagonal integral diverges

Location. Page 50, Section 4.2.1, the unnumbered integral immediately before (4.12), in the case \(\gamma>1\). The asserted absolute integrability includes \[\int_{\mathbb D\times\mathbb D}
 \frac{d^2x\,d^2y}
 {x|x|^u\,y|y|^u\,|x-y|^{2\gamma^2}},
 \qquad u=\gamma\langle\alpha_0-\gamma h_1,e_1\rangle.\] Near \(x=y=1/2\), the absolute values of the other factors are bounded above and below by positive constants. The diagonal integral contains \(\int_0^\eta r^{1-2\gamma^2}\,dr=\infty\) for every \(\gamma\geq1\), including the range being treated.

The actual weight restrictions do not exclude this obstruction. Take \(\gamma=6/5\), \(q=43/15\), and arbitrarily small generic \(\delta>0\), with \[\alpha_0=(2/\gamma-\delta)\omega_1+(q-\delta)\omega_2,\quad
 \alpha=-\gamma\omega_1,\quad\alpha_1=(q-\delta)\omega_2,\] \[\alpha_\infty=(q-\delta)\omega_1+(2/\gamma-\delta)\omega_2.\] Every weight is strictly below \(Q\) in both root coordinates and has at least one root deficit exceeding \(\gamma\). For \(s=\alpha_0+\alpha+\alpha_1+\alpha_\infty-2Q\), \[\langle s,\omega_1\rangle=-\frac{47}{45}-\frac73\delta>-\gamma,
 \qquad
 \langle s,\omega_2\rangle=\frac{14}{45}-\frac83\delta>0\] for \(0<\delta<1/100\). Thus the tuple lies in \(\mathcal A_4\), \(\kappa<q\), and \(\gamma<\langle\alpha_0,e_1\rangle<2/\gamma\). Shrinking \(\delta\) meets the two proximity restrictions; generic choices avoid the pole hyperplanes.

The divergent integral cannot justify the asserted \(O(z^2)\) remainder bound used before identifying coefficients in Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.1. This does not establish divergence of the original damped expectation. A repair must retain sufficient exponential damping or prove a suitable fusion estimate at \(x=y\), with the required uniformity in \(z\) and integrability in \(c\).

# Localized corrections

## P4: positive-moment thresholds (Minor)

Page 18, (2.33)–(2.34), uses a maximum in a moment condition. For (2.34), let \(\gamma=1\), \(\alpha=Q-\frac12\omega_1-\frac74\omega_2\) and \(p_1=1,p_2=0\). Both root deficits are positive and both printed strict inequalities hold, including \(1<\max(2,1/2)\). However \(\langle\alpha,e_1\rangle=5/2\). Normalized subcritical GMC has mean Lebesgue measure on the unit disc centered at zero, so Tonelli gives \[\mathbb E\int_{\mathbb D}|x|^{-5/2}M^{e_1}(d^2x)
 =2\pi\int_0^1r^{-3/2}\,dr=\infty.\] The same local obstruction refutes (2.33). A scalar moment range must enforce both the bulk threshold and every insertion threshold, rather than their maximum.

The impact is limited by counterevidence: the next sentence states the correct sufficient first-moment condition, and the explicit use on page 27 has insertion deficit greater than \(\gamma\). No additional core failure is established from this misstatement alone. The external moment lemma was not fully audited; its publisher PDF fetch was blocked.

## P5: subtraction sign in Lemma 4.3 (Minor)

On page 55, \(r_{a,b}\) has a plus sign before \(\frac b2(1/x+1/\bar x)\) when \(0<a-b<1\). For \(a=3/2,b=1\), all hypotheses hold, but at \(x=re^{i\theta}\) the numerator of (4.13) has leading term \(-2\cos\theta\). On a sector with \(\cos\theta>1/2\) its absolute integral diverges as \(\int^\infty r^{-1/2}\,dr\). The ordinary Lebesgue integral is therefore not defined. A circular principal value is a different, unstated convention.

The correction is a minus sign inside \(r_{a,b}\), since \[|x-1|^b=|x|^b
 \left(1-\frac b2(1/x+1/\bar x)+O(|x|^{-2})\right).\] For \(0<a-b<1\) the corrected subtraction is integrable at zero and infinity. This correction, and the corresponding Taylor subtraction on page 49, must be propagated into the coefficient calculation. It is a localized sign error with an explicit integrability repair, distinct from P3.

# Coverage and remaining limits

Sections 1–2 were read for the definitions, Seiberg domains, Weyl cocycle, pole exclusions, subtractions, Brownian decompositions and real decay estimates. Section 3 was checked for the BPZ dependency, basis normalization and nonresonance assumptions. Sections 4–5 were followed through the three OPE regimes, reflection cancellations, nonempty parameter sets, two shift lengths, dense-period argument and DOZZ residue normalization. Imported reflection asymptotics, Ward identities, full fusion theorems and the DOZZ theorem were not independently reproved. The probabilistic error estimates were read and their dependencies traced; a new proof of every uniform estimate is not claimed. The gamma reflection identity on page 40 was corroborated as correct, so extraction order there is not a finding. Explicit pole discussions in Section 2 prevent interpreting every use of analyticity as a claim that all poles are removable.

An independently written SymPy/mpmath program checks the divergent integrals, exact weight inequalities, sine identity, subtraction expansion and connection matrix. No author code was run. Bounded title, correction, supplement and code searches did not locate a separate relevant supplement, code package or correction in the inspected results. This is a search limitation, not proof of absence.
