Abstract: The inspected author manuscript has one supported local defect: the filtered-ring criterion in Lemma 2.7 omits separatedness. An explicit filtration by primary ideals has an integral, nontrivially graded associated ring but cannot come from a finite-valued valuation. Separatedness restores the criterion and follows directly for the filtration used in Corollary 4.10. This is a Minor finding; the counterexample does not refute the finite-generation theorem. The report also records checks of the complement construction, rational-weight passage, and downstream scope.

# Definitions and dependency structure

The general setting is a normal affine variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, with an effective \(\mathbb Q\)-boundary and a klt singularity at a closed point \(x\). A real valuation takes finite real values on every nonzero element of the function field. The centered valuations in \(\operatorname{Val}_{X,x}\) are nonnegative on the local ring and positive on its maximal ideal. The ideal at threshold \(\lambda\) is \[\mathfrak a_\lambda(v)=\{f:v(f)\geq\lambda\}.\] The normalized volume is \(A_{X,\Delta}(v)^n\operatorname{vol}(v)\). Its scale invariance follows from \(A(cv)=cA(v)\) and \(\operatorname{vol}(cv)=c^{-n}\operatorname{vol}(v)\); uniqueness of a minimizer is therefore understood up to scaling.

The main proof has two substantial branches. First, Lemma 3.3 establishes the threshold identity needed in Lemma 3.2, and Lemma 3.4 makes the minimizing valuation a monomial lc place of a special complement. The ample strict-transform condition avoiding the strata is essential here; the paper does not assert that any lc complement has this consequence. Second, Theorem 3.14 and Corollary 3.18 produce Kollár models, and Propositions 4.5–4.6 construct their compatible degenerations. Lemmas 4.8–4.9 then identify the filtered ideals; Corollary 4.10 supplies finite generation. Theorem 4.1 combines these implications, and Theorem 1.1 applies it to the minimizing valuation.

This dependency map matters for calibration. Lemma 2.7 is invoked inside the final branch, but its missing assumption can be proved from the definitions at that invocation. The defect does not require replacement of the main geometric construction.

# Problem 1: the filtered-ring criterion needs separatedness

## Source claim and suspect step

Equation reference: eq:sup. Lemma 2.7, PDF p. 8, and source lines 425–429 assert that a decreasing multiplicative sequence of ideals of a domain whose associated graded ring is integral is the sequence of ideals of a discrete valuation. The proof defines \[
 v(f)=\sup\{i:f\in\mathfrak a_i\}\qquad(0\ne f\in R)\] and uses products of nonzero initial forms to establish multiplicativity. The product argument is valid when the initial forms exist. What has not been shown is that the supremum in [eq:sup] (#eq:sup) is finite for every nonzero \(f\).

Equation reference: eq:separated. The definition of a graded sequence in the preliminary conventions requires multiplicativity, and the lemma adds decreasingness. Neither requirement supplies \[
 \bigcap_{i\geq0}\mathfrak a_i=0.\]

## Complete counterexample

Work over \(\mathbb C\) and take \[R=\mathbb C[x,y],\qquad \mathfrak a_0=R,\qquad
 \mathfrak a_i=(x,y^i)\quad(i\geq1).\] The ring is an integral domain, indeed a regular finitely generated ring, and its affine spectrum with zero boundary is smooth and klt. Each positive-index ideal is nonzero, proper, and \((x,y)\)-primary. The sequence decreases because \((x,y^{i+1})\subseteq(x,y^i)\). For \(i,j\geq1\), \[(x,y^i)(x,y^j)
  =(x^2,xy^i,xy^j,y^{i+j})\subseteq(x,y^{i+j}).\] The cases with an index zero are immediate. Thus every stated filtration assumption holds, even with the extra primary-ideal property later used in the paper.

Reduction modulo \(x\) identifies the successive quotients with those of the \(y\)-adic filtration of \(\mathbb C[y]\). Consequently \[R/\mathfrak a_1\simeq\mathbb C,\qquad
 \mathfrak a_i/\mathfrak a_{i+1}=\mathbb C\,[y^i]\ (i\geq1),\qquad
 \operatorname{gr}(R,\mathfrak a_\bullet)\simeq\mathbb C[t],\quad \deg t=1.\] Multiplication sends \([y^i][y^j]\) to \([y^{i+j}]\), so this is an isomorphism of graded rings, not just of vector spaces. In particular the graded ring is an integral domain with nonzero positive-degree pieces.

However \(0\ne x\in\mathfrak a_i\) for every \(i\). Any valuation satisfying \(\mathfrak a_i=\mathfrak a_i(v)\) would have \(v(x)\geq i\) for every positive integer \(i\), which contradicts the required finite real value of \(v(x)\). The proposed formula instead assigns \(v(x)=+\infty\). It gives a semivaluation supported on the nonzero prime ideal \((x)\), rather than a valuation of \(\mathbb C(x,y)\). Indeed \(\bigcap_i\mathfrak a_i=(x)\), exactly the missing separation.

## Alternative readings and counterevidence

Allowing a nontrivial support for a semivaluation would make the formula meaningful, but changes the definition of valuation used on p. 7 and the function-field conclusion of the lemma. Requiring positive-degree graded pieces, proper ideals, primary ideals, a Noetherian ring, or a smooth ambient variety does not remove this example. The usual separated filtration convention would remove it, but no such condition is stated in the relevant definition or in Lemma 2.7. The proof itself needs this convention explicitly.

## Supported corrected statement and proof

Let \(R\) be a domain and \((\mathfrak a_i)_{i\geq0}\) a decreasing, multiplicative sequence with \[\mathfrak a_0=R,\quad 0\ne\mathfrak a_1\subsetneq R,\quad
 \bigcap_i\mathfrak a_i=0,\] and suppose \(\operatorname{gr}(R,\mathfrak a_\bullet)\) is a domain. Then [eq:sup] (#eq:sup) defines a nontrivial discrete valuation on \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)\) whose threshold ideals at integral indices are exactly the \(\mathfrak a_i\).

For \(f\ne0\), separatedness and decreasingness give a unique finite largest index \(i\) with \(f\in\mathfrak a_i\). The properness of \(\mathfrak a_1\) gives \(v(1)=0\). If \(v(f)=i\) and \(v(g)=j\), their nonzero initial forms have nonzero product in the graded domain. Thus \(fg\in\mathfrak a_{i+j}\setminus\mathfrak a_{i+j+1}\), proving \(v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)\). Ideal membership proves \(v(f+g)\geq\min(v(f),v(g))\) whenever \(f+g\ne0\). The formula \(v(f/g)=v(f)-v(g)\) is well-defined by the product identity. The value group is a nonzero subgroup of \(\mathbb Z\), hence discrete. Finally, decreasingness gives \(f\in\mathfrak a_i\) if and only if \(v(f)\geq i\). This proves exactly the corrected assertion, with no completeness assumption on \(R\).

## Verification that the actual application is separated

Equation reference: eq:containment. Corollary 4.10 on p. 38 invokes the lemma for the filtration \[I_{s,\lambda}
 =\operatorname{Span}\{f\in R:\textstyle\sum_j s_jv_j(f)\geq\lambda\},
 \tag{4.3 of the manuscript}\] where \(v_j=\operatorname{ord}_{E_j}\) lie in one quasi-monomial cone, and initially \(0\ne s\in\mathbb N^r\) after rescaling rational weights. Write \(\alpha^j\) for the weight vector of \(v_j\). The quasi-monomial valuation \(v_s\) with weight \(\sum_j s_j\alpha^j\) satisfies, for a local monomial expansion, \[v_s(f)
 =\min_\beta\sum_j s_j\langle\alpha^j,\beta\rangle
 \ \geq\ \sum_j s_j\min_\beta\langle\alpha^j,\beta\rangle
 =\sum_j s_jv_j(f).\] The valuation inequality for sums therefore gives \[
 I_{s,\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak a_\lambda(v_s).\] This is also the first observation in the proof of Lemma 4.8, p. 35, and does not presuppose the equality being proved.

Because \(v_s(f)\) is finite for \(f\ne0\), [eq:containment] (#eq:containment) proves \(\bigcap_{i\geq0}I_{s,i}=0\). Centeredness gives \(I_{s,0}=R\) and properness of \(I_{s,1}\). For any \(0\ne h\) in the maximal ideal of \(x\), a sufficiently large power \(h^N\) has \(\sum_j s_jv_j(h^N)\geq1\), proving \(I_{s,1}\ne0\). Thus all extra hypotheses of the corrected lemma hold at the integer-weight invocation. Its integral graded ring is supplied by Propositions 4.5–4.6 as in the manuscript. Lemma 4.8 then identifies the resulting valuation with \(v_s\); Lemma 4.9 treats the remaining real weights.

The counterexample falsifies the unrestricted auxiliary lemma, but this direct separation argument repairs its actual use. The supported severity is therefore Minor. It is not a counterexample to Theorems 1.1–1.3.

# Other checked reasoning and boundaries

## The strict threshold in Lemma 3.3

Set \(A(v_0)=1\), \(q=v_0(f)>0\), \(rD=\operatorname{div}(f)\), and \(V=\operatorname{vol}(v_0)\). The counts on pp. 13–15 give \[N_m\sim\frac{Vm^n}{n!},\qquad
 w_m=mN_m-\sum_{j=1}^mN_j
       \sim\frac{nVm^{n+1}}{(n+1)!},\] and \[\mu_m=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor m/q\rfloor}
            \ell(R/\mathfrak a_{m-jq}(v_0))
       \sim\frac{Vm^{n+1}}{q(n+1)!}.\] Hence \(r\mu_m/w_m\longrightarrow1/(n v_0(D))\). The strict assumption \(v_0(D)<1/n\) makes this limit larger than one and yields domination of \(D\) for sufficiently large \(m\). The argument does not prove the same statement at equality. The simultaneous-basis and K-semistability inputs remain cited dependencies rather than new results verified here.

As an exact check, take the smooth plane with \(v_0=\operatorname{ord}_0/2\), \(D=\frac14\{x=0\}\), \(r=4\), and \(f=x\). Then \(A(v_0)=1\), \(v_0(D)=1/8\), and \(\mathfrak a_m=\mathfrak m^{2m}\). Counting monomials gives \[N_m=m(2m+1),\quad
 w_m=\frac{m(m-1)(8m+5)}6,\quad
 \mu_m=\frac{(2m-1)(2m)(2m+1)}6.\] Thus \(4\mu_m/w_m\to4=1/(2v_0(D))\). The independent program checked all three integer formulas for \(m=2,\ldots,50\) (147 equalities) and the exact symbolic limit. It is a check of these counts, not an empirical reproduction or a substitute for the general argument.

## Local approximation and geometric induction

In Lemma 3.2, the needed Lipschitz bound is local near \(v_0\), which lies in the interior of the relevant cone. Finite convexity supplies local Lipschitz continuity there. A general global Lipschitz assertion on an entire closed cone does not follow from convexity alone; no counterexample to the specific function in this manuscript was established, and the local form suffices for the stated nearby approximants.

The inspection of Propositions 4.5–4.6 included the compatibility of the simultaneous induction, the restriction-surjectivity input from Lemma 3.13, and the use of qdlt normal strata together with connectedness. The integrality claim is not justified by flatness alone, nor is that the paper’s only argument. A complete reconstruction of every relative MMP and adjunction input was not performed.

## Rational weights, real weights, and downstream scope

Lemma 4.9 uses a finite set of weights below a fixed threshold, rather than one perturbation preserving the ordering of infinitely many monomials. The coefficients in the chosen \(\mathbb Q\)-independent weight decomposition distinguish the relevant finite tuples. Positive coordinates and restriction to the active face handle the local finiteness of the weight set. In Corollary 4.10 the input divisorial valuations are the integral orders \(\operatorname{ord}_{E_i}\), so the integral rescaling step has its required meaning. These observations support the specific rational-to-real passage used there. Full globalization of every quasi-monomial model appearing in the general formulation was not independently reconstructed.

The same-open-face conclusion for \(\operatorname{gr}_{v_s}R\) concerns the underlying algebras. The numerical real gradings can change with the Reeb weights. It does not assert a grading-preserving identification across different numerical weights, nor an identification across all boundary faces.

For Theorem 1.3, the \(\mathbb Q\)-boundary and the rational Reeb-volume function are relevant to algebraicity after normalization \(A=1\). The cited results on the minimizer are still external inputs. The theorem is not interpreted here as a result for arbitrary real boundary coefficients. For Theorem 1.6, deleting faces may produce a locally closed polyhedral subset; it need not be a closed cone.

A basic compatible boundary check is the positive monomial valuation on smooth \(\mathbb A^n\): \[A(v)=\sum_iw_i,\qquad \operatorname{vol}(v)=(\prod_iw_i)^{-1},\qquad
 \widehat{\operatorname{vol}}(v)=\frac{(\sum_iw_i)^n}{\prod_iw_i}\geq n^n.\] Equality is attained at equal weights. Irrational weights do not prevent the associated algebra from being a polynomial algebra. This calculation gives no contradiction to the finite-generation assertion or its scaling conventions.
