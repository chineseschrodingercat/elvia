# Source, hypotheses and scope

The reviewed source is S. Ling, Local geometry determines global landscape in low-rank factorization for synchronization, arXiv:2311.18670v2, posted 27 February 2025. The acquired PDF has 26 pages and a title-page date of 3 March 2025; physical and printed pages coincide. Its publication DOI is https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-025-09707-9. The publisher records an online date of 24 April 2025 and journal pagination 1553–1585 in volume 26 (2026). The acquired preprint, rather than an assumed identical journal version, is reviewed here.

The factor problem minimizes \(f(S)=-\sum_{i<j}\langle A_{ij},S_iS_j^\top\rangle\) over \(S_i\in\mathbb R^{d\times p}\), \(S_iS_i^\top=I_d\). For an optimal orthogonal-block candidate \(O\), Theorem 2.8 assumes \[L=\operatorname{BDG}(AOO^\top)-A\succeq0,\qquad LO=0,\qquad
 \lambda_{d+1}(L)>0.\] With \(\kappa=\lambda_{\max}(L)/\lambda_{d+1}(L)\), the sufficient rank is \(p\ge(2\kappa-1)d+2\). Theorem 2.1 gives \(p\ge2\kappa+1\) when \(d=1\). Optimality and uniqueness are statements about the Gram matrix; right orthogonal transformations of a factor are not distinct optimal Gram matrices. For \(d\ge2\), the optimizer need not equal the noisy model’s planted orthogonal factors.

# Problem 1: the semi-random corollary omits necessary centering

Assessment: Major for the literal recovery corollary; the deterministic theorems are not refuted. Equation (2.8), p. 9, correctly defines the score matrix as \(\operatorname{Ber}(p_{\rm in/out})-(p_{\rm in}+p_{\rm out})/2\). Corollary 2.5, p. 10, uses this centered model. The thinning construction on pp. 11–12 instead uses raw Bernoulli edges, and Corollary 2.7 explicitly specifies \(A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Ber}(p_{ij})\) without the subtraction. It asserts that the factorization corresponding to \(A\) has only the planted Gram matrix \(xx^\top\) at its local minima. For the objective actually defined in the paper, this is false.

## Complete admissible counterexample family

Take even \(n\), balanced labels \(x=(1_{n/2},-1_{n/2})\), factor rank \(p=4\), and \[p_{\rm in}=3/4,\qquad p_{\rm out}=1/4,\qquad
 p_{ij}=\begin{cases}3/4,&x_i=x_j,\\1/4,&x_i\ne x_j.\end{cases}\] This ordinary independent stochastic block model is a permitted instance of the stated semi-random model. For every fixed finite constant \(C'_0\), the hypothesis imported from Corollary 2.5 becomes \[\frac{2C'_0\sqrt{n(p_{\rm in}+p_{\rm out})\log n}}
 {n(p_{\rm in}-p_{\rm out})}
 =4C'_0\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}
 \le\frac{p-3}{p+1}=\frac15,\] which holds for all sufficiently large even \(n\). The underlying \(p_{\rm in}\gtrsim n^{-1}\log n\) condition is also satisfied. Neither probability is a degenerate endpoint.

Every entry of the raw off-diagonal \(A\) is nonnegative. For any unit \(u\in\mathbb R^4\), the feasible factor \(S_i=u\) attains \[f_{\rm common}=-\sum_{i<j}A_{ij},\] a global minimum since \(\langle S_i,S_j\rangle\le1\) for every feasible pair. Its Gram matrix is \(J_n\), not \(xx^\top\). Let \(E_{\rm cross}\) count the cross-community edges. Every factor with Gram matrix \(xx^\top\) has objective \[f_{\rm planted}-f_{\rm common}=2E_{\rm cross}.\] Thus on \(E_{\rm cross}>0\) the planted Gram matrix is not even globally optimal, whereas \(J_n\) is a global and hence local minimum. Independence gives \[\mathbb P(E_{\rm cross}>0)=1-(3/4)^{n^2/4}\longrightarrow1.\] This checks feasibility, all probability bounds and the rank hypothesis. It contradicts the corollary even under the charitable interpretation that its unstated probability qualifier is the high-probability qualifier of Corollary 2.5. No assertion about connectivity or uniqueness of all raw-matrix minimizers is needed.

## Alternative reading and verified conditional repair

The preceding centered model makes the intended preprocessing plausible. If corresponding to \(A\) is intended to mean that the raw graph is centered before optimization, this counterexample does not apply to that different objective. The corollary and its coupling argument must say so: the subtraction changes the optimizer and cannot be discarded as a constant on the unconstrained factor space.

Use \(B\) for the raw graph and set \(C=B-cJ_n\), \(c=(p_{\rm in}+p_{\rm out})/2\). Perform the paper’s thinning and augmentation on \(B\), obtaining \(B_M\). Unconditionally, the independent off-diagonal entries of \(B_M\) have the ordinary block-model probabilities. This is not a statement that its law conditional on an already fixed \(B\) is the ordinary block model. Set \[C_M=B_M-cJ_n,\qquad \Delta=B-B_M,\qquad C=C_M+\Delta.\] Then \(\Delta_{ij}\ge0\) within communities, \(\Delta_{ij}\le0\) across communities, and \(\Delta\circ xx^\top\ge0\). On the certificate event supplied by Corollary 2.5, Theorem 2.6 applies to \(C_M+\Delta\) and gives the desired centered recovery statement, with the same probabilistic qualification. Diagonal entries are immaterial to the factor objective. This verifies the logical repair conditional on that earlier concentration result; it does not independently reprove all of its probabilistic constants.

The issue affects a principal semi-random recovery claim and is therefore Major despite the short preprocessing repair. It does not show a spurious local minimum for the centered model, nor a failure of Theorems 2.1, 2.6 or 2.8.

# Deterministic proof checks, including the rank boundary

The tangent construction on pp. 15–17 was reconstructed with \(V_i=O_i\Phi-S_i\Phi^\top O_i^\top S_i\), where the entries of \(\Phi\in\mathbb R^{d\times p}\) are independent standard Gaussians. It satisfies \(V_iS_i^\top+S_iV_i^\top=0\). With \(X_{ij}=\langle O_i^\top S_i,O_j^\top S_j\rangle\), one has \(X\succeq0\), \(X_{ii}=d\), and \[\mathbb E[V_iV_j^\top]=(p-2)O_iO_j^\top+X_{ij}S_iS_j^\top.\] The Schur product and projection estimates give the paper’s bound \[\mathbb E Q(V)\le
 \lambda_{\max}(L)\left(nd^2-\frac{\|X\|_F^2}{n}\right)
 -(p+d-2)\lambda_{d+1}(L)B,\qquad
 B=nd-\frac{\langle X,J_n\rangle}{n},\] where \(Q\) is the Riemannian Hessian quadratic form.

The final coarsening in the printed proof loses strictness at the rank boundary, but its preceding inequality supplies it. Put \(h=\langle X,J_n\rangle\). The Frobenius Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives \[nd^2-\frac{\|X\|_F^2}{n}
 \le\left(d+\frac{h}{n^2}\right)B.\] If \(B>0\) and \(p+d-2\ge2d\kappa\), retaining this factor yields \[\mathbb E Q(V)\le
 \lambda_{\max}(L)\left(\frac{h}{n^2}-d\right)B
 =-\frac{\lambda_{\max}(L)}{n}B^2<0.\] If \(B=0\), equality in the norm of the sum of the equal-norm matrices \(O_i^\top S_i\) forces all of them to agree, and \(SS^\top=OO^\top\). The equality case in the stated rank condition is therefore supported, not an established defect. The Hessian calculation itself does not require the point to be stationary, consistent with the stronger negative-curvature wording.

For the monotone perturbation when \(d=1\), the calculation on pp. 18–19 gives \[\mathbb E Q_\Delta(V)=
 (3-p)\langle L_\Delta,SS^\top\rangle
 -\frac14\sum_{i,j}x_ix_j\Delta_{ij}
       \|x_iS_i-x_jS_j\|^4\le0\] for \(p\ge3\) and \(\Delta\circ xx^\top\ge0\). The base contribution is strictly negative off the optimal Gram matrix under its certificate threshold, so their sum remains negative. This does not assume the condition number improves after the perturbation. The actual certificate hypothesis in Theorem 2.6, rather than a general unspecified benign landscape, is essential.
