Abstract: The frequency-localized estimate (6.5) in Theorem 9 is false as printed: a source-free Schrödinger packet on a zero background violates it by one full power of frequency. The concluding inference of its proof and the stated bootstrap do not establish that estimate. This concerns a quantitative input used in the local well-posedness argument. Substantial counterevidence limits the impact: Theorem 8 and the Morawetz calculation have the correct positive half-power, so failure of the main existence theorems is not established. Other checks cover the scope of scattering, resonance, long-time interpolation and energy accumulation. Full correction and scientific acceptance remain pending.

# Definitions, quantifiers, and boundaries

The QNLS equation is \[i u_t+g(u)u_{xx}=N(u,u_x),\] with a real metric, smooth coefficients, and \(N\) at most quadratic in \(u_x\). The normalized metric has \(g(0)=1\); the cubic convention concerns the nonlinear perturbation of that constant. DQNLS additionally allows derivative dependence in the metric. The local thresholds are strictly \(s>1\) and \(s>2\), respectively (Theorem 2, p. 5). The endpoints are explicitly open, and generic optimality is Conjecture 3, not a proved ill-posedness theorem. Rough uniqueness is formulated for uniform limits of regular solutions, not arbitrary distributional solutions.

For long-time results the cubic symbol \(c(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)\) is phase compatible and conservative: \[c(\xi,\xi,\xi),\quad \partial_{\xi_j}c(\xi,\xi,\xi)\in\mathbb R.\] Defocusing additionally requires the positive lower bound in Definition 1.4; for QNLS it is \(c(\xi,\xi,\xi)\gtrsim\langle\xi\rangle^2\). This is stronger than mere reality or positivity at one frequency. Scattering on p. 3 is explicitly the weak sense of global spacetime estimates, not convergence to a linear solution.

Theorem 6 propagates energy to \(T\ll\epsilon^{-8}\), but its long-time \(L^6\) and bilinear bounds are on subintervals of length \(\epsilon^{-6}\). Theorem 7 supplies such bounds on the entire interval under defocusing. Keeping these two time quantifiers separate is necessary for the energy argument.

# Problem 1: a false frequency gain in the core linear estimate

## Claim and location

Equation reference: bad. Theorem 9, p. 40 (printed p. 260), equation (6.5), asserts, for \(\mu\approx\lambda\), \[
 \|\partial_x(v_\lambda\overline{v_\mu^{x_0}})\|_{L^2_{t,x}}
 \lesssim d_\lambda d_\mu\lambda^{-1/2}(1+\lambda|x_0|).\] Here the functions solve the independent paradifferential equations (4.2), p. 24, and \[d_\lambda^2=\|v_\lambda(0)\|_2^2+
       \sup_{x_0}\|v_\lambda f_\lambda^{x_0}\|_{L^1_{t,x}}\] by (6.1). Theorem 9 imposes the small-solution assumptions on the background \(u\), not a relation \(v_\lambda=P_\lambda u\). Page 40 explicitly allows independent \(v_\lambda\); p. 41 even allows normalization \(d_\lambda=1\).

## Counterexample with every hypothesis checked

Fix any smooth cubic QNLS with \(g(0)=1\), for example \(g(u)=1+|u|^2\) and \(N(u,u_x)=|u|^2u\). Take the background \(u\equiv0\), any \(s>1\), and sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon>0\). This is a smooth exact solution. Every background estimate (3.1)–(3.4) is zero on the left, for any admissible envelope. Fix \(T=1\); it satisfies \(T\ll\epsilon^{-4}\) after decreasing \(\epsilon\). At the zero background, \(g_{[<\lambda]}=1\) for all sufficiently large \(\lambda\).

Choose a nonzero \(\widehat\phi\in C_c^\infty\) supported in a sufficiently narrow compact subinterval of a unit dyadic annulus. Its width is fixed to fit the paper’s fixed step \(\mathfrak c>1\); no assumption that the step is two is used. Normalize \(\|\phi\|_2=1\), and set \[w(\tau,y)=e^{i\tau\partial_y^2}\phi(y),\qquad
 v_\lambda(t,x)=\lambda^{1/2}w(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),
 \quad \lambda=\mathfrak c^k\longrightarrow\infty.\] These are smooth exact solutions of \(i\partial_t v_\lambda+\partial_x^2v_\lambda=0\). Their Fourier supports remain inside the corresponding annuli of size \(\lambda\). Set \(f_\lambda=0\), so all source regularity assumptions hold and \(d_\lambda=1\) exactly. Set \(\mu=\lambda\) and \(x_0=0\); the balanced-frequency and translation hypotheses are then satisfied.

Define the fixed positive finite constant \[A=\|\partial_y|w|^2\|_{L^2([0,1]\times\mathbb R)}.\] Finiteness follows from the Schwartz regularity on a compact time interval. Positivity needs no numerical assumption: if \(\partial_y|\phi|^2\) were identically zero, its Schwartz decay would force \(\phi=0\). Continuity in time therefore gives \(A>0\).

Equation reference: counter. The change of variables \(\tau=\lambda^2t\), \(y=\lambda x\) gives \[\partial_x|v_\lambda|^2
       =\lambda^2(\partial_y|w|^2)(\lambda^2t,\lambda x),
 \qquad dt\,dx=\lambda^{-3}\,d\tau\,dy.\] Consequently, \[
 \|\partial_x|v_\lambda|^2\|_{L^2([0,1]\times\mathbb R)}
 \ge
 \|\partial_x|v_\lambda|^2\|_{L^2([0,\lambda^{-2}]\times\mathbb R)}
 =\lambda^{1/2}A.\] Equation [bad] (#bad) would instead bound this by \(K\lambda^{-1/2}\), with \(K\) independent of the dyadic frequency. It would imply \(A\lambda\le K\) for every \(k\), a contradiction. The initial data are square-integrable Schwartz packets; a nonintegrable plane wave is not being offered as a theorem counterexample.

## Where the proof fails and what its own estimates support

The last paragraph of Section 6, p. 46 (printed p. 266), claims that (6.12), (6.14), and (6.17) imply (6.5). At \(x_0=0\) and \(\mu=\lambda\), the cited boundary and source bounds have size \[\lambda d_\lambda^2d_\mu^2,\] and (6.14)–(6.15) identify the leading spacetime expression with the squared differentiated bilinear norm. Taking a square root gives \(\lambda^{1/2}d_\lambda d_\mu\), not \(\lambda^{-1/2}d_\lambda d_\mu\).

The printed bootstrap also cannot justify the stated continuation. On p. 41, (6.9) has right-hand side \(C^2\epsilon d_\lambda d_\mu\lambda^{-1/2}(1+\lambda|x_0|)\), and the unbalanced bootstrap (6.10) likewise has a negative half-power and an extra \(\epsilon\). Improving to a bound of the form (6.5) with constant independent of \(\epsilon\) does not recover the smaller bootstrap for \(\epsilon\ll_C1\). The free-flow family above shows directly why a uniform continuation of the printed (6.9) cannot be true.

This is a demonstrated quantitative failure and invalid inference in a main proof input, rather than a finding based only on the appearance of an unusual exponent. Section 8, pp. 64–66, invokes Theorem 9 both for \(v_\lambda=u_\lambda\) and for the linearized flow. Under the prescribed core-contribution calibration, the supported impact is Major for this estimate and its stated bootstrap argument.

## Counterevidence and repair limits

There is strong evidence for an intended weaker, correctly scaled result. Theorem 8 on p. 22 and (3.2), (3.6) on p. 20 have the positive half-power. The Morawetz identity in the proof also has exactly that scaling. In the unbalanced case, converting a product estimate to a differentiated-product estimate introduces one frequency, as the first display on p. 46 explicitly shows.

For the balanced estimate, the candidate replacement is \[\|\partial_x(v_\lambda\overline{v_\mu^{x_0}})\|_2
 \lesssim d_\lambda d_\mu\lambda^{1/2}(1+\lambda|x_0|).\] It agrees with the counterexample scaling and the algebra of the displayed Morawetz estimates. The bootstrap must also consistently distinguish differentiated from undifferentiated products and remove an unsupported small factor for the \(v,v\) pair. Correcting only the theorem statement does not repair the printed continuation argument.

This review does not claim that Theorems 2–4 are false. The weaker estimate already matches the bounds they use at the theorem interfaces. Nor does it certify a full variable-coefficient repair: every later occurrence in Sections 6–8 must be audited after a consistent correction. A typographical origin is plausible from the counterevidence, but does not make the quantitative assertion [bad] (#bad) valid. The recorded impact is restricted to the disproved core estimate and the invalid derivation as printed; no failure of global existence or optimal-regularity well-posedness has been demonstrated.

# Other checks and limitations

## Resonance and interpolation

With \(\xi_4=\xi_1-\xi_2+\xi_3\), \[\xi_1^2-\xi_2^2+\xi_3^2-\xi_4^2
 =-2(\xi_1-\xi_2)(\xi_3-\xi_2).\] Thus the resonance description on p. 15 follows, including the double diagonal. The alternating conjugation convention on pp. 16–17 agrees with this computation.

For the nondefocusing interpolation on p. 78, write \(F=|u_\lambda|^2\). The one-dimensional inequality \[\|F\|_{L^3_x}\lesssim
 \|F\|_{L^1_x}^{5/9}\|\partial_xF\|_{L^2_x}^{4/9}\] gives, from the energy and diagonal bilinear bounds, \[\|u_\lambda\|_{L^9_tL^6_x}
 \lesssim C\epsilon c_\lambda\lambda^{1/9-s},\qquad
 \|u_\lambda\|_{L^6_{t,x}}
 \lesssim T^{1/18}C\epsilon c_\lambda\lambda^{1/9-s}.\] On an interval \(T=\eta\epsilon^{-6}\), the ratio of the sixth power to the target \(\epsilon^4c_\lambda^4\lambda^{-4s-1}\) is at most \[\eta^{1/3}C^6c_\lambda^2\lambda^{5/3-2s}.\] For \(s>1\) the frequency exponent is negative, and choosing the interval constant sufficiently small closes this part of the bootstrap. This checks the time and frequency exponents independently, conditional on the bilinear input.

The energy accumulation on pp. 73–74 uses at most \(O(1+T\epsilon^6)\) blocks of length comparable to \(\epsilon^{-6}\). An \(O(\epsilon^4)\) error per block gives \(O(\epsilon^4+T\epsilon^{10})\), which is small relative to \(\epsilon^2\) for \(T\ll\epsilon^{-8}\). The displayed block count \(T\epsilon^{-6}\) on p. 74 has the opposite sign, but the corrected count directly restores the asserted lifespan. This isolated counting transcription is retained as a clarification, not a separate substantive finding.

For the defocusing argument, Lemma 9.2 is the stated input identifying the positive diagonal sixth-order symbol. If that input and the density-flux construction hold, its one-third-order multiplier gives the factor \(\lambda^2\) in the sixth power, converting \(\lambda^{1-4s}\) into \(\lambda^{-1-4s}\) as required by (3.5). The underlying symbol calculation, deferred to the earlier paper, was not fully reproduced.

## The introductory model and an unresolved estimate

For the model on p. 7, \[iu_t+(1+a|u|^2)u_{xx}=bu|u_x|^2+cu|u|^2,\] the cubic right-hand side after moving the metric perturbation is \(-a|u|^2u_{xx}+bu|u_x|^2+cu|u|^2\). In the paper’s Fourier convention its symmetrized symbol has diagonal \[c_{\rm model}(\xi,\xi,\xi)=(a+b)\xi^2+c.\] Thus the printed sign test \(a-b,c>0\) must be replaced by \(a+b,c>0\) for this displayed model. For example \((a,b,c)=(0,-1,1)\) passes the printed test but has diagonal \(1-\xi^2\), equal to \(-3\) at \(\xi=2\); \((0,1,1)\) has the positive diagonal \(1+\xi^2\) despite the printed focusing label. The coefficients are smooth, real where required, cubic and phase compatible, with real symbol derivatives. This is a check of the classification of a model, not a small-data counterexample to Theorem 4: the first model fails Theorem 4’s actual defocusing hypothesis. It is recorded as a local sign clarification, without an additional severity count.

A separate unresolved point is the final estimate for four balanced factors in Lemma 8.3, p. 69. The first displayed Hölder bound contains \(T^{1/2}\); direct insertion of the stated norms gives an \(\epsilon^2T^{1/2}\) factor, whereas the next displayed bound no longer contains \(T\). On \(T\ll\epsilon^{-4}\) this directly gives a small interval constant, not uniformly the claimed \(\epsilon^2\) gain. No function satisfying all the equation and \(X_\lambda\) constraints has been constructed here to refute the lemma, and no full alternative estimate has been verified. This is a Needs Evidence item for a focused follow-up, not a second confirmed defect.

The frequency-envelope construction of rough solutions on pp. 69–71 is conditional on the corrected linear estimates and the higher-regularity bounds. Its use of the square-summable envelope tail is appropriate for convergence. External high-regularity existence, continuous-dependence machinery, and all omitted source-term estimates remain dependencies.
