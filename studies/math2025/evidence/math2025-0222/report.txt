Abstract: Three issues are established in the supplied publisher online-first article of Ruiz. The central nonlinear bifurcation construction uses incompatible notions of boundary mean; this invalidates a stated Banach-space mapping, although a fixed projection repairs that step without changing the desired solutions or linearization. Two local statements also require correction: differentiability of the radial family with values in \(C^4\), and a general spherical-harmonic multiplicity formula. The latter does not alter the low-degree conclusion used in the proof. No counterexample to the principal existence theorem is established.

# The nonlinear range defect

Equation reference: eq:F. Write \(\Sigma=S^{N-1}\) with its fixed surface measure \(d\theta\), and let \(B(1+w)\) have radial boundary \((1+w(\theta))\theta\). Page 6 defines \(C^{k,\alpha}_{G,m}(\Sigma)\) by the fixed condition \(\int_\Sigma w\,d\theta=0\). Let \(q_w(\theta)\) be the outward normal derivative of the solution of \[-\rho\Delta u=u-(u^+)^3,\qquad u|_{\partial B(1+w)}=0,\] pulled back by the radial identification used on pp. 16–17. That identification has surface density \[J_w=(1+w)^{N-1}
 \sqrt{1+\frac{|\nabla_\Sigma w|^2}{(1+w)^2}}.\] Equation (4.4), p. 17, defines \[
F(\rho,w)=q_w-\frac{\int_\Sigma q_wJ_w\,d\theta}{\int_\Sigma J_w\,d\theta}.\] Thus \(\int_\Sigma FJ_w=0\), but this is not the fixed mean-zero condition.

Problem 1 (Major: the map used for inversion has the wrong range).  On p. 23, the proof of Theorem 2.1 asserts that \[S_\rho:w\longmapsto w-F(\rho,w)\] maps a neighborhood of zero in \(C^{2,\alpha}_{G,m}(\Sigma)\) into \(C^{1,\alpha}_{G,m}(\Sigma)\), and applies the inverse function theorem there. Formula [eq:F] (#eq:F) does not define such a map.

## A check within the actual PDE family

Equation reference: eq:mean. This is not just an example using an arbitrary boundary function. Put \(w=\varepsilon h\), where \(h\) is a nonzero smooth \(G\)-invariant function of fixed mean zero. Proposition 4.3 and (3.4), on pp. 17 and 9, give \[F(\rho,\varepsilon h)=-c_\rho\varepsilon H_\rho h+o(\varepsilon),
 \qquad c_\rho=u_\rho'(1)>0.\] Since \(J_{\varepsilon h}=1+(N-1)\varepsilon h+O(\varepsilon^2)\), the exact weighted-mean identity gives \[\begin{aligned}
\int_\Sigma F(\rho,\varepsilon h)\,d\theta
&=-\int_\Sigma F(\rho,\varepsilon h)(J_{\varepsilon h}-1)\,d\theta \notag\\
&=(N-1)c_\rho\varepsilon^2
       \int_\Sigma hH_\rho h\,d\theta+o(\varepsilon^2).
\end{aligned}\] Only the stated first differentiability of \(F\) is needed for this expansion.

Choose any \[\max\{\rho_0,\sigma^{-1}\}<\rho<\bar\lambda_2^{-1},\] an available interval because assumption (G) gives \(\sigma>\bar\lambda_2\). For the extension \(\psi_{\rho,h}\) of Proposition 3.4, Lemma 2.3 and (5.2)–(5.3), pp. 8 and 19, imply \[\rho\int_\Sigma hH_\rho h
 =Q_\rho(\psi_{\rho,h})
 \geq(\rho\sigma-1)\int_B\psi_{\rho,h}^2
       +(N-1)\rho\int_\Sigma h^2>0.\] For a concrete allowed symmetry, take \(N=2\), \(G=D_5\) and \(h(\theta)=\pi^{-1/2}\cos(5\theta)\). All symmetry, smoothness and mean conditions hold. For sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\), the domain remains valid and Proposition 4.1 supplies the actual solution. Equation [eq:mean] (#eq:mean) is strictly positive. Therefore \(\int_\Sigma S_\rho(\varepsilon h)\neq0\) arbitrarily close to the base point.

## Repair, counterevidence and scope

Let \(P_0f=f-|\Sigma|^{-1}\int_\Sigma f\) and replace \(F\) by \[\widehat F(\rho,w)=P_0q_w=P_0F(\rho,w).\] This lies in the fixed mean-zero space. Moreover, \[\widehat F=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ q_w\text{ is constant}
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ F=0.\] Equation (4.6) states that \(H_\rho\) preserves fixed mean zero, so \(D_w\widehat F(\rho,0)=-c_\rho H_\rho\) on that space. Consequently \(\widehat S_\rho=w-\widehat F(\rho,w)\) has the range and linearization needed for the displayed inversion. The proposed correction changes neither the target overdetermined condition nor the spectrum used in the parity argument.

The linearization at the round ball was not itself contradicted: it misses the discrepancy, which starts at second order. Nor can a moving weighted function space silently resolve the printed proof, since p. 6 specifies fixed spaces and pp. 16–17 specify a radial pullback. The classification is Major because a core construction in the proof of Theorems 2.1 and 1.1 is not defined as claimed. A short repair and a plausible surviving theorem do not make that original construction valid. The verified repair is local to this defect; it is not an independent validation of the entire bifurcation proof.

# Regularity of the parameterized radial family

Problem 2 (Minor: \(C^1\) dependence with values in \(C^4\) fails).  Proposition 3.2(b), p. 12, asserts that \(U:(0,\bar\lambda_2^{-1})\to C^4(\overline B)\), \(U(\rho)=u_\rho\), is \(C^1\). The one-line inverse-function justification on p. 13 does not establish this regularity, and the claim is false at an explicit admissible parameter.

## Decisive nodal calculation

Take \(N=3\). The second radial Dirichlet eigenvalue is \(\bar\lambda_2=4\pi^2\). On the negative annulus \(p_\rho<r<1\), the function \(y(r)=r u_\rho(r)\) solves \[y''+\rho^{-1}y=0,\qquad y(p_\rho)=y(1)=0.\] Proposition 3.1(a) specifies exactly one interior node and a negative outer annulus. Hence \[p_\rho=1-\pi\sqrt\rho.\] At \(\rho_*=(16\pi^2)^{-1}\), one has \(p_{\rho_*}=3/4\) and \(p'_{\rho_*}=-2\pi^2\). The nodal slope \(a=u_{\rho_*}'(3/4)\) is negative and nonzero: zero value and zero slope would force the outer ODE solution to vanish identically.

Equation reference: eq:diff. If \(U\) were differentiable into \(C^4\), its derivative \(v=\partial_\rho u_\rho|_{\rho_*}\) would belong to \(C^4\) and differentiation of the nodal identity would give \[v(3/4)=-a p'_{\rho_*}=2\pi^2a\neq0.\] Differentiating the PDE would give \[
 -\Delta u_{\rho_*}-\rho_*\Delta v
   =\bigl(1-3(u_{\rho_*}^+)^2\bigr)v.\] The left side is \(C^2\) near \(r=3/4\). On the right, the second radial derivative has a jump of magnitude \(|6a^2v(3/4)|>0\): the contribution from \(-3(u^+)^2v\) on the positive side tends to \(-6a^2v(3/4)\), and on the negative side it is zero. This contradicts [eq:diff] (#eq:diff). The example satisfies the stated dimension, parameter interval and nodal conditions.

## A sufficient lower-regularity correction

For any fixed \(0<\alpha<1\), the defensible statement is local \(C^1\) dependence into \(C^{2,\alpha}_{0,r}(\overline B)\). Indeed, the map \[(\rho,u)\longmapsto-\rho\Delta u-u+(u^+)^3\] is \(C^1\) from the corresponding parameter–Hölder space into \(C^{0,\alpha}_r\). Its derivative in \(u\) is the radial linearized operator of (3.7), which is invertible by Proposition 3.2(a) and Schauder theory. The implicit function theorem, together with uniqueness of the radial family, gives that correction throughout the interval. It does not give a \(C^4\)-valued derivative.

Individual solutions may still be \(C^{4,\alpha}\), as stated in Proposition 3.1. That fact does not imply the stronger parameter regularity. The \(C^{2,\alpha}\) formulation is compatible with the normal-derivative construction in Section 4. Accordingly, this overstatement is classified as a local Minor issue; no additional failure of the existence conclusion is inferred.

# The four-dimensional multiplicity formula

Problem 3 (Minor: the character average needs to be squared).  The formula for every even degree \(i\) in Section 7.3, p. 27, equals \[\begin{aligned}
 a_i=\frac1{60}\bigg[&
 i+1+20\frac{\sin((i+1)\pi/3)}{\sin(\pi/3)}
 +12\frac{\sin((i+1)\pi/5)}{\sin(\pi/5)}\\
 &+12\frac{\sin(2(i+1)\pi/5)}{\sin(2\pi/5)}
 +15\frac{\sin((i+1)\pi/2)}{\sin(\pi/2)}
 \bigg].
\end{aligned}\] For the full rotation group of the 600-cell specified on p. 26, the required invariant multiplicity is \(a_i^2\), not \(a_i\).

## Representation-theoretic verification

Let \(Y'=2I\) be the binary icosahedral group and \(V_i=\operatorname{Sym}^i(\mathbb C^2)\). The full group and its action on spherical harmonics are \[G=(Y'\times Y')/\{(1,1),(-1,-1)\},\qquad
 \mathcal H_i(S^3;\mathbb C)\simeq V_i\otimes V_i^*.\] Thus \[\dim\mathcal H_i^G
   =\dim V_i^{Y'}\cdot\dim(V_i^*)^{Y'}
   =(\dim V_i^{Y'})^2.\] For even \(i\), the single-factor character average is exactly \(a_i\); for odd \(i\), the central element \(-1\) excludes invariants. This is also explicit in the article’s own reference [21]: Nelson–Widom [nw], printed pp. 123–124, equations (2.37), (2.42)–(2.48). In particular, their (2.47) contains the square before (2.48) gives the single-factor average.

Set \(i=60\), an allowed even degree. Each nonidentity character ratio in the displayed average equals 1, giving \[a_{60}=(61+20+12+12+15)/60=2,\qquad
 \dim\mathcal H_{60}^G=4.\] This exact example distinguishes the claimed dimension from the correct one. The correction is \(m(i)=a_i^2\) for even \(i\) and \(m(i)=0\) for odd \(i\); the factor \(1/60\) must be squared along with the bracket.

The values needed for (G) remain unchanged: \(a_2=a_4=a_6=a_8=a_{10}=0\) and \(a_{12}=1\). Consequently the first nontrivial invariant degree remains 12 with multiplicity one. This is a Minor error in an unnecessary general formula, not a disproof of the four-dimensional application.

# Surviving checks and remaining dependencies

The radial Morse-index argument in Proposition 3.2(a) uses the negative part \(u_\rho^-\), not \(u_\rho\): its zero quadratic-form value and lack of \(C^1\) regularity force a negative eigenvalue. If the second radial eigenvalue were nonpositive, a linear combination of the first two modes vanishing at \(p_\rho\) would have nonpositive form. The inner form is strictly positive unless the function vanishes, whereas the outer annular form is nonnegative with kernel proportional to \(u_\rho\). Matching at the node then gives a contradiction. This mechanism survives Problem 2.

The upper-parameter positive sign of the nonradial form follows from \(\rho\sigma>1\) as above. The Appendix’s low-degree invariant multiplicities were checked for the dihedral, icosahedral and 600-cell groups; the generally incorrect high-degree expression is not needed there. The sign-change and parity framework still depends on the analytic eigenvalue arguments, unique continuation, and the stated Krasnosel’skiı̆ theorem. Those imported results and every regularity assertion in their applications have not been independently certified. No author code or computer algebra notebook was executed.
