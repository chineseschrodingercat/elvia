# Checks on the equations and the new estimates

Put \(u=(-\partial_y\varphi,\partial_x\varphi)\) and \(\omega=\Delta\varphi\). Taking the divergence of the perturbation momentum equation gives \[\Delta\Pi+\operatorname{div}(d\nabla\Pi)+q=0,\qquad
 q=\operatorname{div}(u\cdot\nabla u)+2\partial_xu^2.\] Direct differentiation yields \[\begin{aligned}
 q&=2(\varphi_{xy})^2-2\varphi_{xx}\varphi_{yy}+2\varphi_{xx}\\
  &=2\varphi_{xx}(1-\omega)+2(\varphi_{xx})^2+2(\varphi_{xy})^2.\end{aligned}\] These are the source terms on p. 5. Likewise, \[\operatorname{curl}\bigl((1+d)\nabla\Pi\bigr)
       =d_x\Pi_y-d_y\Pi_x,\] which has the sign used in (1.3). Both identities were independently evaluated symbolically, with zero residuals; no author code was used.

For \(z=x-tv(t,y)\), write \(V=\partial_yv=1+V_1\) and \(D_L=\partial_v-t\partial_z\). Then \[\partial_x=\partial_z,\qquad \partial_y=VD_L,\qquad
 \Delta=\partial_z^2+V^2D_L^2+V(\partial_vV)D_L.\] Consequently \(V_2=V\partial_vV\), as in (2.7), and the transformed elliptic operator in (2.6) and (2.8) is correct. The cancellation of the \(t\partial_z\) terms in the baroclinic bracket gives the factor \(V\) in (2.2). The identity \(H=-V_1-P_0f\) then yields (2.4)–(2.5) with the displayed signs.

The pressure analysis separates the zero mode before dividing by a horizontal frequency. For nonzero \(k\), the constant-coefficient symbol is \[D=k^2+(\xi-kt)^2=k^2\langle t-\xi/k\rangle^2.\] The toy inversion on pp. 5–6 therefore has absolute multiplier \(k^2/D^2\). Its sign is immaterial to the stated energy bounds. The weighted elliptic absorption in Lemma 3.5 uses the small factors \(V_1,V_1^2,V_2\) from Lemma 3.2. The mean pressure is instead recovered from the averaged vertical momentum equation, (3.60)–(3.61), and controlled by Lemmas 3.7–3.8. This avoids an unjustified division by \(k=0\). The coupling terms involving \(a\) are absorbed using Lemma 3.4 and the smallness of \(\epsilon_1\). The final conclusion is conditional on the multiplier estimates cited there: \[E_P+B_P\lesssim_\delta \epsilon_1^4+E_\Theta+B_\Theta.\] No counterexample to this pressure estimate was established.

For the density, write \[A_k^*=A_k b,\qquad
 b(t)=\left(1+\frac{Q}{1+t^2}\right)^{1/2},
 \qquad Q=k^2+|\xi|\ge0.\] The exact logarithmic derivative is \[\frac{\partial_tb}{b}
 =-\frac{tQ}{(1+t^2)(1+t^2+Q)}.\] Thus the additional factor is nonincreasing for \(t\ge0\) and has the sign required by (6.1) and (A.28). The comparison with the existing time weight requires the small exponent \(\sigma_0\) and the frequency bounds in (A.29); it is not a claim that the extra derivative vanishes. The three terms \(N_6,N_7,N_8\) are handled through separate commutator estimates C.1, C.3 and C.4–C.5. The low-frequency factor \(|\rho|\) in C.5 is relevant because \(V_3\) has no compact support. These dependencies were retained rather than replaced by a numerical test of a few frequencies.

# The mean mode in the bootstrap condition

Actual inspection of p. 7 confirms that Proposition 2.1 assumes \[\|\overline{\omega}(t)\|_{H^{10}}\ll1
 \quad\hbox{for all }t\in[0,T],
 \tag{1}\] where the overbar denotes the horizontal average defined on p. 4. It does not assume uniform smallness of the full Eulerian vorticity in \(H^{10}\). The text extraction lost this overbar. A preliminary concern based on that extraction was withdrawn after inspecting the original page, including a higher-resolution view. The superseded TeX, compiled PDF and genuine build records are retained in the per-paper history; they were never marked delivery complete.

Write \(f_0(t,v)=P_0f(t,v)\). Since the change of the horizontal variable is a translation for fixed \(y\), \[\overline{\omega}(t,y)=f_0(t,v(t,y)),\qquad
 \partial_yv=1+V_1(t,v(t,y)).
 \tag{2}\] The weighted profile energy controls \(f_0\) in a fixed Gevrey norm, hence in \(H^{10}\) and the finitely many bounded-derivative norms needed for composition. The same is true of \(V_1\). For small \(\epsilon_1\), the Jacobian \(\partial_yv\) is bounded above and below away from zero. Repeated differentiation of (2) gives, at each order at most ten, a finite sum of products of derivatives of \(f_0\) and derivatives of \(v\). Derivatives of \(v\) beyond the first are bounded recursively by derivatives of \(V_1\); there is no factor \(t\partial_z\) on the zero mode. Place the derivative of \(f_0\) in \(L^2\), the remaining factors in \(L^\infty\), and change variables using the bounded Jacobian. This gives \[\|\overline{\omega}(t)\|_{H^{10}}
       \le C_{\delta_0}\epsilon_1.
 \tag{3}\] Choosing \(\epsilon_1\) small closes this particular auxiliary hypothesis. This verifies the mean-mode point; it is not a replacement for every other continuation and multiplier estimate.

The independent linear diagnostic was retained because it explains why the overbar matters. For \[w(t,x,y)=\varepsilon\cos(x-ty)e^{-y^2/2},\] the equation \(\partial_tw+y\partial_xw=0\) holds exactly, the sheared profile is constant, and the horizontal mean is identically zero. After normalization on one Fourier mode, the squared tenth vertical derivative norm is \[\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_\mathbb R(\eta-t)^{20}e^{-\eta^2}\,d\eta
 =
 \sum_{j=0}^{10}\binom{20}{2j}\frac{(2j)!}{4^j j!}\,t^{20-2j}.\] The leading coefficient is one, so the full derivative norm grows as \(t^{10}\), while the mean norm in (1) stays zero. Symbolic computation verified the residual and polynomial. This is a linear coordinate-norm diagnostic, not a nonlinear Euler counterexample and not evidence against the actual proposition.

# Asymptotics and limits of the conclusion

The pressure decay, the decay of \(\phi_{\ne0}\), and the bounds on \(V_3\) provide integrable right-hand sides in the profile equations (2.36) and (2.3), giving the expected \(\langle t\rangle^{-1}\) scattering tails after a Gevrey radius loss, conditional on the global bootstrap.

For the mean velocity, retain the stronger \(\langle t\rangle^{-3}\) estimates on \(F_1,F_2\) displayed on p. 9. The uniform composition bounds in (2.33), with a fixed Gevrey radius loss, preserve this time decay. Thus \(\partial_t\overline u^1=O(\epsilon_1^2\langle t\rangle^{-3})\), which integrates to an \(O(\epsilon_1^2\langle t\rangle^{-2})\) limiting-shear tail. Also, (2.31) is \[\partial_tv(t,y)=\frac1{t^2}
                 \int_0^t s\,\partial_s\overline u^1(s,y)\,ds.\] The weighted integral is uniformly bounded because \(\int_0^\infty s\langle s\rangle^{-3}\,ds<\infty\). This gives the asserted \(t^{-2}\) coordinate derivative for large time. The weaker bound printed in (2.34), used alone, would not suffice, but the preceding stronger estimates provide the needed rate. No separate substantive defect is inferred from that weakening.
