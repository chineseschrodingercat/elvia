Abstract: This claim-focused review identifies a Major mismatch between the general biset definition and the faithfulness needed by the principal Liouville conclusion, and a Minor missing metric restriction in Proposition 5.6. Explicit counterexamples verify the written hypotheses. Neither refutes the intended faithful-group theorem with a metric in the specified conformal gauge, or the iterated-monodromy applications. A separate basepoint argument is checked and repaired. Initial delivery does not constitute scientific acceptance.

Title: Initial review: Liouville property for groups and conformal dimension

# Identity, scope, and conclusions

The reviewed work is by Nicolás Matte Bon, Volodymyr Nekrashevych, and Tianyi Zheng, Liouville property for groups and conformal dimension, M25-0098. The bound source is arXiv: 2305.14545v4, submitted 5 August 2025, with PDF dated 6 August 2025, 42 pages. Page references below are PDF pages, agreeing with printed pagination. DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01360-x identifies the associated journal article; equality with the journal version has not been established. These findings belong only to the bound author version.

Theorem 1.1 (p. 2) asserts that a finitely generated contracting self-similar group whose limit space has Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension \(\alpha<2\) has the Liouville property for every symmetric measure with finite \(\beta\)-moment for some \(\beta>\alpha\). Theorem 1.3 (p. 3) gives \[\operatorname{Cr}_{\rm Liouv}(G)\le p_c(G,\mathfrak B)\le\operatorname{ARdim}\mathcal J_G.\] The audit followed definitions, contraction coefficients, portrait estimates, tile overlap, capacities, entropy encoding, long-jump decomposition, and applications. All 42 pages were read as text; selected original pages were also inspected as images, as listed in the coverage record. Reading references is not independent verification of all cited theorems.

The decisive distinctions are between an abstract self-similar biset and a faithful group of tree automorphisms, and between an arbitrary Ahlfors-regular metric and one quasisymmetric to a visual metric. Both appear in the preliminaries but are lost in statements used later.

# Problem 1: faithfulness missing from the principal scope (Major)

## Location and source claim

Theorem 1.1, p. 2; Theorem 1.3, p. 3; the general definition in §2.1, pp. 7–8; portrait determination on p. 19; Proposition 5.5, p. 26; Theorem 5.9 and Corollary 5.10, pp. 29–32. The definition on p. 7 allows a \(G\)-biset with free right action and finitely many right orbits. Page 8 defines the faithful quotient separately. No global faithfulness requirement was found in the full text or complete source search. Proposition 5.5, in contrast, explicitly assumes a group of rooted-tree automorphisms.

## Counterexample and all hypotheses

Let \(G=F(a,b)\) be free of rank two, \(X=\{0,1\}\), and \[\mathfrak B=X\times G,\qquad
 (x,h)\cdot k=(x,hk),\qquad g\cdot(x,h)=(x,h).\] The actions commute; the right action is free and has two orbits. This is a self-similar group in the explicitly stated biset sense. For the basis \(\{(0,1),(1,1)\}\) its wreath recursion sends every \(g\) to \((\operatorname{id};1,1)\). Every nonempty section is the identity. The group is finitely generated and contracting with nucleus \(\mathcal N=\{1\}\). Its associated tree action and faithful quotient are trivial. For all \(p>0\) and \(n\ge1\), \(\eta_{p,n}=0\), hence \(\eta_p=0\) and \(p_c=0\).

Asymptotic equivalence on \(X^{-\omega}\) identifies only equal sequences, since the nucleus is trivial. Thus \(\mathcal J_G\) is binary Cantor space. If \(L(\xi,\zeta)\) is common terminal-prefix length, then for any \(s>0\), \[d_s(\xi,\zeta)=e^{-sL(\xi,\zeta)}\] off the diagonal is a visual ultrametric. Bernoulli measure has mass \(2^{-n}\) on length-\(n\) cylinders, giving Ahlfors regularity of dimension \(\log2/s\). These metrics are positive powers of each other, hence quasisymmetric. Therefore \(\operatorname{ARdim}\mathcal J_G=0<2\).

Let \(\mu\) be uniform on \(a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\). It is symmetric, generating, finitely supported, and has every positive word-length moment. Its Cayley graph is the four-regular tree. The probability of returning to the root from a neighbor is \(q=1/3\): radial distance has probabilities \(1/4\) towards and \(3/4\) away from the root, and the decaying hitting-probability solution is \(q^r\). The walk is transient and has an almost sure limiting end.

Let \(f(g)\) be the probability that this end lies in the branch beginning with \(a\). The Markov property makes \(f\) bounded and \(\mu\)-harmonic. Symmetry and the return probability give \[f(1)=\tfrac14,\qquad
 f(a)=1-\tfrac13+\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac34.\] At depth \(r\) in that branch, \(f=1-\frac34\,3^{-r}\); in another branch, \(f=\frac14\,3^{-r}\). The formulas satisfy the mean-value equation at every vertex, including the root. Thus \(f\) is nonconstant on \(\langle\operatorname{supp}\mu\rangle=G\), violating exactly the stated Liouville definition. Amenability also fails: a nonempty finite vertex set \(F\) has at most \(|F|-1\) internal tree edges and at least \(2|F|+2\) boundary edges.

## Proof obstruction, counterevidence, and repair

Tree permutations and sections determine an element of a faithful group, but need not determine an element of an abstract nonfaithful biset group. Here every element has identical trivial positive-level data. Proposition 5.5 therefore controls the image group only; it cannot bound the entropy of the original free group. The assertion after Proposition 4.5 that labeled portraits uniquely determine \(g\) has the same obstruction.

The preliminaries begin with a faithful action before giving the general biset definition. Conventional informal usage of self-similar group often means a faithful automorphism group. Section 6.1 explicitly says faithful, and iterated monodromy groups are defined as faithful quotients. These are strong indications of the intended meaning, but do not add the missing condition to the broader written definition used in Theorems 1.1 and 1.3.

A precise repair is to require the associated action on \(X^*\) to be faithful in the Liouville, entropy, and growth statements, or to state those conclusions for the faithful quotient with its induced measure. The latter does not imply the original claim for \(G\): the kernel can be the entire free group. Further kernel assumptions would require another theorem. This is Major because the central universal guarantee is false under the printed definition, even if repair is a short scope restriction. It does not refute the intended faithful theorem or Corollary 6.4 for iterated monodromy groups.

# Problem 2: regularity alone does not give exponential energy decay (Minor)

## Location and missing hypothesis

Equation reference: eq:energy. Proposition 5.6, p. 27, assumes only an Ahlfors-regular metric \(d\) on \(\mathcal J_G\) of Hausdorff dimension \(\alpha\), together with \(\beta>\alpha\) and \(p>p_c\). It concludes, uniformly in \(g\) and the left-infinite tail, \[
 \sum_{v\in X^n}d(\ldots x_2x_1g(v),\ldots x_2x_1v)^\beta
 \le C\lambda^n l(g)^p,\qquad 0<\lambda<1.\] Its proof uses Proposition 3.8, p. 16, which requires a quasi-conformal metric. Ahlfors regularity alone does not imply this.

## Faithful counterexample

Use the faithful binary adding machine \(G=\mathbb Z=\langle a\rangle\), with \(a=\sigma(1,a)\) and generating set \(\{a,a^{-1}\}\). It is contracting with nucleus \(\{1,a,a^{-1}\}\) and limit circle. At level \(n\), the section exponents of \(a^k\) are \(\lfloor(k+j)/2^n\rfloor\), \(0\le j<2^n\), up to permutation. Letting \(|k|\to\infty\) proves \[\eta_{p,n}=2^{n(1/p-1)},\qquad \eta_p=2^{1/p-1},\qquad p_c=1.\] On \([0,1]/(0=1)\) with ordinary arc metric \(d_0\), define a circle homeomorphism by \[h(0)=0,\qquad h(x)=\frac1{1-\log_2x}\ (0<x\le\tfrac12),
 \qquad h(x)=x\ (\tfrac12\le x\le1).\] It is continuous and strictly increasing, with matching formulas at \(1/2\) and the identified endpoints. The metric \(d(x,y)=d_0(h(x),h(y))\) is isometric to the ordinary circle. Its Hausdorff measure is the pullback of arc length, so it is Ahlfors regular with \(\alpha=1\) and the original topology.

Set \(\beta=p=2\), \(g=a\), and take the zero left-infinite tail. The word \(0^n\) represents \(0\) and its image \(10^{n-1}\) represents \(2^{-n}\) in the limit circle. For \(n\ge2\), \[d(0,2^{-n})=\frac1{n+1}.\] A single summand in [eq:energy] (#eq:energy) is \((n+1)^{-2}\). Because \(l(a)=1\), the claimed estimate would imply \((n+1)^{-2}\le C\lambda^n\) for all \(n\), impossible since the logarithm of this ratio divided by \(n\) tends to \(-\log\lambda>0\).

All printed hypotheses hold, including faithfulness, \(\beta>\alpha\), and \(p>p_c\). The metric is not quasisymmetric to a visual circle metric: the points \(\varepsilon\) and \(1-\varepsilon\), equally far from \(0\) in \(d_0\), have \(d\)-distance ratio comparable to \(1/(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon))\).

## Counterevidence, repair, and impact

Add the quasi-conformal hypothesis to obtain exactly the input required by Proposition 3.8. In Theorem 5.7, choosing \(\alpha<\beta\) from the definition of \(\operatorname{ARdim}\) already permits a metric in this conformal gauge. The repair is therefore available in the core application. This is Minor: it falsifies an overbroad auxiliary statement, without showing another failure of the intended core theorem.

# Proof clarifications and positive checks

## Basepoint change in Corollary 5.8

The proof’s opening paragraph (p. 29; author TeX line 1405) asserts that contraction makes the word sets \(U_{z,n}\) exactly independent of basepoint for all sufficiently large \(n\). Convergence of locations does not imply equality of membership in a fixed neighborhood.

Use the adding-machine model on \(\mathbb R\), with translations by \(\mathbb Z\) and \(I_x(\xi)=(\xi+x)/2\), \(x=0,1\). Give the graph vertices \(\frac12\mathbb Z\) and edges length one. The quotient has two vertices and circumference two, with no adjacent vertices in the same orbit. The radius-\(1/3\) neighborhood of \(0\) is the ordinary circle interval of radius \(1/6\). Level-\(n\) images from basepoints \(0\) and \(1\) are respectively \[j/2^n,\quad (j+1)/2^n,\qquad 0\le j<2^n.\] The word sets differ for every \(n\ge3\): a nonempty proper cyclic interval is not invariant under a one-step cyclic shift. Exact rational checks for \(3\le n\le12\) give symmetric difference two; the interval argument proves the infinite statement.

A complete repair preserves the capacity conclusion. Enlarge each neighborhood to a closed ball of radius \(5/12\). Balls about distinct vertices remain disjoint since centers have distance at least one. Uniform contraction makes images of two fixed basepoints less than \(1/12\) apart at large levels. The original sets are thus contained in the enlarged sets computed from a basepoint in the image of \(\mathcal X_G\). The enlarged sets are preimages of disjoint closed subsets of \(\mathcal J_G\), so Theorem 5.7 applies. Capacity is monotone increasing in either prescribed plate; it bounds the smaller original plates. Finitely many earlier levels are absorbed into the constant. The main theorem can alternatively choose the convenient basepoint from the outset. This is a verified proof clarification with a complete repair, not an additional counterexample to the core theorem.

## Contraction, dimension, and entropy

For Proposition 4.3 (pp. 18–19), enlarge the generators to contain the nucleus and all generator sections. Truncated length vanishes below a threshold; block iteration gives exponential decay, and a bounded number of extra levels moves every short section into the nucleus. This checks uniformity in \(g,n\). Counting nonnucleus sections extends the portrait estimate to \(p>0\); the identity must be treated separately or the root bound use \(1+l(g)^p\). The printed \(p\ge1\) in Proposition 4.5 alone does not justify its later \(p<1\) use, but Proposition 4.3 supplies the same counting argument.

For Theorem 4.6 (pp. 19–21), the essential factor is \(M_n^{\beta-\alpha}\) from tile diameter decay and \(\beta>\alpha\). Summing over \(m\) increments gives \(m^\beta\), not the intermediate displayed \(m^{\beta-1}\) on p. 20; the final bound already has the correct \(m^\beta\) order. Tile overlap is applied after permuting suffixes by partial products. First take \(l(g)\to\infty\) to remove the additive level-dependent term, then choose a block with coefficient below one and use submultiplicativity. No interchange of these limits is required.

For Proposition 5.6 with the repaired metric hypothesis, choose \[0<\varepsilon<\frac{-\log\eta}{\log|X|-\log\eta},
 \qquad |X|^\varepsilon\eta^{1-\varepsilon}<1.\] This controls short suffixes; bounded overlap and exponential tile decay control long suffixes. In Theorem 5.9, capacity decay bounds expected traverses; large jumps are pulled back a fixed number \(k\) of levels and bounded using sections outside the nucleus and the finite \(p\)-moment. The resulting estimate is \[\mathbb E\sum_{v\in X^n}l(g_t|_v)
 \le C|X|^n+C'(\lambda^n+\eta^{n-k})t.\] The entropy argument takes \(t\to\infty\) first, then \(n\to\infty\), for faithful actions. The \(p_c<1\) case uses a smaller exponent below one and the cited growth/entropy theorem, not independently reproved here. The literal traverse definition on p. 13 omits the first intermediate time from its exclusion condition. Correcting that index, or allowing the resulting bounded multiplicity in the expectation estimate, preserves the bound; the exact event identity needs the corrected convention.

## Boundary cases and examples

The Liouville definition uses constancy on the support-generated subgroup, so the identity-supported measure is not a counterexample. Symmetry is needed for the counting-measure Dirichlet form. A length-\(N\) cycle with nearest-neighbor probabilities \(1/2\) has antipodal capacity \(2/N\), consistent with the ordered-energy factor \(1/2\); this was also checked exactly.

The Thurston matrix on p. 23 has eigenvalues \(1,-1/2,(-1\pm i\sqrt7)/4\), giving spectral radius one. The carpet inequality \(2\cdot3^{1-p}<1\) gives \(p>\log6/\log3\); lower and upper bounds are approximately \(1.63093\) and \(1.89279\). The intermediate printed sign does not change the correct final bound.

In Proposition 6.5 (pp. 39–40), the projected masses printed on p. 40 sum to \(8/9\). For the uniform measure on nine generators, the corrected masses are \(1/9\) on each of \(a_0,\ldots,a_7,1\): one fragmented generator acts as \(a_0\) and the other as the identity at an orbit point, with both fixing the distinguished point when appropriate. This restores a probability measure and the same transient comparison. The normalization error alone is not scored as substantive. Nontriviality of the limiting tail event and the complete topological identification were not independently reconstructed.

Corollary 6.4 uses a finitely generated section-closed enlargement of the given subgroup. Each generator has finitely many sections by contraction; adjoining the common nucleus preserves contraction and the limit space. Ambient word length is bounded by a constant times subgroup word length, preserving the finite second moment. The cited quasiconformal conjugacy and strict Julia dimension bounds remain external dependencies.
