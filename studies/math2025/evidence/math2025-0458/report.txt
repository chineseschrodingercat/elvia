Abstract: An explicit four-variable system disproves the strengthened a priori support assertion in Theorem 3.6 and the corresponding full-dimensional degree bound in Corollary 3.7. The permitted positive truncation degree removes both contradictory equations, while the remaining Macaulay row has a finite tropical kernel vector. This is one Major issue affecting a principal degree-bound consequence. The actual-support Nullstellensatz construction is not refuted. Its diagonal certificate, a feasible example, and the two-sided parametric certificate withstand exact independent checks. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Source and mathematical conventions

The reviewed source is M. Akian, A. Béreau and S. Gaubert, The Nullstellensatz and Positivstellensatz for Sparse Tropical Polynomial Systems, arXiv:2312.05859v2, 21 October 2024, 35 PDF pages. Physical and printed page numbers coincide. The assigned journal DOI is https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-025-09708-8. The publisher lists publication online on 1 December 2025 and volume 26 (2026), pp. 1587–1633. No identity of the acquired preprint and journal mathematical contents is assumed.

The paper uses max-plus arithmetic: addition is maximum, multiplication is ordinary addition, and the tropical zero is \(-\infty\). A finite point \(x\in\mathbb R^n\) is a tropical root when at least two distinct monomials attain the maximum. The principal results are toric; solutions with \(-\infty\) coordinates are discussed separately in Remark 3.10. Throughout this report, a displayed ordinary scalar zero is the real number \(0\), not \(-\infty\).

For a column set \(E\) and prescribed supports \(A_i\), Definition 3.3 retains a Macaulay row \((i,\alpha)\) precisely when \(\alpha+A_i\subseteq E\). Its coefficients come from \(X^\alpha f_i\), including \(-\infty\) at absent monomials. This row-selection condition is distinct from using the smaller actual supports of the \(f_i\). Theorem 3.5 requires \(E'\) to contain a nonempty Canny–Emiris set for the actual Newton polytopes. For \(E'=E\), its alternative kernel condition allows some \(-\infty\) coordinates but excludes the all-\(-\infty\) vector. Theorem 4.1 uses support-dependent multiplicities for two-sided relations; Theorem 4.15 combines them with tropical root equations.

# Problem 1: full dimension does not justify the smaller truncation

Assessment: Major; a false strengthened theorem and degree-bound consequence. The final assertion of Theorem 3.6 (p. 8) says that equality of the dimensions of the actual and a priori Newton sums permits replacing the full a priori lattice set by any nonempty Canny–Emiris set of the a priori supports. Corollary 3.7 then claims the bound \(N\ge\sum_i d_i-n\) whenever the actual Newton sum is full-dimensional. Both assertions fail in the following example.

## All hypotheses and the finite kernel witness

Let \(n=4,k=3\), and take ordinary tropical polynomials with functions \[f_1(x)=\max\{0,2x_1\},\qquad
 f_2(x)=\max\{2,2x_1\},\qquad
 f_3(x)=\max\{0,x_2,x_3,x_4\}.\] Their degrees are \(d_1=2,d_2=2,d_3=1\). A root of \(f_1\) must have \(x_1=0\), and a root of \(f_2\) must have \(x_1=1\). Hence there is no common root in \(\mathbb R^4\), independently of \(f_3\). There are no monomial or zero-polynomial equations in this example.

Writing \(e_i\) for the coordinate vectors, the actual Newton sum is \[Q_{\rm act}=[0,4e_1]+\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_2,e_3,e_4\}.\] It has dimension four: \(2e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) are independent directions. Thus the full-dimensional hypothesis is satisfied. With the paper’s degree-based a priori supports \[A_1=A_2=2\Delta_4\cap\mathbb Z^4,\qquad
 A_3=\Delta_4\cap\mathbb Z^4,\qquad
 Q_{\rm prior}=5\Delta_4,\] the a priori Newton sum also has dimension four.

The strengthened degree bound allows \[N=2+2+1-4=1,\qquad
 E=\Delta_4\cap\mathbb Z^4=\{0,e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\}.\] This is a positive integer cutoff and a nonempty column set. It is also a Canny–Emiris set for \(Q_{\rm prior}\): choose a generic \(\delta=(-1+\varepsilon_1,\ldots,-1+\varepsilon_4)\) with \(0<\varepsilon_i<1/5\). The integer points in \(5\Delta_4+\delta\) have nonnegative coordinates and total degree at most one, so they are exactly \(E\). Generic choices exist in this open box and do not change \(E\).

No translate of \(A_1\) or \(A_2\) fits in \(E\): their width in each coordinate is two, while \(E\) has width one. The only retained row is \(f_3\), with shift \(\alpha=0\). In the indicated column order, \[M_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\infty&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.\] The vector \(y=(0,0,0,0,0)\in\mathbb R^5\) belongs to its tropical kernel, since the maximum is attained four times. Thus the linearized problem is feasible although the polynomial system is infeasible. This contradicts both the last assertion of Theorem 3.6 and the full-dimensional clause of Corollary 3.7. Allowing Laurent shifts does not help: coordinate widths are translation invariant.

## Why the proof does not transfer

The Canny–Emiris set for the actual support need not be nonempty under a shift that gives a nonempty a priori set, even when both Newton sums have the same dimension. For every shift in the open box above, an integer point of \(Q_{\rm act}+\delta\) would have nonnegative coordinates, but its last three coordinates would have to satisfy \[q_2+q_3+q_4
 =(\beta_2+1-\varepsilon_2)+(\beta_3+1-\varepsilon_3)
   +(\beta_4+1-\varepsilon_4)>12/5>1,\] contrary to the definition of \(Q_{\rm act}\). The actual shifted lattice set is therefore empty. Theorem 3.5 cannot supply its nonempty certificate on this set. Dimension equality alone does not supply the submatrix required by the strengthened transfer on p. 8.

The example does not rely on a missing genericity choice, an empty a priori set, a zero-degree equation or a non-toric solution. Interpreting Corollary 3.7 as restricted to \(k=n+1\) would remove this particular example, but the corollary is stated without that restriction and Theorem 3.6 explicitly allows general collections. The special \(n+1\)-equation degree claim is not independently disproved here. Requiring the actual Newton polytopes to equal their degree simplices would likewise be a stronger assumption than full dimension of their sum.

## Verified safe replacement and impact

The original larger cutoff \(N\ge D:=\sum_i d_i\) remains supported by the actual-support theorem. Here is the relevant containment argument, with actual and a priori sets kept distinct. Choose a nonempty, sufficiently small generic actual-support Canny–Emiris set \(E_0\subseteq Q_{\rm act}\cap\mathbb Z^n\). If a row has \(\alpha+\operatorname{supp}(f_i)\subseteq E_0\), convexity and support functions give \[\alpha+\operatorname{NP}(f_i)\subseteq
 \sum_j\operatorname{NP}(f_j)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \alpha\in\sum_{j\ne i}\operatorname{NP}(f_j).\] Consequently \(\alpha+A_i\subseteq\sum_j\operatorname{conv}(A_j)\). The actual-support certificate rows are therefore retained in the full a priori set, and have no finite entries outside \(E_0\). This proves the needed transfer for that larger set.

For this example, \(N=5\) has 140 a priori rows and includes the two unshifted conflicting binomials. A sharper, actual-support choice also works: with \(\delta=(1/10,-1/10,-1/10,-1/10)\), up to an arbitrarily small generic perturbation, the actual Canny–Emiris set is \(\{e_1,2e_1,3e_1,4e_1\}\). Its four binomial rows require, among other conditions, \(y_{3e_1}=y_{e_1}\) and \(y_{3e_1}=y_{e_1}+2\), an immediate contradiction. Thus the main actual-support construction correctly detects this infeasibility.

A generally sharp repaired degree formula under mere full dimension is not claimed. The verified options are the larger a priori cutoff, or a justified actual-support certificate set. The false smaller bound can cause a feasibility decision to return the wrong answer, so this is a substantive Major issue rather than a presentation correction.

# Proof reconstruction and exact positive checks

For Theorem 3.5, the contrapositive row construction on pp. 16–19 was traced. At each shifted lattice point the associated supporting normal gives an equation with a uniquely maximizing monomial. Replacing that monomial by any other support point keeps the selected row inside \(E\). Lemma 3.34 gives a strict off-diagonal inequality after subtracting the concave lifting value from each column. The resulting square block is strictly tropically diagonally dominant; its rows must be distinct. A vector with all finite entries cannot satisfy the block’s tropical root equations. For \(E'=E\), the maximal-coordinate argument also excludes every nonzero tropical vector. The distinction between these two kernel conditions is essential.

The explicit matrix in Example 3.36, pp. 20–21, was rebuilt from the polynomial coefficients and the selected monomial shifts. Exact column scaling gives a minimum strict row margin of \(1/10\). Enumerating all \(6!\) permutations gives tropical determinant \(9\) with exactly one maximizing permutation. For the feasible system in Example 3.8, all seven Macaulay rows accept \[\operatorname{ver}(-3,-1)=(0,-3,-1,-6,-4,-2).\] These are independent exact checks, not author-software outputs.

For the two-sided construction, the role of the multiplicity \(r_j\) was checked. An active cell of dimension at most \(r_j-1\) satisfies \(r_j C_j=V_j+(r_j-1)C_j\), with \(V_j\) its vertices: a Carathéodory representation using at most \(r_j\) vertices, scaled by \(r_j\), has at least one coefficient at least one. This permits removal of a vertex while keeping the remaining point in the required Minkowski sum. A generic shift avoids the finitely many translated cell boundaries. The strict coefficient comparison must be oriented according to which side of the selected relation is violated; equality rows permit reversing sides.

For a finite retained linear system, strict inequalities can be replaced by weak ones with a common positive margin \(\lambda\), chosen below the minimum of the finitely many finite positive slacks. This checks the quantifier in the strict-to-weak reduction. The mixed root/two-sided conclusion then uses a maximal finite coordinate after column scaling, applying the appropriate row contradiction at that coordinate. The full generalization to other ordered value groups is not independently certified.

Example 4.3 was checked separately. Reconstructing its supports gives 21 rows on ten columns. The ten selected rows and the printed parametric column scalings give 18 required comparisons, all of the form \(a+b\lambda>0\) for every \(\lambda>0\), with \(a,b\ge0\) and not both zero. This verifies the displayed infeasibility certificate for the strict complement. The implication also follows directly by splitting into \(x_2<0\) and \(x_2\ge0\) in its first two inequalities, then using \(x_1\le3\).
