Abstract: The specified version contains false auxiliary assertions concerning real-time domination, the sampling grid of Pliss times, pressure limits, and cone-coordinate lower bounds. Explicit counterexamples and available local replacement arguments are given below. The first two issues leave a substantive continuous-time gap in the presented Bowen argument. No counterexample to the equilibrium-state uniqueness theorems is claimed. Local normalization, counting, and entrance-time corrections are recorded separately. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Core findings

Problem 1 (Major: Lemma 7.4 fails at arbitrary real times).  Lemma 7.4(b), p. 61, asserts that outside \(W_r\), initial \(d_F\ge d_E\) implies a ratio at least \(\lambda_R^t\) after time \(t\), with \(\lambda_R>1\) and no prefactor. Its proof invokes time-one domination. The stated real-time assertion is false under those hypotheses.

## A smooth, admissible dynamical counterexample

Equation reference: eq:metric. Let \(A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(\lambda=(3+\sqrt5)/2\), and \(\mu=\log\lambda\). Take the unit-roof mapping torus of \(A\) with its suspension flow. Its stable line is \(E^{ss}\), and its flow and unstable directions span \(F^{cu}\). Give the flow unit speed, make the three directions orthogonal, and in suspension coordinates use the norm weights \[w_s(\theta)=e^{-\mu\theta},\qquad
 w_u(\theta)=e^{\mu\theta+h(\theta)},\qquad
 h(\theta)=-6\sin(2\pi\theta).
 \] These define a smooth global metric: at \(\theta+1\) the weights are respectively \(\lambda^{-1}\) and \(\lambda\) times their values at \(\theta\), precisely as required by the \(A\) identification. All derivatives match because \(h\) is smooth and periodic.

Every time-one stable norm is \(\lambda^{-1}<1/2\); the time-one sectional area factor on \(F^{cu}\) is \(\lambda>1\); the normal unstable/stable ratio gains \(\lambda^2\). At general times the bounded factor involving \(h\) supplies the allowed fixed constants in sectional hyperbolicity and domination. Thus the flow satisfies the stated type of hypotheses. It has no singularities, so \(W_r\) is empty. The whole manifold is an Anosov attractor; if a proper attractor is desired, take its product with a contracting normal direction and use the invariant sink slice.

Equation reference: eq:ratio. At the period-one orbit over \(0\) and phase zero, take \(t=13/12\). The normal unstable/stable ratio gains \[G=\exp\{2\mu t+h(t)-h(0)\}
   =\exp(13\mu/6-3)<e^{-2/5}<1.
 \] Indeed, \(\lambda<3<e^{6/5}\), the last inequality following already from the first four terms of the exponential series. Hence \(\mu<6/5\), which proves the strict bound.

At each phase of this periodic orbit the time-one normal return is hyperbolic. The locally invariant fake leaves have tangents equal to its stable and unstable eigendirections. Choose an arbitrarily small transverse displacement whose initial fake-leaf ratio tends to \(11/10\). Its terminal ratio tends to \((11/10)G<1\), since \(e^{2/5}>1+2/5>11/10\). The strict inequality persists for sufficiently small nonzero displacement. Shrinking it also puts it in any specified Bowen ball for this fixed finite time. Initially \(d_F>d_E\), but finally \(d_F<d_E\), contradicting the claimed bound \(\lambda_R^{13/12}>1\). This uses actual leaf lengths through their tangents, not a different definition of \(d_E,d_F\).

## Impact and repair boundary

Pages 54–55 explicitly restrict fake-foliation invariance to integer iterates. Page 62 applies Lemma 7.4 on intervals between singular exits and entries. These have arbitrary real lengths even if the full good segment has integer length. The resulting preservation of a large \(F\)-coordinate is used in Proposition 7.2, the Bowen property in Theorem 5.1, and the route to Theorem C and its applications.

A discrete proof with bounded end-interval factors, or a suitably adapted continuous-time geometry, may repair this route. Such a repair has not been completed here. Simply adding a constant to the exponential estimate does not establish the repeated threshold \(d_F\ge d_E\) needed at the next passage. The finding is a false core intermediate assertion and invalid use of it, not a counterexample to Theorems A–C or the final Bowen property.

Problem 2 (Major: the Pliss conclusion uses the wrong sampling grid).  Definitions 10 and 11 (pp. 31, 33) sample backward from a possibly real endpoint \(t\). Lemma 4.7 (p. 35) nevertheless demands selected times in \([0,t]\cap\mathbb N\). Its argument produces times \(t-j\), which need not be integers.

Use a roof-two suspension of a hyperbolic toral automorphism, taking a suitable power for the prescribed time-one bounds. Start at phase zero, and let \[W=\{\theta\pmod2:0.8<\theta<1.2\},\qquad t=N+\tfrac12 .\] There are no singularities, so this is an open neighborhood of \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)\cap\Lambda\) as allowed by the lemma. Both endpoints lie outside \(W\), and every backward sample \(t-j\) has phase \(1/2\) or \(3/2\), so its \(W\) frequency is zero. For any \(\beta_0<1/2\), however, an odd positive integer endpoint lies in \(W\) and fails recurrence; an even positive integer endpoint has frequency \(1/2\) in its last two samples and also fails. Time zero is not an admissible positive-length Pliss time under the definitions. No positive integer candidate exists, however large \(N\) is. The other Pliss condition need not be tested once every candidate fails recurrence.

The local correction selects candidates on the common endpoint grid \((t-\mathbb Z)\cap(1,t]\) and retains strict slack in the large-\(N\) counts. There is a further interface to repair: \(\mathcal G_B\) on p. 37 is defined for integer durations, whereas the first-exit time \(p(x,t)\) on p. 41 is real. Consequently the core duration \(s-p(x,t)\) used on p. 42 need not be an integer. Choosing all later endpoints on the grid of \(\widetilde s(x,t)\) aligns suffix increments but does not make the core duration integer. A Bowen result for the resulting real-time collection and a coherent decomposition are still required. The bare Lemma 4.8. on p. 35 also needs its intended statement. Integer-grid concatenation follows by splitting the backward products and counts at the join; it does not justify arbitrary phase changes.

A local phase correction is established, while a complete combined repair of Sections 5 and 7 is not. The defect directly affects the good/bad decomposition used by the uniqueness criterion.

Problem 3 (Major: Lemma 5.9 cannot hold for every empirical limit).  The pressure on p. 42 is a limsup over real times. Lemma 5.9 (p. 43) bounds it by the measure pressure of any limit point of the integer-indexed empirical measures. Appendix A (pp. 78–79) incorrectly replaces growth along an arbitrary subsequence by the full limsup.

Take two distinct periodic orbits \(\gamma_0,\gamma_1\) of a transitive Anosov suspension and a smooth potential \(\varphi\) equal to \(0\) and \(1\), respectively, on them. Such examples lie in the same sectional-hyperbolic setting; singularity assumptions are vacuous. For each \(t>0\) let \(\mathcal C_t\) be a singleton on \(\gamma_0\) when \(\lfloor t\rfloor\) is odd and on \(\gamma_1\) when it is even. Every separated partition sum is exactly \(1\) or \(e^t\), hence \(P(\mathcal C,\varphi,\delta)=1\). Along odd integers the empirical limit is the invariant measure on \(\gamma_0\), with entropy zero and potential integral zero. The asserted inequality reads \(1\le0\).

Even replacing the arbitrary limit by an integer subsequence is insufficient for arbitrary \(\mathcal C\): put the singleton on \(\gamma_0\) at every integer and on \(\gamma_1\) at every noninteger. The real-time pressure is still one, but every integer empirical limit has measure pressure zero. No closure under time truncation was imposed on the stated arbitrary collection.

## Verified replacement for the variational step

First choose real times \(t_j\to\infty\) realizing the pressure limsup, with the near-maximizing separated sets and exponential weights of (5.13). Then take a weak limit of their empirical measures. Put \(n_j=\lceil t_j\rceil\) and \(\psi(x)=\int_0^1\varphi(f_sx)\,ds\). A \((t_j,\delta)\)-separated set remains \((n_j,\delta)\)-separated. Choose a finite partition of diameter less than \(\delta\) for the time-one flow metric, with boundary of zero measure for the limiting discrete empirical measure \(\mu^1\). Its \(n_j\)-join separates the set, and Gibbs weights give the entropy identity \[H_{\nu_j}(\mathcal A^{n_j})
   +\int\Phi(x,t_j)\,d\nu_j=\log Z_{t_j}.\] Replacing \(\Phi(x,t_j)\) by \(S_{n_j}\psi(x)\) costs at most \(\|\varphi\|_\infty\). The entropy block inequality, first letting \(j\to\infty\) and then the block size tend to infinity, yields \[P(\mathcal C,\varphi,\delta)
 \le h_{\mu^1}(f_1)+\int\psi\,d\mu^1.\] No assertion that \((x,n_j)\in\mathcal C\) is needed.

The continuous empirical limit is \(\mu=\int_0^1(f_s)_*\mu^1\,ds\): the averaging lengths differ by at most one, hence their bounded-test-function discrepancy is \(O(t_j^{-1})\). Translating a long averaging interval also proves flow invariance. Entropy affinity and conjugacy under the commuting maps \(f_s\) give \(h_\mu(f_1)=h_{\mu^1}(f_1)\); the potential integrals agree. This proves the required existence statement for a maximizing real-time subsequence. Pressure \(-\infty\) is trivial; otherwise use unbounded nonempty times. The missing exponential in the first weight display on p. 78 must be restored consistently with (5.13).

## The occupation estimate needed downstream

The inference on p. 44 from discrete \(W_r\) counts to continuous occupation needs a quantitative loss and the union \(W_r\cup U_{\operatorname{Sing}}\), since a prefix can lie in \(U_{\operatorname{Sing}}\setminus W_r\). Conditional on a consistent grid and the asserted discrete counts, this step has a local repair. Near a singularity, \(|X(z)|\le L\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma)\). A complete visit from radius \(r_0\) to radius \(r\) and back lasts at least \(2L^{-1}\log(r_0/r)\). Decrease \(r\) before fixing the later scales so each such visit lasts at least four. If its length is \(\ell\) and it contains \(K\) unit-grid samples, then \(K\le\ell+1\le5\ell/4\). Occupation is at least \(4/5\) of the discrete count. A larger cutoff absorbs floor errors and retains at least \(0.6\beta_1\) mass in \(W_r\cup U_{\operatorname{Sing}}\), including the prefix and suffix. The bound \(\beta_1/1000\) for the unwanted part of \(U_{\operatorname{Sing}}\) leaves more than \(\beta_1/2\) in the singular balls, sufficient for Lemma 5.4. Weak limits use the corresponding closed neighborhoods.

This repairs the local conversion with the available strict margin. It is conditional on the grid/decomposition correction in Problem 2 and does not solve Problem 1. The pressure-gap proof cannot be declared complete solely from the corrected Lemma 5.9.

Problem 4 (Major: the unconditional lower bounds in Lemma 6.1 are false).  For the orthogonal coordinate splitting on p. 49, Lemma 6.1 asserts \(|y^2|-L_0\alpha|y^1|\le |y^F|\) and its symmetric counterpart. Appendix B drops a nonzero second-order term in (B.7)–(B.8), p. 80.

Equation reference: eq:cones. In \(\mathbb R^2\) take \(E\) horizontal, \(F\) vertical, and any \(a=\alpha>0\): \[y=(0,1),\quad
 u=y^F=\left(\frac{a}{1+a^2},\frac1{1+a^2}\right),\quad
 v=y-u=\left(\frac{-a}{1+a^2},\frac{a^2}{1+a^2}\right).
 \] Both cone hypotheses hold with equality: \(|u_E|=a|u_F|\) and \(|v_F|=a|v_E|\). The normalizations \(|y|=1\) and \(|u|\le1\) also hold. Yet \(y^1=0\), \(|y^2|=1\), and \(|u|=1/\sqrt{1+a^2}<1\), contradicting the bound for every \(L_0\) and arbitrarily small \(a\). Swapping \(E,F\) gives the other failure.

Equation reference: eq:correctcone. Write \(f=|u_F|\), \(e=|v_E|\). The cone conditions give \(f\le |y^2|+ae\), \(e\le |y^1|+af\), and \(|y^2|\le f+ae\). For \(0<a<1\), valid general bounds are \[\frac{|y^2|-a|y^1|}{1+a^2}
 \le |u|
 \le \frac{\sqrt{1+a^2}}{1-a^2}(|y^2|+a|y^1|),
 \] with symmetric bounds for \(v\). A negative lower numerator is harmless. The omitted term is of order \(a^2|y^2|\) and cannot be absorbed into \(a|y^1|\) when \(y^1=0\).

## The conditional cases used later can be restored

Suppose \(A=|u|\ge C_0B\), where \(B=|v|\) and \(C_0>0\) is fixed. Projection estimates directly give \[\left(\frac1{\sqrt{1+a^2}}-\frac a{C_0}\right)A
 \le |y^2|\le(1+a/C_0)A,\qquad
 |y^1|\le(a+C_0^{-1})A .\] For small enough \(a\) the lower coefficient is positive. Inverting proves \(A=(1+O_{C_0}(a))|y^2|\); division by the last bound gives \(|y^2|\ge C_0(1-O_{C_0}(a))|y^1|\). These are the required conditional comparisons. The symmetric argument proves Case (2); the zero case is immediate.

Lemma 6.4, p. 51, uses these conditional cases and the graph length estimate of Lemma 6.3. Its needed coordinate comparison therefore has this independent repair. The Major classification concerns a false intermediate assertion and its use in deriving a core coordinate tool; it does not mean that the conditional conclusion or equilibrium theorem has been refuted.

# Local normalization and boundary corrections

Problem 5 (Minor: absolute value in the time-change potential).  Footnote 5 on p. 11 writes \(|c\varphi|\) for a constant speed change. For \(g_t=f_{ct}\), \(c>0\), the correct potential is \(c\varphi\), because \[h_\mu(g_1)+\int c\varphi\,d\mu
 =c\left(h_\mu(f_1)+\int\varphi\,d\mu\right).\] Pressure and the singular pressure gap scale by \(c\). An absolute value generally changes equilibrium measures.

Use an Anosov suspension of finite topological entropy \(H\) and two periodic orbits. Choose a smooth \(\varphi\in[-B,0]\) equal to \(0\) and \(-B\) on them, with \(B>2H\). Any \(\varphi\) equilibrium has \(\int\varphi\ge-H\), since its pressure is at least zero. Any \(-\varphi\) equilibrium has \(\int\varphi\le H-B<-H\), since its pressure is at least \(B\). The sets are disjoint. Existence here follows from entropy upper semicontinuity. As \(|c\varphi|=-c\varphi\), the printed substitution fails. Removing the absolute value fully repairs this local normalization; it is not a new obstruction to Theorem C.

Problem 6 (Minor: finite endpoint counts need a weak inequality or slack).  Theorem 4.1, p. 30, permits \(A=b_2>b_1\), making its density \(\theta_0=1\), yet requires \(\ell>\theta_0N=N\) among \(N\) indices. This boundary case requires \(\ell\ge\theta_0N\) or a strictly smaller density. Lemma 4.6, p. 34, claims at least \(\kappa(\lfloor t\rfloor+1)\) positive candidates for every \(t\). At \(t=1\), \(W=\varnothing\), and \(\kappa>1/2\), this exceeds one although only the length-one candidate is admissible.

Use a count proportional to \(\lfloor t\rfloor\), or choose an intermediate density \(\kappa'>\kappa\) and enlarge the large-time cutoff. The later intersection argument has strict density slack, so these bounded losses are absorbable once the common grid is fixed. These finite failures do not disprove the asymptotic Pliss mechanism.

Problem 7 (Minor: Lemma 2.16 needs finite backward entrance).  On p. 23 the lemma quantifies over every \(x\in B_r(\sigma)\cap\Lambda\) and evaluates \(x_{-t_x}\) at the supremal backward stay time. For \(x=\sigma\) this time is infinite; the same occurs at local unstable points whose backward orbit stays in the ball. The displayed entrance point is then undefined.

Restrict the assertion to \(t_x<\infty\) and use \(\partial B_{r_0}(\sigma)\), not the printed \(\partial B_{r_0}(x)\). The intended compactness proof applies to finite entrance passages. Later good segments, with endpoints outside the singular neighborhood, use finite completed visits. This is a missing boundary hypothesis rather than a counterexample to the passage conclusion for those visits.

# Checks that survive and explicit remaining limits

Lemma 5.4 was checked using compactness, entropy upper semicontinuity, and affinity after separating singular point masses. Backward recurrence gives zero invariant mass to \(W^u(\sigma)\setminus\{\sigma\}\) in Lemma 5.5. The nested-neighborhood argument of Lemma 5.6 is consistent with these facts. The principal parameter order accommodates the extra long-residence choice before the final Bowen scale is fixed.

Conditional on valid scaled shadowing, the Hölder estimate on pp. 65–69 has the required summable terms \(\lambda_1^{\gamma(i-t)}\) and \(\lambda_E^{\gamma i}\), with \(\lambda_1>1\), \(0<\lambda_E<1\). The factor \(|X(x_i)|^{-1}\) from the projection derivative cancels the factor \(|X(x_i)|\) from the normal displacement bounds, preventing a spurious singular denominator in the Birkhoff-integral bound. In (7.16), p. 68, the initial stable leg must be from \(y^F_{\tau(0)}\) to \(y_{\tau(0)}\); the printed superscript \(E\) is an immediate label error. A nonzero point on the stable leaf makes the printed right side zero and left side positive. The corrected label restores that stable contraction step, conditional on its stated premises.

The finite specification construction admits a consistent choice of radii: choose the output radius in terms of the shadowing scale, then the bridge radius, and finally the long-time cutoff. No proportionality of the first radius to a later input radius is needed. On p. 76 the initial disk center should be the available intersection of the periodic orbit’s unstable manifold with the small stable plaque of \(x^1\); assumption (B1) does not state that \(x^1\) itself is in that unstable manifold. The hyperbolic-time disk lemma already allows a center on this plaque.

For the local branches \(F_j\) in (8.6), set \(z^k=y^k\) and \(z^j=F_j^{-1}(z^{j+1})\) successively. The exact orbit identity follows by induction, with each gap evaluated once at its actual preimage. The two flow-box time corrections are bounded per gap, not accumulated into one gap over all pieces. A positive minimum bridge time may be requested before the inclination construction to make the final gaps nonnegative. These are verified completions of finite gluing conditional on the cited quantitative disk and inclination inputs.

In Appendix C, the deep-visit duration tends uniformly to infinity as \(r\to0\), allowing fixed coordinate factors to be absorbed by a smaller exponential rate. The flow-speed ratio at entry and exit has a uniform positive lower bound on the fixed sphere of radius \(r_0\), using invertibility of \(DX(\sigma)\), not merely an upper Lipschitz estimate. That sphere also supplies a uniform normal-flow-box radius; an infimum over \(W_r^c\) need not stay positive as \(r\) shrinks. The written reduction of a real passage time to an integer and the off-attractor cone extensions are not fully reconstructed here. They must be integrated with the continuous-time repair in Problem 1.

The Lorenz linearization at the origin was checked exactly: its eigenvalues are \((-11\pm\sqrt{1201})/2\) and \(-8/3\). This local calculation is not a rerun of Tucker’s global verified return-map construction. The reductions of Theorems A and B from Theorem C were checked against their stated external Lorenz and periodic-manifold inputs; those reductions do not remove the internal proof issues above.

# Conclusion

The assessment for this exact version is Major. Four core auxiliary assertions have demonstrated failures. Some local tools can be repaired explicitly, but a complete continuous-time Bowen/decomposition repair is not established. None of these conclusions asserts that the main uniqueness theorems are false.
