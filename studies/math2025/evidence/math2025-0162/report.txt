Abstract: The reviewed author manuscript contains a false global uniqueness assertion for lifts: strict inequality between the top exponent on the given subspace and the quotient cocycle average does not exclude intermediate critical directions. A four-dimensional diagonal example satisfies every stated condition and has a continuum of stationary ergodic lifts. This supports Major impact for that classification guarantee, while leaving the stated existence criterion and the filtration-based classification unrefuted. Three narrower issues concern the hypothesis of a recurrence lemma, a positive-recurrence assertion, and passage from projective compactness to linear compactness. Their actual downstream scopes and precise local corrections are distinguished. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

Write \(\Gamma_\mu\) for the closed semigroup generated by \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\) and \(H_\mu\) for its Zariski closure. A stationary lift of \(\bar\nu\) from \(\mathbb P(V/W)\) is a stationary probability on \(\mathbb P(V)\setminus\mathbb P(W)\) projecting to \(\bar\nu\). The top Lyapunov exponent \(\lambda_1(W)\) is distinct from the smallest Furstenberg–Kifer–Hennion exponent \(\beta_{\min}(W)\). That distinction is decisive in Problem 1.

# The lift uniqueness guarantee

Problem 1 (A strict top-exponent gap does not imply uniqueness).  Remark 4.4(1), page 14, retains the hypotheses of Theorem 1.5 and an invariant \(W'\) whose intersection with \(W\) has slower expansion. It asserts that \(\lambda_1(W)>\alpha(\bar\nu)\) gives a unique stationary lift on the full \(\mathbb P(V)\setminus\mathbb P(W)\). The introductory paragraph below Theorem 1.5 on page 4 makes the same distinction between the strict and critical cases. The global uniqueness assertion is false.

## Input and verification of every condition

In the standard basis of \(V=\mathbb R^4\), take \[g=\operatorname{diag}(2,1,1,1/2),\qquad \mu=\delta_g,\qquad
 W=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_4),\qquad W'=\operatorname{span}(e_3,e_4).\] The first moment is finite: \(\int\log\max(\|g\|_2,\|g^{-1}\|_2)\,d\mu=\log2\). All the displayed subspaces are \(\Gamma_\mu\)- and \(H_\mu\)-invariant, because they are coordinate subspaces of a diagonal action. The quotient \(V/W\) is the line generated by \(e_3+W\); let \(\bar\nu=\delta_{[e_3+W]}\). This measure is stationary and ergodic, and \[\alpha(\bar\nu)=\lambda_1(V_{\bar\nu})=0
 <\lambda_1(W)=\log2.\] Moreover, \[W'\cap W=\mathbb Re_4,\qquad
 \lambda_1(W'\cap W)=-\log2<0,\qquad
 W'/(W'\cap W)\simeq\mathbb R(e_3+W).\] Thus the existence condition in Theorem 1.5 and the extra premise of Remark 4.4 are both satisfied, including the exact quotient support-span requirement.

## Stated result versus actual result

For every \(c\in\mathbb R\), define \[\nu_c=\delta_{[e_3+ce_2]}.\] Since \(g(e_3+ce_2)=e_3+ce_2\), each \(\nu_c\) is stationary. A Dirac probability at a fixed point is an extreme stationary probability, hence ergodic. Its point lies outside \(\mathbb P(W)\), and its quotient is always \([e_3+W]\). Distinct \(c\)’s yield distinct projective points. Consequently there are continuously many stationary ergodic lifts, whereas Remark 4.4(1) asserts exactly one. No limiting argument or numerical approximation enters this example.

The mechanism is the invariant direction \(e_2\subset W\) with exponent zero. The strict inequality for the top exponent on \(W\) does not remove this critical direction. Here the FKH exponents of \(W\) are \(\log2,0,-\log2\); replacing all of them by their maximum loses information needed for uniqueness.

## Alternative readings, impact and a bounded replacement

The counterexample is consistent with Theorem 1.5’s existence criterion: a lift does exist. It is also consistent with Theorem 1.3, whose unique lifts are taken across the specific FKH filtration, and with Corollary 1.6, which claims uniqueness on each individual \(H_\mu\)-orbit. In this example each \([e_3+ce_2]\) is its own orbit. These surviving statements do not supply global uniqueness for an arbitrary given \(W\).

The supported impact of Problem 1 is Major: an explicit uniqueness guarantee in the paper’s central classification of lifts is false within its stated hypotheses. The finding does not assert that the critical semisimplicity theorem or every classification result fails.

A precise restricted replacement is: for a fixed invariant \(W'\) satisfying \(\lambda_1(W'\cap W)<\alpha(\bar\nu)\), there is at most one stationary lift concentrated on \(\mathbb P(W')\setminus\mathbb P(W'\cap W)\), conditional on existence. Appendix A supplies the uniqueness argument. This statement does not claim uniqueness on the larger ambient complement. For the example, that fixed carrier is the affine chart \([e_3+ye_4]\), on which \(g\) acts by \(y\mapsto y/2\). Its only stationary probability is \(\delta_0\), since \(\Pr(|Y|>t)=\Pr(|Y|>2^nt)\to0\). Thus only \(\nu_0\) lies in this \(W'\). The stronger assertion in the remark that the lift spans all of \(W'\) also fails here: its span is only \(\mathbb Re_3\). A verified restricted statement uses \(V_\nu\subseteq W'\), with equality requiring a minimal-carrier condition.

# Other proof and hypothesis issues

Problem 2 (Lemma 3.6 assumes irreducibility of the wrong acting group).  Lemma 3.6, page 9, assumes that \(\rho,\rho'\) are strongly irreducible representations of a topological group \(G\), that their pushforwards of \(\mu\) have finite first moments and the same top exponent, and concludes a positive lower bound along a subsequence for \(\|\rho(b_1\cdots b_n)\|/\|\rho'(b_1\cdots b_n)\|\). The statement does not require the support-generated action to be strongly irreducible.

Take \[G=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R),\quad
 \rho=\mathrm{Std},\quad \rho'=\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathrm{Std}),\quad
 \mu=\delta_u,\qquad u=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Both representations of \(G\) are irreducible. For \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\), the diagonal torus separates the three monomial weights and the two unipotent one-parameter subgroups connect them, so any nonzero invariant subspace contains all three weight spaces. Connectedness of \(G\) then makes both representations strongly irreducible: a finite invariant union would give a finite permutation action of a connected group and hence an invariant individual subspace. Both point-mass pushforwards have finite first moment. For the unique sample path, \[\rho(u^n)=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \rho'(u^n)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&n&n^2\\0&1&2n\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\] in the monomial basis. With Euclidean coordinate norms, \[0<
 \frac{\|\rho(u^n)\|_2}{\|\rho'(u^n)\|_2}
 \leq \frac{\sqrt{n^2+2}}{n^2}\longrightarrow0.\] The logarithms of both norms divided by \(n\) tend to zero, so their top Lyapunov exponents are equal to zero. All stated assumptions hold, but every infinite subsequence has infimum zero, contradicting (3.6).

The missing condition concerns \(\rho(\Gamma_\mu)\) and \(\rho'(\Gamma_\mu)\), not only their ambient representations. The proof’s stationary-measure and norm-comparison arguments require that support-generated irreducibility. In the actual application in Proposition 3.7, \(G\) is the connected Zariski closure \(H_\mu\), and the representations are irreducible quotients of its module. Zariski density then provides the stronger condition. This is Minor for the stated overgeneralization: the counterexample lies outside the actual application’s stronger scope. Adding that condition excludes the example and matches the proof’s requirements; a complete independent proof of the repaired general recurrence lemma would still require its cited external results.

Problem 3 (The support reduction invokes a false recurrence assertion).  The first paragraph of page 7 claims that every neighborhood \(O\) of every \(g\in\Gamma_\mu\cap H_\mu^\circ\) is visited infinitely often with positive probability by \(b_n\cdots b_1\).

Let \(V=\mathbb R\), \(\mu=\delta_2\), \(g=2\), and \(O=(3/2,5/2)\). The moment is \(\log2<\infty\), the only nonzero invariant subspace has exponent \(\log2\), and the projective space is a singleton, so its probability is stationary, ergodic and nondegenerate. The Zariski closure of the infinitely many scalars \(2^n\) is \(\operatorname{GL}_1\), which is Zariski-connected, and the return time to its identity component is \(\tau=1\). Yet \(2^n\in O\) only for \(n=1\), and therefore \[\Pr\!\left(\limsup_n\{b_n\cdots b_1\in O\}\right)=0,\] rather than a positive number.

The intended equality \(\Gamma_{\mu^\tau}=\Gamma_\mu\cap H_\mu^\circ\) survives. For its reverse inclusion, approximate an element of the right-hand side by finite products of support elements. Since the identity component is relatively open in the finite component quotient, the approximating products may be chosen in it. Split each finite word at successive first returns to the identity coset. Each block product belongs to \(\operatorname{supp}\mu^\tau\): neighborhoods of its factors have positive product probability, and the finite coset itinerary is locally constant. Taking products and then closure gives the required inclusion. The other inclusion is immediate. This is a verified algebraic replacement for the incorrect probabilistic assertion. Its supported impact is Minor; the support identity and the main reduction are not contradicted.

Problem 4 (Projective compactness is replaced by linear compactness).  In Section 3.4(i), page 12, the sandwich set \(\pi(O\cap\Gamma_\mu)\pi\) is called a compact subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}(\operatorname{Im}\pi)\). Proposition 3.7, pages 10–11, instead provides compact closure of the projective image of its generated semigroup. The literal linear-compactness assertion is false.

Take \(V=\mathbb R^2\), \(\mu=\delta_{2I}\), any line \(W\), and the rotation-invariant probability on \(\mathbb P(\mathbb R^2)\). The measure is stationary and nondegenerate; moments are finite; all subspace exponents equal \(\log2\); \(H_\mu\) is the connected algebraic scalar group; and \(W\) is irreducible. The cone \(C(\Gamma_\mu)\) consists of nonnegative scalar matrices, so its only nonzero idempotent is \(\pi=I\). A nonempty Zariski-open subset of the scalar group omits at most finitely many scalars. Thus \(\pi(O\cap\Gamma_\mu)\pi\) contains unbounded \(2^nI\)’s, while its projective image is the identity.

The needed complete reducibility nevertheless follows from the actual proposition. Normalize each real invertible map on the \(d\)-dimensional image by \[A\longmapsto |\det A|^{-1/d}A.\] This is multiplicative and preserves every invariant subspace. The normalized inverse image of a compact projective group is compact: projective compactness bounds singular-value ratios, and determinant modulus one bounds each singular value away from zero and infinity. Average an inner product over the compact normalized closure. The orthogonal complement of an invariant subspace is invariant, and scalar rescaling preserves this fact for the original semigroup. This supplies exactly the complement used in (3.10)–(3.11). The supported impact is Minor, with the downstream complement step analytically restored.

# Remaining checks and dependence

The finite component quotient is an irreducible finite Markov chain, so its return time has finite expectation; periodicity does not prevent this. If \(M(g)=\log\max(\|g\|,\|g^{-1}\|)\), then \[M(b_\tau\cdots b_1)\leq\sum_{j\leq\tau}M(b_j),\qquad
 \mathbb E\sum_{j\leq\tau}M(b_j)
 =\mathbb E\tau\,\mathbb E M(b_1)<\infty.\] The induced first-moment condition and the return-time scaling of exponents are therefore compatible with the reduction. In Lemma 2.2 one should choose an ergodic component of the constructed stationary extension; its projection remains the given ergodic base measure and its finite-group marginal remains uniform.

The projective-limit construction was checked in the form actually used: normalized-product limits, the two nonzero quotient images, simultaneous eigenvalue-phase approximation, the idempotent limit, measure-preserving sandwich semigroup and its compact projective closure. With the normalization above, the invariant subspace transfer and the finite-dimensional quotient induction recover the intended complement argument. The broader wording of Lemma 3.5(3) for arbitrary \(\pi\in\Pi_b\) is not fully proved by the displayed argument, which treats normalized-product limits; no counterexample to that broader wording was established.

Theorem 1.5’s FKH-layer reduction and the orbit-closure argument in Corollary 1.6 were examined conditional on the stated external contracting-lift and semisimple-orbit results. No contrary example was found for those conclusions. Appendix A independently checks the cocycle-average identity used in Lemma 4.3.

# Two direct checks for lifts

Let \(q:V\to V/W\) be the quotient map with its quotient norm. On the complement of \(\mathbb P(W)\), set \(f([v])=\log(\|v\|/\|qv\|)\geq0\). The difference of the ambient and quotient norm cocycles is \(f(g[v])-f([v])\), whose absolute value is at most \(2M(g)\). For a stationary lift, the bounded truncations \(f_m=\min(f,m)\) satisfy \(\int(f_m(gx)-f_m(x))\,d\mu(g)d\nu(x)=0\). Their increments are dominated by \(|f(gx)-f(x)|\). Dominated convergence therefore gives equality of ambient and quotient cocycle averages without assuming \(f\) itself integrable. This verifies Lemma 4.3’s conclusion directly.

For uniqueness on a fixed carrier \(W'\), put \(Z=W'\cap W\). Given two stationary lifts on \(\mathbb P(W')\setminus\mathbb P(Z)\) of the same ergodic \(\bar\nu\), couple their conditional probabilities over \(\bar\nu\), and average this coupling under the common random action. A stationary limit exists on the compact product of projective spaces. Its marginals are the original stationary measures, hence give zero mass to \(\mathbb P(Z)\); it lies in the closed relation \(qu\wedge qv=0\). Choose representatives with \(qu=qv\ne0\), so \(u-v\in Z\). For common products \(L_n\), projective distance is bounded by \[d([L_nu],[L_nv])
 \leq \frac{2\|L_n|_Z\|\,\|u-v\|}{\|\bar L_n qv\|}.\] Under \(\lambda_1(Z)<\alpha(\bar\nu)\), the numerator and denominator exponents make this tend to zero almost surely, using the stationary ergodic cocycle growth in the source. Stationarity forces the coupling to be diagonal. Its marginals therefore coincide. This proves conditional uniqueness on \(W'\), and says nothing about lifts on other carriers.
