Abstract: The reviewed accepted manuscript contains a factor-of-two error in the curvature absorption step of Theorem 1.5. Its chosen parameter yields half the asserted curvature threshold, so the stated hypothesis does not imply the coefficient sign used in the proof. A different parameter repairs the strict version of that threshold; the equality case in the stated theorem remains unsupported by this argument. This is a demonstrated defect in a core derivation, not a counterexample to the theorem. The principal P-function identities and selected classification reductions were checked separately. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions, assumptions and checked identities

For the power equation \(-\Delta u=u^p\), set \[n=\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1},\qquad
 v=u^{-(p-1)/2},\qquad
 P=\frac{n|\nabla v|^2/2+c_n}{v},\qquad c_n=\frac{2}{n-2}.\] For a positive solution, \(v,P>0\) and \(\Delta v=P\). In the subcritical case \(d\ge3\) and \(1<p<p_S=(d+2)/(d-2)\), one has \(n>d\). For the two-dimensional Liouville equation, the corresponding substitution is \(u=-2\log v\), with \(n=d=2\) and \(c_2=1/2\). These transformations retain positivity and do not require division by \(|\nabla v|\).

Equation reference: eq:checked. Writing \[k[v]=|\nabla^2v|^2-\frac{(\Delta v)^2}{n}
       +\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(\nabla v,\nabla v),\] direct use of Bochner’s identity gives \[
 \mathop{\mathrm{div}}(v^{2-n}\nabla P)=n v^{1-n}k[v].\] Indeed, applying \(\Delta\) to \(vP=n|\nabla v|^2/2+c_n\) gives \[v\Delta P=(n-2)\langle\nabla v,\nabla P\rangle
       +n\bigl(|\nabla^2v|^2+\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(\nabla v,\nabla v)\bigr)-P^2,\] which is equivalent to [eq:checked] (#eq:checked). This independently recovers source (2.16), pp. 8–10. The decomposition \[k[v]=\left|\nabla^2v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}g\right|^2
       +\frac{n-d}{nd}(\Delta v)^2+\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}(\nabla v,\nabla v)\] and the identity \(v\nabla P=n(\nabla^2v-Pg/n)\nabla v\) also recover the coefficient \(t-\tfrac12\) in source (2.19), using \(|\nabla v|^2/(vP)\le2/n\). Thus the defect below is not inherited from an incorrect fundamental P-function identity.

# Substantive finding

Problem 1 (The curvature coefficient in the proof of Theorem 1.5).  The parameter choice in source (3.12) does not imply the nonnegative coefficient asserted immediately before (3.13). The subsequent nonexistence proof therefore does not establish Theorem 1.5 with its stated non-strict curvature threshold.

## Original claim and precise assumptions

Equation reference: eq:claimed. Theorem 1.5 (p. 4) considers a complete, connected, noncompact, boundaryless Riemannian manifold of dimension \(d\ge3\) and a nonnegative \(C^3\) solution of \(-\Delta u=u^p\), where \(1<p<p_S\). It assumes, for some \(\nu>2\) and \(S>0\), \[\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^2(M)}\ge S\|\psi\|_{L^\nu(M)}
 \quad(\psi\in C_c^1(M)),\] the curvature bound \[
 \rho:=\|\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}_-\|_{L^{\nu/(\nu-2)}(M)}
 \le \frac{(p_S-p)(p-1)(d-2)}{48}S^2
 =:K=\frac{n-d}{3(n-2)^2}S^2,\] and \(V_g(B_R)=O(R^{2+8/(p-1)})=O(R^{2(n-1)})\). Here \(\rho\) is the nonnegative norm of the negative curvature part. The claimed conclusion is \(u=0\). A nonzero nonnegative solution is positive by the strong maximum principle, so the proof works with the positive \(v\) defined above.

## Coefficient calculation

Equation reference: eq:c1, eq:c2. Let \(\delta=t-\tfrac12-\varepsilon>0\) and let \(\varphi\) be a compactly supported cutoff. The two coefficients on the left of source (3.9), p. 13, are \[\begin{aligned}
 c_1&=2\left(\frac{2\delta S^2}{3t^2}-\rho\right), \\
 c_2&=\frac1n\left(n-d-\frac{2\delta(n-2)^2}{t^2}\right). \end{aligned}\] They multiply, respectively, a nonnegative \(L^{\nu/2}\) norm and \(\int_M P^{t+1}v^{1-n}\varphi^\theta\). The proof discards the first term and uses the second with \(c_2>0\).

Equation reference: eq:factor. The prescribed values in (3.12) are \[t=n-2,\qquad
 \varepsilon=n-2-\frac12-\frac{n-d}{4},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{n-d}{4}.\] Substitution gives \[
 \frac{2\delta S^2}{3t^2}
   =\frac{n-d}{6(n-2)^2}S^2=\frac K2,\] whereas the unnumbered equality before (3.13) prints \(K\). Consequently [eq:claimed] (#eq:claimed) does not imply \(c_1\ge0\). The actual displayed source images of pp. 12–14 confirm both the coefficients and the parameter choice; this is not an extraction ambiguity.

An exact local diagnostic is \(d=3\), \(n=4\), \(p=3\), \(p_S=5\), \(S=1\) and \(\rho=1/16\). These parameters satisfy the displayed algebraic domains and \(\rho\le K=1/12\). Nevertheless, \(\varepsilon=5/4\), \(\delta=1/4\), \(c_1=-1/24\) and \(c_2=1/8\). The independent rational-arithmetic calculation retained with this report reproduces these values. This diagnostic refutes the coefficient implication. It does not construct a manifold or a solution satisfying the theorem’s geometric hypotheses, and is not a PDE counterexample.

## Counterevidence and supported repairs

The original choice does give \(c_1\ge0\) and \(c_2=(n-d)/(2n)>0\) if [eq:claimed] (#eq:claimed) is strengthened to \(\rho\le K/2\), equivalently if \(1/48\) is replaced by \(1/96\). The subsequent cutoff argument then closes under the original volume assumption.

A less restrictive repair is to replace \(\rho\le K\) by \(\rho<K\). Keep \(t=n-2\) and choose \[\delta=\frac{n-d}{4}+\frac{3t^2\rho}{4S^2},
 \qquad \varepsilon=t-\frac12-\delta.\] For \(0\le\rho<K\), this gives \(0<\delta<(n-d)/2\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), since \(t-\tfrac12-(n-d)/2=(n+d-5)/2>0\). Equations [eq:c1] (#eq:c1)–[eq:c2] (#eq:c2) become \[c_1=K-\rho>0,\qquad
 c_2=\frac{3t^2}{2nS^2}(K-\rho)>0.\] Thus the strict version has a verified repair using the same remaining argument, detailed in Appendix 6 (#app:cutoff).

At \(\rho=K\), however, the requirements \(c_1\ge0\) and \(c_2>0\) imply \[\frac{\delta}{t^2}\ge\frac{n-d}{2(n-2)^2}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \frac{\delta}{t^2}<\frac{n-d}{2(n-2)^2},\] respectively. They are incompatible for this estimate, even if \(t\) is varied. At the common endpoint both useful coefficients vanish. A stronger estimate or a separate endpoint argument might still prove the stated theorem; none is established here.

Theorem 4.5 (p. 16) is not a general replacement: it requires \(V_g(B_R)=o(R^{2p/(p-1)})\). For \(d=3\), \(p=3\), this is \(o(R^3)\), whereas Theorem 1.5 allows \(O(R^6)\). The hypotheses therefore do not supply that alternative proof. The evident negative signs in the growth exponents in parts of the prose on p. 14 do not repair [eq:factor] (#eq:factor); the intended positive growth is explicit in (3.6).

## Impact

The finding is assessed as Major: it is a demonstrated invalid derivation in the proof of the advertised small-negative-curvature nonexistence result. This assessment is confined to that derivation. It does not disprove Theorem 1.5, does not invalidate the P-function identities, and does not transfer to the nonnegative-curvature classification results. No author correction or adoption of the repairs above has been verified.

# Other checked reductions and limits

For Theorem 1.1, the statement requires \(\mathop{\mathrm{Ric}}\ge0\) and either \(d=3,4,5\), or \(d\ge6\) with the specified decay, or \(d\ge6\) with \(u\in L^{2d/(d-2)}\). The dimension-dependent estimates were checked within this scope (pp. 17–22). In dimension four the displayed \(t=7/10\) and \(\gamma=9/5\) give the admissible moment exponents \(14/5\) and \(37/15\). In dimension five, for example, \(\delta=1/100\), \(t=51/100\), \(s=100/53\), \(\gamma=5/4\) satisfy the relevant parameter inequalities; both final powers of \(R\) are \(-51/100\). For the higher-dimensional decay case, the limiting exponent is \[E_0=-\frac{2\alpha(d-4)}{d-2}+\frac{d-6}{2}.\] The strict threshold in (1.7) is exactly \(E_0<0\), and permits a small positive auxiliary parameter in (5.29). At \(\alpha=(d-2)/2\), the big-\(O\) bound alone need not imply critical integrability, as stated locally on p. 21; the proof can instead use a smaller decay exponent still above (1.7). No failure of the stated classification follows from that local inference.

The finite-energy reduction in (5.17)–(5.23), and the implication from constant \(P\) to \(\nabla^2v=(P/d)g\), were checked. The latter uses the rigidity result reproduced as Theorem A.1. Its original external proof was not reverified. In the subcritical nonnegative-curvature case, \(n>d\) makes the cutoff exponent \(d-2n+2\) negative (pp. 10–11). For the Liouville result, (6.5) bounds \(\int_{B_R}P\) by \(CR^2G(2R)\); the choice \(U=P^t\), \(1/2\le t<1\), and \(\tau=1/t>1\) satisfies the exponents of the Karp theorem reproduced as Theorem A.2 (pp. 22–23). The external surface classification and sharpness examples remain outside the independent verification scope. No additional substantive defect is established by these checks.

# Closure of the repaired cutoff argument

For either repair above, \(c_1\ge0\) and \(c_2>0\). Put \(A=P^{n-1}v^{1-n}>0\), \(q=(n-2)/(n-1)\in(0,1)\), and choose \(\theta\ge2(n-1)\). Source (3.9), with \(t=n-2\), yields \[I_\varphi:=\int_M A\varphi^\theta
 \le \frac{C}{R^2}
   \left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_R}A\varphi^\theta\right)^q
   V_g(B_{2R})^{1/(n-1)}.\] The exponent \(\theta-2-q\theta\) is nonnegative, so the indicated Hölder estimate is valid for \(0\le\varphi\le1\). Using the whole cutoff support on the right gives \(I_\varphi\le CR^{-2(n-1)}V_g(B_{2R})\le C\). It follows that \(A\in L^1(M)\). Applying the annular form again, the volume factor combined with \(R^{-2}\) is bounded, while the annular integral tends to zero. Hence \(\int_{B_R}A\to0\), contradicting \(A>0\). This completes the unchanged end of the proof for the two explicitly repaired hypotheses, but not for the unproved endpoint.
