Abstract: The acquired v2 contains a false Gaussian integration-by-parts identity in Lemma 4.7. A one-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter \(1/3\), and two elementary Gaussian increments, give an exact counterexample. Support restriction must be distinguished from orthogonal projection. The acquired text already defines divergence as the true adjoint of its restricted derivative, and the displayed conditional duality argument uses that definition. No direct dependence of the later quantitative estimate on the false identity has been established. The confirmed local defect is assessed as Minor; an allegation of core proof damage remains Needs Evidence. The authors link an erratum, but its text could not be retrieved in this review; the finding therefore must not be represented as an assessment of the corrected article.

# Source and the claim being reviewed

The source is Catellier and Duboscq, arXiv:2207.04251v2, submitted 14 February 2024, a 60-page preprint whose title page is dated 15 February 2024. The associated journal article is Annals of Probability 53 (2025), 79–139, DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1701 (https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOP1701). Page references below are to the acquired preprint and agree with its physical PDF page numbers. Journal-final equivalence is not asserted.

The main argument combines a driftless flow transform, regularity of its averaged field, and conditional Malliavin integration by parts. Theorem 6.1 assumes the covariance regularity, increment correlation conditions, and local nondeterminism in Assumptions 5.5, 5.8, and 5.10, as well as smooth bounded uniformly elliptic \(\sigma\). The fractional Brownian conclusion in Corollary 6.3 is explicitly for \(1/4<H<1/2\), bounded \(C^\kappa\) drift with \[\kappa>0\vee\left(\frac32-\frac1{2H}\right).\] Its exceptional null set may depend on the drift, while the initial condition varies outside that set. These quantifiers do not assert one common null set for every possible drift.

The detailed checks concern Definitions 4.4–4.6 and Lemma 4.7, their role in the conditional calculus, and the final exponent arithmetic. The complete flow-transform proof, all Banach-space estimates, and all higher Malliavin derivative bounds have not been independently certified.

# Problem 1: support restriction is not orthogonal projection

Problem 1 (Minor).  Lemma 4.7 on p. 29 is false for the derivative defined on p. 28 and for fractional Brownian motion in the range used by Corollary 6.3. Its formula with the unchanged \(W(h)\) requires correction. The present counterexample does not establish failure of the later adjoint-based conditional calculus.

## Exact definitions and disputed identity

Equation reference: eq:false. The canonical Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\) has \[\langle 1_{[0,s]},1_{[0,t]}\rangle_{\mathcal H}=R(s,t).\] On p. 28, \(\mathcal H([a,b])\) is described by restriction of step functions to \([a,b]\), with the inherited covariance inner product. Definition 4.6 gives, for a cylindrical variable \(F=f(W(h_1),\ldots,W(h_n))\), \[D_{[a,b]}F
   =\sum_k \partial_k f(W(h_1),\ldots,W(h_n))\,h_k1_{[a,b]} .\] Lemma 4.7 then asserts, for \(F,G\) in the smooth cylindrical class and \(h\in\mathcal H([a,b])\), \[
 \mathbb E\!\left[G\langle D_{[a,b]}F,h\rangle_{\mathcal H([a,b])}\right]
 =-\mathbb E\!\left[F\langle D_{[a,b]}G,h\rangle_{\mathcal H([a,b])}\right]
   +\mathbb E[FGW(h)].\] This is the ordinary Gaussian formula with the full derivative replaced by a support-truncated derivative, while leaving \(W(h)\) unchanged.

## Exact counterexample

Let \(W=B^H\) be scalar fractional Brownian motion on \([0,1]\), with \(H=1/3\), so \[R(s,t)=\frac12(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}).\] Take \[[a,b]=[1/2,1],\qquad F=B^H_{1/2},\qquad G=1,\qquad
 h=1_{(1/2,1]}.\] Half-open indicators represent the same increments in the canonical Hilbert space; the common endpoint contributes no atom for this continuous Gaussian process. Both \(F\) and \(G\) belong to the paper’s smooth cylindrical class, which permits polynomial growth. The deterministic \(h\) belongs to \(\mathcal H([a,b])\).

Equation reference: eq:cov. By the given definition, \[D_{[a,b]}F=0,\qquad D_{[a,b]}G=0.\] Thus the left side and the first term on the right of [eq:false] (#eq:false) vanish. The remaining term is \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[FGW(h)]
 &=\mathbb E\!\left[B^H_{1/2}(B^H_1-B^H_{1/2})\right]\\
 &=R(1/2,1)-R(1/2,1/2)\\
 &=\frac12-2^{-2H}
   =\frac{1-2^{1/3}}2\ne0. \end{aligned}\] In particular it is strictly negative. An exact rational enclosure is \[\frac54<2^{1/3}<\frac43,\qquad
 -\frac16<\frac{1-2^{1/3}}2<-\frac18.\] The first inequalities follow by cubing the positive rational endpoints. No simulation, numerical tolerance, or inference from a missing proof is involved. This directly contradicts the stated identity.

The same calculation gives a nonzero covariance for every \(H\ne1/2\). The choice \(H=1/3\) puts the example strictly inside the paper’s principal rough fractional Brownian range. It tests the calculus of the driving noise itself; no irregular or degenerate diffusion coefficient is needed.

## Why the usual Gaussian formula does not apply

For the full Malliavin derivative, \(DF=1_{[0,1/2]}\), and \[\langle DF,h\rangle_{\mathcal H}
 =\mathbb E[B^H_{1/2}(B^H_1-B^H_{1/2})],\] so the ordinary integration-by-parts identity is consistent. The error is to replace this inner product by the one after support truncation. Disjointly supported increments are generally correlated. Consequently multiplication by \(1_{[a,b]}\) is not the covariance-orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H([a,b])\).

More abstractly, if a support restriction \(P\) is bounded on the relevant Hilbert space and one defines \(D^P=PD\), its adjoint must involve \(P^*\): \[\delta^P(u)=\delta(P^*u).\] Even for deterministic \(u\), this gives \(W(P^*u)\), not automatically \(W(u)\). This observation is conditional on the appropriate operator domains and does not establish those domains or the uniform estimates needed for the complete rough-path proof.

For conditioning on an outside Gaussian sigma-field, another natural construction uses the orthogonal complement of its Gaussian subspace. That construction changes the covariance entering the calculus. In a two-variable illustration, let \(X,Y\) be centered jointly Gaussian with \(\operatorname{Var}X=\operatorname{Var}Y=v\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=c\). The innovation \[R=Y-\frac cvX\] is independent of \(X\) and has variance \(v-c^2/v\). The correct one-dimensional conditional formula is \[\mathbb E[f'(Y)\mid X]
 =\mathbb E\!\left[f(Y)\frac{R}{v-c^2/v}\,\middle|\,X\right].\] This elementary example explains what has to be distinguished; it is not a proof of an amended infinite-dimensional result.

## Dependence of the main proof and limits of the finding

Section 4.3 on pp. 29–30 already defines \(\delta_{[a,b]}\) as the adjoint of \(D_{[a,b]}\), through its domain and duality relation. It does not identify the divergence of a deterministic supported \(h\) with \(W(h)\). This definition is therefore distinct from the false formula in Lemma 4.7; an amended adjoint definition is not needed merely to make that distinction.

The displayed derivation of conditional duality (28), on p. 30, uses that adjoint relation, the product rule, and the locality statement in Lemma 4.12. It does not invoke the erroneous \(W(h)\) term. Proposition 4.17 on pp. 31–32 uses (28) to construct the integration-by-parts weights. Theorem 4.11 supplies the local divergence regularity estimate used there and explicitly in Lemma 4.18. Theorem 6.1, pp. 43–44, in turn invokes Proposition 4.17 and Lemma 4.18.

These are actual downstream dependencies, but no derivation of Theorem 4.11 from the false Lemma 4.7 identity has been established in the inspected text. Their order of presentation alone is not such a dependency. The counterexample does not refute Theorem 4.11’s domains, constants, or its application in Lemma 4.18. Conversely, the abstract adjoint definition alone does not certify those quantitative estimates. A claim of core proof damage requires a direct failure in that missing link and remains Needs Evidence.

The established finding is thus the false local identity, assessed as Minor on the present evidence. For Brownian motion \(H=1/2\), the covariance in [eq:cov] (#eq:cov) is zero and disjoint increments are orthogonal. No pair of distinct RDE solutions has been constructed, and no claim is made that this is a new issue unknown to the authors. The conditional adjoint and innovation formulas above explain the distinction; they are not a completed verification or repair of the full framework.

# Checks that survive and a display clarification

The final exponent arithmetic has the intended strict margin. On p. 44 the averaged field is assigned time regularity \(1/2+\varepsilon/8\) and spatial regularity \[s=\kappa+\frac1\alpha-\frac{3\varepsilon}{8}.\] The deterministic criterion requires \(s+(1/2+\varepsilon/8)>2\), equivalently \[\kappa+\frac1\alpha>\frac32+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.\] A sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\) is available when the main hypothesis is strict. Taking \(\alpha=2H\) gives the stated fractional Brownian threshold. This arithmetic does not validate the probabilistic estimate that supplies the regularities.

Corollary 6.3, p. 45, prints a minus sign before \(\|b-\widetilde b\|_{C^\kappa}\) in the stability estimate. Theorem 2.10, pp. 14–15, instead uses the appropriate sum of the initial difference and the averaged-field difference. With \(\sigma=1\), the same initial condition, and constant drifts \(0\) and \(1\), the two additive solutions differ by \(t\). The printed bound would have negative right side for every positive \(K\). Replacing the minus sign by a plus sign removes this elementary contradiction and agrees with the earlier stability formulation. This is recorded as a display clarification, not a second graded substantive defect or a repair of Problem 1.
