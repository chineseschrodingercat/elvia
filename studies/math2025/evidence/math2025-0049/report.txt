Abstract: The assigned publisher version of Y. B. Kartal’s paper, Inventiones mathematicae 239 (2025), 801–864, is examined. Two demonstrated invalid proof steps concern the periodic-complex replacement and the energy monoid. Both lie on the main proof chain and receive Major under the assigned core-derivation standard. Complete algebraic repairs are provided. Neither finding is a counterexample to the stated Floer-rank theorems. The field embeddings, two-prime argument, local-to-global comparison, and selected applications were also checked, subject to the geometric limitations below.

# Identity, scope, and assumptions

The reviewed source is the 64-page publisher PDF of [K], DOI 10.1007/s00222-024-01308-7, published online on 13 January 2025. Printed page equals PDF page plus 800. The frozen source hash is recorded in Appendix 6 (#app:record). The author preprint [Kv3], dated 17 December 2024, was consulted for targeted comparison; it does not replace this source.

All 64 pages were exposed and read as text, including both appendices. Twenty-nine source pages were actually inspected as images. This is a systematic claim-focused initial review, not independent verification of every transversality, compactness, orientation, or generation theorem in the external Fukaya-category literature. The supplied manifest contains the publisher PDF and metadata, without a separate supplement or author code repository. No author code was run.

Theorem 1.1 retains Assumption 1.2: a smooth Fukaya category with finitely many split generators of minimal Maslov number at least three, with the stated torsion-image or simultaneous Bohr–Sommerfeld monotonicity conditions. The paper’s ambient monotonicity is the global relation \(c_1(M)=\lambda[\omega_M]\) with \(\lambda\ne0\) (p. 809), not merely a vacuous condition on \(\pi_2\). The branes \(L,L'\) remain tautologically unobstructed with minimal Maslov number at least three. Removing their additional Bohr–Sommerfeld condition at pp. 842–843 does not remove these remaining hypotheses.

Theorem 1.9 instead uses Assumption 1.10, including \(\pi_2(M)=0\), a smooth proper uncurved category, split generation, and the specified unobstructed branes. Definition 5.4 imposes that the flux-period group meet the energy group only in zero. It is a condition on the chosen data. The torus translation of Example 1.8 is an acknowledged periodic example outside the relevant hypotheses, not a counterexample discovered here. Replacing a symplectomorphism by an autonomous flux flow is used up to the Hamiltonian invariance of the quantities at issue.

# Problem 1: the periodic resolution lift need not exist

## Claim and precise location

Lemma A.2, pp. 856–857 (PDF pp. 56–57), first reduces to a two-periodic complex \(B\) with \(H^0(B)=0\). It fixes a finite free resolution of \(H^1(B)\) in (A.9), lifts its first terms into the unrolled complex \(B\), and asserts that the lift extends inductively in (A.11). Periodizing then supposedly gives the quasi-isomorphism (A.12). The existence of a finite free resolution of the module is supported by Kedlaya’s Proposition 6.5 [Ked]. It does not imply this particular lift into a periodic complex. Odd-degree cohomology can obstruct the later lifting steps.

## A counterexample to the asserted construction

Equation reference: eq:koszul. Take the exact coefficient ring and residue field \[A=\mathbb Q_3\langle x_1,x_2,x_3\rangle,\qquad
 \mathfrak m=(x_1,x_2,x_3),\qquad k=A/\mathfrak m=\mathbb Q_3.\] All radii are one and satisfy the lemma’s norm hypothesis. Let \(K^{-j}=\bigwedge^j A^3\) be the Koszul resolution of \(k\), with \[d(e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_j})
 =\sum_{a=1}^j(-1)^{a-1}x_{i_a}
 e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\widehat e_{i_a}\cdots\wedge e_{i_j}.
 \] The variables form a regular sequence, so \(H^0(K)=k\) and all other cohomology vanishes. Let \(P\) be its two-periodization: each parity has rank four, with \(H^0(P)=k\) and \(H^1(P)=0\).

Equation reference: eq:cone, eq:coho. Define an odd map \(f:P\to P\) by \(f(e_1e_2e_3)=1\) and zero on the other exterior degrees. Since \(df+fd=0\), it is a chain map \(P\to P[1]\). Set \[B=\operatorname{Cone}(P\xrightarrow{f}P[1])
   =P[1]\oplus P[1],\qquad
 D=\begin{pmatrix}-d&f\\0&-d\end{pmatrix}.
 \] Thus \(D^2=0\). The cone long exact sequence gives \(H^0(B)=0\) and \(0\to k\to H^1(B)\to k\to0\). This extension splits as an \(A\)-module: the two empty-wedge elements \((1,0),(0,1)\) are odd cycles lifting the two generators. Every \(x_i\) multiple of either is a boundary, since \(D(-e_i,0)=(x_i,0)\) and \(D(0,-e_i)=(0,x_i)\). Consequently \[H^0(B)=0,\qquad H^1(B)=k^2.
 \] All module-finiteness and parity assumptions of the reduced argument hold.

An allowed choice for (A.9) is two copies of the ordinary Koszul resolution of \(k\). Its periodization, placed in odd cohomology, is \(F=P[1]\oplus P[1]\) with the block diagonal differential. If (A.11)–(A.12) existed for this resolution, it would give a quasi-isomorphism \(F\to B\).

Equation reference: eq:fiber. Evaluate at \(\mathfrak m\). For \(F\), the differential is zero and the fiber cohomology has dimensions \((8,8)\) in the two parities. For \(B\), the single entry \(f(e_1e_2e_3)=1\) survives. Its differential has rank one from the even to the odd part, and rank zero in the reverse direction. The fiber dimensions are therefore \[(\dim H^0,\dim H^1)(F\otimes_A k)=(8,8),\qquad
 (\dim H^0,\dim H^1)(B\otimes_A k)=(7,7).
 \]

Here this fiber comparison is legitimate, rather than an unjustified ordinary-tensor argument. An acyclic two-periodic complex of finite projective \(A\)-modules is contractible. Indeed, its cycles are finite modules, have finite projective dimension by [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition~6.5]{Ked}], and the two exact sequences with projective middle terms yield two-step dimension shifting. All their positive Ext groups vanish after sufficient shifting, so the cycles are projective and both sequences split. Thus a quasi-isomorphism between finite projective periodic complexes has contractible cone and remains a quasi-isomorphism on every fiber. Equation [eq:fiber] (#eq:fiber) rules out the claimed map. This refutes the lifting procedure, not merely an arbitrary choice of the first lift.

## A complete replacement argument

The statement of Lemma A.2 can be recovered without asserting formality. The following construction also supplies the projective version in Corollary A.5.

Equation reference: eq:syzygy. Let \(B_0=B\). Choose a finite graded free module \(G_j\), with zero differential, and a cycle map \(G_j\to B_j\) surjective on cohomology in both parities. Put \(B_{j+1}=\operatorname{Cone}(G_j\to B_j)\). The long exact sequence gives, for \(i\) modulo two, \[H^i(B_{j+1})=
 \ker\bigl(G_j^{i+1}\longrightarrow H^{i+1}(B_j)\bigr).
 \] These are the successive syzygies, with alternating parity, of the two original finite cohomology modules. Choose the surjections along finite projective resolutions. After finitely many steps both cohomology modules are projective. At that stage they lift to cycles and give a quasi-isomorphism from a finite graded projective module with zero differential to \(B_N\).

Reconstruct \(B_{N-1},\ldots,B_0\) through the triangles \(G_j\to B_j\to B_{j+1}\to G_j[1]\), retaining the connecting maps. A finite complex obtained by finitely many cones from graded projective modules is homotopically projective: applying its Hom complex to an acyclic periodic complex is acyclic. This starts with projectivity of a graded module and is preserved by cones. Therefore each required connecting morphism can be represented by an actual closed chain map from the model already constructed for \(B_{j+1}\). Its cone, shifted once, models \(B_j\). The result has finite projective modules in both parities. Over the Tate algebra those projectives are free by [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition~6.6]{Ked}]. Over the annular algebras, the projective conclusion is precisely what Corollary A.5 needs.

This construction keeps extension information, including the map \(f\) in [eq:cone] (#eq:cone); simply periodizing a resolution of \(H^1\) loses it. It proves the existence statements used later, under their stated finite resolution assumptions.

## Dependency, counterevidence, and assessment

Lemma 3.20 explicitly invokes A.2 at p. 831. Corollaries 3.22–3.23 use it to control convolutions and cones. Corollary A.5 repeats the same argument for the multivariable Novikov annulus, and the proof of Lemma 3.5 at p. 862 invokes A.5 to obtain the finite model needed for semicontinuity. Lemma 4.6 and Proposition 4.1 then use this comparison in the main rank theorem. The annular parameter rank is not uniformly bounded by one or two.

The one-variable Tate computations alone do not exhibit the three-variable obstruction. Moreover, \(B\) above is itself finite free, so it cannot refute the existence conclusion of A.2. The replacement proof supplies that conclusion. The supported judgment is Major: an invalid derivation on the central proof chain, with an algebraically verified repair. It is not a counterexample to Theorem 1.1, Theorem 1.9, or the group-like family construction.

# Problem 2: the strict positive lattice monoid is not finitely generated

## Source and exact check

In Lemma 5.1, p. 844 (PDF p. 44), equation (5.2) imposes \(\omega_i(\beta)>0\) for every \(i\), together with \(\omega_i(\beta)\in N^{-1}\mathbb Z\), and adjoins zero. Using the indicated rational basis, the text asserts that this monoid is finitely generated. The next paragraph on p. 845 applies Lemma 5.2 to its energy image on that basis.

Already for the permitted algebraic parameters \(n'=2\), \(N=1\), the assertion is false: \[S=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a\ge1,\ b\ge1\}\cup\{(0,0)\}.\] For every \(m\ge1\), \((1,m)\) is indecomposable into two nonzero members of \(S\), because their first coordinates would sum to at least two. Any generating set must therefore contain all \((1,m)\). This is an infinite proof, not an inference from a bounded enumeration. Nor does passage to a positive energy functional automatically remove the problem: \((a,b)\mapsto a+b\sqrt2\) is positive on \(S\setminus\{0\}\) and injective, so its image has the same infinite family of indecomposables. The resulting energy set is locally finite and lies in a finitely generated additive group.

This tests exactly the auxiliary lattice-monoid implication used in the proof. It does not purport to construct holomorphic curves realizing every one of these classes, or a symplectic counterexample to Lemma 5.1.

## Verified repair and bounded impact

Replace the auxiliary set by the containing nonnegative orthant \[\overline S=(N^{-1}\mathbb Z_{\ge0})^{n'}.\] It contains all classes that the paper placed in \(S\) and is generated by the \(n'\) coordinate vectors \(e_i/N\). If \(\omega_M=\sum_i c_i\omega_i\) with \(c_i>0\), its image is the monoid generated by the positive numbers \(c_i/N\), all in the required rational energy span. Apply Lemma 5.2 to this larger finitely generated monoid. Its positive rationally independent generators contain all the required energies. Thus the algebraic part of the desired conclusion is restored without asserting finite generation of (5.2) itself.

An intended nonnegative reading is strong counterevidence to serious damage to the theorem. However, the strict inequalities and the finite-generation inference are explicit in the frozen PDF and in [Kv3]. This is a false algebraic premise, not an unverified missing-citation complaint. Lemma 5.1 supplies the generators sent into \(p\mathbb Z_p\) in (5.5); these ensure convergence of the nonmonotone families used for Theorem 1.9. Under the assigned core-derivation criterion this is Major with restricted impact and a verified local repair. The geometric perturbation argument preceding (5.2) is a separate dependency, not certified by the orthant repair. The monotone Theorem 1.1 does not require Lemma 5.1.

# Other checked reasoning

## Arithmetic and field comparisons

For \(p>2\) and \(v=1+pu\), the polynomial coefficients of \(\binom{t}{j}(pu)^j\) have valuation at least \[j-v_p(j!)\ \ge\ j\frac{p-2}{p-1}\ \longrightarrow\ \infty.\] This verifies the Tate convergence used for \(v^t\). The prime-to-\(p\) roots used to extend \(\mathbb Q(T^G)\to\mathbb Q_p\) to \(\mathbb Q(T^{G_{(p)}})\) are compatible under common denominators. The argument does not embed the entire Novikov field in \(\mathbb Q_p\).

The later finite extension \(\widetilde K/K\) is finitely generated over \(K\); \(K\) itself need not be finitely generated over \(\mathbb Q\). Countability permits a transcendence basis to be sent into \(\mathbb Q_p\), then the remaining finite algebraic extension embeds in some finite extension \(\mathbb Q_p'\). Finite perfect presentations justify the relevant field base changes; no unrestricted infinite Hom-base-change assertion is required.

## From local families to integer iterates

The group-like property is first obtained over \(p^n\mathbb Z_p\), an additive subgroup. The geometric Fukaya comparison is initially local in the real parameter. Lemma 4.9 uses a finite subdivision of a compact real interval with subdivision points in the dense subgroup \(p^n\mathbb Z_{(p)}\). Combining these local comparisons with the group-like identity yields the stated rational-time comparison. The paper explicitly does not require a fully coherent group action for this dimension argument.

For a finite periodic free complex over the one-variable Tate algebra, the exact sequence for multiplication by \(t-a\) includes the torsion kernel as well as the cokernel. The PID decomposition and finiteness of zeros of a nonzero Tate function then give finitely many exceptional parameters in each residue class. It would be incorrect to ignore the kernel term at exceptional fibers; the paper retains it.

Here is the last arithmetic step in full. Suppose a sequence agrees outside finite sets with residue values \(c_a\) modulo \(m=p^n\) and \(d_b\) modulo \(n=(p')^{n'}\), where \(\gcd(m,n)=1\). For every pair \((a,b)\), the Chinese remainder theorem gives an infinite progression satisfying both congruences. Choose a term outside the two finite exceptional sets. Then \(c_a=d_b\). Since this holds for every pair, all residue values agree. Hence the sequence is constant off a finite set. This does not imply the same assertion for all real times, a limitation already acknowledged in Remark 4.13.

## Genericity, semicontinuity, and applications

For the nonmonotone construction, disjoint energy and period groups give independent exponent directions. The energy degree tends to infinity, which is the convergence condition needed for the analytic functions in Lemma 5.5. With a common prime-to-\(p\) denominator, the finitely many period monomials at any fixed energy degree are distinct Laurent monomials in analytic roots of \(1+pY_j\); they cannot vanish identically. A nonzero analytic coefficient and the one-variable zero theorem, followed by induction and Fubini, establish a null zero set. Countability of the chosen coefficient ring allows simultaneous avoidance. This does not prove injectivity for the entire uncountable ring \(R_{\rm big}\), which the paper expressly avoids claiming.

The contraction argument of A.1 has a radius uniform in the cycle being solved. The finite-tail valuation argument of B.4 and the two adjacent differential minors of B.6 justify the semicontinuity statements once the finite projective replacement has been supplied correctly.

For Corollary 1.12, the abelian \(\pi_1(L)\) hypothesis is used to split off a \(\mathbb Z\) factor on which the flux is nonzero. The local-system differential \(T^{f\alpha(C)}-1\) is invertible for \(f\ne0\), killing the group cohomology. Semicontinuity can be applied degree by degree, while \(H^1(L;\Lambda)\ne0\) drops. Arbitrarily small nonzero real elements of \(p^n\mathbb Z_{(p)}\) contradict cofinite constancy at the self-Floer rank if a nontrivial iterate is isomorphic to \(L\). This checks the stated empty/singleton/all alternative conditional on the geometric local-system identification. Corollary 1.11 concerns the unweighted categorical entropy computed by (6.1); bounded finitely many generator-pair ranks give zero exponential growth.

# Dependence, limitations, and disposition

The two problems concern distinct algebraic steps and are not duplicate counts. Their repairs establish the needed algebraic assertions, but do not certify every analytic construction of the Fukaya category. The moduli-space compactifications, coherent perturbation choices, all orientation signs, external generation theorems, and reverse isoperimetric estimates were not rebuilt in full. In particular, this review does not independently establish the uniform geometric energy positivity preceding (5.2) for all allowed perturbation data.

Targeted comparison with [Kv3] retains both relevant passages. The limited publisher/arXiv and correction searches did not establish a published correction. This is not proof that none exists and not a claim of priority. Immediate isolated notation slips, such as the inverse parameter in (A.14), are not additional substantive findings; the subsequent general formula (A.15) has the needed product \(T^{c-b}\).
