Abstract: Two core derivation defects are confirmed in the supplied arXiv version. The weighted coupling cost in the final mixing argument has at least power-law growth near the diagonal, contradicting the asserted logarithmic bound. The required integrated bound nevertheless follows by a direct replacement argument. Separately, explicit admissible configurations invalidate the proposed four-shear winding calculation and a case split in the controllability appendix. These steps feed the positive Lyapunov exponent and small-set inputs. Both findings are Major under a dependency-based assessment; neither constitutes a counterexample to the main mixing theorem. The audit is limited to arXiv:2408.02459v1, not the unavailable journal text.

Title: A mathematical audit of
Exponential scalar mixing for the 2D Navier–Stokes equations with degenerate stochastic forcing

# Reviewed version and scope

The source is William Cooperman and Keefer Rowan, Exponential scalar mixing for the 2D Navier–Stokes equations with degenerate stochastic forcing, arXiv:2408.02459v1 [CR]. The exact supplied PDF has 77 pages, an arXiv submission stamp of 5 August 2024, and a title-page date of 6 August 2024. Page numbers below refer to this PDF and coincide with its printed pagination.

The associated journal DOI is 10.1007/s00222-025-01384-3 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01384-3). The journal version was not obtained and textual equivalence has not been established. The publisher lists a correction dated 14 January 2026, DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01402-4 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01402-4) [correction]. The accessible preview established its existence but did not provide its substantive text. No inference is made about its subject or whether it resolves any issue identified here.

Assumption 1.1 requires a finite symmetric force set \(F\subset\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\), integer span \(\mathbb Z^2\), and at least two modes of different lengths. All coefficients \(c_k\) are nonzero and viscosity is fixed positive. The velocity initially belongs to \(H^5_{\mathrm{sol},0}(\mathbb T^2)\). For a fixed mean-zero \(\phi_0\in L^2\), Theorem 1.2 asserts, for every \(p>0\), an exponentially decaying \(H^{-1}\) bound with a random prefactor \(K(p,\phi_0,u_0)\) having a uniform \(p\)-th moment. It does not assert one pathwise prefactor for every \(L^2\) initial scalar simultaneously. Corollary 1.3 obtains a uniform prefactor only after imposing positive Sobolev regularity.

# 0119-F1: the weighted cost is not logarithmic near the diagonal

## The exact construction and its core dependency

Proposition 4.3, particularly p. 29, constructs \[f(u,x,y)=|x-y|^{-1}e^{V(u)},\qquad
 W(z)=\min_{1\le n\le T}a^{-n}\mathbb E_z[f(Z_n)^{p_0}],
 \tag{1}\] for some \(p_0>0\), \(a\in(0,1)\), and finite integer \(T\). Theorem 4.10, p. 32, uses the cost \[d(z,z')=
 \sqrt{d^\beta(z,z')\,[1+W(z)+W(z')]},\qquad
 d^\beta=1\wedge\beta^{-1}d_V,
 \tag{2}\] where \(d_V\) is the path metric from Definition 4.4. Its proof asserts \[d(\delta_{u,0,e_1},\delta_{u,x,y})
 \le Ce^{V(u)}\log|x-y|^{-1}.
 \tag{3}\] It then invokes a triangle inequality and integrates to obtain (2.8). Proposition 2.7 uses (2.8) to pass from two-point Markov mixing to scalar mixing; Theorem 1.2 is deduced immediately after this argument. Thus (3) occurs in a core proof interface.

## A lower bound contradicting the asserted estimate

Fix \(u_0=0\), \(x_0=0\), \(y_0=\varepsilon e_1\), with \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). These are permissible initial conditions for any force set satisfying Assumption 1.1. Let \[I_n=\int_0^n\|\nabla u_s\|_\infty\,ds.\] The spatial flow is Lipschitz for the torus geodesic distance, so \(|x_n-y_n|\le\varepsilon e^{I_n}\). Since \(V\ge0\), \[\mathbb E[f(Z_n)^{p_0}]
 \ge \varepsilon^{-p_0}\mathbb E e^{-p_0I_n}
 \ge \varepsilon^{-p_0}e^{-p_0\mathbb E I_n}.
 \tag{4}\] Corollary 2.6 bounds \(\mathbb E I_n\) for the finitely many \(n\le T\), uniformly in the initial spatial points when \(u_0=0\). Taking the finite minimum in (1) therefore yields \[W(0,0,\varepsilon e_1)\ge c\varepsilon^{-p_0}.
 \tag{5}\]

The path norm controls spatial displacement even if a path changes its velocity coordinate. Indeed, with \(D=\operatorname{diam}\mathbb T^2\), \[|v_x|+|v_y|
 \le |v_x+v_y|+|v_x-v_y|
 \le \max(1,D)\|v\|_{H^5,2},\] and \(e^{V/2}\ge1\). The distance \(d^\beta\) between \(z_*=(0,0,e_1)\) and \(z_\varepsilon=(0,0,\varepsilon e_1)\) is consequently bounded below by a positive constant as \(\varepsilon\to0\). Dirac measures have only one coupling. From (2) and (5), \[d(\delta_{z_*},\delta_{z_\varepsilon})
 \ge c\varepsilon^{-p_0/2}.
 \tag{6}\] This cannot be bounded by \(C\log(1/\varepsilon)\). The contradiction remains if one adds a constant to the logarithm or makes the local waiting-time parameter correction discussed below. It is not a missing-symbol issue.

## A complete replacement for the required integrated bound

The pointwise assertion (3) is unnecessary. Choose the exponent \(b\) in the upper bound of Proposition 4.3 with \(0<b\le1\), so that \[W(u,x,y)\le C|x-y|^{-b}e^{bV(u)}.\] Let \(\pi=\mu^b\times\mathrm{Leb}\), where the superscript in \(\mu^b\) denotes the base-process invariant measure, not the exponent \(b\). Use normalized spatial Lebesgue measure. As \(d^\beta\le1\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \[\begin{split}
 d(\delta_z,\pi)^2
 &=\left(\int d(z,z')\,\pi(dz')\right)^2\\
 &\le\int d(z,z')^2\,\pi(dz')
 \le1+W(z)+\pi(W).
\end{split}
 \tag{7}\] The quantity \(\pi(W)\) is finite: the diagonal singularity \(|x-y|^{-b}\) is integrable in two relative dimensions for \(b<2\), and the base invariant measure has the required exponential moment. Integrating (7) over \(x,y\) gives \[\int d(\delta_{u,x,y},\pi)^2\,dx\,dy
 \le Ce^{bV(u)}\le Ce^{V(u)},
 \tag{8}\] which is precisely the required form of (2.8). No triangle inequality for the weighted coupling cost is used.

Classification: Major, invalid core derivation. Equations (4)–(6) disprove the printed bound with the paper’s actual construction. Equations (7)–(8) repair this particular interface using its existing Lyapunov bound, conditional on the other Harris inputs. They do not repair the independent controllability defects below and do not constitute a new proof of the entire paper.

# 0119-F2: the proposed control recipes do not establish their claims

## A four-shear path that never winds around the circle

Lemma B.6, Step 4, p. 73, claims that four shear segments, with parameters \(T,T,-T,-T\), wind the tangent direction around \(S^1\) and therefore reach every target direction. The printed formula also has phase and elapsed-time slips. Grant the natural corrections that make the shears fix the origin and vary continuously within each segment; the winding claim still fails.

Take the admissible force set \[F=\{\pm(1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm(1,1)\},\qquad c_k=1.\] It is symmetric, spans \(\mathbb Z^2\), and contains lengths \(1\) and \(\sqrt2\). Choose \(k_0=(1,0)\), \(k_1=(0,1)\). The origin-fixing shears available from cosine vorticity modes have physical maps \[f_{0,t}(x,y)=(x,y+t\sin x),\qquad
 f_{1,t}(x,y)=(x-t\sin y,y).\] For row tangent vectors their derivative matrices at zero are \[S_0(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S_1(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-t&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Start at \(v_0=(1,0)\). For \(s\in[0,T]\), the four successive unnormalized tangent segments are \[\begin{array}{ll}
 (1,s),&
 (1-sT,T),\\[2pt]
 (1-T^2,\ T-s(1-T^2)),&
 (1-T^2+sT^3,\ T^3).
\end{array}
 \tag{9}\] For every \(T>0\), their second coordinates are positive except at the initial point. In the third segment the second coordinate lies between \(T\) and \(T^3\), both positive. After normalization, the path never enters the lower semicircle and never reaches \((0,-1)\). Its final vector is \((1-T^2+T^4,T^3)\), in the first quadrant. Thus neither the continuous path nor the endpoint obtained by varying \(T>0\) has the asserted winding property. This is a counterexample to the specific recipe, not to reachability by other controls.

## A concrete replacement for this projective step

Two nonparallel origin-fixing shears become, after a fixed change of basis and nonzero parameter rescaling, the elementary matrices \[U(a)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 L(b)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\b&1\end{pmatrix}.\] For an oriented angle \(\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\), put \(a=\tan(\theta/4)\), \(b=-\sin(\theta/2)\). Direct multiplication gives \[U(a)L(b)U(a)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \cos(\theta/2)&\sin(\theta/2)\\
 -\sin(\theta/2)&\cos(\theta/2)
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{10}\] Repeating this three-shear block gives the desired rotation. The coordinate-direction map from a fixed invertible change of basis is a bijection of the oriented circle. Therefore this construction also reaches any oriented target for nonorthogonal shear axes. Six shears suffice, with bounded parameters, including for antipodal targets.

Each shear can be realized over positive physical time by a smooth single-mode velocity amplitude \(a_j(t)\) with zero endpoints and prescribed integral. The corresponding forcing derivative is proportional to \(a_j'(t)+\nu|k_j|^2a_j(t)\). Stretching the time interval makes it satisfy the control bound; negative parameters use negative amplitudes. Every segment fixes the origin and returns the velocity to zero. The bounded parameters give a common finite duration; zero-velocity padding makes it an integer if needed. The support/stability argument then gives positive-probability approximation. This supplies a valid replacement for the failed Step 4, without asserting that all remaining appendix steps are thereby proved.

## The two-point case split also fails for an admissible force set

Lemma B.8 defines on p. 73 \[R_k=\left\{y:|\sin(k\cdot y)|
       \ge\frac1{100\max_{k'\in F}|k'|}\right\}.\] Step 3 on p. 75 claims that, when \(y_0\) lies in none of these sets and meets the specified separation from zero, the integer-span assumption yields an independent pair with \(\cos(k_2\cdot y_0)<-1/2\).

Take \[F=\{\pm(2,1),\pm(3,1)\},\quad
 K=\sqrt{10},\quad a=\frac1{200K},\quad y_0=(-2a,5a).
 \tag{11}\] Choose nonzero coefficients, for example all equal to one, and \(u_0=0,x_0=0\). The positive modes have unequal lengths \(\sqrt5,\sqrt{10}\) and determinant \(-1\), so every condition in Assumption 1.1 holds. Moreover, \[|y_0|=\sqrt{29}\,a>2a=\frac1{100K},\] and \(y_0\) is a nonzero torus point in the usual coordinate chart. Its mode phases are \[(2,1)\cdot y_0=a,\qquad (3,1)\cdot y_0=-a.\] For every signed mode in \(F\), \[|\sin(k\cdot y_0)|=\sin a<a<2a,
 \qquad \cos(k\cdot y_0)=\cos a>\tfrac12.
 \tag{12}\] Thus \(y_0\) is in none of the \(R_k\), yet no required negative-cosine mode exists. This disproves the stated implication independently of the phase slips in the shear notation. The configuration lies in the class Step 3 explicitly purports to handle.

## Dependency, severity, and bounded consequence

Lemma B.6 is used for projective-process irreducibility in Corollary 2.11 and to exclude invariant finite line families in Theorem 3.6, p. 26. Lemma B.8 supplies the common-target control in Proposition 4.9, p. 31. That proposition is the small-set input to Theorem 4.10, which in turn feeds Theorem 1.2. Appendix placement does not make these steps peripheral.

Classification: Major, invalid core controllability derivations. The examples verify the force-set, state-space, and target conditions. They invalidate the asserted recipes and case split, not the general controllability or mixing conclusions. Equation (10) repairs the projective winding step. A complete replacement for the general two-point construction, with its uniform time and probability bounds, was not established in this audit and should be supplied rather than presumed. The two related failures are consolidated into one finding.

# Bounded positive checks and explicit local completions

## Continuous invariant families and positive-exponent logic

The measure iteration in Proposition 3.3, pp. 23–24, closes with \(\beta=1/4\), a contraction exponent \(\delta<1\), \(\eta=(1-\delta)\beta\), and then sufficiently small smoothing parameter and sufficiently large induction constant. The parameter order is consistent.

The infinite-dimensional base is not locally compact. Nevertheless, pointwise subsequential convergence follows by diagonalizing over a countable dense subset, using compactness of \(\mathcal P(S^1)\) and the uniform local Lipschitz bounds. Those bounds extend convergence to the whole base. Dominated convergence against each fixed transition kernel passes the invariant-family relation.

The backward-shift construction in Proposition 3.1 has the correct stationary marginal laws and twisted pushforward identity. In the actual two-dimensional application, \(\|A^{-1}\|=\|A\|\) for \(A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\) in the Euclidean operator norm. For the general-dimensional formulation, equality of the exponents is unnecessarily strong: \(\|A^{-1}\|\le\|A\|^{d-1}\) suffices to transfer a zero top exponent. The application has the needed log-integrability.

Compactness of invariant-measure approximations requires one-time parabolic regularization at an order above the state-space norm; bounded \(H^5\) balls alone are insufficient. The estimates of Proposition 2.3 supply that regularization. Uniform integrability suffices for the expected stretching limit; the argument need not assume the stronger displayed exponential tail for \(\|\nabla u\|_\infty\). These are bounded completions of the analytic interfaces; the control defects in Section 3 remain separate.

## Lyapunov construction and the weak Harris inputs

In Proposition 4.3, the sublevel of \(V\) is compact in \(H^1\), the topology actually used for the finite-cover argument, because it bounds the velocity in \(H^5\). The short-distance secant comparison can be made in a common torus lift up to the injectivity scale. Exponential moments of the integrated Lipschitz norm control the exceptional event where this scale is exceeded.

The variable waiting-time argument needs the local correction \(a=(1-p_0/2)^{1/T}\), so that \(a^n\ge1-p_0/2\) for \(n\le T\). This supplies the factor required by Lemma 4.1. If \(W(z)\le K\), its minimizing time may depend on \(z\). The estimate \(P_kf^{p_0}\le C_Tf^{p_0}\) for \(k\le T\) gives \(P_Tf^{p_0}(z)\le C_TK\), and hence a common-time event with bounded \(V\) and inverse separation. No compactness in the full \(H^5\) topology is being assumed.

Definition B.1 places the common time and positive probability before the quantifier over initial states. A valid Lemma B.8 would therefore give exactly the uniform small-set input required in Proposition 4.9. The issue is its proof, not an altered quantifier in this deduction.

## Malliavin estimates and the anisotropic derivative norm

The Section 5 audit found no additional substantiated substantive failure in the assigned estimates. Qualitative covariance nondegeneracy \(g(\varepsilon)\to0\) suffices for Lemma 5.28: split the layer-cake integral into a bounded interval, controlled by that nondegeneracy, and a tail controlled by the uniform eighth moment. The needed fourth-moment smallness does not require polynomial inverse-covariance tails.

The high-frequency input tail can be bounded directly through the semigroup decomposition \[J_{1/2,1}\Pi_{\ge R}
 =J_{3/4,1}J_{1/2,3/4}\Pi_{\ge R}.\] The \(L^2\)-to-\(H^n\) smoothing bound and \(\|\Pi_{\ge R}\|_{H^n\to L^2}\le R^{-n}\), followed by Holder’s inequality, give an \(R\)-independent moment constant. This addresses the abbreviated coupled-system justification in Lemma 5.27.

Footnote 5 on p. 48 explicitly says that derivatives are measured in the dual norms. This resolves the apparent reversed inequalities involving the near-diagonal tangent norm \[\|(\xi,v_x,v_y)\|_{H^5,2}
 =\|\xi\|_{H^5}+|v_x+v_y|+\frac{|v_x-v_y|}{|x-y|}.\] The tangent-process control transfers to the two-point process using the stated first-derivative comparison. It is not valid to apply the tangent weights directly to an ordinary Euclidean gradient without this dual interpretation.

## Scalar reduction and all positive moments

The Fourier summation in Proposition 2.7 uses \(\sum_{k\ne0}|k|^{-3}<\infty\) in dimension two, yielding an \(H^{-2}\) second-moment estimate and then the \(H^{-1}\) estimate by interpolation with conserved \(L^2\). The actual path metric controls spatial displacement globally, including between different velocities, which supplies the Lipschitz bound for the Fourier observables used here.

Use a strictly positive burn-in time \(T\in t_0\mathbb N\), with \(T\ge t_0+C\log(2+\|u_0\|_{H^1})\), and condition on the full past at \(T\). This avoids requiring high-norm regularization at time zero and permits a random transported scalar at the restart. Uniform-in-time control follows from the integrated velocity-gradient moments.

For clarity, let the resulting normalized estimate be \[X_t\le\min\{1,K_0e^{-ct}(1+U)^A\},
 \quad \mathbb EK_0\le C,\quad A\ge1,\quad U=\|u_0\|_{H^1}.\] For any desired moment \(p>0\), take \(\vartheta=\min\{1,(Ap)^{-1}\}\). Then \(\vartheta p\le1\), \(A\vartheta\le1/p\), and \[X_t\le K_0^\vartheta e^{-c\vartheta t}(1+U)^{1/p},
 \qquad \mathbb E K_0^{\vartheta p}\le C'.\] This verifies the all-\(p\) conversion without making the prefactor uniform over all \(L^2\) initial scalars. The zero initial scalar is handled separately. For the universal \(H^1\) result, the summable Fourier weights in Proposition 2.12 and operator interpolation are consistent.

# Normalization checks, limits, and acceptance status

Corollary 1.4 on p. 3 prints Batchelor variance bounds linear in \(\|g\|_2\). The isometry calculation on p. 17 and Proposition 2.13 use the mathematically consistent \(\|g\|_2^2\). Indeed, with zero initial scalar, \(\phi^{ag}=a\phi^g\) pathwise, so the projected variance scales as \(a^2\). The quadratic form, with \(N\ge N_0(g)\), is the supported normalization. The dependence of \(N_0\) on \(g\) cannot restore a uniform linear-amplitude law: apply the asserted bounds to \(g\) and \(ag\) at a common \(N\) above both thresholds and let \(a\) be large. Because the proof consistently identifies the quadratic quantity, this is recorded as a normalization correction, rather than added as a standalone finding based on omitted superscripts.

For its lower-bound argument, choose the Fourier cutoff so that \(\|\Pi_{>R}g\|_2\le\|g\|_2/4\), instead of inferring that tail bound from \(\|\Pi_{\le R}g\|_2\ge3\|g\|_2/4\). For example the stronger squared low-frequency mass \(15/16\) suffices. Then \[\|\Pi_{\le N}\Phi_{s,t}g\|_2^2
 \ge \frac{13}{32}\|g\|_2^2
 -\frac{R^2}{2N^2}
       \|\Phi_{s,t}\|_{H^1\to H^1}^2\|g\|_2^2.\] On a sufficiently short logarithmic time window this gives a positive fixed fraction of \(\|g\|_2^2\). Integrating and taking \(N\ge R^2\) recovers the intended logarithmic lower rate with adjusted constants. The independent scalar Brownian forcing permits the stochastic isometry after conditioning on the entire velocity path; no anticipative integral with respect to that Brownian motion is required.

The paper treats zero scalar diffusivity. It expressly defers uniform positive-diffusivity mixing and the corresponding Batchelor result, and presents OU-tower forcing as a proposed extension. None is credited here as a proved theorem. Isolated inverse, phase, time-index, and exponent-label slips with identifiable local corrections were not separately counted. The full external weak Harris and Furstenberg theorems were not independently reproved, and the general replacement for the two-point control construction remains an open requirement of the reviewed proof.
