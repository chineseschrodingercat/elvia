Abstract: The acquired preprint derives local entropy rigidity from a microlocal Hessian estimate and solenoidal injectivity. Three essential steps fail as written. A concrete family of arbitrarily small solenoidal perturbations disproves the cubic absorption estimate at the advertised regularity, and an exact sphere-moment calculation disproves a scalar identity used in the complex-hyperbolic coercivity argument. A two-jet frame rotation also disproves the claimed intrinsic quaternionic operator. These calculations are not counterexamples to the final real or complex rigidity theorem or to the desired coercive inequality. Three groups of additional local corrections concern tensor conventions, projected symbols and the octonionic representative. Explicit contrary evidence and repair boundaries are given throughout. The assessment concerns arXiv:2409.11197v3 only; scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

For a closed negatively curved manifold, write \[\Phi(g)=\operatorname{Ent}_{\rm top}(g)
          -\operatorname{Ent}_{\rm Liou}(g)\ge0.\] Theorem 1, page 2, states that near a real or complex hyperbolic metric \(g_0\), equality of the two entropies forces homothety, in some sufficiently strong \(C^{N(n)}\) neighborhood. This existential regularity statement must be distinguished from Theorem 2, page 4, which specifies a \(C^{3n/2+7}\) neighborhood and a quantitative \(H^{3/2}\) estimate involving \(\Phi\) and the squared volume difference. The latter is restricted to the orthogonal complement of a finite-dimensional kernel in the solenoidal slice. Corollary 1 and Theorem 4, pages 5–6, also print the \(C^{3n/2+7}\) topology.

The proof uses the slice lemma, a pressure/variance expression for the Hessian, the ellipticity of a projected differential operator \(Q\), a volume estimate for its average, and a cubic-remainder absorption. Section 4 proves injectivity in the real and complex cases. Injectivity for the quaternionic and octonionic cases remains explicitly conjectural; this review does not turn that conjecture into a proved claim.

# The stated regularity does not permit the printed absorption

Problem 1 (Proposition 3.4, final display on page 35).  The proof uses \[\|S\|_{C^{5,\alpha}}^3
 \le C\|S\|_{H^{3/2}}^2\|S\|_{C^N},
 \qquad N>\tfrac32 n+6+6\beta,\quad \beta>\alpha,
 \tag{A}\] to absorb the Taylor remainder for \(S=\phi_g^*g-g_0\). This uniform inequality fails even for arbitrarily small perturbations in the exact solenoidal slice, at the regularity specified in Theorem 2.

## A concrete admissible family

Let \(g_0\) be a closed real hyperbolic metric in dimension \(n=4\), with \(\operatorname{Ric}(g_0)=-3g_0\). Use the geometric-sign Laplacian \(\Delta_{\rm geom}=\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Hess}\). For any smooth function \(u\), put \[S_u=\operatorname{Hess}u-(\Delta_{\rm geom}u)g_0+3u g_0.\] The identity \(\operatorname{div}\operatorname{Hess}u=d\Delta_{\rm geom}u+\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla u,\cdot)\) gives \(\operatorname{div}S_u=0\) exactly. This is the same kernel condition as \(D^*S_u=0\), regardless of the constant normalization of the adjoint.

Choose normal coordinates near a point, and a smooth compactly supported function \(\chi\), equal to one near the origin. For small \(\varepsilon>0\), define a global smooth function, extended by zero outside the chart, by \[u_\varepsilon(x)
 =A_\varepsilon\varepsilon^2
   \chi(x/\varepsilon)(x_1/\varepsilon)^7,
 \qquad A_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{14},
 \qquad S_\varepsilon=S_{u_\varepsilon}.\] The leading tensor is a fixed nonzero compactly supported profile of amplitude \(A_\varepsilon\) at scale \(\varepsilon\). Smooth bounded coordinate coefficients and Sobolev scaling give \[\|S_\varepsilon\|_{C^{13}}\le C A_\varepsilon\varepsilon^{-13}
       =O(\varepsilon),\qquad
 \|S_\varepsilon\|_{H^{3/2}}^2
       \le C A_\varepsilon^2\varepsilon.\] At the center, the vanishing of lower jets makes the leading derivative exact: \[\partial_1^5(S_\varepsilon)_{22}(0)
 =-5040 A_\varepsilon\varepsilon^{-5}.\] Comparison with a point outside the support at distance \(O(\varepsilon)\) therefore yields, for fixed \(0<\alpha<1\), \[\|S_\varepsilon\|_{C^{5,\alpha}}
 \ge c A_\varepsilon\varepsilon^{-5-\alpha}.\] Consequently \[\frac{\|S_\varepsilon\|_{C^{5,\alpha}}^3}
      {\|S_\varepsilon\|_{H^{3/2}}^2\,
       \|S_\varepsilon\|_{C^{13}}}
 \ge c\varepsilon^{-3-3\alpha}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{B}\] The perturbations are smooth, solenoidal and tend to zero in \(C^{13}\). Thus \(g_0+S_\varepsilon\) remains positive definite and negatively curved for small \(\varepsilon\), and is already in the affine solenoidal slice. If orthogonality to the finite-dimensional smooth kernel is also imposed, subtract its fixed orthogonal projection. The projection coefficients are \(O(A_\varepsilon\varepsilon^4)\); the resulting smooth finite-rank correction is negligible in all the displayed norms and preserves solenoidality. Thus the stated kernel restriction does not rescue (A). For example, \(\alpha=1/24\), \(\beta=1/12\), \(N=13\) satisfy the printed threshold \(13>12+6\beta\). These are admissible parameters for the claimed estimate.

Indeed, \(\|S_\varepsilon\|_{C^{5,\alpha}}^3/
\|S_\varepsilon\|_{H^{3/2}}^2
\ge c\varepsilon^{-2-3\alpha}\). Smallness in \(C^{13}\) therefore does not itself make this particular cubic bound absorbable.

## Impact and repair boundary

This is Major for the supplied stability derivation. The last estimate is the step used to turn Hessian control into Theorem 2 at its explicit topology, and into the printed topologies of its applications. The counterexample respects the gauge condition; it is not an arbitrary nonsolenoidal test tensor.

The conclusion is deliberately bounded. It does not compute \(\Phi(g_0+S_\varepsilon)\), and hence does not disprove the entropy estimate or rigidity itself. A sharper, tame Taylor remainder might use fewer derivatives than the displayed \(C^{5,\alpha}\) bound. Theorem 1 only asserts the existence of a sufficiently large \(N(n)\), so increasing \(N\) could preserve that statement.

With the usual intended Sobolev embedding \(C^{5,\alpha}\leftarrow H^{n/2+5+\beta}\), interpolation between \(H^{3/2}\) and \(H^N\) gives a sufficient threshold \[N\ge\tfrac32 n+12+3\beta\] for the displayed cubic route, with a strict margin for embeddings. The malformed intermediate Sobolev exponent in the printed display is not used to create the counterexample: even granting the intended embedding, (A) fails. A repair must either strengthen the stated topology or prove a genuinely sharper remainder estimate at the existing one. The Banach-space differentiability needed for the Taylor remainder should also be cited with its precise regularity; its failure has not been established here.

# A scalar identity in the complex coercivity proof is false

Problem 2 (Equation (4.13), page 40).  The expansion of \(H_+Vf\) used to prove Lemma 4.3 has the opposite sign from the defining operators. The resulting scalar identity (4.13) fails for exactly solenoidal tensors on compact complex hyperbolic quotients.

Definition (2.41), page 20, gives \[\eta^\pm_+=\tfrac12(X_+\pm iH_+),\qquad
 H_+=-i(\eta^+_+-\eta^-_+).\] The latter sign is opposite to (2.42). For \(f=f_2+f_0+f_{-2}\), where \(Vf_j=ij f_j\), the definitions imply \[H_+Vf=
 2\eta^+_+f_2-2\eta^-_+f_2
 -2\eta^+_+f_{-2}+2\eta^-_+f_{-2}.\] The expansion on page 40 has its negative.

Here is an exact witness that also respects the solenoidal condition. On a compact complex hyperbolic four-manifold, \(\operatorname{Ric}=-6g_0\). With the positive Laplacian \(\Delta=-\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Hess}\), define \[S_u=\operatorname{Hess}u+(\Delta u+6u)g_0.\] Again \(\operatorname{div}S_u=0\) exactly. In a small chart choose a phase \(t\) with \(|dt|_{g_0}=1\) and a nonzero compactly supported cutoff \(\chi\), and set \(u_N=\chi\cos(Nt)\). The leading term of \(S_{u_N}\) is \[N^2\chi\cos(Nt)(g_0-dt^2).\] At a fiber choose an orthonormal frame with \(dt=e^1\), \(Je_1=e_2\), and write \(a=v_1,b=v_2\). After factoring out the common amplitude, the trace-free fiber polynomial and scalar trace part are \[f=\tfrac14|v|^2-a^2,\qquad h=\tfrac34.\] Let \(P_3\) denote harmonic projection of a cubic polynomial: \[P_3p=p-\frac{|v|^2\Delta_{\mathbb R^4}p}{12}.\] The principal actions are \(X=i a\), \(H=i b\), \(Vf=2ab\). Thus the leading fiber values in (4.13) are \[X_+f=-iP_3(a^3),\qquad
 X_+\mathcal Jf+H_+Vf=iP_3(ab^2).\] With normalized sphere measure on \(S^3\), \[\big\langle X_+\mathcal Jf+H_+Vf,X_+f\big\rangle
   =\frac1{192}.\] Writing \(z=a+ib\), one has \(f_2=-z^2/4\), and the two extreme raising terms each have squared norm \(1/256\). Also \(\|X_+f\|^2=1/64\). The printed right-hand side is therefore \[\frac1{64}-4\left(\frac1{256}+\frac1{256}\right)
 =-\frac1{64},\] which differs from the actual left-hand side by \(1/48\). These fractions follow from \[\mathbb E[a^2]=\tfrac14,\quad
 \mathbb E[a^4]=\tfrac18,\quad
 \mathbb E[a^2b^2]=\tfrac1{24},\quad
 \mathbb E[a^4b^2]=\tfrac1{64}.\] The preserved computation uses exact rational moments.

For the actual smooth fields \(S_{u_N}\), the discrepancy is \[\frac{N^6}{96}\int_M\chi^2\,d\operatorname{vol}_{g_0}
       +O(N^5),\] up to the common positive fiber-measure normalization. It is nonzero for all sufficiently large \(N\). Thus this is a family of genuine solenoidal counterexamples to the identity, not merely an arbitrary symbol comparison.

## The trace-sector identity also needs correction

The conversion (4.11), page 39, uses \(\Lambda_0^n\) as the coefficient of the trace part, although \(S=S_0+hg_0\). With ordinary tensor contraction it should be \(n\Lambda_0^n\); with the literal factorial-weighted norm on page 8, the coefficients are \(\Lambda_2^n/2\) and \(n\Lambda_0^n/2\).

The same principal packet gives \[Q_0S=-e^2\otimes e^2+\tfrac16e^1\otimes e^1,\quad
 \mathbb E[V_2f]=-\tfrac1{32},\quad
 \mathbb E[V_0h]=-\tfrac5{32}.\] Under the ordinary unnormalized-sphere convention used by the displayed \(\Lambda\)’s, their volume-adjusted values are \(\Lambda_2^4=12\), \(\Lambda_0^4=1\). Formula (4.11) therefore gives \(-17/32\). Actual tensor contraction is \(-1\), or \(-1/2\) under the literal page-8 norm. Neither agrees. Correct trace coefficients give exactly those respective answers.

## Core impact, contrary evidence and required repair

This is Major for the supplied complex-hyperbolic injectivity proof. Equations (4.11) and (4.13) feed (4.17)–(4.18), the bound (4.10), and hence Proposition 4.2 and the complex part of Proposition 4.1. The disputed identity supplies a cancellation used to retain a positive Weitzenbock margin.

There is significant contrary evidence to a stronger charge. The compact reconstruction formula (4.2), \(8p=-(X_+\mathcal Jf+H_+Vf)\), is algebraically correct under the defining \(\eta\)’s. An initial proposed reversal of that formula was rejected after direct expansion and is not a finding. The double twisted-derivative identity also retains its sign when both single-derivative signs are corrected. The final arithmetic turning the asserted (4.10) into the positive coefficient \(95n-198\), page 42, is consistent. None of these facts proves the missing estimate, but they locate the defect.

The examples above refute the printed identities, not the weaker inequality (4.10), the coercivity statement (4.5), or final rigidity. The repair must use one coherent sign and norm convention and rederive the estimates, including the smallest relevant real dimension \(n=4\). Changing a sign in the notation while leaving the subsequent scalar calculation unchanged does not provide that proof.

# The quaternionic differential operator depends on the frame

Problem 3 (Theorem 5, (3.4) and footnote 3, page 24).  With definition (2.29) on page 17, the expression \(\sum_{i=1}^3 D^{J_i}D^{J_i}S\) is not independent of the chosen smooth local quaternionic frame. The identification on page 29 that asserts this independence fails even for exactly solenoidal \(S\).

Write \(C_m(J)S=S(J\cdot,\ldots,J\cdot)\) on degree \(m\). Then \(C_m(J)^2=(-1)^m\operatorname{Id}\), including for a varying smooth \(J\), and the source defines \(D_m^J=C_{m+1}(J)D_mC_m(J)\). Consequently \[D_{m+1}^JD_m^J
   =(-1)^{m+1}C_{m+2}(J)D_{m+1}D_mC_m(J).\] The adjacent middle multiplication operators cancel before the next derivative acts. In particular, on two-tensors, \[A(S):=\sum_{i=1}^3D^{J_i}D^{J_i}S
      =-\sum_{i=1}^3 C_4(J_i)D^2C_2(J_i)S.\]

At a point \(p\), fix a quaternionic frame \(I,J,K=IJ\) and a unit vector \(v\). Make the valid local frame change \[I'=\cos\theta\,I+\sin\theta\,J,\qquad
 J'=-\sin\theta\,I+\cos\theta\,J,\qquad K'=K,\] where \(\theta(p)=0\), \(d\theta(p)=0\), and \(H=\operatorname{Hess}_p\theta\). The two frames have identical values and first derivatives at \(p\); any first-jet normalization therefore remains satisfied. Their second-jet difference gives, by direct differentiation, \[[A'(S)-A(S)](v,v,v,v)
   =-2\{H(Iv,Iv)+H(Jv,Jv)\}S(Kv,v).
 \tag{C}\] For example, let \(e_0=v,e_1=Iv,e_2=Jv,e_3=Kv\), take \(\theta=x_1^2/2\) near \(p\), and choose \[S(p)=e^0\otimes e^3+e^3\otimes e^0.\] The \(I'\) term contributes \(-2\), the \(J'\) term contributes zero, and \(K\) is unchanged. Thus (C) is \(-2\). This exact integer calculation is also preserved using the four-by-four quaternionic matrices.

The required tensor value is attained by a global smooth solenoidal field on the actual manifold. Since a quaternionic hyperbolic metric is Einstein, \(\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda g_0\), put \[S_u=\operatorname{Hess}u-(\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Hess}u)g_0-\lambda u g_0.\] Then \(\operatorname{div}S_u=0\). A quadratic polynomial times a cutoff can satisfy \(u(p)=du(p)=0\) and \(\operatorname{Hess}_pu=e^0\otimes e^3+e^3\otimes e^0\), whose trace vanishes. Hence \(S_u(p)\) is exactly the required tensor. A further bump away from \(p\) can fix its integral trace if a zero-volume tangent is desired without changing the local witness.

All other terms of (3.4) agree at \(p\) for the two frames: they are intrinsic or depend only on the frame values, which coincide. Its coefficient \(-1/8\) of \(A\) gives \[[Q'(S_u)-Q(S_u)](v,v,v,v)=\frac14.\] This is a frame dependence of the same operator on the same tensor and metric, not a change of the geometric data.

The defect is Major for Theorem 5’s quaternionic formula and the general stability framework using this \(Q\). Footnote 3 explicitly includes its global definition in the theorem. The proof on page 29 replaces an intrinsic covariant-Hessian contraction by this nonintrinsic iterated operator and expressly says that parallelness of \(J\) was not used.

There is a concrete repair boundary. The preceding expression \[F(S)(v,v,v,v)=
   \sum_{i=1}^3(\nabla^2_{J_i v,J_i v}S)(v,v)\] is intrinsic: it contracts the covariant Hessian over the quaternionic three-plane and uses only frame values. One can retain and symmetrize this expression, or derive the necessary quaternionic-connection correction terms. A second-jet frame convention would itself require a proof that the resulting construction is intrinsic.

The calculation does not show that the corrected Hessian identity is false, or that two possible patched expressions necessarily have different variance modulo coboundaries. The real case has no such frame choice and the complex case has its single parallel \(J\). Thus this defect does not refute the principal real/complex rigidity theorem.

# Three groups of local corrections

Problem 4 (Tensor conventions and the potential ambiguity).  The adjoint convention in Section 2.1.1 and the unrestricted uniqueness assertion in Lemma 2.2 need bounded corrections.

The pointwise scalar product on page 8 is \[\langle A,B\rangle_m=\frac1{m!}\sum A_{i_1\cdots i_m}
                                          B_{i_1\cdots i_m}.\] With the ordinary contraction \(\operatorname{tr}\), its adjoint is \[\operatorname{tr}^*B=(m+1)(m+2)\operatorname{Sym}(B\otimes g_0),\] rather than the \(L\) in (2.3). For \(B=1\) and \(A=e^1\otimes e^1\), \(\langle L1,A\rangle_2=1/2\), whereas \(\langle1,\operatorname{tr}A\rangle_0=1\). The corresponding formal adjoint of \(D\) on degree \(m\) also acquires a factor \(1/m\). The printed harmonic conformality constant (2.7) and the energy calculation for \(S=g_0\) on page 36 use the ordinary, unweighted contraction instead.

Removing \(1/m!\) reconciles those particular conventions; alternatively all adjoints and conformality constants must be changed. For \(S=g_0\), the page-36 energy is \(n(n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(M)/4\) with the literal norm, rather than the printed \(n(n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(M)/2\). Its positivity survives, so this is not a failure of the real-hyperbolic injectivity argument.

The trace calculation on page 31 also has an incorrect coefficient independently of that norm choice. For \(L(g_0)=\operatorname{Sym}(g_0\otimes g_0)\), direct contraction gives \[\operatorname{tr}L(g_0)=\frac{n+2}{3}g_0,\] not \((n+(n+2)/6)g_0\). The needed mean estimate is nevertheless repairable: normalized fiber moments followed by integration against parallel tensors give \[\int_{SM}\pi^*Q(S)\,d\mu_{\rm Liou}
 =\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{n-1}{2n\operatorname{Vol}(M)}
                 \int_M\operatorname{tr}S\,d\operatorname{vol},&\text{real case},\\[4pt]
 \dfrac1{2\operatorname{Vol}(M)}
                 \int_M\operatorname{tr}S\,d\operatorname{vol},&\text{complex case}.
 \end{cases}\] The volume Taylor formula then yields the required absolute-value estimate before it is squared.

Finally, Lemma 2.2, page 13, asserts uniqueness of the potential \(p\) for all tensor degrees. For degree one, \(D(p+c)=Dp\); for degree three, \(D(p+c g_0)=Dp\). One must fix the component in \(\operatorname{Ker}D\), or state uniqueness of \(Dp\) and the solenoidal part. The actual degree-two gauge application has no such nonzero Killing one-form on a closed negatively curved manifold, so this general-degree ambiguity does not invalidate that application. These are Minor local corrections, with the separately demonstrated complex proof consequences already addressed above.

Problem 5 (A false commutation in the ellipticity proof).  On page 34, \(L\) is asserted to commute with \(D^*\) and with the principal solenoidal projection. It does not.

Take \(\xi=e^1\) and \(S=e^2\otimes e^2\). Then \(\iota_\xi S=0\), but \(\iota_\xi L(S)\ne0\), as can be seen from the \((1,1,2,2)\) component of \(\operatorname{Sym}(S\otimes g_0)\). Hence \(P_\xi^{(4)}L(S)\ne L(P_\xi^{(2)}S)\).

There is a complete principal-symbol repair. Identify symmetric tensors with homogeneous polynomials; orthogonal projection onto \(\operatorname{Ker}\iota_\xi\) is restriction to \(\xi^\perp\) followed by extension independent of the \(\xi\)-coordinate. For \(v\in\xi^\perp\), the complex or quaternionic projected symbol is \[\left(\frac{|\xi|^2|v|^2}{4}
       -\frac18\sum_i\langle J_i\xi,v\rangle^2\right)S(v,v).\] The \(J_i\xi\) are orthogonal, so the multiplier is at least \(|\xi|^2|v|^2/8\). If it vanishes as a tensor and \(S\in\operatorname{Ker}\iota_\xi\), then \(S=0\). The corresponding curvature bound supplies the same type of repair in the octonionic principal symbol. Thus this Minor proof correction does not refute the ellipticity conclusion; it replaces the false commutation by a valid projected calculation.

Problem 6 (The octonionic representative and its mean calculation).  The octonionic calculation drops a zero-order term when passing from (3.11) to (3.5) and the final display on page 30.

The initial expression contains \(-\tfrac12\pi_2^*S\), while the weighted trace computation contributes \(+\tfrac16\pi_2^*S\). Their sum is \(-\tfrac13\pi_2^*S\), not the \(+\tfrac16\pi_2^*S\) retained in (3.5). A direct admissible test is \(S=g_0\) on a closed octonionic hyperbolic quotient. The seven exponents of size two and eight of size one give, by (3.8), \[\operatorname{tr}(\nabla_\xi U)=\tfrac12(7\cdot2+8)=11.\] The printed \(Q(g_0)\), with \(\operatorname{Ric}=-36g_0\), gives \[\pi_4^*Q(g_0)=\tfrac16+\tfrac{16}{3}+6
             =\tfrac{23}{2}.\] Distinct constants cannot differ by an \(X\)-coboundary, since an invariant probability measure integrates \(Xu\) to zero. This refutes the asserted representative calculation.

Crucially, both constants have variance zero. This test therefore does not by itself refute the Hessian quadratic identity (3.1). The main real and complex formulas are not affected by this particular omitted term. This is classified as a Minor bounded formula correction, with a required recheck of the general octonionic Hessian assertion.

The related page-32 statement \(R^\circ(L(g_0))=-36g_0\) also has a degree mismatch. From (3.12) and the symmetrized product, \[[R^\circ L(g_0)](v,v,v,v)
   =\tfrac13\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)|v|^2=-12|v|^4.\] The fully symmetrized output is \(-12L(g_0)\), not a two-tensor. The integrated derivative term still vanishes against the appropriate parallel tensor. Thus the displayed coefficient error is not, on its own, a counterexample to the average bound.

# Assessment and remaining limits

The three Major findings concern actual essential derivations: fixed-regularity nonlinear absorption, complex-hyperbolic scalar cancellation, and the intrinsic definition of the quaternionic Hessian operator. Each has an explicit smooth, exactly solenoidal witness and a quantitative contradiction. They justify substantive revision of the supplied proof without asserting that every intended conclusion is false.

Several structural checks support the intended argument. The volume term correctly separates scaling from the zero-entropy gauge directions. Potential tensors yield geodesic coboundaries, while the mean augmentation of the X-ray operator detects constant metric tensors. In the real case, the curvature identities give \(R^\circ S_0=S_0\) and \(S_0\circ\operatorname{Ric}=-(n-1)S_0\); the Weitzenbock estimate therefore yields the positive \((n-2)/4\) margin for \(n\ge3\). The principal-symbol injectivity can be repaired as shown above. The final complex numerical margin is positive if its input estimate is established.

For the quaternionic case, the original mean calculation can be repaired separately by summing the frame-invariant tensor \(\sum_i S\circ J_i\) and integrating its derivatives against \(L(g_0)\); parallelness of each individual \(J_i\) is not needed for that integrated argument. This possible mean repair does not remove the demonstrated pointwise frame dependence. The full corrected quaternionic Hessian must be established with an intrinsic operator.
