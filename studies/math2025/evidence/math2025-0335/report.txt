# Definitions and proof dependencies

For fixed integers \(p<q\), \(\alpha_p(G)\) is the maximum cardinality of a vertex subset inducing no \(K_p\). The normalized Ramsey–Turán density is \[\rho_p(q)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\lim_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\operatorname{RT}_p(n,K_q,\varepsilon n)}{\binom n2},\] with the source’s asymptotic conventions. Conjecture A predicts \(\rho_p^*(q)\) by optimizing the stated multipartite construction. In the range \(q=p+s+1\) with \(0\leq s\leq p\), the relevant predicted value is \(s/(2p)\).

Theorem 1.1 constructs a two-part complex-sphere graph with cross density \(\ell/p\), negligible internal edges, and \(\alpha_p=o(n)\); when \(\ell\leq p/2\) it excludes \(K_{p+\ell+1}\). The proof separates rotation-label consistency, concentration, cross-sector density, and clique exclusion. Theorem 1.3 instead uses \(q\) parts and \(\ell\) coordinate positions, with a one-factorization of the even complete graph assigning the \(p\) special coordinates for each pair of parts. The clique count reduces to \[\sum_i x_i\leq\ell+p,\qquad
 \sum_i 2^{x_i}\leq 2^\ell+2^p+q-2.\] Its edge-density lower bound and its comparison with a conjectured value are logically separate statements.

In Section 5, a \(p\)-weighted graph has zero diagonal and symmetric edge weights in \(\{0,\ldots,p\}\). A positive graph has all off-diagonal weights positive. A valid vertex extension encodes a clique that can be embedded in regular pairs with sufficiently small \(\alpha_p\). For an ordering \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\), an extension with weights \(a_j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\) is dominating when, for each \(j\geq2\), its backward edge multiset dominates \[\left\{
 \underbrace{\frac{p(a_j-1)}{a_j}+1,\ldots,
             \frac{p(a_j-1)}{a_j}+1}_{j-2\ \text{entries}},\
 a_j\right\}.\] The first vertex has no backward condition. Claim 5.5 supplies the common-neighborhood counting step; Lemma 5.6 embeds dominating extensions. Lemma 5.8 is the minimum-degree transfer from weighted graphs to \(\rho_p(q)\). The repairs in Sections 4 and 5 below preserve this transfer without changing its hypotheses.

# The final strict comparison in Theorem 1.3

Problem 1 (Unresolved scope of the final strict comparison).  On PDF p. 5, printed p. 83, Theorem 1.3 assumes \[q\ \text{even},\qquad \ell\geq p(q-1),\qquad
 P=2^\ell,\qquad Q=2^\ell+2^p+q-1\] and gives \[\rho_P(Q)\geq L:=
 \frac{2^p}{2^\ell}\left(1-\frac1q\right).\] After specifying the equality range \(q(q-2)\leq2^p\leq q^2\), its final clause states the strict comparison with \(\rho_P^*(Q)\) whenever \(q>2\). Under an independent, unrestricted quantification of that last clause, the claim is false.

## An admissible parameter tuple

Take \[(p,q,\ell)=(1,4,3).\] All opening hypotheses hold: the parameters are positive integers, \(q\) is even, and \(\ell=p(q-1)\). They give \[(P,Q)=(8,13),\qquad L=\frac3{16},\qquad
 \rho_8^*(13)=\frac{13-8-1}{16}=\frac14.\] The last comparison, on its unrestricted reading, therefore requires \(\rho_8(13)>1/4\). We prove the opposite upper bound independently of Theorem 1.5 and independently of the defective threshold definition below.

## A finite weighted extremal lemma

Let \(W\) be the weight matrix of any \(8\)-weighted graph on \(n\) vertices whose weighted minimum degree exceeds \(2n\). On the probability simplex maximize \[Q(u)=u^{\mathsf T}Wu.\] At the uniform vector this exceeds \(2\), so the maximum \(g\) satisfies \(g>2\). Choose a maximizing vector with minimum support size \(m\).

Every edge on this support is positive. Indeed, if \(w_{ij}=0\) for two positive coordinates, redistributing \(u_i+u_j\) between them makes \(Q\) an affine function, since both diagonal entries and their mutual edge are zero. An interior maximum forces that affine function to be constant. Moving all the mass to one endpoint then preserves \(g\) and reduces the support, a contradiction.

Let \(b\) be the largest edge weight on the support. Order its endpoints first and give the vertices the weights \[8,\ b,\ 1,\ldots,1.\] This is a dominating extension: its second backward edge has weight \(b\), and every later required backward weight is \(1\). Its total weight is \(8+b+m-2\).

If this total were less than \(13\), integer weights would give \(b+m\leq6\), so \(m\in\{2,3,4,5\}\) and \(b\leq6-m\). But \[\begin{split}
 g&=\sum_{i\ne j}w_{ij}u_i u_j
 \leq b\left(1-\sum_i u_i^2\right)
 \leq b\left(1-\frac1m\right)\\
 &\leq \frac{(6-m)(m-1)}m\leq2.
 \end{split}\] The last four values are exactly \(2,2,3/2,4/5\), respectively. This contradicts \(g>2\). Consequently every such weighted graph contains a dominating extension of total weight at least \(13\). If its support has more than \(13\) vertices, use the first prefix whose vertex-weight sum reaches \(13\); Section 5 proves why this preserves all needed conditions.

## Why the lemma yields an actual asymptotic upper bound

For clarity, this step uses the regularity embedding mechanism directly; it does not infer an asymptotic result from finitely many numerical graphs.

Suppose a sequence of \(K_{13}\)-free graphs has \(\alpha_8=o(n)\) and density at least \(1/4+\varepsilon\), with fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). The usual minimum-degree deletion step retains a linear-size subgraph with a fixed positive margin above minimum degree \(n/4\). Apply the regularity lemma with parameters much smaller than that margin. After discarding the exceptional set, irregular pairs, and pairs below the small density threshold, the reduced \(8\)-weighted graph has weighted minimum degree greater than \(2\) times its order. One may assign to a retained pair of density \(d\) the integer \(\lfloor8(d-\eta)\rfloor+1\); this retains a positive density margin over \((a-1)/8\) for its assigned weight \(a\). All discarded contributions can be absorbed in the original fixed margin.

The preceding finite lemma gives a positive weighted pattern with vertex weights \((8,b,1,\ldots,1)\) of total at least \(13\). Its embedding can also be seen directly. First select the singleton vertices successively from typical sets; regularity leaves linear-size common candidate sets in the first two clusters. In one of these sets, find a \(K_8\), possible because \(\alpha_8=o(n)\). Each of its vertices has relative degree greater than \((b-1)/8\) into the other set, with a fixed margin. Counting incidences gives a set of \(b\) of these eight vertices with a linear-size common neighborhood: the average number of neighbors among the eight exceeds \(b-1\), and the finite number of \(b\)-subsets permits pigeonholing. That common neighborhood contains another \(K_8\). Together with the selected singletons these vertices form a clique of size at least \(13\), a contradiction.

Equivalently, this is Lemma 5.8 with its direct regularity proof and the prefix repair below. Parameters may be chosen uniformly because the prefix has at most \(13\) vertices and there are only finitely many such weighted patterns. Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves \[\boxed{\rho_8(13)\leq\frac14.}\] Theorem 1.5 independently agrees: use its parameters \((p,s,t)=(8,4,2)\), which satisfy \(t(t-2)\leq s\leq t^2\) and \(s+t-1\leq p\), obtaining \((s/p)(1-1/t)=1/4\). The proof above does not rely on that theorem.

## Scope, alternative reading, and a sufficient repair

The parameter tuple is outside the stated equality range: \(q(q-2)=8>2^p=2\). If the final strict-comparison clause is intended to remain under that equality-range condition, the counterexample does not apply to that narrower statement. In fact the narrower statement is valid.

For the parameters of Theorem 1.3, the relevant conjectured value is \[C=\frac{2^p+q-2}{2P}.\] The construction’s excess is exactly \[L-C=\frac{(q-2)(2^p-q)}{2qP}.\] Thus \(q>2\) together with \(2^p>q\) is an explicit sufficient range for the strict comparison. In the equality range, even \(q>2\) implies \(q\geq4\) and \(2^p\geq q(q-2)>q\), so the excess is positive there. This sufficient condition is not asserted to be necessary for the unknown actual density outside that range.

The unrestricted reading is refuted, but its applicability to the source’s intended final clause is Needs Evidence. The semicolon and the phrase specifying \(q>2\) favor an independently quantified reading, yet the adjacent equality condition also admits a narrower scope. The construction proof on PDF pp. 15–20 establishes the lower bound without separately resolving this final comparison. The discussion on pp. 5 and 32 asserts infinitely many counterexamples, which is compatible with the narrower reading. These passages do not exclude that admissible interpretation.

No Major finding is therefore established by this scope-dependent item. It does not refute the construction lower bound, the equality range, or the infinite counterexample family to Conjecture A. For example, \(p=3,q=4,\ell\geq9\) gives the established comparison \(6/2^\ell>5/2^\ell\). Clarifying the clause’s quantifier scope would decide whether the proved unrestricted discrepancy applies to the source claim; the narrower mathematical statement already survives.

# The threshold definition in Definition 5.2

Problem 2 (The printed supremum is always one).  Definition 5.2, PDF p. 21 (printed p. 99), defines \(\pi(\mathcal F)\) as the supremum of those \(d\in[0,1]\) for which every sufficiently large \(p\)-weighted graph with \(\delta(G)>dp|V(G)|\) is \(\mathcal F\)-free. For every family \(\mathcal F\), this definition gives \(\pi(\mathcal F)=1\).

For an \(n\)-vertex \(p\)-weighted graph, \[\delta(G)\leq p(n-1)<pn.\] At \(d=1\) there is therefore no graph satisfying the antecedent. The universal assertion is true vacuously, so the set whose supremum is taken contains \(1\). Since it is a subset of \([0,1]\), its supremum is \(1\). This is a proof for all \(n\) and all families, not a finite test.

The following use in Lemma 5.3, PDF p. 22, instead requires a threshold forcing a forbidden induced member. A suitable definition is \[\tau(\mathcal F)=\inf\{d\in[0,1]:\mathcal P_d(\mathcal F)\},\] where \(\mathcal P_d(\mathcal F)\) means that every sufficiently large \(p\)-weighted graph \(G\) with \(\delta(G)>dp|V(G)|\) contains an induced member of \(\mathcal F\). Both the direction of the property and the extremum change. The defining set is upward closed. Use \(d>\tau(\mathcal F)\), rather than asserting attainment at its infimum, and then take a limit.

There is also a direct repair that avoids introducing \(\tau\). In Lemma 5.8, invoke its own stated hypothesis on the reduced weighted graph to obtain a member of \(\mathcal G_p(q)\), then take the bounded-size prefix established in the next section and apply the valid-extension embedding. The original minimum-degree, regularity, and small \(\alpha_p\) margins suffice. Thus the incorrect threshold definition makes its intermediate interpretation vacuous, but the nontrivial upper-bound transfer is restored under the same hypotheses. This is assessed Minor for this paper’s conclusions.

# The literal inclusion in Lemma 5.6

Problem 3 (A missing size restriction in the class inclusion).  The family \(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_p(q)\) in Definition 5.2 is restricted to graphs with at most \(q\) vertices. The family \(\mathcal G_p(q)\) in Definition 5.4, PDF p. 23 (printed p. 101), is defined through dominating extensions of total weight at least \(q\), with no corresponding vertex bound. The literal inclusion asserted in Lemma 5.6, PDF p. 24, is false.

Take \(p=3,q=5\) and the complete graph on six vertices with every edge of weight \(3\). Assign every vertex weight \(3\). For each \(j\geq2\), the required backward multiset consists entirely of \[\frac{3(3-1)}3+1=3\] and the final entry \(a_j=3\). All actual backward weights equal \(3\), so every domination condition holds. The total vertex weight is \(18\). This graph belongs to \(\mathcal G_3(5)\) but cannot belong to \(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_3(5)\) because it has six vertices.

The needed replacement is an induced-containment statement. Given a dominating extension \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) of total weight at least \(q\), let \[r=\min\left\{k:\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_j\geq q\right\}.\] Since every \(a_j\geq1\), we have \(r\leq q\). For each retained vertex \(j\leq r\), its backward edges, its index, and its required multiset are unchanged by taking this prefix. Positivity is preserved. Apply the actual dominating-to-valid embedding proof to this prefix. It gives \[G\in\mathcal G_p(q)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists J\subseteq V(G):\
 G[J]\in\widetilde{\mathcal G}_p(q).\] This does not assume that arbitrary restrictions of valid extensions are automatically valid. It uses the preserved dominating conditions and their embedding proof. It also supplies a uniform finite set of patterns for regularity parameter choices. The replacement is exactly what Lemma 5.8 needs, without strengthening that lemma’s assumptions. The literal family inclusion is a Minor finding; the required existence implication has been proved here.
