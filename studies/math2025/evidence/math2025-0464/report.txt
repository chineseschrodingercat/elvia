Abstract: The acquired author manuscript develops a useful trace-based framework for multisymplectic finite element methods. The local Green-identity argument and the passage from local conservation to global conservation under a strong flux condition withstand the checks described below. A Major problem occurs in the general reduced-space result: a one-sided orthogonal projection of a symmetric penalty need not be symmetric. A complete one-triangle example satisfies the stated hypotheses and all variational equations but has nonzero multisymplectic boundary pairing. Two Minor corrections concern a polynomial trace degree and an exterior-boundary term. The Maxwell discussion is retained as a scope clarification, without a demonstrated defect under its Hodge-operator and symmetry assumptions. The conclusions concern arXiv:2312.03657v2 only; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Structure of the argument

The continuous construction starts from \(D=d+\delta\) and a source with symmetric derivative. The difference \[(Dw_1,w_2)-(w_1,Dw_2)\] then vanishes for solutions of the variational equation. The Green formula converts this difference into the boundary pairing \[[w_1,w_2]_{\partial K}
 =\langle w_1^\mathrm{tan},w_2^\mathrm{nor}\rangle_{\partial K}
  -\langle w_2^\mathrm{tan},w_1^\mathrm{nor}\rangle_{\partial K}.\] The sign is consistent with the one-dimensional identity \[\frac{d}{dx}(q_1p_2-q_2p_1)
 =((-p_1',q_1'),(q_2,p_2))
  -((q_1,p_1),(-p_2',q_2')).\] The two-sided jump and average formulas were also checked algebraically, including the factor \(1/2\) that compensates for integration over both copies of an interior facet.

Lemma 3.9, p. 14, correctly isolates the discrete issue. Testing the two linearized equations against the opposite interior variation and subtracting gives \[[\widehat w_1-w_1,\widehat w_2-w_2]_{\partial K}
       =[\widehat w_1,\widehat w_2]_{\partial K}.\] The symmetry of \(f'\) is essential here. Lemmas 3.12–3.13 then give sufficient flux conditions, provided the stated trace differences belong to the permitted test space. Strong conservativity is a separate requirement: normal traces must reverse sign across an interior facet while tangential traces agree. Under these conditions, the facet terms cancel as required in Theorem 3.17, p. 16. Merely imposing a weak orthogonality condition does not give this cancellation without the trace-space membership argument.

For AFW-H, the trace equality and the Riesz argument on the quotient by tangential bubbles establish the hybrid/conforming equivalence. The two-equilateral-simplex example of Lemma 4.4, p. 19, explains a family-level failure of strong multisymplecticity for conforming scalar elements in dimension greater than one. It does not assert failure for every particular Hamiltonian or every mesh. The \(k=n\) strong-conservation argument uses independently specifiable facet traces; its degree notation needs the correction in Problem 2.

For ordinary LDG-H with the full normal trace space, symmetric penalties give the local cancellation directly. For IP-H, the inclusions \(\delta W_h^k\subset W_h^{k-1}\) and \(dW_h^k\subset W_h^{k+1}\) justify the residual test functions used in Theorem 4.17, pp. 25–26. The residual inner products are symmetric. The primal-hybrid calculation of Theorem 4.27, pp. 31–32, similarly relies on test orthogonality and symmetric penalties. These arguments should be distinguished from the projection step in the next problem.

# Problems

Problem 1 (Major: the normal-containment branch of Theorem 4.21 needs a compatible penalty).  Section 4.2, p. 21, allows a bounded symmetric facetwise penalty operator. Section 4.4, pp. 28–29, sets both approximate trace spaces equal to a broken space \(M\), and equation (49) gives \[\widehat z^\mathrm{nor}=P\bigl[z^\mathrm{nor}-\alpha(\widehat z^\mathrm{tan}-z^\mathrm{tan})\bigr],\] where \(P\) is orthogonal projection onto \(M\). Theorem 4.21 asserts strong multisymplecticity when either \(W_h^\mathrm{nor}\subset M\) or \(W_h^\mathrm{tan}\subset M\). In the first branch, the proof identifies the method with an LDG-H method having projected penalty \(P\alpha\). Symmetry of \(\alpha\) does not imply symmetry of \(P\alpha\).

Here is a complete admissible counterexample. Let the mesh consist of the single triangle \[K=\{(x,y):x\geq0,\ y\geq0,\ x+y\leq1\}.\] Take \(W_h(K)\) to consist only of degree-zero forms in the one-dimensional space \[W_h^0(K)=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{\phi\},\qquad
 \phi(x,y)=x(1-x-y)(2x-1+y),\] with all other form degrees zero. These are smooth polynomial forms and meet the regularity and trace assumptions on p. 11. No subcomplex assumption is imposed on the general LDG-H space. Let both approximate trace spaces be \[M=\prod_{e\subset\partial K}\mathcal P_0\Lambda^0(e).\] The normal trace of every interior zero-form is zero, so \(W_h^\mathrm{nor}=\{0\}\subset M\), precisely the first branch of Theorem 4.21.

On the unit-length edge \(e_0=\{y=0\}\), put \[m=1,\qquad t=x(1-x)(2x-1),\qquad
 \langle t,m\rangle_{e_0}=0,\qquad c=\langle t,t\rangle_{e_0}=\frac1{210}.\] Extend \(m\) by zero to the other edges. The trace of \(\phi\) is \(t\) on \(e_0\) and zero on both other edges. Define the penalty on \(e_0\) by \[\alpha v=v+\langle t,v\rangle_{e_0}m+\langle m,v\rangle_{e_0}t,\] and take the identity on the other edges and on unused form degrees. This is a bounded, symmetric, facetwise operator. It is even strictly positive: its perturbation of the identity has operator norm \(\|t\|\|m\|=1/\sqrt{210}<1\). In particular, \[\alpha m=m+t,\qquad \alpha t=t+cm.\] Nevertheless \(P\alpha m=m\), whereas \(\alpha Pm=m+t\).

Choose \(f=0\), the zero base solution, and the following two variations: \[\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &w_i&\widehat w_i^\mathrm{tan}&\widehat w_i^\mathrm{nor}\\ \hline
 i=1&\phi&0&cm\\
 i=2&0&m&-m .
\end{array}\] They satisfy every equation in Definition 3.6, p. 14. Indeed, for every interior test \(w_h=a\phi\), the bulk term \((w_i,Dw_h)_K\) vanishes by form degree, the test normal trace is zero, and the remaining normal/tangential pairing is a multiple of \(\langle m,t\rangle=0\). Thus (19a) holds. The two unprojected flux residuals in (19b), restricted to \(e_0\), are \[cm+\alpha(-t)=-t,\qquad -m+\alpha m=t.\] Both are orthogonal to \(M\), and all other edge residuals vanish. Finally, there are no interior facets and the approximate tangential test space vanishing on the entire boundary is \(\{0\}\); hence (19c) holds. The system is homogeneous and linear, so these are also variations along actual solution families, not nonintegrable formal directions.

Their multisymplectic pairing is \[[\widehat w_1,\widehat w_2]_{\partial K}
 =\langle 0,-m\rangle_{e_0}-\langle m,cm\rangle_{e_0}
 =-\frac1{210}\neq0.\] Thus this member of the stated class is not even locally multisymplectic. Strong conservativity by itself does not repair the failure. The exact edge integrals, the weak residuals and the positive penalty Gram matrix were independently checked.

This is a Major defect in the general theorem, with a bounded repair. For its normal-containment branch one can require \(P\alpha=\alpha P\), or the weaker symmetry of \(P\alpha\) on the actual trace-difference space. Then the skew pairing of the two projected penalty terms vanishes. Alternatively, a genuinely self-adjoint compression \(P\alpha P\) can be used. The \(W_h^\mathrm{tan}\subset M\) branch is supported by Lemma 3.13 and is not refuted. Nor does the example refute the standard scalar or compatible graded penalties, which commute with the relevant projection. If only those penalties were intended in Section 4.4, that restriction needs to be stated explicitly.

Problem 2 (Minor: the BDM trace degree in Proposition 4.6).  The first displayed trace-space identity in the proof on p. 20 assigns the same facet space \(\mathcal P_r\Lambda^{n-1}(e)\) to both \(\mathcal P_{r+1}\Lambda^{n-1}(K)\) and \(\mathcal P_{r+1}^{-}\Lambda^{n-1}(K)\). For the full-polynomial choice the degree is \(r+1\); for the trimmed choice it is \(r\).

For example, with \(n=2\) and \(r=0\), the form \(x\,dx\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_1\Lambda^1(K)\), and its tangential trace on \(y=0\) is \(x\,dx\), which is not constant. This directly contradicts the displayed \(\mathcal P_0\) trace identity for the BDM branch. Replace the degree by \(r+1\) in that branch and make the same change in the subsequent jump inclusion. The proof only needs the correct full product of facet trace spaces, whose traces can still be specified independently. Accordingly this correction does not refute the strong-multisymplectic conclusion of Proposition 4.6.

Problem 3 (Minor: an exterior-boundary term is missing in the XG formula).  On p. 24, the piecewise expression following (43) gives \(\check z_h^\mathrm{nor}=-\alpha\check z_h^\mathrm{tan}\) on \(\partial\Omega\). But Definition 3.2 makes the tangential jump equal to the trace itself on the exterior boundary. Equation (42b), as well as the strong expression immediately above the piecewise formula, therefore gives \[\check z_h^\mathrm{nor}=\alpha(z_h^\mathrm{tan}-\check z_h^\mathrm{tan})
       \qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\] For scalar boundary values \(z_h^\mathrm{tan}=1\), \(\check z_h^\mathrm{tan}=0\) and \(\alpha=1\), the correct normal correction is \(1\), whereas the printed branch gives \(0\) and leaves a nonzero residual in (42b). Restore \(+\alpha z_h^\mathrm{tan}\). The proof of Theorem 4.15 uses the preceding correct strong expression, so this local displayed error is not a counterexample to the equivalence theorem.

## Constitutive scope of Example 2.24: no established defect

The Maxwell illustration on p. 9 first introduces linear constitutive relations, then identifies \(\epsilon\) and \(\mu\) with Hodge star operators and invokes the reciprocity principle (16). The Hodge star was defined from a symmetric inner product on p. 3, and the derivation of (16) on p. 9 explicitly assumes a symmetric derivative. These qualifications are part of the source claim.

Linearity alone would not suffice. For example, at frequency \(1\), let \(\mu=I\), \[\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\-1&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad E_1=e_1,\quad E_2=e_2,\quad H_i=B_i=0,
 \quad D_i=\epsilon E_i,\quad J_i=-i\epsilon E_i.\] Both curl systems hold, while the two displayed Lorentz expressions equal \(-i\) and \(i\). However, this nonsymmetric \(\epsilon\) does not satisfy the metric Hodge identification used in the example. The calculation therefore demonstrates why the qualification matters; it does not refute the example under its full assumptions.

Making reciprocal transpose symmetry \(\epsilon^T=\epsilon\), \(\mu^T=\mu\), the needed nondegeneracy, and the complex-bilinear convention explicit would improve the application statement. Hermitian symmetry is a different condition. This is an explanatory suggestion, not a counted Minor defect. The initial report’s Minor classification of this item has been withdrawn after checking the source’s qualifying sentences; Proposition 2.12 and the conditional reciprocity argument are not contradicted.

# Assessment and limits

The report assessment is Major because Theorem 4.21, with the general bounded symmetric penalties allowed in the manuscript, contains a false branch. Problem 1 supplies the full spaces, operator, base solution, variations, weak-equation checks and nonzero conservation defect. It also identifies why the ordinary symmetry argument breaks and which additional condition restores it. The conclusion is narrower than a rejection of the framework: the Green identity, the local trace criterion, the strong-conservation cancellation and the compatible-penalty examples are not invalidated by this example.

Problems 2–3 have separate Minor calibration because their demonstrated effects are a local degree identity and a displayed boundary term whose surrounding proof retains the right formula. The Maxwell discussion is a scope clarification and is not counted as a mathematical defect. No unverified conjecture is counted as an established defect. No claim is made that conservation alone proves convergence, coercivity, long-time stability or numerical superiority. No empirical solver experiment was reproduced, and missing optional code is not treated as a mathematical defect.
