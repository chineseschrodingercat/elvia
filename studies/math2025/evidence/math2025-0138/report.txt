Abstract: The acquired publisher version is reviewed through the quaternionic construction, global period conditions, elliptic existence argument and final deformation step. Three local defects are established: periodicity of the parameter does not force the same period for its signed analytic square root; the unrestricted cylinder theorem asserts a translation period absent for permitted nonperiodic parameters; and the discrete transform can collapse an edge at a nonzero parameter. The actual spherical family and the stated angle perturbations satisfy stronger period conditions, and the discrete closure identity survives. The highest supported assessment is Minor. The rank-three assertion and the exclusion of all ambient identifications in the final deformation argument remain Needs Evidence; no failure of that rank or counterexample to the main existence theorems is claimed. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Operative assumptions and positive checks

Equation reference: eq:metric. Write \(z=u+iv\), and use the quaternion convention \(XY=-\langle X,Y\rangle+X\times Y\) for imaginary quaternions. For an isothermic immersion with metric \(e^{2h}(du^2+dv^2)\), \[\alpha=df^*=e^{-2h}(f_u\,du-f_v\,dv).\] The curvature-line equations give \(f_{uv}=h_vf_u+h_uf_v\), hence \(d\alpha=0\). Moreover \(df\wedge\alpha=\alpha\wedge df=0\). These identities imply \[d(f\alpha f)=0,\qquad d[\alpha,f]=0.\] Consequently the three terms in \[df^\pm=-f\alpha f+\epsilon^2\alpha
             \pm\epsilon[\alpha,f]\] are closed. For \(\epsilon\ne0\), quaternion norm multiplicativity gives \[
 ds_\pm^2=(\epsilon^2+|f|^2)^2 e^{-2h}(du^2+dv^2).\] Thus the forward construction is immersive and isometric. The diagonal second fundamental forms agree, giving equal oriented mean curvature. This checks the construction under its prescribed correspondence; it does not classify all other parametrizations of the images.

Equation reference: eq:frame. The explicit cylinders use a rhombic modulus \(\tau=\frac12+i b\), \(0<b<b_0\), with \(b_0\approx0.3547\), and the critical \(\omega\in(0,\pi/4)\) satisfying \(\vartheta_2'(\omega)=0\). Definition 1, PDF p. 17, requires \(w:\mathbb R\to(0,2\pi b)\) real analytic and an analytic real function \(a\) with \[a^2=1-(w')^2.\] Remark 6 explicitly permits \(a\) to change sign. The surface and frame are \[
 f=\Phi^{-1}\gamma(u,w)j\Phi,\qquad
 \Phi' =a(v)W_1(w(v))k\Phi,\qquad
 W_1(w)=\frac{i\vartheta_1'(0)\vartheta_2(\omega-iw)}
                    {2\vartheta_2(\omega)\vartheta_1(iw)}.\] The orthogonal terms in the frame have squared coefficients \(a^2+(w')^2=1\); a zero of \(a\) is not a loss of immersion.

Equation reference: eq:generic. The generic-plane condition can be retained in both examples below. Put \(F(w)=W_1(w)k\) and \(m=\Phi^{-1}i\Phi\). Direct differentiation gives \[
 \det(m,m',m'')=4a^2w'\det(i,F,F_w).\] There are open intervals on which the last determinant is nonzero. After removing their common phase \(e^{i\pi/8}\), the relevant theta Fourier coefficients are real, and \(W_1(w)\sim1/(2w)\) at zero. Thus constant phase on an interval, followed by analytic continuation, would force the normalized \(\vartheta_2(\omega-iw)\) to be real and all odd derivatives of \(\vartheta_2\) at \(\omega\) to vanish. Evenness about both \(0\) and \(\omega\) would give the extra real period \(2\omega\), incompatible with its nonconstant theta Fourier series and \(0<2\omega<\pi/2\). Choose the parameter interval there. At points where \(aw'\ne0\), the plane normals span \(\mathbb R^3\), so the examples do not rely on a degenerate family of parallel planes or a common plane axis.

# Local period statements

Problem 1 (The fundamental-piece lemma needs the signed root’s period).  Lemma 1, PDF p. 20 (printed p. 260), infers periodic coefficients in [eq:frame] (#eq:frame) from periodicity of \(w\) alone. Definition 2 requires \(w(v+\mathcal V)=w(v)\) and that one piece does not already close; it does not impose \(a(v+\mathcal V)=a(v)\).

## A permitted analytic counterexample

Set \[g(x)=\frac{4\cos x+\cos2x}{5},\qquad
 G(x)=\frac45\sin x+\frac1{10}\sin2x.\] Then \(G'=g\), \(g\in[-3/5,1]\), and \[A(x)=\frac{2\sqrt2}{5}\sin(x/2)
       \sqrt{(\cos x+3)((\cos x+1)^2+1)}\] is real analytic, satisfies \(A^2=1-g^2\), and is antiperiodic: \(A(x+2\pi)=-A(x)\). It is positive on \((0,2\pi)\). Choose \(w_0\) in an interval described above, with \(W_1(w_0)\ne0\), and, for small \(\lambda>0\), define \[w_\lambda(v)=w_0+\lambda G(v/\lambda),\qquad
 a_\lambda(v)=A(v/\lambda),\qquad \mathcal V=2\pi\lambda.\] The image lies strictly inside \((0,2\pi b)\), \(w_\lambda'=g(v/\lambda)\), and every admissibility condition holds. Formula [eq:generic] (#eq:generic) supplies the generic-plane condition at suitable phases.

Equation reference: eq:monodromy. Normalize \(\Phi(0)=1\). In the variable \(x=v/\lambda\), its ODE is \[\Phi_x=\lambda A(x)W_1(w_0+\lambda G(x))k\Phi.\] Analytic dependence on \(\lambda\), on the fixed interval \(0\le x\le4\pi\), yields \[
 \Phi(\mathcal V)=1+\lambda C+O(\lambda^2),\qquad
 \Phi(2\mathcal V)=1+O(\lambda^2),\qquad
 C=\left(\int_0^{2\pi}A(x)\,dx\right)W_1(w_0)k\ne0.\] The first-order term over two periods cancels by antiperiodicity. For some \(u\), the profile point \(q=\gamma(u,w_0)j\) does not commute with \(C\): the regular closed planar profile is not contained in the line through \(C\). Therefore \(f(u,\mathcal V)\ne f(u,0)\) for sufficiently small \(\lambda\), as Definition 2 requires.

Lemma 1 with \(k=2,v=0\) predicts, for \(R=\Phi(\mathcal V)\), \(f(u,2\mathcal V)=R^{-2}qR^2\). The actual and predicted quantities are respectively \[q+O(\lambda^2),\qquad
 q+2\lambda[q,C]+O(\lambda^2),\] which differ. This is an admissible failure of the asserted monodromy formula, not an ambiguity about a principal square root.

## Counterevidence, repair and bounded impact

Require \(\mathcal V\) to be a common period of \(w\) and its chosen analytic root \(a\). Then the coefficient in [eq:frame] (#eq:frame) is periodic, and the usual ODE uniqueness proof gives \(\Phi(v+\mathcal V)=\Phi(v)\Phi(0)^{-1}\Phi(\mathcal V)\), restoring the lemma. For a nonzero analytic root of a periodic analytic function, the shift by \(\mathcal V\) changes that root by a constant sign; thus \(2\mathcal V\) is always a common period. The rotational angle must then be recomputed over that common period. If the doubled interval already closes the surface, it is a full period and its zero-angle case must be treated separately.

The actual spherical family has \(a=(s-s_1)(s-s_2)/(\delta s_w)\) on the chosen real branch, so \(a\) has the same period as \(s\) and \(w\). Section 7.3 also uses a winding angle \(\psi(v+\mathcal V)=\psi(v)+2\pi\), making both \(a=\sin\psi\) and \(w'=\cos\psi\) periodic. These are strong operative special-case safeguards. The general lemma needs correction, but this counterexample does not invalidate those actual construction families. The supported impact is Minor. The exact identity and complete native analysis are retained in `verification/0138/root_exact_checks.json` and `verification/0138/native_elliptic_check.md`.

Problem 2 (Arbitrary admissible cylinders need not have a translation period).  Theorem 5, PDF p. 21 (printed p. 261), and Theorem 10, PDF p. 47 (printed p. 287), assert \(v\)-direction translation periods, opposite for the two transforms, for an arbitrary cylinder of Theorem 3. That hypothesis allows nonperiodic \(w\).

## A nonperiodic metric on fully admissible input

At \(u=\omega\), put \[E(w)=e^{h(\omega,w)}
 =\frac{\vartheta_2(iw/2)\vartheta_2(-iw/2)}
        {\vartheta_1(\omega+iw/2)\vartheta_1(\omega-iw/2)}.\] This positive real-analytic function is not constant on any real interval. Its meromorphic continuation has a pole at \(w=2i\omega\): one denominator factor is \(\vartheta_1(0)\), whereas \(\vartheta_1(2\omega)\) and \(\vartheta_2(\pm\omega)\) are nonzero. Choose \(r<t\) inside the admissible interval with \(E(r)\ne E(t)\), also in the generic interval of [eq:generic] (#eq:generic), and choose \(L>(t-r)/2\). The function \[w(v)=\frac{r+t}{2}+\frac{t-r}{2}\tanh(v/L)\] is real analytic on \(\mathbb R\), stays in \((r,t)\), and satisfies \(|w'|<1\). Its positive analytic root is admissible and its global unit-quaternion frame exists; the coefficient is bounded on the compact parameter interval. Here \(w'>0\), so genericity follows from [eq:generic] (#eq:generic).

Set \(\epsilon=1\). Theorem 4 gives \(|f(\omega,v)|=|R(\omega)|\), so [eq:metric] (#eq:metric) has \(u\)-coefficient \[H(v)=\frac{(1+R(\omega)^2)^2}{E(w(v))^2}.\] Its two limits at \(v=\pm\infty\) are distinct. If either transformed surface satisfied \(f^\pm(u,v+T)=f^\pm(u,v)+c\) for \(T>0\) and constant vector \(c\), differentiating in \(u\) would imply \(H(v+T)=H(v)\). A continuous periodic function with a limit at infinity is constant, contradicting these limits. Thus neither surface has the claimed translation period.

## Repair and actual dependency

The explicit formulas and \(2\pi\)-periodicity in \(u\) remain valid. To assert opposite \(v\)-translation periods, require the generating surface \(f\) to be a torus with the corresponding periodic differential data. Its explicit inverse and Christoffel symmetries make the \(\mathcal A\)-period vanish; the remaining \(\mathcal B\)-periods then have opposite signs. The paragraph immediately following Theorem 5 explicitly introduces this torus setting, and the subsequent closing argument uses it. Periodic \(w\) alone is not an adequate substitute for frame closure. The supported defect is local to the theorem’s overbroad period conclusion, with no demonstrated failure of the torus-specific existence argument: Minor. The analytic proof and a separate 70-digit diagnostic are retained in `verification/0138/p0138_foundations.md` and its calculation record.

# The elliptic existence argument and explicit formulas

Equation reference: eq:axial. Write \(a_0=U_1'(\omega)\), \(b_0=U_2(\omega)\), \(c_0=U'(\omega)\), \(d_0=U(\omega)^2\). The axial coefficient in equation (93) simplifies to \[
 \mathcal A(x,y)=(x+y)(a_0xy-c_0)-b_0xy-d_0.\] For large \(x\) with \(a_0x>0\), its roots have leading terms \[y_1=\frac{c_0}{a_0x}+O(x^{-2}),\qquad
 y_2=-x+\frac{b_0}{a_0}+O(x^{-1}).\] These give the required signs of \(Q_3(x)\) and \(Q_3(y)\), a real nonzero axis norm, and the two nondegeneracy conditions in Lemma 8. In particular \(\partial_y\mathcal A=a_0x(x+2y)-b_0x-c_0\). The fixed-interval substitution removes the moving square-root endpoints of the elliptic integrals, justifying the analytic extension of the axial integral divided by \(\delta^2\). The nonzero leading angular coefficient along its zero branch then permits rational angles. These checks support the Lemma 8–Lemma 9–Theorem 8 existence route. The displayed factor \(2\) in the small-root expression (98) is inconsistent with [eq:axial] (#eq:axial), but does not alter these signs or existence conclusions; it is not a separate substantive finding.

Appendix A was checked through the identity, with \(t=(u+iw-\omega)/2\), \[\frac{\gamma}{\gamma_u}
 =C\left(D-(\log\vartheta_2)''(t)\right),\quad
 C=\frac{2\vartheta_2(\omega)^4}
        {\vartheta_1'(0)^3\vartheta_1(2\omega)},\quad
 D=\frac{\vartheta_2''(\omega)}{\vartheta_2(\omega)}.\] Equal elliptic poles and the value at \(t=\omega\) establish it. Integrating gives the stated \(\widehat B\); its derivatives are \(\widehat B_u=\operatorname{Im}(\gamma/\gamma_u)\) and \(\widehat B_w=\operatorname{Re}(\gamma/\gamma_u)\). The residual \(v\)-component is independent of \(u\); evaluating at \(u=\omega\) recovers the formula for \(\widetilde B'\). Twelve allowed parameter points at 70 decimal digits gave residuals below \(10^{-55}\), supplementing this analytic argument. The zero-lower-limit primitive in (127) and the convenient primitive in (121) differ by a function of \(w\), which is absorbed into \(\widetilde B\) with its derivative. The final immersion formula is consistent under that normalization.

The incorrect reciprocal exponent and sign in the preparatory quaternion displays on PDF p. 13 are likewise resolved by the closedness calculation above. The endpoint \(\epsilon=0\) gives coincident transforms and is excluded for a noncongruent Bonnet pair; the text’s rescaling by \(\epsilon\) makes its intended regime clear. These clarifications are not additional numbered Problems.

# A discrete nondegeneracy qualification

Problem 3 (The discrete transform can collapse an allowed edge).  Section 8, PDF p. 44, defines a discrete net to have nonzero edges. Proposition 7, PDF p. 46 (printed p. 286), calls the outputs of (119) discrete nets for every real \(\epsilon\). The formulas need an additional edge nondegeneracy condition for that conclusion.

## Exact global example

Take the square net and its Christoffel dual \[f(n,m)=(2n-1)i+2mj,\qquad
 f^*(n,m)=\tfrac n2 i-\tfrac m2 j.\] Every horizontal and vertical edge is nonzero. On every square the cross-ratio in (116) is \((2i)(2j)^{-1}(2i)(2j)^{-1}=(-k)^2=-1\), so the input meets the stated isothermic-net definition. For \(\epsilon=1\), the edge from \((0,0)\) to \((1,0)\) has endpoints \(-i,i\) and dual difference \(i/2\). Its two transformed quaternions are \[(1+i)(i/2)(1+i)=-1,\qquad
 (-1+i)(i/2)(-1+i)=1.\] Both imaginary parts vanish, giving zero output edges despite nonzero \(\epsilon\) and nonzero original endpoints.

Equation reference: eq:edge. More generally, for imaginary endpoints \(a,b\) and \(d=b-a\ne0\), quaternion norm multiplicativity gives \[
 \left|\operatorname{Im}_{\mathbb H}\frac{(\epsilon-a)d(\epsilon+b)}{|d|^2}\right|^2
 =\frac{(\epsilon^2+a\cdot b)^2+|a\times b|^2}{|d|^2}.\] Thus an edge collapses exactly when \(a\times b=0\) and \(a\cdot b=-\epsilon^2\). The vertical-edge sign and the choice of Bonnet branch do not alter this criterion. Excluding these conditions on every edge restores the nonzero-edge conclusion; a finite net has only finitely many forbidden values of \(\epsilon\).

## Counterevidence and scope

The quad closure identity immediately following (119) remains true. If the proposition is read only as integration to vertex maps, its algebraic assertion is valid. The defect concerns the previously defined immersive-net conclusion and subsequent geometric label. The counterexample even has explicit polynomial integrated maps, saved with exact rational quaternion calculations in `verification/0138/native_0138_discrete_nondegeneracy.md` and `verification/0138/native_discrete_edge_exact_check.json`. No collapse was established for the paper’s displayed compact examples, and the smooth transform has a different immersion mechanism. This local defect is Minor and does not refute the smooth existence theorem.

# Outstanding evidence in the final deformation step

## Fixed-period rank: Needs Evidence

For the base spherical example, Section 7.3 fixes \(\mathcal V\) and perturbs \(\psi=\psi_0+\eta\phi\), with \(\phi\) periodic analytic and vanishing at the two specified zeros. Set \(w=w_0(0)+\int_0^v\cos\psi(t)\,dt\). The constraints are a fixed rational monodromy angle \(\theta\), zero axial period \(b\), and \(c=\int_0^\mathcal V\cos\psi\,dv=0\). The last condition is needed for \(w\) itself to be periodic.

The ODE linearization is valid. For the chosen nontrivial monodromy, \(\theta_0\notin2\pi\mathbb Z\). If \(M=M_0(1+\eta G+o(\eta))\), quaternion multiplication gives \[\delta\theta=2\langle A_0,G\rangle,\quad
 \delta A=\cot(\theta_0/2)
       (G-\langle A_0,G\rangle A_0)+A_0\times G.\] Fubini and the correct commutator variation give analytic integral densities \(\widehat a,\widehat b,-\sin\psi_0\) for the three differentials. The detailed kernels are retained in `verification/0138/native_0138_endpoint_map.md`. This verifies the existence of the densities, not their independence.

PDF p. 43 asserts their linear independence and then chooses three test functions to make the \(3\times3\) derivative matrix invertible. Once that assertion holds, the ensuing finite-dimensional analytic implicit function theorem is appropriate. Vanishing at finitely many points does not itself obstruct the integral functionals: analytic test functions with those zeros are sufficiently rich to detect any nonzero continuous density. The spherical-family rank in Lemma 8 is relevant support, but its period varies, so it is not automatically the required fixed-period rank statement with \(w(0)\) fixed.

This review has neither proved the asserted independence nor found a dependence. A completion would provide three allowed test functions with a rigorously nonzero determinant at one exact closing base, or another proof of surjectivity. This is a material unresolved proof interface, recorded as Needs Evidence rather than a demonstrated invalid derivation or a false Theorem 9.

## All ambient identifications: Needs Evidence

Remark 11 explains why the initial spherical examples are mirror images. The final paragraph before Theorem 9 argues that a generic parameter lacking that reflection symmetry produces images related by no ambient isometry. Breaking the indicated parameter reflection does not, without a further argument, classify all identifications that may include a different domain parametrization. The prescribed Bonnet correspondence being noncongruent is a weaker statement.

A possible completion would characterize every ambient identification through the conformal or anti-conformal automorphisms of the parameter torus and the metric and curvature data, and then show that the chosen perturbation avoids all such symmetry conditions. Functional freedom is supporting evidence, but that exclusion has not been established in this bounded review. No actual ambient congruence of the claimed perturbed examples was found. Rounded numerical parameters and figures illustrate the claim without providing an exact closure or symmetry certificate.
