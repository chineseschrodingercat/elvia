Abstract: The main monotonicity argument is checked through its Laplace-transform reformulation, variance inequality and Fisher-information estimate. Three localized errors are identified in the bound accepted manuscript: an overly strong pointwise limit statement, impossible positive lower bounds for derivatives, and an incorrect exponent in an intermediate differentiation formula. Explicit counterexamples and corrections are supplied. The first error concerns a null-set boundary and does not invalidate the integral limit used by the theorem; the other two do not propagate into the final monotonicity identity. No Major defect is established. Actual source-page inspection in the recovery continuation confirms the displayed formulas.

# Main proof and assumptions

Theorem 1.2 assumes a nonzero, nonnegative, even \(f_0\in L^1(dx)\); it does not assume log-concavity or Gaussian tails. The evolution is \(\partial_t f_t=\Delta f_t+\operatorname{div}(xf_t)\). Theorem 1.5 has \(p=1-e^{-2s}\in(0,1)\) and \(q=1-e^{2s}=p'=p/(p-1)<0\), with even nonnegative inputs. Evenness is essential, and is precisely what centers the measure \(F_t^q\,dx\) in the variance argument. General nonsymmetric inputs are not covered.

Writing \[F_t(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{\langle x,z\rangle/p}f_t(z)^{1/p}\,dz,
 \qquad Q_p(t)=\log\int F_t^q,\] the corrected differentiation gives \[-\frac pq Q_p'(t)
 =-\mathbb E_\mu|x|^2
   -(1-p)\mathbb E_\mu\mathbb E_{h_{t,x}}\Delta\log f_t,
 \qquad \mu=\frac{F_t^q}{\int F_t^q}\,dx .\] Here \(-p/q=1-p>0\). Moreover \(\nabla^2(-\log F_t^q)=-q\,\operatorname{Cov}(h_{t,x})/p^2\). Brascamp–Lieb bounds the first expectation by \((-p^2/q)\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Cov}(h_{t,x})^{-1}\). The matrix Cramér–Rao inequality then gives \[\mathbb E_\mu|x|^2
 \leq -\frac pq\,\mathbb E_\mu\mathbb E_{h_{t,x}}
                    \Delta(-\log f_t).\] Consequently the displayed derivative is nonnegative. The signs, normalization of \(h_{t,x}\), and \(p/q=p-1\) agree. The cancellation of the two dimension terms uses \(p/q=-(1-p)\).

For completeness, the Cramér–Rao step does not require \(h_{t,x}\) to be log-concave: integration by parts gives \(\mathbb E[(Z-\mathbb EZ)(-\nabla\log h)^T]=I\), and the covariance matrix inequality yields \(\operatorname{Cov}(h)^{-1}\preceq
\mathbb E[(\nabla\log h)(\nabla\log h)^T]
=\mathbb E[-\nabla^2\log h]\) under the decay conditions of the bounded-support approximation. The strict log-concavity used in Brascamp–Lieb belongs instead to \(F_t^q\).

# Problem 1: the asserted everywhere limit is false (Minor)

## Claim, location and admissible example

On manuscript p. 10, equation (2.6) is stated for every \(x\) and footnote 7 justifies finiteness of \(F_t^{(p)}(x)\) by claiming Gaussian decay of \(f_t\). Neither statement holds for all the \(L^1\) initial data in Theorem 1.2.

Take \(n=1\), \(f_0(y)=\tfrac12e^{-|y|}\), and any \(t>0\). This input is nonnegative, nonzero, even, and has mass one. Put \(a=e^t\) and \(\sigma^2=1-e^{-2t}\). The actual solution is \[f_t(z)=\frac a2\,\mathbb E e^{-a|z-\sigma Z|},\qquad Z\sim N(0,1).\] With \(C=\tfrac a2e^{a^2\sigma^2/2}\), \[e^{az}f_t(z)\leq C,\qquad
 \lim_{z\to+\infty}e^{az}f_t(z)=C>0 .\] The inequality follows from \(z-|z-\sigma Z|\leq\sigma Z\); dominated convergence gives the limit. Thus \(f_t^\circ(a)=C^{-1}>0\). On the other hand, \[F_t^{(p)}(a)=\int_{\mathbb R}
                  \big(e^{az}f_t(z)\big)^{1/p}\,dz=\infty
       \quad\text{for every }0<p<1.\] Since \(q=p/(p-1)<0\), its \(q\)th power is zero. This contradicts (2.6) at \(x=a\). At \(t=\log2\) the right side is \(e^{-3/2}\). The same example has exponential, rather than Gaussian, tails. The counterexample is exact; numerical tail evaluations are only supporting checks.

## Impact and a verified correction

This is a failure of the everywhere assertion, not a counterexample to the integrated limit (2.7) or to Theorem 1.2. The distinction can be proved without assuming Gaussian decay.

For fixed \(t>0\), let \[D=\{x:\sup_z e^{\langle x,z\rangle}f_t(z)<\infty\}.\] This set is convex. If \(x\) is in its interior, bounds at \(x\pm\delta e_i\) imply \(e^{\langle x,z\rangle}f_t(z)\leq M e^{-\delta\|z\|_\infty}\). The usual large-\(r\) norm limit, with \(r=1/p\), proves (2.6) there. Outside \(D\) the supremum is infinite. Continuity provides a positive-volume set above every finite level, which forces the large-\(r\) norm to diverge, so the limit is again correct and equals zero. The boundary of a full-dimensional convex set has Lebesgue measure zero; if \(D\) has empty interior, \(D\) itself is null. Therefore (2.6) holds almost everywhere.

For \(t>0\), positivity on a cube supplies the exponential majorant used in (2.8), independently of upper Gaussian tails. Dominated convergence now proves (2.7) with the almost-everywhere statement. Thus the integral needed in the main implication survives. The Minor assessment is based on this verified impact boundary, not on the mere ease of editing the text. Replace the universal pointwise claim and footnote 7 by this almost-everywhere argument. No author adoption of this correction has been verified.

# Problem 2: impossible lower derivative bounds (Minor)

Equation (2.11), manuscript p. 12, places \(c_1(1+|x|+|x|^2)\gamma_\beta\) below the list \(f_t,|\nabla f_t|,|\Delta f_t|\), with \(c_1,\beta>0\). The gradient lower bound is false even after the proof’s bounded, compactly supported approximation.

Indeed, choose \(n=1\) and \(f_0=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}\). All the operative assumptions, including evenness, boundedness and compact support, hold. The convolution formula makes \(f_t\) smooth and even, hence \(f_t'(0)=0\) for every \(t>0\). The proposed lower bound at zero is strictly positive for every \(c_1,\beta>0\). This is a direct contradiction.

The subsequent integration by parts requires a lower bound on \(f_t\) and upper bounds on its derivatives, not positive lower bounds on those derivatives. A valid replacement is \[c_1\gamma_\beta\leq f_t,\qquad
 f_t+|\nabla f_t|+|\Delta f_t|
       \leq c_2(1+|x|+|x|^2)\gamma .\] For bounded compact support and fixed \(t>0\), the Gaussian convolution provides the lower bound by integrating over a positive-mass bounded set. For the upper bound, the kernel variance is \(1-e^{-2t}<1\), so its quadratic decay absorbs the bounded-center linear term, including the polynomials from differentiating the kernel. Constants can be chosen locally uniformly for \(t\) in a compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\). The false extra lower bounds are not used in the monotonicity inequality; this limits the defect to the displayed estimate.

# Problem 3: one exponent in the generator expansion (Minor)

On manuscript p. 12, in the expansion of \(L_z[e^{\langle x,z\rangle/p}f_t^{1/p-1}]\) immediately after (2.11), the term multiplying \(\Delta\log f_t\) contains \(f_t^{1/p-2}\). It must instead contain \(f_t^{1/p-1}\).

Put \(\alpha=1/p-1\), \(\ell=\log f_t\), and \(w=e^{\langle x,z\rangle/p+\alpha\ell}\). Direct differentiation gives \[L_zw=w\left[
 \frac{|x|^2}{p^2}+\frac{2\alpha}{p}\langle x,\nabla\ell\rangle
 +\alpha^2|\nabla\ell|^2+\alpha\Delta\ell
 -\frac{\langle x,z\rangle}{p}
 -\alpha\langle z,\nabla\ell\rangle\right].\] Every bracketed term has the same prefactor \(w\). An independent symbolic calculation gives zero residual for this formula.

The discrepancy is nonzero for admissible bounded-support data as well. Use the input of Problem 2, \(t=\log2\), \(p=1/2\), and \(x=z=0\). Here \(0<f_t(0)<1\) and \(f_t''(0)<0\), since the convolution centers lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\) while the kernel variance is \(3/4\). Thus \(\ell''(0)<0\), and replacing \(f_t\ell''\) by \(\ell''\) changes the value. This verifies that the exponent is not an alternative identity.

The very next displayed integrated expression already uses \(f_t^{1/p}\Delta\log f_t\), as the corrected expansion requires after multiplication by \(f_t\). Equation (2.12) also agrees with independent differentiation. The local exponent error therefore does not propagate to the final sign argument. It should be corrected in the displayed expansion; no corresponding revision by the authors is established here.

# Other checks and conclusion

The \(t=0\) step uses truncation \(f_0^{(N)}=f_0\mathbf1_{[-N,N]^n\cap\{f_0\leq N\}}\), monotone convergence for its Laplace transform, and Fatou in the direction appropriate to \(q<0\). For fixed positive time, a fixed nonzero truncation supplies the uniform cube lower bound needed for the inverse-power majorant. The upper end \(t\to\infty\) uses the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equilibrium and the same domination mechanism.

For Corollary 1.6, direct Gaussian integration for \(f(z)=e^{-\langle z,Az\rangle/2}\) verifies both the determinant cancellation and the constant \((2\pi)^{n/p'}(p^{1/p}/|p'|^{1/p'})^{n/2}\). The extension from the endpoint exponents in Theorem 1.5 uses monotonicity of generalized Gaussian-measure norms; the Gaussian tests establishing sharpness are cited from the authors’ prior paper and were not independently reproduced for every outside-range regime. Section 3 keeps its conjectural general-data discussion separate from the proved result and includes a note on later positive-coefficient work.

The three recorded defects are Minor for the specific reasons established above. No Major defect or counterexample to the principal monotonicity theorems has been established.
