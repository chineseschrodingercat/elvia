# Assessment

Assessment: Major. The known error in Proposition B.1 has a published repair for the relevant orthogonal construction. A separate obstruction remains in the printed Lemma 9.1 for symplectic groups at the allowed coefficient prime \(\ell=2\): no auxiliary place satisfying its conditions can exist. This invalidates the stated auxiliary lemma and its use in that part of the main proof. No counterexample to the conclusion of Theorem 1.1 is claimed.

Both findings are Major because their assertions are used to produce the cuspidal global transfer on which the main proof relies. Repairability alone is not the basis for a lower classification. F1 is expressly recorded as a known, reproduced issue; F2 is a bounded finding in the acquired sources, without a claim of priority.

Theorem 1.1 assumes odd residual characteristic \(p\), a prime \(\ell\ne p\), and integral irreducible cuspidal representations with \(r_\ell(\pi_1)\le r_\ell(\pi_2)\). It concludes integrality of the transfers, agreement of supercuspidal supports, and a unique common generic component except for split \(\operatorname{SO}_2\) (journal p. 5174, PDF p. 2). The distinction between these conclusions and equality of the entire reductions is maintained throughout this assessment.

# F2: an impossible auxiliary place at \(\ell=2\)

## Exact location and counterexample

Lemma 9.1, journal p. 5217 (PDF p. 45), requires a finite place \(u\ne w\) with \(u\nmid\ell\) and a unitary cuspidal representation of \(\mathbf G(k_u)\) whose standard transfer is cuspidal. Its symplectic proof chooses \(k_u\simeq\mathbb Q_2\) and invokes Theorem C.1. The condition \(u\nmid\ell\) is also imposed in Section 3.4, p. 5188 (PDF p. 16), and at the start of Section 4, p. 5193 (PDF p. 21). Thus it is not merely an isolated phrase in Lemma 9.1.

Take the allowed data \[F=\mathbb Q_3,\qquad G=\operatorname{Sp}_2(F),\qquad \ell=2.\] For any globalization of the prescribed kind, every permitted \(u\nmid2\) has odd residue characteristic and \(\mathbf G(k_u)\) is a split symplectic group. A cuspidal standard transfer would require an irreducible orthogonal three-dimensional Weil representation. None exists. Consequently the existential assertion of Lemma 9.1 is false for these data, regardless of which permitted \(u\) is tried. The same argument applies to \(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}\) for every \(n\ge1\), whose standard representation has dimension \(2n+1\).

This is a counterexample to the auxiliary existence statement. It does not purport to exhibit two original representations violating the congruence conclusion of Theorem 1.1.

## Proof of the local obstruction

Let \(E\) have odd residue characteristic \(r\), and suppose \(V\) is an irreducible self-dual smooth complex representation of \(W_E\) of odd dimension \(d>1\). The image \(H\) of wild inertia is a finite \(r\)-group. Clifford theory decomposes \(V|_H\) into equally repeated irreducible constituents forming one orbit. Since \(d\) is odd, this orbit has odd size. Duality permutes it by an involution, so one constituent is self-dual.

A finite group of odd order has no nontrivial self-dual irreducible complex representation. Indeed, for a nontrivial irreducible character \(\chi\), the Frobenius–Schur indicator is \[\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h^2)
 =\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)=0,\] because squaring permutes \(H\); a self-dual irreducible constituent would have indicator \(1\) or \(-1\). Hence that constituent is trivial, and orbit transitivity makes wild inertia trivial on all of \(V\).

The remaining inertia characters are tame and form one Frobenius orbit of odd size. Inversion preserves the orbit and has a fixed character, of order at most two. The residue cardinality is odd, so Frobenius fixes this character. Its orbit therefore has size one. Inertia acts by scalars; a Frobenius eigenline is then stable under the entire Weil group. Irreducibility forces \(d=1\), a contradiction.

For a cuspidal \(\operatorname{GL}_{2n+1}\) transfer the Weil parameter is irreducible, with trivial \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) factor; the dual group \(\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}(\mathbb C)\) supplies self-duality. This is exactly the impossible case above. The paper itself states the corresponding local obstruction in Section 1.6, p. 5178 (PDF p. 6). The independent proof makes the limiting example explicit.

## Counter-evidence and repair boundary

Theorem C.1 does supply the needed representation over \(\mathbb Q_2\) (journal p. 5225, PDF p. 53). This is useful counter-evidence against a claim that the auxiliary representation never exists. However, at \(\ell=2\) that place divides \(\ell\), so it is outside the setup of Theorems 3.1 and 4.4. The official corrigendum repairs the orthogonal branch and does not alter this symplectic condition.

The use is direct: p. 5218 (PDF p. 46) chooses \((u,\rho)\) from Lemma 9.1, applies Theorem 4.4, obtains cuspidal global transfers, and invokes Theorem 8.2 at \(w\). Theorem 4.4 on pp. 5195–5196 (PDF pp. 23–24) inherits the setup at p. 5193, and applies the Section 3 lifting argument. In particular, Section 3.6, p. 5190 (PDF p. 18), requires integral type lattices and a reduction isomorphism of the form \[\operatorname{Hom}_K(L_\Lambda,\operatorname{Alg}_{\overline{\mathbb Z}_\ell})
 \otimes\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_K(\overline\Lambda,\operatorname{Alg}_{\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell}).\] Its cited hypotheses must be revalidated if the auxiliary place is allowed to divide \(\ell\).

One repair is to restrict the symplectic coefficient-prime range to odd \(\ell\); this narrows the published statement. To retain \(\ell=2\), a suitable simultaneous globalization at an auxiliary \(u\mid\ell\), or another argument, must be proved. Merely deleting \(u\nmid\ell\) and enlarging the exceptional set \(S\) does not establish that extension. The later Galois step applies at the target \(w\nmid\ell\), which remains true here; the outstanding obligation is the prescribed-type lifting step, not an automatic failure of every later argument. This review has not proved or disproved such an extension.

# F1: the known split even orthogonal error and its correction

## Original assertion and an explicit obstruction

Proposition B.1, journal p. 5224 (PDF p. 52), asserts the existence of a level-zero cuspidal representation with cuspidal transfer for the split orthogonal groups specified on p. 5223. The corrigendum expressly rejects this assertion for split \(\operatorname{SO}_{2n}\), \(n\ge2\), and identifies the error in the Lemma B.2 construction.

Here is a concrete instance. Let \(F=\mathbb Q_3\) and \(G=\operatorname{SO}_4\) split. In the level-zero setting, a cuspidal transfer would give a tame irreducible orthogonal Weil parameter of dimension four with determinant one. More generally, consider a self-dual tame irreducible parameter of dimension \(2n\). In a Frobenius-cycle basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{2n-1}\), write \(\Phi^{2n}=cI\). The distinct inertia characters pair with their inverses at opposite positions. For an invariant \(\varepsilon\)-symmetric nondegenerate form, \[B(e_0,e_n)
 =B(\Phi^ne_0,\Phi^ne_n)
 =B(e_n,ce_0)
 =c\varepsilon B(e_0,e_n).\] Thus \(c=\varepsilon\) and \(\det\Phi=(-1)^{2n-1}c=-\varepsilon\). In the orthogonal case \(\varepsilon=1\), so \(\det\Phi=-1\); inertia has determinant one by reciprocal pairing. The determinant is the nontrivial unramified quadratic character, incompatible with the split even orthogonal parameter. This reproduces the obstruction described in the corrigendum.

The primary executor also checked a finite parameter illustrating the invalid placement in Section B.3: \[P(T)=\Phi_{10}(T)=T^4-T^3+T^2-T+1
 \quad\text{over }\mathbb F_3.\] The powers of \(3\) modulo \(10\) are \(1,3,9,7\), a full orbit, and \(3^2\equiv-1\). Hence \(P\) is irreducible and reciprocal and its roots have order ten. But \(|\operatorname{SO}_4^+(3)|=3^2(3^2-1)^2=576\), which is not divisible by five. Such a parameter cannot lie in that split finite orthogonal group. Exact polynomial, matrix, pairing and determinant checks are retained in the evidence. This is a failure of the claimed parameter placement; it is not misrepresented as a counterexample to a Lusztig-series assertion after assuming an unavailable membership hypothesis.

## What the acquired corrigendum actually repairs

Corrigendum Proposition 1.1, p. 5236 (PDF p. 2), replaces the false range by split odd orthogonal groups and unramified, non-split, quasi-split even orthogonal groups. Its parity and determinant calculation matches the obstruction above.

The character-existence step can be checked explicitly. For the unramified extension \(L/F\) of degree \(2n\), choose a residue character of order \(q^n+1\). Its Frobenius orbit has length \(2n\): for \(0<j<n\), \(q^j-1<q^n+1\); for \(j=n+r<2n\), \(q^j\equiv-q^r\) and \(q^r+1<q^n+1\). Also \(\chi^{q^n}=\chi^{-1}\) and the character is trivial on the residue units of the degree-\(n\) subfield. Choosing its uniformizer value to be \(1\) or \(-1\), with the corrigendum’s unramified quadratic twist before induction, produces the two required parities. The orthogonal choice has the nontrivial unramified quadratic determinant required by the non-split form.

The original Lemma 9.1 chooses a split auxiliary orthogonal completion and calls B.1. Corrigendum Section 1.4 and Lemma 1.2, pp. 5237–5238 (PDF pp. 3–4), instead choose the global discriminant so that an auxiliary \(u\nmid2\ell\) has a non-split unramified even orthogonal completion. Proposition 2.4 and Theorem 2.8 (journal pp. 5181, 5183; PDF pp. 9, 11) allow the discriminant and, where needed, Hasse invariant to retain the prescribed local form at \(w\) while keeping real compactness and finite quasi-splitness.

This verifies the relevant repair of the orthogonal dependency. It changes the choice of global data; it does not assert that every previously fixed global model in the wording of Proposition 1.2 is left intact. The main construction needs an appropriately chosen globalization. The corrected orthogonal branch does not dispose of F2, which concerns a different family and coefficient prime.
