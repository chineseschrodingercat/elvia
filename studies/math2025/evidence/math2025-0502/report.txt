# The exponent equation has an extraneous root

Equation reference: eq:raw. Problem 1 (Major: Theorem 1.4 does not uniquely characterize the exponent as printed).  Theorem 1.4, p. 4, equation (1.8), asserts uniqueness among \(x<1-\kappa/8\), for every \(a>0\) and \(4<\kappa<8\), of the solution to \[
 \sin u=a\sin(ru),\qquad
 u=\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{(\kappa-4)^2+8\kappa x},\qquad
 r=\frac{8-\kappa}{\kappa}.\] The definition of \(\operatorname{Root}(a)\) and the first paragraph of Section 5, p. 36, use this uniqueness.

Equation reference: eq:two-roots. Decisive counterexample. Put \(x_0=-(\kappa-4)^2/(8\kappa)\). Then \(u=0\), so both sides of [eq:raw] (#eq:raw) vanish for every \(a>0\). Moreover \(x_0<0<1-\kappa/8\), so the stated upper bound does not remove this root. At the admissible parameters \(\kappa=6\), \(a=1\), there are two distinct permitted roots: \[
 x_0=-\frac1{12},\qquad x_1=\frac5{48}<\frac14.\] The corresponding radicands are \(0\) and \(9\); at \(x_1\) the sine arguments are \(3\pi/4\) and \(\pi/4\). This proves nonuniqueness using exact algebra and elementary trigonometry.

There is also a direct probabilistic check on the spurious value. For \(a=1\), the expectation in (1.7) is a probability and hence at most one. An asymptotic \(\varepsilon^{-1/12+o(1)}\) would diverge as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Thus \(x_0\) cannot be its exponent at these parameters. ◻

## Counterevidence and correction

Equation reference: eq:moment, eq:sinc. Theorem 1.3, p. 3, gives a ratio of sines, rather than the cross-multiplied equation. With \(R=\operatorname{CR}(0,\widetilde D)\) on \(T^c\), its relevant moment is \[
 M_\kappa(x)=\mathbb E[R^{-x}\mathbf1_{T^c}]
 =\frac{\sin(ru)}{\sin u},\qquad x<1-\kappa/8.\] At \(x_0\) the removable value is \(M_\kappa(x_0)=r\). The intended characterization is \(aM_\kappa(x)=1\). Equivalently, using the entire extension \(\operatorname{sinc}z=\sin z/z\) with \(\operatorname{sinc}0=1\), it is \[
 \operatorname{sinc}u=ar\,\operatorname{sinc}(ru).\] This retains \(x_0\) exactly when \(a=\kappa/(8-\kappa)\); simply forbidding \(x_0\) for every \(a\) would introduce a different error. For negative radicand the ratios have their real hyperbolic-sine continuation.

Conditional on the paper’s moment identity, this correction has a unique solution. Indeed \(0<R<1\) on an event of positive probability, so \(M_\kappa\) is strictly increasing on its finite domain. It tends to zero at the left endpoint \(x\to-\infty\), by the hyperbolic-sine ratio, and to infinity as \(x\uparrow1-\kappa/8\), where \(u\uparrow\pi\) and \(\sin(r\pi)>0\). The moment is continuous on the intervening domain. This also justifies the inverse moment function used in Section 5.

Equation (1.9), p. 5, independently selects \(5/48\) at \(\kappa=6,a=1\). This is positive counterevidence against a failure of the intended exponent formula. A reader may infer that cancellation and a removable limit were intended, but the literal sine equation still has the extra zero. The theorem statement and the definition of \(\operatorname{Root}(a)\) need the corrected condition. This finding does not refute the subsequent large-deviation strategy.

# The stated Brownian covariance is impossible

Equation reference: eq:cov. Problem 2 (Major: the covariance in equation (3.7) is not positive semidefinite).  On p. 20, Section 3.3, the paper sets \[\mathbf a^2=\frac{2}{\sin(\pi\gamma^2/4)}\] and defines its two-dimensional Brownian motion by \[
 \operatorname{Var}(L_t)=\operatorname{Var}(R_t)=t,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(L_t,R_t)
 =-\cos(\pi\gamma^2/4)\mathbf a^2t .\] Here \(\gamma=4/\sqrt\kappa\) and \(4<\kappa<8\). The excursion measures immediately following (3.7) are based on this motion.

Decisive covariance test. Take the interior value \(\kappa=6\), so \(\gamma^2=8/3\), \(\pi\gamma^2/4=2\pi/3\), and \(\mathbf a^2=4/\sqrt3\). For any \(t>0\), [eq:cov] (#eq:cov) gives \[\Sigma_t=t\begin{pmatrix}1&2/\sqrt3\\2/\sqrt3&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det\Sigma_t=-\frac{t^2}{3}<0.\] Equivalently \(\operatorname{Var}(L_t-R_t)=2t-4t/\sqrt3<0\). This contradicts a necessary covariance property of any real random vector, irrespective of Gaussianity. Neither an endpoint convention nor an unspecified drift can correct it. ◻

## Dependency, counterevidence and repair boundary

The excursion laws built from (3.7) enter the mating-of-trees description in Section 3.3 and the proof of the welding result in Section 3.4. Theorem 3.1 then feeds Proposition 4.3 and the conformal-radius calculation. The printed stochastic input is therefore invalid in a core construction.

Equation reference: eq:fixed-cov. The sentence preceding (3.7) calls \(\mathbf a^2\) a variance, strongly suggesting a missing diagonal factor. The coherent covariance \[
 \operatorname{Var}(L_t)=\operatorname{Var}(R_t)=\mathbf a^2t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(L_t,R_t)=-\mathbf a^2
       \cos(\pi\gamma^2/4)t\] has positive eigenvalues for \(\sqrt2<\gamma<2\) and determinant \(4t^2\). It preserves the stated correlation parameter. Alternatively one can use unit variances and remove \(\mathbf a^2\) from the off-diagonal term, with the associated change of time normalization.

These are mathematical normalization options, not a claim that all quantum-area and excursion-measure constants have thereby been audited. The paper should specify the intended time normalization and propagate it through its cited input. The local evidence is compatible with a typographical omission, and supplies no counterexample to Theorem 3.1 or to the final moment formulas.

# The length-moment parameter violates the invoked hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:bad-p. Problem 3 (Major: equations (4.15) and (4.21) use inconsistent exponents).  Lemma 4.9, p. 33, equation (4.15), takes \(Q<\alpha<4/\gamma\) and writes \[
 p=\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}-1
 \ \in\ \left(\frac{\gamma^2}{4}-1,0\right).\] The same definition is repeated at the end of the proof of Proposition 4.4, p. 36. Lemma 4.7, pp. 32–33, requires \(\gamma^2/4-1<p<0\), and Lemma 4.5, pp. 30–31, requires \(-1<p<0\). They are used in Lemma 4.10 and in the proof of Proposition 4.4.

Range and scaling checks. Since \(Q=\gamma/2+2/\gamma\), the actual image of the stated \(\alpha\)-interval under \(\gamma\alpha/2-1\) is \[\left(\frac{\gamma^2}{4},1\right).\] It is strictly positive and disjoint from the asserted interval. For example take \(\gamma^2=8/3\) and \(\alpha=(Q+4/\gamma)/2\). Then \(\gamma\alpha/2=11/6\), so the printed \(p=5/6\), whereas the required interval is \((-1/3,0)\). Consequently the invocations of these negative-moment lemmas are not licensed by the printed parameter.

Equation reference: eq:good-p. There is an internal derivation of the correct power. Lemma 2.22, p. 14, gives the generalized boundary-length density exponent \(\gamma(2\alpha+\beta-2Q)/4-1\). At \(\beta=\gamma\), as used in (4.21), p. 35, this is \[
 p_*=\frac{\gamma}{4}(2\alpha+\gamma-2Q)-1
     =\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}-2.\] Its image is exactly \((\gamma^2/4-1,0)\). At the concrete example \(p_*=-1/6\). The hypotheses of Lemma 2.22 hold here: \(\beta=\gamma<Q\) and \(\alpha+\gamma/2>Q\). ◻

## A checked local repair and its limits

Replacing the two explicit definitions by [eq:good-p] (#eq:good-p) also reconciles the last trigonometric simplification on p. 36 with Proposition 4.4. This can be checked without any quantum-surface calculation. Put \(g=\gamma^2\) and \(v=\gamma\alpha/2\), and define \[A=\frac{g-2}{g}(g/2+2-2v),\qquad
 B=\frac{2-g/2}{g}(g/2+2-2v).\] These are respectively \((\gamma-2/\gamma)(Q-\alpha)\) and \((2/\gamma-\gamma/2)(Q-\alpha)\). With \(p_*=v-2\), the two sine arguments divided by \(\pi\) in the last display on p. 36 satisfy the exact identities \[\begin{split}
 -g/2-4/g-4p_*/g+2p_*&=-A-3,\\
 -g/4-4/g-4p_*/g+p_*&=B-2.
 \end{split}\] Thus their sine ratio is \(\sin(\pi A)/\sin(\pi B)\); also \(-\cos(\pi g/4)=\cos(\pi(1-g/4))\). The resulting expression is exactly (4.5), with the denominator \(2/\gamma-\gamma/2\) as actually printed on p. 29.

This supplies strong internal counterevidence to a false final formula. It is more than an unexplained interval typo: the power of the length difference in (4.21), the definition of the function in (4.15), and the admissibility of the integral evaluations must consistently use \(p_*\). The demonstrated issue is an invalid core derivation as written. The repair above restores these local steps; it does not independently reprove every external welding identity.
