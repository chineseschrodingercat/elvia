# Checks of the closure argument

## Euler-class matching and the doubled summand

The closure uses an auxiliary surface of genus at least three. On p. 10, the contact Euler-class pairing is \(\chi(\Sigma)-2\). Since \(c_1(\mathfrak s_\xi+\operatorname{PD}(A))
=c_1(\mathfrak s_\xi)+2\operatorname{PD}(A)\), the condition \(A\cdot[\Sigma]=1\) gives the required pairing \(\chi(\Sigma)\) in Lemma 3.4. The factor of two and the selected summand agree.

Theorem 3.2 gives two copies of the sutured ECH group, one shifted. Lemma 3.6 supplies the analogous two copies of SFH. Cancellation is valid for the ungraded vector-space conclusion of Theorem 1.3; it is not an argument for the stronger graded or contact-invariant conjecture. The genus-minimizing and non-genus-minimizing cases are separated in Lemmas 3.5–3.6. The underlying adjunction and broken-fibration inputs are external dependencies, not independently reproduced here.

## The index bound and the acyclic summand

In the second case of Lemma 4.16 (pp. 22–23), the displayed final upper bound is \[-2ng+2n+3=-2n(g-1)+3\leq -4n+3\leq-1
 \quad(g\geq3,\ n\geq1).\] Thus the numerical conclusion is strict even at the boundary \(g=3,n=1\). This verifies the final arithmetic, conditional on the geometric Euler-characteristic, relative-Chern-class and asymptotic-index computations that precede it.

For Lemma 4.19, write \(a\otimes1\) and \(a\otimes h_0\) for the two types of generators. Its differential has the form \[d(a\otimes1)=d'a\otimes1,\qquad
 d(a\otimes h_0)=d'a\otimes h_0+a\otimes1.\] Define \(K(a\otimes1)=a\otimes h_0\) and \(K(a\otimes h_0)=0\). Direct substitution gives \(dK+Kd=1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). The claimed acyclicity of \(C_1\) therefore follows from the stated differential; this calculation does not infer that differential from holomorphic geometry.

## The two surviving telescope classes

The seven rules on p. 25 give two families of relations \[d(e_nh_0)=e_{n+1}+e_n,\qquad
 d(h_nh_0)=h_{n+1}+h_n\qquad(n\geq1).\] For either family the direct-sum complex has one surviving class: every finite sum of even coefficient parity is a sum of adjacent differences, whereas a single generator has odd parity and is not a boundary. No infinite sum or boundary at infinity is allowed. The remaining quotient is the mapping cone of the identity on the relative Morse complex. It is acyclic. Consequently the auxiliary homology has precisely the two classes asserted in Lemma 4.22.

An independently written exact GF(2) calculation checks finite invariant subcomplexes for \(g\in\{3,4,7\}\) and \(N\in\{1,2,5,20\}\). At cutoff \(N\), the terminal generators \(e_Nh_0,h_Nh_0\) are omitted so that the differential stays within the subcomplex. In all twelve cases, \(d^2=0\) and \(\dim H=2\). This is a finite consistency check of the transcribed differential; the preceding direct-sum argument supplies the infinite algebraic step. The script and full outputs are retained.

# Refinements, applications and remaining dependencies

Theorem 5.1 (pp. 27–28) is stated for cosets of the subgroup \[K_\psi=\operatorname{Im}(i_{-*}\psi_*-i_{+*}).\] Corollary 5.4 additionally requires the two free-quotient hypotheses of Lemma 5.2 and \(\dim K_\pm\ne1\). It cannot be used as an unrestricted solution of Conjecture 1.1. The integral-lattice construction in Lemma 5.3 was read and its role identified; it was not independently certified in every rank. For the knot case the argument instead uses annular \(R_\pm\) and \(K_\psi=0\) (Corollary 5.6). The zero-flux hypothesis is retained for the periodic-Floer formulation.

For Theorem 6.3 (p. 32), if there are only \(r\) simple hyperbolic orbits and no elliptic ones, the admissible orbit sets are subsets of these orbits, including the empty set. Hence the chain rank is \(2^r\). The lower bound \(2^{k+1}\) on homology therefore forces \(r\geq k+1\). The tautness, irreducibility, depth, \(H_2(M)=0\) and nondegeneracy hypotheses remain part of that argument. The planar assumption in the no-trapping conclusion of Theorem 6.1 is also essential to the stated scope; the nonplanar extension is posed as Question 6.2.

The blocking/trapping arguments, Morse–Bott correspondence, curve enumeration of Lemma 4.20, cobordism maps and contact-topological classification inputs remain substantial analytic/geometric dependencies. Reading their use and checking downstream algebra does not constitute an independent proof of those inputs.
