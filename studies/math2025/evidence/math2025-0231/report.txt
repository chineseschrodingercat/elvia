Abstract: This review checks the principal theorem’s quantifiers and edge balance, then examines the random perfect-matching tool used by its embedding algorithm. The additive cut-count estimate in Lemma 2.7 is false under the stated super-regularity assumptions: an explicit dense bipartite graph family makes it fail for every perfect matching. The estimate is used in the proofs of Lemmas 2.10 and 3.10. The supported assessment is Major for a substantive defect in a core proof tool. This does not disprove the tree-decomposition theorem. A corrected matching statement and verification of downstream error bounds remain necessary. Scientific adjudication is pending.

# Quantifiers and definitions

Theorem 1.1 first fixes a typicality order \(s\), then chooses a tolerance and size threshold for each fixed density \(p>0\). Subject to \(p(n-1)/2\in\mathbb Z\), a tree with \(p(n-1)/2\) edges is to decompose a sufficiently typical \(n\)-vertex graph into \(n\) copies. Its edge balance is consistent: \[n\,p(n-1)/2=p\binom n2.\] For \(p=1\), admissible odd \(n\) gives the stated large-order Ringel result. The theorem is not being interpreted as a uniform statement for arbitrary sparse densities depending on \(n\).

Equation reference: eq:regular, eq:false. The finding concerns the assumptions of Lemma 2.7. For \(B\subseteq X\times Y\), \(|X|=|Y|=n\), write \(\rho=d(B)=|B|/n^2\). The paper defines \(\alpha\)-regularity by the absolute cut condition \[
 \bigl||B[S,T]|-\rho|S||T|\bigr|\leq\alpha n^2
 \quad(S\subseteq X,\ T\subseteq Y).\] Super-regularity additionally requires each degree to lie in \((1\pm\alpha)\rho n\). Lemma 2.7 assumes \(n^{-1}\ll\alpha\ll d\), \(\rho\geq d\), and an auxiliary bipartite graph \(Z\) of maximum degree less than \(n^{0.4}\). It asserts a distribution on perfect matchings avoiding alternating \(MZMZ\) cycles, with approximately uniform edge marginals and, for every fixed \(X'\subseteq X\), \(Y'\subseteq Y\), the high-probability estimate \[
 |M[X',Y']|=\frac{|B[X',Y']|}{\rho n}\pm n^{0.8}.\] The following counterexample refutes this cut estimate, not the separate approximate-marginal assertion.

# A deterministic counterexample

Problem 1 (Degree imbalance invalidates the cut-count center).  The final estimate of Lemma 2.7, p. 1789 (PDF p. 21), is false even for \(Z=\varnothing\). The argument on p. 1790 does not control the bias between the actual expectation and the center in [eq:false] (#eq:false).

## Construction and every hypothesis

Equation reference: eq:parameters. Choose an integer \(q\geq2\), arbitrarily large, and set \(\alpha=1/q\), \(d=1/2\). Choose an arbitrarily large multiple \(n\) of \(2q\) satisfying \[
 n>(4q-2)^5,\qquad m=n/2,\qquad k=n/(2q).\] Let \(X=X_+\sqcup X_-\), where \(X_+=\{a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}\}\) and \(X_-=\{b_0,\ldots,b_{m-1}\}\), and let \(Y=\{y_0,\ldots,y_{n-1}\}\). From \(K_{n,n}\) delete precisely the edges \(b_i y_j\) for which \[(j-i)\bmod m\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}.\] Set \(Z=\varnothing\).

Each \(a_i\) has degree \(n\), each \(b_i\) has degree \(n-2k\), and each \(y_j\) has degree \(n-k\). Thus \[|B|=n(n-k),\qquad \rho=1-\frac1{2q}\geq\frac12.\] The largest relative degree deviation from \(\rho n\) is \[\frac{k}{n-k}=\frac1{2q-1}\leq\frac1q=\alpha.\] All degree conditions are satisfied.

For the cut condition let \(H=K_{n,n}\setminus B\); then \(|H|=nk\). For arbitrary \(S,T\), \[|B[S,T]|-\rho|S||T|=\frac{k}{n}|S||T|-|H[S,T]|.\] Each term before subtraction lies in \([0,nk]\). Hence the absolute difference is at most \(nk=\alpha n^2/2\), proving [eq:regular] (#eq:regular) for every cut. This verifies the paper’s precise definition, including its absolute normalization.

The graph \(Z\) has maximum degree zero and introduces no alternating cycle. A perfect matching exists explicitly: match \(b_i\) to \(y_{(i+k)\bmod m}\) and \(a_i\) to \(y_{m+i}\). Choose \(q\) first, sufficiently large for \(\alpha\ll d\), and then make \(n\) arbitrarily large. The quantified hierarchy \(n^{-1}\ll\alpha\ll d\) is respected; no small-size exception is being used.

## Claimed value versus the exact value

Equation reference: eq:gap. Fix \(X'=X_+\) and \(Y'=Y\) before choosing any matching. Every perfect matching satisfies \[|M[X_+,Y]|=n/2.\] Since \(|B[X_+,Y]|=n^2/2\), the center in [eq:false] (#eq:false) is \(n/(2\rho)\). The deterministic error is \[
 \frac{n}{2\rho}-\frac n2
 =\frac{nk}{2(n-k)}
 =\frac{n}{4q-2}>n^{0.8}.\] The inequality follows directly from [eq:parameters] (#eq:parameters). The claimed event has probability zero for every distribution on perfect matchings. No simultaneous-over-all-cuts reading or adaptive choice of \(X',Y'\) is needed.

## Proof diagnosis and counterevidence

The block-expectation calculation on p. 1790 retains a multiplicative \((1\pm6\alpha^{0.99})\) term. Concentration controls deviation from the expectation; it cannot remove deterministic bias from the proposed center. In [eq:gap] (#eq:gap), that bias is linear in \(n\) for each fixed small \(\alpha\), whereas \(n^{0.8}\) is sublinear.

The original PDF image confirms the additive \(n^{0.8}\) term, so the finding is not an extraction artifact. The notation is \(\subseteq\), which permits \(Y'=Y\). Even with a proper-subset convention, deleting one vertex of \(Y\) changes the discrepancy by a bounded amount and does not resolve the obstruction for large \(n\). The construction satisfies a stronger cut bound than required and has an explicit perfect matching. Approximate uniform marginals with a fixed relative tolerance are compatible with the example; they do not imply [eq:false] (#eq:false).

# Dependence, impact, and repair limits

The affected estimate belongs to a principal probabilistic subroutine: Match is defined through Lemma 2.7 on p. 1781. The proof of Lemma 2.10 explicitly invokes the additive cut estimate on p. 1793 to control evolving hypergraph counts and exclude a bad event. Lemma 3.10 (pp. 1807–1808) uses the same tool for cut counts; its proof states that parts (i)–(iii) follow from Lemma 2.7 and identifies the cut used for (iii). These are dependencies of the approximate-decomposition argument, not an isolated exposition.

The supported assessment is Major: a demonstrated invalid guarantee in a core proof tool, with specific downstream uses. The constructed graph refutes the general matching lemma. It is not claimed to be a typical host graph disproving Theorem 1.1, and the downstream specialized estimates have not all been individually refuted. Whether their additional structure permits a replacement is a separate question.

Any replacement with the same deterministic center must accommodate the degree-imbalance contribution or impose conditions excluding it. Retaining a regularity-dependent bias term is a plausible direction, but this review does not certify a revised switching-and-concentration lemma or its compatibility with all later parameter choices.
