Abstract: Two substantive issues are established in the reviewed journal-formatted version. The stated white-noise variational constant is contradicted by an explicit normalized function; an elementary sharp inequality gives the correct constant and exposes a separate inconsistency in Corollary 1.4. An unequal-parameter maximization in the proof of Lemma 5.1 is false, with a strictly positive finite-dimensional counterexample. That step feeds the concentration argument used for the long-time moment lower bound. Equal-parameter estimates and the separate high-moment argument must be distinguished from this failure. These findings do not constitute a counterexample to the general long-time asymptotic formula.

The equation is \[\partial_t^2u=\Delta u+\dot W(x)u,\qquad u(0,x)=1,\quad
 \partial_tu(0,x)=0,\] in dimensions \(d=1,2,3\), with stationary mean-zero Gaussian noise. The covariance \(\gamma\) is both nonnegative and positive definite, possibly distribution-valued, with convention \(\gamma(x)=\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(d\xi)\). Theorem 1.1 gives existence under \(\int(1+|\xi|^2)^{-1}\mu(d\xi)<\infty\) in the sense of Definition 2.1. Its necessity statement assumes a square-integrable solution admitting the stated Stratonovich expansion. It is not an unrestricted uniqueness assertion.

Equation reference: eq:variational, eq:calpha. For the asymptotic results, \(\gamma(cx)=c^{-\alpha}\gamma(x)\), \(0<\alpha<\min(2,d)\), with the separately included white-noise case \(d=\alpha=1\). Write \[
 \mathcal M=\sup_{\substack{g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R^d)\\ \int g^2=1}}
 \left\{\left(\iint\gamma(x-y)g^2(x)g^2(y)\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/2}
       -\int|\nabla g|^2\right\}.\] This is precisely the energy normalization in source (1.11). The coefficient displayed in Theorem 1.2 is \[
 C_\alpha=\frac{3-\alpha}{2}
       \left(\frac{2\mathcal M^{1/2}}{4-\alpha}\right)^{(4-\alpha)/(3-\alpha)}.\] Theorem (1.9) concerns integer moments as \(t\to\infty\) at fixed integer \(p\ge1\); (1.10) concerns absolute moments as \(p\to\infty\) at fixed \(t>0\). This distinction matters for the dependency assessment below.

# Problem 1: inconsistent white-noise constants

Problem 1 (Major: false variational value and inconsistent specialization).  On printed p. 2147 (PDF p. 4), immediately after (1.11), the source states that for \(\gamma=\delta_0\), \[\mathcal M_{\mathrm{printed}}=\frac14\left(\frac32\right)^{1/3}.\] On the same page, Corollary 1.4 gives coefficient \(C_{\mathrm{cor}}=\frac12(3/4)^{1/4}\) in both asymptotic displays. The first value is false under [eq:variational] (#eq:variational); the second does not follow from [eq:calpha] (#eq:calpha) with either the printed or the correct variational value.

## Admissible counterexample and sharp evaluation

Equation reference: eq:sech. Take \(d=\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=\delta_0\) and \(\mu(d\xi)=d\xi/(2\pi)\). These are exactly the white-noise assumptions of Corollary 1.4 and satisfy Dalang’s condition. For every \(b>0\), let \[g_b(x)=\sqrt{b/2}\,\operatorname{sech}(bx).\] This is a real smooth member of \(W^{1,2}(\mathbb R)\), and substitution \(z=\tanh(bx)\) gives \[
 \int g_b^2=1,\qquad \int g_b^4=b/3,\qquad
 \int(g_b')^2=b^2/3.\] In particular, \(b=1/2\) yields \[\mathcal M\ge\frac1{\sqrt6}-\frac1{12}>\frac3{10}>
        \frac14(3/2)^{1/3}.\] The strict inequalities need no numerical approximation: \(1/6>(23/60)^2\) proves the first, and \(3/128<(3/10)^3\) proves the second. Thus the printed value is already refuted by one admissible function.

The exact value can also be obtained without the unavailable book page cited in the source.

Equation reference: eq:mcorrect. Claim 1.  With the normalization [eq:variational] (#eq:variational) and \(\gamma=\delta_0\), \[
 \mathcal M=\frac{3^{2/3}}{2^{8/3}},\qquad \mathcal M^3=\frac9{256}.\]

Equation reference: eq:sharpGN. Proof. For real \(g\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R)\) with \(\int g^2=1\), set \(F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xg^2(y)\,dy\) and \(B=\int(g')^2\). The one-dimensional Sobolev representative is continuous and tends to zero at infinity; integration by parts and Cauchy–Schwarz give \[\begin{aligned}
 \int g^4
 &=-\int(2F-1)gg'\nonumber\\
 &\le
   \left(\int(2F-1)^2g^2\right)^{1/2} B^{1/2}
   =\frac1{\sqrt3}B^{1/2}. \end{aligned}\] The last equality follows from \(\int_0^1(2v-1)^2\,dv=1/3\). The argument applies directly to \(H^1\) representatives, or by smooth approximation. It follows that \[\left(\int g^4\right)^{1/2}-B
 \le 3^{-1/4}B^{1/4}-B
 \le \frac{3^{2/3}}{2^{8/3}}.\] The family [eq:sech] (#eq:sech) attains equality when \(b=(3/16)^{1/3}\), since it saturates [eq:sharpGN] (#eq:sharpGN) and maximizes \(\sqrt{b/3}-b^2/3\). This proves [eq:mcorrect] (#eq:mcorrect). ◻

## Consequences and alternative normalizations

Equation reference: eq:coeff. At \(\alpha=1\), [eq:calpha] (#eq:calpha) becomes \(C_1=(2\sqrt{\mathcal M}/3)^{3/2}\). Consequently, \[\begin{aligned}
 C_1(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{correct}})&=\frac1{3\sqrt2},
 &C_1(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{correct}})^4&=\frac1{324},\\
 C_1(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{printed}})^4&=\frac1{486},
 &C_{\mathrm{cor}}^4&=\frac3{64}. \nonumber\end{aligned}\] All three fourth powers are distinct. The discrepancy cannot be resolved merely by inserting the printed \(\mathcal M\) into Theorem 1.2.

A plausible alternative is a factor of \(1/2\) in front of the Dirichlet energy. That is indeed the convention of Balan, Chen and Chen [Retained latex: \cite[p.~1592, (1.7)]{BCC}]; their displayed white-noise constant is \(\frac34(1/6)^{1/3}\). Spatial scaling changes this half-energy quantity by \(2^{-1/3}\) when passing to the energy in [eq:variational] (#eq:variational). The result is exactly [eq:mcorrect] (#eq:mcorrect), not the source’s printed value. Thus the common alternative convention does not rescue the statement.

## Severity, repair and limits

This is a substantive error in an explicitly evaluated special case, not a discrepancy due to floating-point rounding or an inadmissible test function. The proof above establishes the corrected variational constant. It also gives the coefficient [eq:coeff] (#eq:coeff) required for consistency with Theorem 1.2. It does not by itself prove either stochastic limit: the affected long-time proof must additionally be repaired as discussed in Problem 2.

The value of \(\mathcal M\) in its defining variational form need not be changed. The printed evaluation, the two displays of Corollary 1.4, and the cited normalization should be corrected together. The source’s general existence statement is not refuted by this calculation.

# Problem 2: a false maximization enters the long-time lower bound

Problem 2 (Major: invalid core derivation for unequal parameters).  Lemma 5.1(1), source (5.6), printed p. 2174 (PDF p. 31), is stated for every \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p>0\). The maximization at the bottom of p. 2177 and top of p. 2178 (PDF pp. 34–35) is false when these parameters are unequal. The asserted uniform bound feeds (5.11) and (5.6), then (5.23)–(5.26) and Lemma 5.3 on pp. 2183–2185 (PDF pp. 40–42). It therefore enters a core lower-bound argument.

## Exact counterexample within the stated domain

Equation reference: eq:objective. Let \[\beta=\frac{2-\alpha}{2}>0,\qquad
 \theta_j=\frac{\lambda_j^{-2}}{\sum_k\lambda_k^{-2}}.\] The objective in the source is \[
 H(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^p
 \left(\frac{x_j+y_j}{x_j}\right)^{x_j}
 \left(\frac{x_j+y_j}{y_j}\right)^{y_j}
 \theta_j^{x_j+y_j},
 \qquad \sum_jx_j=n,\quad\sum_jy_j=\beta n,\] with every \(x_j,y_j\) strictly positive. The source asserts that the maximum occurs at \(x_j=\theta_jn\), \(y_j=\beta\theta_jn\).

Equation reference: eq:counter. Choose the allowed white-noise case \(\alpha=d=1\), \(p=2\), \(\lambda_1=1\), \(\lambda_2=\sqrt3\), and \(n=8\). Then \(\beta=1/2\) and \((\theta_1,\theta_2)=(3/4,1/4)\). The asserted maximizer and a competing point are respectively \[(x^*,y^*)=((6,2),(3,1)),\qquad
 (x^\dagger,y^\dagger)=((7,1),(7/2,1/2)).\] Both points meet every positivity and sum constraint. They also correspond to integer compositions \(k=l=(6,2)\) and \(k=l=(7,1)\) in the subsequent uniform inequality. Since both have \(y_j=\beta x_j\), all factors other than the powers of \(\theta_j\) cancel in their ratio: \[
 \frac{H(x^\dagger,y^\dagger)}{H(x^*,y^*)}
 =\left(\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_2}\right)^{3/2}
 =3\sqrt3>1.\] The square of this ratio is exactly \(27\). There is no boundary issue and no question of a singular choice with \(x_j=0\).

## Correct maximization and an internal consistency test

Equation reference: eq:sup. The correct supremum of [eq:objective] (#eq:objective) is \[
 \sup H=
 \left(\frac{(1+\beta)^{1+\beta}}{\beta^\beta}\right)^n
 \theta_{\max}^{(1+\beta)n}.\] To see this, the log-sum inequality bounds the logarithm of the first two factors by \(n[(1+\beta)\log(1+\beta)-\beta\log\beta]\). The remaining logarithm is at most \((1+\beta)n\log\theta_{\max}\). Concentrating mass on an index with maximal \(\theta_j\) and imposing \(y_j=\beta x_j\) approaches equality. Under strict positivity the supremum need not be attained. If all \(\theta_j\) are equal, every positive allocation with this common ratio attains equality. In that special case the source’s selected allocation is valid, although not unique.

Equation reference: eq:singleterm. There is also a useful check at the level of Lemma 5.1. Let \[I_{n,p}(\lambda)=
 \int_{(\mathbb R_+)^p}e^{-\sum_j\lambda_jt_j}
 \sum_{l_1+\cdots+l_p=2n}
     \mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^p S_{l_j}(g_{l_j}(\cdot,t_j,0))\,dt .\] The nonnegative pairing representation and the term \((l_1,l_2)=(2n,0)\) give the exact inequality \[
 I_{n,2}(1,\sqrt3)\ge\frac1{\sqrt3}I_{n,1}(1).\] The independently derived one-coordinate lower bound in Lemma 5.2, source (5.16), yields \[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
       \log\frac{I_{n,2}(1,\sqrt3)}{n!}
 \ge \log(2\mathcal M^{3/2}).\] In contrast, (5.6) would make the corresponding limsup at most \[\log(2\mathcal M^{3/2})-\frac38\log3.\] Thus (5.6) is incompatible with positivity and the paper’s separate one-coordinate lower bound. This test is explicitly conditional on that lower bound; the finite-dimensional disproof [eq:counter] (#eq:counter) is self-contained and does not require it.

## Dependency, counterevidence and repair boundary

The source explicitly explains on p. 2183 that different \(\lambda_j\) are retained for the proof of Lemma 5.3. The bound (5.6) is used to control a tilted moment-generating function in (5.23). Its particular smooth expression is then used to isolate \(((4-\alpha)/p,\ldots,(4-\alpha)/p)\) as the sole zero-cost point, establish (5.26), and derive (5.22). That lower coefficient asymptotic is used in (5.3)–(5.4) to obtain the fixed-\(p\), long-time limit (1.9). The demonstrated failure occurs before the large-deviation argument; it is not repaired by changing the formulation of the Gärtner–Ellis theorem.

Using [eq:sup] (#eq:sup) in the same exponential estimate gives dependence on \(\max_j\lambda_j^{-2}\) in place of the displayed weighted logarithm. That bound does not supply the claimed differentiable concentration argument at equal parameters. A new lower-bound proof, or an explicitly restricted conclusion, is required.

There is substantial counterevidence to any claim of total failure. The equal-parameter specialization \(\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_p\) survives this particular maximization check. Hence this finding does not invalidate the equal-parameter steps (5.13)–(5.15), or the high-moment upper bound (5.7), on this ground. The high-moment lower bound (5.27) is developed separately through Gaussian exponential tilting in Section 5.3 and is not based on (5.23). Nor does the counterexample refute the final stochastic limit (1.9); it establishes an invalid core derivation of that limit in the reviewed version. The existence proof in Section 4 has a different dependency chain.

# Additional checks, coverage and unresolved limits

The reading covered the positivity and measure-valued interpretation of the wave kernel, the \(L^2\) mollification definition, Wick pairings, Laplace transforms, the continuity and stochastic Fubini arguments, existence and expansion-class necessity, both asymptotic regimes, and the Hu–Meyer appendix. In dimension three the kernel is a positive surface measure, not an ordinary density. The upper endpoint \(\alpha=2\) is excluded. No extension to that endpoint, to unrestricted initial data, or to all possible solution notions is inferred.

Several independent calculations were retained alongside the report. For constant Gaussian potential \(\dot W(x)=Z\) (a valid Dalang example, but outside the positive-homogeneity assumptions of Theorem 1.2), the \(n\)th term is \(Z^nt^{2n}/(2n)!\). Both sides of Theorem 3.3, source (3.10), give \(Z^n\lambda^{-2n-1}\) after Laplace transformation. The independent exact checks covered \(n=0,\ldots,6\), and the factorial identity proves the general expression. For the necessity argument on p. 2172, integrating its time kernel gives \[\frac{t^2}{2r^2}-\frac{1-\cos(rt)}{r^4},\qquad r=|\xi|.\] Its limit at \(r=0\) is \(t^4/24\); at large \(r\) it is comparable to \(t^2/(2r^2)\). This supports the claimed Dalang necessity within the stated expansion class.

The operative normalization in Chen’s Brownian self-intersection theorem [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 1.1]{C23}] was checked against the use on source p. 2175: that reference has half the Dirichlet energy, and spatial scaling yields the factor \(2^{\alpha/(4-\alpha)}\) used in the source. The variational identity in source (5.20) was also checked directly with a unitary Fourier transform: the resolvent denominator imposes \(\|h\|_2^2+\|\nabla h\|_2^2=1\), and amplitude and spatial scaling give the exponent \((4-\alpha)/2\). Fourier factors must be retained when consulting the earlier Riesz-potential formulation [BCR]. The Mittag–Leffler statement used in (5.5) was checked in [Retained latex: \cite[Lemma A.3, p.~1617]{BCC}]; its parameter is positive here.

Reference checks were targeted, not complete rereviews of the cited literature. The acquired reference PDFs and the actual pages read and viewed are recorded separately.
