Abstract: The manuscript develops a substantial incidence method for smooth families with forbidden higher-order tangency. Its cellular estimates, several interpolation exponents, and the intended geometric applications have useful internal consistency. The acquired version nevertheless contains false quantitative statements at important points in the argument. A finite affine-line configuration disproves the uniform exponent in Proposition 2.27; a subscale cluster disproves the technical Theorem 1.11\('\) and its discrete Furstenberg consequence. The ambient function-space packing used in the subsequent localization has no uniform overlap bound. In addition, the imported local-smoothing estimate has an erroneous epsilon gain, and the advertised sharpness example cannot realize the stated contact. Explicit witnesses, counterevidence, and bounded repair requirements are given below. Several local formula and domain corrections are separated from those five Major findings. These conclusions concern the obtained arXiv version only. They do not establish that the final separated-family, continuous maximal-function, or Hausdorff-dimension theorems are false. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# P1. Major: the uniform exponent in Proposition 2.27 is false

## Location and exact quantifier

Proposition 2.27, p. 22, asserts \(c=c(k)>0\), independently of the arbitrary positive two-ends/volume exponent \(\epsilon\). After choosing \(\eta,\delta_0\), it promises an extension with \(\delta\le\tau\le\delta^c\) capturing a fixed positive fraction of the curves incident to one small rectangle. Its proof is completed in Section 3.3, pp. 37–38, and the proposition is used to close the algebraic branch on p. 26.

Here is a counterexample for \(k=1\), including the norm, complexity, incomparability, prism, and all-interval hypotheses. If \(c>1\), the proposed interval of scales is empty. Fix any \(0<c\le1\), put \[\epsilon=c/4,\qquad A=\delta^\epsilon/4,\qquad
 h=\delta^2,\qquad b_0=1/4.\] For every proposed \(\eta>0\), choose \(\delta\) sufficiently small that \(A\ge128\delta\), \(h\le\delta/16\), and \(\delta^{-\eta}\ge8192\), as well as the fixed prism-padding conditions below.

## An admissible finite configuration

Take the affine functions \(f_{b,m}(x)=b+mx\), with \[b\in(b_0+h\mathbb Z)\cap[b_0,b_0+A],\qquad
 m\in h\mathbb Z\cap[A/8,A/4].\] They have degree one and, with the manuscript’s sum norm, \(\left\|f\right\|_{C^1}\le1/4+3A/2<1\). Let \(t_i=3i\delta\) with \(t_i+\delta\le1\), and \(q_l=b_0+A/2+4l\delta\) with \(4l\delta\le A/4\). Use rectangles \[R_{i,l}=[t_i,t_i+\delta]\times[q_l-\delta,q_l+\delta].\] They are pairwise incomparable in Definition 2.3. Different columns require horizontal span at least \(4\delta\), whereas a comparator has length \(2\delta\). Different rows in one column have vertical span at least \(6\delta\), whereas a comparator has height \(4\delta\).

For a fixed rectangle and slope, tangency means \[b\in[q_l-\delta-mt_i,\ q_l+\delta-m(t_i+\delta)].\] This interval lies in the allowed intercept range and has length between \(\delta\) and \(2\delta\). Consequently the original bipartite incidence graph has \[\#\mathcal R_0\le A/(16\delta^2),\quad
 \#F_0\le A^2/(2h^2),\quad
 E\ge A^2/(2048\delta h^2).\] Repeatedly delete a curve of degree below \(E/(4\#F_0)\) or a rectangle of degree below \(E/(4\#\mathcal R_0)\), using the original thresholds. Fewer than \(E/2\) edges can be lost. The nonempty retained graph satisfies \[\deg(f)\ge(4096\delta)^{-1},\qquad
 \deg(R)\ge A\delta/(128h^2).\] Define \(F(R)\) by its retained incidences. A curve meets at most one rectangle in each column. For every interval \(J\) of length \(j\ge\delta\), the fraction of its retained rectangles whose intervals lie in \(J\) is at most \(8192j\). Since \(j\le j^\epsilon\) on \([0,1]\), this is at most \(\delta^{-\eta}|J|^\epsilon\). For \(j<\delta\) the count is zero. Thus the required two-ends condition holds for every interval and every proposed \(\eta\).

For the fixed constant \(K\) in the prism definition, all prisms lie in the four-inequality box \[W=[0,1]\times[b_0+A/2-K\delta,\ b_0+3A/4+K\delta].\] It has volume \(A/4+2K\delta\le\delta^\epsilon\) and fixed complexity, so it obeys the volume and complexity hypotheses.

## Failure of every promised extension

Any \((\tau;2;c)\) rectangle has horizontal length \(L=\sqrt{c\tau}\). If two affine functions are tangent to it, comparison at its two endpoints gives \(\left|m-m'\right|\le4c^{-1/2}\sqrt{\tau}\), independently of its center function. At a fixed small \(R\), there are at most \(3\delta/h\) original incident curves for each slope. The fraction of retained \(F(R)\) captured by any such extension is therefore at most \[6144c^{-1/2}\delta^{c/2-\epsilon}
       +1536\delta^{2-\epsilon}
 \longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\epsilon=c/4).
 \tag{2.1}\] This bound is uniform in \(R\), in \(\tau\le\delta^c\), and in the proposed center. It contradicts any fixed positive fraction in the conclusion.

## Impact and repair boundary

The exact failed input appears on p. 37: Proposition 3.11 requires \(|W|\le\rho\), but is applied with \(\rho=\delta^{1/(k+1)}\) when the stated input gives only \(|W|\le\delta^\epsilon\). For small \(\epsilon\) the latter does not imply the former. Choosing \(\rho\) using \(\epsilon\) loses the asserted uniformity.

This is a false core structural proposition, not merely an unproved estimate. There is meaningful contrary evidence against a stronger allegation: the actual p. 26 algebraic application supplies a volume power close to \(1/k\). A replacement proposition with a fixed stronger volume hypothesis, separated from the small two-ends exponent, may suffice. Merely changing \(c(k)\) to \(c(k,\epsilon)\) does not close the printed hierarchy: the application needs its two-ends exponent small relative to \(c\) times the square of an outer exponent. No counterexample to the final incidence theorem is asserted.

# P2. Major: covering numbers do not control curve multiplicity

Definition 4.2, p. 45, bounds \(E_\delta(E\cap B_r)\), the number of radius-\(\delta\) balls needed to cover a set, for \(r\ge\delta\). Theorem 1.11\('\), p. 49, sums the indicators of all individual curves but adds no separation hypothesis. The global notation on p. 13 does not add one.

Fix the uniformly smooth family of constants \(\mathcal F=\{f_a:a\in[1/4,1/3]\}\). It forbids first-order tangency with constant one. Take \(k=\alpha=\beta=1\), \(\epsilon=1/4\), a sufficiently small dyadic \(\delta\), and \(M=\delta^{-1}\) distinct constants \[a_j=1/4+\frac{j\delta}{100M}\quad(1\le j\le M).\] Their entire set fits in one \(C^1\) ball of radius \(\delta\). Thus it satisfies the curve covering-number bound for every \(r\ge\delta\), for any prescribed \(\eta>0\). Give every curve the same shading \[Y=[0,1]\times[1/4-\delta/4,\ 1/4+\delta/4].\] This lies in every Euclidean \(\delta\)-tube, has area \(\delta/2\), and obeys \(E_\delta(Y\cap B_r)\le4r/\delta\). Choose \(\delta^{-\eta}\ge4\). All the written hypotheses then hold, but (5.1) would imply \[M\sqrt{\delta/2}
 \le \delta^{-1/4}(\delta M)^{1/2}.\] The ratio of the left to right side is \(\delta^{-1/4}/\sqrt2\), tending to infinity.

The same example refutes discrete Lemma 8.2 on p. 59 through Definition 8.1 on p. 58. Its cardinality lower bound holds, and \(|Y|=\delta/2\ge\delta^{1+\eta}\) for sufficiently small \(\delta\). With \(E=Y\), the claimed bound \(|E|\ge\delta^\epsilon\) fails for any fixed \(\epsilon<1\). This is the same multiplicity defect, not a second finding.

The separated refinement in Step 1, p. 49, cannot preserve a fixed power fraction of an arbitrary cluster: choosing \(M=\delta^{-q}\) with \(q>\eta\) leaves at most one curve in a separated subfamily. The repair must impose \(\delta\)-separation or a point-count/mass condition on \(F\), and carry it through Definition 8.1. It should not impose discrete separation on the positive-area shadings. The original point-count Theorem 1.11 and the genuinely separated discretizations used in the continuous applications are not disproved.

# P3. Major: the ambient packing localization is invalid

Step 2 on p. 50 chooses a maximal disjoint family of radius-\(2t\) balls in ambient \(C^k\), and claims uniformly bounded overlap for their radius-\(8t\) enlargements by applying the forbidden-tangency inequality (1.11) to their centers. That inequality holds only for pairs in \(\mathcal F\).

Fix \(t=1/20\), and choose any number \(L\) of smooth, pairwise disjointly supported bumps \(b_j\) in \((1/4,3/4)\), normalized by \(\left\|b_j\right\|_{C^k}=1\). Set \(g_j=5t b_j\). Then \[\left\|g_j\right\|_{C^k}=5t<1,\qquad
 \left\|g_i-g_j\right\|_{C^k}\ge5t>4t\quad(i\ne j).\] Their radius-\(2t\) balls are disjoint and can be extended to a maximal admissible packing. All \(L\) corresponding radius-\(8t\) balls contain zero. Since \(L\) is arbitrary, the asserted multiplicity bound is false. The jets of all these centers at zero are identical, so the claimed jet separation fails directly, even when the underlying \(\mathcal F\) is the family of constants with tangency constant one.

This affects the summation of the local estimates in (5.12)–(5.13), not just an illustrative remark. Arbitrary centers also need not be polynomials, so the p. 51 subtraction does not preserve polynomiality. Division by \(4t\) does not normalize a radius-\(8t\) ball to unit norm.

A bounded repair is available in principle: take a maximal net centered in the actual finite polynomial family. Condition (1.11) then converts separation of those centers to separation of finite-dimensional jets and gives bounded overlap. Use consistent enlargement and division constants. Moreover, assign each spatial point to a net ball containing its whole active ball \(B(x)\), and use the corresponding ball-specific shadings. Otherwise restricting the denominator in (5.8) to an arbitrary ball does not preserve the two-ends inequality (5.10). These changes must be incorporated before the local estimates can be summed. They are substantial repairs to a core proof step; they do not demonstrate that the separated-family conclusion is false.

At p. 51 the invocation of Lemma B.3 is also unsuitable: it gives only derivatives below order \(k\), on its own short interval, rather than the needed global \(C^k\) distance from an arbitrary center. Lemma B.6 can instead be applied to the difference of two actual curves tangent to the same rectangle. It gives the required pairwise diameter \(\lesssim_c T^{-1}\) for \(k\ge2\); the \(k=1\) case follows from the corresponding derivative/sign argument. This supplies a repair route, not validation of the printed citation.

# P4. Major: the epsilon gains in (7.4) and (7.5) are false

On p. 56, Proposition 7.2 prints \[\left\|A_{\zeta,\phi}(P_kf)\right\|_p
 \le C\,2^{-((s+1)/p+\epsilon)k}\left\|f\right\|_p,
 \tag{5.1}\] and (7.5) prints the maximal analogue with exponent \(1/p+\epsilon\). The following admissible example disproves both gains for every positive \(\epsilon\), including arbitrarily small ones.

Take \(s=2\) and \[\zeta(w;t)=t^2/2+w_1t+w_2t^2/2+t^4/24.\] For \(T=(\zeta',\zeta'',\zeta''')\), \(\det[\partial_tT,\partial_{w_1}T,\partial_{w_2}T]=1\) identically. Choose a nonnegative smooth bump \(\phi\), supported sufficiently near the origin and positive on a smaller product neighborhood. Write \(\zeta_0(t)=\zeta(0;t)\). Take a compact smooth \(\chi(X)\) equal to one near the reflected integration support, with \(\left|\zeta_0'(-X)\right|<1/10\) on its support. For a nonzero \(b\ge0\) smooth and supported in \((1,1.1)\), put \[\psi(v)=\int b(\mu)e^{i\mu v}\,d\mu,\qquad
 f_\lambda(X,Y)=\chi(X)
       \psi\bigl(\lambda[Y+\zeta_0(-X)]\bigr),
 \qquad\lambda=2^k.\] An exact vertical change of variables gives \(\left\|f_\lambda\right\|_p=C_p\lambda^{-1/p}\). For \(\left|x\right|,\left|y\right|,\left|w\right|\le c/\lambda\), \[\lambda\{y-\zeta(w;t)+\zeta_0(t-x)\}=O(c)\] uniformly on the integration support. Since \(\Re\psi\) is positive near zero, a sufficiently small fixed \(c\) gives \(\Re A f_\lambda\ge c_0>0\) on an output box of volume comparable to \(\lambda^{-(s+2)}\). With \(w=0\) the same assertion holds on a two-dimensional box for the maximal operator. Thus \[\left\|Af_\lambda\right\|_p\gtrsim\lambda^{-(s+2)/p},
 \qquad
 \left\|Gf_\lambda\right\|_p\gtrsim\lambda^{-2/p}.
 \tag{5.2}\]

The frequency projection is accounted for. The \(Y\)-Fourier support is exactly \(\eta/\lambda\in[1,1.1]\). For such \(\eta\), the remaining transform is that of \(\chi(X)e^{i\eta\zeta_0(-X)}\). When \(\left|\xi\right|\ge\lambda/4\), the phase derivative is bounded below by a positive multiple of \(\left|\xi\right|+\lambda\). Repeated integration by parts gives arbitrarily rapid decay of these tails. A standard annular multiplier that equals one on \(\eta/\lambda\in[1,1.1]\), \(\left|\xi\right|\le\lambda/4\) therefore satisfies \[\left\|(I-P_k)f_\lambda\right\|_\infty=O_N(\lambda^{-N}).\] This error does not change (5.2). The alleged right sides become respectively \(C\lambda^{-(s+2)/p-\epsilon}\) and \(C\lambda^{-2/p-\epsilon}\), which are impossible.

The exact cited reference [CGY], v2, p. 6, Proposition 3.2, actually has \(2^{-dk/p+\epsilon k}\), an epsilon loss. Its normal-form reduction follows that proposition on pp. 6–7, rather than a Proposition 4.2. Changing to a loss and allowing the small Sobolev loss still gives positive maximal high-frequency decay after the total losses are chosen below \(1/p\). Thus the final interpolation on p. 57 may retain its intended \(p>s+1\) range. The Major classification concerns the false quantitative proposition and its claimed consequence, which are central analytic inputs; it is not a claim that this sign correction disproves Theorem 1.8.

# P5. Major: the advertised sharpness construction fails

Appendix A.1, p. 60, supplies the example supporting the sharpness claim on p. 4. Even after correcting its repeated \(w_2\), the proposed polynomial is \[h(x,y,w;t)=y+\sum_{j=1}^s w_j(t-x)^{j+1}.
 \tag{6.1}\] It satisfies \(h(x)=y\) and \(h'(x)=0\). A polynomial of degree at most \(s+1\) whose derivatives \(0,\ldots,s\) vanish at the fixed origin is \(ct^{s+1}\). The condition \(h'(x)=0\) then forces \(x=0\) or \(c=0\), and either case gives \(h(x)=0\). Hence (6.1) cannot produce the asserted contact for any output with \(y\) near one. Nor does it uniquely represent every polynomial: a nonconstant affine polynomial has no critical point, while other polynomials can have several. At \(t=x\), varying \(w_s\) preserves the first \(s+1\) jets and \((x,y)\), violating the stated transversality condition wherever that point lies in the chart.

This invalidates the supplied sharpness derivation. It does not show that the sharp threshold is wrong. For clarity, the following complete replacement verifies that the intended threshold can be recovered with a different admissible family.

Use \[h(u;t)=y+\sum_{j=1}^s w_j(t-x)^j+(t-x)^{s+2},
 \qquad \Phi(u)=(x,y),\qquad m=s+2.
 \tag{6.2}\] The Jacobian of jets \(0,\ldots,s+1\), in the parameters \((x,y,w_1,\ldots,w_s)\), has absolute determinant \[(s+2)!\prod_{j=1}^s j!>0.\] Indeed its last row has the sole nonzero entry \(-(s+2)!\) in the \(x\)-column, and the remaining Wronskian is triangular. A vector in the kernel of jets \(0,\ldots,s\) with \(dx=dy=0\) induces a polynomial of degree at most \(s\) with all those jets zero; it is zero. Thus \(\Phi\) has the required rank on the one-dimensional jet fiber.

Choose \(d\) near \(-1/2\), solve \(y=-(s+1)d^{s+2}\) on its smooth negative branch, and set \(t_0=x-d\). For outputs in a fixed small open rectangle \(U\) about \((0,-(s+1)(-1/2)^{s+2})\), the polynomial \[P(t)=(t-t_0)^{s+1}\{t-t_0-(s+2)d\}
 \tag{6.3}\] belongs exactly to (6.2): its leading coefficient is one, its coefficient of \((t-x)^{s+1}\) is zero, and \(P(x)=y\). The remaining Taylor coefficients give the \(w_j\). These parameters lie in one compact small set. For \(0<u=t-t_0<c_s\), uniformly on \(U\), \[t,P(t)\in[0,1],\qquad a_su^{s+1}\le P(t)\le b_su^{s+1}.\] Consequently \[f_\rho(X,Y)=(Y+\rho)^{-1/(s+1)}\mathbf1_{[0,1]^2}\] has \(L^{s+1}\) norm comparable to \((\log(1/\rho))^{1/(s+1)}\), whereas its curve integrals are at least \(c_s\log(1/\rho)-C_s\) throughout \(U\). For \(p<s+1\), the strip indicator \(\mathbf1_{[0,1]\times[0,\rho]}\) gives output \(\gtrsim\rho^{1/(s+1)}\) and input norm \(\rho^{1/p}\). These are the required divergent ratios. The output set may have negative vertical coordinate; the framework permits it. The compactness, jet rank, and projection conditions are all verified for the replacement. Its availability does not validate the printed family.

## P6. Polynomial, slicing, and conic coordinates

On pp. 3 and 54, equality of jets of \(\sum u_jz^j\) at \(t\) means that the difference is divisible by \((z-t)^{s+1}\), rather than fixing its first \(s+1\) coefficients. For \(m=3,s=1,t=1/2\), the coefficient vector \((1/4,-1,1)\) gives \((z-1/2)^2\). The projected tangent is \((-2t,1)\), not a constant coordinate direction. The required projection is still injective on the jet kernel: a polynomial of degree at most \(s-1\) cannot have a zero of multiplicity \(s+1\).

On pp. 54–55, the slicing tangent space has dimension \(s+1\), and is transverse, generally not orthogonal, to the jet kernel. At \(t=1/1000\) in the preceding example, the kernel is spanned by \((t^2,-2t,1)\), whereas the slice tangent is \(\{du_2=0\}\). The direct-sum condition follows from invertibility of \(\pi D\Phi\) on the jet kernel and proves the needed sliced rank. The output-map uses of immersion should be submersion.

For the circle on p. 59, the actual fiber near \((x,y,r)=(0,0,1)\) is \(x=0,\ y+r=1\). The printed \((e,0,1-e)\) has value \(\sqrt{1-2e}\) and derivative \(e/\sqrt{1-2e}\) at zero, rather than value one and derivative zero. The correct tangent \((0,-1,1)\) projects nontrivially, so the circular example survives.

For the ellipse on p. 60, direct differentiation gives the jets \(0,\ldots,4\) determinant, in \((a,b,x,y,\theta)\), as \[\frac{18b^2(b^2-a^2)}{a^{13}}.\] It is zero at the circle \((a,b)=(1,1)\), where the source claims nonvanishing, but equals \(-27/4096\) at the intended noncircular base point \((2,1)\). The correct local fiber of jets \((1,0,-1/4,0)\) is \(x=\theta=0,\ b=a^2/4,\ y=1-a^2/4\). Its tangent \((1,1,0,-1,0)\) has nonzero center projection. This verifies the intended elliptic application after correcting the base point and fiber. Symbolic determinants and reconstruction identities were actually computed; they agree with the displayed hand derivations.

## P7. Proposition 4.4 needs consistent spatial localization

On p. 45, broadness is imposed only for \(x\in[0,1]^2\), but (4.29) has an unrestricted norm and does not restrict the shadings to that square. Take \(k=N=\alpha=1,\epsilon=1/4,\tau=\delta\), \(F=\{f\}\), \(f=-1/2\), and \[Y=[0,1]\times[-1/2-\delta/4,-1/2+\delta/4].\] The degree and norm bounds hold. It is striped by \([0,1]\), whose covering number is at most \(4r/\delta\). For sufficiently small \(\delta\) this meets every stated striping bound. Broadness in the tested square is vacuous. Nevertheless (4.29) would give \(\sqrt{\delta/2}\lesssim_\epsilon\delta^{3/4}\), which fails. Restrict the norm and shadings to the tested square, or impose broadness throughout their support and use translated charts. The witness has zero norm in the localized formulation, and does not refute that estimate.

## P8. Local semialgebraic corrections and the ODE scope

Two literal semialgebraic statements need corrections that leave their intended applications available. For Proposition 3.1, p. 27, let \(W=[-1,1]\times\{-1\}\) and \(u=1\). The strict bands \(f_i(x)<z<f_i(x)+u\), with \(f_i\in[-1,1]\), cannot cover its bottom face. The exceptional base must have measure at least two, contradicting its \(O(M^{O(1)}/L)\) bound as \(L\) grows. The proof on p. 32 uses closed and centered bands; writing \(\left|z-f_i(x)\right|\le u\) repairs this obstruction.

For Proposition 3.6, p. 29, the stated \(n!L^n\) Lipschitz bound fails for small \(L\). With \(n=2,T=(0,1)^2,L=1/4,f=Lx_1\), the actual Lipschitz constant is \(L>2L^2\). The application can restrict to large \(L\); otherwise use a bound with \(\max(1,L)\).

The proposed long-interval ODE objection is withdrawn. The global convention on p. 13 restricts intervals to subsets of \([0,1]\), and Theorem 3.12 and Lemmas 3.13–3.14 do not state an exception. Thus the previous diagnostic on \([0,T]\) as \(T\to\infty\) does not satisfy their hypotheses.

In fact the displayed bounds hold in the stated scope. Put \(z=\mathcal J_{n-1}f-\mathcal J_{n-1}g\) and \(\ell=|I|\le1\). The shift part of the first-order jet system has Euclidean operator norm at most one. With one residual bounded by \(\rho\), \[|z'|\le(1+L)|z|+\rho,\qquad |z(t_0)|\le\rho .\] For \(d=|t-t_0|\le\ell\), forward or backward Gronwall gives \[|f(t)-g(t)|\le |z(t)|
 \le(1+d)e^{(1+L)d}\rho
 \le 2e\,e^{L|I|}\rho .\] This proves the claimed form in Theorem 3.12 and Lemma 3.13. Two residuals give the same conclusion with \(3e\) in Lemma 3.14. No ODE formulation defect is retained in P8. The separate selected-radius diagnostic for Proposition 3.11 below has a different scope and is not a theorem counterexample.

## P9. Preliminary geometry and derivative bookkeeping

The lower threshold \(\mu\) in Definition 2.4 need not equal \(\#F(R)\), contrary to Remarks 2.5 and 2.8, pp. 13–14. An explicit witness takes \(\delta=10^{-4},\epsilon=1/2,M=10^4\), \(f_j(x)=j(x-1/2)/(4M)\), \(-M\le j\le M\), and the zero-centered rectangle of length \(\delta\). All curves are tangent, have \(C^1\) norm at most \(3/8\), and obey the broadness estimate with \(B=9\): a length-\(T\rho\) rectangle captures a fraction at most \(9/T\le9T^{-1/2}\). Yet the allowed threshold \(\mu=1\) is below the claimed \(9^{-1}(2\delta)^{-1/2}\). Use \(\#F(R)\), or first normalize richness dyadically.

The p. 15 sampling display must have a loss \(\delta^{-\eta}\), since \(T=1,R'=R\) forces \(B\ge1\). Its Chernoff trial count is local \(\#F(R)\), not global \(\#F\): with \(n\) local trials, \(p\le1/2\), and \(\#F=100n\), the zero-success probability is at least \(e^{-2pn}\), larger than the printed \(e^{-12.5pn}\). The subsequent union bound can be made finite by counting incidence subsets, not continuously placed rectangles. For a fixed center, width and length, the admissible left endpoints for each degree-\(D\) polynomial form \(O(D+1)\) intervals. Their endpoints partition all placements into \(O(\#F(D+1))\) incidence types. Summing over centers and dyadic scales is polynomial and is dominated by the available exponential tail.

The exact prism-to-cube identity in Definition 2.18, p. 18, fails when the subtracted center is not its Taylor polynomial. For \(k=1,g(x)=x/4\), the point \((\rho,-K\rho)\) in the Taylor-centered prism maps to \((1,-Kc-c/4)\), outside the asserted cube. The actual algebraic application uses polynomial centers of degree at most \(k-1\), where this identity is valid. General statements need controlled inclusions. After restoring the standard prism radius on p. 26, the rescaled prism is enlarged, not shrunk; use a \(C_k\widetilde\delta^{1/k}\) neighborhood and an explicit bounded refinement for incomparability. The p. 19 rescaling broadness exponent must be \((cT/2)^{-\epsilon}\).

Lemma 2.22, p. 19, omits a \(C^k\) bound on its auxiliary function. For \(k=2\), a zero-centered large prism of scale \(\rho\), \(\delta=\rho^2/16\), and \(f(x)=\min(x/4,\rho/4)\), the small rectangle centered by \(x/4\) on \([0,\sqrt\delta]\) satisfies its printed hypotheses. Its prism contains \((0,0,1/4)\), outside the large prism when \(K\sqrt\rho<1/4\). The actual application uses \(f\in F\) with the missing norm bound, which excludes this example.

Finally, Theorem B.2, p. 61, needs the actual degree: \(P(x)=x,D=2,\lambda=4\) gives sublevel measure eight, larger than its stated \(4\sqrt2\).
