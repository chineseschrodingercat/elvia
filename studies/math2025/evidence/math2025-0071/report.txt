Abstract: Two explicit counterexamples invalidate printed steps in the geometric framework of the central incidence method: the unsigned line distance in (3) does not support (17)–(18), and affine rescaling does not preserve the fixed lattice families in Lemma 3.3. The former comparison is used in the derivation of the phase-space high-low estimate; the latter underlies transport of covering numbers to rescaled configurations. Signed-intercept coordinates and uniformly locally finite cover comparisons provide local replacements. The phase-space isometry and the stated incidence exponents are not refuted. The supported issue assessment is Major for the demonstrated core derivation defects; ease of local repair does not remove their role in the proof.

# Definitions and a resolved candidate

Equation reference: eq:phase. Write \(\omega=(a,b,c)\in\Omega=[-1,1]^3\), with \(p_\omega=(a,b)\) and \(\ell_\omega=\{(a+t,b+ct):t\in\mathbb R\}\). A scale \(u\times v\times w\) is admissible when \(v\geq uw\). Equation (14), printed p. 1064, defines \[
d_{u\times v\times w}(\omega_0\to\omega_1)
=\max\left\{\frac{|a_1-a_0|}{u},
\frac{|b_1-b_0-c_0(a_1-a_0)|}{v},
\frac{|c_1-c_0|}{w}\right\}.\] An omitted coordinate, written \(\square\) in the paper, is equivalent to an infinite denominator. The paper explicitly treats coinciding point or line projections as multisets.

A candidate objection involving coincident tubes was rejected after checking the actual glyph on PDF pages 3 and 42. Theorem 1.4 uses the disjoint union \(\bigsqcup_p\mathbb T_p\). Two identical geometric tubes assigned to different base points remain different labelled elements. Taking many collinear points with the same geometric tube therefore supplies cross-label incidences; it is not a counterexample. Plain-text extraction had obscured this symbol.

# Problem 1: unsigned distance does not support the line comparison

## Claim and location

Equation reference: eq:old, eq:claimed. On printed p. 1047 (PDF p. 3), equation (3) defines \[
d_{\rm old}(\ell_0,\ell_1)
=|\operatorname{dist}(0,\ell_0)-\operatorname{dist}(0,\ell_1)|
+|\theta(\ell_0)-\theta(\ell_1)|.\] The distance to the origin is unsigned. Equation (17) on printed p. 1065 (PDF p. 21) asserts comparison of this line distance with \(d_{\square\times1\times1}\). The second line of (18) further asserts \[
d_{1\times w\times w}(\omega_0\to\omega_1)
\leq (2/w)d_{\rm old}(\ell_{\omega_0},\ell_{\omega_1})+2.\] The issue is the mathematical comparison, not just the word metric.

## Complete check

Choose \[\omega_0=(0,1/4,0),\qquad \omega_1=(0,-1/4,0).\] Both lie in \(\Omega\), their slopes are in \([-1,1]\), and their lines are \(y=1/4\) and \(y=-1/4\). Each line intersects \([-1,1]^2\) and has distance \(1/4<10\) to the origin, meeting the stated restrictions. Their angles are equal, hence \(d_{\rm old}=0\), while \[d_{\square\times1\times1}(\omega_0\to\omega_1)=1/2.\] No finite multiplicative constant makes the asserted comparison true. Taking the admissible scale \(1\times(1/8)\times(1/8)\) gives \[d_{1\times(1/8)\times(1/8)}(\omega_0\to\omega_1)=4,
\qquad (2/(1/8))d_{\rm old}+2=2.\] Thus (18) also fails with its printed constants. These are exact rational computations, independently reproduced in the evidence script.

## Counterevidence, local repair and impact

The phase-space definition [eq:phase] (#eq:phase) and its approximate triangle and symmetry estimates do not use unsigned distance to the origin and are unaffected by this example. Lines through one fixed point also have their offset determined by their slope, so the example does not refute the directional separation used in (38).

On the slope chart \(|c|\leq1\), let \(k=b-ca\) be the signed vertical intercept and put \[d_*(\ell_0,\ell_1)=|k_1-k_0|+|c_1-c_0|.\] This is a genuine distance on that line chart. If \(V=|b_1-b_0-c_0(a_1-a_0)|\) and \(H=|c_1-c_0|\), then \[V=|k_1-k_0+(c_1-c_0)a_1|,\quad
\max(V,H)\leq d_*\leq V+2H\leq3\max(V,H).\] Consequently the local comparison is restored, and the precise substitute for [eq:claimed] (#eq:claimed) is \[\frac{d_*}{3w}\leq d_{1\times w\times w}(\omega_0\to\omega_1)
\leq \frac{d_*}{w}+2.\] The algebra verifies this replacement on \(\Omega\); it is not a claim that every theorem has already been rewritten in the new metric.

The covering-level conclusion has a bounded local repair. In signed normal coordinates \(r=k/\sqrt{1+c^2}\) and \(\theta=\arctan c\), the old distance folds \(r\) to \(|r|\). On the bounded slope and intercept chart, the coordinate change and its inverse have bounded derivatives. Each folded ball therefore lifts into at most two coordinate neighborhoods of comparable radius. This bounds the change in covering and concentration numbers by a universal factor, although it does not restore the false pointwise comparisons.

The printed dependency is explicit: on p. 1067 (PDF p. 23), (21) is inferred from (18); on p. 1080 (PDF p. 36), (21) is invoked to obtain the configuration-space high-low inequality (29), which is then used in Proposition 4.1. Thus the supplied derivation of a central estimate invokes a false comparison. Supported severity: Major. The cover argument or a consistently signed metric supplies a local replacement, and the example does not refute Theorems 1.1 or 1.9. Complete propagation through all later uses remains outside the verified repair scope.

# Problem 2: exact preservation of the fixed lattice fails

## Claim and location

Section 3.3, printed p. 1066 (PDF p. 22), defines \[\mathcal C_{u\times v\times w}
=(2^{-10}u\mathbb Z)\times(2^{-10}v\mathbb Z)\times(2^{-10}w\mathbb Z)\] and the rectangle family \(\mathcal R_{u\times v\times w}\) centered on this fixed lattice. For \(R=R_{u_0\times u_0w_0\times w_0}(\omega_0)\), Section 3.4 defines \[\psi_R(a,b,c)=
\left(\frac{a-a_0}{u_0},
\frac{b-b_0-c_0(a-a_0)}{u_0w_0},
\frac{c-c_0}{w_0}\right).\] Lemma 3.3 on printed p. 1068 (PDF p. 24) correctly states a phase-space isometry, then claims that \(\psi_R^{-1}\) bijects the fixed rectangle families. Its last proof paragraph explicitly claims the corresponding bijection of \(\mathcal C\) lattices. The shear term prevents that last assertion.

## Complete counterexample

Take outer scales and center \[u_0=w_0=\tfrac12,\quad v_0=u_0w_0=\tfrac14,\quad
\omega_0=(0,0,2^{-11}).\] These are admissible dyadic scales. The center belongs to \(\mathcal C_{1/2\times1/4\times1/2}\), since its third coordinate is one third-coordinate lattice step. Take inner scales \(u=v=w=1/2\), also admissible, and center \[q=(2^{-11},0,0)\in\mathcal C_{1/2\times1/2\times1/2}.\] Direct inversion gives \[\psi_R^{-1}(q)=(2^{-12},2^{-23},2^{-11}).\] The claimed target scale is \[uu_0\times vu_0w_0\times ww_0=\tfrac14\times\tfrac18\times\tfrac14.\] Its lattice steps are \(2^{-12},2^{-13},2^{-12}\). Dividing the three coordinates of \(\psi_R^{-1}(q)\) by these steps gives \[(1,\;2^{-10},\;2).\] The middle ratio is not an integer. Thus the image center is not in the target lattice. All centers used here lie in \(\Omega\); no endpoint convention concerning \(2^{-\mathbb N}\) is involved because every chosen scale is a strictly negative power of two. An affine map takes the center of a centrally symmetric rectangle to its center, which is unique. Consequently the claimed bijection of the fixed rectangle families fails as well.

## Counterevidence and verified weaker statement

Direct substitution in [eq:phase] (#eq:phase) does prove \[d_{u\times v\times w}(\psi_R(\omega_1)\to\psi_R(\omega_2))
=d_{uu_0\times vu_0w_0\times ww_0}(\omega_1\to\omega_2).\] For the middle term, the numerator becomes \((b_2-b_1-c_1(a_2-a_1))/(u_0w_0)\). The other two terms rescale directly. Thus the failure concerns the chosen grids, not the geometric isometry.

A valid local replacement compares the two covers up to a uniform constant. To see this, pull the canonical inner cover back by \(\psi_R^{-1}\) and compare it to the canonical target cover. Both cover space by rectangles of the same target shape and volume, and both have uniformly bounded overlap. The isometry transfers the inner overlap bound. The approximate triangle and symmetry bounds in Lemma 3.1 imply that any rectangle of either family meeting a fixed rectangle of the other lies inside a fixed enlargement of it. Integrating overlap multiplicity over that enlargement bounds the number of meeting rectangles by a universal constant: volume of the enlargement divided by the common rectangle volume, multiplied by the overlap bound. The argument works in both directions.

Hence for any finite bounded set \(Y\), the numbers of rectangles meeting \(Y\) in these two covers are comparable by uniform constants. The maximal point counts in a rectangle are likewise comparable, because each rectangle is covered by a bounded number from the other family. This proves the cover comparison needed in place of an exact lattice bijection.

## Dependency and repair limit

The rescaling assertion supplies the transport of canonical covering and concentration counts from \(X\cap R\) to \(\psi_R(X\cap R)\). This transport is used when Lemma 3.11, printed pp. 1078–1079 (PDF pp. 34–35), applies the weak-uniformity estimate of Lemma 3.7 to the rescaled set. Lemma 3.11 then proves closure of the limiting branching functions under the blow-up \(f\mapsto f_Q\). The regular-rectangle blow-up in the proof of Theorem 1.4, printed pp. 1088–1090, uses this rescaling framework. The false fixed-grid assertion therefore lies in a derivation tied to the core incidence method. Supported severity: Major.

The weaker cover comparison proved above explains the repair boundary. Lemma 3.11 needs comparisons up to multiplicative factors; an additional universal factor contributes only \(O(1/|\log\delta|)\) to normalized logarithms. This establishes a local replacement and does not exhibit a failure of the limiting exponents. Full propagation through uniform extraction and every subsequent use has not been certified here. Repairability is separate from the severity of the invalid printed transport argument.

# Checks on the main deductions

In Section 5.2, printed pp. 1086–1091, the argument first uses absence of cross-label incidences to obtain separation in (37)–(39); it does not simply assume that the original point set is separated. This addresses a possible missing-hypothesis objection.

For \(s+t\leq2\) and \(2t+s>3\), the displayed choice \[\rho=\tfrac12\min\{(2t+s-3)/2,t-1\},\quad
\alpha=t-\rho,\quad\beta=t+s-\rho\] gives \(\rho>0\), \(1<\alpha,\beta\leq2\), and \(\alpha+\beta>3\). The minimization argument keeps \(1-x_0-y_0\geq\rho/4\). With \(\varepsilon=(t-1-\rho)/2>0\), the final exponent \((\rho/4)(t-1-\rho-\varepsilon)\) is strictly positive. Thus, conditional on the invoked uniformization and incidence theorem, the upper/lower incidence comparison tends to zero as required. The boundary \(2t+s=3\) is excluded and was not silently included.

The triangle deduction on printed p. 1050 uses disjoint short pairs. If \(n\) is sufficiently large, there are \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor\) pairs of separation \(O(n^{-1/2})\); Corollary 1.2 supplies a different representative within \(O_\varepsilon(n^{-2/3+\varepsilon})\) of one pair’s line. Distinctness follows from disjoint pairing. The area is half base times height, giving \(n^{-1/2-2/3+\varepsilon}=n^{-7/6+\varepsilon}\). The finitely many small \(n\geq3\) cases are absorbed in the constant. This deduction is sound conditional on Corollary 1.2.
