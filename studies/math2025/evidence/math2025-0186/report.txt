Abstract: This review concerns the acquired accepted-author arXiv version. The nonexistence argument is traced through brick partitions, discretization, weak amalgamation and robust winding cycles. The main obstruction has substantial positive support, but its supplied brick-discretization proof contains a false core–star equality. A demonstrated defect in the Appendix A measurability argument affects the supplied proof of the advertised interval and circle classification: its endpoint test accepts a root with no strictly monotone subchain, including on an infinite staircase, and rejects the endpoint of a genuine maximal strictly monotone subchain. An explicit Borel repair is provided. The purported converse to Hjorth’s turbulence theorem and two boundary conventions also need correction, with their limited impact separated from the main theorem. Several initially suspected failures are withdrawn or repaired by direct arguments. Two Major issues affect the core-partition proof and the classification measurability derivation. Explicit repairs do not remove the defects in the printed proofs, and neither example disproves the principal nonexistence or classification conclusion. Adjudication remains pending.

# Positive checks of the main argument

## The closure-based stars and the chain dictionary

The source uses \(\operatorname{Star}(K,\mathcal W)=\{W:\operatorname{Cl}(W)\cap K\ne\varnothing\}\), rather than intersection with open bricks. This initially suggests extra boundary stars, but the alleged counterexample does not survive the prefix witnesses. If \[K\in O(P)=\{K:K\subseteq\bigvee P,\ K\cap W\ne\varnothing
                       \text{ for all }W\in P\},\] then \(\operatorname{Star}(K,\mathcal W)=P\) exactly. For a brick outside \(P\), its open set is disjoint from the closure of the union of \(P\), and its closure cannot meet the open regular join \(\bigvee P\). Conversely, every brick of \(P\) is met by \(K\). For two consecutive distinct first-visit prefixes, only one new brick is added. Any continuum between the corresponding witnesses in a nested chain has its star squeezed between those prefixes. Thus the equality of star families in Proposition 5.2 is supported, after correcting the prefix index noted below. The purported boundary-star defect is withdrawn.

Lemma 5.3 handles its two Hausdorff directions differently. In one direction equal stars give the mesh bound directly. In the other, the least chain member whose star contains the prescribed star either is the root or is the closure of its proper predecessors. The assumed inclusion of star families then puts those predecessors inside the prescribed union of brick closures. This supplies the reverse Hausdorff bound; mere density of the bricks would not suffice.

Theorem 5.8 must use connected, nonempty fibers and edge surjectivity, not merely a surjective vertex map. Its construction assigns core bricks first and attaches every remaining brick to a neighboring core. Conditional on the corrected core construction in Problem 5, connectedness of each fiber and the special paths across adjacent target bricks give precisely those two requirements. The forced-assignment property (5.7) is what makes the simultaneous prefix realization work. Theorem 5.9 then first refines sufficiently that distance two at the fine level projects to distance at most one. This is the needed loss allowance when the two closed-star neighborhoods meet; no exact amalgamation is asserted.

## Winding, robustness and the surface alternatives

For a spaced circular path of circular length at least four, the triangles in the local comparison in Lemma 3.5 have no nontrivial three-cycle. Their signed edge weights telescope, leaving just the two endpoint corrections, each of absolute value at most one. Monotone refinements add zero-weight stutters; backtracking on a spaced path cancels adjacent signed edge weights. These are the precise reasons for Lemmas 3.3–3.5, rather than an appeal to an undefined topological winding invariant for every graph.

In Theorem 6.4 the two-brick buffer around the robust cycle ensures that the final comparison occurs entirely inside the coarse cycle. Before that suffix, the projected competing walk is still in the stem; otherwise the buffer would be violated. The positive and negative long traversals consequently cannot be amalgamated within coarse distance one. Literal concatenation and endpoint bookkeeping require the clarifications discussed below; no counterexample to the resulting nonexistence conclusion was established.

For the three constructions of robust cycles, the hypotheses are used in different ways. Locally nonplanar pieces allow prescribed disjoint arcs. A non-locally-separating planar boundary curve allows the auxiliary path to be disjoined from a compatible circular partition. For a circular covering, the entire coarse nerve is a cycle, so the confinement condition is automatic. The path-doubling step in Theorem 6.12 should first upgrade the given walk to a path using Theorem 6.2, as done explicitly in the preceding two constructions. This preserves refinement of the original walk. The first large winding has positive sign because the initial spaced path already traverses the coarse cycle twice positively; the absolute-value stopping description alone is not the justification.

The final surface classification retains its topological distinction. The sphere and projective plane are excluded by unicoherence. The torus and Klein bottle carry circular coverings, and the connected-sum operation can be placed in the exclusive interior of one covering element. Surfaces with boundary use a boundary component in the planar alternative. The cited continuum-theoretic arc and brick-partition theorems remain imported dependencies; this audit does not independently reprove all those references.

# Confirmed mathematical problems

Problem 1 (A false endpoint test in the classification proof: Major).  Lemma A.2, p. 33, claims that conditions (a)–(c) characterize the maximum of a maximal nontrivial strictly monotone subchain. Condition (b) checks a southeast rectangle for horizontal or vertical pairs; condition (c) requires such a pair in every northwest rectangle. These conditions accept a vacuous rectangle at the root and exclude a legitimate maximum at the top of the chain.

In the triangle \(T=\{(x,y):0\leq x\leq y\leq1\}\), consider \[\mathcal C_L=\{(x,\tfrac12):0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\}
       \cup\{(0,y):\tfrac12\leq y\leq1\},\qquad
 p=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12).\] This is a maximal chain of intervals, and it has no nontrivial strictly monotone subchain. Every nontrivial subarc contains a horizontal or vertical pair. Condition (a) holds at \(p\). For every \(n\geq1\), the southeast rectangle in (b) meets \(\mathcal C_L\) only at \(p\), so (b) holds vacuously. For every \(n\), choose \(m\geq\max\{n,2\}\) and the two points \[(\tfrac12,\tfrac12),\qquad
 (\tfrac12-\tfrac1m,\tfrac12).\] They satisfy the northwest bounds and the prescribed horizontal difference in (c). Thus the alleged equivalence gives a false positive.

The failure also occurs on the infinite-piece locus actually used in Theorem A.5. Begin at \(p\), join it horizontally to \(q_0=(1/4,1/2)\), and, for \(n\geq0\), join \[q_n=(2^{-n-2},1-2^{-n-1})
 \quad\hbox{horizontally to}\quad
 r_n=(2^{-n-3},1-2^{-n-1}),\] then vertically to \(q_{n+1}\). Include the limit \((0,1)\). This compact monotone staircase has infinitely many maximal horizontal and vertical pieces, and no nontrivial strictly monotone piece. Its parameter \(t=y-x\) runs continuously and bijectively from \(0\) to \(1\); hence it is a maximal chain, not just a partial collection of intervals. At its root, the same test works with \(m\geq\max\{n,4\}\). The source’s false predicate would therefore mark a nonexistent strict piece even on \(\Phi^\infty([0,1])\).

There is also a false negative. For \[\mathcal C_s=\{((1-t)/2,(1+t)/2):0\leq t\leq1\},\] the entire chain is a maximal strictly monotone subchain with maximum \((0,1)\). Every northwest rectangle at this point meets the triangle only at that point, so (c) fails.

These computations refute the stated characterization, not the Borelness of the genuine endpoint set. Theorem A.5, pp. 34–36, invokes precisely that Borel endpoint set to apply Lusin–Novikov and construct its countable invariant; Theorem A.6 then transfers classification to the circle. The false test is therefore an essential measurability step in the supplied derivation of an advertised result. Its impact is Major in that scope, even though a replacement is available. It does not affect the separate robust-cycle argument.

Here is an explicit Borel replacement. For every maximal interval chain \(\mathcal C\), let \(c_\mathcal C(t)=(x_\mathcal C(t),y_\mathcal C(t))\) be its unique member with \(y_\mathcal C(t)-x_\mathcal C(t)=t\), \(0\leq t\leq1\). This evaluation is jointly continuous in \((\mathcal C,t)\): every convergent subsequence of the selected points lies in the limiting chain and has the prescribed length, which determines it uniquely. Let \(S(\mathcal C,a,b)\) mean that both coordinates vary strictly in the appropriate directions on \([a,b]\). It suffices to check this on all rational pairs in \((a,b)\); monotonicity and continuity recover the endpoints. Thus \(S\) is Borel, including when an endpoint varies. For \(p=c_\mathcal C(t)\), \[\begin{aligned}
(\mathcal C,p)\in E^S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad&
\exists q\in\mathbb Q,\ 0\leq q<t:\ S(\mathcal C,q,t),\\
&\forall r\in\mathbb Q\cap(t,1],\ \neg S(\mathcal C,q,r).
\end{aligned}\] The rational test on the right is interpreted as vacuous when \(t=1\). Maximal extension to the left supplies the corresponding maximal subchain. This formulation excludes the root and accepts a strict piece ending at the top.

Lemma A.3, also on p. 33, needs compatible counting language. Arbitrarily many disjoint subsegments can be selected inside one horizontal block, so mere pairwise Euclidean separation does not count maximal horizontal pieces. For a correct closed-witness construction, require \(n\) horizontal segments of length at least \(1/m\) whose heights differ pairwise by at least \(1/m\). Their union over \(m\) counts distinct maximal horizontal pieces and is \(F_\sigma\). Use distinct horizontal coordinates for vertical pieces. Since distinct strict pieces are separated by a flat piece, \[\Phi^\infty=\bigcap_{n\geq1}
   \bigl(\{\text{at least }n\text{ maximal horizontal pieces}\}
       \cup\{\text{at least }n\text{ maximal vertical pieces}\}\bigr).\] This gives the required Borel partition, indeed the advertised \(\Pi^0_3\) bound, without counting subdivisions of one block. It is included in the same measurability repair, not counted as a duplicate core failure.

Problem 2 (The stated converse to Hjorth’s theorem: Minor).  Theorem 7.4, p. 28, asserts generic turbulence if and only if nonclassification by countable structures for an arbitrary continuous Polish action. The converse is false.

Take the translation action of the Polish group \(\ell^1\) on \(\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}\) and adjoin one isolated fixed point \(*\). The translation action is turbulent: finitely supported translations give dense orbits, \(\ell^1\) is meager in the product space, and any sufficiently small convex cylinder is contained in the closure of a local orbit by splitting finitely supported translations into small increments. Hjorth’s forward theorem makes its orbit relation nonclassifiable by countable structures. A classification of the disjoint-union action would restrict to one of this translation action, so the union is also nonclassifiable. But it is not minimal, as the source’s definition of generic turbulence requires: the isolated point is a fixed invariant open component.

The repair is to retain the implication from generic turbulence to nonclassification. This is the only direction used for Corollary 7.9. The overstatement is therefore a real local theorem error, with no demonstrated damage to that downstream conclusion.

Problem 3 (The degenerate continuum is not excluded: Minor).  The explicit definition on p. 5 allows a one-point Peano continuum. Corollaries 1.5, 1.7, 1.8 and their Section 7 versions exclude the circle, sphere and projective plane, but do not exclude this case.

For \(X=\{p\}\), the homeomorphism group is trivial and the unique chain \(\{\{p\}\}\) has an open, comeager orbit. The space is homogeneous and strongly locally homogeneous: every relevant neighborhood and rigid-stabilizer orbit is \(\{p\}\). Its universal minimal flow is a singleton, and the chain equivalence relation has a constant countable-structure classification. Thus the unqualified corollaries fail on this boundary case. Requiring a nondegenerate continuum, or adding the singleton to the exceptions, repairs their stated range. The three additional alternatives in Theorem 1.2 already exclude it, so this is not a counterexample to the main theorem.

Problem 4 (The allowed two-edge circular walk has ambiguous weight: Minor).  On p. 7 a circular path need only have sequence length at least three. Thus \(\langle a,b,a\rangle\) is allowed in the two-vertex reflexive connected graph. In Definition 3.2, pp. 8–9, the pair \((a,b)\) satisfies both the \(+1\) and the \(-1\) cases, using respectively the two oriented steps of this circular path.

Consequently the assertion that the weight is well-defined is false for the stated domain. Require circular length at least three (sequence length at least four), or explicitly exclude this backtracking two-edge walk from the weight definition. The robust-cycle argument and Lemma 3.5 already impose circular length at least four. Their weights are unambiguous, so this correction does not challenge the core obstruction.

Problem 5 (A false core equality in the discretization proof: Major).  Proposition 5.7, pp. 14–15, defines its core-building seed as \(\operatorname{Star}(T_V\cap U,\mathcal W_0)\), but the following path argument uses \(\operatorname{Star}(T_V,\mathcal W_0)\). The resulting assertion \(\operatorname{Core}(V,\mathcal W)=\operatorname{Star}(T_V,\mathcal W)\) is false for the printed construction (source TeX lines 1141–1174). This equality supplies core connectedness for Theorems 5.8–5.9.

Proposition 5.7 assumes only that \(X\) is a Peano continuum and \(\mathcal U,\mathcal V\) are brick partitions. It does not require the absence of locally separating points. The following circle therefore lies within its actual hypotheses. On \(\mathbb R/12\mathbb Z\), take \(\mathcal U=\{(0,8),(8,12)\}\), \(\mathcal V=\{(6,14),(2,6)\}\), unit-arc refinement \(\mathcal W_0\), and trees \([7.5,12]\) and \([3.5,4.5]\). Write \(I_j=(j,j+1)\) modulo \(12\) and denote the two trees by \(T_0,T_1\). Each coarse and fine brick is connected, regular open and uniformly locally connected; each regular join of two bricks is uniformly locally connected. Both trees lie in their assigned \(V\) and meet every required coarse \(U\). The closure-based definition gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Star}(T_0,\mathcal W_0)&=\{I_0,I_7,I_8,I_9,I_{10},I_{11}\},\\
 \operatorname{Star}(T_1,\mathcal W_0)&=\{I_3,I_4\},\\
 \operatorname{Star}(\partial V_0,\mathcal W_0)
   &=\operatorname{Star}(\partial V_1,\mathcal W_0)=\{I_1,I_2,I_5,I_6\}.
\end{aligned}\] Thus the exact fineness requirement holds. The printed local seeds produce \(\mathcal C^{U_0,V_0}=\{I_7\}\), \(\mathcal C^{U_1,V_0}=\{I_8,I_9,I_{10},I_{11}\}\), \(\mathcal C^{U_0,V_1}=\{I_3,I_4\}\) and \(\mathcal C^{U_1,V_1}=\varnothing\). The remaining components in \(U_0\) are \(\{I_0,I_1,I_2\}\) and \(\{I_5,I_6\}\); none remain in \(U_1\). The printed seeds produce \(\mathcal W=\{(0,3),(3,5),(5,7),(7,8),(8,12)\}\). For \(V=(6,14)\) the core is \(\{(7,8),(8,12)\}\), but \(\operatorname{Star}(T_V,\mathcal W)\) also contains \((0,3)\), whose closure meets \(12=0\) and is not contained in \(V\). Thus the auxiliary equality fails with the displayed local seeds. The path argument fails at the same point: \(I_0\) is already in the global tree star but not in the local seed. Its one-vertex path to that star crosses no boundary-star brick. In this example the final core is nevertheless connected. The example refutes the stated equality and the supplied proof step, not the conclusion of Proposition 5.7.

Use \(\mathcal W_0(U\cap V)\cap\operatorname{Star}(T_V,\mathcal W_0)\) as the seed instead. Fineness puts every seed brick in the core. Each resulting core component meets the global tree star, whereas every remaining component meets the boundary star by the first-boundary-crossing argument. After amalgamation this proves \(\operatorname{Core}(V,\mathcal W)=\operatorname{Star}(T_V,\mathcal W)\) and its connectedness, together with adjacency of the remaining bricks to a core. Every global tree-star brick is now assigned to a core component, so no remaining component touches the tree. Conversely, every core component contains a seed and touches the tree. Finiteness of the amalgamation identifies the closure of each amalgam with the union of its constituent closures, giving the claimed equality. The graph star of a connected tree is connected, and the remaining components have neighbors among the core components. This restores exactly the input needed by Theorem 5.8.

The defect is Major because the false equality is the printed justification of core connectedness, an essential input to the monotone-epimorphism discretization and hence Theorem 5.9 and the main obstruction. The availability of this replacement construction does not make that proof step valid. No counterexample to the proposition’s existential conclusion or to the main theorem is claimed.

# Repaired proof steps and nonfindings

The generic-turbulence induction on p. 30 ends by applying Lemma 7.2 to a coarse walk \(u\), although that lemma assumes a spaced path and \(u\) was only chosen to cover the coarse partition. The conclusion can be justified without that application. The fine walk \(v\) is spaced. If \(D_j\) is its current prefix witness, the moved continuum \(h_j[D_j]\) joins the first and last fine-prefix bricks and stays in their regular join. Lemma 7.2 applied to this fine path supplies every already visited coarse-prefix witness.

For a later coarse first-visit set \(P\), take its original witness \(A_P\) in the current chain. It meets a brick not yet visited by \(D_j\), so chain ordering forces \(D_j\subseteq A_P\). The support of \(h_j\) lies in the newly visited coarse union, which is contained in \(\bigvee P\). Its image of \(A_P\) therefore stays in \(\bigvee P\), contains the new witness of all visited bricks, and retains the intersections with all future bricks outside the support. This verifies every coarse prefix and preserves membership in \(\mathbf O_{\mathcal U,u}\). The preliminary root adjustment can likewise be supported in a sufficiently small connected neighborhood inside \(u(0)\), ensuring membership in the prescribed identity neighborhood directly. These arguments fill the shortcut; they are not a counterexample to Theorem 7.7.

Appendix B uses a different mechanism: irrational separating circles cut the carpet into pieces which are again S-curves. The boundary-extension theorem is applied to three pieces with matching boundary maps, followed by the complementary piece. The maps send the root piece into \(U_0\), the selected later point into \(U_1\), and preserve the chosen larger component inside \(V\). The actual Figure 3 and the gluing domains on pp. 39–40 were checked. This supports the minimality construction subject to its cited Whyburn extension and carpet-characterization results.

Several displayed conventions should be corrected without being inflated into additional confirmed core defects. Definition 5.1 must test the nonempty prefixes \(1\leq i\leq\operatorname{lh}(w)\), rather than including \(i=0\) and omitting the last prefix. Repeated circular walks must suppress the duplicated joining endpoint, or consistently use a stutter convention. In Theorem A.6 the intersection defining the endpoint must omit \(C=S^1\); its inclusion contributes an empty set. For Proposition A.4, approximate a finite ordered list by nearby points on a strictly decreasing graph, allowing both coordinates to move. Holding \(x_i\) fixed cannot approximate two different \(y_i\) with the same \(x_i\); this does not prevent Hausdorff density. The infinite pasting step can use the order and density of domain and range to obtain continuity, rather than an unrestricted pasting lemma.

The literal joining-edge bounds in Claim 6.4.1 require separate bookkeeping: for ordinary concatenation, \[\operatorname{wd}(a^\frown b)=\operatorname{wd}(a)+\operatorname{weight}(a(-1),b(0))+\operatorname{wd}(b).\] The bounded calculation gives a projected circular word \(\langle0,3,0,1,2,3,0\rangle\) on the oriented four-cycle, with net weight \(N=4\) and maximal absolute prefix weight \(M_c=5\). A stem ending at label \(1\) has \(M_v=0\) and joins the initial \(0\) with weight \(-1\). For \(k=2\), the printed choice satisfies \(kN=8>3M_v+M_c+2=7\), but the complete concatenation has weight \(7\), not the strict bound \(>2M_v+M_c+2=7\) claimed in Claim 6.4.1(b0). Subdividing transitions with stutters yields a finite graph realization compatible with the distance buffer (6.3), without changing these numbers.

This is retained as Needs Evidence, not as a confirmed counterexample under every hypothesis. The bounded graph has not been realized as all the Peano-space brick partitions and robust-cycle witnesses required by the claim. The earlier concern about (a0)–(a1) is withdrawn: the definition of \(M_c\) already controls prefixes in both orientations and provides the necessary slack there. For a repaired proof, enlarge the stem allowance and choose \(k\) with an additional fixed margin, then include each joining edge explicitly. This indicates the repair boundary; it is not certification of a complete repaired topological argument.
