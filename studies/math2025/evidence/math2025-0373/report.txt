Abstract: This focused initial review identifies one substantive defect in the published proof of the optimal Gaussian rigidity tail. The deterministic event inclusion at the end of Section 6.4 fails for an explicit family of spectra, including open sets of positive probability for a valid real Gaussian Wigner ensemble. The discrepancy is a leading-order coefficient, not a vanishing smoothing error. A local change of threshold suggests a repair, but a complete revised probability argument is not certified here. The theorem itself is not disproved. Scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Normalization and the claimed implication

Equation reference: eq:tail. Let \[\rho(x)=\frac{\sqrt{(4-x^2)_+}}{2\pi},\qquad
 F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x\rho(y)\,dy,\qquad
 F(\gamma_k)=\frac{k-\frac12}{N},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{\sqrt{\log N}}{N}.\] The eigenvalues are in increasing order. For a fixed compact bulk range, put \(c_k=\sqrt2/(\pi\rho(\gamma_k))\). Theorem 1.8(ii), printed p. 166, seeks a bound of the form \[\mathbb P\{|\lambda_k-\gamma_k|>u c_k\delta\}
 \leq C\exp\bigl(-(1-\varepsilon)u^2\bigr),
 \qquad 0\leq u\leq A\sqrt{\log N}.
 \] Here \(C\) may depend on the fixed parameters. Part (i) is a separate, signed-maximum statement and is not the finding below.

Equation reference: eq:smoothing. The centered trace on p. 197 is \(\overline{\operatorname{Tr}}f(H)=\sum_j f(\lambda_j)-N\int f(x)\rho(x)\,dx\). Equations (5.38)–(5.40), p. 206, use smoothing width \(\eta_1=\delta\). The first step of Section 6.4, p. 214, specifies the increasing transition as \[f_E(y)=\int_0^1 h(s)\mathbf1_{[E+s\delta,\infty)}(y)\,ds,
 \quad h\geq0,\quad \int_0^1h(s)\,ds=1,
 \] with smooth \(h\) supported in \([0,1]\). The prescribed upper cutoff above \(2.5\) has no effect on the spectra or centering integrals used below.

# Problem 1: a false event inclusion in the core tail proof

Equation reference: eq:claim. Classification: Major. Location: printed p. 215 (physical page 55), paragraph immediately after (6.19), and the resulting Markov bound continuing to p. 216. For fixed \(\varepsilon\), sufficiently large \(N\), and any \(u>1\), the paper claims \[\lambda_k-\gamma_k>u c_k\delta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}}f_E(H)>(1-\varepsilon)\frac{u\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\log N},
 \qquad E=\gamma_k+u(c_k-1)\delta.
 \] The factor \(u\) multiplies the entire parenthesis in the actual PDF. This placement is also present in the publisher HTML consulted for version checking.

## A decisive family satisfying the relevant conditions

Equation reference: eq:spectrum, eq:upper. Take arbitrarily large odd \(N\), \(k=(N+1)/2\), \(\kappa=1/4\), \(A=1\), \(u=4\), and \(\varepsilon=1/4\). Then \(\gamma_k=0\), \(\rho(0)=1/\pi\), \(c_k=\sqrt2\), and \(u\leq A\sqrt{\log N}\) for large \(N\). Define the strictly increasing spectrum \[b=(4\sqrt2+1)\delta,\qquad
 \lambda_j=
 \begin{cases}
  \gamma_j,&j<k,\\
  \gamma_j+b,&j\geq k.
 \end{cases}
 \] The antecedent of [eq:claim] (#eq:claim) is strict. At its printed threshold \(E=4(\sqrt2-1)\delta\), all eigenvalues with \(j<k\) lie below \(E\), and \[\lambda_k-(E+\delta)=4\delta>0.\] All eigenvalues are below \(2.5\) for sufficiently large \(N\). Thus, for every smoothing density in [eq:smoothing] (#eq:smoothing), \(\sum_j f_E(\lambda_j)=(N+1)/2\). Fubini’s theorem and \(F(0)=1/2\) give \[\begin{aligned}
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}}f_E(H)
 &=\frac12+N\int_0^1h(s)\bigl(F(E+s\delta)-\tfrac12\bigr)\,ds \notag\\
 &\leq\frac12+\frac{4\sqrt2-3}{\pi}\sqrt{\log N},
 \end{aligned}\] where only the exact bound \(\rho(x)\leq1/\pi\) was used. The claimed lower bound is \[\frac{3\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\log N}.\] Its difference from the upper bound in [eq:upper] (#eq:upper) is \[\frac{3-\sqrt2}{\pi}\sqrt{\log N}-\frac12>0\] for all sufficiently large odd \(N\). This contradicts the implication with strict margins. It is a uniform analytic construction, not an extrapolation from a finite numerical search.

To connect this spectrum to the paper’s probabilistic hypotheses, take the real Gaussian Wigner ensemble with independent upper-triangular entries, off-diagonal variance \(1/N\), and diagonal variance \(2/N\). This satisfies Definition 1.1, including the uniform subexponential tail condition. The diagonal matrix with spectrum [eq:spectrum] (#eq:spectrum) is a point in the support of this ensemble; it is not itself claimed to be a random Wigner ensemble. Gaussian density is positive on the entire real symmetric matrix space. Eigenvalues and the smooth trace are continuous, so the strict antecedent and strict failure persist in an open neighborhood of that diagonal matrix. That neighborhood has positive probability for each sufficiently large odd \(N\).

The example also lies inside the ordinary rigidity bound (2.9) on p. 175 for sufficiently large \(N\): its displacement is \(O(\sqrt{\log N}/N)\), whereas the smallest permitted scale is of order \(\varphi/N\), with \(\varphi=\exp(C_0(\log\log N)^2)\) and \(\varphi/\sqrt{\log N}\to\infty\). Invoking that rigidity event alone does not remove the counterexample. No lower bound on the probability of the violating neighborhood is claimed.

## Dependency and repair boundary

Equation [eq:claim] (#eq:claim) converts the centered-trace moment bound (6.19) into the final Markov estimate and the sharp exponent in [eq:tail] (#eq:tail). Its failure therefore invalidates this core step of the displayed proof. The lost coefficient persists as \(N\to\infty\); it cannot be absorbed by choosing a larger \(N\) for every fixed \(\varepsilon\). This is the reason for the Major classification, even if a short correction ultimately suffices. The construction does not disprove Theorem 1.8(ii), nor does it refute the characteristic-polynomial maximum theorems.

Equation reference: eq:repair, eq:repairbound. A concrete local repair candidate is \[E_*=\gamma_k+(u c_k-1)\delta,
 \] subtracting one smoothing width outside the factor \(u\). On the spectral event that makes the upper cutoff irrelevant, the antecedent then implies \[\overline{\operatorname{Tr}}f_{E_*}(H)
 \geq \frac12+N\bigl(F(E_*)-F(\gamma_k)\bigr)
 =\left(\frac{u\sqrt2}{\pi}-\rho(\gamma_k)+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\log N}.
 \] The Taylor remainder is uniform on a fixed compact bulk range for \(u\leq A\sqrt{\log N}\): before division by \(\sqrt{\log N}\), it is \(O((1+u)^2\log N/N)=o(\sqrt{\log N})\). Consequently the desired relative coefficient follows for \(u\) above a sufficiently large constant depending on \(\varepsilon\). The bounded-\(u\) range may be absorbed into a larger prefactor \(C\) in [eq:tail] (#eq:tail). A complete repair must still supply the range split, the cutoff exceptional-event estimate, and a consistent moment optimization. In particular, the full low-moment and comparison estimates were not verified here. Only the deterministic correction [eq:repair] (#eq:repair)–[eq:repairbound] (#eq:repairbound) is checked; a repaired complete theorem is not certified.

# Verification and limitations

An independently written scalar script checks rational bounds for \(\sqrt2\), the strict margins, and an illustrative odd dimension \(N=100000001\). It returns an upper bound \(4.129700\) and claimed lower bound \(5.796145\), with positive gap \(1.666445\). These floating-point values illustrate the analytic proof; they are not its justification. No author code was executed and no huge matrix was constructed.

A bounded publisher/arXiv correction search did not identify a correction. The arXiv record lists v2 dated 10 August 2024, but that PDF was not compared with the assigned publisher PDF.
