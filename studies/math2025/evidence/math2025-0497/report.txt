Abstract: This review concerns Stefan Junk’s 36-page arXiv version 2202.02907v3. The exceptional-site construction, conditional concentration argument, quadratic variation and moment-growth proof were examined. Six groups of locally repairable issues are identified, including an auxiliary lower bound at zero temperature parameter, an incorrect moment exponent calculation, and missing qualifications in background statements. Explicit counterexamples and repairs are supplied. These findings are classified as one Major and five Minor findings. The Major finding concerns an invalid core moment-growth derivation; no false main theorem is demonstrated.

# Reviewed version, scope and dependencies

The source is [paper], arXiv v3 dated 10 July 2023, with internal date 11 July. Its entire body and appendix, pp. 1–34, were read; the bibliography on pp. 35–36 was read as entries. All 36 source pages were inspected as images. The later journal article is Annals of Probability 53(2), 557–596 (2025), DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1716. The acquired 36-page preprint is not certified equivalent to that 40-page article. The official arXiv history acknowledges a correction to Theorem 1.4(i) in v2 and additional proof details in v3. This review addresses v3 itself and does not identify its findings with that earlier correction.

The model assumes IID real weights with all absolute exponential moments, nearest-neighbor simple random walk, and \(\beta\geq0\). Write \[\eta_{t,x}=e^{\beta\omega_{t,x}-\lambda(\beta)},\qquad
 W_n=E^{\rm SRW}\prod_{t=1}^n\eta_{t,X_t},\qquad
 a(p)=\lim_n n^{-1}\log\mathbb E W_n^p .\] Weak disorder means \(W_\infty>0\) almost surely. The critical indices are \(p^*=\sup\{p\geq1:\sup_n\mathbb E W_n^p<\infty\}\) and \(q^*=\inf\{p\geq1:a(p)>0\}\). The main theorem additionally requires an upper bounded environment and \(\beta>\beta_{L^2}\). These restrictions have different roles from the broader hypotheses printed for Theorem 1.4.

The review checked the geometry and independence of the backward cones, the signed test function, the moment and stopping-time inequalities, and all parameter regimes used by the proof. The relevant construction in Yoshida’s author-corrected reference [yoshida] was checked on pp. 3–8 and 13–18: its matrix assumptions specialize to the polymer, and \(\gamma=e^{\lambda(2\beta)-2\lambda(\beta)}>1/\pi_d\) supplies the finite convolution kernel used for the drift estimate. This construction does not require strong disorder. Its bounded-process and conditional second-moment estimates justify the particular dependency used here. This is a check of that dependency, not a review of every result in Yoshida’s paper.

A later paper by Junk and Lacoin [tail], pp. 3–6, 8–9, 14 and 25, provides relevant counterevidence: it states \(p^*>1\) and \(p^*=q^*\) throughout weak disorder, uses the normalized overshoot factor, and extends the fluctuation result. Its hypotheses and indicated corollary proofs were checked; its complete proof was not independently reproduced. The findings below therefore distinguish an incomplete or false displayed step from a false fluctuation theorem.

# Problem 1: the parameter range needs two qualifications (Minor)

## Zero effective disorder in Theorem 1.4(i)

The model permits \(\beta=0\) (p. 2), while Theorem 1.4(i), p. 6, (19), assumes only \(d\geq3\), weak disorder and a nonzero \(f\in C_c(\mathbb R^d)\). Its following paragraph expressly removes the main theorem’s \(\beta>\beta_{L^2}\) restriction. Take any nonconstant bounded IID environment and set \(\beta=0\). Then, exactly, \[W_n^{0,x}=1,\qquad \mathcal X_n^f=0,\qquad a(p)=0,\qquad q^*=\infty .\] With \(1/\infty=0\), the probability in (19) is one for every \(n\), whereas the asserted limit is zero. Without this reciprocal convention the displayed bound is undefined. The same obstruction occurs for a constant environment at positive \(\beta\), also not excluded explicitly.

This refutes the literal auxiliary theorem at a permitted boundary. It does not refute Theorem 1.1, whose beyond-\(L^2\) condition excludes this case. The proof on p. 19 itself invokes a nonconstant centered environment factor, showing the intended qualification. One should require nonconstant \(\eta\) for the nontrivial lower bound. Then \(q^*<\infty\) follows directly: choose \(a>0\) with \(\mathbb P(\eta\geq1+a)>0\), and a finite box whose killed-walk spectral radius \(\rho\) satisfies \(\rho(1+a)>1\). The event that every weight in the box is good through time \(n\) has probability \(p_0^{|B|n}\), and on it \(W_n\geq c[\rho(1+a)]^n\). A sufficiently large fixed moment consequently grows exponentially.

## Finite critical points are not automatic

Theorem A(i)–(ii), p. 2, asserts finite positive critical points under the standing assumptions. Nondegeneracy alone does not suffice. Let \(\pi_d<1\) be the positive-time meeting probability of two independent walks in \(d\geq3\), and set \(r=(1+\pi_d)/2\). Choose \(\omega\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\mathbb P(\omega=1)=r\). This law is nonconstant, bounded and has every exponential moment. For \(t=e^{-\beta}\in[0,1]\), \[R_\beta=e^{\lambda(2\beta)-2\lambda(\beta)}
 =\frac{r+(1-r)t^2}{[r+(1-r)t]^2}\leq\frac1r.\] Indeed the cleared numerator of this inequality is \((1-r)t[2r+(1-2r)t]\geq0\). The number \(L\) of positive-time meetings is geometric with \(\mathbb P(L=k)=(1-\pi_d)\pi_d^k\), by transience and the renewal property. Thus \[\sup_n\mathbb E W_n^2
 \leq \mathbb E R_\beta^L
 \leq \frac{1-\pi_d}{1-\pi_d/r}<\infty\] for every finite \(\beta\). The \(L^2\) martingale limit has expectation one; the zero-one law then gives weak disorder at every \(\beta\). Both critical points can be infinite.

The correction is to allow extended critical values and qualify assertions of a finite strict gap. The later formulation in [tail], p. 3, explicitly permits infinite critical values. For this example the beyond-\(L^2\) hypothesis is empty, so it is not a counterexample to the main result. The impact is on the stated model range and background theorem, with a complete boundary diagnosis.

# Problem 2: the Gaussian normalization is inconsistent (Minor)

Theorem A(iii), p. 2, scales the endpoint by \(\sqrt n\) and calls \(\varphi\) the standard normal density. The same convention is used in (27), p. 10, and in Lemma 2.6, pp. 13–15. A nearest-neighbor walk on \(\mathbb Z^d\) has one-step covariance \(I_d/d\). At \(\beta=0\), the bounded continuous test \(f(x)=\cos x_1\), permitted in Theorem A(iii), gives \[E\cos(X_n^{(1)}/\sqrt n)
 =\left[1-\frac{1-\cos(1/\sqrt n)}d\right]^n
 \longrightarrow e^{-1/(2d)},\] instead of the printed standard-Gaussian value \(e^{-1/2}\). This is an exact characteristic-function calculation; the independent numeric check in \(d=3\) converges to \(0.8464817249\), whereas the latter value is \(0.6065306597\).

Use \[\varphi_d(x)=\left(\frac d{2\pi}\right)^{d/2}e^{-d|x|^2/2}\] throughout these displays, or consistently rescale the walk. The nonvanishing Fourier-transform argument used to choose \(z\) still works for \(\varphi_d\). For a signed \(f\), \(z\) must be chosen again with \((f*\varphi_d)(z)\ne0\); the old choice for a different covariance need not work. The existence argument and all polynomial fluctuation exponents survive. An intended informal use of standard would explain the notation but does not make the literal normalization correct.

# Problem 3: Proposition 2.1 overstates the fractional domain (Minor)

Proposition 2.1(i)–(ii), p. 8, gives \(a(p)\in[0,\lambda(p\beta)-p\lambda(\beta)]\) and a nondecreasing function on \(\mathbb R_+\). Its proof explicitly includes \(p\in[0,1]\) and says the section does not require weak disorder. The nonnegative bounds are valid for \(p\geq1\); for \(0<p<1\), Jensen’s inequality gives instead \[\lambda(p\beta)-p\lambda(\beta)\leq a(p)\leq0.\] The printed interval can be empty for a nonconstant environment.

For an actual failure of monotonicity, take \(d=1\), standard Gaussian weights, \(\beta=4\), and \(p=1/2\). All standing exponential moments exist. Expanding over \(2^n\) paths and using \((\sum_i z_i)^p\leq\sum_i z_i^p\) gives \[\mathbb E W_n^p
 \leq \exp\{n[(1-p)\log2+\lambda(p\beta)-p\lambda(\beta)]\},
 \qquad
 -2\leq a(1/2)\leq\tfrac12\log2-2<0.\] Yet \(a(0)=a(1)=0\), so \(a\) is not nondecreasing on \(\mathbb R_+\). The superadditive argument supplies existence of the fractional limit. Restrict the nonnegative and monotonicity claims to \([1,\infty)\) and state the reversed fractional bounds separately. This is also the plausible intended reading: the later \(q^*\) argument uses \(p\geq1\). No failure of that argument or of the main theorem follows.

# Problem 4: repair the general lower-bound proof (Minor)

## The unstated moment assumption and its replacement

Lemma 2.6, p. 13, assumes weak disorder but immediately chooses \(p\in(1,p^*)\). This is not justified by the acquired paper’s stated general hypotheses: pp. 2–3 and 6–7 explicitly distinguish weak disorder from \(p^*>1\). Its use of Hölder on p. 15 also requires the bad event \(A_{t,x}^c\), rather than the printed good event. For the main theorem, the upper-bound assumption and Theorem B already provide \(p^*>1\). The later result [tail] supplies another reason not to regard this gap as a false theorem.

The following replacement proves the needed step under uniform integrability alone. Put \(c=|(f*\varphi_d)(z)|>0\), retain the good CLT event \(A_{t,x}\), and write \[b_n=\sup_{t,x}\mathbb P(A_{t,x}^c)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 u_n=\sup_{t,x}\mathbb E[W_t\mathbf1_{A_{t,x}^c}].\] The supremum uses the source’s space-time window, enlarged by the \(o(n)\) and \(o(\sqrt n)\) shifts from \(\ell_n\). For every \(R>0\), \[u_n\leq Rb_n+\sup_t\mathbb E[W_t\mathbf1_{\{W_t>R\}}]\longrightarrow0,\] first choosing \(R\) large, then \(n\) large. Let \(h_n=\sqrt{u_n}+n^{-1}\). Conditionally on the last \(\ell_n\) layers, the endpoint probabilities \(\mu_x\) sum to one and are independent of the older variables. Consequently \[\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_x\mu_xW_x\mathbf1_{A_x^c}\,\middle|\,
             \text{last layers}\right]\leq u_n .\] Markov’s inequality bounds its probability of exceeding \(h_n\) by \(u_n/h_n\leq\sqrt{u_n}\); the corresponding bad signed-\(f\) sum is bounded with the additional factor \(\|f\|_\infty\). On good sites the normalized \(f\) averages have a common sign and absolute value at least \(c/2\). The argument in (44)–(47) therefore reduces to a lower-tail event for \(\sum_x\mu_xW_x\) at a threshold \(C(h_n+n^{-\varepsilon/8})\to0\). Lemma 2.3 and \(\inf_tW_t>0\) almost surely under weak disorder finish the estimate. Only \(p_n\to0\), without a rate, is needed for the later choice of \(N_n\).

## Signed amplitudes and geometric details

In the proof of (55), pp. 19–20, the concentration scale and the event for \(\widehat Z_k\) omit its absolute value. For a nonzero nonpositive \(f\), the coefficients are nonpositive, so \(\{\widehat Z_k\leq\text{positive threshold}\}\) is automatic. It cannot imply the subsequent small-absolute-amplitude event. Use \(Q_{cY}(u)=Q_Y(u/|c|)\) for \(c\ne0\), and replace the scale and event by \(|\widehat Z_k|\). The already absolute truncation-error estimate then supplies the intended conclusion for either sign.

The following local corrections preserve this proof’s power estimates. In Section 2 use \(\xi_q=d/2-(d+2)/(2q^*)\) until equality \(p^*=q^*\) is established; the printed \(\xi\) was defined through \(p^*\). Before (35), p. 12, choose \(\mathbb P(\inf_mW_m>2a)>3/4\), which weak disorder permits, to obtain the stated \(1/2\) bound. On pp. 15–16 the number of nearby spatial grid points is at most \[C\left(1+\frac{n^{(1-\delta)/2}}{\ell_n}\right)^d,\] rather than the printed bound linear in the radius. Dividing by \(|S_n|\asymp n^{d/2}/\ell_n^d\) gives \(O(n^{-d\delta/2})\); hence the same conditioning geometric sum is eventually at most two. The inclusion on p. 18 is \(E_n\subseteq\widehat E_n\).

In (64), p. 21, enlarging visits to near visits can leave an extra \(\eta\) in the path energy. Increase the integration constant to cover both \(\mathbb E|\eta-1|\) and \(\mathbb E[\eta|\eta-1|]\). These moments are finite. The selection-density formula on p. 22 must retain the chosen site’s good-event indicator and the translated time block. That indicator is fixed on the integrated coordinates; the other factors decrease, preserving the product-measure FKG inequality. The correct heat-kernel factor is \(C(\Delta t)^{-d/2}\exp(-c|\Delta x|^2/\Delta t)\). Since the construction gives \(|\Delta x|^2/\Delta t\geq c n^{5\delta/6}\), polynomial factors are still dominated. These corrections support the conditional-independent singleton decomposition and its negligible multiple-visit error. The complete arguments and exact locators are retained with the evidence.

The dependency is the lower bound in Theorem 1.4(i), and thence the main theorem after \(p^*=q^*\). The explicit repairs and later moment result limit severity: the written proof needs correction, but no nondegenerate positive-\(\beta\) counterexample to its conclusion has been obtained.

# Problem 5: the moment-growth exponent and renewal indices (Major)

Lemma 4.7, p. 32, obtains \(C\alpha^{-\lambda(2+\eta)k}\), then sets \(\lambda=p^*(1-\eta/2)\) to claim \(C\alpha^{-2(p^*+\delta)k}\) with \(\delta=\eta^2p^*/2>0\). But the exact calculation is \[\lambda(2+\eta)=p^*(2-\eta^2/2)<2p^*.\] Thus this bound is weaker than the scale it must beat. For example \(p^*=3/2,\eta=1/2\) gives power \(45/16<3\). The independently executed symbolic check confirms the identity; it is not a numerical approximation.

For every \(p^*>1\) and \(0<\eta<1\), set \[L=\max\left\{1,\frac{2p^*}{2+\eta}\right\},\qquad
 \lambda=\frac{p^*+L}{2},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{\lambda(2+\eta)-2p^*}{2}>0.\] Then \(1<\lambda<p^*\), so Doob’s inequality supplies the finite maximal moment. Apply the two stopping-time estimates to the joint event \(\{\sigma_i<\infty,\tau(\alpha^{2k})<\infty\}\), rather than its incorrectly labeled marginal, and take the union over \(i\). This proves (93) with corrected constants.

In the proof of Theorem 1.5, p. 33, further choose \[\eta=\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{2(p^*+\varepsilon+1)},
                   \frac{p^*-1}{2}\right\}.\] This makes the subsequent \(\lambda=p^*-\eta\) admissible. Since \(\sigma<\tau(\alpha^{l+1})\), the remaining gain used for (95) is \(\alpha^{2k-l-1}\); the omitted factor only changes a constant. The renewal variables must also accumulate their increments: \[\begin{split}
 \sigma_{i+1}&=\sigma_i+\sigma\circ\theta_{\sigma_i,Z_i},\\
 Z_{i+1}&=Z_i+Z\circ\theta_{\sigma_i,Z_i}.
 \end{split}\] The printed definitions omit these additions, although (97) uses absolute times and positions.

With these corrections, successive durations and relative locations use fresh independent time layers. If the first \(\lfloor n/T\rfloor\) durations are at most \(T\), their cumulative time is at most \(n\). Each contributes at least \(c_8\alpha^{l-k\eta}\), and the remaining future infimum contributes a fixed positive factor with probability at least \(1/2\). Equations (95)–(96) then give a moment-growth base \(Ck^{-1}\alpha^{k\varepsilon/2}>1\) for large \(k\), preserving the conclusion of Theorem 1.5 conditional on its localization and tail lemmas. These invalid steps materially interrupt the core derivation: the printed exponent cannot make the bad-event probability smaller than the \(\alpha^{-2kp^*}\) lower-tail scale used on p. 33. Thus the acquired proof of Theorem 1.5, and its use to identify the main fluctuation exponent, requires correction. This is Major under a calibration that includes invalid core derivations; proving the final theorem false is not required. The admissible replacement and cumulative renewal construction above repair this segment. The later \(p^*=q^*\) result in [tail] is counterevidence to a false-conclusion claim, not validation of the printed proof.

# Problem 6: upper-bounded weights require normalized constants (Minor)

On p. 27, Lemma 4.3 uses \(W_{n+1}\leq e^{\beta K}W_n\) and \(\alpha=2e^{\beta K}\). The actual bound under \(\omega\leq K\) is \[W_{n+1}\leq MW_n,\qquad M=e^{\beta K-\lambda(\beta)}.\] No centering or \(\lambda(\beta)\geq0\) assumption appears in pp. 1–3. This is not remedied by the upper-bound hypothesis alone. From any nondegenerate admissible model in the intended weak-disorder, beyond-\(L^2\) range, shift the weights by their finite upper bound. All normalized polymer quantities and disorder regimes remain unchanged, but the weights become nonpositive. Since \(W_1\) is nonconstant with mean one, \(\mathbb P(W_1>1+r)>0\) for some \(r>0\). Any \(0<K<\log(1+r)/\beta\) is now an eligible positive upper bound, and the displayed \(n=0\) inequality fails on that event. An executed two-point-law calculation illustrates the same normalization failure; it is not asserted to be a beyond-\(L^2\) parameter witness.

Set \(\alpha=2M\). The overshoot used in (79) is then correctly \(W_{\tau(\alpha^{i-1})}\leq M\alpha^{i-1}=\alpha^i/2\). Also \(0<H_nW_{n-1}\leq1\) gives the valid lower increment bound \(-1\), rather than the printed \(-\alpha^{-1}\). Bounded increments and the quadratic-variation estimates still hold. The exponential-supermartingale step follows directly from the bounded Taylor remainder and conditional mean-zero increments.

In Appendix (98), p. 34, replace \(e^{\beta K}\) by \(M\) as well. The same contradiction supplies an adequate positive tail constant \(\tfrac12M^{-p^*}\), or \(\tfrac12M^{-2}\) when \(p^*<2\). This diagnoses the proof’s normalization; it does not independently disprove the printed tail constant. The later overshoot formula [tail], (2.11), uses precisely the normalized factor.

A related repair to the finite moment calculation on pp. 31–32 is to retain the walk transition probability and use \[A(x)=\frac1{2d}\sum_{|y|_1=1}\eta_{m,x+y}^2 .\] Jensen gives \(\mathbb EA^4\leq\mathbb E\eta^8\) and \(\mathbb E(\sum_x\mu_x\eta_x)^{-8}\leq\mathbb E\eta^{-8}\). Cauchy–Schwarz consequently yields \[\mathbb E(\mathcal R_m^2\mid\mathcal F_{m-1})
 \leq e^{[\lambda(8\beta)+\lambda(-8\beta)]/2}\mathcal R_{m-1}^2.\] This is finite under (1), and is stronger than the required constant. These repairs preserve the route through Lemmas 4.3–4.5. The issue concerns stated constants and normalization, not a demonstrated failure of Theorem 1.5.

# Positive checks and limits of the conclusion

The quadratic variation (69) was independently recovered by conditioning on the next IID environment layer. For the upper bound, the exact finite support is contained in \([-n-L\sqrt n,n+L\sqrt n]^d\), still of order \(n^d\); a maximal moderate-deviation estimate controls the remote portion. The level sum yields power \((d+2)/p\) for \(p\leq2\) and \(1+d/2\) for \(p>2\), up to an arbitrarily small loss. One must retain the dropped \(d\delta\) in the last \(p^*>2\) display on p. 25 and shrink \(\delta\); for \(p^*=\infty\), choose a finite \(p>2\). Stopping before the next predictable-variation threshold and applying Markov to the square gives both tails in (21).

The appendix’s homogenization argument also checks: with \(N=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), uniform integrability controls the truncation and the difference \(W_n-W_N\). The nearby-pair contribution is \(O(M^2N^dn^{-d/2})=O(M^2n^{-d/6})\), which vanishes at fixed \(M\); the distant pairs contribute the squared truncation bias.

The primary reference and later-result checks constrain the findings, but do not certify every historical theorem used by the paper. Publisher full-text access was unsuccessful, and no exhaustive absence of later corrections is claimed. No author code was executed. Problem 5 is Major because its wrong exponent invalidates a core probability comparison used to establish \(p^*=q^*\); its explicit repair does not validate the original derivation. Problems 1–4 and 6 remain Minor in their stated scope: the parameter and fractional-moment qualifications, covariance, signs, geometric counts and normalized constants have the bounded impacts and repairs described above. In Problem 4 the missing moment implication concerns the broader auxiliary range, while the main bounded-environment hypotheses already supply it. The auxiliary boundary assertion is false as printed; the main nondegenerate beyond-\(L^2\) theorem has not been refuted. Independent focused acceptance has checked these existing findings and corrected the severity of Problem 5. Scientific disposition remains pending.
