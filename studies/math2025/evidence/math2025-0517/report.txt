Abstract: The exact energy representation in Lemma 9.1 has an incorrect coefficient on the terminal square. This is a Major defect in a core derivation: it gives a wrong short-time coefficient within the paper’s own Gaussian model. Correcting that coefficient preserves the form of the subsequent small-amplitude estimates, so the defect does not disprove the final asymptotic theorem. Five Minor issues concern signs in the comparison equation, the Fourier phase, the rotational invariance argument, initialization and uniformity of a proxy moment bound, and a motivational statement about finite-range Gaussian fields. The review gives explicit repairs and counterevidence, checks the operative Gaussian and homogenization assumptions, and records the finite-time monotonicity step as an unresolved verification limit.

# A false exact energy representation

Problem 1 (Major: terminal energy coefficient).  On page 21, Lemma 9.1, equations (90) and (93), and the energy-test line preceding (93), the coefficient of \(\mathbb Ev(T)^2\) is incorrect. The same coefficient enters the energy definition on page 22 and its expansion (99) on page 23.

Here \(g=\epsilon b\cdot\xi\) and the stationary parabolic solution is defined by the source’s (89): \[\partial_t v-\Delta v-\epsilon b\cdot\nabla v=g,\qquad v(0)=0.\] These hypotheses include smoothness from the ultraviolet cutoff, Gaussian finite moments, stationarity and \(\nabla\cdot b=0\). They justify the following expectation identities, for instance by localization followed by the corresponding finite-moment bounds.

Equation reference: correct-energy. Claim 1.  Writing \(D(T)=\tfrac12\mathbb E(\xi\cdot X_T)^2\), the energy representation compatible with (89) is \[
 D(T)=T+\frac12\mathbb Ev(T)^2+
             \int_0^T\mathbb E|\nabla v(t)|^2\,\mathrm dt .\] The printed representation has coefficient \(1\) in place of \(1/2\) on the terminal square.

Proof. Testing the parabolic equation against \(v\) gives \[\frac12\frac{\,\mathrm d}{\,\mathrm dt}\mathbb Ev^2+\mathbb E|\nabla v|^2=\mathbb E(gv),\] because stationarity and incompressibility remove the transport term. The source’s preceding Itô and stationary heat-kernel calculation gives \(D(T)-T=\int_0^T\mathbb E(gv(t))\,\mathrm dt\). Integrating the displayed energy test, with \(v(0)=0\), proves [correct-energy] (#correct-energy). ◻

Equation reference: variance, short-time. There is a direct contradiction within the specified ensemble, rather than merely a counterexample using a different drift. By [cov] (#cov), \[
 \mathbb E(b\cdot\xi)^2
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^1r\,\mathrm dr\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\theta
 =\frac14.\] The small-time expansion is \(v(T)=Tg+O_{L^2}(T^2)\), and the integrated gradient energy is \(O(T^3)\). Hence the printed right-hand side is \(T+\epsilon^2T^2/4+O(T^3)\). Independently, apply the generator \(\mathcal L=\Delta+\epsilon b\cdot\nabla\) twice to \(F(x)=(\xi\cdot x)^2\). At the origin, \[\mathbb E\mathcal LF(0)=2,\qquad
 \mathbb E\mathcal L^2F(0)=2\epsilon^2\mathbb E(b\cdot\xi)^2,\] since the mean spatial derivative of \(b\) vanishes. Consequently \[
 D(T)=T+\frac{\epsilon^2}{8}T^2+O(T^3).\] For every fixed nonzero \(\epsilon\), the two coefficients disagree.

An independent spectral check at \(T=1\) confirms the discrepancy. For \(v_1=\partial_\epsilon v|_{\epsilon=0}\), put \(V=\mathbb Ev_1(1)^2\), \(G=\int_0^1\mathbb E|\nabla v_1(t)|^2\,\mathrm dt\), and let \(D_2\) be the coefficient of \(\epsilon^2\) in \(D(1)-1\). The exact integral formulas are \[\begin{aligned}
 V&=\frac12\int_0^1
      \frac{(1-e^{-r^2})^2}{r^3}\,\mathrm dr,\\
 D_2&=\frac12\int_0^1
      \left(\frac1{r^2}-\frac{1-e^{-r^2}}{r^4}\right)r\,\mathrm dr,
 &G&=D_2-\frac12V.\end{aligned}\] The saved 70-digit numerical integration gives \[V=0.1614377782128111,\quad
 G=0.0264611504249972,\quad
 D_2=0.1071800395314027.\] This is corroborating quadrature, not an interval certificate or a simulation of the stochastic differential equation. The analytic energy calculation and [short-time] (#short-time) are the decisive proofs.

#### Alternative readings and impact.

The term is not hidden in a normalization: (89) fixes \(v\), the Brownian coefficient is \(\sqrt2\), and the source itself uses a correct \(1/2\) in the comparison energy test on page 22. A one-symbol error in a preliminary computation might be Minor; here the false identity is the explicit bridge from the parabolic problem to the displacement observable and is used in the main proof. This establishes a Major invalid core derivation as printed.

The repair is to replace every terminal-square coefficient consistently, including the energy definition and the terminal terms in (99). The comparison estimate (98) still controls the corrected terminal contribution. The leading \(1+\mathbb E|\nabla\widetilde\phi_L|^2\), the integrated cross term, and the \(O(|\epsilon|\widetilde\lambda_L)\) error estimates retain their form. Thus this calculation does not refute the \(T\sqrt{\log T}\) scale, the small-amplitude asymptotics, or Theorem 1.2 on the stationary effective diffusivity.

#### Remaining all-amplitude verification limit.

The last paragraph of the proof on page 23 invokes monotonicity of finite-time energy in \(\epsilon^2\) by the argument of Lemma 8.1. The stationary proof on page 20 is valid in its setting. Its parabolic analogue has an additional time pairing. Indeed, for \(w=\partial_\epsilon v\) and \(\epsilon\ne0\), \[(\partial_t-\Delta-\epsilon b\cdot\nabla)w
       =\epsilon^{-1}(\partial_t-\Delta)v,\qquad w(0)=0.\] Testing with \(w\) introduces \(\int_0^T\mathbb E(w\partial_t v)\,\mathrm dt\); differentiating the corrected terminal energy introduces \(\mathbb E(v(T)w(T))\). Identifying these also involves \(\int_0^T\mathbb E(v\partial_t w)\,\mathrm dt\). The stationary calculation does not remove their difference. This review has not proved that the claimed finite-time monotonicity is false for this Gaussian model, nor established a counterexample to the all-amplitude lower bound. A separate justification is needed before that part of the proof can be regarded as independently verified. It is not counted as another demonstrated defect.

# Signs in the parabolic comparison equation

Problem 2 (Minor: equation (97)).  On page 22, the last two forcing terms of (97) have signs opposite to those obtained from the fixed definitions (12), (50) and (89).

Equation reference: correct-difference. The hypotheses are the same model, \(L=\sqrt T\geq1\), the time-independent proxy \(\widetilde\phi_L\), and the residual defined with the plus sign in \[a_L(\xi+\nabla\widetilde\phi_L)
   =\widetilde\lambda_L\xi+J\nabla\widetilde\sigma_L+f_L.\] Since \(\nabla\cdot(a_L\nabla u)=\Delta u+\epsilon b_L\cdot\nabla u\), taking divergence gives \[\Delta\widetilde\phi_L+
 \epsilon b_L\cdot(\xi+\nabla\widetilde\phi_L)=\nabla\cdot f_L.\] Therefore \(u=v-\widetilde\phi_L\) satisfies \[
 (\partial_t-\Delta-\epsilon b\cdot\nabla)u
 =\epsilon(b-b_L)\cdot\xi
  +\epsilon\nabla\cdot\bigl(\widetilde\phi_L(b-b_L)\bigr)
  +\nabla\cdot f_L .\] The source prints minus signs on the last two terms.

This is not an innocuous simultaneous redefinition of \(f_L\). At the first geometric step \(L=M>1\), \(\widetilde\phi_M=\epsilon\phi'_1\). The omitted low-frequency field \(b-b_M\) is independent of all high-shell quantities and has covariance \(I/(4M^2)\). For the difference between the correct and printed forcing, conditioning on the high shell and then taking expectation yields \[\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}\!\left(
 2\epsilon(b-b_M)\cdot\nabla\widetilde\phi_M
       +2\nabla\cdot f_M\mid\text{high shell}\right)
   =\frac{\epsilon^4\log M}{2M^2}>0\] when \(\epsilon\ne0\). Here \(\mathbb E|\nabla\phi'_1|^2=\tfrac12\log M\). Thus the two right-hand sides genuinely differ in the assigned model.

The subsequent estimate bounds each forcing term in norm, so reversing these two signs does not change the resulting Gronwall bound or its order. Correct the signs locally; this is a Minor error in the comparison formula, with no demonstrated false final theorem.

# Fourier phase in the stream-function coupling

Problem 3 (Minor: equation (20)).  On page 6, (20) omits the factor \(i\) required by \(b_L=-J\nabla\psi_L\) and the stated Fourier convention.

Equation reference: phase. For \(k\ne0\), the real stream function satisfies \(\widehat b_L(k)=-iJk\,\widehat\psi_L(k)\). Hence \[
 \widehat\psi_L(k)=
 i|k|^{-2}\mathbf1_{\{|k|\geq L^{-1}\}}\,Jk\cdot\widehat b(k).\] The printed formula lacks \(i\). An odd real Fourier multiplier applied to a real field does not preserve the Hermitian symmetry required of a real stream function; the missing phase is essential.

For an exact mode check, take \(k=(3/5,4/5)\), \(\psi(x)=\cos(k\cdot x)\), and \(b(x)=Jk\sin(k\cdot x)\). Then \(\widehat b(k)=(2i/5,-3i/10)\). The printed multiplier gives \(-i/2\) at both \(k\) and \(-k\), whereas the correct coefficients of \(\psi\) are \(1/2\). Multiplication by \(i\) restores both coefficients. The mode is an operator check, not a claim that a single deterministic wave has the paper’s Gaussian law; the Hermitian-symmetry argument applies to that law itself.

Equation (11), the real Gaussian covariance (13), and the later real-valued energy arguments identify the intended reading. With [phase] (#phase), disjoint Fourier annuli remain independent and all the displayed covariance normalizations are unchanged. The repair is local, so the severity is Minor.

# Rotational invariance and a scalar effective matrix

Problem 4 (Minor: proof of Lemma 3.2).  On page 6, commutation with every planar rotation is used to conclude that the homogenized matrix is a scalar multiple of \(I\). That linear-algebra implication alone is false.

A real \(2\times2\) matrix commuting with all rotations has the form \(\alpha I+\beta J\). For example, \(I+J\) commutes with every rotation and is not scalar. No symmetry of the homogenized matrix has been established by this argument.

The actual field supplies the missing information. For every orthogonal \(Q\), the centered radial Gaussian stream function \[\psi_L^Q(x)=\det(Q)\psi_L(Qx)\] has the same law as \(\psi_L\), and \[Q^\mathsf T a_L(Qx)Q
     =I+\epsilon\psi_L^Q(x)J.\] Covariance of the corrector construction under this change of coordinates implies that the homogenized matrix also commutes with reflections. Taking \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) changes \(\beta J\) to \(-\beta J\), forcing \(\beta=0\). This proves the intended scalar conclusion using an assumption already satisfied by the model.

Counterevidence to any stronger allegation is present in the source: (40), (44) and (67) explicitly use the determinant factor for orthogonal transformations. The later continuum note [Retained latex: \cite[p.~3, (15)]{MOW24}] also distinguishes the scalar-times-rotation consequence of rotation invariance. Insert the reflection step; the lemma’s conclusion and its use in the effective diffusivity are not disproved.

# Initialization and the range of uniform moment bounds

Problem 5 (Minor: initial scale and the constants in (70)).  Page 17, (77), omits the exceptional initial step. Together with the stated constant convention, page 15, (70), also overstates uniformity as \(M\) ranges over all sufficiently large values. The first iteration display on page 14 separately omits the initial constant \(1\).

Let \(M>1\), \(L=M^n\), \(n\geq0\), and \(a=\epsilon^2\log M\). The source starts with \(\widetilde\lambda_1=1\). The last inequality in (77) is \[\frac{a}{1+\epsilon^2\log L}\leq1.\] At \(L=1\) this asserts \(a\leq1\), which is not an assumption. It is valid for \(L\geq M\), or at the initial scale when \(a\leq1\).

Equation reference: base-ratio. The effect can be checked exactly. At the first step, \[\widetilde\lambda_M=1+\frac a2,\qquad
 \widetilde\phi_M=\epsilon\phi'_1,\qquad
 \mathbb E(\phi'_1)^2=\frac{M^2-1}{4}.\] Consequently, for \(\epsilon\ne0\), \[
 \frac{\widetilde\lambda_M^2\mathbb E\widetilde\phi_M^2}
      {\epsilon^2 M^2}
   =\frac14(1+a/2)^2(1-M^{-2}).\] This is unbounded as \(|\epsilon|\to\infty\) at fixed \(M\). More decisively for the earlier convention on page 9, it is also unbounded as \(M\to\infty\) at any fixed nonzero \(\epsilon\). Thus a constant depending only on \(\epsilon\), as defined on page 9, cannot make (70) uniform over every \(M\geq M_0\). The universal-constant convention on page 13 is stronger still. This is not a counterexample to a reading in which one fixes a bounded admissible range of \(M\).

Equation reference: recurrence. For the other initialization error, put \(z_n=\widetilde\lambda_{M^n}^{\,2}\). Recurrence (51) gives \[
 z_{n+1}=z_n+a+\frac{a^2}{4z_n},\qquad
 z_0=1,\qquad
 z_N-(1+Na)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{a^2}{4z_n}.\] The first display on page 14 bounds \(z_N-Na\) by a sum which is empty for \(N=0\), asserting \(1\leq0\). Subtracting \(1\) repairs it. The correctly stated (59) already includes that \(1\), and is supported by [recurrence] (#recurrence). Exact rational checks for four initial steps at \(a=1/100,1/2,2\) agree with this identity.

For the main proofs, \(M\) is selected in a fixed bounded range of sufficiently large constants. Restrict the moment lemma accordingly, treat the first step separately, and allow the declared \(C_\epsilon\) for large \(\epsilon\); for sufficiently small \(\epsilon\) in that fixed range, \(a\leq1\) handles the initial step directly. These repairs address the defective auxiliary statements without changing the claimed asymptotic scale. The issue is Minor under that explicitly restricted use; a uniform bound in the full printed \(M\)-range is false.

# A motivational finite-range assertion

Problem 6 (Minor: introductory Gaussian assertion).  The paragraph following (1) on page 1 asserts uniqueness of white noise among Gaussian processes with a finite range of dependence. With the usual meaning of finite range, that statement is false.

Let \(W\) be scalar spatial white noise and choose a nonzero, nonconstant radial \(K\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\), supported in a ball of radius \(R\). The centered stationary Gaussian field \(G=K*W\) has covariance \(K*\widetilde K\), supported in a ball of radius \(2R\). Values on sets separated by more than \(2R\) are independent, because all cross-covariances vanish and the field is Gaussian. Yet \(G\) is smooth and has a nontrivial continuous covariance, so it is not white noise. Even the vector field \(J\nabla G\) is a smooth, isotropic, divergence-free Gaussian example with finite range and nonzero covariance.

This example concerns the introductory universal assertion; it does not satisfy, and is not presented as a counterexample to, the particular spectrum [cov] (#cov). If finite range was intended to mean independence at every positive separation, that is a different, zero-range statement. Clarify that distinction or remove the uniqueness claim. There is no operative proof dependency on it, so its impact is limited to the motivation.

# Positive checks, reference checks and limits

## Gaussian structure and external hypotheses

The spectral cutoff gives finite moments of every spatial derivative. Its absolutely continuous spectral measure supplies ergodicity; for finite \(L\), the annular Gaussian stream function has all finite moments. The elementary identities \[\mathbb E|b|^2=\frac12,\qquad
 \mathbb E|b-b_L|^2=\frac1{2L^2},\qquad
 \mathbb E|\nabla(b\cdot\xi)|^2=\frac18\] agree with independent angular and radial integration. Disjoint annuli are independent, not merely uncorrelated non-Gaussian objects. The real phase repair in Problem Problem 3 (#p3) is required when constructing their coupling.

The nonsymmetric coefficient satisfies \(\eta\cdot a_L\eta=|\eta|^2\). In the notation of Bella–Fehrman–Otto [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~1--2, condition (1) and Lemma 1]{BFO}], the lower ellipticity variable is \(1\) and the upper variable is \(1+\epsilon^2\psi_L^2\). Choosing, for example, their exponents \(p=q=3\) gives \(1/p+1/q=2/3<2/d=1\) and finite required moments. Their corrector has finite energy. The resulting flux belongs to \(L^{3/2}\), by the \(L^2\) corrector gradient and the \(L^6\) stream function. For Fehrman’s [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~1--2, (1.11); pp.~13--14,
Proposition 2.4]{Fehrman}] construction, take bounded \(A=I\) and the skew Gaussian stream matrix; stationarity, ergodicity, its defining divergence/gauge relations, and \(L^{2+\delta}\) integrability hold. The needed potential construction can therefore use a finite exponent strictly above one. These are hypothesis checks against the acquired preprints, not a claim to have reproved all their results or checked journal-version equality.
