# Definitions, quantifiers, and dependencies

For CUE, the model is Haar measure on \(U(n)\); consecutive eigenangle gaps include the wraparound gap. For GUE, the density has Gaussian factor \(\exp(-n\sum_i\lambda_i^2/2)\), and both endpoints of a counted gap must lie in a fixed nondegenerate interval \(I=[a,b]\), with \(-2<a<b<2\) [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~1--3]{paper}]. Write \[S=\min_{y\in I}\sqrt{4-y^2}>0,\qquad L=\log n,\quad h=\log(2L).\] The order \(k\) and the thresholds \(x_j\) are fixed as \(n\to\infty\). The conclusions do not provide uniformity for growing \(k\), shrinking intervals, or intervals approaching the spectral edge. The separate general-\(\beta\) statement on page 3 is a conjecture.

Equation reference: eq:density. The proof first obtains mixed excess-gap moments, then uses the finite-difference squeeze of Lemma 1 to recover factorial moments of tail counts [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~3--5]{paper}]. The finite differences are nonnegative, so their products preserve the required inequalities. For \(f(x)=e^{c-x}\), the limiting tail-count mean is \(-f'(x)=e^{c-x}\). Differentiating the Poisson tail yields \[
g_k(x)=\frac{e^{k(c-x)}}{(k-1)!}\exp(-e^{c-x}).\] The substitution \(t=e^{c-x}\) gives total integral one, since the resulting integral is \(\Gamma(k)/(k-1)!\). This verifies both the sign and normalization of the stated order-statistic law; \(k=1\) gives the ordinary Gumbel density.

The determinant argument requires finite-rank self-adjoint operators, \(I-B>0\), and \(I-A\geq0\). The compressed projection kernels and positive gap determinants supply these conditions. Disjoint interval supports make \(B\) block diagonal. The CUE splitting calculation uses fixed distinct locations; the GUE lower bound instead uses locations separated by at least \(\varepsilon_0/\log n\), including their separation from both endpoints [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~12--15,27,30--32]{paper}]. Since \(\sqrt{\log n}/n=o(1/\log n)\), the short empty intervals are disjoint and contained in \(I\). In the symmetric case, coincident absolute rescaled coordinates are excluded in Lemma 13; they form a Lebesgue-null set and do not invalidate Fatou’s lemma.

# Independent mathematical checks

## The two logarithmic corrections

The paper defines \(F_n\) and \(G_n\) in equations (7) and (35). Direct squaring, independently checked symbolically, gives the exact identities \[\begin{aligned}
\frac{n^2F_n(x)^2}{32}
 &=L+x+\frac38h+\frac{(8x+3h)^2}{256L},\\
\frac{n^2G_n(x)^2}{32}
 &=L+x-\frac58h+\frac{(8x-5h)^2}{256L}.\end{aligned}\] For fixed \(x\), the last terms vanish. In the uniform gap asymptotic (6), the additional logarithm is \(-\tfrac14\log(n\sin(\alpha/2))=-h/8+o(1)\) when \(\alpha=F_n(x)/2\) or \(G_n(x)/2\). The Taylor remainder \(n^2\alpha^4=O(L^2/n^2)\) vanishes, and \(1/[n\sin(\alpha/2)]=O(L^{-1/2})\). Thus the arguments lie within the stated uniform range \(s_0/n<\alpha<\pi-\varepsilon\), and \[\begin{aligned}
D_n(F_n(x)/2)&=n^{-1}(2L)^{-1/2}e^{c_0-x}(1+o(1)),\\
D_n(G_n(x)/2)&=n^{-1}(2L)^{1/2}e^{c_0-x}(1+o(1)).\end{aligned}\] Replacing \(G_n(x)/2\) by \((1+z/L)G_n(x)/2\), with fixed \(z\), adds \(2z+o(1)\) to the leading negative exponent. This gives the factor \(e^{-2z}\) in Lemma 8 [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~6--7,17--18]{paper}]. The opposite signs of the logarithmic corrections are therefore consistent.

## Endpoint localization and the symmetry factor

Suppose \(a+b<0\), so the least density occurs at \(a<0\). With \(w(y)=\sqrt{4-y^2}/S\), one has \[w(a)=1,\qquad w'(a)=\frac{|a|}{S^2}.\] Writing \(y=a+t/L\), the stretched-gap limit contributes \(\exp(-2|a|t/S^2)\). Consequently the endpoint factor is \[2\int_0^\infty \exp(-2|a|t/S^2)\,dt=\frac{S^2}{|a|}.\] The change of variables and exponential domination in Lemma 10 make this local calculation rigorous: the midpoint cutoff stays strictly negative, so its Jacobian is bounded; the remainder away from the minimizing endpoint is exponentially suppressed in \(L\) [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~19--20]{paper}].

For \(a+b>0\), reflection gives the corresponding factor \(S^2/|b|\). For \(a=-b\), the two endpoints contribute equally, giving \(2S^2/|a|\) [paper, p. 21]. For example, \(I=[-1,0]\) gives \(M(I)=3\), while \(I=[-1,1]\) gives \(M(I)=6\). Since the excess-gap conversion supplies \(S/4\), the intensity constants are \(e^{c_0}3\sqrt3/4\) and \(e^{c_0}3\sqrt3/2\), respectively. This checks the factor of two and the three branches of \(M_0(I)=\log(M(I)S/4)\).

## Determinant comparison and the cutoff scale

For the self-adjoint conjugate \(C=(I-B)^{-1/2}(A-B)(I-B)^{-1/2}\), positivity of \(I-A\) gives \(\lambda_j(C)\leq1\). The scalar inequality \[1\geq e^\lambda(1-\lambda)\geq(1-\lambda^2)_+,\qquad \lambda\leq1,\] and multiplication of nonnegative factors give the lower error \(1-\sum_j\lambda_j(C)^2\). The Hilbert–Schmidt ideal inequality then gives the error in Lemma 6. This derivation does not require \(A\) and \(B\) to commute [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~12--13]{paper}].

As an independent finite-block check, let \[B=\begin{pmatrix}b_1&0\\0&b_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}b_1&\epsilon\\\epsilon&b_2\end{pmatrix}.\] Then \(\operatorname{Tr}((A-B)(I-B)^{-1})=0\) and \[\frac{\det(I-A)}{\det(I-B)}
=1-\frac{\epsilon^2}{(1-b_1)(1-b_2)}.\] With \(b_1=1/2,b_2=1/3,\epsilon=1/10\), both required positivity conditions hold and the ratio is \(97/100\). This corroborates the second-order nature and sign of the splitting error, without replacing the operator proof.

The truncation used in Lemma 12 sets \(D_n(\alpha_n)=(n\log n)^{-1}\) and applies the comparison at \(\delta_n'=\alpha_n/\pi\) [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~24--25]{paper}]. Equation (6), evaluated at fixed multiples of \(\sqrt{L}/n\) and combined with monotonicity, gives \[\delta_n'\sim \frac{\sqrt8}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt L}{n}.\] The cutoff therefore lies on the same admissible scale. A sufficiently small fixed lower constant in the comparison estimate includes it, even when the initially chosen \(c_*\) is larger. This resolves the apparent parameter-range concern in the cutoff step. The GUE lower-bound error estimates also decay as required: powers of \(L\) times \(e^{-\sqrt L/2}\) or \(e^{-\sqrt L}\) dominate the needed \(L^{-2}\) bounds, and the extra-empty-interval error is \(O(n^{-1}\sqrt L)\) for fixed \(k\) [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~30--33]{paper}].
