Abstract: The leading coefficient in the critically tangential asymptotic formula has an incorrect evaluation scale. A direct calculation on a bounded finite-type ellipsoid disproves the printed formula. The associated model-domain identification has the same normal-depth normalization problem. We give a consistent replacement and check that the ensuing model equation and algebraic contradiction can survive it. Two local preliminary statements also need corrections. No counterexample to the ball characterization is established. Scientific decision remains pending.

# The leading coefficient and its verified correction

Problem 1 (Major: the critical tangent vector has the wrong scale).  Theorem 4, (3.7)–(3.8), pp. 8–9, uses the prefactor \((-2\rho)^{-2-(2+w'\cdot\alpha')/r}\) but evaluates the model coefficient at \(z_{1,V}\). For the prescribed path \(\widehat x=\varepsilon z_{1,V}+O(\varepsilon^2)\), \(\rho=-\varepsilon^r\), the corresponding evaluation point is \(2^{-1/r}z_{1,V}\).

Equation reference: eq:scale. The error is already visible in the leading diagonal term of (3.13). Its phase is \(e^{2\rho t}\) and its leading amplitude is \(t^{1+2/r}B_p(t^{1/r}\widehat x)\). Setting \(s=-2\rho t\) gives \[(-2\rho)^{2+2/r}K_D
 \ \sim\ \frac1\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-s}s^{1+2/r}
 B_p\left(s^{1/r}\frac{\widehat x}{(-2\rho)^{1/r}}\right)\,ds.
 \] The quotient in this formula tends to \(2^{-1/r}z_{1,V}\), not \(z_{1,V}\). The failure is not merely an informal concern about manipulating the parametrix: the following exact example tests the statement on a domain satisfying all of its hypotheses.

## A bounded finite-type witness

Equation reference: eq:norm, eq:ellipsoid. For an integer \(m\ge2\) let \[E_m=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb C^2:|z|^{2m}+|w|^2<1\}.\] It is bounded, smoothly bounded, pseudoconvex and of finite type. At \((0,1)\) the type is \(r=2m\). Coordinates whose tangential linear parts are \(\Re z,\Im z\) and whose normal is unit give \(p(z)=|z|^{2m}/2\). Monomial orthogonality and a beta integral yield, for \(a,b\ge0\) and \(q=(a+1)/m\), \[\|z^a w^b\|_{L^2(E_m)}^2
 =\frac{\pi^2}{m}
   \frac{\Gamma(b+1)\Gamma(q)}{\Gamma(b+q+2)}.
 \] Indeed, integrate first over the \(z\) disk of radius \((1-|w|^2)^{1/(2m)}\), then put \(u=|w|^2\) in the remaining radial integral. Summing the reciprocal monomial norms first in \(b\) and then in \(a\) gives the exact diagonal kernel \[K_{E_m}(z,w)=
 \frac{(m+1)-(m-1)u}
 {m\pi^2(1-|w|^2)^{2+1/m}(1-u)^3},
 \qquad
 u=\frac{|z|^2}{(1-|w|^2)^{1/m}}.
 \] The numerator follows from \(\sum_{a\ge0}(a+1)(a+m+1)u^a
=[(m+1)-(m-1)u]/(1-u)^3\).

Along the paper’s path, \(z=\varepsilon V+o(\varepsilon)\) and \[1-|w|^2=\varepsilon^{2m}(2+|V|^{2m})+o(\varepsilon^{2m}).\] To check the factor two, write \(F=|z|^{2m}+|w|^2-1\). On the boundary \(F=0\), and \(U_0F\to2\) near \((0,1)\). Moving normal time \(-\varepsilon^{2m}\) therefore gives \(F=-2\varepsilon^{2m}+o(\varepsilon^{2m})\). This calculation respects the prescribed unit normal; it is not a freely rescaled defining function.

Equation reference: eq:actual. Take \(m=2\) and put \(v=|V|^2\). Formula [eq:ellipsoid] (#eq:ellipsoid) gives \[\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}(-2\rho)^{5/2}K_{E_2}
 =\frac{2^{5/2}(3-u)}
 {2\pi^2(2+v^2)^{5/2}(1-u)^3},
 \qquad u=\frac{v}{\sqrt{2+v^2}}.
 \] Its expansion at \(v=0\) begins \[\frac{3}{2\pi^2}+\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi^2}v+O(v^2).\] For \(p=|z|^4/2\), the monomial model formula of Theorem 14 instead gives \[\widetilde B_{p,0}(V)=
 \frac{3}{2\pi^2}+\frac4{\pi^2}|V|^2+O(|V|^4).\] The coefficients differ. Replacing \(V\) by \(2^{-1/4}V\) makes them agree, as [eq:scale] (#eq:scale) requires. The domain need not have a Kähler–Einstein Bergman metric: Theorem 4 is asserted for every smoothly bounded pseudoconvex finite-type domain. Thus this is a counterexample to its printed coefficient formula, not to the differently hypothesized main Theorem 1.

## The related model-domain identity

Page 11 identifies \(\widetilde B_{p,0}(z)\) with \(K_{D_p}(z,i[1+p(z)])\), where \(D_p=\{\Im w>p(z)\}\). With the stated definitions the correct depth is \(1/2\).

Equation reference: eq:fourier. For clarity, Fourier decomposition in \(\Re w\) and \(\int_{p(z)}^\infty e^{-2ty}\,dy=e^{-2tp(z)}/(2t)\) give \[K_{D_p}(z,iy)
 =\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t\,e^{-2t(y-p(z))}B_{tp}(z,z)\,dt,
 \] where \(B_{tp}\) here denotes the unweighted-space projector kernel for \(\overline\partial+ t\,\overline\partial p\), called \(B_t\) in (4.3). Homogeneity gives \(B_{tp}(z,z)=t^{2/r}B_p(t^{1/r}z)\). Putting \(y=p(z)+1/2\) in [eq:fourier] (#eq:fourier) is exactly the definition of \(\widetilde B_{p,0}\). At \(p=|z|^2/2\), one can also check without a general Fourier argument that \(B_p(z,z)=1/\pi\), so \(\widetilde B_{p,0}=2/\pi^2\). The corresponding Siegel kernel at \((0,i)\) is \(1/(4\pi^2)\), a factor eight smaller; its value at \((0,i/2)\) is \(2/\pi^2\). The discrepancy is a normal-depth normalization, not a convention that leaves the printed identification true.

## Repair and the interface with the main proof

Set \[\eta=\widehat x/(-2\rho)^{1/r}.\] Retain the prefactor in (3.7), but evaluate the differentiated model coefficient in (3.8) at \(\eta\to2^{-1/r}z_{1,V}\). The normal derivatives \((U_0/2)^{\alpha_0}\) contribute \(t^{\alpha_0}\) in the leading phase; the tangential derivatives contribute the corresponding derivatives of the model integral. This supplies the corrected leading coefficient under the same parametrix input.

The commutator term in the determinant must be scaled in the same variable. Since \(\Delta p\) is homogeneous of degree \(r-2\), \[\Delta p(\widehat x)
 =(-2\rho)^{(r-2)/r}\Delta p(\eta).\] Consequently its leading contribution \((\Delta p/2)\widetilde B_{p,1}\) has exactly the same total weight as \(\partial_\eta\partial_{\bar\eta}\widetilde B_{p,0}\). Factoring all entries in (5.1) gives equation (5.2) with every entry evaluated at \(\eta\), including \(\Delta p(\eta)\). Because \(2^{-1/r}z_{1,V}\) ranges over all of \(\mathbb C\) as \(V\) varies, the universal model identity (5.2) retains its printed form after renaming this variable. No new hypothesis on \(V\) is required.

This is a verified repair of the leading-scale mismatch and its determinant interface. Its Major severity concerns a false stated asymptotic theorem used in the central derivation; an explicit local correction does not erase that failure. The correction does not refute the ball characterization, and it does not independently reconstruct every remainder estimate in the imported all-orders parametrix.

# Two local preliminary corrections

Problem 2 (Minor: the Bergman kernel is not globally square integrable).  Page 3 places \(K_D(z,w)\) in \(L^2(D\times D)\). A bounded domain has an infinite-dimensional Bergman space, so its orthogonal projector is not Hilbert–Schmidt.

If \((e_j)\) is an orthonormal basis, the reproducing identity and Tonelli’s theorem give \[\int_D\int_D|K_D(z,w)|^2\,dw\,dz
 =\int_D K_D(z,z)\,dz
 =\sum_j\|e_j\|_{L^2(D)}^2=\infty.\] The polynomials already give infinitely many independent square-integrable holomorphic functions on a bounded domain. The correct local statement is \(K_D\in C^\infty(D\times D)
\subset L^2_{\rm loc}(D\times D)\), with its usual distributional Schwartz-kernel interpretation. The projector remains a bounded operator. This correction does not alter the later interior derivatives or boundary asymptotics.

Problem 3 (Minor: the general-dimensional frame formula omits a sign).  Equation (2.4) defines \(J(u)\) with the factor \((-1)^n\). The frame determinant in (2.5), p. 4, omits that factor.

In the coordinate frame take \(u=1-\sum_{j=1}^n|z_j|^2\). The block determinant is \((-1)^n(u+\sum_j|z_j|^2)=(-1)^n\), while \(J(u)=1\). For odd \(n\), including \(n=3\), (2.5) is therefore false. Insert \((-1)^n\) in that formula, or explicitly restrict it to the even-dimensional case being used. The main proof has \(n=2\), so its (5.1) has the correct sign.

# Independent checks of the surviving proof

The proof of Proposition 3 uses localization at a strongly pseudoconvex boundary point, the limiting Ricci constant \(-1\), and pluriharmonicity of \(\log B\). The maximum principle on attached analytic disks makes \(B\) constant on an interior open set; real analyticity and connectedness then extend the constant. The argument is coherent with its stated localization, disk-filling and boundary-asymptotic inputs.

The scaling identity (4.4) follows by a change of variables in the weighted holomorphic space. At points where \(\Delta p>0\), Proposition 6 imports the positive-curvature expansion. The passage from its scalar expansion to differentiated coefficients and the restriction to that open set were checked. The full external derivation of the coefficient \(b_3\) was not independently repeated here.

Equation reference: eq:pole. The algebraic argument after that input can be verified directly. Write \(q=p_{z\bar z}\) and \(Q=q q_{z\bar z}-q_zq_{\bar z}\). If the complexification of \(q\) has a nonzero linear root \(z+a\zeta\) of multiplicity \(k\ge1\), with leading coefficient \(\lambda\ne0\), then \[Q\sim (z+a\zeta)^{2k-2}(-ak\lambda^2).\] Applying the derivative, multiplication and division rules of Lemmas 12–13 to (4.9) gives its leading coefficient \[-\frac{a^3k(k+2)}{\lambda^3}
 \bigl[4k^3+32k^2+79k+60\bigr].
 \] It is nonzero. The bracket equals the three-term bracket printed on p. 16; every coefficient is positive. Thus \(b_3=0\) excludes every such nonzero root. Hermitian homogeneity then forces \(q=c_0(z\bar z)^{r/2-1}\), with \(c_0>0\) by subharmonicity and non-harmonicity. The constant-\(q\) case gives \(r=2\) directly. The rational identity is first valid where \(q>0\) and then extends after clearing denominators; no division at a zero of the original real polynomial is being assumed.

The logarithmic comparison on p. 19 is also decisive. Each entry of the \(3\times3\) determinant has at most first powers of \(\log t\) in its polyhomogeneous expansion, so the determinant has log degree at most three. On the right, the first possible \((\log t)^4\) term has coefficient proportional to \(b_3^4\). No higher-order logarithmic term can contribute at that same first order. Uniqueness of the expansions forces \(b_3=0\) where \(\Delta p>0\). The radial model then follows from Theorem 8 and the absence of pure holomorphic terms in \(p\).

For \(p=c|z|^r/2\), the monomial integral on the plane produces the orthonormal basis and series in Theorem 14. Its decomposition into the \(r/2\) residue classes and the incomplete-gamma differential equation have the stated solution with zero initial condition. The values and mixed derivatives at zero used on p. 20 also follow directly from the first two terms of this series, without requiring off-diagonal branch choices.

For \(r>2\), \(\Delta p(0)=0\). Substitution into (5.2) and the gamma recurrence give \[(1+4/r)(2+2/r)=\frac94(1+2/r)^2 .\] The difference of the two sides is exactly \[-\frac{(r-2)^2}{4r^2}.\] Thus \(r=2\) is the only positive solution, contradicting \(r>2\). The strongly pseudoconvex characterization then gives the ball, conditional on the cited Fu–Wong and Nemirovski–Shafikov results. The finite-type reduction for a bounded real-analytic boundary is likewise an external background input, not a new calculation in this review.
