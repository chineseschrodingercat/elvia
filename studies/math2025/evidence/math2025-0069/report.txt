Abstract: One Major finding is established in arXiv:1408.0546v2. Theorem 6.3, and hence the stated generality of Theorem 6.2, fails for an admissible class of groups because the listed closure assumptions do not control central quotients. An explicit countable torsion-free peripheral group yields an abelian subgroup of its partial-conjugation group that violates the claimed alternative. The classical virtually solvable case, the RAAG application, and the 2025 journal version are not refuted by this example. Other inspected proof transitions and a countability clarification are recorded with their limits.

# Hypotheses and the dependency under review

The relative Tits alternative for a class \(\mathcal C\) means that every subgroup either belongs to \(\mathcal C\) or contains a rank-two free subgroup. Infinitely generated subgroups are expressly included (Definitions 0.1–0.2, pp. 1–2). The factors may be countable and infinitely generated; only their number and the rank of the free part are finite (p. 4).

For \(\mathcal F=\{[G_1],\ldots,[G_k]\}\), the group \(\operatorname{Out}(G,\mathcal F^{(t)})\) consists of outer automorphisms acting as a conjugation on each peripheral factor. Theorem 6.3, p. 24, assumes that each \(G_i\) satisfies the relative alternative and that \(\mathcal C\) contains \(\mathbb Z\) and is closed under isomorphisms, subgroups, extensions and finite-index supergroups. It concludes the same alternative for \(\operatorname{Out}(G,\mathcal F^{(t)})\). Theorem 6.2, p. 23, adds permitted restriction groups \(A_i\leq\operatorname{Out}(G_i)\); taking all \(A_i=1\) recovers Theorem 6.3. Theorem 6.1 additionally assumes the relative alternative for the full \(\operatorname{Out}(G_i)\).

The proof of Theorem 6.3 starts on p. 24 by declaring the sporadic cases to follow from Propositions 2.1–2.2. Proposition 2.1 on p. 14 identifies one such group as \[\operatorname{Out}(G_1*G_2,\{[G_1],[G_2]\}^{(t)})
\cong G_1/Z(G_1)\times G_2/Z(G_2).\] Central quotients also enter Theorem 3.2, p. 14, and the use of Corollary 3.3 in the general-\(\mathcal C\) induction on p. 25. Closure under extensions alone does not justify those quotient steps.

# Problem F01: missing control of central quotients

## Judgment and exact claim

Major; an explicit counterexample to Theorem 6.3 and to Theorem 6.2 with trivial \(A_i\). The decisive locations are Theorems 6.2–6.3, pp. 23–24, the sporadic-case sentence in Section 6.2, p. 24, and Proposition 2.1, p. 14. The failure concerns an expressly stated relative generalization central to the inductive proof. It does not require refuting the usual virtually solvable alternative.

## An admissible class: verification of every closure hypothesis

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the class of groups whose finite-order elements have bounded orders: \[\mathcal C=\{B:\ \exists M_B\in\mathbb N\ \forall b\in B,\
        \operatorname{ord}(b)<\infty\Rightarrow \operatorname{ord}(b)\leq M_B\}.\] The bound depends on the group. This class contains every torsion-free group, including \(\mathbb Z\), and every finite group. It is invariant under isomorphism and closed under subgroups.

For an extension \(1\to N\to E\to Q\to1\) with bounds \(M_N,M_Q\), let \(g\in E\) have finite order \(m\). Its image has order \(d\leq M_Q\); then \(g^d\in N\) has order \(m/d\leq M_N\). Thus \(m\leq M_NM_Q\), proving closure under extensions.

If \(B\leq E\) has finite index \(r\) and \(B\in\mathcal C\), the cyclic subgroup generated by any finite-order \(g\in E\) has intersection with \(B\) of index at most \(r\). Consequently \(\operatorname{ord}(g)\leq rM_B\). This proves closure under finite-index supergroups, without requiring \(B\) to be normal. Every printed class hypothesis holds.

## A countable torsion-free factor with a bad central quotient

For each integer \(n\geq2\), let \(\sigma_n\) cyclically permute the \(n\) standard basis vectors of \(\mathbb Z^n\), and define \[E_n=\mathbb Z^n\rtimes_{\sigma_n}\mathbb Z.\] Write elements as \((v,k)\), with multiplication \[(v,k)(w,\ell)=(v+\sigma_n^k w,\ k+\ell),\qquad t_n=(0,1).\] If \((v,k)\) has finite order \(m\), its second coordinate gives \(mk=0\), so \(k=0\), and then \(mv=0\) gives \(v=0\). Thus \(E_n\) is torsion-free.

Commuting with every \((w,0)\) requires \(\sigma_n^k=1\), equivalently \(n\mid k\). Commuting with \(t_n\) requires \(\sigma_n v=v\), equivalently \(v=c(1,\ldots,1)\). These conditions also suffice, so \[Z(E_n)=\{(c(1,\ldots,1),\,n\ell):c,\ell\in\mathbb Z\}.\] It follows that \(t_nZ(E_n)\) has exact order \(n\).

Now set \(A=\bigoplus_{n\geq2}E_n\), the restricted direct product. It is countable, since each element has finite support, and torsion-free. Every subgroup of \(A\) is torsion-free and hence belongs to \(\mathcal C\); therefore \(A\) satisfies the Tits alternative relative to \(\mathcal C\). Its centre is \(\bigoplus_{n\geq2}Z(E_n)\), and \(A/Z(A)\) contains \[L=\bigoplus_{n\geq2}\langle t_n Z(E_n)\rangle
\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq2}\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.\] The group \(L\) is abelian, so it contains no rank-two free subgroup, but it has elements of order \(n\) for every \(n\geq2\), so \(L\notin\mathcal C\). Therefore \(A/Z(A)\) does not satisfy the relative alternative.

## Placing the obstruction inside the claimed automorphism group

Take \[G=A*(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z),\qquad \mathcal F=\{[A],[\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z]\}.\] This is a countable group with two nontrivial peripheral factors and a trivial, hence finitely generated, free part. The factor \(A\) satisfies the relative alternative as just proved, and every subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) is finite and belongs to \(\mathcal C\). These are all the peripheral hypotheses of Theorem 6.3. For Theorem 6.2 choose \(A_1=A_2=\{1\}\); these restriction groups also satisfy the relative alternative.

By Proposition 2.1, the resulting group \(\operatorname{Out}(G,\mathcal F^{(t)})\) is isomorphic to \(A/Z(A)\) and therefore contains \(L\). The conclusion fails. In particular this is an actual bad subgroup, not merely a failure of an attempted proof of closure.

The required embedding can also be checked without relying on the cited proposition. For \(a\in A\), let \(\alpha_a\) fix \(A\) pointwise and conjugate the second free factor by \(a^{-1}\). Then \(\alpha_a\alpha_b=\alpha_{ab}\), so \(a\mapsto[\alpha_a]\) is a homomorphism into \(\operatorname{Out}(G,\mathcal F^{(t)})\). If \(\alpha_a\) is inner, its conjugating element centralizes \(A\), and thus lies in \(Z(A)\) by free-factor malnormality. Comparing its action on the nontrivial second factor shows that this element equals \(a^{-1}\): the discrepancy lies both in \(A\) and in the centralizer of the second factor, which is that factor itself. Hence \(a\in Z(A)\). Conversely, for \(a\in Z(A)\), \(\alpha_a\) is inner conjugation by \(a^{-1}\). The kernel is exactly \(Z(A)\), giving the asserted embedding of \(A/Z(A)\).

## Counterevidence and precise impact

The obstruction \(L\) is infinitely generated. Every finitely generated subgroup of \(L\) is finite and belongs to \(\mathcal C\). Thus replacing the paper’s all-subgroups convention by a finitely generated-subgroups convention would change the theorem and would remove this particular witness. The manuscript explicitly insists on the stronger convention, so that reading is unavailable.

The factors are allowed to be infinitely generated; the construction uses only two factors, not infinitely many factors in the free-product decomposition. The infinite direct sum is internal to the single allowed factor \(A\). The class \(\mathcal C\) need not exclude free groups under the definition: its required closure properties, not disjointness from the free-group class, are the stated assumptions.

This counterexample establishes failure of Theorems 6.3 and 6.2 in their general-\(\mathcal C\) scope. It identifies a false input in the stated general induction towards Theorem 6.1. It does not verify the additional hypothesis on \(\operatorname{Out}(A)\) required to refute Theorem 6.1 directly. For the classical virtually solvable or virtually polycyclic classes, central quotients stay in the class. Indeed each \(E_n\) is metabelian, and so are \(A\) and \(A/Z(A)\); there is no contradiction to the ordinary virtually solvable alternative here. No counterexample to the RAAG application or the virtually polycyclic relatively hyperbolic application is established.

## A justified repair condition and its boundary

Add the requirement that \(\mathcal C\) be closed under quotients by arbitrary central subgroups. This repairs the demonstrated closure inference: if \(B\) satisfies the relative alternative and \(Z\leq Z(B)\), consider any \(J\leq B/Z\) and its preimage \(\widetilde J\leq B\). If \(\widetilde J\in\mathcal C\), central-quotient closure gives \(J\in\mathcal C\). Otherwise \(\widetilde J\) contains a free group \(F_2\). Its intersection with \(Z\) is central in \(F_2\), hence trivial; its image in \(J\) is again \(F_2\). Thus \(B/Z\) satisfies the relative alternative.

The printed assumptions do not imply this additional condition, as the example proves. This is a verified local repair of the quotient step, not a complete new proof of every theorem under strengthened hypotheses. The tree-stabilizer input, its finite-product interpretation, and the rest of the induction still need independent assessment if a fully corrected general theorem is claimed.

# Other checked reasoning and scope clarifications

The Kurosh identities on p. 9 give \[K'=K-K_H+1,\qquad N'=N-N_H\] for the quotient-side free-factor system. If \(K_H\geq2\), then \(K'<K\); if \(K_H=1\) for a proper nonperipheral factor, then \(N_H=1\) and the free rank decreases. The factor-side complexity is also smaller. The lexicographic decrease is sound; an original finite arithmetic check covered 646 admissible cases with \(0\leq k,N\leq6\). This is corroboration of the formula, not a proof of the geometric inputs.

For the surface-tree step on pp. 15–16, the finite-generation hypothesis in Proposition 4.2 can be supplied as follows. Intersect the finitely generated orbifold group with a conjugate of a relative free factor. Kurosh decomposition for its action on that free splitting makes the nontrivial intersection a free factor of the orbifold group, hence a retract and finitely generated. Its use in the subsequent finite-index argument is therefore not disproved merely because peripheral groups can be infinitely generated.

The positive-mass construction in Corollary 5.7, pp. 22–23, should not be read as assigning positive mass to every element of an arbitrary uncountable group. For the main use, \(\operatorname{Out}(G,\mathcal F^{(t)})\) is countable: a representative is determined by one conjugating element for each of the finitely many peripheral factors and the images of a finite free basis, all in the countable group \(G\). Thus its subgroups admit the stated discrete random walk.

More generally a countable reduction supplies the orbit-limit argument. There are countably many proper relative free-factor conjugacy classes, since each is specified by finitely many elements and conjugates of the finitely many fixed factors. If \(H\) has no finite orbit on this set, choose countably many elements witnessing infinitely many images of each class, and let \(H_0\) be the subgroup they generate. Then \(H_0\) has no finite orbit on any nonempty finite collection of such classes. The proof of Proposition 5.6 uses precisely this condition; it does not use the boundary-orbit part of nonelementarity. Apply its maximum-principle argument and the Cesaro construction to \(H_0\). The resulting arational limit point is also a limit point of the \(H\)-orbit. This is a proof clarification, not a separate theorem-refuting finding.

The boundary identification, systems-of-isometries theorem, finite-dimensional length-measure simplex, Guirardel–Levitt tree-stabilizer input, and JSJ/application structure theorems were not independently reproved. In particular manuscript reference [27] is an in-preparation work and its proof was not supplied; this limits whole-proof verification, but F01 is independent of that source.
