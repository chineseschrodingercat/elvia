# Definitions and main proof chain

The killed motion is \[dX_t=-dt+dW_t,\qquad X_t>0,\qquad \tau=\inf\{t:X_t=0\},\] with generator \(L=\frac12\partial_x^2-\partial_x\). Its minimal quasi-stationary distribution and killing rate are \[\pi_{\min}(dx)=xe^{-x}\,dx,\qquad \lambda_{\min}=\frac12.\] Here minimal refers to the survival time; its killing rate is the largest among the source’s family of QSD rates.

For the \(N\)-particle system, each boundary hit replaces the dying particle by a uniformly chosen one of the other \(N-1\) particles. Write \(K_t^N\) for the number of deaths, \(J_t^N=K_t^N/N\) for the number per particle, and \(m_t^N\) for the empirical measure. Theorem 1.4 gives the ordinary-time stationary distribution \(\psi^N\) and the limiting per-particle death rate \(\lambda_N\). The proof of selection uses \(N\geq12\), for which the required first moment is finite. It does not claim this threshold is necessary for finiteness at smaller \(N\).

The proof passes to the post-jump chain with stationary distribution \(\phi^N\), whose mean empirical measure is \(\varpi^N\). If \(\xi^N\) is the ordinary-time mean empirical measure, Proposition 2.4 gives \[\xi^N=\lambda_N\varpi^NG.\] This yields tightness of the jump-time measures at infinity. The boundary estimate then prevents mass at zero. A hydrodynamic limit identifies subsequential jump-time limits as invariant measures of the survival-time flow. A mean return-time inequality and the domain of attraction identify the only possible limit as \(\delta_{\pi_{\min}}\). Finally the Green identity transfers tightness to ordinary time, and exponential moments identify the ordinary-time empirical limit.

# A corrected normalization in Proposition 2.3

Problem 1 (The displayed jump-chain identity has the wrong mass).  In the proof of Proposition 2.3, p. 10, after the correctly normalized definition \[\phi^N=\frac1{N\lambda_Nt}
 \mathbb E_{\psi^N}\!\sum_{0<\tau_n^N\leq t}\delta_{Y_n},\] the next display uses \(\lambda_Nt\,\phi^NQ\) on the left and a sum starting at \(n=0\) on the right, with \(\tau_0^N=0\). Its two sides cannot have the same total mass.

Fix any \(N\geq12\), for example \(N=12\), start in the actual stationary distribution \(\psi^N\), and take a positive integer \(t\). These are precisely the hypotheses in the proof. Stationarity and the integrable jump count give \[\mathbb E_{\psi^N}K_t^N=N\lambda_Nt,\qquad 0<\lambda_N<\infty.\] Testing the printed measure identity against the constant function \(1\) gives \[\lambda_Nt
 \quad\text{on the left, whereas the right side equals}\quad
 N\lambda_Nt+1.\] The difference is \((N-1)\lambda_Nt+1>0\). Testing a finite-measure identity against \(1\) does not require \(1\) to belong to the killed generator’s domain. This is an exact contradiction for the stipulated stationary process, without an estimated value of \(\lambda_N\) or a particle simulation.

## Full repair under the same assumptions

Use the definition of \(\phi^N\), apply \(Q\), and condition at each jump: \[\begin{split}
 N\lambda_Nt\,\phi^NQ
 &=\mathbb E_{\psi^N}\sum_{n\geq1}
       1_{\{\tau_n^N\leq t\}}Q(Y_n,\cdot)\\
 &=\mathbb E_{\psi^N}\sum_{n\geq1}
       1_{\{\tau_n^N\leq t\}}\delta_{Y_{n+1}}.
 \end{split}\] For each path with \(K=K_t^N\geq1\), \[\sum_{n=1}^K(\delta_{Y_{n+1}}-\delta_{Y_n})
 =\delta_{Y_{K+1}}-\delta_{Y_1},\] and for \(K=0\) the difference is zero. The total variation norm is at most \(2\) in either case. Consequently \[\|\phi^NQ-\phi^N\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\frac2{N\lambda_Nt}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(t\to\infty).\] Time stationarity makes the normalized \(\phi^N\) independent of \(t\). Thus \(\phi^N\) is stationary exactly as required. The jump-chain uniqueness argument uses the original ordinary-time ergodicity and the jump-count ergodic theorem; this display correction does not change that argument.

The finding is Minor: the local displayed equality is false, but the full stationarity step is proved above with the original normalization and hypotheses. In particular, the \(\lambda_N\) in Proposition 2.4 is already the correct per-particle rate; replacing it by \(N\lambda_N\) there would create a new error. No author adoption of the correction is asserted.

# Independent checks of the central identities

## The post-jump martingale is correctly normalized

Equation (2.27), pp. 11–12, subtracts \(N^{-1}\sum_{\tau_n\leq t}m_{\tau_n}^N(\varphi)\), using the post-jump empirical measure. This initially delicate choice is correct.

At a death, put \(S=\sum_{j\ne i}\varphi(X^j_{\tau-})\) and let \(B\) be the value at the uniformly chosen parent. Since \(\varphi(0)=0\), \[m_{\tau-}^N(\varphi)=\frac SN,\qquad
 m_\tau^N(\varphi)=\frac{S+B}N,\qquad
 \mathbb E[B\mid\text{pre-jump information}]=\frac S{N-1}.\] The corrected martingale’s jump is therefore \[\frac BN-\frac{S+B}{N^2}
 =\frac{(N-1)B-S}{N^2},\] whose conditional expectation is zero. This cumulative post-jump term is not itself a predictable compensator, but the difference has the required martingale property. Localization and the nonnegative-function integrability argument on pp. 11–12 supply the passage from the local to the true martingale.

Stationarity and the Palm definition give \[\xi^N(L\varphi)=-\lambda_N\varpi^N(\varphi).\] For nonnegative \(f\in C_0(0,\infty)\), the finite-time potential \(G_tf=\int_0^tP_sf\,ds\) is in the generator domain and satisfies \(LG_tf=P_tf-f\). Letting \(t\to\infty\) yields the asserted Green identity; \(P_tf(x)\to0\) and bounded convergence justify its vanishing term. Increasing compactly supported tests then recover total mass. There is no missing factor \(N\) in this identity.

## An explicit Green kernel

For this one-dimensional killed diffusion, \[g(x,y)=\bigl(e^{2\min(x,y)}-1\bigr)e^{-2y}
 =\begin{cases}
  1-e^{-2y},&y\leq x,\\
  (e^{2x}-1)e^{-2y},&y>x.
 \end{cases}\] The kernel has the killed boundary value and the derivative jump giving \(L_xg=-\delta_y\). Direct integration gives \[G1(x)=x,\qquad
 \int_0^\infty xe^{-x}g(x,y)\,dx=2ye^{-y}.\] In particular, \[\lambda_{\min}\pi_{\min}G=\pi_{\min},\qquad
 \lambda_N\int x\,\varpi^N(dx)=1.\] The lower rate bound from Lemma 2.1 therefore really controls mass at infinity. For \(f\in C_c(0,\infty)\), \(0\leq g(x,y)\leq1\) gives \(|Gf(x)|\leq\int|f(y)|\,dy\), and dominated convergence gives continuity. When \(x\) exceeds the support of \(f\), \(Gf(x)\) is constant. Thus \(G(C_c)\subset C_b\), as used on p. 15; it generally is not a subset of \(C_0\), and the proof does not need that stronger assertion.

## The rate and attraction interfaces

The discounted empirical-measure identity in (2.19)–(2.21) uses \[\iota_N=-N\log(1-1/N),\qquad
 \mathbb E e^{-\iota_NJ_t^N}=\mathbb P_{m_0^N}(\tau>t).\] For deterministic initial configurations, \(m_0^N\) has compact support. Its survival rate is \(1/2\), so Markov’s inequality and Borel–Cantelli give \(\lambda_N\geq(2\iota_N)^{-1}\); the monotonicity of \(J_t^N\) extends the integer-time conclusion to all times. Integrating over initial conditions is legitimate because the pointwise result holds for every deterministic configuration.

Appendix A’s necessary implication can be checked from the hitting-time transform \[\mathbb E_xe^{z\tau}
 =\exp\!\bigl((1-\sqrt{1-2z})x\bigr),\qquad 0<z<\frac12.\] If survival decays with upper exponential rate \(-1/2\), this transform is integrable for every such \(z\), giving all moments \(\int e^{ux}\mu(dx)<\infty\) for \(u<1\). The equivalence with the upper tail exponent follows by exponential Markov bounds in one direction and tail integration in the other. The converse attraction theorem is the external result [3] used by the source; its full proof was not independently reproduced here. The sign must be \(\limsup t^{-1}\log\mathbb P(\tau>t)\leq-1/2\), consistent with the source’s Appendix A. Actual inspection of p. 2 confirms that the final numerical equality in (1.5) prints \(-\lambda_{\min}=1/2\), missing the minus sign before \(1/2\). Appendix A on p. 16 explicitly uses \(\exp((-1/2+\varepsilon)t)\) and therefore fixes the intended criterion. This is a local sign correction; it does not alter the attraction or selection argument.

# The invariant-measure selection step

Put \(H_\mu(t)=-\log\mathbb P_\mu(\tau>t)\) and \(T_y(\mu)=\inf\{t:H_\mu(t)>y\}\). The survival function is continuous and strictly decreasing to zero; hence the inverse relation and the cocycle \[T_{y+z}(\mu)=T_y(\mu)+T_z(\vartheta_y\mu)\] are valid despite the strict inequality in the definition. The continuous hydrodynamic limit has this strictly increasing clock, so inversion at each fixed \(y\) is legitimate. The discrete inverse uses \(\tau^N_{\lfloor Ny+1\rfloor}\); the extra jump gives the factor \((N+1)/(N\lambda_N)\) in the expectation of \(T_1^N\).

Proposition 2.8 proves integrability of a stationary inter-jump time by first applying Birkhoff to its bounded truncations. It then obtains \(\mathbb E_{\phi^N}\tau_1^N=(N\lambda_N)^{-1}\). Fatou’s lemma only needs convergence in distribution after a Skorokhod representation; no unproved uniform integrability is used. For a subsequential limit \(\Upsilon\) this gives \[\mathbb E_\Upsilon T_1
 \leq\frac1{\limsup\lambda_N}\leq2.\]

This is an average constraint, not a pointwise bound. For the \(\vartheta_1\)-invariant measure \(\Upsilon\), Birkhoff gives \[T_n/n\longrightarrow a(\mu)=
 \mathbb E_\Upsilon[T_1\mid\mathcal C](\mu).\] Let \(F=\{a\leq2\}\), an invariant measurable set. For \(\mu\in F\) the attraction criterion gives \(\vartheta_n\mu\to\pi_{\min}\). Applying bounded continuous test functions to the invariant finite measure \(\Upsilon|_F\) proves \[\Upsilon|_F=\Upsilon(F)\delta_{\pi_{\min}}.\] Therefore \(\int_Fa\,d\Upsilon=2\Upsilon(F)\). On \(F^c\), \(a>2\) almost everywhere. If \(\Upsilon(F^c)>0\), then \[\mathbb E_\Upsilon T_1
 =2\Upsilon(F)+\int_{F^c}a\,d\Upsilon>2,\] a contradiction. This proves \(\Upsilon=\delta_{\pi_{\min}}\) and rules out \(\limsup\lambda_N>1/2\).

On p. 15 the intermediate complement display can be kept as an inequality; its equality is a consequence of the completed argument, not an assumption. The inequality already suffices for the contradiction. This is not a failure of the invariant-set reasoning. For the final ordinary-time limit, the mean measure is \(\pi_{\min}\). Taking a countable increasing sequence \(u_j\uparrow1\) makes the simultaneous almost-everywhere exponential-moment assertion on p. 16 precise. Almost every initial measure is then attracted to \(\pi_{\min}\), and invariance forces the ordinary-time limit to be the same point mass.

# Boundary estimates, dependencies, and limitations

The comparison in Appendix B uses independent reflected drift-\(-1\) Brownian motions \(Y^i\), started at zero, with \(X^{N,i}\geq Y^i\). For a group of four, let \(r=|y|\) and \(f(y)=1/r\). An independent calculation gives \[Lf=r^{-3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^4y_i-\frac12\right)
 \leq \frac2{r^2}-\frac1{2r^3}\leq\frac{128}{27}.\] The maximum of the last expression occurs at \(r=3/8\). At a reflecting face \(y_i=0\), \(\partial_if=0\) away from the origin. The bounded density at time \(1\) makes \(\mathbb Ef(Y_1)<\infty\); the source’s displayed occurrence of \(Y_0\) in that sentence must be read at time \(1\), as its following actual estimate already does.

On \([1,2]\), localization of \(f(Y_t)+(128/27)(2-t)\) gives a nonnegative supermartingale. If all four coordinates are at most \(2^{-k}\), then \(f\geq2^{k-1}\). The maximal inequality therefore gives \(C2^{-k}\), and a positive-probability path remaining away from zero provides a second uniform bound \(q<1\). Independence of \(r_N=\lfloor N/4\rfloor\) groups yields \[\mathbb P(\text{all particles approach the boundary})
 \leq [C2^{-k}\wedge q]^{r_N}.\] The death-count summation has tail ratio \(2^{2-r_N}\), so \(r_N>2\) is sufficient, exactly \(N\geq12\). The \(L^p\) variant requires \(p<r_N/2\). These calculations do not establish divergence for smaller \(N\). The second-order-statistic grouping and the exponential large-deviation bound in Appendix B.2 were also traced.

The fixed-\(N\) recurrence argument in Appendix B.1 was read through its ancestral bound, Gaussian maximum estimate, drift inequality, and minorization. The main selection argument avoids a uniform-in-\(N\) Lyapunov function; the fixed-\(N\) ergodicity appendix does use one. This distinction is retained when reading the introductory description.

The source’s Appendix C was compared with Theorem 2.10 and Conditions 2.3–2.4 in the publicly available Tough–Nolen preprint [4]. The half-line has the required smooth boundary and uniform interior balls, and the constant drift is bounded, continuous, and Lipschitz in its measure argument with Lipschitz constant zero. Their hydrodynamic theorem permits unbounded domains and tight random initial measures. This checks the applicability interface; it does not constitute a full reproof of that 80-page preprint or verification of equality with its 72-page published version. The 1998 attraction paper’s public publisher page exposed metadata and abstract, not its full proof. PDE uniqueness, non-explosion, Harris theory, and the classical regularity results remain cited dependencies whose full original proofs were not recreated.
