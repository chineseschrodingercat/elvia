Abstract: This initial review of the acquired arXiv version identifies an invalid rounding argument in the proof of Proposition 4.13, a false lattice-normalized Bessel asymptotic in Lemma 3.4, and an inadmissible endpoint in Lemma 3.7. Explicit counterexamples and replacement arguments are supplied. The first two defects occur in derivations supporting the main lower- and upper-tail results; the proposed local repairs preserve the needed intermediate conclusions. No counterexample to either final large-deviation theorem is established. Coverage is claim-focused, with substantial unverified parts recorded below. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and operative assumptions

Equation reference: eq:rate. Throughout, \(0<q<1\), \(\eta_q=\log(q^{-1})>0\), time \(t\) is real and positive, and the growth process has droplet initial condition. For the principal theorems the nucleation intensity is \(2(1-q)\). Theorem 1.2, pages 3–4, concerns upper deviations \(\mu\ge2\), with speed \(t\) and rate \[
 \Phi_+(u)=2u\mathop{\mathrm{arccosh}}(u/2)-2\sqrt{u^2-4},\qquad u\ge2.\] Theorem 1.3, page 4, concerns \(0\le\mu\le2\), with speed \(t^2\), a convex lower-tail rate \(\Phi_-\), and \(\Phi_-(0)=1-q\). These are distinct claims from the intermediate assertions tested here.

Equation reference: eq:weight, eq:T. A partition is finite, padded with zeros as needed. For a skew partition \(\lambda/\rho\), \(\rho\subseteq\lambda\), write \(s_{\lambda/\rho}(\gamma)\) for its exponential Schur specialization. Definition 2.4 and (2.11), pages 16–17, give \[
 P_t(\lambda/\rho)=K_t q^{|\rho|}s_{\lambda/\rho}(\gamma)^2,\quad
 \gamma=t(1-q),\quad K_t=e^{-t^2(1-q)}(q;q)_\infty.\] Here \(s_{\lambda/\rho}(\gamma)=\gamma^{|\lambda/\rho|}f^{\lambda/\rho}/|\lambda/\rho|!\), with \(f\) the number of standard tableaux. All these weights are positive. Equation (4.35), page 48, defines \[
 \mathcal T_t(a)=-t^{-2}\log\sup_{\rho\subseteq\lambda,\ \lambda_1\le at}P_t(\lambda/\rho)
 \quad(a\ge0),\qquad \mathcal T_t(a)=+\infty\quad(a<0).\] Keeping \(K_t\) explicitly avoids any change of probability normalization.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Uncontrolled accumulated rounding in a core midpoint argument).  The proof of Proposition 4.13 uses a false bound on the total size change under coordinatewise rounding. Severity: Major, for an invalid core derivation; this is not a finding that Proposition 4.13 or Theorem 1.3 is false.

## Original claim, location, and hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:mid. Proposition 4.13, pages 49–50, claims that for every \(a,a'\in\mathbb R\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), for all sufficiently large \(t\), \[
 \mathcal T_t\left(\frac{a+a'}2+\frac1t\right)
 \le\frac{\mathcal T_t(a)+\mathcal T_t(a')}2+\varepsilon.\] Its proof considers arbitrary admissible \(\lambda/\mu,\nu/\rho\) with \(\lambda_1\le at\), \(\nu_1\le a't\). It chooses the ceiling or floor averaged skew shape with larger unweighted Schur square and uses \[\Delta=|\text{chosen averaged inner partition}|-\frac{|\mu|+|\rho|}{2}\le1.\] The operation is coordinatewise, as expressly stipulated in Proposition 4.12. The displayed logarithmic bound on page 50 additionally has \(+t^{-2}\log q^\Delta\); converting the preceding probability inequality requires the opposite sign.

## Decisive evidence

Take \(q=1/2\), \(t=2\), \(\gamma=1\), \(a=0\), \(a'=1\), and \[\lambda=\mu=\varnothing,\qquad \nu=(2,2,2),\qquad \rho=(1,1,1).\] Every partition inclusion and first-row restriction holds. Both rounded outer partitions equal \((1,1,1)\). The ceiling inner partition is \((1,1,1)\), whereas the floor inner partition is empty. Thus the ceiling skew shape is empty and has Schur value \(1\); the floor shape is a column of length three and has Schur value \(1/6\). The ceiling is the unique maximizer required by the proof, and \[\Delta=3-\frac32=\frac32>1.\] Selecting the floor shape is therefore not a valid reinterpretation of that selection rule.

The problem is not confined to small time. For any sufficiently large \(t\ge2\), retain \(q=1/2,a=0,a'=1\), set \(m=\lceil t^3\rceil\), and take \(\nu=(2^m),\rho=(1^m),\lambda=\mu=\varnothing\). The ceiling value is \(1\); the floor value is \((t/2)^m/m!<1\), since \[(t/2)^m/m!\le \left(\frac{et}{2m}\right)^m<1.\] All first-row constraints still hold, but \(\Delta=m/2\), so \(\Delta/t^2\) is not a uniform vanishing error for the arbitrary inputs used in the proof. These configurations are not asserted to maximize the supremum in [eq:T] (#eq:T); they disprove the intermediate estimate, not [eq:mid] (#eq:mid).

For the finite example, using the actual normalized weights, the chosen configuration’s negative logarithm exceeds the incorrectly signed displayed per-configuration upper bound by \[\frac14\log(4/3)>0.\] This independent exact calculation confirms that the sign is consequential at the stated intermediate inequality.

## A proved replacement and counterevidence

Equation reference: eq:product. The same Schur inequality used by the authors provides a direct repair if both rounded shapes are retained. Denote them by \(A_+\) and \(A_-\). The coordinatewise floor and ceiling preserve partition order and inner containment. Proposition 4.12, or Theorem 11 in the inspected preprint of Lam–Postnikov–Pylyavskyy [LPP], yields \[s_{A_+}(\gamma)s_{A_-}(\gamma)
 \ge s_{\lambda/\mu}(\gamma)s_{\nu/\rho}(\gamma).\] Schur positivity implies this numerical inequality because the exponential specialization is nonnegative. Moreover, the two rounded inner sizes add exactly to \(|\mu|+|\rho|\). Equation [eq:weight] (#eq:weight) therefore gives \[
 P_t(A_+)P_t(A_-)\ge P_t(\lambda/\mu)P_t(\nu/\rho).\] Both averaged outer first rows are at most \((a+a')t/2+1\). If \(S_t(b)\) is the supremum in [eq:T] (#eq:T), then \[S_t\left(\frac{a+a'}2+\frac1t\right)^2
 \ge P_t(A_+)P_t(A_-)
 \ge P_t(\lambda/\mu)P_t(\nu/\rho).\] Taking independent suprema, or positive near-maximizers, proves [eq:mid] (#eq:mid) with \(\varepsilon=0\), for every \(t>0\) and \(a,a'\ge0\). If an input is negative the extended-real right-hand side is infinite and the original claim is automatic. No maximum-attainment assumption or bound on \(\Delta\) is needed.

## Dependency and impact boundary

Theorem 4.15 and Proposition 4.16 on pages 52–53 invoke Proposition 4.13; the proof of Theorem 1.3 on page 54 invokes it again to establish the convexity hypothesis for the deconvolution argument. This is an explicit dependency of a principal result, which supports Major severity for the printed derivation. The proof above repairs this particular input and is strong counterevidence against declaring the final theorem false. Problem 2 (A lattice phase omitted from the normalized Bessel limit).  Equation (3.14) in Lemma 3.4, page 24, is false for its real-time limit. Severity: Major for a false assertion used in the core trace derivation, with a verified local repair and no demonstrated failure of the final upper-tail rate.

## Claim and decisive analytic check

Equation reference: eq:falseb. For fixed \(u>2\), the displayed assertion is \[
 2\pi t\sqrt{u^2-4}\,J_{\lfloor ut\rfloor}(2t)^2e^{t\Phi_+(u)}
 \longrightarrow1\qquad(t\longrightarrow\infty).\] Here \(J_n\) is the Bessel function of the first kind, and the source imposes no integer-time or integer-\(ut\) restriction.

Equation reference: eq:correctb. Put \(n=\lfloor ut\rfloor\) and \(r=n/t\). A direct saddle calculation gives, uniformly for \(r\) in compact subsets of \((2,\infty)\), \[
 J_n(2t)^2=
 \frac{e^{-t\Phi_+(r)}}{2\pi t\sqrt{r^2-4}}(1+o(1)).\] For completeness, start from the generating-function coefficient integral \[J_n(2t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint e^{t(z-z^{-1})}z^{-n-1}\,dz.\] Take \(z=e^{\alpha+i\theta}\), with \(\alpha=\mathop{\mathrm{arccosh}}(r/2)>0\). The exponent becomes \[2t\sinh\alpha\cos\theta-n\alpha
       +in(\sin\theta-\theta).\] Its real part has a unique maximum at \(\theta=0\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\). On \(|\theta|\le t^{-2/5}\), the quadratic term is \(-t\sinh\alpha\,\theta^2\), with uniformly vanishing cubic-phase and quartic-real errors. Outside this interval, the absolute integral is exponentially smaller than the saddle contribution, uniformly when \(\alpha\) stays in a compact positive interval. Gaussian integration gives \[J_n(2t)=
 \frac{\exp(2t\sinh\alpha-n\alpha)}
      {\sqrt{4\pi t\sinh\alpha}}(1+o(1)),\] which proves [eq:correctb] (#eq:correctb). This derivation does not use the disputed lemma.

Equation reference: eq:phase. Since \(\Phi_+'(u)=2\mathop{\mathrm{arccosh}}(u/2)\), Taylor expansion shows that the left-hand side of [eq:falseb] (#eq:falseb) equals \[
 \exp\!\big(\Phi_+'(u)\{ut\}\big)(1+o(1)).\] At \(u=3\), along \(t_n=n/3\) its limit is \(1\); along \(s_n=(n+1/2)/3\) it is \[e^{\Phi_+'(3)/2}=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}>1.\] Both sequences satisfy every operative hypothesis. Thus the asserted limit does not exist.

## Alternative interpretation, repair, and dependency

Restricting the limit to \(ut\in\mathbb Z\) would remove this counterexample, but no such restriction appears and time is continuous in the model. Merely dropping a bounded phase is legitimate at logarithmic speed \(t\), not in a normalized limit equal to one.

The proof of Proposition 3.2 invokes (3.14) in (3.30), pages 29–30, with \(d=\lfloor2t\cosh(p/2)\rfloor/t\) and \(p>0\). Formula [eq:correctb] (#eq:correctb) supplies a valid lower bound of the same required order. Writing \(u_*=2\cosh(p/2)\), one has \[pdt-t\Phi_+(u_*)\ge t\big(pu_*-\Phi_+(u_*)\big)-p
 =4t\sinh(p/2)-p.\] This is the lower estimate needed for the ensuing exponential lower bound. The nearby printed upper inequality does not by itself justify a lower bound. The corrected Bessel formula and this elementary lower estimate restore this local passage. The lattice factor in [eq:phase] (#eq:phase) is bounded between fixed positive constants, so it does not alter the rate \(4\sinh(p/2)\). The remainder of the Fredholm analysis is outside the fully verified scope; the final upper-tail theorem is not refuted.

Problem 3 (The endpoint \(v=0\) makes the double sum infinite).  Lemma 3.7, page 25, includes \(v\ge0\), although its asserted finite bound fails at zero. Severity: Minor; no use of the erroneous endpoint was identified in the inspected principal argument.

## Statement, assumptions, and counterexample

The lemma assumes \(r>0\), \(2+r<\gamma_1<\gamma_2\le\infty\), \(v\ge0\), and \(t\ge1\), and bounds \[\sum_{a\in\mathbb N'}\ 
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z':\ a+\ell\in[\gamma_1t,\gamma_2t]}
 J_{a+\ell}(2t)^2 e^{v\ell}\] by a finite constant times an exponential involving \(U_v\). Page 20 defines \(\mathbb N'=\{1/2,3/2,\ldots\}\) and \(\mathbb Z'=\mathbb Z+1/2\). Take \[r=\tfrac12,\quad \gamma_1=3,\quad\gamma_2=4,\quad v=0,\quad t=1.\] For every \(j\ge0\), the admissible pair \(a=j+1/2\), \(\ell=5/2-j\) has \(a+\ell=3\). Consequently the sum contains infinitely many copies of \(J_3(2)^2\). The alternating series gives \[J_3(2)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{m!(m+3)!}
 >\frac16-\frac1{24}=\frac18.\] Terms decrease strictly in magnitude, so the displayed strict lower bound is rigorous. The sum diverges, with its first \(N\) selected terms exceeding \(N/64\). An independent numerical evaluation gives \(J_3(2)=0.128943249474402\ldots\).

## Counterevidence and bounded repair

The preceding definition of \(U_v\) is for \(v>0\), and the lemma’s proof uses the factor \((1-e^{-v})^{-1}\). These facts support an intended positive-\(v\) interpretation, but cannot make the explicit endpoint correct. Replacing \(v\ge0\) by \(v>0\) removes the demonstrated defect. Inspected applications use \(p>0\), \(M>1\) (hence \(v=\log M>0\)), or positive multiples of \(\eta_q\); see pages 28–30. The present finding does not independently certify the full positive-\(v\) estimate and does not show damage to a final theorem.

# Assessment and limitations

The highest supported severity is Major, grounded in the explicit dependency of the first two defective derivations. This classification is separate from rejection, acceptance, or whole-paper falsity. Problem Problem 1 (#pr:round) has a complete replacement proof of the needed midpoint inequality. Problem Problem 2 (#pr:bessel) has a complete corrected local asymptotic and restores the exponential lower estimate at the inspected use. Problem Problem 3 (#pr:zero) has a precise domain correction. None of these statements amounts to verification of every theorem in the paper.

The upper rate also passes the elementary Legendre check: \(\sup_{p\ge0}\{p\mu-4\sinh(p/2)\}\) has optimizer \(p=2\mathop{\mathrm{arccosh}}(\mu/2)\) for \(\mu\ge2\), giving [eq:rate] (#eq:rate). This is a consistency check, not a proof of the probabilistic limit. The lower-tail endpoint and deconvolution hypotheses were traced through pages 48–54 and 59–61, but the entire variational construction and all external convex-analytic inputs remain unverified.
