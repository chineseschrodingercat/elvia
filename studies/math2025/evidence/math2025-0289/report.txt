# Scope and assessment

This review concerns the exact author manuscript identified above, not an assumed equivalent publisher text. The frozen PDF has SHA256

The normal publisher download did not supply the version of record. The pinned arXiv source was subsequently acquired and the disputed formulas were checked in its TeX as well as in the PDF.

Outcome after focused independent verification: Major. Lemma B.5 has an incorrect variational constant, and the resulting abstract pointwise estimate (3.6) is false under its stated hypotheses. Section 3 below gives both the correct variational argument and a full analytic counterexample to (3.6). The defect propagates to quantitative coefficients in several principal statements. Six further findings are described in Section 4. P4 and P5 also invalidate steps explicitly used in the central logarithmic-resolvent proof and are classified as Major. P2, P3, P6 and P7 remain Minor for the bounded reasons given with each finding.

The counterexample is an abstract finite-dimensional contraction semigroup. It is not a counterexample to the geometric PDE statement of Theorem 5.1. The qualitative logarithmic lower bound remains accessible by the paper’s method after changing the optimization constant and making the local corrections below. Nor is the vector-valued averaged estimate in Section 6 refuted. This revised report incorporates focused independent AI verification of the reported Problems and their proof dependencies. Scientific adjudication remains pending; no human review or author adoption is claimed.

# Coverage, hypotheses and proof structure

In the preserved initial review, all 53 pages were read as extracted text, including the appendices and bibliography. The source-page images recorded as inspected in that review were 1, 3–5, 9–14, 18–20, 23, 27, 31–36, 38–39, 41, 45–46 and 48–50. The review distinguishes text reading, actual image viewing, independent calculation, and imported results. The initial review also checked the source TeX at the variational lemma, Theorem 3.1, the smoothing construction, the diagonal damping and the transformed initial condition. The focused independent verification revisited P1–P7 and their relevant source passages, including the principal-symbol convention on p. 43; it does not claim a second exhaustive review.

The geometric setting is a smooth compact connected manifold without boundary, with nonpositive Laplacian, damping \(b\geq0\), and the mass parameter treated separately for \(m>0\) and \(m=0\). For \(m>0\), the energy is a norm; for \(m=0\), the displacement must be taken modulo constants while the velocity remains unrestricted. The first-order formulation uses \(\Lambda_m=\sqrt{-\Delta+m}\) and \[\mathsf P_m=\Lambda_m\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)
       +\frac{ib}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.\] The conjugations, damping sign, energy factors, and the distinct roles of generators, resolvents and semigroups were checked. The zero-mode formulas require the repairs in P2.

Section 3 starts with a selfadjoint \(P_h\) on an \(h\)-independent domain, a bounded accretive \(Q_h\), and \[\mathsf P_h=P_h+ihQ_h,\quad
 \Pi_\varepsilon=\chi((P_h-1)/\varepsilon),\quad
 \left\|(\mathsf P_h-\tau)^{-1}\right\|\leq G(h)/h.\] The normalization of the semiclassical Fourier transform, contraction estimate, division by \(T_h\), and the off-window remainder were traced. The unoptimized time-average estimate (3.4) and the ratio in (3.5) are consistent with this argument. The subsequent optimization is not. The higher-order off-window parametrix in Section 3.2 explicitly assumes a smooth time cutoff; its use for the stated \(H^1\) weights needs the clarification in Section 5.

The logarithmic propagation argument has the strict loss condition \[d=\mu\Upsilon_1+\max(\rho,\nu)<\tfrac12.\] Its Egorov remainder is \(O(th^{1-2d})\), and coherent-state evaluation costs \(O(h^{1/2-d})\). Smoothing contributes \(O(t\omega(\kappa h^\nu))\), which tends to zero at \(t=\mu\log(1/h)\) under the stated modulus condition. In the resolvent comparison, choosing \(\widetilde\delta=h^{(1-2\rho)/4}\) makes \[\widetilde\delta^{-2}h^{1-2\rho}\log(1/h)
 =h^{(1-2\rho)/2}\log(1/h)\longrightarrow0.\] Thus the strict restrictions on \(\rho,\nu,\mu\), rather than endpoint equality, are essential. The symbolic calculus and imported functional-calculus results were read as dependencies; they were not all independently reproved.

Section 6 is logically separate from the erroneous optimization. Its weighted resolvent hypotheses, accretivity, vector-valued Plancherel argument, dyadic summability conditions and damping regularity assumptions were traced. The incorrect operator-norm restatement P6 is distinct from the valid vector inequality used in Theorem 1.5.

# P1 — Major: the optimization constant and an abstract counterexample

## The exact variational value

Lemma B.5, pp. 49–50, claims the infimum \(2\) for \[I=\inf\left\{\int_\mathbb R|\psi'(t)|^2\,\mathrm dt:
 \psi\in H^1_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathbb R),\
 \operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[0,\infty),\
 \int_0^1|\psi(t)|^2\,\mathrm dt=1\right\}.\] In fact, \[I=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.\] For the lower bound, \(\psi(0)=0\). Reflect its restriction to \([0,1]\) about \(1\) to obtain \(v\in H^1_0(0,2)\). The sine-series Dirichlet inequality on \((0,2)\) gives \[2\int_0^1|\psi'|^2
 =\int_0^2|v'|^2
 \geq\frac{\pi^2}{4}\int_0^2|v|^2
 =\frac{\pi^2}{2}.\] The energy outside \([0,1]\) is nonnegative. For the upper bound, take \(L>1\) and \[\psi_L(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  \sqrt2\sin(\pi t/2),&0\leq t\leq1,\\
  \sqrt2(L-t)/(L-1),&1<t\leq L,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}\] Then \(\int_0^1\psi_L^2=1\) and \(\int_\mathbb R|\psi_L'|^2=\pi^2/4+2/(L-1)\). These admissible \(H^1\) functions establish the infimum; smoothing and a small translation, followed by normalization, give the same limiting ratio if smooth cutoffs away from zero are needed. The infimum is not attained by a compactly supported function: equality on \([0,1]\) forces a nonzero value at \(1\), while equality in the total energy would require zero tail energy.

The paper’s proposed first segment \(\sqrt2\,t\) has squared \(L^2(0,1)\) norm \(2/3\), not \(1\). Its linear tail also gives energy \(2/(L-1)\), not the printed tail contribution. The problem is therefore not a harmless choice of normalization in an otherwise sharp variational proof.

## A counterexample to Theorem 3.1, equation (3.6)

The following construction verifies every hypothesis of the abstract theorem. It uses a finite-dimensional space of fixed, large dimension; no matrix of that dimension was allocated or numerically simulated.

Let \(n=100000000\), \(\mathcal H=\mathbb C^n\), and let \(J\) be the right shift: \(Je_j=e_{j+1}\) for \(j<n\), \(Je_n=0\). Put \[A=J-I,\qquad D=\frac{A-A^*}{2i},\qquad
 Q=-\frac{A+A^*}{2}.\] Since \(\left\|J\right\|\leq1\), \(Q\geq0\) and \(\left\|Q\right\|\leq2\). Also \(D=D^*\) and \(iD-Q=A\). Set \[P_h=I+hD,\qquad \mathsf P_h=P_h+ihQ=I-ihA.\] The domain is the entire fixed space \(\mathcal H\). All real numbers belong to \(\rho(\mathsf P_h)\), since its only eigenvalue is \(1+ih\), and \[e^{it\mathsf P_h/h}=e^{it/h}e^{tA}\] is a contraction. Choose \(\varepsilon=1/2\) and an allowed \(\chi\) which equals \(1\) near \(0\). For all sufficiently small \(h\), the functional-calculus cutoff \(\Pi_\varepsilon\) is exactly \(I\).

The exact uniform resolvent constant for \(A\) is \[G:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb R}\left\|(is-A)^{-1}\right\|
 =\left\|(I-J)^{-1}\right\|
 =\frac1{2\sin(\pi/(4n+2))}.\] Here the finite expansion \((is-A)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+is)^{-k-1}J^k\) has entrywise absolute value bounded by the nonnegative matrix \((I-J)^{-1}\). This implies the operator-norm upper bound, with equality at \(s=0\). For completeness, \(B=I-J\) has \(B^*B\) tridiagonal, with diagonal \(2\) except for the final entry \(1\), and off-diagonal \(-1\). The eigenvector recurrence has \(v_j=\sin(j\theta)\), \(v_0=0\), \(v_{n+1}=v_n\); its smallest eigenvalue corresponds to \(\theta=\pi/(2n+1)\) and is \(4\sin^2(\pi/(4n+2))\). This proves the displayed formula for every \(n\). Scaling gives \(\left\|(\mathsf P_h-\tau)^{-1}\right\|\leq G/h\) for every real \(\tau\). Thus \(G(h)=G\) satisfies (3.3).

Take \[T=95000000,\qquad a=100000,\qquad m=4000000,\qquad
 u=m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_j.\] The exponential series can be written \[e^{TA}u=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-T}\frac{T^k}{k!}J^ku.\] If \(K\) is a Poisson variable of mean and variance \(T\), then \(\Pr(|K-T|\leq a)\geq1-T/a^2=1981/2000\), by the elementary second-moment bound. For each integer \[T+a+1\leq j\leq T-a+m,\] the \(j\)-th coordinate contains every term with \(|k-T|\leq a\). These \(m-2a\) coordinates all lie within \(\{1,\dots,n\}\). Consequently \[\left\|e^{TA}u\right\|^2
 \geq \frac{m-2a}{m}\left(\frac{1981}{2000}\right)^2
 =\frac{74562859}{80000000}
 =0.9320357375.\]

For \(\delta=1/100\), the leading right-hand side in (3.6) is strictly smaller: \[(2+\delta)\frac{G^2}{T^2}<0.903545.\] This inequality was checked with exact rational bounds, not floating-point evidence alone. Specifically, put \(z=(157/50)/(4n+2)\). Since \(\pi>157/50\) and \(\sin x\geq x-x^3/6\) in this small positive range, \[(2+\delta)\frac{G^2}{T^2}
 \leq\frac{201}{400T^2(z-z^3/6)^2}
 =0.903544499442533318\ldots .\] The exact rational difference from \(74562859/80000000\) is positive; the recorded arithmetic gives a gap exceeding \(0.0284912\). Finally, the remainder asserted in (3.6), \(C_QhT/(\delta^2\varepsilon^2)\), tends to zero as \(h\to0\), with every other quantity fixed and \(\left\|Q\right\|\leq2\). It cannot cover the fixed positive gap. This contradicts (3.6) under its actual quantifiers.

## Propagation and repair

The error enters the optimization on p. 23, the final assertion of Theorem 3.3, Theorem 1.6 (1.10), Theorem 3.7 (3.34), and the use of (5.6) in deriving (5.15)–(5.16). The ratio in (3.5) supports a coefficient arbitrarily close to \(\pi^2/4\), with the same kind of cutoff-dependent remainder. The long tail of \(\psi_L\), with \(L-1\) of order \(\delta^{-1}\), has \(L^1\) norm of order \(\delta^{-1}\), so the \(\delta^{-2}\) remainder order can be retained after changing its constant.

With this repair, the method yields a logarithmic resolvent coefficient approaching \(2\mu/\pi\), rather than \(\mu/\sqrt2\). For positive \(\Upsilon_{\max}\), the corresponding leading coefficient in the first part of Corollary 5.2 becomes \(1/(\pi\Upsilon_{\max})\), rather than \(1/(2\sqrt2\,\Upsilon_{\max})\). These are statements about what the corrected proof supplies. No PDE counterexample to the stronger coefficients has been established here. A separate argument might prove them, but the displayed one does not. Theorem 1.1’s qualitative logarithmic growth is not negated by changing a positive constant. The failure of a central abstract estimate and its quantitative consequences nevertheless warrants Major.

# Further findings and repairs

## P2 — Minor: zero-mode reconstruction and generators

Corollary 2.5(6), p. 13, identifies the reduced state with \((u,u_t)\) while requiring \(u\) to remain mean-free. Take \(b=1\), a normalized constant function \(e_0\), and reduced initial data \((0,e_0)\). The reduced semigroup gives \((0,e^{-t}e_0)\), but the actual wave displacement is \((1-e^{-t})e_0\), which is not mean-free. The correct reduced state is \((\Pi_+u,u_t)\). The displacement is reconstructed by integrating its constant-mode velocity, as in Lemma 2.8, p. 14.

The additive kernel projectors in the generator identities (2.20) are also incorrect. Apply the first identity to \((e_0,0)\): the actual generator gives zero, while the printed right-hand side gives \((e_0,0)\). The analogous test uses the kernel vector \((e_0,-e_0)\) for \(\mathsf P\). Remove these additive projectors from the generator identities. They do belong, with the appropriate scalar factors, to the resolvent and semigroup identities. A displayed undamped upper-right identity on a mean-free first component must likewise be read as the projection \(\Pi_+\). The reconstructed solution and energy equivalence in Lemma 2.8 explain why these corrections do not invalidate the intended massless high-frequency argument.

## P3 — Minor: the transformed initial condition

In the proof of Proposition 2.11, pp. 18–19, \(W=(I-K)V\), with \(K=O(h)\), is followed by the unsupported assumption \(W(0)=V(0)=V_0\). In fact, \[W(0)=(I-K)V_0.\] The Duhamel formula must therefore contain \[W(t)=e^{it\mathsf P_{\rm diag}}V_0
       -e^{it\mathsf P_{\rm diag}}KV_0
       +i\int_0^t e^{i(t-s)\mathsf P_{\rm diag}}R e^{is\mathsf P_{\rm cut}}V_0\,\mathrm ds .\] The omitted term is bounded by \(Ch\,e^{Cth^{1-2\nu}}\left\|V_0\right\|\), which is already allowed by the proposition’s final estimate. This is a repairable proof defect, not a counterexample to that estimate.

## P4 — Major: loss of the half-damping factor

In Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 5.1, \(\mathsf P^\nu\) contains \(ib_{h^\nu}/2\) in every damping-matrix entry. In Step 2, (5.9) and the definition of \(\mathsf P_{\rm hw}\), p. 34, its diagonal damping has become \(ib_{h^\nu}\). The resulting comparison is false. The following argument first identifies the discrepancy and then realizes it under the theorem’s geometric assumptions.

For constant damping \(b>0\), consider \[P_\lambda=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
              +\frac{ib}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad M_\lambda=P_\lambda-\frac{ib}{2}I .\] Direct multiplication gives \(M_\lambda^2=(\lambda^2-b^2/4)I\). For \(\beta_\lambda=\sqrt{\lambda^2-b^2/4}\), \[e^{itP_\lambda}
 =e^{-bt/2}\left(\cos(\beta_\lambda t)I+
              i\frac{\sin(\beta_\lambda t)}{\beta_\lambda}M_\lambda\right).\] For fixed \(b,t>0\), its norm on \(e_1\) tends to \(e^{-bt/2}\); the printed diagonal evolution has norm \(e^{-bt}\). Constant positive damping alone would satisfy GCC, so this calculation by itself is only a test of the general diagonalization formula.

To obtain an example satisfying the non-GCC assumption, let \(M=(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^2\) have its flat metric, put \(m=1\), and choose a fixed smooth \(b=b(y)\in[0,1]\) equal to \(0\) near \(y=0\) and to \(1\) near \(y=\pi\). The horizontal geodesic \(y=0\) never meets the damping region. A Lipschitz modulus satisfies the required logarithmic condition, and the flat flow has \(\Upsilon_{\max}=0\); take \(\rho=0\), \(\mu=1\) and \(\nu=1/4\). Choose \(f\in C^\infty(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)\) of unit \(L^2\) norm with support strictly inside the plateau \(b=1\), and set \[v_k=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{ikx}f(y),\qquad
 \lambda_k=\sqrt{k^2+1},\qquad h_k=\lambda_k^{-1},\qquad U_k=(v_k,0).\] For all sufficiently large \(k\), the regularization (A.10) satisfies \(b_{h_k^\nu}f=f\): every point sampled by the mollifiers on \(\operatorname{supp}f\) lies in the same constant plateau. Fourier expansion in \(y\) gives the explicit residual estimate \[\left\|(\Lambda_1-\lambda_k)v_k\right\|_{L^2}
 \leq\frac{\left\|f''\right\|_{L^2}}{2\lambda_k},\] since \(\sqrt{\lambda_k^2+\ell^2}-\lambda_k\leq\ell^2/(2\lambda_k)\). Smoothness of \(f\) also gives \(\chi_0(h_k\Lambda_1)v_k=v_k+O(k^{-N})\) for every \(N\), and the same fact holds for \(\widetilde\chi_0\).

The contractive Duhamel estimate applied to these residuals therefore yields, for each fixed \(t>0\), \[e^{it\mathsf P^\nu}U_k
   =v_k\otimes(e^{itP_{\lambda_k}}e_1)+O_{L^2}(k^{-1}),\qquad
 e^{it\mathsf P_{\rm diag}^{\rm printed}}U_k
   =e^{it\lambda_k-t}U_k+O_{L^2}(k^{-1}),\] where the constant matrix in the first expression has \(b=1\). Consequently the norm of the difference, also after the displayed \(\chi_0\) cutoff, is bounded below by \(e^{-t/2}-e^{-t}+o(1)>0\). The asserted bound \(C(th_k^{1-2\nu}+h_k)\) tends to zero. Fixed \(t\) lies in \(0\leq t\leq\mu\log(1/h_k)\) for large \(k\). Thus the discrepancy occurs with all the geometric, regularity, time and localization requirements retained.

This comparison is the link from the actual wave evolution to (5.10), the scalar Egorov formula (5.11), and the coherent-state estimate (5.14). Its demonstrated failure is a central proof error and supports Major. Restore \(b_{h^\nu}/2\) in the diagonal and scalar operators. The squared-norm transport factor then becomes \(\exp(-\int_0^t b_{h^\nu}\circ\varphi^{-s}\,\mathrm ds)\). It equals \(1\) on an undamped trajectory for a zero-preserving regularization, so the corrected comparison preserves the intended lower-bound strategy. No failure of the qualitative logarithmic conclusion is inferred from this comparison error, and this repair does not repair P1.

## P5 — Major: the stated regularization does not preserve zeros

Proposition A.5(2), p. 45, asserts a property of the specific function defined by (A.10): it vanishes throughout the metric \(\varepsilon\)-neighborhood of \(\{b=0\}\). The proof checks that the pre-convolution function vanishes at the evaluation point and then treats this as vanishing of its convolution. This inference is false, even at a point of the original zero set.

Take a circle with arclength coordinate \(x\) near \(0\), set \(b(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,0)\), and choose the valid modulus \(\omega(r)=r\). Use an admissible chart \(y=x/4\), with partition weight \(1\) near \(0\) and auxiliary cutoff \(1\) on a larger neighborhood. Choose an even, nonnegative, normalized smooth mollifier \(N\) supported where \(1/2<|z|<3/4\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), every point in the convolution at \(0\) satisfies \[b(4\varepsilon z)=4\varepsilon|z|>2\varepsilon,\qquad
 \chi\bigl(b(4\varepsilon z)/\omega(\varepsilon)\bigr)=0.\] All coordinate and partition cutoffs are \(1\) on this support. Hence (A.10) gives the exact expression \[b_\varepsilon(0)=4\varepsilon\int |z|N(z)\,\mathrm dz,\qquad
              2\varepsilon<b_\varepsilon(0)<3\varepsilon ,\] although \(b(0)=0\). The atlas in Appendix A.1 has no metric-normalization restriction that excludes this chart. This refutes Proposition A.5(2) for its specified construction. It does not refute the existence of a different regularization satisfying Corollary A.6.

The asserted zero preservation is used explicitly through Corollary A.6 in (5.12), and then to replace the transport weight by \(1\) in the core coherent-state calculation (5.13). The provided construction fails to supply that stated premise. This invalid derivation in the main proof supports Major, independently of the availability of a repair.

For an explicit repair, choose a uniform constant \(L\) controlling distances under the inverse charts on the relevant compact neighborhoods, enlarge it for the finitely many remaining scales if needed, and take \(C\geq1+L\). Replace the cutoff denominator in (A.10) by \(\omega(C\varepsilon)\). If \(\operatorname{dist}(x,\{b=0\})\leq\varepsilon\), each sampled point is at distance at most \(C\varepsilon\) from that zero set. Its \(b\)-value is at most \(\omega(C\varepsilon)\), so the whole convolution integrand vanishes. Derivatives still fall on the mollifier, giving the same \(O(\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|})\) bounds; the cutoff and convolution errors are bounded by a constant times \(\omega(\kappa'\varepsilon)\). If the denominator vanishes at a relevant positive scale, connectedness and the modulus inequality force \(b\) to be constant, in which case take \(b_\varepsilon=b\). These choices preserve the logarithmic smallness assumption.

There is also useful counterevidence against claiming that the logarithmic conclusion itself fails: the approximation estimate alone gives \(0\leq b_{h^\nu}\leq C\omega(\kappa h^\nu)\) along an undamped trajectory. Therefore its transport factor is \(1+O(t\omega(\kappa h^\nu))\), which tends to \(1\) at the allowed logarithmic times. This supplies an alternative bounded repair of the final asymptotic; it does not make the exact zero assertion or its original proof valid.

## P6 — Minor: supremum and time integration

The line introduced as another formulation of Lemma 6.3, p. 38, uses \(L^2(\mathbb R;\mathcal L(\mathcal H))\). The lemma instead controls the norm of a map \(\mathcal H\to L^2(\mathbb R;\mathcal H)\). These norms cannot be interchanged.

Here is a counterexample satisfying the lemma’s hypotheses. Let \[\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{n\geq1}\mathbb C^{N_n},\qquad
 N_n=2^n,\qquad q_n=2^{-n}.\] In block \(n\), with \(0\leq j<N_n\), define \[P_0e_{n,j}=(10n+jq_n)e_{n,j},\quad
 B_n=q_n I,\quad P=P_0+iB,\quad
 C_nu=q_n\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N_n-1}u_j\Big)e_{n,0}.\] The real diagonal operator \(P_0\) is selfadjoint on its natural domain, \(B\) is bounded nonnegative, and \(e^{itP}\) is a contraction. All real \(\tau\) belong to \(\rho(P)\): each finite block has positive imaginary part, and the real parts of the blocks escape to infinity. Also \(\left\|C_n\right\|=\sqrt{q_n}\to0\), so \(C\) is bounded and compact.

For every real \(\tau\) and every \(\alpha\geq0\), \[\left\|C_n(P_n-\tau+i\alpha)^{-1}\right\|^2
 =\sum_{j=0}^{N_n-1}
 \frac{q_n^2}{(10n+jq_n-\tau)^2+(q_n+\alpha)^2}
 \leq1+\pi^2 .\] To see the uniform bound, divide by \(q_n^2\), extend the finite sum to the integer lattice, bound the closest lattice point by \(1\), and the two points at each subsequent distance by \(2(k-\tfrac12)^{-2}\). Their sum is \(1+\pi^2\). Thus (6.5) holds with \(C_0=\sqrt{1+\pi^2}\).

On the other hand, \[\left\|C_ne^{itP_n}\right\|^2=q_n e^{-2q_nt}.\] For \(2^n\leq t\leq2^{n+1}\), this is at least \(2^{-n}e^{-4}\). Each of the disjoint intervals contributes at least \(e^{-4}\) to \(\int\left\|Ce^{itP}\right\|^2\,\mathrm dt\), so that integral diverges. Take \(\psi(t)=\min(t,1)\) for \(t\geq0\), and \(0\) for \(t<0\). It satisfies the stated \(H^1_{\rm loc}\) and support conditions, with \(\left\|\psi'\right\|_2=1\). The printed operator-valued left-hand side is infinite and its right-hand side is finite. Compactness of \(C\) also ensures norm continuity here; the issue is not measurability.

The valid formulation places the norm in \(\mathcal L(\mathcal H,L^2(\mathbb R;\mathcal H))\). Equation (6.6) and its vector-valued Plancherel proof use that correct order, as does the application to Theorem 1.5. Accordingly this isolated false reformulation is classified as Minor.

## P7 — Minor: the time scale in Lemma B.1

Lemma B.1, p. 48, defines \(P_Q=P+iQ\) but prints an estimate for \(e^{itP_Q/h}\) with exponent \(C_0th^{1-2\nu}\). The principal-symbol convention matters here: Appendix A.4 takes the order-zero symbol modulo \(h^{1-2\nu}S_\nu^{-1}\).

An admissible counterexample is obtained on \(M=\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z\), with \(N=1\), \(\nu=0\), \(P=0\) on all of \(L^2(M)\), and \[Q_h=-h\chi(hD_x),\qquad D_x=-i\partial_x,\qquad
 \chi\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R;[0,1]),\quad \chi(0)=1 .\] The Fourier multiplier \(\chi(hD_x)\) is semiclassically smoothing, so \(Q_h\in h\Psi^{-\infty}\subset h\Psi^{-1}\). Its order-zero principal symbol is therefore exactly the zero class under the paper’s definition and admits the nonnegative representative \(0\). The optional stronger assumption \(\operatorname{Re}Q_h\geq0\) is not imposed in the first assertion of the lemma and is not claimed here. For the normalized constant function \(e_0\), \[P_Qe_0=-ih e_0,\qquad
 \left\|e^{itP_Q/h}e_0\right\|=e^t,\] which exceeds \(e^{C_0ht}\) for every fixed \(t>0\) and all sufficiently small \(h\), regardless of the proposed finite \(C_0\). The unscaled evolution on the same mode is \(e^{ht}\), as required by the intended estimate.

The corrected general statement is \(\left\|e^{itP_Q}\right\|\leq e^{C_0th^{1-2\nu}}\); for the scaled evolution its exponent is \(C_0th^{-2\nu}\). With the paper’s sign convention, \(iP_Q-C_0h^{1-2\nu}I\) is dissipative and generates a contraction. In Section 4.2, p. 30, the scaled operator is explicitly \(P_Q=h\widetilde P_Q\), where \(\widetilde P_Q=\Lambda\widetilde\chi_0(h\Lambda)+iQ\). Applying the corrected unscaled statement to \(\widetilde P_Q\) gives precisely the growth estimate used on p. 32; Section 2.3 likewise uses an unscaled evolution. Thus the literal displayed lemma is false, while this defect has not been shown to invalidate the actual semigroup bound required by the main proof. This is the reason for the Minor classification.

# Unresolved questions and limits of the conclusions

Theorem 3.3 assumes \(\psi\in C^\infty_c((0,\infty))\), and its improved remainder depends on \(\|\psi^{(N)}\|_1\). Theorems 1.6 and 3.7 state arbitrary \(H^1_{\mathrm{comp}}\) weights. Approximation in \(H^1\) alone does not uniformly control the higher-derivative remainder. An explicit extension argument or a restriction of the stated weight class is needed. This review has neither a counterexample nor a complete repair of that extension; it remains Needs Evidence, rather than a confirmed additional failure.

Remark 5.3 uses a small positive upper threshold for \(\omega(\varepsilon)\log\varepsilon\), a nonpositive quantity for small \(\varepsilon\). The proof’s error budget involves \(\omega(\varepsilon)\log(1/\varepsilon)\). The sign and the precise intended quantitative condition should be clarified; this is not used here to assert failure of Theorem 1.1.

The hypotheses of all imported pseudodifferential, spectral and semigroup results were traced where used, but a complete independent reconstruction of every cited theorem is not claimed. No numerical PDE solution or empirical experiment was performed. Missing released code or a publisher PDF is not counted as a mathematical defect.
