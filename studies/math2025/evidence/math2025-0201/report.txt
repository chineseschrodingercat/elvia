Abstract: This review identifies a finite-rank counterexample to the lowest-index assertion of the abstract Toda theorem, a coordinate normalization error in the stated PNG equation, and a discontinuous approximation used in the backward-equation proof. A fourth, local issue concerns the distributional initial velocity in the narrow-wedge example. The first three issues affect a central theorem or a principal proof dependency and are classified Major; this classification does not assert that the intended PNG results are false or irreparable. The review concerns the identified publisher version and the explicitly documented portions of its proofs. It is an initial technical assessment, with scientific decision pending and no independent human acceptance.

# Objects and conventions

The paper studies PNG height probabilities \(F_r(t,x)=\mathbb P(h(t,x)\leq r)\) in the scalar case and their multipoint analogues. The abstract theorem uses trace-class kernels \(K_r\) on a finite direct sum of copies of \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z_{>0})\) and matrices \[Q_r=\big[(I-K_r)^{-1}\big](1,1).\] The abstract index \(r\) is a simultaneous shift of all height thresholds in its PNG application. This distinction matters at the lower endpoint. In Problem 2 only one spatial point is used, so no convention about differentiating a multipoint centre coordinate is needed. In Problems 3–4 the nucleation intensity is the paper’s intensity \(2\), and the deterministic lateral speed is \(1\).

Problem 1 (Major: the lowest interior index in Theorem 3.1).  Location. Theorem 3.1, p. 4974 (PDF 24), assumptions (1)–(3) and conclusion; the asserted mixed identity (3.4), p. 4975 (PDF 25); the application with three indices in Section 3.2, p. 4979 (PDF 29).

Equation reference: eq:toda. Claim being checked. The theorem assumes kernels indexed from \(\underline r\) to \(\overline r\), with \(\underline r<\overline r-1\), satisfying \[\begin{aligned}
K_{r+1}(u,v)&=K_r(u+1,v+1),\\
\partial_\eta K_r(u,v)&=K_{r-1}(u+1,v)-K_r(u+1,v),\\
\partial_\zeta K_r(u,v)&=K_{r-1}(u,v+1)-K_r(u,v+1).\end{aligned}\] The first identity is required up to \(r=\overline r-1\); the derivative identities start at \(r=\underline r+1\). With the stated trace-class, differentiability, and invertibility assumptions, the theorem claims, for every interior \(r\), \[
\partial_\zeta\big(\partial_\eta Q_r\,Q_r^{-1}\big)
 +Q_rQ_{r-1}^{-1}-Q_{r+1}Q_r^{-1}=0.\]

Decisive evidence. Take one copy of \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z_{>0})\), the open parameter set \(\mathbb R^2\), \(\underline r=0\), \(\overline r=2\), and \(P=|1\rangle\langle1|\). Set \[K_0=\tfrac12P,\qquad K_1=K_2=0\] at every parameter value. These are trace-class kernels, with trace norms \(1/2,0,0\). All parameter derivatives, including mixed derivatives, exist in trace norm and vanish. Every required shift or derivative relation holds: \(P(u+1,v)\), \(P(u,v+1)\), and \(P(u+1,v+1)\) vanish for positive integers \(u,v\). Moreover, \[(I-K_0)^{-1}=I+P,\qquad (I-K_1)^{-1}=(I-K_2)^{-1}=I,
\quad (Q_0,Q_1,Q_2)=(2,1,1).\] Thus all required inverses exist, but the left side of [eq:toda] (#eq:toda) at \(r=1\) is \[0+\tfrac12-1=-\tfrac12.\] This is a counterexample to the theorem as quantified, not a numerical failure or a hypothetical objection.

The proof’s asserted identity \[\partial_\eta\partial_\zeta K_r=K_{r+1}-2K_r+K_{r-1}
\tag{3.4 in the article}\] would give \(0=\tfrac12P\) here. Deriving it by differentiating a first-order identity at the lowest interior index needs information on the derivative of \(K_{\underline r}\), which the theorem does not supply.

Impact and repair boundary. Section 3.2 applies the theorem with \(\underline r=-1\), \(\overline r=1\), at \(r=0\), exactly the affected endpoint. Restricting the theorem’s conclusion to \(r\geq\underline r+2\) avoids this particular logical step but leaves that three-kernel application empty. Adding the requisite mixed identity at the indices used is another possible repair. It must then be verified for the actual PNG kernels, including their forcing terms and trace-norm regularity. The rank-one example proves neither that a strengthened abstract theorem fails nor that the intended PNG probability identity is false. The remaining resolvent algebra was checked conditionally on the missing mixed identity; that conditional check does not repair the printed hypotheses.

Problem 2 (Major: a factor of four in the Toda coordinates).  Location. Definition of \(\zeta,\eta\), p. 4957 (PDF 7); Theorem 2.1 and equation (2.13), p. 4958 (PDF 8); the derivative statement preceding (2.19), p. 4959 (PDF 9); the kernel derivative calculation (3.15), p. 4979 (PDF 29). The narrow-wedge model and its Poisson/permutation description are on pp. 4954–4955 (PDF 4–5); the determinant-ratio identity is on p. 4980 (PDF 30).

Decisive chain-rule check. At a single point the printed coordinates are \[\zeta=(t-x)/2,\qquad \eta=(t+x)/2.\] Consequently \(t=\eta+\zeta\), \(x=\eta-\zeta\), and ordinary coordinate differentiation gives \[\partial_\eta=\partial_t+\partial_x,\qquad
\partial_\zeta=\partial_t-\partial_x,\qquad
\partial_\zeta\partial_\eta=\partial_t^2-\partial_x^2.\] The article instead writes the last operator with a factor \(1/4\). The kernel calculation and the scalar equation (2.18) use the half-sized vector fields. These conventions are inconsistent.

An explicit admissible test. The inconsistency affects the displayed equation, not merely a change of notation in an unused remark. Take narrow-wedge initial data \(h_0(0)=0\), \(h_0(x)=-\infty\) for \(x\ne0\), and \(|x|<t\). Write \(s=t^2-x^2\). The accessible Poisson area has mean \(s\), and \[F_k=e^{-s}G_k(s),\qquad
G_k(s)=\sum_{m\geq0}\frac{a_{m,k}}{(m!)^2}s^m,\] where \(a_{m,k}\) counts permutations of \(m\) letters whose longest increasing subsequence has length at most \(k\). Thus \[F_0=e^{-s},\quad
F_1=1-\frac{s^2}{4}+\frac{s^3}{9}+O(s^4),\quad
F_2=1-\frac{s^3}{36}+O(s^4),\quad
F_3=1+O(s^4).\] The threshold \(r=1\) is strictly above the deterministic height \(0\), as required. With \[Q_1=F_2/F_1,\qquad
U_1=F_2F_0/F_1^2,\qquad U_2=F_3F_1/F_2^2,\] the determinant-ratio identity also supplies the adjacent ratio at threshold \(0\), where the relevant determinants are positive. For any \(f(s)\), ordinary differentiation in the printed coordinates gives \[\partial_\zeta\partial_\eta f(s)=4\{f'(s)+sf''(s)\}.\] The residual of the scalar version of (2.13) is therefore \[\partial_\zeta\partial_\eta\log Q_1+U_1-U_2
=3s+O(s^2).\] It is nonzero for sufficiently small positive \(s\).

As a bounded quantitative check, independent exact rational arithmetic at \(t=1/10\), \(x=0\) proves \[\frac{29}{1000}
<
\partial_\zeta\partial_\eta\log Q_1+U_1-U_2
<
\frac{30}{1000}.\] The actual computed interval is approximately \([0.029628224985503042,\,0.02962822498558042]\); these decimal endpoints are only a display of a rational interval, not the basis of the assertion. Appendix A gives the count and tail method.

Impact and repair boundary. One coherent convention is \[D_\eta=\tfrac12(\partial_t+\partial_x),\qquad
D_\zeta=\tfrac12(\partial_t-\partial_x);\] at one point it corresponds to coordinates \(\eta=t+x\), \(\zeta=t-x\). Alternatively, retaining the printed coordinates requires a factor \(1/4\) on the mixed derivative in the corresponding scalar equation. All matrix and multipoint conventions must be reconciled consistently. This local normalization repair does not fix the missing endpoint hypothesis in Problem 1.

Problem 3 (Major: the spatially averaged indicator is not continuous).  Location. State-space topology and generator setup, pp. 4986–4989 (PDF 36–39); Proposition 4.6 and its proof, pp. 4996–4997 (PDF 46–47); the use in Proposition 4.8 and identification of the probability with the determinant, pp. 4998–4999 (PDF 48–49). Appendix B.1, p. 5012 (PDF 62), is relevant counterevidence.

Claim being checked. The proof spatially averages a threshold indicator, \[f_\varepsilon(h)=\frac{1}{(2\varepsilon)^n}
\int_{|z_i-x_i|<\varepsilon\ (1\leq i\leq n)}
\mathbf 1\{h(z_i)\leq r_i\text{ for all }i\}\,d\vec z,\] asserts \(f_\varepsilon\in C_0\), invokes the generator/backward equation for such functions, and integrates by parts. The state space inherits the upper-semicontinuous hypograph topology. The restricted step configurations have locally finite, distinct unit jumps; no uniform bound on their number along a convergent sequence is imposed.

Counterexample to continuity. Use \(n=1,x_1=r_1=0,\varepsilon=1\), and, for \(m\geq2\), define \[S_m=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb Z}
\left[\frac{j}{m}-\frac{1}{2m^3},\,
      \frac{j}{m}+\frac{1}{2m^3}\right],
\qquad h_m=\mathbf 1_{S_m},\qquad h_* \equiv1 .\] Each \(S_m\) is closed and locally finite as a union of disjoint intervals. Each \(h_m\) is integer-valued and upper semicontinuous, with distinct locally finite jumps of heights \(+1\) and \(-1\); \(h_m(0)=h_*(0)=1\). In the stated topology \(h_m\to h_*\): every upper limit along \(x_m\to x\) is at most \(1\), and nearest grid points \(j_m/m\to x\) attain height \(1\). Nevertheless, \[|S_m\cap(-1,1)|=\frac{2}{m^2},
\qquad f_1(h_m)=1-\frac1{m^2}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad f_1(h_*)=0.\] Thus \(f_1\) is not continuous and cannot belong to the continuous-function domain used in the argument. Continuity on a stratum with a fixed list of jumps would not establish continuity on the declared state space.

A separate false convergence assertion in the same step. The proof also asserts uniform convergence of \(P_t f_\varepsilon\) to the point-evaluation probability. Fix \(t_0>0\) and initial data \(h(y)=\mathbf 1\{y\geq t_0\}\). At time \(t_0\), the deterministically expanding height-one step reaches \(0\), so \[F_0(t_0,z)=
\begin{cases}e^{-2t_0^2},&z<0,\\0,&z\geq0.\end{cases}\] Indeed for \(z<0\) the backward cone lies strictly below the initial step and has area \(t_0^2\); absence of intensity-two nucleations is exactly the event of height at most zero. Hence for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[P_{t_0}f_\varepsilon(h)=\tfrac12e^{-2t_0^2},
\qquad F_0(t_0,0)=0.\] Even pointwise convergence fails at this shock.

Adverse checks, impact, and repair boundary. The measure-zero shock argument and dominated-convergence discussion on p. 4997 can potentially replace the convergence assertion by a suitable almost-everywhere statement under the integration measure. They do not establish \(f_\varepsilon\in C_0\). Appendix B.1 explicitly distinguishes continuity from above from unrestricted continuity; it does not supply the missing premise for this sequence, which approximates from below. The later determinant-side regularity theorem does not independently prove the probability-side backward equation.

Proposition 4.8 explicitly uses (4.20) and (4.22) to obtain (4.25), leading to \(F=\widehat F\) on p. 4999 and then the general-initial-data statement of Theorem 2.3. The failed approximation therefore leaves a gap in a principal proof dependency. A valid repair could use a proved generator-domain approximation, or establish an appropriate weak/integrated backward equation directly. Such a replacement requires its own convergence and domain argument; none is certified here. This is a proof-gap finding, not a counterexample to the Fredholm formula.

Problem 4 (Minor: the narrow-wedge initial velocity needs a height restriction).  Location. The initial-velocity discussion on p. 4959 (PDF 9) and the narrow-wedge example on p. 4960 (PDF 10).

Decisive evidence. The narrow-wedge paragraph states an unqualified distributional velocity \(\partial_tF_r(0,x)=-2\delta_0\). In the same paragraph, the threshold-zero formula gives \(F_0(t,x)=e^{-(t^2-x^2)}\) on \(|x|\leq t\), and \(F_0=1\) outside. For a compactly supported smooth test function \(\varphi\), \[\left|\int_{\mathbb R}(F_0(t,x)-1)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|
\leq\|\varphi\|_\infty\int_{-t}^t(t^2-x^2)\,dx
=\tfrac43\|\varphi\|_\infty t^3.\] After division by \(t\) the limit is zero. For every \(r\geq0\), the inequality \(0\leq1-F_r\leq1-F_0\) proves the same zero derivative. For \(r<0\), \(F_r(t,x)=\mathbf 1\{|x|>t\}\), up to endpoints irrelevant to distributions, and the derivative is \(-2\delta_0\). The locally verified correction is \[\partial_t F_r(0,\cdot)=-2\mathbf 1\{r<0\}\delta_0.\]

The preceding jump-height expression also needs a scope restriction if it is meant to cover arbitrary integer jumps. For \(h_0(x)=2\mathbf 1\{x\geq0\}\) and threshold \(0\), \(F_0(t,x)=e^{-2t^2}\mathbf 1\{x<-t\}\), whose initial derivative is \(-\delta_0\), whereas weighting the threshold change by the jump height gives \(-2\delta_0\). The article introduces this discussion as applying in simple cases; if that phrase is intended to mean distinct unit jumps only, this latter example requests an explicit restriction rather than disproving that restricted claim. The narrow-wedge discrepancy does not depend on that interpretation.

Impact and repair boundary. The correction concerns displayed initial data and their interpretation. These boundary displays are not used in the inspected proof of the Fredholm formula. This is therefore classified Minor. No claim of a complete well-posedness theory or a repaired general jump formula is made.

# Assessment and limits

The central issues have different logical statuses:

Problem | Severity | Established result

1 | Major | Exact finite-rank counterexample to the printed abstract theorem at its lowest interior index.

2 | Major | Inconsistent coordinate normalization, with an admissible scalar PNG counterexample to the printed equation.

3 | Major | Exact counterexamples to continuity and pointwise approximation used in a principal proof.

4 | Minor | Incorrect unqualified distributional initial velocity, with a verified height-dependent correction.

A Major issue need not make the entire paper false or impossible to repair. Here Problem 1 is logically independent of the normalization in Problem 2, and repairing both would still leave the probability-side argument in Problem 3 to justify. Conversely, these findings do not by themselves refute a consistently normalized PNG Toda identity or the intended Fredholm formula.

The scientific decision remains pending. This document is an initial review, not an independent acceptance record. They also list unexamined arguments and the absence of an exhaustive independent reconstruction.
