# Claim, hypotheses, and scope

Theorem 1.1, p. 2, concerns finite integrally weighted plumbing trees of disk bundles over \(S^2\). All coefficients are in \(\mathbb F=\mathbb Z/2\). It asserts \[\mathbb{HF}(G)\cong \boldsymbol{HF}^{-}(Y(G))\] as \(\mathbb F[[U]]\)-modules, where the right side is completed in \(U\) and the lattice complex uses the product completion specified on p. 9. Negative definiteness is not a hypothesis. The relative-grading assertion is restricted to \(b_1(Y(G))=0\). An integral-coefficient theorem, a general extension to graphs with cycles or positive-genus vertices, and compatibility with the exterior homology action are not asserted here. The last compatibility remains Conjectures 1.2 and 7.2.

The primary conversation concentrated on the main reduction in Section 6, its hypotheses, the older lattice/surgery identification, and degenerate examples. The auxiliary checks covered the bordered algebra and Hopf models, the homology actions, and the finite-generation argument. Their actual roles and source-page observations are separately recorded. The foundational analytic construction of Heegaard Floer theory and the entirety of the lengthy external surgery-formula papers were not re-proved. In particular, the geometric left/right arc and twist conventions remain imported from the pairing theory. The final argument allows either factor: it selects a null-homologous factor when \(b_1=0\), and eliminates the correction on either finite factor when \(b_1>0\). A left/right label discrepancy alone does not demonstrate a failure of that argument.

# The main reduction and its critical steps

## The vertex complexes and the coefficient ring

Proposition 2.3, p. 11, identifies the cube obtained from vertex homology and induced length-one maps with \(\mathbb{CF}(G)\). The cited Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó result was checked directly in [OSS], Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.4, pp. 10 and 12–14. The former supplies the completed rank-one vertex homology for plumbing links; the latter identifies the two edge maps and their powers of \(U\). The framing-independence comparison in its Lemmas 4.5–4.7 matters: this input is not restricted to the negative-definite framings used in an intermediate computation.

There is a genuine extension issue if one only proves collapse of a spectral sequence. The discussion in [Retained latex: \cite[Section 4.2]{OSS}] explicitly acknowledges it. Zemke’s Proposition 6.3 constructs a chain homotopy equivalence over the retained variable \(U_1\), rather than deducing a module isomorphism from spectral-sequence collapse. Thus that possible objection does not apply to the proof given here.

## The contraction and completion

For fixed cube coordinate \(\varepsilon\) and Alexander grading \(\mathbf s\), the uncompleted complex in (6.1), pp. 40–41, has Hopf levels \[\mathcal F^\ell\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal F^1,
 \qquad H_*\cong\mathbb F[U_1],\] with homology generated at the lowest level. Over \(\mathbb F[U_1]\) the homology and the intervening kernels are free, so the exact sequences split. The splitting can be chosen homogeneous: the differential and the modules are graded, and homogeneous lifts and a homogeneous basis of the graded submodules give the required sections. With \(\mathcal F^i=L^i\oplus R^i\), the restriction \[\partial:R^{i+1}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }L^i\] defines \(h^i\) by inversion. Extend it by zero on the other summands. This gives \[\pi i=\mathrm{id},\qquad i\pi=\mathrm{id}+\partial h+h\partial,\qquad
 h^2=\pi h=hi=0.\] In particular, no equivariance in all variables \(U_j\) is claimed for this splitting.

The continuation to the completion on p. 41 has an applicable continuity argument. At fixed \((\varepsilon,\mathbf s)\) there are finitely many polynomial generators, each \(U_j\) has grading \(-2\), and the grading filtration and \((U_1,\ldots,U_\ell)\)-adic filtration are cofinal. The homogeneous maps therefore extend continuously. Taking the product of these coordinate maps respects the product over Alexander gradings. This does not replace that product by a direct sum or assume an arbitrary ungraded splitting to be continuous.

## Why higher arrows disappear

The mixed alpha–beta Hopf model is essential. In (3.5) and Propositions 3.9–3.10, pp. 23–25, the bottom generators are \(a,d\) and the upper generators are \(b,c\). The length-one maps preserve these levels. In the mixed model, the term \(c\mapsto d\,\mathcal V_1^{\lambda_1-1}\mathcal V_2^{\lambda_2}\) is deleted; the remaining length-two terms raise the level. The alternate model retains a lowering term and would not justify the last step. Remark 6.4 correctly distinguishes the two models.

This property propagates through a merge. For a map involving the merged root, its length is \[r=r_1+r_2-1.\] If \(r=1\), both factors have length one and preserve the total Hopf level. If \(r\ge2\), at least one factor has higher length and raises that level, while the other does not lower it. Nonroot maps inherit the property from one tensor factor.

In Lemma 6.1 each path is strictly increasing in a finite cube, so all perturbation sums are finite. Every summand transferring a higher-length map either contains an \(h\) or already contains a higher-length arrow. It consequently raises Hopf level before reaching \(\pi\), which is supported only at the bottom. It vanishes. Exactly the induced length-one differential remains. This verifies the mechanism needed for Proposition 6.3; it is stronger than a numerical test or a dimension comparison.

## The two gluing cases

Lemma 6.5, p. 42, follows because one knot surgery changes first Betti number by at most one. If the final plumbing is a rational homology sphere, the sum of the Betti numbers of the branch manifolds is at most one. At each relevant merge at least one knot is rationally null-homologous. Corollary 4.10 can therefore be applied to the appropriate left or right pair-of-pants model. The explicit homotopies in this case respect the relative grading used in Section 6.3.

For positive \(b_1\), the paper does not reuse that null-homology argument without its hypothesis. It invokes Propositions 5.1 and 5.3 instead. The finite-generation input can also be checked in [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 9.1]{General}], including partial fillings; it imposes no additional negative-framing or rational-homology-sphere restriction. The algebraic simplification is examined next. The resulting proof supports the ungraded completed-module claim; Remark 6.6 expressly leaves stronger grading compatibility open.

# Additional algebraic checks and boundary cases

## Finite generation and the Euler derivation

The coefficient ring relevant to the \(h\)-map in Section 5 is \[S=\mathbb F((\mathcal V^{-1}))[[\mathcal U]],
 \qquad U=\mathcal U\mathcal V,\] a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer associate to \(U\). It is not an arbitrary two-variable Laurent power-series ring. For a finite free complex over \(S\), the square-zero differential splits into one-step summands and two-step summands with differential \(U^j\).

The apparent extra \(\mathbb Z/2\) grading hypothesis in Lemma 5.5 is not an obstruction to the application in Corollary 5.6. Even without an assigned parity, \(\operatorname{im}\partial\) is free, so \(0\to\ker\partial\to C\to\operatorname{im}\partial\to0\) splits. Smith normal form for the inclusion \(\operatorname{im}\partial\subset\ker\partial\) then yields the same decomposition.

The formal derivation \(E=\mathcal U\partial_{\mathcal U}+\mathcal V\partial_{\mathcal V}\) satisfies \[E(U^j)=2jU^j=0\] in characteristic two. Differentiating a change-of-basis chain equation gives the conjugating chain homotopy. Thus \(\mathcal U\Phi+\mathcal V\Psi\) is null-homotopic over precisely the enlarged coefficient ring allowed for the \(h\)-map. This supplies the extra-term elimination in Proposition 5.1. It is not a claim that the analogous action vanishes over every smaller coefficient ring.

## Homology actions

The torsion argument in Lemma 4.1 uses an integer-valued function on the Alexander lattice with prescribed increments. It does not divide by the order of a torsion class in \(\mathbb F\), which would be invalid for even torsion. Section 7 admits the same translation to characteristic vectors.

The exterior relations can also be checked directly. Write \[a_v(K,E)=g(K,E-v)-g(K,E).\] For distinct \(v,w\), the exponent in \(A_vA_w\) telescopes to \(g(K,E-\{v,w\})-g(K,E)\), independently of the order. The mixed terms with the other half of the lattice differential telescope in the same way, using the shifted characteristic vector. Thus \(A_v\) is a chain map, distinct actions commute in characteristic two, and \(A_v^2=0\) because a vertex cannot be deleted twice. As in the differential itself, the expression is zero when \(v\notin E\). These checks support well-definedness of the lattice action, without proving the expressly conjectural compatibility under Theorem 1.1.

## A degenerate one-vertex check

For the single vertex of weight zero, write \(K(v)=2n\). The two targets of the lattice differential coincide, and the coefficient is \[U^{\max(0,-n)}+U^{\max(0,n)}.\] For \(n\ne0\) it is \(1+U^{|n|}\), a unit in \(\mathbb F[[U]]\); that component is contractible. For \(n=0\) it vanishes, leaving one copy of \(\mathbb F[[U]]\) in each of the two cube levels. This gives \(\mathbb F[[U]]\oplus\mathbb F[[U]]\), as required for the completed theory of \(S^1\times S^2\). Completion is essential to the unit argument. The retained auxiliary calculations also examine positive weight and a three-vertex zero-weight tree, where the positive-\(b_1\) gluing argument is needed. These examples test boundary assumptions and do not replace the general proof.

# Local clarifications and assessment

The following are local corrections, not numbered substantive Problems. In Section 3.2, p. 16, the first displayed \(\tau\mathcal V\) relation should read \(\tau\mathcal V=\mathcal U\mathcal V^2\tau\), as follows from the immediately following \(\phi^\tau(\mathcal V)=U\mathcal V\) and the symmetric-variable description. In the merge formula of Section 3.6 the output of the \(\tau,\tau\) operation should have idempotent \(i_1\), consistently with the balanced tensor product. The caption of Figure 3.1 reverses the alpha/beta order of the two input arcs stated in the surrounding text; the pairing hypothesis requires opposite types. Finally, on p. 41 the inverse defining \(h^i\) is the inverse of \(\partial|_{R^{i+1}}\), not of its zero restriction to \(L^{i+1}\); the displayed splitting diagram already specifies the correct map. No downstream use of these literal misprints producing a substantive failure was established.
