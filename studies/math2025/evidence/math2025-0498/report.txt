Abstract: This review concerns the 77-page version arXiv:2212.13746v2 by Seonwoo Kim and Insuk Seo. Two Major findings concern the additional models in Section 5: the stated pairwise hitting times and limiting rates are mutually inconsistent, and the printed directed-Ising rates do not preserve the asserted Gibbs measure. Several local domain, normalization and proof corrections are also established. The arguments below distinguish these findings from a disproof of the principal Metropolis–Hastings or purely cyclic metastability results. Scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Framework and operative assumptions

Equation reference: eq:H. The state space is \(S^\Lambda\), with \(\Lambda=\mathbb T_K\times\mathbb T_L\), \(11\leq K\leq L\), \(S=\{1,\ldots,q\}\), and \(q\geq2\). The limit \(\beta\to\infty\) is taken at fixed \(K,L,q\). The Hamiltonian and Gibbs probability are \[
 H(\sigma)=\sum_{\{x,y\}:x\sim y}{\bf1}_{\{\sigma(x)\ne\sigma(y)\}},
 \qquad \mu_\beta(\sigma)=Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}.\] Each unordered nearest-neighbor edge is counted once. Bold spins denote monochromatic ground states. These conventions, periodic boundaries, and the positivity of the rates are retained in every example below.

For MH dynamics the edge height is the larger endpoint energy. For the cyclic dynamics, \(q\geq3\), only the forward rotation \(\tau_x\) is permitted, and the edge height is the maximum energy over the entire local spin orbit. Thus the conductance identity (3.3) uses different heights for the two chains. The respective barriers are \(2K+2\) and \(2K+4\). The principal prefactor is \(\kappa=b+2e\), where \(b>0\) is the bulk constant and \(e>0\) the edge constant. Nonempty disjoint ground-state sets are required for the potential-theoretic statements.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (P1: incompatible hitting times and limiting rates).  Severity: Major, localized to Theorem 5.2. Theorem 5.2(2)–(3), pages 18–19, equations (5.2)–(5.3), assigns both the limiting mean hitting time \(\kappa_{|b-a|}\) and the limiting jump rate \(\kappa_{|b-a|}^{-1}\). Together with its stated prefactor asymptotics these assertions cannot all hold.

## Decisive calculation

Equation reference: eq:uv, eq:four. Choose the permitted parameters \(q=4\), \(p=1/2\) and \(L=K+1\). Write \[u=\kappa_1=\kappa_3,\qquad v=\kappa_2.\] Since \(\widehat p=1\) and \(\nu_0=1\), equation (5.3) gives \[
 u\longrightarrow \frac38,\qquad v\longrightarrow\frac12
 \quad(K\to\infty).\] The chain specified in part (3) has rate \(a=1/u\) to either neighboring spin on the four-cycle and rate \(b_0=1/v\) to the opposite spin. Label its states \(0,1,2,3\) for this calculation only. With target 0, symmetry gives mean hitting times \(x\) from 1 or 3 and \(y\) from 2. The exact backward equations are \[2ax-ay=1,\qquad -2ax+(2a+b_0)y=1.\] Their solution is \[
 x=\frac{u(u+3v)}{2(u+v)},\qquad
 y=\frac{2uv}{u+v},\qquad
 x-u=\frac{u(v-u)}{2(u+v)}.\] This was also checked by independently forming the full \(4\times4\) generator and solving its killed \(3\times3\) system exactly.

For every sufficiently large fixed \(K\), [eq:uv] (#eq:uv) implies \(v>u\), hence \(x>u\). The original accelerated hitting time is pathwise at least its ground-state trace hitting time, because the trace deletes time. Part (3) invokes the usual Skorohod convergence of Theorem 3.4, page 10. For a finite-state limiting chain with positive finite rates, this implies convergence of the target hitting-time distribution. Applying Fatou’s lemma to the integrals of the survival probabilities gives \[\liminf_{\beta\to\infty} e^{-\Gamma\beta}
       {\mathbb E}_{\mathbf1}\tau_{\mathbf2}
 \ \geq\ {\mathbb E}_{1}\tau_0^Y=x>u.\] This contradicts part (2), which sets the limit equal to \(u\). No uniform-integrability assumption or interchange of the \(K\) and \(\beta\) limits is used: first choose a sufficiently large fixed lattice, then let \(\beta\) tend to infinity. The limiting values in [eq:four] (#eq:four) are \[x\to\frac{45}{112},\qquad y\to\frac37,
 \qquad u\to\frac{42}{112}.\]

## Counterevidence, dependency and repair

Equal off-diagonal rates do give the advertised mean \(u\). Thus the principal purely cyclic case and the equal-rate cases are not counterexamples to themselves. The problem arises from using unequal pair-specific quantities simultaneously as inverse rates and target hitting times. The all-other-states exit-time formula, by itself, is consistent with the total jump rate.

This disproves the joint assertion of the additional-model theorem, but does not determine which of its rates, hitting-time formula, or prefactor formula is the correct one. Repair requires deriving a consistent effective generator and solving \(-Qg=1\) on the complement of the target. Alternatively, if the pairwise hitting constants are retained, the generator must be derived separately. The comparison in Remark 5.3 needs reassessment accordingly. The \(p=1\) principal cyclic results and the MH results are not refuted by this calculation.

Problem 2 (P2: the printed directed rates fail Gibbs stationarity).  Severity: Major, localized to Section 5.2. Pages 19–20 define one exponential of a weighted directional energy increment and assert that \(\mu_\beta\) is invariant for every \(p\in[0,1]\). That assertion is false for the displayed generator and Hamiltonian.

## Exact admissible counterexample

Equation reference: eq:incoming. Encode the two spins as \(s_x\in\{-1,1\}\). Put \[A_x=s_x(s_{x+e_1}+s_{x+e_2}),\qquad
 B_x=s_x(s_{x-e_1}+s_{x-e_2}).\] These are precisely the NE and SW increments of [eq:H] (#eq:H) when \(s_x\) flips. The printed outgoing rate is \[c_p(\sigma,x)=e^{-\beta[pA_x+(1-p)B_x]}.\] Since \(H(\sigma^x)-H(\sigma)=A_x+B_x\), the incoming rate weighted by the Gibbs ratio is \[
 \frac{\mu_\beta(\sigma^x)c_p(\sigma^x,x)}
      {\mu_\beta(\sigma)}
 =e^{-\beta[(1-p)A_x+pB_x]}=c_{1-p}(\sigma,x).\] Stationarity therefore requires equality of the sums of \(c_p\) and \(c_{1-p}\) at every configuration.

Equation reference: eq:residual. Take \(K=11,L=12\), with negative spins exactly at \[\{(4,5),(5,5),(5,6),(6,5)\},\] and positive spins elsewhere; coordinates are taken modulo \(K,L\). The increment pairs and their multiplicities over all 132 sites are \[\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
(A_x,B_x)&(-2,0)&(0,-2)&(0,0)&(0,2)&(2,-2)&(2,0)&(2,2)\\
\hline
\text{count}&2&1&1&4&1&3&120 .
\end{array}\] With \(t=e^{\beta p}\), \(u=e^{\beta(1-p)}\), this finite table gives the exact stationarity residual \[\begin{aligned}
 R(\sigma)
 &=\sum_x\left[
       \frac{\mu_\beta(\sigma^x)c_p(\sigma^x,x)}{\mu_\beta(\sigma)}
       -c_p(\sigma,x)\right]\notag\\
 &=-\frac{(t^2-1)(t^2-u^2)(u^2-1)}{t^2u^2}.
 \end{aligned}\] It is nonzero for every \(\beta>0\) and \(p\in(0,1)\setminus\{1/2\}\). For an entirely rational check, set \(p=1/3,\ \beta=3\log2\). Then \[\sum_xc_p=\frac{255}{8},\qquad
 \sum_x\frac{\mu_\beta(\sigma^x)c_p(\sigma^x,x)}{\mu_\beta(\sigma)}
   =\frac{645}{16},\qquad R(\sigma)=\frac{135}{16}.\] These are rates and a residual divided by \(\mu_\beta(\sigma)\); the raw probability-flux residual is \(\mu_\beta(\sigma)135/16\). A second implementation recomputed the full Hamiltonian before and after each of the 132 flips, independently of the local-spin formula. Both implementations agreed. At \(p=1/3\), writing \(z=e^{\beta/3}\), the symbolic result is \[R(\sigma)=\frac{(z-1)^3(z+1)^3(z^2+1)}{z^4}>0.\]

## Alternative readings and the cited reference

The source rates contain no positive part and no sum of exponentials; both details were checked in the page image. Changing the temperature convention or multiplying all rates by a common positive factor cannot make the nonzero balance residual vanish.

The cited reference [19], Godrèche–Bray [GB], Section 3.3, equations (3.13)–(3.14), pages 10–11, treats the purely NE directed rate and a three-neighbor extension. Its exponential interpolation (2.30), page 8, is for the one-dimensional chain. These are different statements from the four-neighbor interpolation printed here. The energy conventions match after using \(J=1/2\), since a disagreement indicator is \((1-s_xs_y)/2\).

There is substantial valid counterevidence at the endpoints. For \(p=1\) and \(p=0\), expanding the NE and SW exponentials into constants, nearest-neighbor products and diagonal products gives equal sums by translation on the torus; this is also the calculation in [GB], (4.6)–(4.8), page 13. For \(p=1/2\), detailed balance holds. Consequently a convex mixture of the endpoint rates preserves the Gibbs measure, but it is a different generator from the exponential of their weighted increments. The description of the \(p=1/2\) rate as heat-bath Glauber is also inaccurate in the usual convention: \(e^{-\beta\Delta H/2}\) and \(1/(1+e^{\beta\Delta H})\) are different rules, as [GB], (3.8) and (3.10), explicitly distinguish.

## Impact and repair boundary

The claimed invariant distribution is a core prerequisite for applying the Gibbs-based potential-theoretic derivation to this extension. Its failure is demonstrated even in the low-temperature regime, not merely at one moderate temperature. This warrants Major severity for the extension’s derivation and model identification. It does not, by itself, prove that the numerical barrier or every asymptotic assertion in Theorem 5.4 is false. One must either analyze the actual invariant law of the printed generator or explicitly change the generator and rederive its barrier, time normalization and prefactor. Neither correction is supplied by relabeling the rates. The main MH and purely cyclic models have separately valid invariant measures.

Problem 3 (P3: harmonicity is asserted on a nonharmonic boundary).  Severity: Minor, with a concrete repair of the proof domain. Lemma 9.10(1), page 55, asserts \(\phi_1=0\) on the indicated union of \(\overline{\mathcal Z}\)-sets outside \(\mathcal N(S)\). It fails to remove the regular-strip boundary \(\overline{\mathcal R}\), which overlaps \(\overline{\mathcal Z}\).

## Witness and proof

Use \(q=3,K=11,L=12\), \(a=1,b=2\), \(\mathcal P=\{\mathbf1\}\), \(\mathcal Q=\{\mathbf2\}\), and a width-two \(b\)-strip in rows 0 and 1. Let \(\sigma\) have one \(a\)-hole at \((0,0)\) and spin \(a\) outside the strip. Its hole set is a nonempty ladder tree (Proposition 6.17, page 29), so \[\sigma\in\overline{\mathcal B}^{a,b}_0
 \subset\overline{\mathcal Z}^{a,b}\cap\overline{\mathcal R}^{a,b}_2,
 \qquad \sigma\notin\mathcal N(S).\] Here \(\overline{\mathcal B}^{a,b}_0\) denotes the auxiliary target set called \(\overline B^{a,b}_0\) in Definition 8.13, not the bulk set. Membership follows also from Lemma D.4, pages 73–74, and the disjointness of the auxiliary targets. The other ground-state neighborhoods are excluded by the edge-region decomposition.

Write \(f=f_{\overline A,\overline B}\). Definition 9.2, page 51, gives \[\widetilde h(\sigma)=1-\frac e\kappa,\qquad
 \widetilde h(\zeta)=1-\frac e\kappa+\frac e\kappa f(\zeta)
 \quad(\zeta\in\overline{\mathcal Z}^{a,b}).\] The energy of \(\sigma\) is \(24\). Exactly four outgoing rotations have orbit height at most \(\Gamma=26\): filling the hole, of height 25, and rotating the \(b\)-spins at \((1,0),(10,0),(0,1)\) to 3, each of height 26. Filling the hole leaves \(\widetilde h\) unchanged. Each other destination has \(f\geq0\), and at least one has \(f>0\): after rotating \((1,0)\) to 3, rotate it to 1 and extend the contiguous hole interval along row 0 to length \(K-1\). All intervening orbit heights are 26. The final configuration belongs to \(\overline A\), because closing the last remaining \(b\)-site has orbit height 25 and leads to a subbarrier path to \(\mathbf a\). This path avoids \(\overline B\), so it has strictly positive auxiliary-chain probability.

Equation reference: eq:boundary. The definition of \(\phi_1\), page 53, sums over destinations in \(\widehat{\mathcal N}(S)\), not just edges of height at most \(\Gamma\). This distinction was checked explicitly. The additional 21 outgoing destinations of energy at most 26 have minimum incident orbit height 27; they cannot belong to \(\widehat{\mathcal N}(S)\). All remaining destinations have energy above 26. Thus no omitted term changes the sign, and \[
 \phi_1(\sigma)
 =-\frac{e\,e^{-\Gamma\beta}}{\kappa Z_\beta}
       \sum_{j=1}^{3}f(\zeta_j)<0.\] Full local energy enumeration and the explicit positive-probability path are retained in the independent checks.

## Dependency and repair

Restrict the harmonicity claim to the auxiliary interior, excluding both \(\mathcal N(S)\) and the regular-strip boundary. The latter must be handled through the aggregate cancellation of Lemma 9.9 and the potential flatness of Lemma 9.5. The final proof on page 56 already invokes those two ingredients. For a regular orbit, \(\sum\phi_1=0\), each term is \(O(e^{-\Gamma\beta})\), and the potential varies by \(o(1)\); hence its total weighted contribution is \(o(e^{-\Gamma\beta})\) on the fixed finite lattice. This supplies the needed repair. A false local lemma is established, while the principal \(H^1\)-approximation conclusion is not refuted.

Problem 4 (P4: missing exclusions in quantifiers and projection domains).  Severity: Minor. The relevant statements have an evident intended restricted domain, but their literal domains include counterexamples.

First, Theorems 3.1–3.2, page 8, need distinct ground states in their pairwise formulas: (3.7) gives \(\Phi(\mathbf a,\mathbf a)=0\), and \({\mathbb E}_{\mathbf a}\tau_{\mathbf a}=0\), whereas their displayed barrier and prefactor are positive. Similarly, the disjointness clauses in Proposition 6.4, page 21, and Propositions 6.16 and 8.12, pages 27 and 49, require the appropriate distinct labels; a nonempty set does not have empty intersection with itself. In the three-label clauses, the endpoint labels must be distinct and \(a\notin\{b,c\}\). Definition 8.1, page 44, already implicitly excludes \(a=b\) by requiring \(1\leq r\leq q-1\). These qualifications preserve the intended inter-well statements.

Second, Proposition 6.21, page 31, excludes only the single configuration \(\mathbf a\), although its projection \(\Pi\) collapses all of \(\mathcal N(\mathbf a)\). Take the \(11\times12\) torus, \(q=3\), and let \(\eta_1\) be the all-1 state with one 2-spin at \((0,0)\), and \(\eta_2\) have an additional 2-spin at \((1,0)\). Their energies are 4 and 6. Both are distinct from \(\mathbf1\), lie in \(\mathcal N(\mathbf1)\), and are MH neighbors. Nevertheless, \[r(\Pi\eta_1,\Pi\eta_2)=r(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)=0,\qquad
 \mu_\beta(\eta_1)r_\beta(\eta_1,\eta_2)
       =Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-6\beta}>0.\] Thus (6.15) fails on the stated domain; (6.16) has the same issue. Require the uncollapsed inputs to lie outside \(\mathcal N(\mathbf a)\), equivalently \(\Pi\eta_i\ne\mathbf a\). The proof on page 66 and the applications to noncollapsed graph vertices already use that interpretation. These are domain repairs, not evidence against the intended graph approximation.

Problem 5 (P5: the trace-capacity multiplier in (D.8)).  Severity: Minor. In the proof of Proposition 8.14, page 74, the trace is explicitly taken on the unbarred vertex set \(\mathcal Z_\ell^{a,b}\). Equation (D.8), page 75, instead multiplies its capacity by \(|\overline A_\ell^{a,b}|+|\overline B_\ell^{a,b}|\).

Equation reference: eq:trace. For a chain uniform on \(\overline V\), its trace on \(Z\subset\overline V\) is uniform on \(Z\). For disjoint \(A,B\subset Z\), the exact identity is \[
 |\overline V|\,\operatorname{cap}_{\overline V}(A,B)
       =|Z|\,\operatorname{cap}_{Z}(A,B).\] Indeed, tracing preserves the equilibrium potential on \(Z\) and the boundary flux after multiplication by the stationary mass \(\mu(Z)=|Z|/|\overline V|\). Applying the boundary-flux definition of capacity proves [eq:trace] (#eq:trace) directly.

Here \(Z\) has interior vertices. For example, two adjacent \(a\)-holes in the width-two strip form a ladder tree, are not a single-hole \(B\) configuration, and are not adjacent by an energy-lowering flip to \(\mathcal N^{\rm MH}(\mathbf a)\); Lemma D.4 therefore excludes membership in \(A\). Capacity is positive, so the two different multipliers cannot give the same value.

Replace the printed multiplier by \(|\mathcal Z_\ell^{a,b}|\). A possible alternative is to define a further trace on \(A\cup B\), but that is not the trace defined in the preceding paragraph. The corrected cardinality and unit-rate graph are still independent of the spin labels, so the proof of label independence survives. The separate path-energy argument proving \(e_0<2\), later on page 75, does not use the erroneous multiplier and remains valid.

Problem 6 (P6: local path-index and energy bookkeeping).  Severity: Minor. Three explicitly repairable proof steps require correction; none supplies a counterexample to the associated asymptotic conclusion.

Arbitrary path length. Proposition 8.5 allows a path \((\omega_n)_{n=0}^N\), but its proof (D.1) and the threshold-index choice on page 69 use \(KL\); the same restriction recurs on page 71. General paths can include arbitrarily many local cycles before reaching the threshold number of \(b\)-spins. Replace the index ranges by \(0,\ldots,N\) and \(0,\ldots,N-1\), as applicable. The count changes by at most one at each step and goes from 0 to \(KL\), so the last threshold crossing exists over the full path. The subsequent local configuration argument is unaffected. This is an invalid indexing restriction, not a constructed subbarrier transition.

Maximum rather than minimum. In the last estimate for Lemma 7.16, page 43, the condition on a nonzero test-function difference must use \(\max\{H(\eta),H(\xi)\}\geq\Gamma\), not the printed minimum. A width-one strip of energy \(2K\) is in \(\mathcal N(\mathbf a)\); adding a one-site protuberance gives a neighboring edge state of energy \(2K+2=\Gamma\). The latter has positive probability to grow to the width-two strip before returning to \(\mathcal N(\mathbf a)\), so its edge potential is strictly below the neighborhood value 1. The minimum energy is \(2K<\Gamma\). The intended estimate follows directly from the MH conductance \(Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta\max(H(\eta),H(\xi))}\), so changing minimum to maximum repairs the bound.

A negligible term is not exactly zero. Appendix C, page 68, assumes \(q=2\) and finally equates the whole-space gateway energy sum to its restriction to \(\mathcal E\cup\mathcal B\). On the \(11\times12\) torus, take a regular width-three 2-strip in a 1-background. Its energy is 22, and flipping an interior 2-spin with four 2-neighbors raises the energy to 26. The destination is outside \(\widehat{\mathcal N}(S)=\mathcal E\cup\mathcal B\), since the MH barrier is 24, and has test value 1 by Definition 7.2. The strip has test value strictly below 1. This crossing contributes a strictly positive term \(Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-26\beta}(1-\widetilde h(\eta))^2\). The exact equality is therefore false. All such crossing terms are \(O(e^{-(\Gamma+1)\beta})\) at fixed volume by Lemma 6.5, page 21; adding this remainder preserves Proposition C.1. The appendix itself states that this proposition is not needed for the present paper.

# Positive checks, coverage and limits

The core approximation mechanism was checked independently of the extension findings. On a finite chain, \(D_\beta(f)=\langle f,-L_\beta f\rangle_{\mu_\beta}\) is a nonnegative quadratic form even without reversibility. If \(h\) is harmonic off the boundary and \(\widetilde h=h\) on that boundary, stationarity and harmonicity give \[D_\beta(h-\widetilde h)
       =D_\beta(\widetilde h)-\langle h,-L_\beta\widetilde h\rangle_{\mu_\beta}.\] Thus the use of the \(H^1\) seminorm and the energy comparison in Sections 4 and 9 does not secretly require self-adjointness. The finite-graph capacity/mean-hitting-time and trace-rate algebra was checked with the paper’s probability normalization; in particular the factor \(q\) cancels in the stated complete-graph capacity constant.

The MH and pure cyclic invariant-measure identities were verified directly. The local cyclic orbit has constant Gibbs-weighted rate, and reversing an edge by the rest of its orbit preserves its height. The canonical strip calculation was independently enumerated for all 132 updates of an \(11\times12\), three-spin path: every case of (8.2) agreed, with maximum cyclic height 26 and MH height 24. This is a check of that exact path, not a computational proof of the global minimum barrier.

The lower-barrier arguments, ladder-tree characterization, edge/bulk decomposition, test-function boundary matching, and fixed-volume higher-energy error estimates were read through their appendix proofs. In the bulk, the MH endpoint increments and the cyclic \(K-2\) interpolation produce the indicated harmonic cancellations. The auxiliary path lower bounds for capacity, including the \(K\) edge-disjoint paths in the final proof of \(e_0<2\), were checked by Cauchy–Schwarz. The regular-orbit aggregate cancellation, rather than pointwise zero flux, is the relevant mechanism at the overlap identified in Problem Problem 3 (#prob:p3).

Some historical results cited for general metastability and cycle estimates were not re-proved in their complete original generality. The finite-chain identities actually used above were independently derived, and the cited directed-Ising dependency was checked in its original PDF. The open-boundary extension summarized in Section 3.5 has no complete proof in the reviewed text; no new full open-boundary proof is claimed here. No author program was executed, and no absence of code is counted as a defect.

The assessment is Major for the two Section 5 issues, with the stated local Minor repairs elsewhere. Problem Problem 1 (#prob:p1) proves that the assertions of Theorem 5.2 are mutually inconsistent. Problem Problem 2 (#prob:p2) disproves its extension’s invariant-measure assertion and invalidates the proposed Gibbs-based derivation, while leaving the final numerical assertions of Theorem 5.4 unadjudicated. Neither finding proves failure of the whole paper or of the intended principal MH and purely cyclic conclusions.
