Abstract: The entropy submartingale in Proposition 48 is false: an explicit three-state example has a strictly positive, rigorously certified reversal of the claimed convexity. The variance assertion survives. A different conditional-entropy functional supplies a valid annealing statement under a stronger endpoint hypothesis and repairs the product-endpoint Ising applications. A separate correlation-matrix argument contains an invalid operator-norm step. Several technical inequalities and definitions need corrected constants or hypotheses. The hardcore endpoint citation does not supply the stated entropy implication. These findings concern the exact supplied author version; they do not establish that its final mixing theorems are false. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The kernel and conservation identities that do work

For a localization martingale \(\nu_t\), the kernel \[\nu(dx)P(x,dy)=\mathbb E[\nu_\tau(dx)\nu_\tau(dy)]\] has the stated invariant measure and is reversible. In particular, \[\langle\varphi,P\varphi\rangle_\nu
   =\mathbb E[\nu_\tau(\varphi)^2]\ge0.\] Thus these kernels are positive semidefinite. The variance decomposition proves Proposition 19’s spectral-gap identity. Jensen’s inequality proves its entropy lower bound \[\operatorname{Ent}_\nu f-\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(Pf)\ge \mathbb E[\operatorname{Ent}_{\nu_\tau}f].\] The distinction between a lower bound and an equality is essential later. Functions with zero entropy or variance are omitted from the corresponding quotients; nonnegative functions are handled with \(0\log0=0\), and finite-entropy truncations justify limiting steps.

For a fixed partition \(\Sigma\), the quadratic quantity \[\sum_{A\in\Sigma}\frac{\mu(\varphi\chi_A)^2}{\mu(A)}\] is convex in \(\mu\). Averaging over a partition law independent of \(\mu\) retains convexity. This verifies the variance half of Proposition 48 and the variance annealing mechanism of Theorem 46. At a zero-mass cell the closed convex perspective is assigned the value zero when its numerator also vanishes. This extension is continuous along the cell measures for bounded \(\varphi\), since \(|\mu(\varphi\chi_A)|\le\|\varphi\|_\infty\mu(A)\); it is not jointly continuous for arbitrary numerator and denominator. Bounded stopping times follow directly; extension to a stopping time requires the usual integrability or truncation justification, rather than an unqualified appeal to optional stopping.

The coordinate and Brownian conservation calculations have the expected structure: entropy loss is controlled by the mean displacement of the normalized tilted measure \(d\mu=f\,d\nu/\nu(f)\). Coordinate reveal gives the sum of binary marginal divergences divided by the number of remaining coordinates. For Brownian localization the instantaneous quadratic variation uses the actual noise matrix \(C_t\) on both sides of the covariance. The negative-fields entropy calculation in Appendix A.2 uses the globally valid upper scalar inequality discussed below, so it does not depend on the defective lower inequality.

# A counterexample to entropy submartingality

Problem 1 (Major; Proposition 48, p.33, and Appendix A.1, pp.53–54).  The asserted submartingale \(\int (P^{(t)}f)\log(P^{(t)}f)\,d\nu_t\) fails for a Doob localization with a fixed, measure-independent partition law.

On \(\Omega=\{0,1,2\}\), choose each of \[\Sigma_1=\{\{0,1\},\{2\}\},\qquad
 \Sigma_2=\{\{0\},\{1,2\}\}\] with probability \(1/2\). The associated kernel acts by \(P_\mu f=\frac12\mathbb E_\mu[f\mid\Sigma_1]
+\frac12\mathbb E_\mu[f\mid\Sigma_2]\). Set \[a=(1/5,1/10,7/10),\quad b=(3/5,3/10,1/10),\quad
 m=(a+b)/2=(2/5,1/5,2/5),\quad f=(10,0,1).\] All measure coordinates are strictly positive, and direct rational calculation gives \[\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & (P_\mu f)_0 &(P_\mu f)_1 &(P_\mu f)_2\\ \hline
a&25/3&181/48&15/16\\
b&25/3&83/24&5/8\\
m&25/3&11/3&5/6
\end{array}\] For \(F(\mu)=\sum_i\mu_i(P_\mu f)_i\log(P_\mu f)_i\), the first coordinate cancels in the midpoint difference. Exactly, \[\begin{split}
 F(m)-\tfrac12(F(a)+F(b))
  ={}&\tfrac{11}{15}\log\tfrac{11}{3}
       +\tfrac13\log\tfrac56
       -\tfrac{181}{960}\log\tfrac{181}{48}\\
    &-\tfrac{21}{64}\log\tfrac{15}{16}
       -\tfrac{83}{160}\log\tfrac{83}{24}
       -\tfrac1{32}\log\tfrac58\\
  \in{}&(0.0339892404532605,\,0.0339892404532606).
\end{split}\] This is a strict reversed Jensen inequality. It is not based on floating-point sign detection. Each rational logarithm was reduced by a power of two to \([1,2]\), and evaluated using \[\log q=2\sum_{j=0}^{59}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R,\qquad
 z=\frac{q-1}{q+1},\qquad
 |R|\le\frac{2|z|^{121}}{121(1-z^2)}.\] All interval arithmetic was exact rational arithmetic, followed by outward decimal rounding. The independent script and certificate are retained. Continuity also gives a strictly positive \(f\) example by replacing its zero coordinate with a sufficiently small positive number, if \(\mathbb R_+\) is read as excluding zero.

This midpoint split is an admissible initial localization: start from \(m\), reveal a fair choice of \(a\) or \(b\), and then sample from that conditional law and reveal the resulting point. For the final Doob scheme, first sample \(X\sim\mu\) and choose \(\Sigma\) independently. At time one reveal \(\Sigma\) and its cell containing \(X\); at time two reveal \(X\) itself. The partition law is independent of \(X\) and of the starting measure \(\mu\). Thus the time-one conditional law is precisely \(\mu(\,\cdot\mid A_\Sigma(X))\), giving the displayed kernel. Consequently the example satisfies the structural hypotheses, including eventual localization, and contradicts Proposition 48.

The error on p.53 is the replacement of entropy of an average conditional expectation by average entropy of the conditional expectations. These are generally related by Jensen, not equality. That display also subtracts \(\mu(f)\log\mu(f)\) on only one side; constant \(f\) already detects this normalization error. Removing that subtraction does not cure the midpoint counterexample.

## A valid replacement and its precise scope

Define the average conditional-entropy functional \[D_\mu(f)=\operatorname{Ent}_\mu f-
    \mathbb E_\Sigma\operatorname{Ent}_\mu\!\left(\mathbb E_\mu[f\mid\Sigma]\right).\] The normalization terms cancel, and \[D_\mu(f)=\mu(f\log f)-
 \mathbb E_\Sigma\sum_{A\in\Sigma}\mu(f\chi_A)
             \log\frac{\mu(f\chi_A)}{\mu(A)}.\] The sum is a convex perspective, so \(D_\mu(f)\) is concave in \(\mu\). Also Jensen gives \[\operatorname{Ent}_\mu f-\operatorname{Ent}_\mu(P_\mu f)\ge D_\mu(f).\] It follows that if the endpoint satisfies the stronger inequality \(D_{\nu_a}(f)\ge\delta\operatorname{Ent}_{\nu_a}f\) for every \(f\), then entropy conservation with factor \(\varepsilon\) yields \[\operatorname{Ent}_\nu f-\operatorname{Ent}_\nu(P_\nu f)
 \ge D_\nu(f)\ge\mathbb ED_{\nu_a}(f)
 \ge\delta\varepsilon\operatorname{Ent}_\nu f.\] The printed assumption about the one-step coefficient \(\rho_{\rm LS}(P_{\nu_a})\) does not automatically provide this conditional-entropy inequality. Thus this is a corrected theorem under a changed hypothesis, not a proof of Theorem 47 as written. For product endpoints, tensorization supplies \(D\ge\operatorname{Ent}/n\), which is enough for the Ising applications below.

# The spectral-independence stability argument

Problem 2 (Confirmed invalid step; equation (67), p.45).  A bound on the symmetric correlation matrix is used as though it bounded a similar nonsymmetric matrix in Euclidean operator norm. The resulting displayed inequality is false.

Write \(E=\operatorname{diag}\operatorname{Cov}(\nu)\). The matrices \[E^{-1/2}\operatorname{Cov}(\nu)E^{-1/2},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(\nu)E^{-1}\] have the same eigenvalues, but generally different singular values. For the two-spin law with masses \[\nu(++ )=1/4,\qquad \nu(-+)=3/8,\qquad \nu(--)=3/8,\] one has \[E=\operatorname{diag}(3/4,15/16),\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_{12}=3/8,\qquad
 \alpha=1+1/\sqrt5.\] Every feasible pinning has correlation norm at most \(\alpha\); one free coordinate has correlation norm one, and deterministic coordinates are discarded. Taking the proof’s tilt parameter \(v=h(4/3,16/15)\) and sending \(h\downarrow0\), its equation (67) would imply \[\left\|(7/5,3/2)\right\|^2
 \le 2(1+1/\sqrt5)^2,
 \quad\hbox{that is}\quad
 \frac{421}{100}\le\frac{12}{5}+\frac4{\sqrt5}.\] The reverse inequality is strict. Pin probabilities are \(O(h)\); the integrand divided by \(h\) tends to the displayed diagonal expression on the no-pin event, and the other events contribute only a vanishing correction. This checks the actual process limit, not merely an unrelated matrix example.

No complete replacement of this step under the original all-pinnings correlation hypothesis was established in this review. Strengthening that hypothesis to a Euclidean operator bound on \(\operatorname{Cov}E^{-1}\) repairs the route, but changes the theorem. Accordingly the first assertion of Lemma 68, the first branch of Theorems 42/67, and the part of Theorem 44 relying on that branch need evidence. Their conclusions have not been disproved.

## Two false technical inequalities

Lemma 69’s first conclusion on p.44 is missing a factor two. For the uniform law on \(\{-1,1\}\), its hypothesis holds with \(\varepsilon=1\), whereas \[\tanh^2v=v^2+O(v^4),\qquad
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_v\nu\Vert\nu)=v\tanh v-\log\cosh v
                 =v^2/2+O(v^4).\] The duality proof on p.47 actually proves \(|b(T_v\nu)-b(\nu)|^2\le2\varepsilon D_{\mathrm{KL}}\). This correction alone can be absorbed by the advertised stability constant, provided the preceding mean-shift estimate is proved.

Lemma 71’s lower comparisons (57)–(58), pp.44 and 58–59, fail for positive base mean \(y\). With binary divergence \(H\) and \(\Phi(s)=(1+s)\log(1+s)-s\), taking \(x=-1,y=.99\) gives \[\tfrac12 H(x,y)=\tfrac12\log200>1.99
       =(1+y)\Phi((x-y)/(1+y)).\] For (58), use \(\varepsilon=1,y=1-10^{-4}\), since \(\frac14\log20000>1.9999\). The lower comparisons are valid after restricting \(y\le0\). For \(G=2(1+y)\Phi((x-y)/(1+y))-H(x,y)\), \[G_{xx}=\frac{1-2x}{1-x^2}.\] Convexity up to \(1/2\), followed by concavity, reduces the proof to \(G(y,y)=G_x(y,y)=0\) and \(G(1,y)=3\log(2/(1+y))-2(1-y)\ge3\log2-2>0\). The printed endpoint expression misses the factor two. The scaling inequality used afterward is true, although its negative-\(s\) derivative argument has the wrong sign. For \(s=-r\), use \(\Phi(-r)=\sum_{k\ge2}r^k/[k(k-1)]\in[r^2/2,r^2]\); for \(s\ge0\), use the decreasing second derivative of \(\Phi\). The upper comparison with \(2H\) is globally valid and suffices for Proposition 41’s entropy-decay calculation.

There is also a substantive issue with the intended vector form of Lemma 69’s second conclusion. The printed formula has a free coordinate \(i\), whereas the proof and Theorem 67 require a sum over coordinates. That summed conclusion is false under the printed assumptions. Let \(n=1000\), \(X=Y(1,\ldots,1)\), and \(\Pr(Y=-1)=p=e^{-4n}\). Put \(y=1-2p\), \(a=\sum_i v_i\) and \(m(a)=\mathbb E_{T_v\nu}Y\). Set every \(\varepsilon_i=2\) and \(C=2\). For \(|a|\le n\), \(m'(a)\le4pe^{2n}/(1-p)<2/n\); for \(|a|\ge n\), \(|m(a)-y|\le2\). Hence in both ranges \[a(m(a)-y)\le2a^2/n
       \le\sum_i2v_i^2e^{4|v_i|},
 \qquad 2\le\varepsilon_i\le2(1+y).\] The first hypothesis (54) also holds with \(\varepsilon=n\), because \(\|\operatorname{Cov}(T_v\nu)\|_{\rm OP}\le n\) for every tilt. Nevertheless \(\sum_iH(m(a),y)=nD_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_v\nu\Vert\nu)>384D_{\mathrm{KL}}\) for \(a\ne0\). This is a counterexample to the intended lemma, not to Theorem 67: its correlation parameter here is \(n\), and deterministic pinnings change its additional marginal hypotheses.

## A repair of the second stability branch

The weighted Cauchy–Schwarz argument proving Lemma 68’s second estimate does not use the invalid Euclidean step. It gives the coefficients \(B_i=4\alpha K^3C(1+y_i)\). Reflect coordinates with \(y_i>0\), so their new base means are \(y_i'=-|y_i|\). The corrected scalar argument needs only \(B_i\ge1+y_i'\). For negative increments this ensures that their absolute scaled size is at most one; positive increments do not require that restriction. The hypothesis \(1-y_i\ge1/C\) gives \[\frac{B_i}{1+y_i'}\le4\alpha K^3C(2C-1).\] The corrected duality argument therefore proves \[H(b(T_v\nu),b(\nu))
 \le768\alpha K^3C(2C-1)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_v\nu\Vert\nu).\] This retains the displayed constant when \(C=1\) and gives at most \(1536\alpha K^3C^2\) generally. It does not prove the printed linear-\(C\) constant. The original PDFs on pp.30 and 43 print \(768\alpha K^3C\): \(\alpha\) is linear, not cubed. An initial helper misreading of that exponent was withdrawn after enlarged-source inspection and is not a finding.

Theorem 44 also needs care with its bounded-marginal constants. The claim that \(b\)-marginal boundedness implies the odds condition with \(K=2/b\) is too strong. For \(b=1/2\), take independent uniform \(Y_1,Y_2\) and let \(\Pr(X=+\mid Y_1=Y_2=+)=3/4\), with value \(1/4\) on the other three pairs. Every nontrivial conditional spin probability lies in \([1/4,3/4]\), but the odds for \(X\) increase from \(3/5\) to \(3\), a factor five, exceeding \(2/b=4\). This inference can be bypassed using \[H(x,y)\le\sum_i\frac{(x_i-y_i)^2}{1-y_i^2}
       \le |x-y|^2/b.\] Together with the first advertised stability branch it gives \(16\alpha/b\), within the claimed \(48\alpha/b\) slack. That is a conditional repair because the first branch still requires the missing argument identified above. The proof’s product estimate and its use of \(\log(1-x)\ge-2x\) also need a justification over the full stated range \(\varepsilon>8\alpha/(bn)\); larger sufficient thresholds cannot silently replace that range.

# Quantifiers, normalization and domain repairs

These issues are mostly local or concern overgeneralized preliminary statements; their existence is not evidence against every application.

Fact 13, p.16, needs a simultaneous almost-sure excitation condition. Let \(U\) be a uniformly random line known at time zero in \(\mathbb R^2\), let \(\nu=N(0,I)\), and take \[C_t=P_{U^\perp}+e^{-t}P_U.\] Every \(C_t\) is positive definite. For each fixed deterministic unit vector \(\theta\), almost surely \(\int_0^\infty|C_t\theta|^2dt=\infty\). Nevertheless the Gaussian posterior covariance along \(U\) tends to \((1+\int_0^\infty e^{-2t}dt)^{-1}=2/3\); localization fails. Require a common full-probability event on which the excitation diverges in every direction, equivalently here divergence of the least eigenvalue of the integrated information matrix. The fixed identity and fixed positive-definite controls used in the applications satisfy the stronger condition.

The Brownian calculation in Section 3.1.1, p.24, confuses \(C_t\) with its square root and loses a squared norm. A one-dimensional test is decisive: for \(\nu=N(0,1)\), \(C_t=c>1\) and \(\varphi(x)=x\), the actual variance is \((1+c^2t)^{-1}\). Equation (20)’s printed rate instead yields the larger claimed lower bound \((1+c^2t)^{-1/c}\). The correct covariance rate is \(\|C_t\operatorname{Cov}(\nu_t)C_t\|_{\rm OP}\). Even after that correction, the passage from a random integrating factor to the particular reciprocal-expectation bound is not established by a generic drift inequality alone. No counterexample to that corrected SDE-specific assertion was found. A deterministic upper envelope proves the bounds needed in the applications and should be stated explicitly, also in Proposition 39.

Proposition 18, pp.18 and 55–56, overstates uniqueness without specifying its generator. When \(v\equiv0\) and \(\nu\) is uniform on two points, both the constant process and a process that reveals a random sign at an independent exponential time are martingales with the printed zero conditional mean increment. The latter’s reveal is a pinning. Specify that an unpinned coordinate jumps only to \(-\operatorname{sign}v_i'\), with the rates given in Appendix A.2, and exclude additional jumps. The driving curve also needs locally integrable variation, for example \(C^1\) regularity: the differentiable curve \(v(t)=t^2\sin(t^{-4})\), \(v(0)=0\), has nonintegrable \(|v'|\) near zero, so the displayed hazard gives a first jump at zero rather than the asserted strictly later time. All actual straight-line applications meet the corrected regularity.

The sentence asserting Dirac convergence under every pinning process also fails: the uniform measure on \(\{(+,-),(-,+)\}\) is unchanged by the tilt \(v(t)=(t,t)\) with no pinnings. For the specified generator, convergence follows instead from the bounded measure martingale and the fact that a nondegenerate limiting marginal, together with diverging driving variation, would have infinite integrated pinning hazard. This is a construction scope repair, not a finding that the intended negative-fields localization does not exist.

The maximum-entropy principle on p.25 needs its actual domain. Fact 30 is false for arbitrary real-valued \(g\). For masses \(9/20,1/10,9/20\) on \(-1,0,1\) and \(g\equiv-1\), its two infima are \(-\log10\) and \(-\log(20/9)\). At a boundary mean, the minimizing measure also need not be a finite exponential tilt. Lemma 31, with no continuity condition on its nonnegative cost, is false: for the uniform two-point law, let \(\psi(x,y)=\chi_{\{x=1,y=0\}}\). Every finite tilt has zero cost, so any \(\alpha>0\) satisfies Definition 29; but \(\mu=\delta_1,\alpha=1/2\) violates Lemma 31. For the actual finite-support continuous quadratic and binary-divergence costs, minimize entropy at relative-interior means and approximate \(\mu\) by \((1-t)\mu+t\nu\). Continuity, or appropriate lower semicontinuity, then passes to the boundary. Degenerate affine directions must first be removed. Lemma 33’s assumption of invertible covariance alone does not ensure that all exponential tilts exist: a symmetric heavy-tailed law with finite nonzero variance is an immediate obstruction. Compact support, or appropriate exponential-integrability and Legendre-domain hypotheses, restores the uses made here.

The influence convention needs one consistent factor. For \(\{-1,1\}\)-valued coordinates, \[\mathbb E[X_i\mid X_j=1]-\mathbb E[X_i\mid X_j=-1]
      =\frac{2\operatorname{Cov}_{ij}}{1-b_j^2}.\] Thus (17) has diagonal two and is twice the matrix used on p.9, in Fact 23 and in Section 6. Inserting \(1/2\) in (17) yields the intended covariance convention. This factor is distinct from the invalid similarity-to-operator-norm inference. The Appendix A proof of Lemma 36 likewise needs \(\operatorname{diag}\int xx^{\top}d\nu=I\) and \(\operatorname{diag}\operatorname{Cov}=\operatorname{diag}(1-x_i^2)\); the full matrix identities printed there are false. With those diagonal corrections, the inverse-Hessian comparison and the continuous-cost limiting argument give the intended all-tilts implication.

Several readily identifiable notational corrections remain. The halfspace example on p.11 must continue bisection rather than freeze at time \(n\). Definition 11’s \(\ell^1\)-distance on the sign cube is two, not one. The outer expectation in the Doob definition on p.14 would make \(\nu_t\) constant and must be removed. The coordinate construction needs a consistent starting index and the opposite sign for the killed face. In Proposition 16 the Gaussian center is \(y_t/t\), not \(y_t\). Equation (9) needs denominator \(d\nu_t\), and the Brownian small-time remainder must be understood through Itô/conditional drift estimates. Proposition 21’s conditioning argument works recursively with nonnegative factors; unconstrained negative factors cannot be multiplied through lower bounds. Proposition 41 requires nonnegative \(f\) and the normalized measure \(f\nu/\nu(f)\). In Section 4 the concatenated time is \(t-a\), and the variance proof integrates the conditional quantity against \(\nu_a\). Fact 74 requires a positive denominator for its literal ratios. For a nonnegative denominator use the closed lower-semicontinuous convex perspectives: assign value zero at \((N,M)=(0,0)\) and \(+\infty\) at \(M=0,N\ne0\), with \(N\ge0\) in the entropy case. Integrability is additionally needed for an ordinary finite-valued submartingale; bounded tests and appropriate truncations suffice in the intended applications. These extensions are not jointly continuous at the origin: \(N=t,M=t^2\) gives \(N^2/M=1\), while \(N=t,M=t e^{-1/t}\) gives \(N\log(N/M)=1\). Fact 2’s gap-to-total-variation assertion requires a nonnegative spectrum or an absolute spectral gap: the deterministic two-state flip is a counterexample otherwise. The actual localization kernels have the needed positivity, as proved above.

# Applications: retained bounds and remaining obligations

## Ising models

The general Theorem 49 inherits Theorem 47’s unresolved entropy hypothesis. Its product-endpoint specialization is supported by the corrected conditional-entropy argument. With \(C_t=(2J)^{1/2}\), the quadratic interaction decreases to zero at time one. Deterministic covariance envelopes give the entropy-conservation factor, and product tensorization supplies \(D\ge\operatorname{Ent}/n\) at the endpoint. This establishes the route used by Corollaries 51 and 54 without the false Proposition 48.

Lemma 50’s covariance conclusion can also be retained with a replacement proof. For \(0<J<I/2\), introduce a joint pair \((\sigma,X)\) with density proportional to \[\exp\{-\tfrac12 a^2X^{\top}(2J)^{-1}X
                  +(v+aX)\cdot\sigma\}.\] The spin marginal is the intended Ising law and \(X\mid\sigma=N(2J\sigma/a,\,2J/a^2)\). Consequently \[a^2\operatorname{Cov}X=2J+4J\operatorname{Cov}(\nu)J.\] The marginal potential of \(X\) has Hessian at least \(a^2((2J)^{-1}-I)\). The Brascamp–Lieb covariance estimate, after a linear change of variables, bounds \(\operatorname{Cov}X\) by its inverse. Substitution and conjugation by \((2J)^{-1}\) give \[\operatorname{Cov}(\nu)\preceq(I-2J)^{-1}
       \preceq(1-2\|J\|_{\rm OP})^{-1}I.\] This avoids the unjustified claim on p.61 that a componentwise nonlinear contraction necessarily contracts covariance in Loewner order. The missing external field in the marginal normalizer and the extra factor in the p.60 exponent are local corrections. Integrating this bound yields Corollary 51’s \((1-2\|J\|_{\rm OP})/n\); its mixing constant depends on the distance from \(1/2\).

The ferromagnetic susceptibility reduction additionally uses the stated external covariance comparison [dss] and nonnegativity, then entrywise comparison with a nonnegative matrix. That theorem is a named dependency, not independently reproved here. The localization integration and product endpoint are consistent with Corollary 54.

For graphical Ising, the identities on p.37 require repair. The matrix \((D+A)/2\) does not encode the displayed edge-agreement energy with the asserted factor; it is not generally positive definite, nor equal to the claimed normalized-Laplacian expression. Here \(D\) and \(A\) are the degree and adjacency matrices. For either sign of the interaction parameter \(\beta\), use \[J=|\beta|(D\mathbin{\pm}A)/4+I/8,\] with the matching sign. It is strictly positive definite and encodes the original energy up to a constant. The interpolating interaction is \((1-t)\beta\), so uniqueness is preserved.

The external influence bound must also be translated with its zero-diagonal probability convention. Definition 1.2 and Lemma 8.3 of [cfyz] directly give the sufficient covariance bound \(1+2/\delta\); it is unnecessary to identify their matrix literally with this manuscript’s inconsistent \(\Psi\). Since \(\|J\|\le|\beta|\Delta/2+1/8\), uniqueness gives \[2\|J\|(\delta+2)
 \le (|\beta|\Delta+1/4)(\delta+2)
 <6\log3+3/4<8.\] For the middle estimate, the function \((\delta+2)\log((\Delta-\delta)/(\Delta-2+\delta))\) decreases in \(\delta\), and \(\Delta\log(\Delta/(\Delta-2))\le3\log3\) for \(\Delta\ge3\). Thus the product-endpoint argument still yields \(e^{-8/\delta}/n\), the constant stated in Corollary 56, using only the checked weaker covariance conversion. The case \(\beta=0\) is a product measure.

## The restricted Gaussian oracle

Theorem 58 does not require entropy annealing. With \(C_t=I\), strong convexity gives \(\|\operatorname{Cov}(\nu_t)\|\le(\mu+t)^{-1}\); Proposition 39 and Proposition 19 then give \[\rho_{\rm LS}(P)\ge
   \exp\!\left(-\int_0^{1/\eta}\frac{dt}{\mu+t}\right)
   =\frac{\mu}{\mu+1/\eta}.\] The Gaussian conditional center correction above is needed in the identification of the oracle. The following step-count prose on p.38 has the factor reversed. Put \(a=\eta\mu\). For an initial density bounded by \(\beta\ge1\), entropy contraction and Pinsker give \[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho_k\Vert\nu)\le(1+a)^{-k}\log\beta,\qquad
 \|\rho_k-\nu\|_{\rm TV}
 \le\sqrt{\tfrac12(1+a)^{-k}\log\beta}.\] Consequently, when \(0<a<1\), a sufficient number of steps is \[O\!\left(a^{-1}
 \{\log\max(1,\log\beta)+\log(1/\epsilon)+1\}\right).\] For the source’s \(O(\exp n)\)-warm starts this is \(O((\eta\mu)^{-1}(\log n+\log(1/\epsilon)+1))\). The theorem’s coefficient itself is correct.

## Negative fields and hardcore

Problem 3 (Confirmed proof defects; Theorem 60 and Lemma 66, pp.39–43).  The general duration is omitted from entropy conservation, and the cited endpoint result controls a different entropy functional.

Theorem 60 permits arbitrary \(s>0\), but its integration drops that duration. This cannot be a valid uniform conservation assertion. For one uniform sign, \(v(t)=-t\) and \(f=\chi_{\{+\}}\), the no-pin probability is \((1+e^{-2t})/2\); on that event the positive probability is \((1+e^{2t})^{-1}\). Therefore \[\mathbb E\operatorname{Ent}_{\nu_t}f
     =\tfrac12e^{-2t}\log(1+e^{2t})\longrightarrow0.\] The spectral and tameness parameters can be kept bounded uniformly in this example. Correct integration includes \(s\) in the exponent. This refutes the proof’s conservation assertion, not the final theorem’s conclusion by itself.

With the repaired second stability branch, \(C=K\) gives a sufficient general exponent of order \(sK^5\eta\), whereas the printed \(K^4\eta\) dependence has not been recovered. For the hardcore application \(s=2\) and \(K\) is an absolute constant, so these two changes alone would preserve the form \(e^{c/\delta}\). They do not settle the endpoint issue.

Lemma 66 claims the manuscript’s one-step coefficient is at least \(1/(4n)\), citing Proposition 51 of [ajk] and Fact 3.5 of [clv]. The former uses the Dirichlet/continuous-time MLSI constant. The latter assumes approximate tensorization and derives both that constant and one-step entropy contraction as separate consequences; it does not infer tensorization or one-step contraction from a Dirichlet MLSI. The underlying Ricci result [ehmt] likewise concerns continuous time. The distinction is explicit in [ajk]’s discussion of the easier measure. The cited implication is therefore incomplete. A direct proof of the appropriate endpoint conditional-entropy inequality, or another valid annealing mechanism, is needed. Neither Lemma 66 nor the final hardcore mixing bound is declared false on this basis.

The combinatorial marginal estimates in Lemma 64 work for a free vertex \(u_v=0\). ## Uniform starting states

The repeated appeal to Fact 2 alone leaves an apparent dependence on the smallest stationary mass, which may be arbitrarily small with external fields or fugacity. It can be removed without asserting that the mixing conclusions are false.

For hardcore, condition on a fixed scan sequence that updates every vertex at least once. For a specified final independent set \(x\), its occupied vertices must all succeed at their last updates, each with conditional probability at most \(q=\lambda/(1+\lambda)\). Order these last-update events chronologically and use iterated conditional expectations. Thus the output law satisfies \(\mu(x)\le q^{|x|}\), and \[\mu(x)/\nu(x)\le Z/(1+\lambda)^{|x|}
       \le Z\le(1+\lambda)^n\le5^n\] in the stated uniqueness range. Random scan fails to cover all vertices by \(n\log(2n/\epsilon)\) steps with probability at most \(\epsilon/2\). Conditioning on coverage changes the scan law, not the update randomness. The conditional output has relative entropy at most \(n\log5\); any subsequently justified \(c_\delta/n\) entropy contraction gives the desired order of mixing independently of tiny \(\lambda\).

For Ising, compare each last-update probability with its external-field-only product marginal \(p_i\); their ratio is at most \(\exp(4\sum_{j\ne i}|J_{ij}|)\). Multiplication over chronological last updates and comparison of the target with that product measure give warmness at most \[\exp\{4\sum_{i\ne j}|J_{ij}|+n\|J\|_{\rm OP}\}
       \le\exp\{C\|J\|_{\rm OP}n^{3/2}\}.\] The logarithm of its relative-entropy bound is \(O(\log n)\) for bounded interaction norm. This supplies the needed warmup uniformly in arbitrary external fields, followed by the supported Ising entropy contraction. This argument repairs a uniformity omission; it does not repair an unproved contraction coefficient.
