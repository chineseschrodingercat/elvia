Abstract: This review concerns Gerber and Kunde’s 82-page arXiv manuscript 2109.06086v2, dated 9 December 2024. A complete construction shows that the fixed-strip analytic diffeomorphism space defined in Section 10.1 is nonseparable in the stated metric. An ergodic modification also makes the relation itself nonseparable, disproving the analytic classification in Theorem 27 under the manuscript’s definition. This does not refute the non-Borel reduction, the measurable theorem, or the smooth theorem. Two local corrections concern a matching inequality and the convention for continuous analytic completeness. The publisher version has not been textually compared. Independent scientific acceptance and human review remain pending.\par

\section*{Major: the declared analytic ambient space is not Polish}
\subsection*{Precise location and scope}
Section 10.1, p. 68, defines \(C^\omega_\rho(\mathbb T^2)\) using bounded holomorphic extension to the open strip \(\Omega_\rho=\{|\operatorname{Im}z_1|,|\operatorname{Im}z_2|<\rho\}\), with its supremum norm. Both a diffeomorphism and its inverse must have such displacement functions. The metric \(d_\rho\) takes the maximum of their displacement distances, allowing integer changes of lift. The paragraph then infers that this space is Polish from completeness. The following family satisfies all these stated conditions and disproves separability.\par
\subsection*{Complete separated-family construction}
Fix any \(\rho>0\), put \(R=e^{2\pi\rho}>1\), and choose an integer \(N\ge1\) such that \(R^{-2N}\le1/4\). For \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\) and \(|\operatorname{Im}z|<\rho\), define \[\begin{aligned}
 u(z)&=R^{-1}e^{2\pi iz},\\
 H_\theta(z)&=u(z)^N
 \exp\left(-\frac{e^{i\theta}+u(z)}{e^{i\theta}-u(z)}\right),\\
 h_\theta(z)&=\frac{H_\theta(z)+\overline{H_\theta(\bar z)}}{16}.\end{aligned}\] Here \(|u|<1\), and \[\operatorname{Re}\frac{e^{i\theta}+u}{e^{i\theta}-u}
 =\frac{1-|u|^2}{|e^{i\theta}-u|^2}>0.\] Consequently \(H_\theta\) is holomorphic, one-periodic and bounded by \(1\). The reflected term is holomorphic as well. The function \(h_\theta\) is real-valued on the real axis, is one-periodic, and satisfies \(\|h_\theta\|_\rho\le1/8\).\par
Consider the torus shear \[F_\theta(x,y)=(x+h_\theta(y),y)\pmod{\mathbb Z^2}.\] It is a real-analytic diffeomorphism, preserves area since its Jacobian determinant is \(1\), and is homotopic to the identity through the shears with displacement \(t h_\theta\). Its inverse is \((x,y)\mapsto(x-h_\theta(y),y)\). Both displacement functions have exactly the required bounded holomorphic extensions on \(\Omega_\rho\).\par
For \(\theta\ne\phi\), approach the lower strip boundary radially, choosing \(z_r=\theta/(2\pi)-i(2\pi)^{-1}\log(Rr)\) as \(r\uparrow1\). Then \(u(z_r)=r e^{i\theta}\). Thus \[H_\theta(z_r)\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 |H_\phi(z_r)|\longrightarrow1 .\] Indeed, the exponent for \(\theta\) is \(-(1+r)/(1-r)\), whereas for \(\phi\ne\theta\) the boundary quotient has real part zero. On the reflected approach, \(|u(\bar z_r)|\to R^{-2}\), so the difference of the two reflected terms has limiting superior at most \(2R^{-2N}\). The reverse triangle inequality gives \[\|h_\theta-h_\phi\|_\rho
 \ge \frac{1-2R^{-2N}}{16}\ge\frac1{32}.\] Also \(\|h_\theta-h_\phi\|_\rho\le1/4\). Every nonzero integer change of lift therefore has norm at least \(3/4\). Taking the infimum over lifts does not remove the lower bound: \[d_\rho(F_\theta,F_\phi)\ge1/32 .\] A separable metric space has no uncountable uniformly separated subset: balls of radius \(1/100\) about these points are pairwise disjoint, and each would contain a different point of a countable dense set. The stated ambient space is therefore nonseparable, hence not Polish.\par
\subsection*{The relation itself is nonseparable}
The defect also affects the ergodic relation, not only its ambient space. Put \(\beta=\sqrt2\bmod1\) and \(\alpha=\sqrt3\bmod1\), and replace the shears by \[E_\theta(x,y)=(x+\alpha+h_\theta(y),\,y+\beta).\] Their inverse displacements are \(-\alpha-h_\theta(y-\beta)\) and \(-\beta\); hence they have all the required extensions and the same separation bound \(d_\rho(E_\theta,E_\phi)\ge1/32\).\par
Since \(H_\theta\) has a Taylor series in \(u\) with zero constant term, \(h_\theta\) has zero mean. Write its Fourier coefficients as \(a_{\theta,k}\). The disk bound gives \(|a_{\theta,k}|\le R^{-|k|}/16\) for \(k\ne0\). For a nearest integer \(m\) to \(k\sqrt2\), \(k\ge1\), the nonzero integer \(2k^2-m^2\) yields \[\operatorname{dist}(k\sqrt2,\mathbb Z)\ge(4k)^{-1},
 \qquad |e^{2\pi ik\beta}-1|\ge k^{-1}.\] The same estimates hold with \(|k|\) for negative \(k\). Thus the Fourier coefficients \[b_{\theta,k}=\frac{a_{\theta,k}}{e^{2\pi ik\beta}-1}\quad(k\ne0),
 \qquad b_{\theta,0}=0\] define a real-valued real-analytic periodic function \(g_\theta\) on every narrower strip. It satisfies \(g_\theta(y+\beta)-g_\theta(y)=h_\theta(y)\). The area-preserving shear \(G_\theta(x,y)=(x+g_\theta(y),y)\) therefore obeys \(G_\theta R_{(\alpha,\beta)}G_\theta^{-1}=E_\theta\). Rational independence of \(1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3\) makes this translation ergodic; it follows, for example, by testing invariant functions on Fourier characters. Hence every \(E_\theta\) is ergodic.\par
The relation in Theorem 27 (p. 19) contains every diagonal pair \((E_\theta,E_\theta)\), an uncountable uniformly separated family. It is a metric subspace and is therefore nonseparable. Every Borel subset of a Polish space is separable and second countable, and its continuous image is separable. The relation cannot be analytic under Definition 16, which defines analyticity by such a continuous image. It consequently cannot be complete analytic as stated.\par
\subsection*{Impact and limited repair}
This is a Major defect in the real-analytic conclusion of Theorem 27 for the specified topology. The construction checks ergodicity as well as every membership requirement, so it does not rely on a nonergodic counterexample to a statement restricted to ergodic maps.\par
The continuous-reduction argument for non-Borelness can still work in a non-Polish target: the inverse image of a Borel set under a continuous map is Borel. Accordingly this finding does not refute that conclusion, the measurable result, or the smooth result. The complete-analytic formulation itself is false for this metric relation; recovering it requires a changed category or topology.\par
A limited correction is to state the non-Borel conclusion in the stated topology, subject to its existing reduction proof. To retain a Polish complete-analytic formulation, one must specify a separable complete analytic category, prove its relevant descriptive properties, and verify that the realization and its continuity land there. A closed-strip continuous-extension category, or an appropriate separable closure of the realization class, is a possible route; these are repair obligations, not a fully verified replacement theorem in this review.\par
\section*{Minor: matching size versus matching deficit}
In Lemma 14, p. 12, cases (I) and (II), and p. 13, Case 1, the printed implication replaces the factor \(1-\alpha\) by \(\alpha\). For a best match of size \(L(C,D)\), \[\overline f(C,D)=1-\frac{2L(C,D)}{|C|+|D|}.\] Thus \(\overline f(C,D)\ge\alpha\) implies, for every match \(K\), \[2|K|\le(1-\alpha)(|C|+|D|),
 \qquad |C|+|D|-2|K|\ge\alpha(|C|+|D|).\] If the premise is strict, the appropriate upper bound is strict. For example, \(C=0000\), \(D=0001\), and \(\alpha=1/10\) give \(\overline f(C,D)=1/4>\alpha\) and a best match of size \(3\); the printed bound \(6<\alpha\cdot8\) fails. This example tests that local implication, not all hypotheses of Lemma 14.\par
The final deficit estimate on p. 16 uses precisely the corrected form. There are at most \(2n\) short pairs, each of total length less than \(7L/(3R)\); hence their contribution to the discarded length is at most \(14Ln/(3R)\). Since \(n\le m\), this is at most \(3L(n+m)/R\). The resulting normalized estimate has loss \(3\alpha/R<1/R\) because \(\alpha<1/7\). Replacing the three matching-size inequalities and respecting non-strict premises restores this local step. No counterexample to Lemma 14 or its intended separation bound was found.\par
\section*{Minor: convention for complete analyticity}
Definition 17, p. 16, says that every analytic set can be continuously reduced to the target, without restricting the source space. Fact 20, p. 18, then calls ill-founded trees complete analytic under this wording. Take the analytic subset \(\{0\}\) of the connected Polish space \([0,1]\). Every continuous map from \([0,1]\) into the zero-dimensional tree space has connected, hence singleton, image. It cannot reduce \(\{0\}\) to ill-foundedness. This proves that the unrestricted wording is too strong.\par
The standard usable correction is to restrict the source spaces of the continuous-reduction definition to zero-dimensional Polish spaces, or to use Borel reductions for arbitrary standard Borel spaces. The reduction and the non-Borel argument on p. 18 survive either conventional correction. This is a definition/scope correction, not a counterexample to the measurable anti-classification result.\par
