Abstract: The assessment of the exact acquired author version is Major. A missing factor of eight makes a central comparison lemma false on an effective primitive half-fiber. A general weak Jacobi transformation lemma used in the geometric modularity argument has an explicit counterexample. The exponential rewriting in the Vafa–Witten proof also has the wrong sign, contradicted by an actual primitive pair moduli space. These findings concern essential derivations; none is presented as a counterexample to the intended main enumerative formulas. Explicit repairs are given for the normalization and sign errors, while the weak Jacobi issue requires a stronger input theorem. Two further substantive local corrections, two notation clarifications, and a conditional degeneration-normalization check are separated from these core findings. All 58 physical source pages were read. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The normalization in the central comparison

Problem 1 (Major: equation (6.2) makes Lemma 6.5 false).  Equation (6.2), p. 41, omits the factor \(8\) present in the large \(F^{\mathrm{KM}}\) series and in Proposition 5.16. With the definitions actually printed, Lemma 6.5 on p. 43 has left side \(7\) and right side \(0\) for an effective primitive half-fiber.

Let \(f\) be the effective primitive isotropic half-fiber of Section 4.1, and put \(\beta=f\). Thus \(\beta^2=0\) and \(\operatorname{div}(\beta)=1\). Write \[R(z,\tau)=
 \frac{\Theta(z,2\tau)^2\eta(2\tau)^8}
      {\Theta(z,\tau)^2\eta(\tau)^{16}}.\] The two theta functions have the same leading term at \(q=0\), and the eta quotient has valuation \((2\cdot8-16)/24=0\) and leading coefficient \(1\). Hence \[[R]_{q^0}=1,\qquad f_f^{\mathrm{KM}}=1
 \quad\hbox{under the printed definition (6.2).}\] This also agrees with the calculation of \(\omega_g(0)\) on p. 43.

For every \(v=(r,f,n)\) the relevant divisibility is \(m(v)=\gcd(r,1,2n)=1\). The odd-cover transformation is therefore trivial, and Proposition 5.10 gives \[\mathop{\mathrm{dt}}(r,f,n)=\mathop{\mathrm{DT}}(r,f,n)
 =8[\eta^{-12}]_{q^{(-r^2-2rn)/2}}.\] Consequently every \(\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{dt}}}(r,f,n)\) defined on p. 42 is zero. In equation (5.5), p. 38, the \(r=0\) terms vanish. Among \(r\ge1\), \(n\ge0\), the inequality \(-r^2-2rn\ge-1\) holds only for \((r,n)=(1,0)\), since \(\eta^{-12}\) begins with \(q^{-1/2}\). The only contribution is thus \(8\), giving \[f_f^{\mathrm{PT}}=f_f^{\mathrm{GW}}=8,\qquad
 \widehat f_f=8-1=7.\] The GW value can also be checked from the two elliptic half-fibers and the fourfold comparison with the threefold. The right side of Lemma 6.5 is a linear combination of the identically zero \(\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{dt}}}\), so the printed lemma asserts \(7=0\) on this actual allowed class. No zero class or hypothetical DT data are involved.

This discrepancy matters to the proof. Lemma 6.5 transfers the low-genus vanishing of the large difference series to the DT recurrence in the divisibility-two case. The coefficient identification on p. 44 likewise uses the incorrectly normalized small difference. The proof as printed cannot make that transfer. The Major classification reflects this essential dependence, although the normalization has a short repair.

## Repair and a separate coefficient check

Define \(f_\beta^{\mathrm{KM}}=8[R]_{q^{\beta^2/2}}\). Insert the same \(8\) in the expansion of \(f^{\mathrm{KM}}\) in the proof of Lemma 6.5; retain the already correct \(8\) in \(F^{\mathrm{KM}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{dt}}}\). Then subtraction of (5.5) gives the intended lemma, and the primitive example has \(\widehat f_f=0\). The closing sentence of the proof of Proposition 5.16, p. 41, also needs its omitted \(8\) restored.

A second exact algebra check is needed on p. 45. The constant term in Lemma 6.5 contains \(r\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{dt}}}(r,\beta_d,0)\). Substitution \(r=2u\) gives \[-\sum_{u>0}2u(-1)^{u-1}a(d-u^2/2),\] where the displayed formula on p. 45 omits \(2u\). After lower terms vanish by induction, the two correct equations are \[0=-\frac14a(d)-2a(d-\tfrac12),
 \qquad 0=\frac18a(d).\] Their coefficient matrix has determinant \(1/4\), so the intended elimination still works. This is a coefficient correction, not an assertion that an arbitrary nonzero sequence \(a\) is an actual DT sequence. Similarly, the odd-\(n\) sum in the proof of Lemma 5.11, p. 36, should use \[\sum_{\substack{n>0\\n\ {\rm odd}}}np^n
   =\frac{p(1+p^2)}{(1-p^2)^2}.\] Restoring the square makes the subsequent pole-cancellation argument and the stated final rational function consistent.

# Weak Jacobi forms and the geometric modularity step

Problem 2 (Major: Lemma 2.6 does not preserve the stated cusp condition).  Definition 2.3(ii), p. 8, requires nonnegative Fourier exponents at every cusp. Footnote 8 explicitly uses the weak Jacobi convention. Under exactly those definitions, Lemma 2.6, p. 9, is false. The proof through Lemma A.7, pp. 50–52, overlooks the effect of elliptic translation on this condition.

Let \(\Theta_E=\Theta_{E_8}(x,\tau)\), of weight \(4\) and index \(Q_{E_8}/2\), and let \(E_4=240G_4\), with constant term \(1\). Consider the ordinary full-level Jacobi Hecke transform \[B(x,\tau)=8\Theta_E(2x,2\tau)
 +\frac12\bigl(\Theta_E(x,\tau/2)
                  +\Theta_E(x,(\tau+1)/2)\bigr).\] Its finite-coset slash construction gives weight \(4\) and index \(Q_{E_8}\). This uses a regular Jacobi input and does not depend on the defective Laurent truncation discussed below. The only \(q^0\) coefficient of \(B\) is \(9\). Therefore \[F(x,\tau)=\frac{E_4(\tau)B(x,\tau)-9\Theta_E(x,\tau)^2}
                  {\Delta(\tau)}\] is an actual weak Jacobi form for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\ltimes(\mathbb Z^8\oplus\mathbb Z^8)\), of weight \(-4\) and index \(Q_{E_8}\). The numerator’s entire \(q^0\) coefficient vanishes, \(\Delta\) has no zeros in the upper half-plane, and the level-one group has one cusp. These verify holomorphy in \(x,\tau\) and every weak cusp hypothesis of the lemma with \(m=1\).

Choose \(\alpha_0\in E_8\) of norm \(4\); for example, in the standard Euclidean realization one can take \(\alpha_0=(2,0,\ldots,0)\). Then \[[q^1\zeta^{-\alpha_0}]B=1,\qquad
 [q^1\zeta^{-\alpha_0}]\Theta_E^2=0.\] Indeed, the averaged two summands in \(B\) contribute the norm-four lattice vector once; the first summand would require \(\alpha_0/2\) of norm one in the even lattice. At degree one, \(\Theta_E^2\) has only zero or norm-two charges. Thus \([q^0\zeta^{-\alpha_0}]F=1\).

Since \(F\) is a Jacobi form, its elliptic anomaly derivatives vanish. The actual operator of Lemma 2.6 is therefore \[M_{\alpha_0,1}(F)
   =q^2\zeta^{\alpha_0}F(x+\alpha_0\tau,2\tau),
 \qquad
 [q^{-2}\zeta^0]M_{\alpha_0,1}(F)=1.\] Output charge zero forces input charge \(-\alpha_0\), and the specified output exponent then forces input exponent zero. There can be no cancellation. This violates the claimed weak cusp condition at infinity and is a full-hypothesis counterexample to the stated lemma.

The same failure occurs already in Lemma A.7: take the usual weak form \(\phi_{-2,1}(x/2,\tau)\), index \(1/4\), with lattice \(2\mathbb Z\oplus2\mathbb Z\), and translate by \((1,0)\). Its transformed \(q^{-1/4}\) coefficient is nonzero. The formal transformation law is not the disputed step; cusp growth is.

## Actual dependence and limits of a repair

Page 24 explicitly applies this lemma to \(\Delta^{-m}\mathop{\mathrm{QJac}}_{mQ_{E_8}}\) and deduces Corollary 4.7. That corollary supplies the modularity and pole bound in Theorem 4.3, used again in Proposition 6.3 and the main divisibility induction. A false general preservation assertion does not justify that step. This is the basis for the Major assessment. The example above is not claimed to be a relative GW series, and no counterexample to the intended geometric formula has been established.

A precise sufficient repair can be stated. Let \(\Phi\) be the almost-holomorphic completion of the numerator \(H\), weight \(w\), index \(mQ_{E_8}\), \(m>0\). Require each of its coefficient functions in the nonholomorphic variables to have support \[n\ge\frac{\|\alpha\|^2}{4m}.\] At infinity the translated exponent then satisfies \[2n+\alpha\cdot\alpha_0+2m
       \ge\frac{\|\alpha+m\alpha_0\|^2}{2m}\ge0.\] At the other cusp, direct substitution in the slash operator, with \(S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), gives for the transformed completion \(G\) \[G|_{w,mQ_{E_8}/2}S
     =2^{-w}\Phi\left(\frac{x+\alpha_0}{2},\frac{\tau}{2}\right).\] Thus cusp zero has no new negative powers. Under this stronger hypothesis the denominator \(\Delta(2\tau)^m\) remains sufficient.

There is a plausible geometric route, but it requires proof for the actual inputs. On a generic rational elliptic surface, for a horizontal irreducible curve \(C\) with \(d=F_{\rm fib}\cdot C>0\) and \(E_8\) charge \(\alpha\), adjunction gives \(C^2\ge d-2\). With \(W=B+F_{\rm fib}/2\), \[W\cdot C\ge\frac{\|\alpha\|^2}{2d}+\frac12-\frac1d
           \ge\frac{\|\alpha\|^2}{2d}-\frac d2.\] Adding effective components and using Cauchy–Schwarz gives, at total degree \(2m\), \(W\cdot\beta\ge\|\alpha\|^2/(4m)-m\). This supplies the desired bound for the holomorphic numerator \(\Delta^m F_R\). It does not by itself establish it for all iterated \(G_2\) and elliptic anomaly coefficients, including the relative and rubber terms. That additional step remains unverified here. Merely enlarging the allowed pole order also requires rechecking later modular arguments.

# The pair-invariant exponential

Problem 3 (Major: the proof of Theorem 7.2 reverses a nonzero pair count).  Equation (7.1), p. 47, contains \((-1)^\ell/\ell!\). The exponential at the bottom of that page and the one at the top of p. 48 both have a positive sign in the exponent. They must both have a negative sign. This error is distinct from the v3 correction to Theorem 5.14.

Put \(A_\delta=(-1)^{\chi(\delta v(n))}
\chi(\delta v(n))\mathop{\mathrm{DT}}(\delta v)\). Expanding the ordered products in (7.1) gives \[f^Q_{v,n}(t)=
 \exp\left(-\sum_{\delta>0} A_\delta t^\delta\right)
      +O(t^{1+}),\] rather than the printed positive exponential. There is no corresponding sign change in the definition of \(t\) or of \(f^Q\).

An actual geometric example removes any dependence on a purely formal test sequence. Let \(v=(1,0,0)=\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal O_Y)\) and use the generic polarization \(H\) of Section 7. Choose a sufficiently large even twist \(n\) and set \[N=\chi(H^n)=1+\frac{n^2H^2}{2}.\] Then \(N\) is positive and odd, and Serre vanishing gives \(h^0(H^n)=h^0(\omega_Y\otimes H^n)=N\). On each of the four Enriques sections of \(Q\) there are the two torsion line bundles \(\mathcal O_Y,\omega_Y\). They give exactly eight stable sheaves in this class, as also computed on p. 36. A K3 fiber has twice the primitive fiber class and cannot contribute. Each sheaf is rigid: \(H^1(\mathcal O_Y)=H^0(\omega_Y)=0\) makes its \(\operatorname{Ext}^1\) on \(Q\) zero. The pair space is therefore eight copies of \(\mathbb P^{N-1}\), with no strictly semistable rank-one decomposition.

The symmetric obstruction theory gives virtual degree \((-1)^{N-1}N\) for each smooth projective space. Hence \[P_v^Q(n)=8(-1)^{N-1}N=8N.\] Equation (7.1) gives this value. The printed positive exponential instead gives \(8(-1)^N N=-8N\) for the coefficient of \(t\). No fractional rank class with \(0<\delta<1\) can obscure this coefficient.

This false rewriting is the bridge to the asserted Vafa–Witten formula. Comparing its positive exponent with the negative expansion in Conjecture B would reverse the sign of \(\mathop{\mathrm{VW}}\). It therefore damages the proof of Theorem 7.2, even though a consistent local correction preserves that theorem.

Indeed, retain Lemma 7.5, \(f^Q=(f^{K_Y})^4\), and take the unique fourth root with constant term one: \[f^{K_Y}_{v,n}(t)=
 \exp\left(-\frac14\sum_{\delta>0} A_\delta t^\delta\right)
       +O(t^{1+}).\] This matches Conjecture B and gives \(\mathop{\mathrm{VW}}(v)=\mathop{\mathrm{DT}}(v)/4\). For the primitive example, the local pair space has two copies of \(\mathbb P^{N-1}\), so \(\mathop{\mathrm{VW}}(1,0,0)=2=8/4\). The general degeneration theorem underlying Lemma 7.5 was not independently reproved in full; the sign contradiction and this primitive check do not require such a reconstruction.

# Local corrections and notation clarifications

## Notation clarification (original Problem 4): Laurent Hecke exponents

Equation (2.7), p. 10, sums only \(v\ge0\) despite starting with a Laurent series. For \(\ell=s=1\), index zero, and \(h=\Delta^{-1}\in\Delta^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{QJac}}_{0,0}\), it gives \[h|V_1=h-q^{-1},\qquad
 \Delta(h|V_1)=1-\Delta/q=24q+O(q^2).\] The latter is nonconstant, whereas the asserted weight-zero, index-zero holomorphic quasi-modular target consists of constants.

The finite-coset definition in Appendix A.3 is the identity for \(\ell=1\), and its formula (A.3) has unrestricted exponents. Include every supported integral \(v\) in (2.7). The actual \(F^{\mathrm{KM}}_{g,1}\) in Section 6 has no negative powers at infinity, so the truncation does not change that input. Corollary 7.3 refers to Appendix A.3’s full operator. The complete definition already appears in Appendix A.3, and no necessary calculation has been shown to use the truncated operator. This inconsistency is retained as a notation clarification and is not counted as a substantive Minor finding.

Problem 4 (Minor: a congruence subgroup in Lemma 2.11 is too large).  The proof on p. 11 uses \(\Gamma_0(2m^2)\subset\gamma^{-1}\Gamma_0(2)\gamma\) for \(\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/m\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For \(m=5\), let \[A=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\50&17\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_0(50),
 \qquad
 \gamma A\gamma^{-1}
   =\begin{pmatrix}13&9/5\\50&7\end{pmatrix}.\] The conjugate is not integral, although \(\det A=1\).

Use the principal congruence subgroup \(\Gamma(2m^2)\) instead. For a matrix in it, the entries \(a+c/m\), \(b+(d-a)/m-c/m^2\), \(c\), and \(d-c/m\) are integral, and the lower-left entry remains even. This gives the needed common finite-index subgroup. The completion comparison, density argument for \(m>2\), and weight-zero constant alternative then survive. The lemma’s conclusion has not been refuted.

Problem 5 (Minor: genus-zero vanishing needs nonzero curve class).  Remark 4.1(c), p. 19, asserts genus-zero vanishing without excluding \(\beta=0\). Take the same Enriques surface, \(g=0,n=3,\beta=0\), with insertions \((1,1,p)\), where \(\int_Yp=1\). These satisfy Definition 4.2. Then \(\overline M_{0,3}(Y,0)=Y\), its obstruction group is zero, its virtual class is \([Y]\), and the invariant is \(1\).

Page 21 itself retains this constant-map contribution. Restrict the vanishing assertion to \(\beta\ne0\). A rational tail that becomes unstable after forgetting the map must have positive map degree; thus this example does not invalidate the ancestor–descendant comparison or the anomaly equation.

## Normalization clarification (original Problem 7)

Equation (2.1), p. 6, defines \(F_2=G_2-2G_2(2\tau)\). On p. 21 the equality \(2G_2(q)-2G_2(q^2)=G_2(q)+F_2(q)/24\) is false with that definition. The first expression has constant term zero and \(q\) coefficient \(2\); the second has constant term \(-23/576\) and \(q\) coefficient \(25/24\).

Replace the last expression by \(G_2+F_2\). The first expression and the subsequently used \(G_2\) derivative are already correct. No necessary computation is shown to fail because of the redundant rewriting. It is therefore a normalization clarification, not a substantive Minor finding.

# Further checks and remaining limits

## Ordered degeneration: a convention that must be explicit

The relative invariants on p. 22 use ordered contact points and plain virtual integrals. Equation (4.7), p. 29, does not display the usual contact-order and ordering factors or define \(\eta^\vee\) separately. Under an ordinary, unweighted dual interpretation there is a concrete discrepancy.

Take \(g=1,k=1\), no insertions, and the coefficient \(q^0\zeta^0\), the primitive half-fiber \(s\) of the other pencil. The smooth invariant is \(2\). In the degeneration, the relevant rational ruling fibers on \(X_1\) and \(R\) have contact profile \((1,1)\). Their ordered evaluation families in \(E\times E\) are \[\{(e,e+t)\},\qquad \{(e,a-e)\},\] where \(t\) is nonzero two-torsion. Their ordinary intersection has four transverse points, since \(2e=a-t\) has four solutions. The generic ruling can be chosen smooth at these points. The etale double cover on \(X_1\) excludes profile \((2)\); singular ruling components and coincident-contact boundary terms do not contribute at these transverse intersections. Thus the raw ordered contraction is \(4\), while \((1\cdot1)/2!=1/2\) restores \(2\).

The supporting record gives the class selection and genericity conditions. Because the text leaves the dual/sum convention implicit, this is not counted as an unqualified additional confirmed error: a weighted \(\eta^\vee\) or weighted sum resolves it. The convention should be stated and matched to the cited anomaly compatibility. Constant gluing factors do not change the immediate modular-space containment, so this calculation alone does not refute Theorem 4.3.

## Checks that did not produce a further counterexample

The Mukai pullback \((r,\beta,n)\mapsto(r,\pi^*\beta,2n+r)\) has square \(2(\beta^2-r^2-2rn)\) and divisibility \(\gcd(r,\operatorname{div}\beta,2n)\). The odd/even type conditions and the representatives used in Corollary 5.9 were checked with these factors. The orientation-reversing isometries fixing the normal-form vectors account for the passage from full to orientation-preserving lattice orbits. The external orbit and Torelli theorems remain dependencies, rather than new theorems proved by this review.

In the current Toda formula, the weighted pairing contributes \((-1)^{n+r-1}(n+r)\); the change to \((-p)^n\) absorbs \((-1)^n\). The surviving \((-1)^{r-1}\) is consistent. Dividing by an odd cover preserves this parity and leaves the factor \(1/k\). The eight equivariant line-bundle choices and the compensating division by eight were traced separately.

The elliptic anomaly commutator in Appendix B was checked including the two mixed relative-marking terms with ratios \(\eta_i/\eta_j\): they cancel with opposite signs. Its general statement is conditional as written. In the divisibility-two argument the scalar factor has weight \(2g-6\); the extra half-translation reduces it to a level-one cusp form. The stated low-genus vanishing follows, conditional on the earlier modularity input and the repairs above. For divisibility greater than two, the sparse-series argument retains the substantive \(m>2\) condition.

Appendix C was read with the distinction between integral homology and its torsion-free quotient preserved. Its odd-degree equality uses the cited deformation classification; for even multiples of a half-fiber, the maps lie in fibers and only the even integral lift is represented. This is not an identification of the two refined torsion invariants in every class. Statements with any curve class should explicitly retain the effective nonzero scope where needed; the zero-class multiple-cover sum is not defined by the same formula.
