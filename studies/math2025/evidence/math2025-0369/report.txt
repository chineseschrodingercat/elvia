Abstract: This review concerns the exact 74-page preprint arXiv:2112.09044v3. The concentration-plane argument in the proof of the higher-dimensional lower box bound makes a false geometric inference. A fixed pair of separated, uniformly two-dimensional measures in \(\mathbb R^4\), neither charging any hyperplane, violates its required estimate at infinitely many scales. A separate issue in the cylindrical-slicing mechanism identifies an oriented ray slice with a full-plane slice; an elementary rectangle gives a counterexample. These are Major proof issues in the stated scopes. Four further findings concern a generic slicing bound and local normalization, robustness, and entropy bookkeeping. No counterexample to a main distance-set conclusion is established. All source pages were read as text, and 33 source pages, including all four figures, were actually inspected. Independent scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Identity, hypotheses, and proof structure

The reviewed work is by Pablo Shmerkin and Hong Wang [SW], with frozen annual identity M25-0369. The exact source is arXiv:2112.09044v3, dated 13 August 2024. Its actual PDF has 74 pages. The arXiv comment says 71 pages, while the associated journal article, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-024-00696-5, has pagination 283–358 and an online date of 9 January 2025. These are distinct records; no equivalence of the preprint and journal text is assumed. All locations below use one-based PDF pages, agreeing with the printed numbering.

Write \(\Delta^y(x)=|x-y|\), \(\pi_x(y)=(y-x)/|y-x|\), and \[\phi(u)=\frac{u+\sqrt{4+u^2}-2}{2}.\] Theorem 1.1 treats planar Borel sets of Hausdorff dimension \(u\le1\). Theorem 1.2 gives a Hausdorff bound in \(\mathbb R^3\), whereas Theorem 1.3, for \(d\ge4\) and \(\dim_{\mathrm H}X\ge d/2\), gives a lower box bound. Theorem 1.4 assumes \(\dim_{\mathrm H}X=\dim_{\mathrm P}X=d/2\); its assertion that the supremum is attained almost everywhere additionally uses positive critical Hausdorff measure. Dimension one and positive Lebesgue measure are not interchangeable here. The radial-projection results retain their restrictions on \(k\), the dimension interval, and containment in a \(k\)-plane.

The proof uses normalized dyadic restrictions \(\mu^Q\), quantitative Frostman bounds, and robust measures. Definition 2.2 says that \(\rho\) is \((\delta,s,r)\)-robust when \[\rho(E)>r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |E|_\delta>\delta^{-s}.\] The multiscale entropy propositions require full-rank \(C^2\) maps and disjoint intervals with \(B_j\le2A_j\). Their nonlinear applications use an adapted family of derivative directions and a combinatorial profile in \(\mathcal L_{d,u}\). This class consists of nondecreasing piecewise-linear \(d\)-Lipschitz functions on \([0,1]\), vanishing at zero and lying above \(ux\). The thin-tube statements allow restrictions of positive mass; their strong versions also require compact incidence sets.

The planar bootstrap combines the improved Kaufman estimate with the profile optimization. Higher-dimensional arguments then use orthogonal and cylindrical slices, the radial mechanism in Theorem 6.7, and the exclusion of line-supported slices. The special argument in Section 7.4 replaces quantitative hyperplane nonconcentration by an asserted single-scale substitute. That substitute is the subject of the first finding.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (A false transversality inference in the proof of Theorem 1.3).  Section 7.4.3, page 69, claims that a hyperplane meeting \(Z\) and \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\setminus V^{(c_\varepsilon)}\) has an intersection with \(V\)’s small neighborhood coverable by a bounded number of \((k-1)\)-plates. This is used in (7.12), then (7.15), and finally (7.20)–(7.23), pages 69–71. The assertion fails when the concentration plane has dimension at most \(d-2\).

## The elementary obstruction

If \(x\notin V\) and \(\dim V=k\le d-2\), a hyperplane can contain \(\operatorname{aff}(V,x)\). It then contains \(V\) as well as \(x\). Its intersection with \(V^{(\delta)}\) contains an entire bounded \(k\)-dimensional patch, requiring an unbounded number of \((k-1)\)-plates as \(\delta\downarrow0\). Separation of \(x\) from \(V\) gives no transversality of this hyperplane to \(V\).

A planar measure by itself illustrates this obstruction, but an exact-plane example could obscure the preliminary nonconcentration assumptions. The following construction avoids that issue and supplies fixed measures, not measures changed independently at each tested scale.

## A fixed admissible pair

Equation reference: eq:mixture. Set \(d=4\), \(Q=[-1/10,1/10]^2\), and let \(\lambda_Q\) be normalized planar Lebesgue measure on \(Q\). Define \[F_*(u,v)=(u,v,1+u^2,v^2),\qquad
 \mu=(F_*)_*\lambda_Q.\] For \(j\ge1\), put \[m_j=2j,\quad a_j=\frac1{j+2},\quad e_j=2^{-4j},\quad
 F_j(u,v)=(u,v,a_j+e_j u^2,e_j v^2),
 \quad \nu_j=(F_j)_*\lambda_Q,\] and \[
 \nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_j}{j(j+1)}.\] The weights sum to one. The patches \(F_j(Q)\) are disjoint: their third-coordinate intervals have length at most \(e_j/100\), much less than the separation of consecutive \(a_j\). Their only additional limiting patch is \(Q\times\{(0,0)\}\). Thus \(\nu\) has compact support. The supports of \(\mu,\nu\) are separated by more than \(0.6\). A single fixed similarity puts the construction in any required bounded coordinate cube; it changes only fixed comparison constants.

Every graph map has the first two coordinates \((u,v)\), with a uniformly bounded Lipschitz constant. Hence \[\mu(B_r),\ \nu_j(B_r),\ \nu(B_r)\le C r^2.\] In particular they satisfy the \(2-\varepsilon\) Frostman assumptions and have finite \(s\)-energy for every \(s<2\), by integrating \(r^{1-s}\) near zero. The union of the patches and their limiting patch has Hausdorff and packing dimension exactly two.

Neither measure charges any affine hyperplane. Indeed, the restriction of a nonzero affine functional to a graph \(F_j\) is \[Au+Bv+C(a_j+e_j u^2)+D e_jv^2+E.\] Since \(e_j>0\), this polynomial can vanish identically only if all five coefficients of the affine functional vanish. Its zero set otherwise has planar Lebesgue measure zero. The same argument applies to \(F_*\). Countable summation proves the assertion for \(\nu\). It also implies the uniform small-neighborhood condition (7.11) for \(\mu\), by compactness of the relevant family of hyperplanes.

The construction has stronger thin tubes than needed. Fix \(x\in F_*(Q)\). For the radial image of \(F_j(Q)\), use the first two gnomonic coordinates \[g_{j,x}(u,v)=
 \left(\frac{u-x_1}{x_3-a_j-e_j u^2},
       \frac{v-x_2}{x_3-a_j-e_j u^2}\right).\] The denominator is bounded above and below uniformly in \(j,x,u,v\). The first coordinate is independent of \(v\), and its derivative in \(u\) is \[\frac{x_3-a_j+e_j u^2-2e_jux_1}
      {(x_3-a_j-e_j u^2)^2}>c>0.\] The derivative of the second coordinate in \(v\) is the reciprocal denominator. These formulas give a uniformly Lipschitz inverse on the image. The spherical chart has uniformly bounded distortion because the third directional coordinate stays away from zero. Consequently \[(\pi_x\nu_j)(B_r)\le C r^2,\qquad
 (\pi_x\nu)(B_r)\le C r^2\] uniformly for \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\). Equivalently, \((\mu,\nu)\) has strong \(2\)-thin tubes with the full compact product as incidence set. Since the exponent used in dimension four is \(s_4=1+\phi(1)<2\), all preceding thin-tube requirements are met, with \(Y_x=\operatorname{supp}\nu\) and \(Y_0=\operatorname{supp}\nu\).

## The actual contradiction

Fix the small positive \(\varepsilon'\) used in Section 7.4, and take \(j\) sufficiently large. Set \[\delta_j=2^{-\varepsilon'm_j},\quad
 Y=F_j(Q),\quad
 V_j=\mathbb R^2\times\{(a_j,0)\}.\] Then \[\nu(Y)=\frac1{j(j+1)}>\frac1{m_j^2},\qquad
 \nu_Y=\nu_j,\qquad
 Y\subset V_j^{(\delta_j)}.\] For a line \(L\), projection to the first two coordinates gives \(\nu_j(L^{(\delta_j)})\le C\delta_j\); for a point the bound is \(C\delta_j^2\). Exponential decay therefore makes these quantities smaller than \(m_j^{-8}\) and \(m_j^{-10}\), respectively. These are precisely the lower-dimensional thresholds in (7.10) for \(d=4\). The smallest concentration dimension is thus \(k=2\), and \(V=V_j\) is an allowed choice. Here \(Z=Y\).

Equation reference: eq:massone. For any \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\), let \[H_{j,x}=\operatorname{aff}(V_j,x).\] This is a three-dimensional hyperplane. It contains \(x\), contains \(V_j\), and meets \(Z\) at \(F_j(0,0)\). Also \(x\) is a fixed positive distance from \(V_j\). A normal vector is \[n_{j,x}=(0,0,-x_4,x_3-a_j).\] Direct substitution, also checked symbolically, gives \[n_{j,x}\cdot\bigl(F_j(u,v)-(0,0,a_j,0)\bigr)
 =e_j\bigl(-x_4u^2+(x_3-a_j)v^2\bigr).\] It follows that the whole patch \(Z\) is within \(\sqrt2 e_j/100\) of \(H_{j,x}\), hence within \(\delta_j\) for all large \(j\). Therefore \[
 \nu_Z\bigl(H_{j,x}^{(\delta_j)}\bigr)=1,\] contrary to (7.12)’s \(O_\varepsilon(m_j^{-2})\). The corresponding angular mass in a \(\delta_j\)-neighborhood of the linear hyperplane \(H_{j,x}-x\) is also one, contradicting (7.15). Every fixed integer \(T\) divides infinitely many \(m_j=2j\), so the restriction to multiples of \(T\) does not avoid the example.

There is a separate arithmetic error in (7.12): \[\frac{m^{-2(d-k)}}{m^{-2(d-k+1)}}=m^2,\] whereas the display gives \(m^{-2}\). Using the threshold for dimension \(k-1\) would put \(d-k+2\) in the numerator exponent. That correction cannot resolve [eq:massone] (#eq:massone); the required geometric covering is false.

## Impact and repair boundary

Equation (7.15) is the precise input used for Lemma 3.11 in (7.20). Its failure leaves the lower-dimensional entropy branch, and thus the printed proof of Theorem 1.3, unsupported. This is a Major issue in a principal result’s proof. The example does not show that Theorem 1.3, Theorem 1.4, or the Falconer conjecture is false; its smooth patches themselves have large distance sets.

A replacement must handle the possibility that the chosen concentration plane and the pin lie in a common hyperplane. Merely changing the exponent ratio, moving the pin farther from \(V\), or adding the assumption that both measures charge no hyperplane does not suffice. A new dimension-reduction or concentration argument is needed. No complete repair of this branch is established here.

Problem 2 (Oriented cylindrical fibers and full-plane slices).  Lemma 6.5, page 51, and the fiber identities immediately preceding it identify the oriented cylindrical slice with a full affine-plane slice. Theorem 6.6, page 52, then uses a false angular density identity. The definitions on pages 31 and 50 explicitly use the sphere-valued oriented projection.

Take \(d=k=2\), \(V=\mathbb R^2\), \(x=0\), and \[d\nu=16\,1_{[1/4,1/2]\times[-1/8,1/8]}\,d\mathcal L^2.\] This is a probability measure supported inside the unit disk, separated from \(x\). Both slices exist. At \(\theta=-e_1\), the cylindrical slice in (6.3) is zero: sufficiently small angular caps around \(-e_1\) see no part of the rectangle. The orthogonal slice on the full line \(\mathbb Re_1\), defined by (6.1), is \(16\,1_{[1/4,1/2]}\,dr\), of mass \(4\). The two measures cannot be mutually absolutely continuous. At \(+e_1\), the cylindrical slice instead has mass \[16\int_{1/4}^{1/2}r\,dr=\frac32.\] Thus this is a support error, not just a missing normalization factor.

For the integrated full-line measure \(\Lambda=\int_{S^1}\nu_{\mathbb R\theta}\,d\theta\), polar coordinates give \(d\Lambda(y)=2|y|^{-1}\,d\nu(y)\). The oriented angular density of \(\pi_0\Lambda\) is \(8\) at \(+e_1\) and \(0\) at \(-e_1\). The final identity in the proof of Theorem 6.6 gives \(4\) at both. The discrepancy holds on angular neighborhoods, not merely at an isolated point. Replacing the pin by a small smooth measure near zero gives the same orientation obstruction on a positive set of pins.

This is a Major issue in the stated disintegration argument: a false equivalence of measures and a false pushforward identity are used in the mechanism underlying Theorem 6.7. A bounded repair of this particular step is available. If \(W=x+V^\perp+\mathbb R\theta\), compare \[\nu_W\quad\hbox{with}\quad
 \nu^{\mathrm{cyl}}_{V,x,\theta}
 +\nu^{\mathrm{cyl}}_{V,x,-\theta},\] rather than either summand alone. On separated bounded supports the radial weights are bounded above and below, so the two-sided comparison follows from the neighborhood definitions. Integration gives the required comparison with \(\nu\), up to a fixed factor. The full-slice mass is bounded by a constant times the sum of the two antipodal angular densities; the inequality \((a+b)^p\le2^{p-1}(a^p+b^p)\) then gives the desired \(L^p\) bound using the same external radial-projection input. At the end of Theorem 6.7, use two antipodal caps instead of one. This repair preserves the desired estimates; it does not validate the printed identities or remove Problem 1.

Problem 3 (The wrong extremum in the generic box-slicing bound).  Lemma 6.13, page 61, has \(\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm B}(E\cap W)\le\min(\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm B}E-k,0)\). For \(E=[-1,1]^3\) and \(k=1\), every plane cutting the interior has a two-dimensional section, while the displayed bound is zero. Such planes form a set of positive \(\lambda_{3,2}\)-measure.

The usual corrected bound is \(\max(\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm B}E-k,0)\), with the appropriate empty-section convention. To see the positive-difference case directly, cover \(E\) at scale \(r\) by at most \(C_\epsilon r^{-D-\epsilon}\) cubes, where \(D=\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm B}E\). The measure of affine codimension-\(k\) planes hitting one such cube is \(O(r^k)\). Markov’s inequality and summation over dyadic \(r\) give an almost-everywhere section count bounded by \(r^{-D+k-2\epsilon}\) eventually. Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) gives the corrected bound. When \(D<k\), almost every section is empty. This is Minor in its demonstrated impact: the subsequent applications use the intended positive dimension difference, and no extra loss of their target exponent follows from this transcription error.

Problem 4 (Two incorrect profile normalizations).  The rescaling before (5.15), page 47, is printed as \(\widetilde f(x)=a f(x/a)\). The dimension profile in Section 7.4.6, page 70, omits its factor \(1/\ell\).

In the first location \(f\) is defined only on \([0,1]\), and the actual \(a\) lies in \((0,1/2]\). The printed expression is undefined for \(x>a\). The required function is \[g(x)=\frac{f(ax)}a,\qquad 0\le x\le1.\] It belongs to the same \(\mathcal L_{2,u}\): it is nondecreasing, vanishes at zero, remains \(2\)-Lipschitz, and satisfies \(g(x)\ge ux\). A \(\sigma\)-superlinear interval \([A,B]\) for \(g\) gives a \(\sigma\)-superlinear interval \([aA,aB]\) for \(f\); its length and allowance threshold are multiplied by \(a\). This is exactly the transport needed at (5.15).

In the second location, taking \(d=4\), \(\ell=8\), and all \(\beta_i=2\) gives the printed profile \(f(1)=16\) and slope \(16\), contrary to its declared range and Lipschitz constant \(4\). The correct formula is \[f(j/\ell)=\ell^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^j\beta_i,\] already printed in Section 4.6.1, page 26. It gives \(f(1)=2\) and slope \(2\) in this example. These are Minor normalization errors with explicit local corrections. Correcting them does not supply the missing geometry of Problem 1.

Problem 5 (A vacuous robustness threshold).  Lemma 3.10, page 18, asserts \((\delta,t/d+\eta,\delta^{-\eta})\)-robustness and then adaptedness. Under Definition 2.2, its robustness clause is vacuous for every \(0<\delta<1\), \(\eta>0\).

A probability measure has no set of mass greater than \(\delta^{-\eta}>1\). For example, \(\delta=2^{-16}\), \(\eta=1/4\) gives threshold \(16\). The preceding clause therefore does not provide the asserted nonvacuous implication. A positive mass exponent is required, in the form supplied by the cited discretized projection theorem. This is a Minor sign error in the recorded formulation. This review does not replace the full proof of that external theorem merely by changing the sign.

Problem 6 (The conditional-entropy covering is reversed).  In Lemma 3.11, page 19, the proof says that each dyadic cube meets \(O_d(a^d)\) atoms of the joint projection partition. The factor tends to zero as \(a\downarrow0\), and the stated direction does not control the conditional entropy actually used.

The needed quantity is the number of dyadic cubes meeting one joint atom. For \(d=2\), rows \((1,0)\) and \((\sqrt{1-a^2},a)\) form a projection matrix of determinant \(a\). A joint atom has area \(2^{-2m}/a\) and crosses on the order of \(a^{-1}\) dyadic squares. More generally the Gram–Schmidt condition gives determinant at least \(a^{d-1}\). If \(A\) is this matrix, the union of dyadic cubes meeting \(A^{-1}(2^{-m}Q)\) lies in its enlargement by a cube of side \(O(2^{-m})\). Applying \(A\) puts that enlargement in a cube of side \(O_d(2^{-m})\), since the rows have unit norm. Volume comparison gives at most \(O_d(a^{-(d-1)})\le O_d(a^{-d})\) dyadic cubes. Hence \[H(\mu,\mathcal D_m\mid\mathcal D'_m)
 \le d\log(1/a)+O_d(1),\] as needed. The lemma survives this correction. The issue is Minor, with no separate reduction of its stated entropy bound.
