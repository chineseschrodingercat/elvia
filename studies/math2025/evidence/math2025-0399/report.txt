# Assessment and scope

The manuscript combines a convex density increment with homogeneous subset-sum structure to obtain the exponent \(1/4+o(1)\) for non-averaging subsets of an interval, and the exponent \((d-1)/(d+1)+o(1)\) in higher-dimensional boxes. The geometric mechanism and the distinction between ambient dimension and subset-sum dimension are central to the argument.

Major revision is required for this exact version. Three Major findings concern the weighted zonotope step in the proof of Theorem 4, the final cardinality bookkeeping in the iteration, and Lemma 6 without an origin condition. Four Minor findings concern affine approximation, the normalizations in the density-increment proof, the range of the lifted translation in Appendix A, and the non-averaging claim after adjoining zero. Each finding below has an explicit calculation or proof, a precise scope, and a repair boundary. None of these findings is asserted to be a counterexample to the main non-averaging bound. In particular, the numerical iteration witness is a counterexample to a claimed implication between estimates, not a non-averaging set.

The complete assigned PDF was read, including definitions, proofs, Appendix A and references. All 20 source pages were also actually inspected as rendered images. The relevant complete proof units of Conlon–Fox–Pham [CFP], in the exact version stated in Section 5 (#sec:evidence), were examined. The imported structural theorem is treated as a cited theorem; a recursive certification of all its literature dependencies is not claimed. No author software was executed.

# Major findings

## P1. The retained coefficient mass and the centre used in Theorem 4 are not justified

Location. Source pp. 13–15: (13), Lemma 14 and its penultimate mass inequality, and the centres immediately after (15).

There are two distinct defects. Lemma 14 assumes only \(|\bar A_1|\ge c|A_1|\), whereas its proof needs a positive amount of coefficient mass on \(\bar A_1\). Moreover, the centre used on p. 15 is twice the centre in Lemma 14. These are genuine failures of displayed steps even under the structural and irreducibility assumptions of Theorem 4.

#### A family satisfying the setup.

Let \(M\to\infty\), \(N=2M+1\), and take \[A=[-M,M]\cap\mathbb Z,\quad P(A)=A,\quad \widehat A=A,
 \quad A_0=B=[0,2M]\cap\mathbb Z,\quad a=M.\] The identification is \(x\mapsto x+M\). The set may be embedded in \(\mathbb Z^2\) if the notation \(\ell>1\) in Corollary 5 is read literally. Fix the structural constant \(c_0\le1/100\), reducing it if necessary. This is legitimate Corollary 5 data for all sufficiently large \(M\): put \(s=N/\log^2N\), \(k=\lfloor s/4\rfloor\), and \[E=\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm k\}\cup
   \{\pm(M-k+1),\ldots,\pm M\}.\] Then \(|E|=4k\le s\). The first block has every subset sum in \([-R,R]\), where \(R=k(k+1)/2\). The positive partial sums of the second block have gaps at most \(M\) and end at \(T=kM-k(k-1)/2\); use their negatives as well. Since \(2R\ge M\) for large \(M\), the translated intervals overlap and give \[[-k(M+1),k(M+1)]\cap\mathbb Z\ \subseteq\ \Sigma(E).\] This contains \(c_0sP(A)\), which is proper. Thus the origin, subset budget and homogeneous-translate requirements are all met.

Here is also a uniform verification of irreducibility, rather than a finite test of selected subsets. Fix \(\delta>0\). If \(T\subseteq A_0\), \(|T|=t\ge\delta N\), and \(x\in B\), then \(T-x\subseteq[-2M,2M]\). In a reduced structural GAP for \(T-x\), delete coordinates of width one. Its size is at least \(t/2\) for large \(N\). If its rank were \(r\ge2\), properness of \(c_0s(T)P(T-x)\) would give \[s(T)^r t\ll |\Sigma(T')|\ll Ns(T),\qquad
 |T'|\le s(T)=t/\log^2t,\] which is impossible uniformly for \(t\ge\delta N\) as \(N\to\infty\). Thus rank one can be chosen for every such \(T,x\), and \(|P(T-x)|\ge t/2\ge\delta N/2\). With these coherent reduced choices, \(A\) is \((\delta,\gamma)\)-irreducible for every fixed \(\gamma\le\delta/2\). Choose \(\delta\le\mu^C\) and \(\gamma\le\min(\delta/2,\delta^C)\); then take \(N\) large enough for the inverse-logarithmic lower bound on \(\gamma\) in Theorem 4. Choose the fixed \(\delta,\gamma\) using, for example, the lower bound \(\mu>1/11\) that holds for large \(M\); these choices are uniform in the limit.

Put \(L=\lfloor M/10\rfloor\), \(\mu=2L/N\), and assign \[c_{M+i}=c_{M-i}=\frac1{2L}\quad(1\le i\le L),\qquad c_x=0
 \quad\hbox{otherwise}.\] Then \(\sum c_x=1\), \(\sum c_x(x-a)=0\), and \(c_x\le1/(\mu N)\), with equality on the support. The median \(a\) lies in no half-space meeting \(A_0\) in at most \(\mu N\) points, so \(A_0\) is not in \(\mu\)-convex position. The limiting value \(\mu\to1/10\) permits the preceding fixed choices of \(\delta,\gamma\).

Equation reference: eq:weightedsets. Order the zero coefficients first, in pairs \((M-j,M+j)\) for \(j>L\), and then the nonzero coefficients in pairs \((M+i,M-i)\) for \(1\le i\le L\). This is a permitted nondecreasing order in (13). The resulting two sets satisfy \[
 A_1-a=a-A_2=\{-M,\ldots,-L-1\}\cup\{1,\ldots,L\}.\] Choose \(\bar A_1=\{M-j:L<j\le M\}\). Its cardinality is \(M-L\ge0.9|A_1|\), but all its coefficients vanish. Hence the centred retained zonotope in the proof of Lemma 14 is \(\{0\}\). It cannot contain \(\xi NB\) for any \(\xi>0\), and the displayed lower bound \[\sum_{x\in(\bar A_1-a)\setminus H}\Delta_x\ge\mu N/4\] has left side zero. This disproves the stronger retained-zonotope assertion in the proof. The printed statement of Lemma 14 has the full \(Z_{A_1-a}\) on its right side; the preceding example is not represented as a disproof of that weaker statement.

#### The doubled centre fails even after the actual small deletion.

Equation reference: eq:badcentre. For the full \(A_1\) in [eq:weightedsets] (#eq:weightedsets), the centre defined on p. 15 is \[
 z_1=\mu N\sum_{x\in A_1}c_x(x-a)=\frac{L(L+1)}2
       =\max Z_{A_1-a}.\] Thus \(z_1+\xi NB\) leaves the full zonotope for every \(\xi>0\). The proof actually deletes \(O(N/\log N)\) points to form its retained set. Under the natural, consistent interpretation of its shifted indices, the resulting centre obeys \[\bar z_1\ge z_1-O(N^2/\log N).\] The right-hand displacement in \(\xi NB=[0,2\xi NM]\) is of order \(\xi N^2\). For these admissible \(\mu\to1/10\) and fixed \(\gamma>0\), \(\xi=c^\prime\mu\gamma\) stays bounded below by a positive constant, so this eventually exceeds the deletion error. Consequently the displayed inclusion \(\operatorname{conv}(\bar z_1+\xi NB)\subseteq Z_{A_1-a}\) on p. 15 is still false for the actual permitted trimming. The mismatch with the retained zonotope needed in (15) is an additional logical problem.

For a reproducible finite coefficient calculation, \(M=1000\), \(L=100\) gives a retained fraction \(9/10\), retained mass zero, the asserted mass lower bound \(50\), and \[Z_{A_1-a}=[-495450,5050],\qquad z_1=5050,
 \qquad z_1/2=2525.\] The large-\(N\) structural verification above, not this one finite calculation, supplies all asymptotic hypotheses.

#### Impact and repair boundary.

This is a Major proof defect in the implication from irreducibility to intersecting nonempty subset sums, a core input to the main argument. It does not refute the conclusion of Theorem 4: the interval in this example certainly has averaging relations.

A plausible restricted repair must use the actual small deletion and prove a coefficient-mass condition. Alternating a nondecreasing sequence gives mass at least \(1/2-O((\mu|A_0|)^{-1})\) on each side; deleting \(O(N/\log N)\) points loses only \(O((\mu\log N)^{-1})\). Thus each actual retained side has mass at least \(1/3\) for large \(N\). This is substantially stronger than the arbitrary cardinality condition in Lemma 14. Use, for example, \(\lambda=\mu|A_0|/4\), so that \(\lambda c_x\le1/4\), and retain the same interior centre \(\lambda\sum c_x(x-a)\) throughout. The factor \(|A_0|\) also avoids the unsupported assertion \(\mu|A|c_x\le1\) when \(\widehat A\ne A\).

A complete repair must establish the interior inclusion in the retained zonotope, use a symmetric displacement body such as \(B-B\) in the slab argument, account for low-rank lattice slices and the additive endpoint term in lattice counting, and quantitatively produce at least two lattice intersection points. The last printed condition that the intersection differs from \(\{z_1\}\) alone does not rule out \(\{0\}\). These remaining geometric and lattice steps are not certified as repaired here.

## P2. The final iteration confuses two different retention ratios

Location. Source pp. 17–18, Case 3 and the paragraph beginning Finally, we verify.

Equation reference: eq:correctstep. Write \(M=|A|\), \(V=|B|\), and distinguish the preprocessing retention \(r\) from the geometric volume ratio \(\eta\). The preceding Case 3 estimates have the form \[
 M_{\mathrm{new}}\ge c\,\eta^p rM,\qquad
 V_{\mathrm{new}}\le C\,\eta r^K V,\qquad
 p=\frac{d-1}{d+1}+\varepsilon.\] The final paragraph instead defines \(\rho_j=M_{j+1}/M_j\) for the whole step and asserts \(V_{j+1}\ll\rho_j^K V_j\). This does not follow from [eq:correctstep] (#eq:correctstep).

Equation reference: eq:ratio. An exact numerical family makes the failure explicit. Set \(d=2\), \(\varepsilon=1/100\), \(p=103/300\), \(K=100\), \(r=1\), and, for \(t\downarrow0\), \[\eta=t^{300},\qquad \rho=t^{103},\qquad
 V_{\mathrm{new}}/V=\eta,\qquad M_{\mathrm{new}}/M=\rho.\] These satisfy both one-step inequalities with constants one, while \[
 \frac{V_{\mathrm{new}}/V}{\rho^K}=t^{-10000}\longrightarrow\infty.\] The finite check \(t=1/2\) gives the exact ratio \(2^{10000}\). Initial \(M,V\) can be taken arbitrarily large to accommodate polynomial size lower bounds; the obstruction is the missing geometric factor, not rounding. This is an independent disproof of the asserted algebraic implication. No actual non-averaging set realizing this numerical sequence is claimed.

There is a second defect in the same final deduction. After inserting \(\max(1,C\rho_j^K)\), the displayed product has factors equal to one whenever \(C\rho_j^K<1\). The inequality with that maximum provides no lower bound on \(\prod\rho_j\). Also, an upper bound \(\rho_j\le\mu^{c(d)}\) is not a consequence of the displayed lower bound on the selected set’s size. Deliberate trimming could enforce a size quota, but would not validate the missing \(\eta\) factor.

#### Impact and a conditional bookkeeping repair.

The intended contradiction requires all intermediate sets to remain large enough for the structural theorem and the common parameters. The printed argument does not establish that requirement, so this is Major. It does not disprove Theorems 1 or 2.

Equation reference: eq:potential. The following replacement verifies the needed size control conditional on a correct geometric Case 3. In a block of fixed dimension \(d\ge2\), choose \[p_*=\alpha_d+2\varepsilon<1,\qquad
 2\varepsilon<\zeta_0,\qquad Kp_*>1,
 \quad \alpha_d=\frac{d-1}{d+1}.\] For \(F=\log M-p_*\log V\), [eq:correctstep] (#eq:correctstep) gives \[
 F_{\mathrm{new}}-F
 \ge\varepsilon\log(1/\eta)+(Kp_*-1)\log(1/r)-O(1).\] Since \(\eta\le\mu^\tau\to0\) with the original input size, this is nonnegative for sufficiently large input, uniformly in \(r\le1\). At entry to the block, \(M\ge V^{\alpha_d+\zeta}\) and \(\zeta\ge\zeta_0\) imply \[F\ge\frac{\zeta-2\varepsilon}{\alpha_d+\zeta}\log M.\] At every later step, \(M\le V\) implies \(F\le(1-p_*)\log M\). Hence every set in this block retains a fixed positive power of its entry size. There are only boundedly many changes of dimension, by the dimension-increment argument and Observation 15; each such step retains at least a \((1-\varepsilon)\) power. Thus \(M_j\ge M_{\mathrm{original}}^{c(\zeta_0,d)}\) for some \(c(\zeta_0,d)>0\). In particular, \(\log\log M_j=\log\log M_{\mathrm{original}}+O_{\zeta_0,d}(1)\), which is sufficient for the common inverse-logarithmic parameters. The one-dimensional branch can be treated directly: for \(\mu<1/2\) a median of a finite set cannot be in \(\mu\)-convex position.

This proves a useful replacement for the size bookkeeping, not the manuscript’s precise near-original exponent with an absolute \(c\). It remains conditional on the preceding geometric/lattice step, including the repair required by P1.

## P3. Lemma 6 is false for an arbitrary containing affine GAP

Location. Source p. 10, Lemma 6 and its proof; uses in Lemma 10 on pp. 11–13; the translation at the start of Appendix A.

The assertion \(\Sigma(A')\subseteq sP\) requires that shorter subset sums can be padded with zeros in \(P\). The hypothesis \(A\subseteq P\) does not supply \(0\in P\). This affects the stated bound, not just its written proof.

Equation reference: eq:Pzero. Let \(m\to\infty\), \(N=m^4\), and \[A=N+[1,m],\qquad P_{\mathrm{aff}}=N+[1,m],\qquad
 s=m/\log^2m.\] The input GAP has dimension one and size \(m\). It is contained in a box of size \(m\), so the polynomial box-size hypothesis is satisfied; again one may embed it in \(\mathbb Z^2\). There is admissible symmetric rank-two structural data \[
 P_0=\{uN+v: |u|\le1,\ |v|\le m\},\qquad \widehat A=A.\] Both \(P_0\) and \(c_0sP_0\) are proper for fixed \(0<c_0\le1/100\) and large \(m\).

Here is a constructive verification of the remaining structural requirement. Put \(t=\lfloor s/2\rfloor\) and use the \(2t\le s\) elements \[A'=(N+[1,t])\cup(N+[m-t+1,m]).\] For a subset using \(l\) elements of the first block and \(h\) of the second, with \(k=l+h\), its offsets from \(kN\) fill every integer between \[\begin{aligned}
 L_{k,h}&=\frac{l(l+1)}2+h(m-t)+\frac{h(h+1)}2,\\
 U_{k,h}&=\frac{l(2t-l+1)}2+hm-\frac{h(h-1)}2.\end{aligned}\] The fixed-cardinality sums of consecutive integers have no gaps: subtracting the minimum sum identifies them with sums of a nondecreasing sequence of \(l\) integers between \(0\) and \(t-l\), which realize every integer from \(0\) to \(l(t-l)\). The same argument applies to the high block.

For \(|k-t|\le c_0s\) and \(t/3\le h\le2t/3\), the interval length is \(l(t-l)+h(t-h)\ge13t^2/36\) after harmless endpoint rounding. Adjacent lower endpoints differ by \(m+O(t)\); since \(t^2/m\to\infty\), these intervals overlap. Their union contains \([0.4tm,0.6tm]\) for all sufficiently large \(m\). Consequently \[x_0+c_0sP_0\subseteq\Sigma(A'),\qquad
 x_0=tN+\lfloor tm/2\rfloor.\] This is a homogeneous translate. Thus \(d(A)=2\) and \(P(A)=P_0\) are allowed by the manuscript’s definition, with every Corollary 5 conclusion explicitly verified. Lemma 6 would give \[3(2m+1)=|P_0|\ll m/s,\] which is impossible as \(s\to\infty\). The implied constant cannot absorb the unbounded factor \(s\).

The phenomenon is not removed by insisting on a rank-one structural choice. A rank-one GAP containing \(0\) and more than \((m+1)/2\) points of \(N+[1,m]\) must have integer step one and span at least \(N\). Its \(c_0s\) dilate contains an integer interval of length comparable to \(sN\). But every subset sum of at most \(s\) elements of \(A\) lies in one of the separated bands \([k(N+1),k(N+m)]\), each of width at most \(sm\ll N\). No such interval fits. Hence a reduced rank-one choice is impossible for large \(m\).

The independent finite endpoint certificate uses \(m=2^{20}\), \(t=\lfloor m/392\rfloor=2674\), and exact rational bounds \(13<\log m<14\). It verifies 113,157 intervals and the inclusion for all \(|k-t|\le63\), with offset radius 65,059,860. This is a concrete check of the interval construction. It is not used to assert a sufficiently-large-\(m\) hypothesis at this single numerical value; the asymptotic proof above supplies that part.

#### Impact and repair boundary.

This is a false core structural lemma, used to obtain the up-move savings and the subsequent iteration bounds, and is therefore Major. The set \(A\) here need not be non-averaging: Lemma 6 has no such hypothesis. No conclusion about a counterexample to the main theorem follows.

A valid version can assume an origin-containing, appropriately centered input GAP. Then every subset of at most \(s\) elements has its sum in \(sP\) and the counting proof applies, after deleting width-one coordinates. All applications must be checked with the origin fixed at the point at which their subset-sum dimensions are defined. Translating a set preserves the non-averaging property, but not the subset-sum structure with an adjoined origin. In Appendix A, the initial reduction of an arbitrary box to \([n]^\ell\) therefore also needs justification. One must either supply the higher-dimensional structural proof with its actual origin convention or retain cardinality information when transporting subset sums through a translation. A full repair of every such use is not asserted here.

# Minor findings

## P4. Lemma 2 needs affine functions, a nonempty index set, and a corrected derivative bookkeeping

Location. Source pp. 4–6, Lemma 2, Claim 3 and the proof of Lemma 2.

With the strict meaning of linear, Lemma 2 is false. Take \(d=2\), \(m=1000\), \(c=1\), \(I=[m]\) and \(H\equiv1\) on \((-1,2)\). All displayed domain, range and convexity hypotheses hold. On \(Q_v=[v/m,(v+1)/m]\), the minimum uniform error of \(ax\) is \(1/(2v+1)\), obtained by equating the endpoint errors. Therefore every \(v\in I\) has error at least \(1/2001\), whereas the claimed bound is \[\frac{4d^4m^{d-3}}{c|I|}=\frac{64}{10^6}=\frac1{15625}.\] An affine constant approximates \(H\) exactly. Also require \(I\ne\varnothing\), replace the unrestricted function in Claim 3 by an affine function, and evaluate the base gradient at \(v/m\) rather than at \(v\).

The intended affine assertion has a short complete repair. For smooth \(H\), Claim 3’s local convexity argument says that a cube with approximation error greater than \(\Delta\) has a coordinate derivative increment at least \(\Delta m/d^2\) on a segment of length \(d/m\) beginning at its base point. For each coordinate and each fixed choice of the other grid coordinates, such segments overlap with multiplicity at most \(d\). Convexity and \(0\le H\le1\) give derivative bounds \(-1/c\le\partial_iH\le2/c\) throughout the relevant range \([0,1+d/m]\). Summing all the increments therefore gives \[|I|\Delta m/d^2\le 3d(d-1)m^{d-2}/c,\qquad
 \Delta\le\frac{3d^3(d-1)m^{d-3}}{c|I|}
       <\frac{4d^4m^{d-3}}{c|I|}.\] This proves that not all cubes can be bad. Smooth mollification can use a domain loss tending to zero, with \(c'\) arbitrarily close to \(c\) and still above \(2d/m\); the strict constant slack absorbs that loss. Pass to the limit in the affine approximants. The case \(d=1\) is a one-point domain and is immediate. This also avoids the missing final endpoint in the printed telescoping sum. The repair changes local wording and bookkeeping, not the exponent used later.

## P5. Three geometric normalizations in Lemma 1 require correction

Location. Source p. 7, from the width assertion through the definition of \(H\); p. 9, the final display in the small-\(K\) case.

First, minimum width \(w\) does not imply inradius at least \(w/2\). An equilateral triangle of altitude \(h\) has minimum width \(h\) and inradius \(h/3\). Thus the claimed factor on p. 7 is not a general convex-geometric implication. A dimension-dependent constant suffices: John’s ellipsoid gives an inscribed ball of radius at least \(w/(2d)\), since \(P\) lies in a \(d\)-dilate of that ellipsoid. Adjust the subsequent constant in \(u\) accordingly. This corrects the intermediate geometric assertion; it is not a counterexample to Lemma 1.

Second, from \[H(x)=1-\frac{h(u(2x-\mathbf1))}{2\sqrt d}\] the inverse coordinate is \(x=y/(2u)+\mathbf1/2\). The printed domain uses \(y/(2u)+\mathbf1\). For example, \(u=1/4\), \(y=0\) maps back to \(y=1/4\) under the printed expression, whereas the correct inverse maps back to zero. The grid is consequently the \(2m\) grid with the correct half-unit translation.

Third, the first case on p. 9 drops the factor \(\eta^{-\alpha_d-\varepsilon}\) and ends with a lower bound greater than one for \(|A\cap\Omega''|/|A|\). A fraction of the original finite set cannot exceed one. The preceding chain controls the normalized fraction \[\frac{|A\cap\Omega''|}{|A|}\eta^{-\alpha_d-\varepsilon},\] and retaining that factor proves the intended conclusion. The power gain is consistent: \(m^{\varepsilon(d+1)}u^2\) has order \(\delta^{-0.3\varepsilon}\delta^{0.2\varepsilon}\) up to constants, which tends to infinity.

Two related presentation points should be made explicit in the same local repair. The selected boundary points need not all be distinct; use an indexed multiset or the bounded overlap of the fixed dilates of grid boxes before replacing their number by \(|Y|\). In the large-\(K\) case, \(h(Q_v)\) can have zero length, so the asserted two-sided volume comparison for \(Q_v\times h(Q_v)\) is not automatic. Use an enclosing interval of controlled length and the required upper volume bound, retaining the exponent slack for constants. Intersect the resulting set with the original convex body to maintain \(\Omega'\subseteq\Omega\). These are bounded repairs to the geometric normalization, and no refutation of the corrected density-increment lemma is asserted.

## P6. The lifted translation in Appendix A is missing a factor of n

Location. Source p. 20, the sentence claiming \(x=\phi^{-1}(x_0)\in[-s,s]^\ell\).

The established inclusion is \(x_0\in sQ\) with \(Q=\phi([-n,n]^\ell)\). It gives \(x\in[-sn,sn]^\ell\), not the printed range. This can occur for actual admissible structural output, rather than just an arbitrary point of \(sQ\).

Take \(\ell=2\), \(A=\{(i,1):1\le i\le n\}\), \(H=n^\kappa\) with the appendix’s \(\kappa=80\), and \(\phi(i,j)=i+Hj\). Then \(A_0=H+[1,n]\). Apply the explicit block construction in P3 with \(m=n\), \(N=H\) and \(s=n/\log^2n\). It has all the stated structural conclusions for large \(n\), and its homogeneous translation is \[x_0=tH+\lfloor tn/2\rfloor,\qquad
 \phi^{-1}(x_0)=(\lfloor tn/2\rfloor,t),\qquad t=\lfloor s/2\rfloor.\] The first coordinate is asymptotic to \(sn/4\), so it exceeds \(s\) by an unbounded factor. The polynomial range and the admissible interval for \(s\) hold for sufficiently large \(n\).

Replace the range by \([-sn,sn]^\ell\) and carry it through the no-carry estimate. This does not require changing the chosen exponent \(\kappa\): since \(s\le |A|\le n^\ell\), \[ns^2(sn)^\ell\le n^{\ell^2+3\ell+1}\ll n^{10\ell^3}.\] The fixed dimension constants can be absorbed by sufficiently large \(n\). This repairs the local range error, without resolving the separate initial translation issue identified in P3.

## P7. Adjoining zero can destroy non-averaging even for every large retained subset

Location. Source p. 10, the first sentence of Section 3.1.

The claimed non-averaging property of \(\phi_P(\widehat A\cup\{0\})\) does not follow from that of \(A\). Here is an arbitrarily large example satisfying a polynomial box bound and surviving the deletion allowed in Corollary 5. Let \(m\to\infty\), \(N=m^3\), and \[p_i=(N+i,(N+i)^2),\qquad
 A=\{p_i,2p_i:1\le i\le m\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^2.\] This set has \(2m\) distinct points and lies in an axis-aligned box of size \(O(m^9)\), hence is a \((2,10)\)-set for large \(m\).

To prove it is non-averaging, suppose \(cp_{i_0}\) is the average of \(k\) other points \(c_jp_{i_j}\), where \(c,c_j\in\{1,2\}\) and \(k\le2m-1\). The first coordinates give \[N\left(kc-\sum_jc_j\right)=\sum_jc_ji_j-kci_0.\] The right side has absolute value less than \(4m^2<N\). Thus \(\sum_jc_j=kc\), which forces every \(c_j=c\). Comparing the remaining first-coordinate terms and the second coordinates then gives \[\sum_ji_j=ki_0,\qquad \sum_ji_j^2=ki_0^2,\qquad
 \sum_j(i_j-i_0)^2=0.\] Every selected point would equal the excluded target, a contradiction.

Every subset of \(A\) with more than \(m\) elements contains one of the pairs \(p_i,2p_i\). The retained set in Corollary 5 has more than \(m\) elements for sufficiently large \(m\). Adjoining \(0\) therefore creates \(p_i=(0+2p_i)/2\). Moreover \(csP\) proper with \(cs\ge2\) and the centered representation ensures \(2P\) is proper. Its identification preserves this two-term relation, so the image also fails to be non-averaging. This checks the exact retained-subset and identification issues that the elementary example \(\{1,2\}\) alone would leave open.

The independent finite test at \(m=8\), \(N=512\) checks all 65,535 nonempty subsets of the 16-point set, finds no averaging relation in \(A\), and verifies all eight doubling pairs. The analytic argument above proves the arbitrary-large family and the structural deletion threshold.

The repair is to claim non-averaging only for \(\phi_P(\widehat A)\), as the actual reduction subsequently does, while keeping \(0\) in the bounding progression for subset-sum structure. The inverse identification is affine, so an averaging relation among image points would imply one among original points. Definition 9 should also restrict the domain of \(\phi_P\) to the retained set unless \(A\subseteq P\) has first been arranged. These are local domain corrections, with no new obstruction to the main iteration.

# Proof audit and excluded candidates

## The dimension-change inequality is valid

Observation 15 is not a confirmed issue. Here is an all-parameter check that also covers the displayed range beyond small \(\zeta\). Set \(h=\widehat d-d\ge1\), \(a=\alpha_d\), \(b=\alpha_{d+h}\), and \[f=(b+\zeta)\bigl((a+\zeta)^{-1}-(1-\varepsilon)h\bigr).\] It increases with \(\varepsilon\), so it suffices to take \(\varepsilon=\zeta/100\). Direct expansion gives \[\begin{aligned}
 (a+\zeta)(1-f)
 &=a-b+hab+h(a+b-ab/100)\zeta\\
 &\quad+h(1-(a+b)/100)\zeta^2-h\zeta^3/100.\end{aligned}\] The constant term is nonnegative. For \(d\ge2\) it equals \[\frac{h((d-1)(d+h-1)-2)}{(d+1)(d+h+1)};\] for \(d=1\) it is \((h-1)(h-2)/(4(h+2))\). Also \(a+b\ge7/12\), \(ab\le1\), \(a+b\le2\), and \(0<\zeta<1\). The quadratic and cubic terms together are nonnegative. Hence \[1-f\ge \frac{43}{150}\zeta>\zeta/4,\] using \(a+\zeta\le2\). Thus \(c(\zeta)=\zeta/4\) is admissible. The independent script also checked 8,000 exact rational instances and the polynomial identity; those samples supplement, rather than replace, this proof.

## Lower bounds, rounding and dependency interfaces

The stated one-dimensional lower-bound family has the expected variance mechanism.
