Abstract: The full automorphism range asserted in the reviewed author manuscript does not ensure the ribbon structure needed to define the twisted invariant. A sixteen-dimensional Hopf algebra already used in the paper gives an explicit obstruction: a degree-preserving, cointegral-fixing involution exchanges every possible square root of the distinguished grouplike. This Major finding concerns existence and scope, rather than a numerical counterexample to a defined genus invariant. Three local corrections concern a right cup, the sign of a crossing tensor, and the variable and writhe factors in the ADO comparison. The Seifert-surface and filtration argument receives positive checks under the required ribbon hypotheses. This is a version-limited initial review; independent acceptance remains pending.

# The representation range needs a ribbon restriction

Problem 1 (Major; M25-0230-P1).  The claim on p. 3 that the degree-twisted invariant is defined for every \(\rho\) with values in \(\operatorname{Aut}^0_{\mathbb N^m}(H)\), and the inference to \(G'\)-ribbon in §3.4, p. 13, are too broad. Fixing the cointegral does not guarantee the requisite invariant ribbon data.

Section 2.4, p. 8, correctly requires a subgroup \(G\) fixing a chosen Kauffman–Radford pair \((b,\beta)\), together with a square-root homomorphism for \(r_H|_G\). Section 3, p. 9, likewise assumes a \(G\)-ribbon structure. Section 3.4 then takes \(G=\operatorname{Aut}^0_{\mathbb N^m}(H)\) and obtains \(G'\)-ribbon solely from the displayed square root of \(r_{H'}\). The latter condition does not supply the former one. The same full \(G\) is used at the start of §4, p. 20.

## A counterexample within the paper’s own family

Take \(p=2\) in §5, pp. 24–25. Thus \[H_2=\mathbb C\langle k,E\rangle/(k^8-1,E^2,kE-iEk),\qquad
 \Delta(E)=E\otimes k^2+1\otimes E,\quad \Delta(k)=k\otimes k.
 \tag{1}\] The basis is \(k^jE^e\), \(0\le j<8\), \(e=0,1\), and \(|E|=1\), \(|k|=0\). Define \[\tau(k)=k^5,\qquad \tau(E)=E.                 \tag{2}\] Since \(i^5=i\), \((k^5)^2=k^2\), and \(25\equiv1\pmod8\), this is a degree-preserving Hopf involution. The left cointegral \[\Lambda=\sum_{j=0}^7 k^j E\] is fixed by \(\tau\), which merely permutes its eight summands. Consequently \(\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}^0_\mathbb N(H_2)\).

For completeness, the distinguished grouplike can be obtained without guessing it from the proposed pivot. Define the right integral by \[\lambda(k^6E)=1,\qquad
 \lambda(k^jE^e)=0\quad\hbox{on the other basis vectors}.\] The identity \[\Delta(k^jE)=k^jE\otimes k^{j+2}+k^j\otimes k^jE\] gives \[(\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(x)=\lambda(x)1,\qquad
 (\operatorname{id}\otimes\lambda)\Delta(x)=\lambda(x)k^6.\] Thus the distinguished grouplike is \(a=k^6\).

All grouplikes of \(H_2\) are powers of \(k\). Indeed, if a grouplike had a nonzero highest homogeneous component of positive degree \(d\), its tensor square would have a nonzero component of bidegree \((d,d)\), whereas its coproduct has total degree at most \(d\). Hence it lies in the degree-zero group algebra, whose grouplikes are exactly the \(k^j\). The only possibilities for \(b^2=a\) are therefore \[b=k^3\quad\hbox{or}\quad b=k^7.\] They satisfy \[\tau(k^3)=k^7,\qquad \tau(k^7)=k^3,      \tag{3}\] so neither is fixed.

This example does satisfy the earlier assumption that \(D(H_2)\) is ribbon. The paper’s pair is \[b=k^3,\qquad \beta(k)=e^{3\pi i/4},\qquad\beta(E)=0.\] Here \(b^2=k^6\), \(\beta^2(k)=-i\) is the distinguished character, and both \(\operatorname{ad}_b\) and \(\operatorname{ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\) multiply \(E\) by \(-i\), giving \(S^2(E)=-E\).

## Why another ribbon choice does not remove the obstruction

The neutral ribbon element must be fixed by \(\varphi_\tau\), by condition (4) on p. 7. The canonical Drinfeld element \(u\) of \(D(H_2)\) is invariant under this action, since the canonical \(R\)-tensor and the antipode are natural under Hopf automorphisms. Thus its pivot must be invariant as well. Any ribbon pivot has the form \(\beta'\otimes b'\), with \((b')^2=a\). Equivalently, the full correspondence in [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition 2.3, p.~9]{dep2}] requires \(G\)-invariant grouplikes \(b',\beta'\). Equation (3) rules out every possible \(b'\). There can be no compensating scalar in the other tensor factor: \(\beta'(1)=1\), so equality of the two simple tensors forces equality of their \(H_2\) factors.

This is consistent with the earlier cited version: [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition 2.3, p.~9]{dep1}] already explicitly requires \(G\) to fix \(b,\beta\). The reference number Proposition 2.4 used in the reviewed manuscript does not match the ribbon proposition in either acquired version of [23]; the mathematical hypothesis, rather than the numbering, is the substantive point.

For any knot, abelianization followed by reduction modulo two gives a representation sending a meridian to \(\tau\). It lies in the stated \(\operatorname{Aut}^0_\mathbb N(H_2)\) range. Adding the degree twist does not repair the obstruction: \(\theta\) fixes \(k\), so \(\tau\theta\) still exchanges both roots in (3), even if one restricts to the image generated by \(\tau\theta\).

## Impact and checked repair

This disproves the asserted availability of the needed structure for the full advertised representation range. It does not produce a numerical violation of the genus inequality for an already defined invariant. Reading the opening \(G\)-ribbon assumption of §3 as an additional implicit restriction saves a conditional result, but then the quantifiers of the introduction and Theorem 1 must retain that restriction.

A concrete repair is to specify \[G\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}^0_{\mathbb N^m}(H),\qquad
 G\text{ fixes a valid }(b,\beta),\qquad \operatorname{im}\rho\subseteq G,
 \tag{4}\] with the required square-root character. The graded extension is compatible with these data: grouplikes have degree zero, and a character vanishes on the positive-degree nilpotent ideal, so \(\theta\) fixes \(b,\beta\). The displayed square root in §3.4 then has the required multiplicativity.

The obstruction does not affect the standard degree-scaling subgroup used for ADO, which fixes \(k\) and its selected pair. Nor does it invalidate the exterior-algebra specialization with its canonical pair. These are material counterchecks on the scope of the finding.

# Two local tangle formulas

Problem 2 (Minor; M25-0230-P2).  The right cup on p. 29, §5.6, is not inverse to the displayed right cap. Its exponent of the basis index has the wrong sign.

Write \(q=\zeta_p=e^{\pi i/p}\). The printed coefficients are \[a_i=q^{\alpha(1-p)-2i},\qquad
 b_i=q^{-\alpha(1-p)-2i}.\] With the stated ordinary dual basis, the snake composite on \(v_i\) is multiplication by \[a_i b_i=q^{-4i}.                \tag{5}\] At \(p=3\), \(i=1\), this is \(q^2\ne1\), independently of \(\alpha\); a generic \(\alpha\) may therefore be used. At \(p=2\) the same check is identically one, which explains why that endpoint alone cannot detect the error. Since (5) depends on \(i\), an overall normalization cannot fix it.

The cap agrees with the pivot. In the paper’s notation \(\mathbf k=q^{\alpha/2}\bar k\) and \(\mathbf k v_i=q^{\alpha/2-i}v_i\), so \[g_\alpha v_i
 =q^{-\alpha(p-1)}\bar k^{\,2p+2}v_i
 =q^{\alpha(1-p)-2i}v_i.\] The corrected cup is consequently \[1\longmapsto
              \sum_{i=0}^{p-1}q^{-\alpha(1-p)+2i}
                            v_i^*\otimes v_i.             \tag{6}\] Its snake composite is the identity on every basis vector. The proof of Proposition 5.4 says that the cap and cup use \(g_\alpha^{\pm1}\), which is the correct conceptual prescription. The finding is a local correction to the printed construction; it is not a counterexample to the corrected ADO invariant.

Problem 3 (Minor; M25-0230-P3).  The formal tensor \(R_D\) on p. 12 omits the inverse for negative crossings, despite the correct rule on p. 11.

The displayed tensor uses \(\bigotimes_j R_{\alpha_j,\beta_j}\) for all crossings, even though the paragraph distinguishes the label conventions at positive and negative crossings. It must use \(R_{\alpha_j,\beta_j}^{\operatorname{sign}(c_j)}\) with the conventions already given on p. 11.

This discrepancy is visible in an ordinary example without super signs. Let \(H=\mathbb C[C_3]\), \(G=1\), and give \(H\) degree zero. For a generator \(g\) and the dual functions \(\delta_j\), the double has \[\begin{split}
 R&=\sum_{j=0}^2(\varepsilon\otimes g^j)\otimes(\delta_j\otimes1),\\
 R^{-1}&=\sum_{j=0}^2(\varepsilon\otimes g^{-j})\otimes(\delta_j\otimes1).
 \end{split}                                             \tag{7}\] The terms with \(\delta_1\) distinguish the tensors. In a single negative open crossing, each strand has one bead, the crossing action is trivial, and the subsequent permutation is invertible; hence it cannot erase the difference. The correct inverse in (7) multiplies \(R\) to the unit. Since p. 11 already supplies the correct rule, this is a local repair of the total formula, not a demonstrated failure of the invariant defined by those rules.

# The ADO comparison and its scalar factors

Problem 4 (Minor; M25-0230-P4).  The proof of Proposition 5.4, pp. 29–30, changes the declared variable in one line and omits a writhe factor in its final comparison.

The definition immediately above the proposition uses \(t=q^{2\alpha}\). Thus the proof’s \(ADO_p(K,q^\alpha)\) should read \(ADO_p(K,q^{2\alpha})\) under the convention chosen in this paper.

More substantively, let \(w=w(D)\). The last display on p. 29 gives \[Z_\alpha v_0
   =q^{w\alpha^2}
        \sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij0}q^{\alpha(i-j)/2}v_0.\] The sum is the counit evaluation used in the next paragraph. The correct comparison is therefore \[\left.\varepsilon(Z^\theta_{D(H_p)})\right|_{t=q^{2\alpha}}v_0
                  =q^{-w\alpha^2}Z_\alpha v_0,            \tag{8}\] rather than the printed equality without that factor. For example, \(p=3\), \(\alpha=1/2\), \(w=1\) gives the nontrivial phase \(q^{w\alpha^2}=e^{\pi i/12}\). At zero writhe this issue disappears.

There is a positive repair check: combine the preceding ADO normalization \(q^{w(-\alpha^2+\alpha(p-1))}\) with the factor \(q^{w\alpha^2}\) relating \(Z_\alpha\) to the counit. The result is \[q^{w\alpha(p-1)}
                    =t^{w(p-1)/2},\] exactly the factor in equation (2), p. 14, and in the definition of \(P_{H_p}^{\theta}\) on p. 29. Thus the corrected calculation supports the intended final equality; these two formula corrections do not disprove it.

# Positive checks on the proof chain

## Seifert surfaces and the filtration

Under (4) and the correct tangle rules, the essential degree mechanism in §4 can be reconstructed. A genus-\(g\) Seifert surface is presented as a disk with \(2g\) bands. The two boundary strands of a band have opposite meridian homology classes, so \[\theta(\mu_i'\mu_i'')=1.\] Thus the invariant of the underlying \(2g\)-component tangle \(T\) has no \(t\)-powers. The partial longitudes in the stated surface-framing construction have zero knot homology. The coproduct, the modified antipode, the crossing maps and the permutation in Proposition 4.1 then give the boundary invariant. The source diagrams for these operations, including Figures 4–8, were actually inspected.

Put \(N=d(H)\). For a homogeneous term \((p\otimes a)t^{-J}\), use the budget \[d(a)+d(J).\] In multiplication, the extra exponent is \(-|a_{(3)}|\), while the remaining \(H\) factor is \(a_{(2)}b\). Nonnegative grading gives \[d(a_{(2)})+d(a_{(3)})\le d(a),\] so Lemma 4.2 preserves the sum of the two input budgets. In Lemma 4.3 the two factors \[t^{-|a_{(2)}|}
                  t^{|\alpha^{-1}(a_{(2)})|}\] cancel because \(\alpha\) preserves degree. The residual multiplication is controlled by Lemma 4.2. These are inequalities for every homogeneous summand; no absence of cancellation is assumed.

Each of the \(2g\) modified antipodes contributes \(t^{|\Lambda|/2}\) through the inverse pivot. Removing the common \(t^{g|\Lambda|}\) leaves a term of filtration degree at most \(2gN\). Consequently the total exponents of surviving monomials lie in \[[\,g\,d(\Lambda)-2gN,\;g\,d(\Lambda)\,],
                                                               \tag{9}\] whose width is \(2gN\). Evaluation and cancellation can only shrink this interval, and the framing normalization is one common monomial. The argument does not need the additional assertion \(d(\Lambda)=N\) to obtain this width.

For \(g=0\), the straight unknot gives \(Z=1\) and \(P=1\) directly; one need not interpret the iterated-multiplication symbol with exponent \(4g-1=-1\). The zero-polynomial case needs a conventional assignment of degree, or a statement for nonzero invariants, because the max/min definition on p. 3 otherwise has empty support.

The displayed filtration is not exhaustive on the whole Laurent coefficient module, since it contains only nonpositive integral powers. This does not invalidate the argument: after removing the explicit pivot monomial, all terms used in the proof lie in the specified part. Similarly, p. 19’s language about exact extrema must allow coefficient cancellation; the required upper bound survives.

## The quantum-group comparison

Several delicate components of §5 also pass direct checks. The group indexing the degree-scaled family is the additive group \(\mathbb C\). The character \(q^{\alpha(p-1)}\) is a square root there; at even \(p\) its possible failure to descend to the image in \(\operatorname{Aut}(H_p)\) is not an error in this explicitly \(\mathbb C\)-indexed construction.

For \(J_{\alpha,\beta}=1\otimes\mathbf k^\alpha\), both sides of the cocycle identity reduce to \(1\otimes\mathbf k^\alpha\otimes\mathbf k^{\alpha+\beta}\). Conjugation by \(J\) removes the extra scalar factors in the coproducts of \(E\) and \(F\). The diagonal conjugations in the braid argument cancel under the stated partial closures, with the unchanged pivot.

The finite Fourier sum on p. 28 uses the character \[\sum_{j=0}^{4p-1}q^{(-b-i/2)j}.\] It is \(4p\) when \(i\equiv-2b\pmod{4p}\) and zero otherwise. Substitution gives \[q^{\alpha\beta-a\beta-b\alpha+2ab}
   =q^{\alpha\beta/2}
      q^{(\alpha-2a)(\beta-2b)/2},\] as asserted. Also, \(p\ge2\) and \(0\le m<p\) keep the factors in \([m]!\) away from \([p]=0\). These checks support the intended ADO identification once the local formulas above are corrected; they do not amount to an independent recomputation of all knot tables.
