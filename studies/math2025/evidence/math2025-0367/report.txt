Abstract: The review concerns the exact 25-page preprint arXiv:2405.17194v2. An admissible genus-three construction has irreducible intersection polynomial of degree four but fails the strict signature criterion used in the proof of Theorem 14. This establishes a Major gap in the parameter range of that auxiliary theorem’s proof; an independent calculation confirms that this particular example still has stretch factor degree eight. Two further, locally repairable issues concern substitution in Remark 8 and the distinction between the full characteristic polynomial and the Perron factor in the proof of Theorem 5. No counterexample to the principal Torelli existence theorems is established. All source text and the relevant source images were read; computational and external-dependency limitations are retained.

# Identity, scope, and dependency structure

The associated journal identifier is DOI 10.4171/jems/1752; its publication page records 8 December 2025. The journal text and the author’s separately linked manuscript have not been assumed identical to this input. The frozen annual identifier is M25-0367. Page numbers below are one-based PDF pages and agree with the printed numbering.

Theorems 1 and 2 concern existence in the Torelli group for every genus \(g\geq2\), with trace degrees \(1\leq d\leq3g-3\) and stretch degrees \(2d\). Theorem 3 uses filling multicurves; its Torelli construction applies for \(g\geq3\), while the genus-two Torelli case is supplied separately by the words on page 3. For an intersection matrix \(X\), write \(\mu\) for the Perron eigenvalue of \(XX^\top\). The intersection degree is \([\mathbb Q(\mu):\mathbb Q]\), whereas the bipartite degree is \([\mathbb Q(\sqrt\mu):\mathbb Q]\). These are distinct from the stretch factor degree.

Theorem 4 obtains the quadratic extension over the trace field after choosing powers of the multitwists. Its norm argument, the nonzero roots of the reciprocal minimal polynomial, and the eventual-square argument were followed with their quantifiers retained. In the Torelli application the exponent signs are opposite. Separating twists act trivially on homology, and matched bounding pairs give equal actions for the two multitwists; their opposite powers therefore remain in Torelli. Section 6 instead studies same-sign products without the adjustable power supplied by Theorem 4. The paper explicitly makes that section independent of Theorems 1–3.

# Findings

Problem 1 (The signature argument does not cover all allowed parameters).  Theorem 14, page 22, is stated for an example described in Section 6.1. In Case 2 of its proof, page 23, a one-dimensional block parameter is then set to \(3\) without retaining that restriction in the statement or in the preceding construction.

#### Exact admissible construction.

Equation reference: C. Take \(g=3\), \(d=4\), so that \(f=2g-d=2\) in Section 6.1, Case 2. In Figure 11 take \(k=2\) and remove both handle-separating red components. The remaining one-dimensional block parameters may be any positive distinct integers, as page 22 expressly allows. Choose \[y_1=5,\qquad y_2=6,\qquad y_{\rm inner}=10.\] There are \(10^2-2=98\) copies of the inner connecting blue curve. The matrix before closure is \[A=\begin{pmatrix}400&2&2\\2&5&0\\2&0&6\end{pmatrix}.\] Close the surface by the construction on pages 12–13, using \(y_{\rm close}=20\). Since \(A_{11}/4=100\), the new blue curve has \(20^2-100=300\) copies, a positive integer. The resulting matrix is \[
 C=XX^\top=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 400&200&1&1\\200&400&2&2\\1&2&5&0\\1&2&0&6
 \end{pmatrix}.\] Thus this is the stated filling construction, with its permitted positive multiplicities; it does not rely on a hypothetical matrix unrelated to the curves. More explicitly, an intersection matrix has the following column types and multiplicities: \[\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{column}&(1,2,1,0)^\top&(0,0,1,0)^\top&(1,2,0,1)^\top&
(0,0,0,1)^\top&(1,2,0,0)^\top&(1,0,0,0)^\top\\
\text{multiplicity}&1&4&1&5&98&300
\end{array}\] These give a \(4\times409\) matrix \(X\) and reproduce [C] (#C) exactly.

Independent exact factorization over \(\mathbb Q\) gives irreducibility of both \[\begin{aligned}
 \chi_A(t)&=t^3-411t^2+4422t-11956,\\
 \chi_C(t)&=t^4-811t^3+128820t^2-1341545t+3586800.\end{aligned}\] In particular the required intersection degree is exactly four, and the two specializations satisfy the irreducibility requirements of the construction.

#### Decisive calculation.

Equation reference: failure. The successive leading principal minors of \(C-4I\) are \[396,\qquad116816,\qquad115636,\qquad230092.\] Sylvester’s criterion proves that every eigenvalue of \(C\) exceeds \(4\). For \(\Omega=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&X\\X^\top&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), the eigenvalues other than zero are the four pairs \(\pm\sqrt{\mu_j}\). Consequently \(\Omega+2I\) has \(409\) positive and four negative eigenvalues, and no zero eigenvalues. Its dimension is \(413\), so \[
 \sigma(\Omega+2I)+\operatorname{null}(\Omega+2I)=405
 =\dim\Omega-2d.\] The strict lower inequality in equation (1), page 22, fails. This is a counterexample to the assertion that the stated construction automatically satisfies the criterion used to prove Theorem 14.

The issue is substantive for this proof: replacing a freely chosen block parameter by \(3\) changes the example. It cannot prove the asserted conclusion for the original parameters. The shifts in the signature notation on pages 23–24 should also be written consistently as \(\Omega'+2I\); restoring that shift does not change [failure] (#failure).

#### What is and is not disproved.

This calculation does not disprove the stretch degree conclusion. Indeed, the independently calculated polynomial \[\begin{split}
 z^4\chi_C(2+z+z^{-1})={}&z^8-803z^7+123982z^6-838374z^5\\
 &+1660480z^4-838374z^3+123982z^2-803z+1
\end{split}\] is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Thus this particular example has the claimed degree eight. The confirmed defect is failure of the proof’s decisive criterion over the theorem’s declared parameter range, assessed as Major. No false universal stretch-degree statement is inferred from failure of a sufficient criterion.

#### Bounded repair.

An existentially chosen subfamily can retain one remaining one-dimensional block parameter \(y_i\leq3\), with the other parameters fixed distinctly and the large free parameters selected by the same irreducibility construction. A Rayleigh quotient then gives an eigenvalue below \(4\); sufficiently large free parameters also give an eigenvalue above \(4\). Hence the number of eigenvalues above \(4\) lies strictly between \(0\) and \(d\), which is precisely the signature requirement. This repairs a suitably restricted existence formulation. It does not supply a proof of the unrestricted Theorem 14. The main Torelli existence argument through Theorem 4 is not contradicted, and the maximal-degree construction of Section 6.3 is not the low-degree family in this example.

Problem 2 (Zero is allowed as the simple eigenvalue in Remark 8).  Remark 8, page 7, extends Proposition 7 by replacing the characteristic-polynomial variable \(t\) with \(t^n\) for every \(n>0\). Under the proposition’s stated hypotheses this extension is false.

Take \[M=\begin{pmatrix}-2&1&0\\-5&2&1\\-2&0&2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad N=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.\] These are integer matrices, and \(N\) is exactly the prescribed principal submatrix. Their characteristic polynomials are \(\chi_M=t(t-1)^2\) and \(\chi_N=(t-2)^2\), so they have no common eigenvalue and \(M\) has the simple eigenvalue \(\rho=0\). Choose the allowed values \(c=1\), \(p=2\), and put \(\widetilde M=M+y^2E_{11}\). Direct determinant calculation gives \[\chi_{\widetilde M}(t^2,y)
 =\bigl[t(t^2-1)-y(t^2-2)\bigr]
  \bigl[t(t^2-1)+y(t^2-2)\bigr],\] a nontrivial factorization in \(\mathbb Z[t,y]\).

The proposed Eisenstein element \(\mu_\rho(t^n)\) need not be irreducible; when \(\rho=0\), substitution also multiplies its root multiplicity. A sufficient repair is to require a nonzero simple eigenvalue. Its minimal polynomial has nonzero simple roots, so after substitution each irreducible factor still occurs with multiplicity one and is coprime to the substituted coefficient polynomial. One such factor can be used in Eisenstein’s criterion. The positive simple Perron eigenvalues in Lemmas 10–11 meet this repaired condition. Thus the broad remark is false, but no additional loss of the intended positive-matrix applications is established; the issue is Minor.

Problem 3 (The full characteristic polynomial is not always the Perron factor).  The opening assertion in the proof of Theorem 5, page 20, says the preceding construction gives an irreducible full characteristic polynomial of degree \(d\) for every degree. The preceding low-degree construction explicitly retains a factor \((t-4)^f\).

For example, take \(g=4\), \(d=2\), and \(f=g-1-d=1\) in Section 5.2.3, with the two repeated parameters equal to one. The displayed family is \[R(y)=\begin{pmatrix}64y^2&32&32\\32&4&0\\32&0&4\end{pmatrix},\] and exact calculation gives \[\chi_{R(y)}(t)
 =(t-4)\underbrace{\bigl[t^2-(64y^2+4)t+256y^2-2048\bigr]}_{P(t,y)}.\] Positive copy counts are available, for example at \(y=3\); the parameter \(\rho\) in the source then equals \(y^2-6=3\). The polynomial \(\chi_{R(y)}(t^2)\) always retains \(t^2-4\) and cannot be the minimal polynomial of the Perron square root as claimed. This is an actual factorization, not a possible exceptional Hilbert specialization.

The local correction is to use the factor containing the Perron eigenvalue. For the displayed example, \(P(t^2,y)\) is irreducible, as checked exactly. More generally, if \(s=f+1\) identical leaves have diagonal entry \(4\), the equal-leaf subspace has an integer quotient matrix. Its first-row coupling to that leaf is multiplied by \(s\), while the reverse coupling is unchanged. The other leaf entries are distinct. Removing the first row and column gives a diagonal matrix with distinct entries; evaluation of the quotient characteristic polynomial at any of them is a nonzero coupling product times the remaining differences. Hence there is no common eigenvalue. The zero-parameter quotient is nonnegative and irreducible, with a positive simple Perron eigenvalue. Proposition 7 and the corrected nonzero-eigenvalue version of Remark 8 apply to this quotient. The discarded difference subspaces account for \((t-4)^f\).

This restores the low-degree algebraic step with its multiplicity weights retained. At degree one, duplicating one of a separating filling pair of curves twice gives \(\mu=2m^2\), where \(m\) is the original intersection number, and hence bipartite degree two. The polynomial-identification error is Minor: a concrete surviving Perron-factor argument is available. No counterexample to Theorem 5 is claimed.
