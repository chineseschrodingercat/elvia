Abstract: One auxiliary maximum estimate has a false boundary coefficient. A smooth quadratic integrand with fixed structural constants and constant boundary data gives an explicit counterexample. Retaining the factor two actually obtained by the source’s proof repairs the estimate and preserves its use in the approximation scheme. The issue is Minor. The internal exponent, absorption, and limiting arguments were checked across the stated regimes; no counterexample to Theorem 1 or Theorem 2 is established. External regularity and sharpness inputs are identified as limitations, and the scientific decision remains pending.

# The auxiliary maximum estimate

Equation reference: eq:false. Problem 1 (Minor: Lemma 8.1, p. 35, (8.16)–(8.18)).  Under (7.1)\(_1\), the printed claim \[
 \|u\|_{L^\infty(B)}
 \leq\max\{\|u_*\|_{L^\infty(B)},\,c\mu|B|^{1/n}\}\] cannot hold with \(c=c(n,p,q,\widetilde L)\). The boundary term cannot retain coefficient exactly one in this maximum.

## A fixed-data counterexample

Equation reference: eq:counter, eq:growth. Let \(B=B_1(0)\subset\mathbb R^2\), and fix \[p=q=2,\quad \alpha=1,\quad \mu=1,\quad
 L=\widetilde L=L_*=4.\] For \(M>0\), set \[
 F(x,z)=2|z+x/2|^2+2,\qquad
 u_*(x)=M,\qquad u(x)=M+\frac{1-|x|^2}{4}.\] The integrand is smooth, independent of \(M\), and satisfies more than the growth hypothesis required by Lemma 8.1. Writing \(H=|z|^2+1\), direct completion of squares gives \[\begin{aligned}

 F-H&=|z+x|^2+1-\tfrac12|x|^2\geq\tfrac12,\\
 4H-F&=2|z-x/2|^2+2-|x|^2\geq1.\notag\end{aligned}\] Thus \(H\leq F\leq4H\) on \(B\). For (7.1)\(_1\), the required lower bound is \((L_*^{-1}+\widetilde L^{-1})H=H/2\) and the upper bound is \(2\widetilde L H=8H\), so both hold. The original bound (1.11)\(_2\) holds as well. Also \[D_{zz}F=4I,\qquad D_zF(x,z)=4z+2x,\] which verifies both ellipticity bounds and the coefficient Hölder bound for these constants. The strict gap is \(1<1+1/2\). If a larger ambient domain is desired, the same hypotheses hold on \(B_{5/4}\), which contains \(\overline B\).

Equation reference: eq:energy. The zero-boundary difference \(u-u_*=(1-|x|^2)/4\) belongs to \(W_0^{1,2}(B)\), and \(Du=-x/2\). For every \(\eta\in W_0^{1,2}(B)\), \[
 \mathcal F(u+\eta,B)-\mathcal F(u,B)
 =2\int_B|D\eta|^2\,dx.\] Hence \(u\) is the unique minimizer in (8.15), not just a formal solution of its Euler equation.

Its essential supremum is \(M+1/4\). For any proposed structural constant \(c\), choose \(M>c\sqrt{\pi}\). The right side of [eq:false] (#eq:false) is then \(M\). All structural data, the integrand, and the ball remain fixed while \(M\) varies. This disproves the stated quantifier on \(c\), not merely a particular numerical value.

The source defines \(\lesssim\) separately on p. 8; (8.17) uses the literal \(\leq\), with \(c\) only in its second entry. Reading a constant outside the entire maximum would avoid this counterexample, but changes the printed statement. No condition \(D_zF(x,0)=0\) is assumed; imposing it would unnecessarily narrow the paper’s setting.

## Complete local repair and downstream use

Equation reference: eq:repair. On the unit ball the source chooses \[\kappa_\infty\geq\|u_*^+\|_\infty,\qquad
 \kappa_\infty\geq C\mu,\] and its level-set iteration concludes \(u_+\leq2\kappa_\infty\), as printed immediately after (8.18). Choose \(\kappa_\infty\) to be the maximum of those two bounds. Apply the same argument to \(-u\), and rescale. This proves the corrected statement \[
 \|u\|_{L^\infty(B)}
 \leq2\max\{\|u_*\|_{L^\infty(B)},\,C\mu|B|^{1/n}\}.\] The corresponding two one-sided statements have the same factor two. At \(\mu=0\), the original coefficient-one maximum principle already follows from (8.18) and Poincaré; no iteration is needed. Equation [eq:repair] (#eq:repair) remains valid at that endpoint. This repair uses exactly the argument in the source and requires no new assumption.

Equation reference: eq:downstream. In the approximation \(\operatorname{App}(B_\rho)\), (8.2) gives \[\|\widetilde u_\varepsilon\|_\infty
 \leq(1+\rho)\|u\|_\infty+\rho,\qquad
 0<\rho\leq1,\quad \mu_\delta\leq2.\] Applying [eq:repair] (#eq:repair) to \(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\) gives \[
 \|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|_\infty
 \leq C\bigl(\|u\|_\infty+\rho\bigr),\] which is precisely the form of (8.12), p. 34. The structural constant can absorb the factor two. Consequently (8.13)–(8.14), the ordered limits, and the Theorem 2 estimate retain the same exponents and dependencies.

This is classified Minor because the false assertion is an overly precise constant in an auxiliary bound; the needed weaker estimate is already proved and is explicitly the form used downstream. Neither principal theorem is refuted.

## Approximation and ordered limits

Writing \(X=\|D\widetilde u_\varepsilon\|_q^q\), the artificial coercivity coefficient obeys \[\sigma_\varepsilon X
 =\frac{X}{1+\varepsilon^{-1}+X^2}
 \leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{-1}}}
 \leq\frac{\sqrt\varepsilon}{2}.\] Its energy contribution vanishes even if the recovery gradients have unbounded \(L^q\) norms. The mollification error is \(O(\delta^\alpha(1+|z|)^q)\); the estimate on minimizing gradients is available for each fixed \(\varepsilon\). The order \(\delta\to0\) followed by \(\varepsilon\to0\) is therefore essential and is respected.

The weak limits retain the relevant zero-boundary differences. Lower semicontinuity, minimality of the assigned relaxed minimizer, and strict gradient convexity identify the final limit with that minimizer. Estimates are not transferred to an unspecified solution. The p. 34 sentence about gradients being bounded in \(W^{1,q}\) has the functions in view; the displayed bounds and subsequent weak convergence require only gradients bounded in \(L^q\). ## Fractional comparison and the sharp absorption

The main gap implies the autonomous-estimate range \(q/p<1+2/(n-1)\). The improved autonomous exponent is \[s=\frac{2q}{(n+1)p-(n-1)q}\] in dimensions two and at least four, with the strict enlargement specified in dimension three. Thus the Caccioppoli coefficient has power \(M^{s(q-p)}\), which must be retained quantitatively.

The subquadratic comparison remainder has the larger integrability power, since \[\frac{p(q-1)}{2(p-1)}-\frac{2q-p}{2}
 =\frac{(2-p)(q-p)}{2(p-1)}\geq0
 \quad(1<p<2).\] The comparison uses a large but finite gradient moment. The choice \(\beta_0=1/(1+\alpha k-p\delta)\) balances the two difference-quotient powers. The cube covering has uniformly bounded overlap after dilation, so summing does not introduce a factor proportional to the number of cubes. The fractional Sobolev step uses \(\beta<\widetilde\alpha\), retaining the divergent factor at equality rather than invoking that endpoint.

Equation reference: eq:absorb. For \(0<\alpha<1\), choose \(k\) near one and \(\delta\) near zero so that \[\frac{\alpha}{2}<\beta<\widetilde\alpha
 <\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}.\] Set \(R=q/p\), \(D=n+1-(n-1)R\). The exponent of the unknown Lipschitz norm in (10.29) is \[
 \mathfrak t=\frac{sn(R-1)}{4\beta}
 =\frac{R}{D}\frac{n(R-1)}{2\beta}<1.\] Indeed \(D-R=1-n(R-1)>0\), so the first factor is below one; the second is below \(\alpha/(2\beta)<1\). For \(n=3\), replace \(R/(4-2R)\) by \(R/(4-2R)+\epsilon\), choosing \(\epsilon>0\) small enough to keep it below one. This is allowed because \(R<4/3\). For \(q=p\), \(\mathfrak t=0\), and Young absorption is unnecessary. For \(\alpha=1\), choose \(\alpha'\in(n(R-1),1)\) and use the local \(\alpha'\)-Hölder bound. This gives the claimed Lipschitz and nonoptimal Hölder conclusions without asserting \(C^{1,1}\).

The nonlinear potential bound is applied strictly above its integrability threshold. Young’s inequality and the radius iteration then close the Lipschitz estimate. Constants may deteriorate as the strict gap closes. The final weak limits can be taken at each finite gradient exponent before passing to infinity, as the source’s footnote explicitly explains.
