\maketitle
Abstract: We examine the performance-difference and entropy-decay arguments in the acquired journal article on Fisher–Rao policy flows. The central decay identity and its stated value and policy bounds pass the checks described here. An extraneous discount factor makes the estimates of Lemma 4.1 false, including in an elementary two-action example. The preceding derivative estimate supplies a direct correction preserving the local Lipschitz property needed for the existence argument. A separate static inspection finds a lagged-value discrepancy in the linked numerical source. Neither finding establishes failure of the principal continuous-time convergence theorem.\par
\section*{Source and precise scope}
The paper is B. Kerimkulov, J.-M. Leahy, D. Šiška, L. Szpruch and Y. Zhang, A Fisher–Rao Gradient Flow for Entropy-Regularised Markov Decision Processes in Polish Spaces, DOI 10.1007/s10208-025-09729-3 [paper]. The reviewed source is the 75-page publisher version of record, published online 11 August 2025 and appearing on journal pages 2395–2469 in the 2026 volume. File pages below are one-based PDF pages; journal pages equal file pages plus 2394.\par
The detailed review covers the bounded-logit policy class, the performance-difference identity, value dissipation, the entropy argument for Theorem 2.8, and the local Lipschitz estimate used in the existence proof. The full differentiability construction, all measure-theoretic lifting details for arbitrary primal solutions, the complete inexact and parameterized-flow analyses, and the discrete theorem are not independently certified. The acquisition record reports no separate supplement found in its bounded search; Appendices A–D are included in the acquired article.\par
\section*{Definitions, hypotheses, and a successful core check}
The state and action spaces are Polish, the transition kernel is measurable, the cost \(c\) is bounded measurable, \(0\le\gamma<1\), \(\tau>0\), and the action reference \(\mu\) is a probability measure (PDF pp. 15–18). The relevant policies have bounded logits: \[\pi(f)(da\mid s)=\frac{e^{f(s,a)}}{\int e^{f(s,a')}\,d\mu(a')}\,\mu(da),
 \qquad f\in B_b(S\times A).\] Thus each log density is bounded, above and below. The result does not start from an arbitrary singular policy. With \[Q_\tau^\pi=c+\gamma PV_\tau^\pi,\qquad
 a_\pi=Q_\tau^\pi+\tau\log(d\pi/d\mu)-V_\tau^\pi,\] the Bellman relation gives \(\int a_\pi\,d\pi(\cdot\mid s)=0\). The flow is \(\dot\pi=-a_\pi\pi\); the centered identity preserves mass. The dual flow on bounded logits and its gauge freedom are stated on PDF p. 19.\par
For completeness, subtracting the two Bellman equations gives \[(I-\gamma P_\pi)(V_\tau^\pi-V_\tau^{\pi'})
 =\int\left(Q_\tau^{\pi'}+\tau\log(d\pi'/d\mu)\right)
                  d(\pi-\pi')
   +\tau\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\mid\pi').\] Since \(\left\lVert\gamma P_\pi\right\rVert\le\gamma<1\), the Neumann resolvent exists. Its normalized kernel is the occupancy kernel \(d^\pi=(1-\gamma)\sum_{n\ge0}\gamma^nP_\pi^n\). This reproduces Lemma 2.3, PDF p. 18, including the orientation of the KL divergence and the factor \(1/(1-\gamma)\).\par
The dissipation calculation in PDF pp. 40–41 then yields \[\frac{d}{dt}V_\tau^{\pi_t}(s)
 =-\frac1{1-\gamma}\int_S\int_A a_{\pi_t}(s',a)^2
                \,\pi_t(da\mid s')d^{\pi_t}(ds'\mid s)\le0.\] The centering term is essential for the square; it is present in the article. Given the bounded-logit differentiability framework used there, the algebra and sign are correct.\par
Equation reference: eq:entropy. Let \(\pi^*\) be the entropy-regularized optimal policy and fix an initial distribution \(\rho\). Set \[D(t)=\int_S\mathrm{KL}(\pi^*(\cdot\mid s)\mid\pi_t(\cdot\mid s))
                      \,d_\rho^{\pi^*}(ds),\qquad
 G(t)=V_\tau^{\pi_t}(\rho)-V_\tau^{\pi^*}(\rho).\] The occupancy weight here is fixed at the optimal policy; it is not differentiated with the current flow. The log-partition calculation and performance difference give, as on PDF pp. 44–46, \[
 D'(t)=-(1-\gamma)G(t)-\tau D(t).\] Since \(G\ge0\) is nonincreasing, \[D(t)\le e^{-\tau t}D(0),\qquad
 G(t)\le\frac{\tau D(0)}{(1-\gamma)(e^{\tau t}-1)}.\] Pinsker’s inequality in the full total-variation norm gives \[\int_S\left\lVert\pi_t-\pi^*\right\rVert_{M(A)}^2\,d_\rho^{\pi^*}
 \le2e^{-\tau t}D(0).\] These are the quantitative conclusions of Theorem 2.8 (PDF p. 20). The derivation does not require convexity of the value in the policy.\par
As a separate exact check, in a one-state model with action costs \(c_i\), the action log-density differences satisfy \[\frac{d}{dt}(Z_i-Z_j)=-(c_i-c_j)-\tau(Z_i-Z_j).\] Their solution is \[Z_i(t)-Z_j(t)=e^{-\tau t}(Z_i(0)-Z_j(0))
              -\frac{1-e^{-\tau t}}{\tau}(c_i-c_j).\] This agrees with convergence to the Gibbs policy. The case \(\gamma=0\) remains a valid member of the stated problem class.\par
\section*{A false auxiliary bound with a direct correction}
Problem 1 (Lemma 4.1, PDF p. 41).  The displayed value-function Lipschitz estimate has an extra factor \(\gamma\); the corresponding \(Q\)-estimate inherits it.\par
Writing \(K=\left\lVert c\right\rVert_\infty+2\tau(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_\infty+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_\infty)\), the lemma asserts \[\left\lVert V_\tau^{\pi(f)}-V_\tau^{\pi(g)}\right\rVert_\infty
 \le\frac{2\gamma K}{(1-\gamma)^2}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_\infty,
 \qquad
 \left\lVert Q_\tau^{\pi(f)}-Q_\tau^{\pi(g)}\right\rVert_\infty
 \le\frac{2\gamma^2 K}{(1-\gamma)^2}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_\infty.\] Take one state, two actions, self-transition, \(c=0\), \(\mu=(1/2,1/2)\), \(f=(1,-1)\), \(g=(0,0)\), and any \(\tau>0\). All logits and costs are bounded, and both policies belong to \(\Pi_\mu\). Put \[H=\mathrm{KL}(\pi(f)\mid\mu)=\tanh1-\log\cosh1>0.\] Then exactly \[V_\tau^{\pi(f)}-V_\tau^{\pi(g)}=\frac{\tau H}{1-\gamma},
 \qquad
 Q_\tau^{\pi(f)}-Q_\tau^{\pi(g)}=\frac{\gamma\tau H}{1-\gamma}.\] At \(\gamma=0\), the first printed bound has zero right side and strictly positive left side. The problem is not confined to that endpoint. Indeed \[H=\int_0^1 u\,\operatorname{sech}^2u\,du>\tfrac18,\] because \(\cosh1<2\). For \(\gamma=1/100\), validity of either printed inequality would require \(H\le4/99\), which contradicts \(H>1/8\).\par
Equation reference: eq:correct. The first mean-value inequality in (4.3) inserts the same unsupported factor \(\gamma\). Proposition 3.8, PDF p. 35, instead gives \[\left\lVert dJ(h)\right\rVert\le
 \frac2{(1-\gamma)^2}(\left\lVert c\right\rVert_\infty+2\tau\left\lVert h\right\rVert_\infty),
 \qquad J(h)=V_\tau^{\pi(h)}.\] Applying this along \(h=g+\theta(f-g)\), \(0\le\theta\le1\), and using \(\left\lVert h\right\rVert_\infty\le\left\lVert f\right\rVert_\infty+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_\infty\) proves the corrected bounds \[
 \left\lVert V_\tau^{\pi(f)}-V_\tau^{\pi(g)}\right\rVert_\infty
 \le\frac{2K}{(1-\gamma)^2}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_\infty,\qquad
 \left\lVert Q_\tau^{\pi(f)}-Q_\tau^{\pi(g)}\right\rVert_\infty
 \le\frac{2\gamma K}{(1-\gamma)^2}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_\infty.\] The second follows from \(Q=c+\gamma PV\). This establishes the correction without a numerical experiment.\par
The supported assessment is Minor. The estimates as printed are false, but the same preceding proposition supplies finite local Lipschitz constants. The existence argument uses local Lipschitzness and the independent a priori bound of Proposition 4.2 (PDF pp. 41–42), rather than requiring the erroneous extra factor. This finding therefore does not undermine the local existence mechanism, the continuation bound, or the verified identity [eq:entropy] (\#eq:entropy).\par
\section*{A discrepancy confined to the acquired numerical release}
Appendix D (PDF pp. 70–72) compares Euler, midpoint and fourth-order Runge–Kutta updates for \[f(Z)=-Q_\tau^{\pi(Z)}-\tau Z+V_\tau^{\pi(Z)}.\] The linked repository was acquired at commit `5624f106bc249044533dbce79137bb4574c72f3f` (recorded 27 January 2025). It is not established that this exact revision generated the published figures.\par
In `mirror\_descent.py`, function `mirror\_f`, lines 84–85, the current policy evaluation is assigned to `V`, but the advantage call receives `V=old\_V`. The evaluator in `softmax\_PIA.py` begins with `V=np.copy(V\_old)`, so mutation of the incoming array does not make these values interchangeable. The returned current value is used as a subsequent starting value, not as the value in that same vector-field calculation.\par
This is not generally an innocuous state-only gauge change. If \(U\) is the supplied old value and \(V\) the current one, the two directions differ by \[f_U(s,a)-f_V(s,a)
 =(U-V)(s)-\gamma\int(U-V)(s')P(ds'\mid s,a).\] The first term is independent of the action and disappears on applying the mirror map. The second can depend on the action. For example, in an abstract three-state MDP, take two actions from a state \(s\) leading respectively to absorbing states of per-step costs zero and one, with zero immediate cost at \(s\). Under uniform initial actions, the two absorbing values differ by \(1/(1-\gamma)\). With \(U=0\), the stale direction gives zero action contrast at \(s\); the current direction gives contrast \(\gamma/(1-\gamma)\). This is an analytic demonstration of the formula’s possible effect, not an execution of the Gridworld class.\par
The acquired Euler, midpoint and RK4 routines call this function; the source also contains matching figure drivers. Its parameters \(n=11,\gamma=0.8,\tau=0.1\) agree with Appendix D. Passing the freshly evaluated `V` into the advantage calculation would remove this particular lag, subject to policy-evaluation tolerance. No source was modified or executed here, and no corrected experiment was reproduced.\par
This is a Minor discrepancy in the available numerical implementation. The current source alone does not establish an error in the historical figures or a failure of the analytic flow theorem. The release’s use of \(\tau\log\pi\) instead of KL against a uniform reference differs by an action-independent cost constant; that convention is not itself a policy-update defect.\par
\section*{Interpretation and remaining limits}
The assessed issues are limited to an auxiliary constant and the current code-to-vector-field correspondence. The principal entropy decay argument passes the bounded checks above. This is not a full certification of every existence, differentiability or approximation claim in the article.\par
The continuous-time result does not automatically supply an exponential rate for every discretization. Theorem 2.14, PDF p. 24, explicitly adds an occupancy-density condition for its exponential discrete estimate. Likewise the unregularized discussion, PDF p. 25, identifies necessary existence and absolute-continuity restrictions; simply setting \(\tau=0\) does not give the same theorem for arbitrary policies. Inexact and parameterized-flow bounds require the stated error and concentrability controls; those quantities were not measured here.\par
Overall assessment: Minor. The local analytic correction [eq:correct] (\#eq:correct) is verified. The numerical routing correction is identified statically, with its experimental effect unverified. Author adoption is unknown. Scientific adjudication remains pending; no human review or independent acceptance is recorded.\par
