Abstract: The inspected shooting argument supports the stated radial, subcritical, \(n\ge3\) scope. One peripheral dimension-scope defect is demonstrated: Section 2 permits real \(n>1\), but Proposition 2.5(iii) asserts a strict positivity which fails at \(n=2\). An analytic construction supplies an admissible nodal solution and an interior point where the asserted positive quantity is zero. The main \(n\ge3\) uniqueness theorem is not refuted by this example. Singular-node and tail steps in the main proof admit the explicit justifications recorded below.

Title: A scope and shooting-proof audit of
Uniqueness of bound states

# Identity, scope, and outcome

This report concerns Moxun Tang’s Uniqueness of bound states to \(\Delta u-u+|u|^{p-1}u=0\) in \(\mathbb R^n\), \(n\ge3\), DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01379-0 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01379-0) [Tang]. The exact supplied publisher PDF has 47 pages, printed 245–291; PDF page \(r\) means printed page \(244+r\). The record was first published online on 10 October 2025.

A bound state is defined here to be a sign-changing radial solution. The main equation has \(n\ge3\) and \(1<p<(n+2)/(n-2)\); uniqueness is up to translation and change of sign. It is not a uniqueness claim for arbitrary nonradial sign-changing solutions. The finite-ball conclusion is also radial. Theorems 1–2 and their hypotheses are on PDF pp. 4–5.

The primary reviewer checked the preliminary energy identities, the conditional shooting reduction, selected phase and tail calculations, and the dimension counterexample. The ledger distinguishes text reading, analytic checks, image inspection, and externally cited dependencies. No numerical shooting or author code was executed. The outcome is one Minor finding in a broader preliminary assertion, with no additional substantive defect established in the inspected main-model proof. Independent scientific acceptance remains pending.

# Problem 1: strict positivity exceeds the stated dimensional range

## Claim and locations

Equation reference: eq:ivp, eq:P2. Section 2 explicitly allows the dimension to be a real variable in \((1,\infty)\) (PDF p. 6, printed p. 250). It studies \[
 u''+\frac{n-1}{r}u'+f(u)=0,\qquad u(0)=\alpha>0,\quad u'(0)=0.\] The definition on PDF p. 12, equation (2.20), is \[
 P_2(r)=r^n\left((u')^2+\frac{n-2}{n}uf(u)\right)
             +(n-2)r^{n-1}uu'.\] Under (C1)–(C3) and (C7), Proposition 2.5(iii) on PDF p. 13 claims \(P_2(r)>0\) and \(-[P(r)/r^n]'>0\), for all \(r>0\) for a ground/bound state, and up to the largest zero for a nodal solution. The proof on PDF p. 14 uses \[nP_2(c_i)=(n-2)c_i^n u(c_i)f(u(c_i))>0\] at a nonzero critical point. This strict sign needs \(n>2\).

## All assumptions of a counterexample

Equation reference: eq:choice. Set \[
 n=2,\qquad f(s)=s^3-s,\qquad
 F(s)=\frac{s^4}{4}-\frac{s^2}{2}.\] Then \(f\) is smooth and odd, negative for \(0<s<1\), and positive for \(s>1\), as required by (C1). Its unique positive primitive-zero is \(\alpha_*=\sqrt2>1\), giving (C2); \(-f'(0)=1>0\) gives (C3). Moreover \[h_2(s)=(n+2)f(s)-(n-2)sf'(s)=4(s^3-s),\] so (C7) holds with threshold \(\widetilde\alpha=1\). Condition (C4), although not needed in the displayed statement, also holds: \(f'(1)=2>0\).

Equation reference: eq:limit, eq:explicit. For completeness, the initial datum can be fixed analytically, rather than chosen from an uncertified numerical shooting plot. Let \(w_0\) be the unique regular solution of \[
 w_0''+\frac1t w_0'+w_0^3=0,\qquad w_0(0)=1,\quad w_0'(0)=0.\] Let \(c_-\) be its first negative minimum and \(c_+\) its following positive maximum. Both are finite, as proved below. Define \[
 T=c_+,\quad m=\min\{-w_0(c_-),w_0(c_+)\}>0,\quad
 \varepsilon=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{3m}{4(e^{3T^2}-1)}\right\},
 \quad \alpha=\varepsilon^{-1/2}.\] These formulas specify a positive real initial datum in terms of a fixed, parameter-free ODE. No decimal value of \(\alpha\) is asserted.

Equation reference: eq:counter-u. Let \(w_\varepsilon\) solve \[w_\varepsilon''+\frac1t w_\varepsilon'
       +w_\varepsilon^3-\varepsilon w_\varepsilon=0,\qquad
 w_\varepsilon(0)=1,\quad w_\varepsilon'(0)=0.\] Then \[
 u(r)=\alpha w_\varepsilon(\alpha r)\] solves [eq:ivp] (#eq:ivp) with [eq:choice] (#eq:choice). The construction below proves that \(u\) has at least two simple zeros and only finitely many zeros. Thus it has a largest zero \(z_k\), and a critical point \(c\) strictly between its first two zeros lies inside the precise range \((0,z_k]\) in Proposition 2.5.

## Existence and nodal properties of the specified solution

The energy for [eq:limit] (#eq:limit) decreases: \[\left(\frac{(w_0')^2}{2}+\frac{w_0^4}{4}\right)'
       =-\frac{(w_0')^2}{t}.\] Hence the regular solution is global and \(|w_0|\le1\). While it is positive, the flux \(tw_0'\) is strictly decreasing because \((tw_0')'=-tw_0^3\). If it stayed positive forever, after any fixed positive time \(w_0'\le-a/t\) for some \(a>0\), forcing a zero by logarithmic divergence. That zero is simple by ODE uniqueness.

After this zero, if \(w_0'\) never became zero, \(w_0\) would remain below a fixed negative value and \((tw_0')'\ge b^3t\) for some \(b>0\); this forces \(w_0'>0\), a contradiction. Thus the negative minimum \(c_-\) is finite. After the minimum the positive flux similarly forces a second zero. Following that zero, a persistently positive derivative is impossible because \((tw_0')'\le-b^3t\) once \(w_0\) has a fixed positive lower bound. The positive maximum \(c_+\) is therefore finite. The relevant extrema are strict and have the signs used in [eq:explicit] (#eq:explicit).

Equation reference: eq:gronwall. For \(0<\varepsilon\le1/4\), decreasing energy with potential \(s^4/4-\varepsilon s^2/2\) also gives \(|w_\varepsilon|\le1\) globally. The Volterra formula is \[w_\varepsilon(t)=1-\int_0^t s\log(t/s)
              \bigl(w_\varepsilon(s)^3-\varepsilon w_\varepsilon(s)\bigr)\,ds.\] For \(D(t)=|w_\varepsilon(t)-w_0(t)|\), use \(|a^3-b^3|\le3|a-b|\) for \(|a|,|b|\le1\), and \(s\log(t/s)\le T\) when \(0<s<t\le T\). Gronwall’s inequality gives \[
 D(t)\le\frac{\varepsilon}{3}(e^{3Tt}-1)\le\frac m4
       \qquad(0\le t\le T).\] Thus \(w_\varepsilon(c_-)<0\) and \(w_\varepsilon(c_+)>0\). Together with \(w_\varepsilon(0)=1\), this supplies two distinct zeros. They are simple, since a simultaneous zero of the solution and its derivative at positive time would force the solution to vanish identically.

The finite-zero conclusion used here is the cubic \(n=2\) instance of the energy argument in Proposition 2.4(ii), PDF pp. 9–11. That instance can be checked without the strict-positivity claim under review. If there were infinitely many zeros, decreasing energy would have a limit \(E_\infty\ge0\). When \(E_\infty>0\), consecutive extrema have uniformly bounded separation: the speed on the central crossing is bounded below, and outside \(|u|\le\sqrt2\) the cubic satisfies \(|f(u)|\ge f(\sqrt2)>0\). Each crossing dissipates a fixed positive multiple of \(1/r\); the resulting harmonic series contradicts finite total energy loss.

When \(E_\infty=0\), extrema approach absolute height \(\sqrt2\). For \(\widehat E=r^2E\), \(\widehat E'=2rF(u)\). The intervals above height \(\sqrt2\) have bounded length and their maximum \(F\)-value tends to zero. Each central passage through \(1/2\le|u|\le1\) has a length bounded below, since \(|u'|\) is bounded, and there \(F\le F(1/2)<0\). Over successive zero-to-zero intervals, these estimates give \(\Delta\widehat E\le-d r_i\) for some \(d>0\) and large \(i\). This forces \(\widehat E<0\), contrary to \(E>0\) under the infinite-zero hypothesis. All signs used here are valid at \(n=2\), where the weight exponent \(2n-3\) is \(1\). Thus the solution in [eq:counter-u] (#eq:counter-u) meets the largest-zero qualification of Proposition 2.5.

## Stated and actual values, impact, and local repair

At the critical point \(c\) between the first two zeros, [eq:P2] (#eq:P2) becomes \[P_2(c)=c^2(u'(c))^2=0,\] whereas the proposition asserts \(P_2(c)>0\). The identity (A.8), PDF p. 41, also gives \[-\left(\frac{P(r)}{r^2}\right)'
     =\frac{2(u'(r))^2}{r},\] so its asserted strict sign likewise becomes equality at \(c\).

At \(n=2\), the exact replacement is nonnegativity, with equality at critical points. These statements follow directly from the definitions and are a verified local correction. Retaining \(n>2\) for the strict statement excludes this demonstrated boundary failure; this report does not thereby certify every other generalization in Section 2.

The alternative reading that the original \(n\ge3\) restriction remains implicit is understandable, and would remove this example from scope. However, Section 2 explicitly enlarges the dimensional range, and no restriction is restated before Proposition 2.5. The supported severity is Minor: the broader preliminary strict-positivity assertion is false as written, while the main uniqueness theorem and Section 5 already restrict to \(n\ge3\). No core failure at the main theorem’s parameters follows from this test.

# Main-model checks and resolved concerns

## Conditional shooting reduction

Lemma 3.1 (PDF pp. 16–18) reduces the result to the ordering of zeros of \(v=\partial_\alpha u\), and its eventual divergence for a bound state. At a node \(z_i(\alpha)\), the checked sign \(u'v>0\) yields \[z_i'(\alpha)=-v(z_i(\alpha),\alpha)/u'(z_i(\alpha),\alpha)<0.\] Continuity and a strictly negative energy value beyond the last zero give the local constancy of the node count for oscillatory solutions. For a bound state, the diverging variation gives the one-sided jump through the maximum-principle comparison on a tail where \(f'<0\). The existence of prescribed-node solutions and the large-initial-value zero-radius limit remain the external existence/asymptotic dependencies cited in that section; they were not independently reproved here.

## The apparent singular endpoint in Lemma 4.2

On PDF p. 20, the first-zero argument for \(M\) involves \(T_1=Q-g_1(u)M\), although \(g_1(u)\) is singular at a node. For the actual model, this endpoint has a valid limiting justification. If both \(u(z_1)\) and \(v(z_1)\) vanish, put \(a=u'(z_1)<0\), \(b=v'(z_1)>0\), and \(s=z_1-r\). Then \(u=-as+O(s^2)\), \(v=-bs+O(s^2)\), and \[M'=(p-1)r^{n-1}u^pv=O(s^{p+1}),\qquad M=O(s^{p+2}).\] Since \(g_1(u)=O(s^{1-p})\), its product with \(M\) tends to zero. Consequently \(T_1\to Q(z_1)=z_1^nab<0\), contradicting the established positivity before the node. This resolves the apparent endpoint problem for every \(p>1\) in the main range.

## Phase bridge and tail

The identities \[Q'=2r^{n-1}f(u)v,\qquad
 M'=r^{n-1}(uf'(u)-f(u))v,\qquad
 B_a'=-\frac{2F_a(u)Q_n}{r(u')^2}\] were independently differentiated on their stated open intervals. They support the bridge in Lemma 4.3 (PDF pp. 21–22): at a hypothetical first \(Q_1\)-zero \(q\), positive energy gives \(B_0(q)=-2q^{n-1}(v/u')E(q)<0\), whereas monotonicity gives \(B_0(q)>B_0(z_1)=Q(z_1)>0\).

The Section 5 audit checked the phase-parity signs, the separate \(n=3,\ 1<p<2\) case, and the final induction to Lemma 3.1. It did not identify an omitted exponent range. In the tail argument on PDF pp. 38–39, assuming \(v\) bounded allows a direct estimate from \((r^{n-1}v')'=-r^{n-1}f'(u)v\): \[|r^{n-1}v'(r)|
 \le |R^{n-1}v'(R)|+\frac{CM}{n}(r^n-R^n).\] Thus \(v'=O(r)\). Exponential decay of \(u,u'\) implies \(Q\to0\); also \(F(u)=O(u^2)\) and \(u'/u\to-1\) imply \(B_0-Q\to0\). This contradicts the positive increasing tail of \(B_0\). These are analytic checks, not numerical reproductions. Isolated symbol slips whose intended values are fixed by the defining identities are not counted as substantive findings.
