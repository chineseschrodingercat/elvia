Title: Initial review of a stochastic differential equation
for local times of super-Brownian motion

# Scope and assessment

The principal source is Le Gall and Perkins [LP], arXiv:2309.06899v1 (13 September 2023), an acquired 32-page preprint. Its complete text was read. The additional 36-page journal PDF [LPj], obtained from the second author’s publication site, was compared at selected locations: the main theorem, the drift-constant computation, the end of the SDE proof, and the diffusion reconstruction. It was not reviewed in its entirety. Page references without a journal designation refer to the preprint.

No substantive mathematical defect was established in the checked arguments. In particular, the factors \(4\) and \(8\) in the SDE, the conditioning on one half of the local-time derivative, the localization at positive local time, and the final diffusion’s invariant density are mutually consistent. Two local display corrections are explained below; neither supplies evidence of a failed core derivation. This assessment does not certify the external excursion-theoretic inputs or every probabilistic detail of the paper.

# The statement and its boundary conditions

The normalization in Section 3, p. 11, is branching mechanism \(2u^2\) with initial measure \(\alpha\delta_0\), \(\alpha>0\). At the origin the derivative means the right derivative; the jump between right and left derivatives is \(-2\alpha\). Theorem 1, p. 2, asserts \[\begin{aligned}
 d\dot L^x&=4\sqrt{L^x}\,dB_x+
       g(L^x,\dot L^x/2)\,dx,& dL^x&=\dot L^x\,dx,\\
 g(t,y)&=8t\,\frac{p_t'(y)}{p_t(y)},&&t>0,\end{aligned}\] with \(g(0,y)=0\). Here \(p_t\) is the density of the spectrally positive \(3/2\)-stable process whose Laplace exponent is \(\sqrt{2/3}\lambda^{3/2}\). Uniqueness is asserted for solutions absorbed at \(R=\inf\{x\geq0:L^x=0\}\), with the given initial pair. It is not a claim of unrestricted uniqueness for every continuation from the degenerate boundary. The possible enlargement of the probability space to supply Brownian motion after \(R\) is explicit.

Theorem 2, p. 3, is stated under the probability measure obtained from the canonical excursion measure by conditioning on \(\ell^0>\delta\). This conditioning must be retained. The statement is not, by itself, an SDE theorem for a tree conditioned to have total volume one.

The Lévy measure coefficient is \(c_1=\sqrt{3/(8\pi)}\); indeed \(c_1\Gamma(-3/2)=\sqrt{2/3}\). The sign-function martingale representation on p. 13 gives quadratic variation \(16\int L^x\,dx\), as stated in Proposition 11 on p. 14. Its square root is the noise coefficient \(4\sqrt L\). The moment argument uses \(q<4/3\), not the endpoint: the occupation estimate requires \(q/2<2/3\). These restrictions agree with the estimates used for stopped increments.

# An explicit verification of the drift constant

Equation reference: eq:constant. The constant calculation left to the reader in Lemma 16, p. 24 (journal p. 382), is a decisive normalization check. Write \[\chi(u)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}(u^{3/2}+u^{1/2})
       -2u(u+3/2)e^u\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt u),\qquad
 \gamma(u)=-\frac83\sqrt\pi\,u^{3/2}(\chi(u)+u\chi'(u)).\] The required identity is \[
 c_1\int_0^\infty
       \left(2-\gamma(3z/2)\right)\frac{dz}{\sqrt z}=8.\] It can be checked without numerical quadrature. Set \[E(v)=e^{v^2}\operatorname{erfc}(v),\quad
 P(v)=-2v^6+3v^4-6v^2+6,\quad
 Q(v)=2v^5-4v^3+12v .\] Using \(E'(v)=2vE(v)-2/\sqrt\pi\), direct differentiation gives \[\int_0^{v^2}u\chi(u)\,du
       =P(v)E(v)+Q(v)/\sqrt\pi-6.\] The erfc expansion displayed on p. 18 implies \[P(v)E(v)+Q(v)/\sqrt\pi=3v/\sqrt\pi+O(v^{-1}),\qquad
 \chi(R)=\frac{3}{2\sqrt\pi}R^{-3/2}+O(R^{-5/2}).\] Integration by parts therefore yields \[\begin{aligned}
 \int_0^R(2-\gamma(u))\frac{du}{\sqrt u}
 &=4\sqrt R+\frac83\sqrt\pi
       \left(R^2\chi(R)-\int_0^R u\chi(u)\,du\right)\\
 &\longrightarrow16\sqrt\pi .\end{aligned}\] The endpoint bounds on pp. 18–19 justify convergence. Changing variables \(z=2u/3\) in [eq:constant] (#eq:constant) multiplies this limit by \(c_1\sqrt{2/3}=1/(2\sqrt\pi)\), proving the value \(8\). An independently written symbolic check verifies the antiderivative, the cancellations, and the excursion-mean derivative in Proposition 13; it does not execute author code.

The compact-uniform limit in Lemma 16 is also consistent with its stated domain \(1/K\leq t\leq K\), \(|y|\leq K\). On this set \(p_t(y)\) has a positive lower bound and its first two derivatives have uniform bounds. The integrable majorant involving \(|2-\gamma(3z/2)|/\sqrt z\) then applies. No uniform assertion down to \(t=0\) is needed.

# Conditional expectations, stopping, and reconstruction

Proposition 14, pp. 19–20, handles the signed sum of excursion derivatives by truncation. It does not replace integrability of that signed sum by an unproved assertion that the sum of absolute derivatives is integrable. On the events \(H_n\), only the specified finitely many large excursions can contribute at the level in question. Conditional independence, the moment bound of Lemma 12, and the separate integrable bound on \(\sum_j Z_j|\gamma(3Z_j/(2h^2))|\) justify the two limiting expectations. This is relevant counterevidence to a possible objection based solely on exchanging an infinite signed sum.

In Section 6 the first localization keeps \(L\) bounded away from zero and both coordinates bounded. The finite drift limit at the boundary is obtained only afterwards. Lemma 17, p. 25, bounds its negative part by \(8C(L^x)^{1/3}\) on a finite interval; the stopped SDE on p. 28 then forces the positive part to have finite integral as well. The resulting absolute integrability is stronger than merely assigning a formal value to a divergent signed integral. Local Lipschitz coefficients on \(L>0\), followed by the stated absorption condition, give the claimed uniqueness.

For Proposition 18, pp. 29–30, put \(Z=\dot L L^{-2/3}\) before \(R\) and use the inverse clock of \(\int_0^x(L^y)^{-1/3}dy\). Itô’s formula gives noise \(4L^{-1/6}dB\) and drift \(L^{-1/3}b(Z)\,dx\), where \[b(z)=8\frac{p_1'(z/2)}{p_1(z/2)}-\frac23z^2.\] The time-changed noise coefficient is consequently \(4\). The proposed density \[\rho(z)=C\,p_1(z/2)^2 e^{-z^3/36}\] satisfies \((\log\rho)'=b/8\), the stationary zero-current identity for generator \(8\partial_z^2+b\partial_z\). The tail formulas on p. 30 are integrable at both ends, while the scale function has unbounded range in both directions.

There is also a useful check on the reconstruction in Proposition 19. With \(u=z/(2\,6^{1/3})\), the Airy representation on p. 2 reduces the stationary weight, up to a positive constant, to \(w(u)=(u\operatorname{Ai}(u^2)+\operatorname{Ai}'(u^2))^2\). For \(u>0\), \[w(u)-w(-u)=4u\operatorname{Ai}(u^2)\operatorname{Ai}'(u^2)<0 .\] Thus the stationary mean of \(Z\) is negative. Together with the finite moments furnished by the displayed tails, this supports the exponential decay of the reconstructed local time and finiteness of its inverse clock. This check is not a substitute for every boundary-continuity step in Proposition 19; that short final reconstruction argument has not been independently expanded into a complete proof here.

# Local display corrections and their limits

In the introduction on p. 3 the excursion-derivative argument is printed as \(3h/(2z^2)\). Proposition 13, equation (33), p. 17, uses \(3z/(2h^2)\), which is the expression obtained by differentiating equation (32) and is used in Proposition 14. The working proof therefore already contains the correct expression.

In the Brownian construction on p. 28, for \(0<\varepsilon'<\varepsilon\), one has \(S_{\varepsilon'}\geq S_\varepsilon\). The displayed variance should be \(\mathbb E[S_{\varepsilon'}\wedge t-S_\varepsilon\wedge t]\); the printed subtraction has the reverse order. The corrected nonnegative expression still tends to zero by bounded convergence and gives the asserted Cauchy property. These are local formula corrections with identified surviving applications, not demonstrated failures of the main theorem. No claim is made here that their status is identical in every version.

# Conclusion

The inspected argument has coherent normalizations and an appropriate absorbed-solution range. The drift constant and diffusion identities have independent analytic support. No substantive issue is assigned. The excursion disintegration and cited foundational results remain external inputs; reading the preprint is not a certification of those sources. The limited journal comparison confirms the inspected core formulas, but does not establish complete version equivalence.
