Abstract: The residual, normalization and displayed Jacobian are inconsistent, and the resulting iteration violates the principal quadratic-convergence estimate on the principal eigenline. Independently, the proposed projected descent cannot solve its own update equations, and the contour formula for the resolvent action encloses an additional pole and returns the wrong value. These are Major findings concerning central components of the proposed framework. The stated power-iteration initialization theorem also fails for admissible initial data, giving a fourth Major finding. The valid operator reductions, conditional perturbation arguments and explicitly acknowledged limits of full certification are distinguished from these defects.

# P1. The written Newton iteration does not satisfy its convergence theorem (Major)

## Two inconsistent definitions

Equation (3.1), PDF p. 17/printed p. 705, fixes \[\|Cu^\circ\|=2.\] The residual on the next page is \[R(u,\nu)=
\begin{pmatrix}(I-\nu C)u\\ 1-\|Cu\|^2/2\end{pmatrix}.\] Consequently \(R(u^\circ,\lambda^\circ)=(0,-1)\), not zero. The proof of Theorem 3.10 explicitly uses \(R(u^\circ,\lambda^\circ)=0\) on PDF p. 32/printed p. 720.

There is a separate derivative error. Over the real space, the actual derivative is \[DR(u,\nu)[h,\eta]
=\begin{pmatrix}(I-\nu C)h-\eta Cu\\-\langle Cu,Ch\rangle\end{pmatrix}.\] Equation (3.2), repeated in (3.5), uses \(-\langle Cu,h\rangle\) in its lower row. The missing \(C\) cannot be removed by changing the eigenvector normalization. The positive conclusions about invertibility of that displayed block matrix do not make it the derivative of the stated residual.

## Direct contradiction of the quadratic estimate

Set \(u=ae\) and take \(\lambda\) close to \(\lambda^\circ\), with \(1-\lambda\mu\ne0\). The explicit updates (3.7)–(3.8) give \[\langle Cu,(I-\lambda C)^{-1}Cu\rangle=\frac{\mu^2a^2}{1-\lambda\mu},
\qquad
a_{\rm next}
=\left(1-\frac\mu2\right)a+\frac1{\mu a}.\] This identity follows by cancellation and is independent of \(\lambda\). At the paper’s target amplitude \(a^\circ=2/\mu\), \[a_{\rm next}=\frac2\mu-\frac12.\] Choose \(u_0=u^\circ\) and \(\lambda_0=\lambda^\circ+\varepsilon\), with \(\varepsilon\ne0\) arbitrarily small and avoiding the other resolvent poles. The initial error is \(|\varepsilon|\), while the eigenvector error after one update is exactly \(1/2\). This contradicts (3.59), \[\|(u_1,\lambda_1)-(u^\circ,\lambda^\circ)\|
 \le \frac{\bar\beta\gamma}{2}|\varepsilon|^2,\] for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). It also covers the explicit-resolvent scheme’s requirement \(\lambda_0\ne\lambda^\circ\). For an exact rational corroboration, \(\mu=3\), \(a=2/3\) gives \(a_{\rm next}=1/6\).

Replacing the target norm \(2\) by \(\sqrt2\) alone does not repair the scheme. Its residual root on the eigenline is then \(a_*=\sqrt2/\mu\), but \[\left.\frac{d a_{\rm next}}{da}\right|_{a_*}=1-\mu.\] Except when \(\mu=1\), this is not a quadratic local map; for \(\mu>2\) the root is locally repelling. Such eigenvalues are permitted by the fission scaling above.

For comparison, using the true derivative on the eigenline gives \[a_{\rm next}^{\rm Newton}=\frac a2+\frac1{\mu^2a},
\qquad
(a_{\rm next}^{\rm Newton})'(a_*)=0.\] This verifies the local scalar correction. It does not certify the paper’s original neighborhood, inverse bounds, tolerances or general constants after changing the full Jacobian. Those parts require a consistent rederivation. The effect is central: Theorem 3.10 and subsequent uses of its exact error estimate are invalid for the formulas as written.

# P2. The proposed descent cannot realize arbitrary update accuracy (Major)

The residual least-squares functional in (4.29), PDF p. 40/printed p. 728, is \[Q(w,\nu)=\frac12\left(\|M_\lambda w-\nu f+g\|^2
 +(s-\langle f,w\rangle)^2\right),
\quad f=Cu,\quad g=M_\lambda u,\quad s=1-\|f\|^2/2.\] Its actual negative gradient has first component \[-M_\lambda^*(M_\lambda w-\nu f+g)
 -(\langle f,w\rangle-s)f.\] The displayed direction (4.30) instead has a plus sign on the second term. More substantially, the text asserts that the exact update lies in \(f^\perp=\{w:\langle f,w\rangle=0\}\), and proposes projected descent in that space. The second block of the update equation requires \[\langle f,w\rangle=s.\] These requirements conflict whenever \(s\ne0\).

A decisive admissible test uses \(\mu=1\), \(\lambda=1\), \(u=ae\), where \(a>0\) and \(s=1-a^2/2\ne0\). The initial value prescribed on PDF p. 41 is \(f-P_{\langle f\rangle}u=0\), with \(\nu=0\). On \(w=be\), \[Q(be,\nu)=\frac12\left(a^2\nu^2+(s-ab)^2\right),\] whose exact update is \((b,\nu)=(s/a,0)\). At the prescribed initial point, the printed first direction is \(-as\,e\), which equals the positive gradient there. Its directional derivative is \[DQ(0,0)[D(0,0)]=a^2s^2>0.\] Thus the unprojected direction is strictly ascending. The proposed projection annihilates this direction, while its scalar component is zero; the projected method remains at the initial point.

Even independently of this stationary example, every update constrained to \(f^\perp\) has \(Q\ge s^2/2\). In the eigenline example its distance from the exact update is at least \(|s|/a\), so it cannot meet arbitrary target tolerances. One may take \(a=2\), the paper’s own target amplitude, or take \(a\) arbitrarily near \(\sqrt2\) after repairing the normalization in P1. Hence P2 is a failure of the claimed update realization, not merely a consequence of the earlier normalization error.

A valid least-squares method must use the actual negative gradient and permit the required component along \(f\); for instance, the affine constraint \(\langle f,w\rangle=s\) can be separated from its orthogonal complement. This identifies necessary corrections but does not establish all claimed iteration counts or adaptive implementation bounds.

# P3. The contour formula encloses a forbidden pole (Major)

The alternative construction in (4.33), PDF p. 42/printed p. 730, claims \[(I-\bar\lambda C)^{-1}C\bar u
=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma
  \frac{\bar\mu\zeta}{\bar\mu-\zeta}
  (\zeta I-C)^{-1}\bar u\,d\zeta,
\qquad \bar\mu=\bar\lambda^{-1}.\] Its contour is the boundary of a disc centered at \(\bar\mu\), chosen to enclose the nearby principal eigenvalue. The scalar factor has a pole at the centre \(\zeta=\bar\mu\). It is therefore not holomorphic on the enclosed spectral region, as the claimed functional-calculus identity would require.

Take \(C\bar u=\mu\bar u\) with \(\bar\mu\ne\mu\). With positive orientation, the scalar integrand on this eigenline is \[\frac{\bar\mu\zeta}{(\bar\mu-\zeta)(\zeta-\mu)}.\] The two enclosed residues are \[\frac{\bar\mu\mu}{\bar\mu-\mu},
\qquad
-\frac{\bar\mu^2}{\bar\mu-\mu},\] so their sum is \(-\bar\mu\). The claimed left side is instead \(\bar\mu\mu/(\bar\mu-\mu)\). The clockwise convention printed in Section 3.2 reverses the integral’s sign, giving \(+\bar\mu\), and still does not recover that left side.

For example, after scaling \(\mu=1\), choose \(\bar\mu>1\) sufficiently close to one to meet the paper’s gap-dependent disc conditions. The desired coefficient becomes arbitrarily large as \(\bar\mu\downarrow1\), whereas the contour returns a coefficient tending to \(-1\) or \(+1\). The rational values \(\bar\mu=101/100\), \(\mu=1\) illustrate the discrepancy \(101\) versus \(-101/100\) whenever the stated disc encloses these two points.

This defect remains even with exact resolvent evaluations and a known gap. Convergent quadrature of the proposed integral would converge to the wrong operator action. A repair must exclude the scalar pole or account for its residue and handle the complementary spectral part; the original formula and subsequent error claim are not justified. No repaired implementation or complexity guarantee is certified here.

# P4. The global initialization guarantee fails for permitted initial data (Major)

The setup (5.1), PDF p. 43/printed p. 731, assumes a unit initial vector with \(\langle a_0^\circ,u_1\rangle\ne0\). For a nonnormal operator, the condition that selects the principal spectral component is \(E_C(\mu_1)a_0^\circ\ne0\), determined by the left eigenvector. Right-eigenvector overlap alone is insufficient.

There is an example inside the transport class. Take \(d=1\), \[D=(0,1),\qquad
V=[-3/2,-1]\cup[1,3/2],\qquad
\sigma=s_0>0,\quad \kappa=0,\quad \varphi=c>0.\] The velocity set has Lebesgue measure one, is compact, excludes zero and contains \(\{-1,1\}\). The remaining positivity, boundedness and continuity hypotheses hold. Let \(P_v\) be velocity averaging, the orthogonal projection onto velocity-independent functions in the constant-\(\sigma\) metric. Then \(F=cP_v\) and \(C=T^{-1}cP_v\).

A nonzero principal eigenfunction \(u_1\) cannot be independent of velocity. Otherwise the transport eigen-equation for two distinct velocities implies its spatial derivative is zero; the inflow conditions then force that constant to vanish. Hence \(h=(I-P_v)u_1\ne0\). The initial state \[a_0^\circ=h/\|h\|\] has norm one and \(\langle a_0^\circ,u_1\rangle=\|h\|>0\), yet \(Fa_0^\circ=0\) and \(Ca_0^\circ=0\). The first normalization in (5.1) is undefined. This is a direct failure under the written initial-data condition.

The later Lemma 5.6 (PDF pp. 49–50) explicitly assumes \(a_0^\circ\ge0\) and uses a positive principal Riesz component. This supplies a restricted result that excludes the counterexample. However, Section 5 makes initialization into the Newton convergence neighborhood an overarching objective and explicitly presents Theorem 5.1 as its main result. Under the theorem’s written condition, an admissible transport input prevents even the first normalization. This materially invalidates the stated global initialization guarantee and supports Major severity. The availability of a short positivity restriction does not make that central failure local. Under a nonzero correctly oriented principal component and a strict spectral gap, the qualitative complementary-spectral-radius argument remains sound; power initialization for nonnegative data is not rejected.

# Valid components, coverage and limitations

Appendix A’s weighted contractivity estimate can be recovered from the two kernel integral bounds and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, with \(\rho\le1-\alpha/\sup\sigma<1\). The resulting Neumann series justifies positivity of \(B^{-1}\) and \(C\). The compactness reduction \[C=(I-T^{-1}K)^{-1}T^{-1}F\] is valid conditional on the cited compactness result for the latter factor. Algebraic simplicity and Fredholm theory also justify bounded invertibility of the compressed resolvent in Lemma 3.1; no complete eigenbasis is needed for that step.

For an actual Newton map at a genuine zero, bounded inverse derivative and Lipschitz derivative yield the standard bound \(e_{n+1}\le(\bar\beta\gamma/2)e_n^2\). An update error of at most \(\eta_n\) adds at most \(\eta_n\) to this estimate. These conditional perturbation principles are valid, but they cannot be applied to the incompatible definitions in P1 or the unsuccessful realization in P2. Likewise, a complementary spectral component with radius below \(\mu_1\) decays at any larger ratio below one, with a possibly large constant. This supports the correctly restricted qualitative power-method statement without certifying every printed Schatten constant.

The paper expressly says in Sections 5.2 and 6 that it does not provide a directly applicable fully certified eigensolver and that the needed spectral information remains difficult to obtain. This acknowledged limitation is not counted as a defect. The bounded acquisition search located no separate author repository or supplement; that status is not a claim of global absence and has no bearing on the mathematical severity.
