Abstract: This review concerns the manifest-pinned arXiv author manuscript 2304.05881v3, not an independently verified journal-final version. Three defects are supported: a missing value-group condition in the axiomatization used for decidability transfer, an incorrect discriminant identity, and a characteristic-two boundary failure in an illustrative example. A countermodel, an exact cubic calculation, and a cyclotomic identity establish the respective defects. Explicit local repairs are justified. No failure of the main elementary-embedding or Fontaine–Wintenberger conclusions is demonstrated. The outcome is Major for the invalid proof of the advertised decidability transfer; the other two defects are Minor. These judgments are limited to the recorded inspection scope.

# Definitions, quantifiers, and main checks

A perfectoid field here is a non-discretely valued, complete, real-valued field of residue characteristic \(p>0\), with surjective Frobenius on \(\mathcal O_K/(p)\). Its tilt is denoted \(K^\flat\). The enlarged class \(\mathcal C\) permits higher-rank henselian valuations, with a distinguished nonzero nonunit \(\varpi\), semiperfect \(\mathcal O_v/(p)\), and algebraically maximal \(\mathcal O_v[\varpi^{-1}]\). Rank one must not silently be imposed on arbitrary models of this elementary class.

The valuation \(w\) in Theorem 6.2.3 is the coarsest coarsening of the ultrapower valuation \(v_U\) with \(w\varpi>0\). The assertion that finite extensions are unramified concerns \((K_U,w)\). It does not assert that every finite extension is unramified for \(v_U\) or for the original valuation of \(K\). The infinitesimal convex subgroup relative to \(v\varpi\) is collapsed in passing to \(w\).

The following checks support the scope of the review.

1. In Theorems 5.1.2 and 5.1.4, the quotient-ring and value-group conditions are substantive. Taking reduced quotients and then fraction fields retains the induced residue valuation. The resplendent embedding step must retain that extra structure. Regular density explains where value-group conditions can be omitted; it is not automatic for an arbitrary higher-rank model of \(\mathcal C\).

2. In Lemma 6.1.2, for a compatible system \(x=(x_n)\), \(v(x_n)=p^{-n}v(x_0)\). For nonzero \(x\) these values become infinitesimal relative to a fixed positive standard value. Thus \(x^\natural\) is a \(w\)-unit. Addition modulo \(p\) on integral elements gives addition in the residue field. General elements reduce to this case after multiplication by a common integral denominator, whose image remains a \(w\)-unit.

3. In Theorem 6.1.3, the nonstandard iterated Frobenius on \(\mathcal O_{v_U}/(\varpi)\) is surjective with kernel generated by \(\pi=\operatorname{ulim}\varpi^{1/p^i}\). Its induced isomorphism, together with the elementary diagonal map, proves the claimed elementarity of the truncated-ring map. The containment \(\mathfrak m_w\subseteq\pi\mathcal O_{v_U}\) justifies the next quotient. The final application of Theorem 5.1.4 uses the stated regular-density and class-\(\mathcal C\) hypotheses, independently of Problem 1.

4. In Lemma 6.2.1, the discriminant congruence and the strict inequality \(v(\operatorname{disc}f)<v\varpi\) imply \(v(\operatorname{disc}g)=p^{-1}v(\operatorname{disc}f)\). The Hensel–Rychlik step produces a root of \(f\) in \(K(\beta)\). Then \([K(\beta):K]\leq\deg g=\deg f\), while containment of a root of irreducible \(f\) gives the reverse bound. This supplies the degree argument implicit in the conclusion. Integral-generator normalization is needed when passing to general monic polynomials in the subsequent discussion.

5. In Theorem 7.2.5, countable cofinality of positive values for the original rank-one field matters in identifying the kernel of the ultrapower-localization functor with almost-zero modules. Tensor maps are initially natural comparison maps; their being isomorphisms is established under the appropriate uniform finiteness hypotheses. Faithfulness reflects isomorphisms of morphisms, and does not assert that arbitrary isomorphic image objects lift to isomorphic objects.

6. In Lemma 7.2.19, the choice \(p\,w\varepsilon<w\varpi<2p\,w\varepsilon\) gives both required ideal containments. Two successive lifts yield \(x=y_1^p+\varepsilon^p y_2^p+\varepsilon^{2p}x_2\). Reduction modulo \(\varpi\) removes the final term and the mixed binomial terms, since \(p\in\varpi\mathcal O_v\).

Full independent reconstructions of the external tame-field embedding theorems, all resplendence arguments, and the general Gabber–Ramero almost-module results were not performed.

# Problem 1: the theory used for decidability transfer is incomplete

## Source claim and suspect inference

Corollary 5.3.5, PDF page 37, claims that \(\operatorname{Th}(K^\flat,v^\flat)\) is decidable relative to \(\operatorname{Th}(K,v)\). Its proof constructs a theory \(T\) of perfect henselian characteristic-\(p\) valued fields \((F,u,t)\) for which \(\mathcal O_F[t^{-1}]\) is algebraically maximal, and adds \[\mathcal O_F/(t)\equiv\mathcal O_{K^\flat}/(t)\cong\mathcal O_K/(p).
 \tag{3.1}\] It then asserts, using Theorem 5.1.7, that \(T\) decides every \(L_{\rm val}\)-sentence exactly as \(K^\flat\) does.

Theorem 5.1.7 on page 34 additionally requires elementary equivalence of the pointed value groups \((\Gamma,u(t))\). That information is not among the listed axioms of \(T\). Source lines 1567–1572 confirm the omission; it is not an extraction artifact.

## A countermodel satisfying every listed condition

Fix a prime \(p\), let \(K=\mathbb C_p\), and let \(E=K^\flat\). The rank-one value group is \(\Gamma_E=\mathbb Q\), normalized so that \(v_E(t)=1\), where \(t=(p,p^{1/p},p^{1/p^2},\ldots)\). This choice exists in \(\mathbb C_p\), and \[\mathcal O_E/(t)\cong\mathcal O_K/(p).\] Set \(G=\mathbb Z[1/p]\) with its usual order and form the full Hahn field \[F=E((z^G)).\] Give it the Hahn valuation \(w_z\), with residue field \(E\), and then the composite valuation \(u=w_z\circ v_E\). Explicitly, if \(f=\sum c_gz^g\neq0\) has least exponent \(g_0\), then \[u(f)=(g_0,v_E(c_{g_0}))\in G\mathbin{\mathrm{lex}}\mathbb Q.
 \tag{3.2}\] Regard \(t\) as a constant series. The assumptions are checked as follows.

1. \(F\) has characteristic \(p\) and is perfect: \(E\) is perfect, \(G\) is \(p\)-divisible, and taking coefficientwise \(p\)-th roots and dividing every exponent by \(p\) preserves well-ordered support.

2. The full Hahn field is spherically complete for \(w_z\). Indeed, a nested family of balls prescribes compatible initial coefficient segments. Their union has well-ordered support: a hypothetical decreasing sequence in that support would lie below one prescribed radius and hence in a single center’s well-ordered support. Choosing all unspecified coefficients to be zero gives a point in every ball. Thus \(w_z\) is maximal, in particular henselian and algebraically maximal. The coefficient valuation \(v_E\) is henselian because \(E\) is a complete rank-one valued field. Their composite \(u\) is henselian.

3. \(u(t)=(0,1)>0\), so \(t\) is a nonzero nonunit. Inverting \(t\) clears any negative leading-coefficient value when the leading exponent is zero, while it cannot change a negative leading exponent. Therefore \[\mathcal O_u[t^{-1}]=\mathcal O_{w_z}.
 \tag{3.3}\] The localization is algebraically maximal, as required.

4. The residue map \(\mathcal O_u\longrightarrow\mathcal O_E\) for \(w_z\) is onto, and its kernel \(\mathfrak m_{w_z}\) is contained in \(t\mathcal O_u\): division by the constant \(t\) preserves positive \(z\)-order. Consequently \[\mathcal O_u/(t)\cong\mathcal O_E/(t)
 \tag{3.4}\] as rings, which is stronger than (3.1).

Thus both \(E\) and \(F\), with these choices of \(t\), satisfy the listed theory \(T\).

Choose a prime \(\ell\neq p\). The interpretable value-group sentence \[\sigma_\ell:\quad
 \forall\gamma\ \exists\delta\;(\ell\delta=\gamma)
 \tag{3.5}\] holds in \(E\), since \(\mathbb Q\) is divisible. It fails in \(F\): the element \((1,0)\) of \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\mathbin{\mathrm{lex}}\mathbb Q\) has no \(\ell\)-th part. Hence neither \(\sigma_\ell\) nor its negation follows from \(T\). Enumerating proofs of those alternatives from the stated \(T\) need not terminate.

## Counterevidence, precise repair, and impact

The higher-rank field \(F\) is not claimed to be perfectoid. That is not an escape from the example: the proof explicitly axiomatizes a larger elementary class, whose members need not have rank one. The countermodel concerns completeness of this \(T\), not the truth of the corollary.

There is a direct repair using the same oracle. In mixed characteristic choose \(t\in\mathcal O_{K^\flat}\) with \(\mathcal O_{K^\flat}/(t)\cong\mathcal O_K/(p)\) and \(v^\flat(t)=v(p)\); the first coordinate of the distinguished Witt-vector generator supplies such a choice. Add to \(T\) the complete theory \[\operatorname{Th}(\Gamma_K,v(p))
 \quad\text{on the pointed group }(\Gamma_F,u(t)).
 \tag{3.6}\] The ring \(\mathcal O_K/(p)\) and the pointed value group \((\Gamma_K,v(p))\) are uniformly interpretable in \((K,v)\), with the fixed integer \(p\) supplying the parameter. Their theories can therefore be enumerated using the same \(L_{\rm val}(K)\) oracle. The resulting theory \(T^*\) is consistent because \(K^\flat\) is a model. Every model now meets all the hypotheses of Theorem 5.1.7 relative to \(K^\flat\), so its \(L_{\rm val}\)-reduct is elementarily equivalent to \(K^\flat\). Proof enumeration from this oracle-recursive theory terminates for every sentence or its negation. In characteristic \(p\), tilting identifies \(K^\flat\) with \(K\), so the decidability assertion is immediate.

The incomplete theory \(T\) invalidates the proof-enumeration procedure used to establish the decidability transfer advertised as Corollary 1.7.6 (page 9) and proved as Corollary 5.3.5 (page 37). This is a demonstrated invalid derivation of an explicitly promoted model-theoretic transfer result and is assessed as Major. The countermodel does not refute the corollary itself: the augmented theory \(T^*\) supplies a corrected proof conditional on the stated AKE theorem, whose proof is not independently reconstructed here. The main embedding Theorem 6.1.3 applies Theorem 5.1.4 directly and does not depend on the incomplete theory \(T\); no failure of the main embedding or Fontaine–Wintenberger conclusions is established.

# Problem 2: a derivative square is substituted for a norm

## Source claim

In the proof of Fact 2.4.7, page 18, implication \((iii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)\), the manuscript writes \[\operatorname{disc}(m_a)=m_a'(a)^2=\delta_{K'/K}(a)^2.
 \tag{4.1}\] The same formula appears at source line 715. Page 17 defines a signed discriminant: \[D(f)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^2.\] This convention is respected here; the issue is not its sign.

## Complete exact check

Take the henselian field \(K=\mathbb Q_7\), \(f(X)=X^3-2\), and \(a\) a root, with \(K'=K(a)\). The cubes modulo \(7\) are \(0,1,6\), so \(f\) has no residue root and is irreducible modulo \(7\). It is therefore irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_7\). Characteristic zero gives separability, and \(a\) is integral and a unit. Thus all assumptions needed for the displayed identity hold, with extension degree three.

The derivative is \(f'(a)=3a^2\), and \[f'(a)^2=9a^4=18a.
 \tag{4.2}\] Multiplication by \(3a^2\), in the basis \(1,a,a^2\), has matrix \[\begin{pmatrix}0&6&0\\0&0&6\\3&0&0\end{pmatrix},\] whose determinant is \(108\). Accordingly \[D(f)=\operatorname{N}_{K'/K}(f'(a))=108\neq18a=f'(a)^2.
 \tag{4.3}\] The inequality follows also because \(a\notin\mathbb Q_7\). The ordinary unsigned-convention discriminant is \(-108\). An independent exact symbolic check reproduced the determinant, resultant, and polynomial remainders; no author code was run.

## Correct criterion and impact

The valid identity under the manuscript’s convention is \[D(m_a)=\operatorname{N}_{K'/K}(m_a'(a)).
 \tag{4.4}\] Because the valuation is henselian, all conjugates of \(m_a'(a)\) have the same extended valuation. For \(n=[K':K]\), \[v(D(m_a))=n\,v'(m_a'(a)).
 \tag{4.5}\] The derivative is integral, so this gives exactly \[D(m_a)\in\mathcal O_K^\times
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m_a'(a)\in\mathcal O_{K'}^\times.\] In the explicit example, both quantities are units; the example disproves the algebraic equality, not the unramifiedness criterion. The subsequent uses inspected require that criterion or valuation bounds for discriminants, not the false square identity. Replacing (4.1) by (4.4)–(4.5) restores the local equivalence: Minor, with no demonstrated main-theorem failure.

# Problem 3: the distinguishing element in Example 5.3.2 fails for \(p=2\)

## Location and exact calculation

Example 5.3.2, page 36, source lines 1533–1534, compares \[K=\widehat{\mathbb Q_p(p^{1/p^\infty})},
 \qquad
 K'=\widehat{\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^\infty})},\] and distinguishes them by the asserted absence of \(p^{1/p}\) from \(K'\). No odd-prime restriction is imposed there.

For \(p=2\), let \(\zeta=\zeta_8\). This element belongs already to the indicated cyclotomic union, before completion. Since \(\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}=0\), \[(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^2=2.
 \tag{5.1}\] Thus \(K'\) contains a square root of \(2\). Both fields satisfy \(\exists x\;(x^2=2)\), so this proposed distinguishing sentence does not distinguish them. The exact remainder of \((z-z^3)^2-2\) modulo \(z^4+1\) is zero.

## Alternative readings and a supported restriction

The earlier assumption \(p\neq2\) in the separate example of Section 1.6.1 does not govern Example 5.3.2; it is also explicitly restated for the other relevant example in Section 7. No claim is made here that these two fields are elementarily equivalent when \(p=2\), or that no different distinguishing sentence exists.

The displayed argument is valid after restricting this example to odd primes. Indeed \(X^p-p\) is Eisenstein. If \(\mathbb Q_p(p^{1/p})/\mathbb Q_p\), of degree \(p\), were Galois, it would contain \(\zeta_p\), forcing \(p-1\) to divide \(p\), impossible for odd \(p\). Each finite subextension of the cyclotomic \(p\)-power union is abelian and hence Galois. Further, completion does not add algebraic elements over \(\mathbb Q_p\) to that union: if an algebraic \(\alpha\) lay in its completion, approximation by an element of one finite cyclotomic field and Krasner’s lemma would place \(\alpha\) in such a finite field. Consequently \(p^{1/p}\) is absent for odd \(p\), as required.

This is a boundary defect in an illustrative argument: Minor. Restricting that argument to odd \(p\) preserves the claimed existence of non-elementarily-equivalent untilts with isomorphic tilts. A replacement witness for \(p=2\) was not constructed and is not asserted.
