Convex polytopes from fewer points

# Identity and summary

The reviewed paper is Convex polytopes from fewer points, by Cosmin Pohoata and Dmitrii Zakharov, associated with Duke Mathematical Journal 174 (2025), 449–471, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2024-0034 (https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2024-0034). The actual source is the 15-page author manuscript arXiv:2208.04878v1, dated 9 August 2022 [paper]. Page numbers below are one-based PDF pages, agreeing with the printed numbers. The journal version was not acquired, so the findings are not attributed to its text.

Assessment: Major, concerning the displayed main derivation. The half-space construction in Proposition 3.1 excludes the point groups it is meant to contain. The subsequent cap assembly uses undefined or repeated endpoint triples and does not retain the claimed vertex count if those endpoints are merely removed. Explicit local corrections are verified below, and the corrected counting argument still yields the qualitative \(2^{o(n)}\) conclusion, conditional on the preliminary geometry. No counterexample to Theorems 1.1–1.3 is established.

A separate, local counterexample disproves Proposition 2.3 under its printed projection hypotheses. Its downstream application has additional projection structure excluding this example. The positive-fraction argument was checked for the stronger conclusion of separation of whole convex hulls, and its counting exponents are consistent after two local proof clarifications.

# Scope and operative hypotheses

The entire manuscript text was read. Actual source images and exact reviewer-written calculations were used to check the findings. These agents are recorded as AI assistants within this initial review, not as independent acceptance reviewers or different verified models.

General position means no four points are coplanar in \(\mathbb R^3\). It does not by itself guarantee that projection onto the first two coordinates is injective or in planar general position. A \(C\)-free set has every line through two of its points disjoint from \(C\); a \(C\)-cap has each point outside the convex hull of \(C\) together with the other points. A collection in convex position has each entire convex hull disjoint from the convex hull of all the other sets (pp. 2, 4). These distinctions matter.

The cited planar support-region prerequisite was compared with Suk’s Theorem 2.4, pp. 2–3 of arXiv:1604.08657v2 [suk]. Taking its parameter \(r=k+1\) gives \(k\) consecutive regions of density \(2^{-32(k+1)}\ge2^{-40k}\) for \(k\ge4\), covering the large-\(k\) application here. This substitution alone does not certify the separately stated \(k=3\) endpoint. The Four Color Theorem, the general Ramsey theorem, and the full original proofs of the imported geometric theorems were not re-proved.

# Problems and evidence

Problem 1 (Major: the constructed half-spaces have the wrong containments).  Proposition 3.1, pp. 8–9, requires \(Z_1\cup Z_3\subset P_J^1\) and \(Z_2\cup Z_4\subset P_J^2\). The proof orients \(H_1\) with \(Z_1\subset H_1^+\) and \(H_2\) with \(Z_2\subset H_2^+\), then defines \[P_J^1=H_0^-\cap H_1^+\cap H_2^+,\qquad
 P_J^2=H_0^-\cap H_1^-\cap H_2^-.\] These definitions conflict with the preceding separation requirements.

Indeed, \(H_2\) separates \(Z_1\cup Z_3\cup X_{j_2}\) from \(Z_2\cup Z_4\). With the printed orientation, the former group is on its negative side. Thus nonempty \(Z_1\) or \(Z_3\) is excluded from the proposed \(P_J^1\); the analogous problem occurs for \(P_J^2\).

Here is an exact geometric witness to the construction failure. Take singleton clusters \(X_i=\{(t,t^2,t^3)\}\), for \(t=-2,-1,0,1,2\) in order, and \(J=\{2,3,4\}\). All four-point affine determinants are nonzero by the Vandermonde formula; the planar projections are consecutive points on a parabola, and the above/below orientation of interlacing chords is consistent. Define the positive half-spaces by \(f_i\ge0\), where \[f_0=\tfrac12-y,\quad
 f_1=-z+\tfrac12y+\tfrac94x-\tfrac98,\quad
 f_2=z+\tfrac12y-\tfrac94x-\tfrac98.\] Their exact signs, without floating-point tolerance, are as follows: \[\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 t&-2&-1&0&1&2\\ \hline
 2f_0&-7&-1&1&-1&-7\\
 8f_1&35&-15&-9&5&-21\\
 8f_2&-21&5&-9&-15&35
\end{array}\] Consequently all the required preconstruction separations and printed orientations hold, while \(X_1,X_4\not\subset P_J^1\) and \(X_2,X_5\not\subset P_J^2\). The sign table and general-position determinants were independently evaluated in the saved exact-calculation record.

#### Impact and verified repair.

These polyhedra are used immediately in the chain/antichain coloring and then to certify convexity of the union of caps. Their containments are therefore part of the core derivation, rather than an unused label. This is the basis for the Major assessment; it is not a claim that the existence proposition or the final theorem is false.

Reversing the stated \(H_2\) orientation restores the intended point containments, but is insufficient for the asserted separation: the manuscript defines \(H^\pm\) to be closed half-spaces (p. 6). In the witness above, the two orientation-corrected polyhedra share the line \(y=9/4,\ z=9x/4\). In particular, \((0,9/4,0)\) belongs to both, and its line to the middle point \((0,0,0)\) meets all three sets.

A verified repair adds a positive margin. Put \(A=Z_1\cup Z_3\), \(B=Z_2\cup Z_4\), and \(C=\operatorname{conv}(X_{j_2})\). Choose affine defining functions \(h_0,h_1,h_2\) for the strictly separating planes, oriented so that their sign triples on \(C,A,B\), respectively, are \[(+,-,+),\qquad(-,+,+),\qquad(-,-,-).\] By finiteness of the input, choose \(\eta>0\) smaller than every absolute value of these functions at the input points. Define \[Q_1=\{h_0\le-\eta,\ h_1\ge\eta,\ h_2\ge\eta\},
 \qquad
 Q_2=\{h_0\le-\eta,\ h_1\le-\eta,\ h_2\le-\eta\}.\] Then \(A\subset\operatorname{int}Q_1\) and \(B\subset\operatorname{int}Q_2\). The inequalities for \(h_0,h_1,h_2\), respectively, strictly separate \(C\) from \(\operatorname{conv}(Q_1\cup Q_2)\), \(Q_1\) from \(\operatorname{conv}(Q_2\cup C)\), and \(Q_2\) from \(\operatorname{conv}(Q_1\cup C)\). Hence a line cannot meet all three sets: one of its three intersection points would lie between the other two, contradicting the corresponding strict separation. Each \(Q_i\) still uses three half-spaces, so the bounded truncation and asymptotic estimates below apply to these strengthened polyhedra. In the displayed witness, \(h=(f_0,f_1,-f_2)\) and \(\eta=1/4\) work. This repair does not independently settle every other preliminary lemma.

Problem 2 (Major: endpoint triples do not support the cap count).  On p. 9 the proof uses \(\{j_{l-1},j_l,j_t\}\) for every \(l\in[t]\), then forms the union of odd-indexed caps of size \(2n/t\). The triple is undefined at \(l=1\) and has a repeated index at \(l=t\), so the corresponding color and cap cannot be invoked.

Merely deleting the invalid endpoints can also delete the required cardinality. For instance, with \(t=5\), the printed odd indices are \(1,3,5\); only index \(3\) is valid, and a cap of size \(2n/5\) does not supply \(n\) vertices. This is a defect in the actual concluding inference, not a counterexample to the existence of a convex \(n\)-set by other means.

#### A complete local index correction.

Let \[I=\{2,4,\ldots\}\cap\{2,\ldots,t-1\},\qquad
 m=|I|=\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 a=\left\lceil\frac nm\right\rceil.\] For a red clique use \(\{j_{l-1},j_l,j_t\}\) for \(l\in I\). Every other selected cluster lies in the associated \(P_J^1\), because selected indices are nonadjacent and interior. For a blue clique use \(\{j_1,j_l,j_{l+1}\}\); the analogous containment is in \(P_J^2\). A relative cap of size \(a\) in each selected cluster therefore gives a convex union with \(ma\ge n\) vertices. Every triple now has three distinct indices.

The manuscript applies Proposition 2.1, stated for a polytope, to an unbounded intersection of three half-spaces. A sufficient local justification is to intersect each such set with a large box containing the entire finite input. The resulting bounded three-dimensional polytope has at most nine facets and at most \(3\cdot9-6=21\) edges. Freeness persists under taking a subset; all other selected caps remain inside the truncation. A cap relative to this truncation consequently suffices for the union argument. This correction replaces the displayed exponent \(3\) by an absolute constant, without changing the qualitative conclusion.

#### Quantifiers and asymptotic counterevidence.

Choose an integer \(k_0\) asymptotic to \(n^{1/4}\). The initial loss is \(2^{40k_0+k_0^3}=2^{o(n)}\); for fixed \(\epsilon>0\), cluster sizes are at least \(|X|^{1-\delta}\), with \(\delta\to0\). The two fixed Ramsey inversions give \(t\to\infty\). With the corrected cap size and the truncation bound, a sufficient estimate for large \(n\), \(\delta\le1/2\), is \[\frac{\log_2|X|}{n}
 \le
 \frac{84\log_2 21}{n}+
 84\left(\frac1m+\frac1n\right)
 \log_2\!\bigl(e(m+1)\bigr)
 \longrightarrow0.\] Thus the local repair preserves the contradiction with \(|X|\ge2^{\epsilon n}\), conditional on the relative-cap proposition and the other preceding inputs. The exact \(n/\log_{(5)}n\) refinement in the p. 10 remark was not certified by this check; the qualitative theorem does not require it.

Problem 3 (Minor: Proposition 2.3 allows endpoint projection collisions).  Proposition 2.3, pp. 5–6, assumes four disjoint sets in spatial general position and that every segment \(x_1x_3\) lies above \(x_2x_4\). It concludes that \(\operatorname{conv}(X_1\cup X_3)\) and \(\operatorname{conv}(X_2\cup X_4)\) are disjoint. The definition of above on p. 5 allows a projected intersection at a common endpoint; projected general position is not imposed.

Take \[\begin{split}
 X_1&=\{(0,0,101)\},\qquad X_2=\{(0,0,-103)\},\\
 X_3&=\{(1,1,0),(5,2,1),(2,6,3),(4,5,8)\},\\
 X_4&=\{q\},\qquad q=(3,7/2,3).
\end{split}\] All seven points are distinct, and all \(35\) four-point affine determinants are nonzero; the minimum absolute value is \(51/2\). The saved exact rational calculation verifies these statements.

For each \(v\in X_3\), the segments from \((0,0)\) to \(\pi(v)\) and to \(\pi(q)\) meet only at \((0,0)\): their two-dimensional determinants are respectively \(1/2,23/2,-11,-1\). At this projected intersection the two spatial heights differ by \(204>0\). Thus every required above relation holds, with a unique projected intersection and disjoint spatial segments.

Nevertheless \[q=\tfrac14\bigl((1,1,0)+(5,2,1)+(2,6,3)+(4,5,8)\bigr),\] so \(q\in\operatorname{conv}(X_3)\). Both convex hulls in the asserted conclusion contain \(q\). This is a counterexample to the standalone proposition.

#### Boundary and correction.

The proof’s inference that the four projected sets are in convex position fails here. Requiring the nondegenerate, properly crossing projected configuration used in the subsequent application excludes this witness. Corollary 2.4 starts with points whose projections are consecutive vertices of a convex polygon, and Section 3 begins with a generic projection. Accordingly this example does not refute that application or the main theorem. A corrected general statement should make the projection condition explicit; a full unrestricted replacement is not certified.

# Preliminary geometry requiring further justification

In Proposition 2.1, p. 4, spatial general position does not ensure that the relation defined by an edge-parallel projection is antisymmetric. For \[P=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2,e_3),\quad
 X=\{(2,2,0),(2,2,1),(3,2,0),(2,3,0)\},\] every pair-line misses \(P\), and the four-point determinant is \(1\). Projection along \([0,e_3]\) identifies the first two points, making each related to the other. The relation is a preorder on that fiber. Resolving projection fibers by an ordered refinement is a possible local remedy, but it should be stated.

Nor does a projected antichain automatically satisfy planar general position. Replacing \(X\) by \[\{(2,2,0),(3,2,1),(4,2,0),(2,3,0)\}\] gives a \(P\)-free spatially general set, determinant \(2\), whose projection is an antichain with three collinear points. Thus the planar Theorem 2.1 cannot be applied to that projected set without a degeneracy argument.

For compact full-dimensional \(P\), these projection issues admit a bounded repair. Refine the preorder by ordering each equivalence class; incomparable pairs are unchanged. For each pair-line choose the manuscript’s strictly separating witness edge direction, which is not parallel to that line. Its projected line has positive distance from the compact projected \(P\). Perturb the finitely many directions sufficiently little to preserve every assigned strict separation, while avoiding pair collisions and projected collinear triples. This retains \(E=e(P)\) orders and the \(|X|^{1/E}\) antichain bound.

A weaker relative-cap estimate can also be proved explicitly. Put \(B=\binom{a+b-4}{a-2}\), with \(a,b\ge2\). For a planar-general-position antichain \(S\) and compact polygon \(C\), partition \(S\) by a strictly violated facet inequality. Within one part every segment misses \(C\); if a full pair-line met \(C\) beyond an endpoint, that pair would be comparable. Thus all pair-lines avoid \(C\). Fix \(c\in C\). All of \(C\) has the same strict orientation signs as \(c\) against these lines; triangle membership then shows that a \(c\)-cap in this part is a \(C\)-cap. In coordinates \(c=0\) and \(x>0\) on the part, the projective map \[(x,y)\longmapsto (y/x,1/x)\] turns \(c\)-caps into lower cups and preserves convex position in this chart. The ordinary cups/caps theorem applies. Since the projected polygon has at most \(E\) facets, this proves the sufficient modified threshold \[|X|>(E B)^E\] for compact full-dimensional \(P\), after the preceding projection correction. All assumptions are needed; a projective map alone does not remove collinearity. For the main-proof truncations \(E\le21\), the added factor contributes only \(84\log_2(21)/n\) to the normalized exponent displayed above. This gives substantive counterevidence against a claimed failure of the qualitative main asymptotic. The sharper printed threshold \(B^E\), and degenerate-polytope extensions, are not certified; no counterexample to that existence assertion is established.

# Positive-fraction proof: checked reasoning and limits

Write \(M=\operatorname{ES}_3(8n)\), \(N=|\mathcal X|\). The double count of convex \(8n\)-subsets, the independent set obtained from the planar polytope graph, and the common \(6n\)-point core lead to the visibility-cell count in Section 4. The resulting lower bound for an assigned fiber is \[\frac{N^{2n}}{M^{8n}}\,
 \frac{n^{4n}}{(3^{10}e^{20})^n}.\] For the numerical factor alone, the coarse constant \(3^{30}\) and \(n\ge6561\) suffice. The threshold for all other arguments is kept separate.

Two statements in this chain should be read with their valid weaker replacements. On p. 11 the formula \(\binom{dt}{t}/((d-1)t+1)\) is not the exact count of rooted subtrees in the \(d\)-regular tree: at \(d=2,t=3\) the actual count is \(3\), whereas the formula gives \(5\). It counts the rooted \(d\)-ary comparison tree and therefore supplies the required upper bound. On p. 13, the assigned fiber injects into the product of the visibility cells, giving \[|\text{fiber}|\le \prod_{i=1}^{2n}|\mathcal X\cap R(P,S_i)|.\] Equality is unnecessary and is not established by arbitrary recombination, which need not preserve the prior assignment or every vertex of the fixed core. This inequality is in precisely the direction needed for the subsequent product lower bound.

The conclusion is strong convex-hull separation. For each selected nonempty, pairwise disjoint visibility set \(S_i\), choose a facet \(F_i\in S_i\). Its supporting functional is strictly positive on the whole cell \(R(P,S_i)\cap\mathcal X\) and nonpositive on every other selected cell. It separates their entire convex hulls. The product lower bound and the upper bound \(N\) on each cell imply at least \(n\) cells of size \(N/M^8\).

For Theorem 1.3 the exceptional small \(n\) are handled by \[\epsilon_n=\operatorname{ES}_3\!\left(8\max(n,n_0)\right)^{-8}.\] If \(n<n_0\), use Theorem 1.2 at \(n_0\) for sufficiently large inputs, and otherwise use \(n\) convex singletons. A generic projection from \(\mathbb R^d\) to \(\mathbb R^3\) preserves cardinality and four-point affine independence under the paper’s general-position convention. An intersection of original convex hulls would project to an intersection, so projected separation lifts. This verifies the stated dimension-independent choice and the \(2^{-o(n)}\) behavior, conditional on Theorem 1.1.

# Conclusion and state

The standalone above/below proposition requires an additional projection hypothesis. No false final theorem is claimed. The unverified exact relative-cap threshold and sharp iterated-log refinement remain separate from the established findings. The bounded-loss repair above covers the full-dimensional truncations needed for the qualitative main argument.
