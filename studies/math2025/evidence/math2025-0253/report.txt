Abstract: This report examines the 65-page author manuscript arXiv:2210.00878v2 of Beliakova, Putyra, Robert and Wagner. Three substantive failures in intermediate assertions are established. The total Koszul homology in Proposition 3.13 lacks the asserted natural identification with the gl\(_0\) state-space functor; Proposition 4.21 fails at the permitted specialization \(t=1\); and Theorem A.2 loses filtered extension data while its comparison principle is invoked in the completed Kauffman-state argument. Their actual proof dependencies support Major assessment. They do not establish a counterexample to the completed conclusion of Theorem 5.11 or the final DGR dimension inequality. Six further corrections concern an exact couple, a Schur basis and normalization formulas. Supported Bockstein arguments and the limits of this review are recorded separately. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The total Koszul functor does not have the claimed naturality

Problem 1.  Proposition 3.13 and the last paragraph of its proof (pp. 30–31, equations (26)–(29)) assert a natural isomorphism \(H^{d_0}\cong S_0\), induced by the identity on the reduced ordinary closed-web Soergel space. The assertion is false for the total complex defined in (26). Assessment: Major.

Work over \(\mathbb Q\), within the characteristic-zero hypothesis of Section 3.5. Let \(D_2\) be the annular closure of one dumbbell, with the outer output \(y_1\) marked. This is an elementary pointed web of index two; the proof’s convention sets \(y_1=0\). Its reduced open Soergel algebra is \[B=\mathbb Q[x_1,x_2,y_2]/(x_1+x_2-y_2,\;x_1x_2).\] There is one Koszul factor in (26), with differential \(x_2-y_2+1=1-x_1\). Thus the complex is, up to shifts, \(B\xrightarrow{1-x_1}B\). This multiplication is injective: in \(\mathbb Q[x_1,x_2]\), the polynomial \(1-x_1\) is relatively prime to \(x_1x_2\), so divisibility of \((1-x_1)h\) by \(x_1x_2\) implies divisibility of \(h\). Its cokernel is \[H^{d_0}(D_2)=
 \mathbb Q[x_1,x_2]/(x_1x_2,1-x_1)\cong\mathbb Q,
 \qquad x_1=y_2=1,\quad x_2=0.                   \tag{2.1}\] The source correctly obtains dimension one. The decoration actions are the obstruction.

The state space \(S_0(D_2;\mathbb Q)\) is one-dimensional and graded [1, Theorem 3.11(4), p. 29], so every thin dot of quantum degree two acts as zero. Directly, the two omnichrome colorings give \(S_1(D_2;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q[a]/(a^2)\), with the thin variables \(a,-a\). The marked multiplication has image \(\mathbb Qa\), annihilated by either dot. In (2.1), however, the inner output dot \(y_2\) acts as the identity. A natural isomorphism would intertwine identity and zero. To respect the degree-zero morphism convention, view the dotted cylinder as a morphism to the appropriate formal grading shift. The naturality square still has an invertible map composed with \(1\) on one side and a map composed with \(0\) on the other.

Ordinary annular closure also imposes \(x_2=y_2\), whereas (2.1) has \(x_2-y_2=-1\). Thus the total homology cannot be the asserted identity-induced quotient of the ordinary closed-web space.

#### Counterevidence and repair boundary.

The numerical dimension one is correct. Iterated homology is different: putting \(u=x_2\), \(v=y_2\) gives \(B=\mathbb Q[u,v]/(u(v-u))\). For the ordinary differential \(u-v\), \(\operatorname{HH}_0=\mathbb Q[u]\) and the kernel is the ideal \((u)\) in \(B\). The additional identity differential has image \((u)\) in \(\mathbb Q[u]\), so its iterated homology is \(\mathbb Q\) with \(u=v=0\). An associated-graded or iterated-homology identification may support a corrected argument, but requires a compatible filtration and naturality proof. Regrading (2.1) cannot turn identity into zero.

Page 31 uses the false functorial identification to pass from web calculations to a knot spectral sequence. This is a substantive proof defect even though the separately cited Robert–Wagner result may provide another route to the first spectral sequence. The example does not disprove that cited result, Theorem B, or the ultimate dimension inequality.

# Specialization invalidates the coefficient range of Proposition 4.21

Problem 2.  Proposition 4.21 (p. 42) asserts a finite Kauffman-state basis after quotienting by \(\Bbbk\)-torsion for a planar singular knot. Definition 4.8 (p. 35) permits any commutative \(\Bbbk\) and invertible \(t\). The assertion fails for \(\Bbbk=\mathbb Q\), \(t=1\). Assessment: Major.

Take the connected planar single-dumbbell closure \(D_2\), with the marking on the outer thin edge and a trace vertex on the inner edge. It has no real crossings and is a planar singular knot in the source’s sense. At the singular crossing label outgoing outer/inner variables \(a,b\) and incoming variables \(c,d\). Choose \(a=U_0\), matching the marked outgoing outer edge. Exchanging \(a,c\) leads to the same conclusion.

At \(t=1\) the twisting is trivial. The planar model has a unique degree-zero empty-multicycle generator \(x_0\). All boundaries into its degree are given by \[\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{generator or factor}&\text{coefficient of }x_0\\ \hline
\text{singular-crossing deviation}&\pm(ab-cd)\\
\text{inner trace deviation}&\pm(b-d)\\
\text{inner coherent cycle}&\pm(a-c)\\
\mathcal L_S\text{ factor}&a+b-c-d .
\end{array}\] This list is complete: a degree-minus-one planar generator has one deviation and no cycle, or one coherent cycle and no deviation. The inner cycle is the only coherent cycle avoiding the marked edge. The paired local disks have opposite signs by Lemma 4.6. The coherent-cycle disks have counts of magnitude one and opposite signs, as in the degree-zero calculation on pp. 50–51. This uses the low-degree planar calculation, not the asserted higher-homology vanishing of Proposition 4.21.

Hence \[\begin{aligned}
 H_0(\widehat{\operatorname{CFK}}(D_2,t=1)\otimes_R\mathcal L_S)
 &\cong \mathbb Q[a,b,c,d]/
 (a,\;b-d,\;ab-cd,\;a-c,\;a+b-c-d)\\
 &\cong\mathbb Q[b].\end{aligned}\] The last isomorphism sends \(a,c\) to zero and \(d\) to \(b\). Every nonlocal relation can also be checked independently. Assign value zero to the outer circular strand, value \(b\) to the inner strand, and multiset \(\{0,b\}\) to the thick edge. A transverse closed curve has equal incoming and outgoing counts for each closed colored strand. Its products coincide, including the case where both contain a zero factor. Since \(q=t=1\), the coefficient \(q^{2i}\) is one. No additional nonlocal relation can impose a polynomial equation on \(b\).

Over \(\mathbb Q\) the torsion submodule is zero with the intended definition using a nonzero annihilator. The computed space is infinite-dimensional, while a finite diagram has finitely many Kauffman states. The source’s omission of the word nonzero in its torsion definition is not used to manufacture the contradiction.

#### Impact and correction.

The unrestricted coefficient claim and the asserted reduction to \(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]\) fail at this specialization. A torsion quotient and concentration of homology cannot be passed through arbitrary nonflat specializations. Since the proposition is used for concentration at vertices of the Floer cube, its valid coefficient range matters to the proof.

The example does not refute the generic completed calculation. A repair must specify a completion or localization where the relevant \(1-t^m\) are units and prove concentration there. Page 43 uses a completion with positive powers of \(t^{1/2}\), whereas Theorem 5.11 uses \(\mathbb Z((q))\) with \(t=q^{-2}\). An infinite formal series cannot simply be substituted across these opposite directions. A symmetry or a separate contraction/completion argument is needed. That direction issue is a remaining proof obligation, not an additional counterexample to Theorem 5.11 over its stated ring.

# Filtered modules and the exact couple

Problem 3.  Theorem A.2 (p. 56) omits a compatible map of abutments: an abstract spectral-sequence isomorphism does not determine an isomorphism of completed filtered modules. Proposition 4.21 invokes this comparison principle on p. 43 for its completed Kauffman-state conclusion. Assessment: Major.

Let \(M=\mathbb Z/4\), with \(F^0M=M\), \(F^1M=2M\), \(F^2M=0\), and \(N=(\mathbb Z/2)^2\), with \(F^0N=N\), \(F^1N\) one direct summand, \(F^2N=0\). Extend both filtrations by the whole module in negative degrees and zero in higher degrees. Regard these as degree-zero complexes with zero differential. They are exhaustive, Hausdorff and complete, and have identical constant spectral sequences with two associated-graded copies of \(\mathbb Z/2\). The pagewise identity satisfies the printed hypothesis. Yet only \(M\) has an element of order four; completion changes neither.

The preceding source paragraph correctly assumes an actual filtered map inducing the associated-graded isomorphism. That premise cannot be discarded: the completed modules in this example are not even isomorphic as groups.

The failure enters the principal argument directly. On p. 43, after the associated-graded computation for Proposition 4.21, the source invokes uniqueness of the spectral-sequence limit, referring to Appendix A.1, to conclude that completed homology is free on Kauffman states and concentrated in degree zero. Neither pp. 42–43 nor A.2 supplies a compatible filtered comparison map or constructs the required lifts. Proposition 4.21 is then used in Section 5, including Theorem 5.11. This is an invoked core proof step, not an unused appendix assertion.

A repair must retain an appropriate filtered chain or abutment map and verify its graded comparison and convergence, or construct a separate lifting argument for the particular completed modules. Their free graded pieces may permit this, but the possible repair does not validate the printed inference or reduce its severity. The finite-extension example refutes the general comparison theorem; it does not refute the particular completed conclusion of Theorem 5.11 or the DGR inequality. The finite PID Bockstein argument does not require this invalid uniqueness principle.

Problem 4.  The proof of Proposition A.7 (p. 58) incorrectly says the \((k+1)\)st exact couple is zero when \(p(q)\) is not a unit. Its terminal spectral-sequence page can still be zero. Assessment: Minor.

Take \(L=\mathbb Q[q,q^{-1}]\) and \(C=(L\xrightarrow{(q-1)(q+1)}L)\). These are finite free modules, \(k=1\), and \(p(q)=q+1\) is coprime to \(q-1\). The homology is \(L/((q-1)(q+1))\). The first Bockstein differential between the two copies of \(\mathbb Q\) is multiplication by \(p(1)=2\), so \(E_2=0\). But the next \(D\) term is \[(q-1)L/((q-1)(q+1))\cong L/(q+1)\ne0,\] and multiplication by \(q-1\) on it is multiplication by \(-2\). In general, the coprime torsion remains in \(D\). Replace the zero-couple assertion by vanishing of its \(E\) term, or split off the coprime torsion first. Smith normal form then proves the stated finite stabilization and terminal free-rank formula. Proposition A.7 itself is not refuted. The formula on p. 57 must also use \(g^{(n)}\circ h^{(n)}\) as the differential on \(B^{(n)}\): \(h\) has domain \(B\), whereas the printed \(g^{(n)}\circ f^{(n)}\) has domain \(A\) and is not an endomorphism of \(B\).

# Local algebraic and normalization corrections

Problem 5.  Example 2.5 (p. 10) puts the Schur polynomial in the wrong block. Assessment: Minor.

Take \((3)\prec(2,1)\), \(i=r=1\), over \(\mathbb Q\). The printed formula places \(s_\lambda\) in block two. By the definition of \(T(a,b)\) on p. 8, \(\lambda=(1,1)\) is the maximal rectangle in \(T(1,2)\). The alleged basis element is \(1\otimes e_2(x_3)=0\), yet its stated trace is one. This is impossible for a linear map. Place the Schur polynomial in block \(i\), adjusting the counts of unit tensor factors, or transpose the rectangle and adjust the trace convention when choosing the other block. The standard freeness and Frobenius-extension result survives; this displayed basis error does not refute the later categorical existence statement.

Problem 6.  Example 5.4 (p. 46) loses a factor of two in its exponent and annihilator. Assessment: Minor.

Equation (39), p. 44, uses \(q^{2i}\). Take two nested thin circles, mark the outer circle, work over \(\mathbb Q\), and set \(q=-1\). The inner component has index one. Set every edge variable to zero. All local relations vanish. For a curve meeting the web both nonlocal products vanish; for a curve meeting no edges the relation is \(1-q^{2i}=0\). Thus the algebra has a nonzero augmentation to \(\mathbb Q\), which its printed annihilator \(1-q=2\) cannot annihilate. The corrected exponent is \(2(w_i+\cdots+w_r)\) and a supported annihilator is \(1-q^{2\gcd(w_1,\ldots,w_r)}\). The text after Lemma 5.8 (p. 49) already has \(2w\). Vanishing under the hypothesis that all \(1-q^d\) are units is unchanged, so the generic disconnected-web argument survives.

Problem 7.  The trace-relation sentence in Proposition 5.12’s proof (p. 52) reverses the exponent; the proposition’s map is consistent. Assessment: Minor.

At a nontrace crossing, the incoming edges have level \(r\) and outgoing edges level \(r+1\). The stated map \(x_\alpha\mapsto t^{-n(\alpha)}x_\alpha\) sends \(t(x_a+x_b)-x_c-x_d\) to a unit multiple of \(x_a+x_b-x_c-x_d\). At the trace the incoming level is \(n\) and outgoing level zero. Thus \(x_a=x_c\) becomes \[\widetilde x_a=t^{-n}\widetilde x_c
              =q^2\widetilde x_c,\qquad q=t^{-n/2}.\] The proof prints \(t^n=q^{-2}\) instead. For \(n=1\) the factors \(t,t^{-1}\) are already different in the Laurent polynomial ring. Correcting this sentence to \(t^{-n}\) and \(q^2\) agrees with Example 5.2. Reversing the proposition’s map would spoil the nontrace check.

Problem 8.  Appendix B has an exponent sign error in the cyclic operator’s description and an endpoint-index error in (53), pp. 59–60. Assessment: Minor.

Its definition is \[t_n(a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_n)
   =q^{-|a_n|}a_n\otimes a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}.\] Applying this \(n+1\) times gives \(q^{-d}\) on internal degree \(d\), not the printed \(q^d\). For \(A=\mathbb Q[x]\), \(|x|=2\), \(q=2\), the zero-chain \(x\) is sent by \(T_0\) to \(x/4\), not \(4x\). Also \(\sigma_n=t_{n+1}s_n\) inserts \(1\) at the front, so \(d_i\sigma_n=\sigma_{n-1}d_{i-1}\) for \(1\le i\le n\), and \(d_{n+1}\sigma_n=t_n\). These are the corrected endpoint and range in (53).

The homotopy still satisfies \(\partial h+h\partial=1-T\) with \(T=q^{-d}\). On each positive-degree summand, \((1-q^{-d})^{-1}h\) is a contraction under the source’s unit hypotheses. Nonnegative grading identifies the degree-zero summand with the Hochschild complex of \(A_0\). Thus Proposition B.2 and the generic vanishing mechanism survive. The scalar sign in Proposition 2.11 was also examined, but the scalar-subscript twisting notation leaves a convention issue. It is not counted as another confirmed failure.

Problem 9.  The normalization sentence following the cap formula on p. 18 has the wrong sign in its claimed preimage of one. Assessment: Minor.

Take \(a=b=1\) over \(\mathbb Q\), with \(P(x)=x=e_1\) and \(Q=1\). The denominator in the displayed definition is \(x_j-x_i\). It gives \[e_1\star1=\frac{x_1}{x_2-x_1}+
           \frac{x_2}{x_1-x_2}=-1,\] whereas the sentence claims \(e_a^b\star1=1\). All size and coefficient hypotheses hold. In this case \(-e_1\) is the required preimage of one, so surjectivity is unchanged. More generally the rectangular normalization must account for the sign introduced by the denominator orientation. Changing a global foam convention would require checking its other relations; the supported correction here is to the stated normalization, not a claim that the entire foam functor is invalid.

# Supported arguments, computations and limitations

The quantum Hochschild contraction is chain-level and needs no additional base-ring flatness assumption. Reduction from Bott–Samelson objects through annular web decompositions supports the vanishing strategy of Theorem 2.36, under its invertibility hypotheses.

For pseudo-completion, write \(R=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]\), \(S=\mathbb Z((q))\), \(M=\mathcal A_R\) and \(K=\ker(M\to M\otimes_RS)\). Right exactness gives \((M/K)\otimes_RS\cong M\otimes_RS\), since the image of \(K\otimes_RS\) is zero. This justifies the elementary base-change step of Proposition 5.14 without calling \(S\) a field. For a field \(\mathbb K\), however, \(\mathbb K((q))\) contains the fraction field of the PID \(\mathbb K[q,q^{-1}]\). Finite freeness of the web modules and the finite cube then support the rank and finite-stabilization mechanism of Theorem B. The completed Floer comparison and compatible coefficient changes must be supplied within their valid range; this sound algebraic step does not remove Problems 1 and 2.

The signs in Lemma 4.6 were checked against its two boundary-degeneration identities. Initial-diagram admissibility was checked through the periodic-domain inequalities, including connecting necks. The skein maps and planar local/nonlocal relations were checked against actual diagrams. This is not a new analytic proof of the cited orientation and gluing theorems.

The two trefoil matrix pairs on pp. 60–61 were transcribed from actual page images and multiplied exactly. Both products are zero. Their ranks are \((3,2)\) and \((2,3)\) respectively over both \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb F_2\), giving homology dimensions \((1,1,1)\) in the three occupied degrees, as reported. The figure-eight and cinquefoil grading and Euler-characteristic arguments on pp. 62–63 were inspected; not all figure-eight foam maps were independently recomputed. The \(T(3,4)\) discussion and knot-detection consequences retain their stated external dependencies and characteristic assumptions. No author code was executed.
