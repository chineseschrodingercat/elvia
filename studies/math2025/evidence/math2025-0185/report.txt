Abstract: The acquired second arXiv version is reviewed with its precise regularity and source-version limits. Three demonstrated defects concern the one-form used to extend the Schläfli identity, the admissibility conditions for the signed-volume extension, and the reflected Beltrami coefficient in the distance estimate. A translating-circle calculation disproves the former pointwise identity. A smooth interior extension of the round-circle data satisfies every printed admissibility condition but has infinite positive and negative volume contributions. The third example satisfies the exact small-Schwarzian hypothesis but produces an unbounded coefficient. These affect the supplied proof, definition of the central volume functional, and distance-bound construction. None of these examples disproves the intended smooth-curve identity, and explicit correction boundaries are given. The curvature, disjointness, approximation and gradient-flow arguments are assessed separately. The supported assessment is Major; adjudication remains pending.

Let \(\gamma\) bound \(\Omega\) and \(\Omega^*\), and let \(\rho_\Omega\) be the Poincaré metric of curvature \(-1\). The normal convention is \(B=-\nabla n\) and \(H=\operatorname{tr}(B)/2\). The symbol \(da\) is the signed area form determined by the lifted Epstein normal; it must not be replaced by the unsigned area measure at a fold. The main equality is \[\widetilde S(\gamma)=4V_R(\gamma),\qquad
 V_R=V-\frac12\int_{\Sigma_\Omega\cup\Sigma_{\Omega^*}}H\,da ,\] for \(C^{5,\alpha}\) curves, \(0<\alpha<1\). For general Weil–Petersson curves the source proves only \(\widetilde S\geq4V_R\) and expressly leaves equality open. That distinction is retained throughout this report.

# Checks that support the stated geometry

Write \(q=\|\mathcal S(f^{-1})\|_{\rho_\Omega}\). The eigenvalues of the shape operator at infinity are \(1\pm2q\). Inverting \(B=(1+\widehat B)^{-1}(1-\widehat B)\) gives, where the surface is immersed, \[da=(1-q^2)\,d\widehat a,\qquad
 H\,da=q^2\,d\widehat a,\qquad
 \det B\,da=-q^2\,d\widehat a .\] Thus the nonnegative mean-curvature integral is precisely the weighted Schwarzian \(A_2\) norm. The apparent poles of \(H\) at \(q=1\) cancel in \(H\,da\); the smooth form is the object that extends. The exceptional set has measure zero: \(q^2\) is real analytic, and \(q\equiv1\) on an open set would make \(\log|\mathcal S(f^{-1})|=\log\rho_\Omega\) there, contradicting harmonicity on the left and \(\Delta\log\rho_\Omega=2\rho_\Omega\) on the right. The circular case has \(q=0\) and a totally geodesic surface.

The disjointness argument in Proposition 4.2, p. 21, retains a genuine strictness test. After normalizing two distinct endpoints to \(0,\infty\), Grunsky’s inequality gives \(|g'(\infty)|\geq|f'(0)|\). Equality forces both nonnegative integrands to vanish, hence \(f'/f=g'/g=1/z\). The boundary must then be a circle. For a noncircle, the strict inequality separates the associated horospheres and therefore their Epstein points. No assumption that the entire Epstein surfaces are embedded is used here.

For smooth curves, the osculation calculation of Proposition 4.5 gives second-order agreement of the two boundary collars. In the coordinates \[G(s,a,b)=\gamma(s)+ia\gamma'(s)+jb,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb H^3}=\frac{1-a\,k(s)}{b^3}\,ds\,da\,db ,\] the gap \(a_{\Omega^*}-a_\Omega=O(b^3)\) makes the unsigned collar volume integrable. This is a geometric argument for suitably regular extensions. It does not supply the missing absolute integrability for every map allowed in Section 4.2, as Problem 2 shows.

The approximation argument in Section 5.5 has a one-sided conclusion. The equipotential actions increase to the limiting action, the two \(A_2\) Schwarzian norms converge in the Weil–Petersson topology, and the truncated signed volumes converge at a fixed positive height. With the controlled signed-volume definition, these yield the stated upper bound for \(V\) and hence \(\widetilde S\geq4V_R\). They do not establish continuity of the full volume or equality for all Weil–Petersson curves.

# Confirmed problems

Problem 1 (The one-form in Lemma 8.6 is not a divergence primitive).  On p. 45, Lemma 8.6 explicitly defines \[i_W(u)=\langle DE(u),W\rangle,\qquad W=\nabla_\xi n,\] and claims \[(\operatorname{div}W)\,E^*da=d i_W .
 \tag{*}\] Its proof identifies the exterior derivative of this metric-dual one-form with divergence times area. Equation (8.5), p. 46, then uses \[\operatorname{tr}\langle\nabla_\xi(B\cdot),DE(\cdot)\rangle E^*da
 =-d i_W-2\langle\xi,n\rangle E^*da .
 \tag{**}\] Both identities are false with the stated definition.

Consider the admissible \(C^\infty\) family \(\gamma_t=t+S^1\). Every curve has Euclidean length \(2\pi\). For its interior Epstein surface, use the source’s Example 3.15 (p. 20) and write, for \(u^2+v^2<1\), \[E=(X,Y,Z)=
 \left(\frac{2u}{1+r^2},\frac{2v}{1+r^2},
       \frac{1-r^2}{1+r^2}\right),\qquad r^2=u^2+v^2 .\] Then \(E_t=E+(t,0,0)\), \(n=Z(X,Y,Z)\) and \(\xi=(1,0,0)\). All these surfaces are totally geodesic, so \(B_t=0\). The upper-half-space connection gives \[W=\nabla_\xi n=(-Z,0,X),\qquad
 \phi=\langle\xi,n\rangle=\frac{X}{Z}=\frac{2u}{1-r^2}.\] The induced metric is \(\rho(du^2+dv^2)\) with \(\rho=4/(1-r^2)^2\). Direct differentiation gives \[W^\flat=-d\phi,\qquad dW^\flat=0,\qquad
 \Delta_{\mathbb H^2}\phi=2\phi,\qquad
 (\operatorname{div}W)\,da=-2\phi\,da .\] At \((u,v)=(1/2,0)\), \[\phi=\frac43,\qquad da=\frac{64}{9}\,du\wedge dv,\qquad
 (\operatorname{div}W)\,da=-\frac{512}{27}\,du\wedge dv\ne0.\] This contradicts \((*)\). In \((**)\) the left side is zero, since \(B_t=0\), whereas the right side is \(-512/27\) times \(du\wedge dv\). The test is on the immersed interior branch, so it does not exploit an undefined trace at a singular point. An exact symbolic and rational calculation verifies the connection, metric, normal, one-form and residual.

Equation reference: eq:correct-form. The appropriate replacement is the area-contraction one-form \[\alpha_W(u)=\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb H^3}\bigl(n,W,DE(u)\bigr)
            =(\iota_W da)(DE(u)).
 \] With compatible orientation, \(d\alpha_W=(\operatorname{div}W)da\) on an immersed surface. Unlike the induced inverse metric, this ambient expression stays defined when \(DE\) loses rank. It is therefore also a suitable candidate for the smooth extension that Section 8 needs. The accompanying Stokes boundary term must use its conormal component; retaining the printed metric-dual form and merely changing the prose would not repair the argument.

There is specific counterevidence against a false main-theorem claim. For translating circles the total volume and action remain zero; the local residual does not create a nonzero global renormalized volume. The curvature-commutation formula of Proposition 8.4 is compatible with the corrected contraction form. The usual divergence theorem supplies exactly the missing rotation of the one-form. Nevertheless, the printed identity is an essential step in the nonimmersed Schläfli extension, then Theorem 5.2, the variation of \(V_R\), and Corollary 5.12. This is a Major defect in that supplied derivation. A local correction’s availability does not eliminate the core dependency.

Problem 2 (The printed extension class does not define a finite signed integral).  Section 4.2, pp. 22–23, permits any map \(\varphi\) continuous on the compactification, differentiable on the open \(\mathbb H^3\), with the prescribed Epstein boundary values and nonnegative Jacobian near \(\gamma\). It then concludes from compactness of the image above a cutoff that \(\int_{\mathbb H^3}\varphi^*\operatorname{vol}_\varepsilon\) is finite and independent of the chosen extension. These conditions do not ensure that the signed integral exists.

Take \(\gamma=S^1\). Put \(R^2=x^2+y^2\), \(D=R^2+z^2+1\), and \[F_0(x,y,z)=
 \left(\frac{2x}{D},\frac{2y}{D},
       \frac{\sqrt{D^2-4R^2}}{D}\right),\qquad z>0 .\] This is smooth in \(\mathbb H^3\), takes values in the upper unit hemisphere, and extends continuously to the compactification. At \(z=0\), its restriction is \[\left(\frac{2x}{1+R^2},\frac{2y}{1+R^2},
       \frac{|1-R^2|}{1+R^2}\right).\] These are precisely the interior and exterior circular Epstein boundary data. At the point at infinity it tends to \((0,0,1)\). Its Jacobian is identically zero.

For \(n\geq4\), take disjoint cubes centered at \(p_n=(0,0,2^{-n})\) with half-width \(h_n=2^{-n}/16\). They accumulate only at the boundary point \(0\), away from \(\gamma\). On the concentric inner cube use affine unit-cube coordinates \(U=(U_1,U_2,U_3)\in[0,1]^3\), and set \[a_n=\frac{2^{-n}}{100},\qquad m_n=2^{2n},\qquad Q=(0,0,1),\] \[P_n(U)=Q+a_n
 \bigl(\sin(2\pi m_nU_1),\sin(2\pi m_nU_2),\sin(2\pi m_nU_3)\bigr).\] A smooth cutoff equal to one on the inner cube and zero near the outer cube’s boundary interpolates between \(P_n\) and \(F_0\). Outside the outer cubes retain \(F_0\). The resulting map \(\varphi\) is smooth at every interior point, because the cubes are locally finite there. Since \(a_n\to0\) and \(F_0(p)\to Q\) as \(p\to0\), it is continuous at the sole accumulation point and hence on the compactification. It has unchanged boundary values and agrees with \(F_0\) near \(S^1\); its Jacobian is therefore nonnegative there. Its image on every altered cube stays above height \(1/2\).

For \(\varepsilon\leq1/4\) the cutoff weight is one on these cubes. After cancellation of the affine domain-coordinate Jacobian, the signed density on an inner cube is \[\frac{a_n^3(2\pi m_n)^3
       \prod_{j=1}^3\cos(2\pi m_nU_j)}
      {(1+a_n\sin(2\pi m_nU_3))^3}\,dU_1dU_2dU_3 .\] The positive and negative parts each have integral at least \[\frac{32a_n^3m_n^3}{(1+a_n)^3}.\] Indeed, the integral of the positive part of the product of three cosines on the unit cube is \(4/\pi^3\), and the negative part has the same integral. Here \(a_n^3m_n^3=2^{3n}/10^6\), so both sign integrals diverge. Contributions in the cutoff transition regions cannot remove these lower bounds. Thus the ordinary signed Lebesgue integral is undefined as \(\infty-\infty\), despite all the printed hypotheses.

This does not contradict the intended value \(V(S^1)=0\) obtained from the well-behaved extension \(F_0\). Nor does it show that a suitably defined geometric volume fails to exist. It disproves the stated finiteness and extension-independence assertion for the actual admissible class. Choosing an exhaustion that groups complete oscillatory cubes could conceal the divergent positive and negative parts; no such conditional regularization is specified in the source.

A sufficient correction is to require controlled smoothness up to the compactified boundary away from \(\gamma\), with an absolutely integrable Jacobian on each cutoff region, and to use homotopies with the same control. The collar positivity and subsequent geometric estimates can then address the limit near \(\gamma\). Alternatively, a precisely defined finite-mass current or boundary-based volume could replace the integral, but that requires its own agreement and independence proof. If the authors intended smoothness on the closure away from \(\gamma\), the counterexample is excluded; that is a substantive strengthening of the printed admissibility conditions.

The defect enters Definition 4.4 and the definition of \(V_R\), so it affects the formulation of the central object used in the volume identity and inequality. It is Major in that scope, without a claim that the intended geometrically controlled volume or the final identity is false.

Problem 3 (The displayed Ahlfors–Weill coefficient is unbounded).  The proof of Theorem 6.3, p. 38, takes \(q=\mathcal S(g_{\alpha(\tau)})\) with \(\|q\|_\infty<\delta/2\) and writes \[\widetilde\mu_{\rm print}(z)
 =-\frac{2}{\bar z^4}
       \left(\frac{q}{\rho_{\mathbb D^*}}\right)(1/\bar z).
 \tag{AW}\] It asserts that this is a Beltrami coefficient of supremum norm less than \(\delta\) and uses \([s\widetilde\mu_{\rm print}]\) to construct the short path required for the distance bound. Formula (AW) fails even for an explicitly realized analytic Jordan curve.

For each permitted \(0<\delta<1\), choose \(a=\delta\), put \(k=\sqrt{a/2}\), and set \[g(w)=k\cot(k/w),\qquad |w|>1,\qquad q(w)=\frac{a}{w^4}.\] The Schwarzian chain rule gives \(\mathcal S(g)=2k^2/w^4=q\). The function is univalent on a neighborhood of the closed exterior disk: two cotangent arguments with equal values differ by an integral multiple of \(\pi\), whereas their distance is less than \(2k<\pi\). The pole at \(w=\infty\) is simple, \(g(w)=w+O(w^{-1})\) there, and no other pole occurs in the exterior domain. The boundary is therefore an analytic Jordan curve and defines a Weil–Petersson point. Postcomposition by a Möbius map can impose the source’s three-point normalization without changing \(q\).

With the stated density \(\rho_{\mathbb D^*}(w)=4/(1-|w|^2)^2\), \[\|q\|_\infty=\frac a4<\frac\delta2,\qquad
 \|q\|_2^2
 =\frac{\pi a^2}{2}\int_1^\infty
       (r^2-1)^2r^{-7}\,dr
 =\frac{\pi a^2}{12}.\] Thus \(c=4\|q\|_2=2a\sqrt{\pi/3}\) satisfies the exact permitted range \(0<c<2\delta\sqrt{4\pi/3}\). All the smallness and finite-energy assumptions used at the path construction are met. Start the flow at this point: its allowed first hitting time is then \(\tau=0\), since \(\|V\|=c\) initially. But (AW) becomes \[\widetilde\mu_{\rm print}(z)
 =-\frac{a(1-|z|^2)^2}{2|z|^4},\] which is unbounded as \(z\to0\). In particular, \(s\widetilde\mu_{\rm print}\) fails to be a Beltrami coefficient for every \(s>0\). This is not just the separate factor-four overestimate of path length mentioned below.

The correct coefficient is \[\widetilde\mu_{\rm corr}(z)
 =-\frac{(1-|z|^2)^2}{2\bar z^4}\,q(1/\bar z)
 =-2\frac{z^2}{\bar z^2}
       \left(\frac{q}{\rho_{\mathbb D^*}}\right)(1/\bar z).\] Its modulus is \(2\|q(1/\bar z)\|_{\rho_{\mathbb D^*}}\). For the example it is simply \(-a(1-|z|^2)^2/2\) and is bounded by \(a/2<\delta\). The singularity of the displayed reflected formula at zero is interpreted by the holomorphic behavior of \(q\) at infinity; there is no unbounded coefficient.

A bounded repair preserves \(K=\sqrt2/(1-\delta)^2\). Define \[Jq(z)=\frac{\overline{q(1/\bar z)}}{z^4},\qquad
 \eta(w)=-2\,\frac{\overline{q(w)}}{\rho_{\mathbb D^*}(w)}.\] Reflection \(J\) preserves the normalized supremum and \(A_2\) norms. Inverse welding gives \(\beta(x^{-1})=Jq\) [Retained latex: \cite[Appendix A, pp.~49--50]{TTII}]. The Ahlfors–Weill section [TTI, p. 8] gives \[y(s)=[s\eta],\quad \beta(y(s))=sJq,\quad y(1)=x^{-1},
 \quad \|\eta\|_\infty<\delta,\quad \|\eta\|_2=c/2 .\] The supremum norm in [TTI] is four times the present normalized norm, so its radius-two section has precisely the needed range. Right-translate the velocity to the identity and apply the contracting harmonic projection. The direct right-translation estimate [Retained latex: \cite[Corollary 3.6, p.~29]{TTI}] gives \[\|\dot y(s)\|_{\mathrm{WP}}\leq
 \frac{\sqrt2}{(1-\|s\eta\|_\infty)^2}\|\eta\|_2\leq Kc/2 .\] Constant right translation and reversal of a path imply \(d_{\mathrm{WP}}(e,x)=d_{\mathrm{WP}}(e,x^{-1})\), without making inversion an isometry between arbitrary pairs. Hence \(d_{\mathrm{WP}}(e,x)\leq{\rm length}_{\mathrm{WP}}(y)\leq Kc/2<Kc\). The source’s energy and triangle inequalities yield its advertised weaker bound, including the initially-small-gradient case. This repair uses the actual right-translation bound, not the exact inverse-Bers norm asserted in Lemma 6.2. The inverse section has a factor two at the origin under these norms; the source’s existential choice of \(\delta\) prevents this observation alone from disproving that lemma. The distance theorem itself is not disproved. The printed essential construction of its short path, however, is not defined in the asserted class, so the defect is Major for Theorems 6.3 and 1.8.

# Additional verification and limitations

The real first-variation formula gives \[V=-4\,\frac{\overline{\mathcal S(g_\mu)}}{\rho_{\mathbb D^*}},
 \qquad dS(V)=-\|V\|_{\mathrm{WP}}^2.\] On \(T_0(1)\) the smooth Kähler potential and strong Hilbert metric give a locally well-posed gradient equation. The universal WP metric is Cauchy complete [Retained latex: \cite[Corollary 5.3, p.~29]{GMR}]; this differs from the finite-genus situation. If a maximal forward curve had finite lifetime \(T\), then, for \(s<t<T\), \[d_{\mathrm{WP}}(\alpha(s),\alpha(t))
 \leq\sqrt{t-s}
 \left(\int_s^t\|V\|_{\mathrm{WP}}^2\,du\right)^{1/2}
 \leq\sqrt{t-s}\sqrt{S(\alpha(0))}.\] It is Cauchy, reaches an endpoint by completeness, and extends by the local Hilbert ODE. This supports forward global existence on \(T_0(1)\) independently of the ambient flow claim.

Finite total energy yields \(t_n\to\infty\) with \(\|V(\alpha(t_n))\|_{\mathrm{WP}}\to0\). The exterior Schwarzians tend to zero in \(A_2\), hence so do the standard Bers coordinates of the inverse weldings. The topological-group theorem [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem A.8, p.~65]{TTI}] gives \(\alpha(t_n)\to e\); continuity and monotonicity yield \(S(\alpha(t))\to0\). The energy-dependent quasicircle bound [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition 2.9, p.~9]{RW}] gives normalized quasiconformal compactness. Lower semicontinuity [Retained latex: \cite[Lemma 2.12, p.~13]{RW}] makes every limit a zero-action circle; the three marked points identify it with \(S^1\). Carathéodory convergence makes the conformal-radius term tend to zero, so the remaining nonnegative integrals in \(S\) tend to zero. The pre-Schwarzian Hilbert chart [Retained latex: \cite[Corollary A.4, p.~49]{TTII}] then gives full WP convergence. These are bounded checks of the imported statements, not full independent reviews of those references.

The ambient all-\(T(1)\) flow assertion on p. 36 remains Needs Evidence. Bounded speed and completeness extend a locally well-posed field; they do not establish its local regularity. Indeed [Retained latex: \cite[Remark 2.3, p.~9]{TTI}] warns that right-translated fixed tangent vectors need not form a smooth field on full \(T(1)\). This does not show that the particular Schwarzian-dependent field fails to be smooth; its varying coefficient may compensate. No counterexample to that field was established. The bounded finite-type flow references checked by the audit do not provide the missing universal Banach-chart theorem.

For the distance estimate, \(\|V\|_{\mathrm{WP}}=c\) implies \(\|\mathcal S(g)\|_2=c/4\). The straight Schwarzian path therefore has \(A_2\) length \(c/4\), not the displayed \(c\) on p. 38. This overestimate is harmless for the weaker bound \(Kc\) used in Theorem 6.3. The exterior Bers coordinate must be compared using reflection and the inverse Teichmüller point; right invariance alone gives \(d_{\mathrm{WP}}(e,x)=d_{\mathrm{WP}}(e,x^{-1})\), without asserting that inversion is an isometry between arbitrary pairs.

Section 7 is independent of the volume proof. If the principal curvatures at infinity are greater than \(-1\) and have arithmetic mean \(e^{-2t}<1\), their product is less than one, giving the positive mean-curvature sign used for the barrier argument. For a minimal surface with principal curvatures \(\pm\lambda\), \(|\lambda|<1\), the visual metric has curvature \(-(1+\lambda^2)/(1-\lambda^2)\leq-1\); the stated Ahlfors–Schwarz comparison follows. The convex-hull estimate uses compactly contained round disks, the monotonicity of \((1-k)/(1+k)\) for \(k>-1\), and then a distance-\(t\) normal displacement to obtain the factor \(2t\). These arguments do not depend on the erroneous divergence primitive.

Some local displays require correction without generating additional independent findings. Naturality should read \(\operatorname{Ep}_\rho\circ h=h\circ\operatorname{Ep}_{h^*\rho}\); the source itself uses the correct composition in Lemma 3.7. The cutoff can be chosen monotone; alternatively Fatou’s lemma for infinite nonnegative mass and dominated convergence for finite mass establish the collar limit. Neither observation repairs the oscillatory interior counterexample. The proof of Theorem 5.9, p. 32, must differentiate \(H\,da\) as \(\delta H\,da+H\,\delta(da)\), with a plus. For \(f_a(z)=z+az^3\), \(0<a<1/3\), the printed sign gives \(-162a^3/(4-9a^2)\) at \(z=0\), whereas the correct density is zero. The immediately following at-infinity formula gives the correct zero; this is a display clarification, not a further independent core defect.

A second Needs Evidence item concerns Theorem 5.7, p. 30. Put \(h=1-r\). A nondegenerate cap of horizontal width \(O(h^3)\), affinely parametrized over the permitted interval of length \(1/r-r\asymp h\), has \(|E_s|=O(h^2)\). Its height is comparable to \(h\), so \[I_{ss}=O(h^2),\qquad (I^{-1})^{ss}\geq 1/I_{ss}=\Omega(h^{-2}),\] contrary to the printed inverse-metric estimate in these parameters. A fully documented global noncircle example satisfying both immersion hypotheses and the nondegenerate-cap conditions was not completed. The conditional calculation is therefore not a confirmed counterexample. In physical arclength and horizontal-distance coordinates the Euclidean metric is uniformly nondegenerate, and the hyperbolic inverse does have order \(h^2\). The moving coordinates and variation terms still need checking; no failed boundary limit is claimed.

The derivative-tail assertion on p. 31 has positive support. If the gap \(q=a_\Omega-a_{\Omega^*}\) is jointly \(C^{3,\alpha}\) and its boundary two-jets vanish, then \(\partial_tq=O(b^{2+\alpha})\), giving \[\int_0^\varepsilon b^{-3}|\partial_tq|\,db=O(\varepsilon^\alpha).\] A cubic remainder need not survive time differentiation. The moving ceiling has \(\partial_t b_r=O(h)\) and contributes \(O(h)\) using the original \(O(b^3)\) gap.
