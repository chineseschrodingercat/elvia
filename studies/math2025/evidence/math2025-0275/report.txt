# F1: false equality family in Theorem 14 (Major)

Location. Theorem 14, p. 11, the necessary and sufficient equality clause; the same power occurs after (15), p. 9. For \(\alpha>n\), the claimed family is \[f(x)=a(1+|\varphi(x-x_0)|^2)^{-(n-\alpha)/2}.\] Every member with \(a>0\) and \(\varphi\in GL(n)\) grows at infinity, so it lies outside \(L^{2n/(n+\alpha)}(\mathbb R^n)\). There is also an admissible nonzero equality example excluded by this classification.

Complete counterexample. Take \(n=2\), \(\alpha=4\), \(p=2/3\) and \[f(x)=(1+|x|^2)^{-3}.\] This is nonnegative, nonzero and in \(L^p\). Direct polar integration gives \[M_0=\int f=\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad
 M_2=\int |x|^2f=\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad \int f^{2/3}=\pi.\] The first moment vanishes by symmetry. All relevant moments are finite, so \[\iint f(x)f(y)|x-y|^2\,dx\,dy=2M_0M_2=\frac{\pi^2}{2}.\] Formula (2), p. 1, gives \(\gamma_{2,4}=1/(2\pi)\), while \(\|f\|_{2/3}^2=\pi^3\). Thus the theorem’s left side is \(\pi^2/2\).

By rotation invariance, \(S_4f\) is a ball. If its radius is \(R\), the defining polar identity (11), p. 6, gives \(2\pi R^4=\pi^2/2\), hence \(R^4=\pi/4\). Its affine expression is \[2\pi^{-1}|S_4f|^2=2\pi R^4=\frac{\pi^2}{2}.\] All three terms of Theorem 14 are equal. The printed growing profile cannot represent this decaying \(f\). The saved independent symbolic calculation verifies the integrals and equalities exactly; no author code was executed.

Counterevidence, impact and repair boundary. The proof on p. 12 uses \(f^\star(x)=a(1+\lambda|x|^2)^{-n/p}\), whose power is \(-(n+\alpha)/2\). That agrees with the example and identifies the apparent intended correction. The Major grade concerns the false sharp optimizer classification in a principal theorem, which excludes every nonzero admissible optimizer; it does not assert that the inequality bound is false. Replacing \(n-\alpha\) by \(n+\alpha\) repairs this example and restores integrability. A complete global equality classification still requires a correct equality argument, including F2; it is not certified merely by changing the sign.

# F2: the shared affine map is lost in equality lemmas (Major)

Locations. Lemma 8, p. 7; Lemmas 9–10, p. 8; Lemma 11, p. 10; Lemmas 12–13, p. 11. They assert that equality requires \(f\) to be a translate of \(f^\star\). Theorem 1’s proof on p. 9 invokes Lemma 10 for its affine equality classification, and Theorem 14’s proof on p. 12 invokes Lemma 13.

Admissible counterexample. In dimension two set \[A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2),\quad g(x)=e^{-|x|^2},
 \quad f(x)=g(A^{-1}x),\quad K=AB^2.\] Since \(\det A=1\), one has \(f^\star=g\) and \(K^\star=B^2\). The function belongs to every positive \(L^p\) space, its positive superlevel sets have finite measure, and \(g\) is strictly symmetric decreasing. The singular kernel integral for \(q=1\) in Lemma 8 and the polynomial kernel integral for \(q=2\) in Lemma 11 are finite.

Under \(x=Au\), \(y=Av\), the Jacobian is one and \(\|x-y\|_K=|u-v|\). Consequently, \[\iint\frac{f(x)f(y)}{\|x-y\|_K}\,dx\,dy
 =\iint\frac{g(u)g(v)}{|u-v|}\,du\,dv\] and \[\iint f(x)\|x-y\|_K^2f(y)\,dx\,dy
 =\iint g(u)|u-v|^2g(v)\,du\,dv.\] These are equality cases of the two printed rearrangement inequalities. But \(f\) is not a translate of \(g\): the Hessian of \(\log f\) is \(-2\operatorname{diag}(1/4,4)\), whereas every translate of \(\log g\) has Hessian \(-2I\).

The radial definition similarly gives \[S_\alpha f=A S_\alpha g,\qquad |S_\alpha f|=|S_\alpha g|<\infty
 \quad(\alpha>0).\] Thus Lemma 10 at \(\alpha=1\) and Lemma 13 at \(\alpha=4\) have equality without the claimed translate condition. The printed \(p=2\alpha/(n+\alpha)\) in Lemma 10 does not exclude the Gaussian.

Counterevidence and actual damage. Theorem 1 itself correctly includes \(GL(n)\) in its equality family, the source states affine covariance on p. 2, and its quoted Burchard theorem on p. 4 retains a common volume-preserving linear map. These support the intended theorem but contradict the auxiliary classifications. The damage is to the central proof of the affine sharp equality families, not a demonstrated failure of the volume bounds. The Major grade reflects loss of the essential affine degree of freedom in this proof chain; local editability does not remove that damage.

Verified bounded repair. In the finite positive-volume, strictly decreasing-profile setting of Lemma 8, retain the same \(A\), \(|\det A|=1\), in \[K=AK^\star,\qquad f(x)=f^\star(A^{-1}(x-x_0)).\] Necessity follows from the common map in the quoted Theorem 5; equality of the outer functions makes their translations agree and removes the homogeneous kernel’s translation. Sufficiency is the exact substitution above. The corrected finite-volume equality statement for Lemma 10 then follows from this and covariance.

For the reverse layer-cake argument, the Burchard condition must also be applied over a nonempty interval \(|r_1-r_2|<r_3<r_1+r_2\) of the kernel-level radius, not at an arbitrarily small radius when the outer level radii differ (p. 11). The full generality of that repaired equality proof is not independently certified here. The counterexample does not depend on assuming the repair.

# F3: compact support does not imply the asserted finiteness (Minor)

Location. Lemma 10, pp. 8–9, especially the sentence after (14). Its printed exponent \(p=2\alpha/(n+\alpha)\) differs from Theorem 1’s \(2n/(n+\alpha)\).

Take \(n=2\), \(\alpha=1\), define \(f(0)=0\), and set \[f(x)=|x|^{-2}\mathbf 1_{\{0<|x|<1\}}.\] Then \(\int f^{2/3}=3\pi<\infty\), but \(\int f=\infty\). For every \(k\ge1\), the compact-support truncation \(f_{(k)}=f\mathbf 1_{kB^2}\) is unchanged. For each \(\xi\in S^1\) and \(0<t<1/2\), integration near \(x=0\) gives \[\int f(x)f(x+t\xi)\,dx=\infty:\] the second factor is bounded below by a positive constant there, and the first has a logarithmically divergent integral. Every radial value of \(S_1f_{(k)}\) is therefore infinite, contrary to the asserted finiteness.

This refutes the approximation sentence on its stated domain. It does not refute the volume comparison, which reads \(\infty\le\infty\) for this radial example. The singular \(f\) is also outside Theorem 1’s intended \(L^{4/3}\) domain. This limited demonstrated effect supports Minor.

A verified replacement is \(f_k=\min(f,k)\mathbf 1_{kB^n}\). Boundedness and compact support bound each directional autocorrelation uniformly and restrict its \(t\)-support, giving \(|S_\alpha f_k|<\infty\). Monotone convergence then passes radial volumes and rearrangements to the limit. This also supplies the comparison at Theorem 1’s actual exponent without assuming an unjustified inclusion between the two global \(L^p\) spaces.

# F4: reciprocal error in the Mellin substitution (Minor)

Location. Equation (29) and accompanying monotonicity sentences, p. 16. The substitution gives \[\int_0^\infty t^{\beta-1}\omega(t/\zeta(\alpha))\,dt
 =\zeta(\alpha)^\beta\int_0^\infty u^{\beta-1}\omega(u)\,du,\] where the source prints \(\zeta(\alpha)^{-\beta}\).

Take \(\omega(t)=e^{-t}\), \(\phi(t)=t/2\), \(\alpha=1\), \(\beta=2\). All stipulated positive and regularized negative moments are finite; \(\phi\) increases and \(\phi(t)/t\) is nondecreasing. Then \(\zeta(1)=2\) and the integral is \(4\), whereas the printed expression is \(1/4\). The uses of increasing on that page likewise conflict with the lemma’s decreasing conclusion. This is a confirmed calculation error, not a counterexample to the monotonicity lemma or reverse inequalities.

For the exponential-weight case used in the log-concave branch, an independent proof is available. Normalize \(F(t)=g(t\xi)/g(0)\), where \(g=f*f^-\), and define \[I_p(F)=
 \begin{cases}\int_0^\infty t^{p-1}F(t)\,dt,&p>0,\\
 \int_0^\infty t^{p-1}(F(t)-1)\,dt,&-1<p<0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad Z(p)=\bigl(I_p(F)/\Gamma(p)\bigr)^{1/p}.\] For \(a=Z(p)\), \(H(t)=F(t)-e^{-t/a}\) changes sign from nonnegative to nonpositive and has zero \(p\)-weighted integral. Multiplication by the increasing factor \(t^{q-p}\) makes its \(q\)-weighted integral nonpositive for \(q>p\). If \(q<0\), division by \(\Gamma(q)<0\) and taking the negative power \(1/q\) reverse direction twice; if \(q>0\) neither reversal occurs. Both give \(Z(q)\le Z(p)\). The regularized expansion supplies continuity at zero. Compact support is permitted with \(-\log0=+\infty\). Thus the needed log-concave conclusion survives; the demonstrated damage is a local proof calculation, supporting Minor.

# Positive checks and coverage limits

For the simplex exponential \[f(x)=e^{-(x_1+\cdots+x_n)}\mathbf 1_{\mathbb R_+^n}(x),\] product integration gives \(\|f\|_1=1\), \(\|f\|_2^2=2^{-n}\) and \(g(y)=2^{-n}e^{-\|y\|_1}\). As \(|B_1^n|=2^n/n!\), the first and middle expressions of Theorem 2 both equal \(2^{\alpha-n}\). An amplitude \(a\) and invertible map \(A\) multiply both by \(a^2|\det A|^{1+\alpha/n}\). These verify its sufficient equality examples without using F2.

The normalization in (30)–(32) yields Theorem 2’s stated constant. The negative Mellin parameter \(p=-2s\) gives the range \(0<s<1/2\) in Theorem 19; the negative volume power reverses inclusion as required. The endpoint \(p=-1\) can diverge even for the exponential example. Normalized bodies are used for nonzero functions; the zero case of the unnormalized inequality is separate.
