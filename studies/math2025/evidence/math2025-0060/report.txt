Abstract: Equation (1.1) of the acquired author manuscript omits the coordinate action from a purported cocycle on a semidirect product. A normalized cocycle on \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\) and three finite-support elements give cocycle-identity sides \(1\) and \(-1\). The same example, realized by Pauli matrices, shows that Theorem C is false when its defining formula is read literally. Inserting the action gives a normalized cocycle for arbitrary base groups and matches the projective-representation construction in the proof. The supported assessment is Major for the literal definition and its main-theorem use, with a precise and short correction. This does not refute the intended corrected rigidity theorem or Theorems A and B. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The displayed formula fails the cocycle identity

Equation reference: eq:literal. Problem 1 (The semidirect action is absent in equation (1.1)).  On page 3, for arbitrary \(\omega_0\in Z^2(H,\mathbb T)\), the manuscript declares a cocycle on the full wreath product by the formula \[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{lit}}((a,(g,h)),(b,(g',h')))
   =\prod_{k\in\Gamma}\omega_0(a_k,b_k).\] The second coordinate is actually printed without an action-dependent index. Formula [eq:literal] (#eq:literal) need not satisfy [eq:cocycle] (#eq:cocycle).

## Admissible base cocycle and group

Take \(\Gamma=F_2=\langle r,s\rangle\). Free groups of rank at least two are explicitly included in the paper’s class \(\mathcal C\) (p. 1). Let \[H=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2,\qquad
 \omega_0(u,v)=(-1)^{u_1v_2}.\] The base group is finite and countable, and \(\omega_0\) is normalized. Its cocycle identity is the elementary identity modulo two \[u_1v_2+(u_1+v_1)w_2=u_1(v_2+w_2)+v_1w_2.\] Thus the base input is a genuine cocycle. With \(a=(1,0)\), \(b=(0,1)\), \(\omega_0(a,b)=-1\) and \(\omega_0(b,a)=1\). Since coboundaries on an abelian group are symmetric, this cocycle is not cohomologous to one.

Equation reference: eq:values. Let \(a_e,b_e\in D\) be supported only at the identity coordinate. In \(G\) put \[x=(0,(r,e)),\qquad
 y=(a_e,(e,e)),\qquad
 z=(b_e,(e,e)).\] All base vectors have finite support. The base vector of \(xy\) is supported at \(r\), whereas that of \(yz\) is \((a+b)_e\). Normalization and \(r\ne e\) give \[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &\Omega_{\mathrm{lit}}(x,y)&\Omega_{\mathrm{lit}}(xy,z)&\Omega_{\mathrm{lit}}(x,yz)&\Omega_{\mathrm{lit}}(y,z)\\ \hline
 \text{value}&1&1&1&-1 .
 \end{array}\] Consequently [eq:cocycle] (#eq:cocycle) reads \(1=-1\). This is an exact counterexample with the stated action, not a numerical residual or finite-quotient approximation.

Equation reference: eq:trivial. In fact the same pure-shift construction works for arbitrary \(a,b\) in any base group: if the unshifted expression [eq:literal] (#eq:literal) is a cocycle, it forces \[
 \omega_0(a,b)=1\quad\text{for every }a,b\in H.\] The obstruction is therefore not particular to a poorly chosen representative of the Pauli cocycle.

# The literal consequence for Theorem C

Theorem C (p. 3) allows any nontrivial amenable tracial base algebra \((A_0,\tau_0)\) and explicitly uses \(\omega_0^\Gamma\) as defined by (1.1). The discrete-abelian base restriction occurs in Theorem B and is not a restriction of Theorem C. Section 8 recalls (1.1) for Theorem 8.1 (p. 54).

## The base algebra is an allowed matrix algebra

Let \[X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \rho(u_1,u_2)=Z^{u_2}X^{u_1}.\] Since \(XZ=-ZX\), \[\rho(u)\rho(v)=\omega_0(u,v)\rho(u+v).\] The four matrices \(I,X,Z,ZX\) form a basis of \(M_2(\mathbb C)\); their normalized traces vanish away from the identity label. They therefore realize a trace-preserving isomorphism \[(L_{\omega_0}(H),\tau)\simeq(M_2(\mathbb C),\operatorname{tr}/2).\] All \(16\) multiplication pairs and the three nonidentity traces were also checked exactly. The algebra \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) is finite dimensional, nontrivial, amenable, and tracial, satisfying Theorem C’s base assumptions.

## One side holds, while the literal other side cannot

Equation reference: eq:LHS. Take \[A_0=M_2(\mathbb C),\qquad
 \Lambda=H^{(\Gamma)}\rtimes(\Gamma\times\Gamma),
 \qquad M=(A_0,\operatorname{tr}/2)^\Gamma\rtimes K.\] Use the genuine corrected cocycle \(\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}\) proved in Section 4 below. In \(M\), let \(U_t\) implement the coordinate action and set \[V_{(a,t)}=\left(\bigotimes_{k\in\Gamma}\rho(a_k)\right)U_t.\] The tensor has finite support. These unitaries have multiplier \(\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}\), trace zero off the identity, and generate \(M\): the local matrices span every tensor factor and the \(U_t\) generate the group part. Their trace inner products are the canonical orthonormal ones. Completing the regular tracial representation gives \[
 M\simeq L_{\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}}(\Lambda).\] Thus the left side of Theorem C is satisfied for a genuine cocycle.

Suppose the right side held with the printed definition (1.1), possibly using another countable group \(H'\) and another base cocycle \(\omega'_0\). Pullback by a group isomorphism and multiplication by a coboundary preserve the cocycle identity in both directions. Since the theorem requires the pullback of \((\omega'_0)^\Gamma\) to be cohomologous to \(\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}\), its unshifted formula would have to be a genuine cocycle. Equation [eq:trivial] (#eq:trivial) then forces \(\omega'_0\) to be identically one.

The other requirement is \(L_{\omega'_0}(H')\simeq M_2(\mathbb C)\) as tracial algebras. The canonical group unitaries form an orthonormal basis in \(L^2\), whereas \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) has dimension four. Hence \(|H'|=4\). Every group of order four is abelian, so \(L_1(H')\) is commutative and cannot be \(M_2(\mathbb C)\). This contradiction shows that the literal right side fails, despite [eq:LHS] (#eq:LHS).

This argument addresses alternative base groups, not just the failure of the initially chosen pair \((H,\omega_0)\). It establishes a consequence for the main statement as defined, while leaving open the intended theorem with the corrected extension.

# The corrected extension and what it proves

Equation reference: eq:corrected. Define on the base group \[B(a,b)=\prod_k\omega_0(a_k,b_k).\] Finite support and normalization make the product finite. It is a normalized cocycle on \(D\), and it is invariant under simultaneous coordinate permutations. The correct extension is \[
 \boxed{\quad
 \Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}((a,(g,h)),(b,(g',h')))
 =B(a,\alpha_{(g,h)}b)
 =\prod_k\omega_0(a_k,b_{g^{-1}kh}).
 \quad}\] This formula works for arbitrary base groups, including nonabelian ones.

To prove it, write \(x=(a,s)\), \(y=(b,t)\), \(z=(c,u)\). The base cocycle identity and simultaneous invariance give \[\begin{aligned}
 \Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}(x,y)\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}(xy,z)
 &=B(a,\alpha_s b)\,B(a\alpha_s b,\alpha_{st}c)\\
 &=B(a,\alpha_s b\,\alpha_{st}c)\,B(\alpha_s b,\alpha_{st}c)\\
 &=B(a,\alpha_s(b\alpha_t c))\,B(b,\alpha_t c)\\
 &=\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}(x,yz)\Omega_{\mathrm{cor}}(y,z).\end{aligned}\] Normalization follows directly. This is a general proof, not an inference from the single witness.

On page 56, the source first constructs a projective representation \(\theta\) of the base and then extends it by the unitaries implementing the Bernoulli action. Multiplying \(\theta(a)U_s\theta(b)U_t\) produces the multiplier \(B(a,\alpha_s b)\) in [eq:corrected] (#eq:corrected). The proof-level construction therefore supports precisely this repair.

The unshifted expression is valid on the base subgroup, where \(s=e\). However, equation (1.1) expressly defines it on the full semidirect product. Simultaneous permutation invariance cannot remove an action in only the second input. Reversing the shift convention still moves the identity coordinate and does not remove the obstruction. The decisive formula was visually checked, so an OCR omission is not the explanation.

The repair restores the cocycle definition, the canonical crossed-product multiplication, and removes the demonstrated literal counterexample. It does not independently prove the deep necessity direction of Theorem 8.1 or all the classification results on which it depends. The supported assessment is Major for an invalid definition explicitly used by a main theorem. The short length of a correction does not make the printed identity true. No counterexample to Theorems A or B, or to the intended action-corrected theorem, is established.

## Triple comultiplication

Pages 3–4 define \(\bar a=(a^*)^{\mathrm{op}}\), an antilinear multiplicative map, and \(\Delta_3(u_g)=u_g\otimes\bar u_g\otimes u_g\). Consequently the middle multiplier is \(\overline{\omega(g,h)}\) and the product multiplier is \[\omega(g,h)^2\overline{\omega(g,h)}=\omega(g,h).\] Thus the elementary multiplicativity check passes. It does not establish the coarse-embedding properties.

## Faithfulness and amplification

Theorem A(ii) concerns a nonzero bifinite module. Section 2.1 and Corollary 6.13 distinguish this from faithfulness for arbitrary target algebras; a nonzero bimodule is automatically faithful when both algebras are factors, not for every nonfactor target. Conjugating a finite-dimensional projective representation inverts its cocycle, so the inverse presentations of the multiplier ratio impose the same finite-type condition.

Theorem 6.15 (pp. 47–48) retains a central support projection \(q\) and a decomposition \(L_\omega(\Lambda_{\rm fc})q\simeq M_d(\mathbb C)\otimes\mathbb C^k\), with \[(\operatorname{Tr}\otimes\tau)(p)=\frac{\tau(q)}{dk}.\] In the unamplified full-algebra isomorphism case, \(p=q=1\) gives \(dk=1\), hence \(d=k=1\) and a trivial finite virtual center. This specialization agrees with Theorem A(i). It cannot be obtained for arbitrary amplifications by discarding central summands.
