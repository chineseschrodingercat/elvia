Abstract: This report reviews the exact 87-page, author-hosted, publisher-typeset online-first article by Maria Aguareles, Inmaculada Baldomá and Tere M-Seara. The assessment is Major. An analytic energy bound for the degree-two vortex proves that the paper’s prescribed interval contains no root of its exact dominant phase-matching equation. The sign responsible for that failure propagates to the main prefactor derivation. Independent checks identify an incorrect parameter-universality claim for the physical wavenumber, non-equivalent inner fixed-point equations, an overly broad uniform outer estimate, and a reversed matching inverse used in the final Brouwer construction. There are five Major and two Minor findings; the earlier zero-twist candidate is retained only as a scope clarification because its counterexample leaves the printed real-valued class. A fully admissible boundary example disproves a dominant-profile monotonicity assertion. The evidence distinguishes false statements and failed proof constructions from claims about a fully repaired global theorem. All source text pages were read; decisive source pages were also inspected as images. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

Problem 1 (Major: the prescribed dominant matching interval has no root).  Theorem 2.5, p. 15, defines \[C_n=\lim_{r\to\infty}

## The exact expansion and a nonzero constant

Equation reference: innercorrect. Write \(f=f_0\) and \[A(r)=\int_0^r t f^2(1-f^2)\,dt,\qquad

 v_0^{\rm in}=-\frac{qn^2(1+k^2)\log r}{r}
       -\frac{qC_n}{r}+\frac{qk^2r}{2}
       +qO(r^{-3}\log r)+qk^2O(r^{-1}).\] Appendix B, pp. 84–85, itself yields these signs. They are not an alternative convention for the printed definition of \(C_n\).

Equation reference: groundstate, cnnegative. To exclude the possibility that the constant vanishes, define \[E_R(u)=\int_0^R

Problem 2 (Major: the physical wavenumber is not a function of the twist alone).  Theorem 1.5 and Remark 1.6, p. 6, assert a universal physical selection depending only on \(q\). The exact change of variables in Lemma 2.3, pp. 12–13, contradicts that statement and its physical oddness claim.

Use \(A\) for the physical dispersion parameter, to distinguish it from the outer cutoff exponent. For any normalized branch \((F,v)\) with \(0<k<1\), put \[D=1-Aq(1-k^2),\qquad
 \beta=\frac{A+q}{1-Aq},\qquad a=\sqrt D,\] \[\delta^2=\frac D{1-Aq},\qquad
 \Omega=-\frac{A+q(1-k^2)}D.\] If \(\chi'=v\), then \[\mathcal{A}(t,r,\varphi)=
 \delta^{-1}F(r/a)
       e^{i\Omega t+in\varphi+i\chi(r/a)}\] solves the original CGL equation by direct substitution. The primary symbolic calculation verifies both real and imaginary equations. Its physical wavenumber and far-field amplitude satisfy \[K(A,q)=\frac{k}{\sqrt{1-Aq(1-k^2)}},\qquad
 \frac{1-k^2}{\delta^2}=1-K(A,q)^2.\] For \(0<q<1/3\), the parameter pairs \[(A,\beta)=(0,q),\qquad
 (A,\beta)=\left(1,\frac{1+q}{1-q}\right)\] have the same twist and both satisfy \(|A-\beta|<1\). Both frequencies are nonzero, both scalings are positive, and \(0<K<1\) because \(D-k^2=(1-q)(1-k^2)>0\) for the second pair. The regular core, integer winding, unique zero and far-field conditions survive this positive rescaling. Nevertheless, \[K(0,q)=k,\qquad

This is an exact contradiction to existence plus universal \(q\)-only selection, without assuming a numerical approximation or an unrelated branch.

Likewise, normalized oddness \(\kappa(-q)=-\kappa(q)\) gives \[K(A,-q)=

For each bounded fixed \(A\), \(K/\kappa=1+O(q)\), so this defect alone does not change the leading relative accuracy \(O(|\log q|^{-1})\). Problem 1 concerns that leading coefficient independently.

# A confirmed local range defect

Problem 5 (Minor: the dominant outer monotonicity starts too early).  Proposition 4.2(iii), p. 24, asserts \(\partial_rv_0^{\rm out}>0\) when \(kqr\ge2e^{-\pi/(2nq)}\). The endpoint admits a real, regular counterexample.

Take \(n=\mu=1\), \(\epsilon=e^{-\pi/(2q)}\), \(k=\epsilon/q\) and \(r=2\). Then \(R=2\epsilon\) is precisely that endpoint. The convergent series (A.5)–(A.8) give \[K_{iq}(R)=q^{-1}(1+o(1))>0,\qquad
 RV_q(R)=-\gamma q^2+o(q^2).\] Using the exact Riccati equation, rather than differentiating an uncontrolled remainder, gives \[\frac{R^2V_q'(R)}{q^2}
 =\frac{R^2}{q^2}-\frac{RV_q(R)}{q^2}
   -\frac{(RV_q(R))^2}{q^2}-1\longrightarrow\gamma-1<0.\] Hence \[\frac{\partial_rv_0^{\rm out}(2;k,q)}q
            \longrightarrow\frac{\gamma-1}{4}<0.\] The dominant amplitude is real, since \(F_q(R)^2\to3/4>0\). The bounds \(1-\log2\le\gamma\le3/2-\log2<1\) suffice; no decimal evaluation is used.

The cotangent comparison on p. 78 has its inequality reversed. The stronger threshold \(2e^2e^{-\pi/(2nq)}\) used for the Green estimates avoids this example, as does the actual matching point. A corrected monotonicity domain requires a corrected proof.

There is also a literal amplitude-domain obstruction in Proposition 4.2(iv) and Lemma A.3. Take \(n=1\), \(\mu=100\) in a fixed interval \([99,101]\), \(\alpha=1/2\), and \(R_{\min}=2e^2e^{-\pi/(2q)}\). For small \(q\) the stated domain inequalities hold, but \[F_0(R_{\min})^2
 \le1-\left(\frac{100}{2e^2}\right)^2<0.\] An explicit physical-radius or reality restriction is needed. The narrower domain of Theorem 4.3 avoids this case.

# Matching inverse and other checks

The following checks distinguish a core construction failure, a bounded substitution correction and an unconfirmed scope issue.

#### P6 (Major): matching inverse.

For the matching system on pp. 30 and 34, \[Ka-Ib=\Delta,\qquad K'a-I'b=\Delta'/\sqrt2,\qquad
 d=KI'-K'I\ne0,\] direct elimination gives \[a=\frac{I'\Delta-I\Delta'/\sqrt2}{d},\qquad

The actual core map and its consistency estimates therefore require correction. This is Major even though suitable absolute-value bounds may survive the corrected sign. No additional actual nonlinear matched solution or false global existence conclusion is manufactured from an arbitrary \(\Delta\).

#### Local correction P7 (Minor): displayed remainder substitutions.

On p. 33, \(q^3\log^3r_0/r_0=O(\rho^3/r_0)\), not \(O(\rho^3/r_0^2)\). The valid bound still gives \(O(|\log q|^{-1})\) after multiplication by \(r_0/q\). #### Scope clarification P8: the zero-twist evolution.

The exact transformation at \(q=0\) is \[\mathcal{A}=e^{-iA t}U
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 U_t=(1+iA)(\Delta U+U-|U|^2U).\] However, the equation on p. 5 explicitly restricts the rotated unknown to be real-valued. If \(A\ne0\) and \(U\) remains real, the imaginary part forces \(\Delta U+U-U^3=0\) and hence \(U_t=0\); these restricted solutions also satisfy the displayed real GL equation. For \(A=0\) the transformed equation already is real GL.

The earlier spatially constant solution with \(A=1\), \(U(0)=1/2\) has \(U'(0)=3(1+i)/8\) and immediately leaves that real-valued class. It is therefore not an all-hypothesis counterexample to the restricted statement. Its previous confirmed-Minor status is withdrawn. The rotation does not conjugate the general complex dynamics to real GL, but the intended scope of the prose needs clarification; no additional confirmed error is counted here.
