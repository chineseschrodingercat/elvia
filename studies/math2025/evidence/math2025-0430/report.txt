Abstract: The characteristic-class framework connects equivariant Riemann–Roch, lattice sums and differential Euler–Maclaurin operators. Four substantive corrections are established. Three affect explicit formulae under their stated hypotheses: primitive quotient-ray normalization in proper-face operators, the unnormalized weighted face prefactor, and repeated vertex translation in local cone formulae. The fourth is a false Cox-group product used as a core descent step in both printed characteristic-class derivations. All four findings are Major. A separate divisor-sign discrepancy is an uncounted editorial clarification because the actual subsequent character calculation already uses the correct direction. Exact examples, counterchecks and repairs delimit the conclusions affected. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Framework and positive checks

The orbit decomposition weights the dualizing-sheaf contribution of an orbit closure by \((1+y)^{\dim O}\). Proper pushforward and equivariant Riemann–Roch then express twisted characteristic classes as lattice sums. The source distinguishes localization statements for general toric varieties from the explicit Cox formulae and facet operators requiring simplicial fans, hence simple full-dimensional lattice polytopes. Completeness is used for the relevant global pushforwards. With the paper’s left action, a monomial \(\chi^m\) has character \(\chi^{-m}\) and \(s(m)=-c(m)\); these signs matter in both translation checks.

The abstract operator statements, including Theorems 5.18 and 7.10, require a power series representing the actual equivariant Todd class of the chosen sheaf. An incorrect proposed representative does not disprove that conditional theorem. Polynomial insertions permit finite-degree truncation. Exponential cone integrals additionally require a convergence chamber; the real negative parameter used below satisfies it. External Riemann–Roch, localization, resolution and quotient-form results were examined as stated inputs, not independently reacquired or reproved.

Retained exact checks verify the finite-group Molien projection, the single vertex shift in weighted Brion summation, and the global Todd operator on the singular triangle used below. Its weighted count is \(4+y\), including the relative-interior point on its long edge. The interval tests cover 63 exact classical cases; separate checks address interior and weighted formulae. Lattice-normalized Stokes identities and the multiplicity in the face-integral identity were also checked. In particular, at the triangle’s singular vertex the multiplicity \(2\) cancels the mixed-volume derivative \(1/2\). These checks are finite tests with explicit hypotheses, not an exhaustive verification of every characteristic-class identity.

# Established corrections

## P01 (Major): quotient-ray normalization in face operators

Equations (5.29)–(5.36), pp. 74–75, use the ordinary fan \(\operatorname{Star}(\sigma_E)\) but differentiate in the original ambient facet variables. A projected ray need not remain primitive. The source explicitly uses primitive ray generators, the saturated lattice \(N_\sigma=N\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\sigma\), and the quotient \(N/N_\sigma\) (pp. 16–19). These conventions preclude silently replacing the ordinary quotient fan by a different nonprimitive presentation.

Let \[P=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\},\qquad
 E=\{(0,t):0\leq t\leq1\},\qquad M=N=\mathbb Z^2.\] The primitive inward normals are \(u_1=(1,0)\), \(u_2=(0,1)\) and \(u_3=(-1,-2)\). This is a simple full-dimensional lattice polytope, as required; no Delzant assumption is present. For \(\sigma_E=\mathbb R_{\geq0}u_1\), one has \(\operatorname{mult}(\sigma_E)=1\). In \(N/N_{\sigma_E}\cong\mathbb Z\), the other normals project to \(1\) and \(-2\), while the primitive rays of \(\operatorname{Star}(\sigma_E)\) are \(1,-1\). Thus its toric variety is \(\mathbb P^1\) and its finite local group contribution is trivial.

Writing \(\partial_i=\partial/\partial h_i\), the paper’s ambient dilation and lattice-normalized volume give \[V(h)=\frac{(2+h_1+2h_2+h_3)^2}{4},\qquad
 W(h)=\partial_1V(h)=1+\frac{h_1}{2}+h_2+\frac{h_3}{2}.\] For \(Q(t)=t/(1-e^{-t})=1+t/2+\cdots\), the printed closed-face operator therefore gives \[\left.Q(\partial_2)Q(\partial_3)W\right|_{h=0}
 =1+\frac12+\frac14=\frac74,
 \qquad |E\cap M|=2.\] The dual series \(Q^\vee(t)=t/(e^t-1)=1-t/2+\cdots\) gives \[\left.Q^\vee(\partial_2)Q^\vee(\partial_3)W\right|_{h=0}
 =\frac14,\qquad |\operatorname{Relint}(E)\cap M|=0.\] These evaluations are exact: \(W\) is affine, so all higher derivatives vanish.

In this example the intrinsic upper facet offset is \((h_1+h_3)/2\). Replacing the affected derivative by \(2\partial_3\) repairs both values. An alternative nonprimitive quotient presentation with weights \(1,-2\) has a \(\mu_2\) contribution; the nonidentity term supplies the missing \(+1/4\) and \(-1/4\), respectively. That alternative requires a different, explicitly justified construction. A general repair cannot be obtained merely by renaming the ordinary star fan.

This is a false explicit closed- and open-face guarantee under the stated hypotheses, hence Major. It also affects the weighted face formulae at \(y=0\), including (6.18). It does not refute global Brion summation, the general Riemann–Roch theorem, or the leading fundamental-class factor in the face-integral identity. The proof of Theorem 7.12 cites the erroneous full formula (5.34), but its limit (7.13) only needs the lowest-codimension Todd component, namely \([V_{\sigma_E}]\). That component equals \(\operatorname{mult}(\sigma_E)\prod_{\rho\in\sigma_E(1)}F_\rho\) and supplies the required limit directly. Thus the full-series error should not be propagated to Corollary 7.13.

## P02 (Major): the unnormalized weighted face prefactor

Equations (6.14)–(6.17), p. 80, retain the ambient factor \((1+y)^{n-r}\) in an operator for a proper face. Take \(P=[0,1]\), \(E=\{0\}\) and \(f=1\), with \(n=1\), \(r=2\). The zero-dimensional star fan has no rays or nontrivial group. The ambient dilated volume is \[V(h)=1+h_L+h_R.\] The printed unnormalized operator is consequently \((1+y)^{-1}\partial_L\). At \(y=1\) it gives \(1/2\), whereas the right side of (6.16) is the single point’s weight \(1\). The parameter avoids the excluded value \(y=-1\). This smooth example is independent of P01.

Using the face fan’s own dimension and number of rays gives exponent \(0\) for this point, correcting this test. The issue is Major because the explicitly stated weighted face identity is false under its stated hypotheses, even in the smooth case. It is not evidence against every normalized formula. The normalized expression printed below (6.17) removes this prefactor: its point operator is \(\partial_L\), acting on \(P_y(h)\) of length \(1+y+h_L+h_R\), and gives \(1\) as required in (6.18). P01, rather than this point test, supplies the separate \(y=0\) failure of (6.18).

## P03 (Major): a vertex translation is counted twice

The source defines \(\operatorname{Tan}(P,v)=v+C_v\) on p. 66. Equations (5.22), (5.25), (5.28), (6.6) and (6.13) retain an additional \(e^{\langle v,z\rangle}\) in front of a lattice sum already over this translated cone (pp. 73–74, 78, 80). This is one repeated error.

Take \(P=[1,2]\), \(v=1\), \(C_v=[0,\infty)\) and real \(z<0\) arbitrarily near zero. The dilated tangent cone is \([1-h,\infty)\), and \[I(h)=\int_{1-h}^{\infty}e^{xz}\,dx
      =-\frac{e^{(1-h)z}}{z},\qquad
 \left.Q(\partial_h)I(h)\right|_{h=0}
      =\frac{e^z}{1-e^z}.\] The printed additional factor instead produces \[e^z\sum_{m\geq1}e^{mz}=\frac{e^{2z}}{1-e^z}.\] For \(e^z=99/100\), these are \(99\) and \(9801/100\). The relative-interior operator similarly gives \(e^{2z}/(1-e^z)\), while the additional factor gives \(e^{3z}/(1-e^z)\). The weighted versions have the same duplicated shift. The discrepancy occurs within the stated convergence chamber and can be made arbitrarily close to zero; it is not a boundary-value or divergent-series artifact.

One should either remove the exterior exponential from the already translated sum or sum over the unshifted \(C_v\). The repeated explicit local Euler–Maclaurin identities are false, which supports Major severity despite this short correction. The general Proposition 5.22 uses an untranslated local characteristic class and is not the erroneous equation (5.22). Likewise, the global Brion formula (4.19) contains a single shift multiplying an unshifted cone series. Neither statement is refuted by this example.

## P04 (Major): an algebraic-group product need not exist

The proof of Lemma 3.20, p. 42, and the alternative localization argument on p. 61 assert \(G\cong G_\sigma\times(\mathbb C^*)^{r-n}\). For the same triangle fan the Cox map is \[\beta(t_1,t_2,t_3)=(t_1/t_3,t_2/t_3^2),\qquad
 G=\{(t,t^2,t):t\in\mathbb C^*\}.\] For the maximal cone generated by \(u_1,u_3\), the finite stabilizer is \(G_\sigma=\mu_2\). The group \(G\cong\mathbb C^*\) is connected; \(\mu_2\times\mathbb C^*\) is not. The relevant sequence is \[1\longrightarrow\mu_2\longrightarrow\mathbb C^*
 \xrightarrow{\,t\mapsto t^2\,}\mathbb C^*
 \longrightarrow1.\] An algebraic section would require an integer exponent \(k\) with \(2k=1\). Thus neither occurrence is a valid product argument.

There is a direct invariant-module replacement. Let \(I\) be the maximal cone’s rays, \(J\) their complement, \(T_I=(\mathbb C^*)^I\), and \(H=\ker(T_I\to\mathbb T)\). The isogeny \(T_I\to\mathbb T\) has finite kernel \(H\); it need not split. For a Cox character twist \(\widetilde m\), restriction to the slice \(y_J=1\) identifies \[\bigl(\mathbb C[x_I,y_J^{\pm1}]\otimes\mathbb C_{\widetilde m}\bigr)^G
 \ \cong\
 \bigl(\mathbb C[x_I]\otimes\mathbb C_{\widetilde m_I}\bigr)^H.\] To verify surjectivity without a product assumption, an \(H\)-invariant monomial \(x^a\) satisfies \(\widetilde m_I-a=\beta_I^*u\) for a unique \(u\in M\). Its unique Laurent extension has exponent \(b=\widetilde m_J-\beta_J^*u\). This proves the identification on monomial bases and preserves the residual \(\mathbb T=T_I/H\) action. The same argument applies to the coordinate cotangent wedges; the invariant logarithmic forms \(dy_j/y_j\) contribute \((1+y)^{|J|}=(1+y)^{r-n}\).

This replaces the false product step in Lemma 3.20 and the local character calculation. The quotient-form input invoked in the source remains an explicit external dependency. Source p. 43 obtains the main Theorem 3.21 from Lemma 3.20 via Lefschetz–Riemann–Roch; the alternative localization derivation repeats the false splitting on p. 61. The missing valid descent is therefore a core dependency in both printed derivations, which supports Major severity. The independently checked invariant-module replacement restores that bridge. The witness disproves the product assertion and the original proof step; it does not disprove the characteristic-class identity, and the availability of a repair does not retroactively validate the printed derivations.

# Editorial clarification excluded from findings

The paragraph immediately before Corollary 3.16, p. 40, sets \(P'=P_D-m_0\) but prints \(\mathcal O(D-\operatorname{div}\chi^{m_0})
 \cong f^*\mathcal O(D')\) as an equivariant identity. The sign should be plus. For \(P_D=[1,2]\) and \(m_0=1\), the coefficients of \(D\) for the normals \(1,-1\) are \((-1,2)\), while those of \(\operatorname{div}\chi^{m_0}\) are \((1,-1)\). Thus \[D+\operatorname{div}\chi^{m_0}=(0,1)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P'=[0,1],\] whereas the printed minus gives \((-2,3)\) and \([2,3]\). The source’s formula (2.16), p. 20, already has the correct plus sign. The proof on p. 41 also uses the correct factor \(e^{s(m_0)}=e^{-c(m_0)}\). This is a local equivariant translation error, not a counterexample to the underlying nonequivariant line-bundle class or to Corollary 3.16. The discrepancy is confined to this transitional sentence: the actual character calculation follows the correct direction. No essential calculation or conclusion is shown to use the wrong sign. It is therefore retained as an editorial clarification and excluded from the substantive findings.

# Extent of the conclusion

The four Major findings concern three explicit local or face formulae central to the Euler–Maclaurin applications and an invalid Cox-descent bridge in the main characteristic-class derivations. The verified replacement for that bridge preserves the stated result while correcting its original proof. The isolated divisor-sign discrepancy is excluded from the findings because the subsequent character computation already uses the correct sign. Repeated manifestations of one defect have been grouped, and no issue count was imposed.

Several tempting extensions of these objections are unsupported. The ambient face-integral multiplicity is correct; the failed ordinary star Todd series does not invalidate its leading term. The smooth point test does not refute the separately normalized point operator. The source explicitly invokes polynomial continuation for its \(y=-1\) specialization; literal negative dilation should not be substituted for that continuation. The acquired preprint, rather than an unverified journal text, is the sole version covered by these findings.
