# Problem 1: the unrestricted statement of Lemma 4.1

## Claim and exact failure

On pp. 12–13, Lemma 4.1 asserts for an arbitrary field \(F\) and finite \(H\)-module \(B\) that \((B\otimes F(V)^\times)^H\) is generated by the constant invariants and all \[N_{H/H_x}(b\otimes f_x),\qquad b\in B^{H_x},\quad \operatorname{div}(f_x)=x .\] The statement is in released `main.tex`, line 561; its proof is lines 565–573. A listed element need not be invariant.

Take \(F=\mathbb R\), \(H=C_2=\{1,s\}\), \(V=\mathbb A^1_\mathbb R\) with coordinate \(t\) and faithful sign action \(s(t)=-t\), and \(B=\mathbb Z/2\) with trivial action. All groups and modules have the finiteness required in the lemma. Let \(x\) be the codimension-one origin. Then \(H_x=H\), \(f_x=t\), and \(\operatorname{div}(t)=x\) on this affine line. With \(b=1\), the prescribed norm is just the identity, so the proposed generator is the class \([t]\) in \[B\otimes \mathbb R(t)^\times=\mathbb R(t)^\times/\mathbb R(t)^{\times2}.\] But \(s[t]-[t]=[-1]\ne0\). Indeed, if a real rational function squared were \(-1\), evaluation at any real point avoiding its finitely many poles would give a negative real square. Thus the purported generator is not in the group it is claimed to generate. Multiplying \(f_x\) by a real nonzero constant does not change this obstruction.

The proof correctly describes the invariants of the permutation divisor module. It does not, in this generality, show that those invariant divisor generators lift to invariant elements of \(B\otimes F(V)^\times\). Taking invariants is only left exact.

## Counterevidence and verified restricted correction

This does not invalidate the actual use with \(B=A(-1)\) in Theorem 1.3. Write \(n=e(A)\) and \(e=e(H)\). Its hypotheses include \(\mu_{ne}\subset F\). For \(h\in H_x\), the character calculation on pp. 13–14 gives \[h(f_x)/f_x=\chi_x(h)\in\mu_e\subset F^{\times n}.\] If \(nB=0\), then \(b\otimes\chi_x(h)=0\), so \(b\otimes f_x\) is \(H_x\)-invariant. Its coset norm is consequently a well-defined \(H\)-invariant lift of the stated divisor norm. Decomposing an invariant divisor into its finite set of orbits, subtracting these invariant lifts, and using exactness of the tensorized divisor sequence leaves precisely a constant invariant. This proves the generation assertion under these additional hypotheses, including its needed application.

The disposition is Minor, local overstatement. The counterexample refutes the independent general lemma; the main theorem already supplies the condition needed by the verified restricted argument. The classification is based on this dependency check, not on how easy correction might be. No author adoption or journal-version defect is asserted.

# Problem 2: restriction in Lemma 5.3

On p. 15, `main.tex` line 697, the alternative proof says that every element of the preimage \(E\) preserves \(\widetilde V\) and therefore defines a restriction map to \(\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(\widetilde V)\). For \(p=3\) and rank-one summands, \[g=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\3&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_2(\mathbb Z/9)\] reduces to the identity, so belongs to \(E\). But \(g(1,0)=(1,3)\) does not belong to the first summand. Literal restriction is therefore not the asserted endomorphism.

The required map nevertheless exists as the upper-left block, with projection back onto \(\widetilde V\) understood. In general write \[g=\begin{pmatrix}A&pB\\pC&I+pD\end{pmatrix}\in E.\] Since \(\bar A\) is invertible, \(A\) is invertible over \(\mathbb Z/p^2\). For two such matrices, the upper-left block of their product is \(AA'+p^2BC'=AA'\). Therefore \(\pi(g)=A\) is a group homomorphism, reduces to the required identity on \(\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(V)\), and restricts on the kernel to the linear projection \(\mathfrak{gl}(V\oplus W)\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)\). The extension diagram commutes. Pullback followed by this pushforward proves Lemma 5.3, and the same map applies to a particular lifted representation, proving the pointwise use on p. 20.

The judgment is Minor, incorrect map description; the needed block-map argument has been checked for arbitrary finite ranks, not only for the displayed example. The paper also cites an external proof, which was not relied upon here. An author-hosted October 2017 version of that reference has different numbering, so the cited Lemma 3.4 was not certified by that lookup.

# Problem 3: locally incorrect formulas

## Inflation target

Equation (2.3), p. 6, released `main.tex` lines 305–306, has target \(H^2(L,A)\) and is called a short exact sequence. The inflation target in the displayed Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre sequence must instead be \(H^2(K,A)\), and the displayed three terms are an exact segment, without asserted injectivity or surjectivity at the ends.

For a direct check, take \(L=\mathbb C\), \(K=\mathbb R\), \(H=C_2\) and trivial \(A=\mathbb Z/2\). Then \(H^1(L,A)=H^2(L,A)=0\) but \(H^2(C_2,A)=\mathbb Z/2\). The printed sequence is \(0\to\mathbb Z/2\to0\) and is not exact at its middle term. With target \(H^2(\mathbb R,A)\), inflation is the identity under \(\Gamma_\mathbb R=C_2\), restoring exactness. The preceding spectral sequence already has abutment \(H^{i+j}(K,A)\), and Proposition 2.1 has the fixed-field target. These are strong internal counterevidence against a deeper error in Corollary 2.4. The consequence is a Minor formula/terminology correction.

## Character and cross-reference

The first lines of p. 19 identify \(H^2(Z,\mathbb Z)\) with the character \(\tau_{13}\); the action convention on p. 18 instead gives \(\tau_{31}\). For \(t=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3)\), \[(t\chi_{13})(I+cE_{13})=\chi_{13}(t^{-1}(I+cE_{13})t)
=(t_3/t_1)c .\] This is \(\tau_{31}\), which differs from \(\tau_{13}\), for example at \(p=5\) and \(t=\operatorname{diag}(2,1,1)\). With the inverse character, the ensuing invariant subspace and vanishing calculation on p. 19 are correct. At \(p=3\) the two characters coincide; the paper separately excludes the extra \(E_{31}\) vector because it is not in \(A^Z\). This is a local label error, not failure of the invariant-subspace conclusion. Similarly, the application of Lemma 3.4 on p. 11 should refer to (3.3), whose terms are trivial \(H\)-modules, rather than (3.2). Both observations are retained within this Minor formula item rather than counted as independent core failures.

# Positive checks, boundaries, and dependency chain

Negligibility quantifies over every extension \(K/F\) and every continuous map \(\Gamma_K\to H\). Kummer lifting in Example 2.2 works over every such \(K\), since the roots of unity remain present and \(K^\times/K^{\times mn}\to K^\times/K^{\times m}\) is surjective. Its alternating use of \(F\) and \(K\) in the prose does not defeat that argument.

The construction of \(\beta_f\) uses \(\mu_e\subset L^{\times n}\), so changing \(f\) by \(\mu_e\) changes its tensor by zero. The transgression sign in Proposition 3.1 agrees with minus the extension factor-set convention. Lemma 3.4 uses trivial coefficient actions; applying it to (3.3) supplies the required input. Constants are killed by \(\gamma\) after base change to an algebraic closure, because a finite module tensored with its divisible multiplicative group is zero and the right-hand \(H^2(H,A)\) map remains the identity. These checks support the two inclusions in Theorem 1.3 subject to Proposition 2.1 and the standard functoriality of cohomology.

For Lemma 5.2, conjugation by \((I+N)^i=I+iN\), with \(N^2=0\), is quadratic in \(i\). For \(p>3\), all sums of powers of degrees \(0,1,2\) vanish in \(\mathbb F_p\), so the norm is zero and the order-\(p\) lift is impossible. The hypothesis \(p>3\) is essential for this argument. For \(p=3\), the independent symbolic calculation reproduces \[N_{\sigma_{12}}(a)=a_{21}E_{12},\qquad
\bigl(-(\sigma_{13}-1)a+(\sigma_{12}-1)b\bigr)_{23}=a_{21}.\] The order condition forces \(a_{21}=-1\) and commutation forces \(a_{21}=0\). The calculation uses arbitrary symbolic entries, not a sample of lifts.

The centralizers \(A^U=\langle I,E_{13}\rangle\) and \(A^N=\langle I,E_{12},E_{13}\rangle\) follow by commuting with the indicated elementary matrices. Exactness of \(T\)-invariants uses \(p\nmid(p-1)^3\). The character products for the \(U\) term have no trivial constituent for odd \(p\). The \(N\) term has the two stated invariant generators. For the first, the norm of \(E_{12}\) is \(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(E_{12}-jE_{13})=0\); projection gives zero. For the second, the norm of \(\chi_{13}\) vanishes since \(p\) and \(p(p-1)/2\) are zero modulo \(p\). Finally, \(A^Z\) is upper triangular, so with the corrected dual character its only relevant invariant is \(E_{13}\otimes\partial\chi_{13}\); restriction followed by corestriction multiplies it by \(p\) and kills it. The script’s character checks for \(p=3,5,7\) supplement these general calculations.

Theorem 5.1 may enlarge \(F\) to adjoin \(\mu_{p^2}\): non-negligibility over that larger base implies non-negligibility over the smaller one. This is the correct direction of Lemma 2.3(5). Theorem 1.4 only assumes \(\mu_p\subset F\); the stronger root assumption is used after this enlargement for the obstruction, not imposed on the final rational function field. For odd \(p\), \(U_3(\mathbb F_p)\) has exponent \(p\). Its faithful \(p\)-dimensional representation is obtained from the cyclic shift and diagonal matrices on a basis indexed by \(\mathbb F_p\), with primitive \(\zeta_p\) diagonal entries; their commutator is a nontrivial scalar. Identification of the invariant field as purely transcendental still depends on Chu–Kang. The publisher result was identified, but direct page retrieval returned HTTP 403, so its exact Theorem 1.6 and proof remain unverified here.
