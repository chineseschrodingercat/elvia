Abstract: The reviewed document is Frantzikinakis and Kuca’s arXiv:2207.12288v2, 18 December 2024, 66 pages. Two complete counterexamples disprove the qualitative-to-uniform-quantitative assertions in Propositions A.2 and A.1. The second counterexample uses the exact Host–Kra seminorm in a single fixed ergodic system. The false uniformity result is used in Corollary 8.5, so the seminorm-smoothing proof has a Major dependency gap. These examples do not refute the main theorem for pairwise independent polynomials. Several local statement and proof corrections are also identified. All findings are confined to this author manuscript; identity with the 2025 publisher version has not been established.

Date: 26 October 2026

Title: Initial mathematical review:
Joint ergodicity for commuting transformations

# Identity and scope

M25-0053. Nikos Frantzikinakis and Borys Kuca, Joint ergodicity for commuting transformations and applications to polynomial sequences, arXiv:2207.12288v2 [paper]. Associated journal DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-024-01313-w. Page numbers below are one-based PDF pages, equal to the printed numbers.

All 66 pages were actually read across the primary reviewer and three native session assistants, assigned pp. 1–22, 23–44 and 45–66. The primary reviewer independently checked the retained counterexamples, the exact statements and proof errors, the principal dependency on Appendix A, and selected source-page images. Full reading is distinguished from exhaustive independent reproof. The coverage ledger names the remaining external and technical dependencies.

The hypotheses distinguish commuting invertible transformations, pairwise independent zero-constant integer polynomials in Theorem 2.8, and linearly independent polynomials in Theorem 2.10 (p. 9). Characteristic-factor approximation in (8) is different from existence of the average limit in (9); Corollary 2.3 explicitly includes rational convergence. These distinctions are essential to the findings below.

# Major: the uniformity assertions in Appendix A are false

## A complete counterexample to Proposition A.2

The statement is on p. 57. Set \(\ell=2\), \(m=2\), and \[X_1=X_2=X'_1=X'_2=\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb R),\qquad
 A(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle,\qquad
 q(y)=\left(\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{y_j^2}{j^2}\right)^{1/2}.\] Use the ordinary Hilbert norm for all four norms appearing in the hypotheses. These spaces are separable, reflexive Banach spaces. The auxiliary subspaces are dense, their norms dominate the ambient norms with constant one, and their unit balls are closed. The bilinear functional is continuous, since \(|A(x,y)|\le\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\). Moreover \(q(y-z)\le\|y-z\|_2\), so norm convergence controls the seminorm on the required ball.

The qualitative hypothesis holds: \(q(y)=0\) implies \(y=0\). But for the standard unit vectors \(e_n\), \[q(e_n)=1/n\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \sup_{\|x\|_2\le1}|A(x,e_n)|=1.\] For \(\varepsilon=1/2\), no \(\delta>0\) can satisfy the quantitative conclusion. This checks every stated hypothesis and refutes the claimed equivalence, rather than merely identifying an incomplete argument.

The precise invalid step is the last scaling on p. 60. After obtaining a bound only for \(x_\ell\in B_{X'_\ell}\), the proof applies it to \((2/r_0)^{\ell-1}x_\ell\). A small value of the controlled seminorm does not put that scaled vector in \(B_{X'_\ell}\). The parameter \(r_0\) depends on the earlier Baire argument, and replacing \(\varepsilon\) by an unspecified smaller number does not cure this failure.

## A direct counterexample to Proposition A.1

The assertion on p. 56 is false even with the stated ergodic seminorm. Use the fixed compact group \(X=\mathbb Z_3\) of 3-adic integers with Haar probability measure, and the odometer \(T(x)=x+1\). Put \[T_1=T_2=T,\quad a_1(n)=a_2(n)=n,\quad
 \mathcal Y_1=\mathcal Y_2=\mathcal X,\quad s=2,\quad m=2.\] These transformations commute and are invertible. Characters of finite quotients \(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z\) span a dense subspace of \(L^2(X)\): cylinder functions are dense and each finite quotient has a Fourier basis. The only \(T\)-invariant character is the constant one, so \(T\) is ergodic and has pure point spectrum.

For every bounded \(f_1,f_2\), \[\mathbb E_{n\le N} T^n f_1\,T^n f_2
 =\mathbb E_{n\le N}T^n(f_1f_2)\longrightarrow\int f_1f_2\,d\mu
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2.\] Thus the mean-convergence hypothesis holds. The Kronecker factor is the whole space, so \(\lVert f\rVert_{2,\mathrm{HK}}=0\) implies \(f=0\). This follows also from \(\lVert f\rVert_{2,\mathrm{HK}}^4=\sum_\chi|\widehat f(\chi)|^4\). Therefore the qualitative seminorm-control hypothesis holds for all bounded inputs.

For \(M=3^k\), define the 1-bounded cylinder function \[f_M(x)=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i(x\bmod M)^2}{M}\right).\] Using the order-two cube formula (21) on p. 18, its second derivative is \(\exp(2\pi i\,2h_1h_2/M)\), independent of \(x\). Hence \[\lVert f_M\rVert_{2,\mathrm{HK}}^4
 =\frac1{M^2}\sum_{h_1,h_2\bmod M}e^{2\pi i\,2h_1h_2/M}
 =\frac1M.\] For each fixed \(h_1\), the inner geometric sum is zero unless \(M\mid2h_1\). Since \(M\) is odd, only \(h_1=0\) contributes, with sum \(M\). This proves the identity for all \(k\), without a numerical approximation.

Choose \(f_1=\overline{f_M}\) and \(f_2=f_M\). Every term of every average is exactly \(1\), whereas \(\lVert f_2\rVert_{2,\mathrm{HK}}=M^{-1/4}\to0\). Thus for the same fixed system and subspaces, no uniform \(\delta\) exists for \(\varepsilon=1/2\). This is precisely the dependence claimed in the remark following A.1, so allowing \(\delta\) to depend on the system does not repair it.

An independently written finite calculation corroborated the cube identity for \(M=3,9,27,81\) by reducing the integer root-of-unity polynomial modulo the cyclotomic polynomial. The exact results were \(1/3,1/9,1/27,1/81\); the averaged product was \(1\) in every case. Script, exact inputs and results are saved. This calculation is supplementary to the proof above; no author code was run.

## Core impact, counterevidence and restricted repair

Corollary 8.5, p. 49, explicitly invokes A.1 to obtain a \(\delta\) uniform over the functions and dual sequences. This is a necessary step in the stated seminorm-smoothing induction of Section 8. The failure therefore creates a Major proof dependency gap in the characteristic-factor argument and results that invoke it.

The odometer example has \(p_1=p_2=n\), so it does not satisfy pairwise independence. It is not a counterexample to Theorem 2.8, Theorem 2.10 or Corollary 8.5 itself. The specific polynomial application could admit a different uniform estimate; this review does not supply one. The pointwise-in-the-other-inputs conclusion proved in the first step of A.2 is also not refuted.

Here is a valid limited repair of A.2. Add the hypothesis that the induced linear map \[L:X_m\longrightarrow
 \mathcal B(X_1,\ldots,\widehat X_m,\ldots,X_\ell;\mathbb R),
 \qquad L(y)=A(\,\cdot\,,y,\,\cdot\,),\] is compact in the norm of bounded multilinear forms. If the quantitative conclusion failed, choose \(y_n\in B_{X'_m}\) with \(q(y_n)\to0\) and \(\|Ly_n\|\ge\varepsilon\). Reflexivity gives a weakly convergent subsequence \(y_n\rightharpoonup y\). The norm-closed convex ball is weakly closed. Mazur convex combinations of tails converge in norm to \(y\) inside that ball, and the seminorm continuity plus \(q(y_n)\to0\) gives \(q(y)=0\). The qualitative hypothesis and density imply \(Ly=0\). Compactness then gives \(Ly_n\to Ly\) in norm, a contradiction. Thus the added hypothesis suffices. No such compactness is established for the paper’s ergodic application, and the odometer example shows that it cannot be silently assumed.

# Minor: Corollary 2.7 claims too much convergence

On p. 8 the irrational-equidistribution branch refers to (9), the existence of a limit on p. 7. It lacks rational convergence. A full counterexample is already available for one nilrotation.

Let \(N_0=0\), \(N_k=2^{2^k}\) for \(k\ge1\), and set \(b(n)=k\bmod2\) for \(N_{k-1}<n\le N_k\), \(a(n)=2n+b(n)\). For irrational \(t\), the exponential sum on each block has modulus at most \(2/|1-e^{4\pi it}|\), by the geometric-series formula. There are \(O(\log\log N)\) blocks up to \(N\), including the final partial block, so \(\mathbb E_{n\le N}e^{2\pi i a(n)t}\to0\). This is exactly irrational equidistribution as defined in the paper.

However, \[\left|\frac1{N_k}\sum_{n\le N_k}(-1)^{a(n)}-(-1)^k\right|
 \le \frac{2N_{k-1}}{N_k}\longrightarrow0.\] On the allowed circle nilmanifold take \(T(x)=x+1/2\) and \(f(x)=e^{2\pi ix}\). Then \(T^{a(n)}f=(-1)^{a(n)}f\), whose averages do not converge in \(L^2\). The footnote defining a nilrotation does not exclude rational rotations.

A limited correction is to replace the reference to (9) by the characteristic-factor approximation (8). Alternatively, add rational convergence when asserting existence of the limit. In this example the rational Kronecker factor is the whole space, so (8) holds identically. The surrounding Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 already make this distinction; the example does not refute them.

# Local proof and notation corrections

## Dual-function index in Lemma 3.3 (Minor)

The proof on p. 22 uses \(\mathcal D_s\) to generate \(\mathcal Z_s\), whereas the definition and identity (32) on p. 21 place these dual functions in \(\mathcal Z_{s-1}\). For \(s=1\) and an ergodic irrational circle rotation, \(\mathcal D_1f=\int\bar f\,d\mu\) is constant, while \(\mathcal Z_1\) contains every Fourier character. Thus the claimed dense set in that proof does not have the stated density. Use \(\mathcal D_{s+1}\) throughout this construction, with the matching component identity and density theorem. Choose the approximating family countable and dense in each bounded continuous ball, and rational complex coefficients when working over \(\mathbb C\). On uniformly bounded sets the dual operation is continuous in \(L^2\) by a telescoping estimate, which justifies passage from the dense family. This repairs the local construction; the lemma’s conclusion is not refuted.

## Ties in Proposition 4.3 (Minor)

On p. 25, the sets \(E_k\) are defined by equality with the maximum of finitely many coordinate moduli. They can overlap. On a two-point ergodic cycle take \(\chi_1=1\), \(\chi_2=(-1)^x\), and \(f=(\chi_1+\chi_2)/2\). Both coordinates have modulus \(1/2\), so the printed definition gives \(E_1=E_2=X\). The constructed sum \(\chi_1+\chi_2\) has values \(2,0\), violating the required modulus \(0\) or \(1\) of a non-ergodic eigenfunction (p. 22). Replace \(E_k\) by the invariant measurable set where \(k\) is the least maximizing index. The sets then partition the space; the maximum estimate and phase correction survive, and the pasted function is an admissible eigenfunction. This is a local construction repair, not a refutation of the inverse theorem.

## Difference-box estimate in Lemma 5.2 (Minor)

The statement on p. 28 indexes \(a\) by positive multi-indices, but uses \(a_{h-h'}\) and then an average over \([H]^s\). Differences include zero and negative coordinates. For a sequence extended to \(\mathbb Z^s\), the exact identity is \[\mathbb E_{h,h'\in[H]^s}a_{h-h'}
 =H^{-s}\!\!\sum_{\substack{d\in\mathbb Z^s\\|d_i|<H\ \forall i}}
 \prod_{i=1}^s(1-|d_i|/H)\,a_d .\] The printed positive-box identity and inequality fail, for example, for \(s=1\), \(a_0=1\), and \(a_d=0\) otherwise: the left side is \(1/H\), the printed right side zero. For nonnegative \(a\), the safe bound is the same sum over the full difference box without its weights. Any replacement by a positive-box average requires explicit symmetry and boundary terms. Those application-level adjustments were not independently completed in this review; no further main-theorem counterexample is claimed from this local error.

## Relative density in Corollary 2.14 (scope clarification)

The final sentence on p. 11 uses positive lower density in the relative sense supplied by the preceding paragraph: density within \(\mathbb P-1\). Every subset of \(\mathbb P-1\) has ordinary natural density zero, since the primes do. The preceding paragraph explicitly uses relative density inside \(\mathbb P\), which supplies the intended interpretation. This is a scope clarification, not a separately established substantive defect; the existence assertion and the intended relative-density conclusion are unaffected.

# Supporting checks

The audits traced the non-ergodic eigenfunction and ergodic-decomposition quantifiers, the subsequence choices in Sections 6–7, the induction types and zero-constant convention in Section 8, and the Appendix B PET hypotheses. In particular, the ability to pass to subsequences while preserving the averaged strong-convergence property is explicitly provided by Proposition 5.6. No additional failure was established in that degree-lowering chain. An attempted Appendix B example with a nonzero polynomial constant term was rejected because it violated the standing assumption; it is not a finding.

The review does not independently reconstruct all Host–Kra structure theory, external PET/convergence results, all smoothing parameter cases, the prime-number inputs or every application. No identity with the journal text or subsequent correction is asserted. The primary arXiv record still lists v2; the targeted search produced no verified separate correction. Failed follow-up page retrievals are recorded as failures, not as evidence that corrections are absent.
