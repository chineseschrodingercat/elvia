Abstract: The manuscript develops a geometric automaton method for fixed-point properties of random groups, with small-cancellation and Rips-type applications. Several useful mechanisms survive detailed checking, including checkpoint separation, productive-language counting, fixed-point permanence, and the intended distinction between a fixed dimension and all dimensions. The acquired version nevertheless needs substantive corrections. Its growth definition admits an edgeless automaton, making a central simultaneous-intersection event have probability zero. A cube family disproves the uniform visible-clique growth bound used in the quantitative argument, although an action-dependent reading of the auxiliary lemma is true. The explicit empty-word conventions also give a counterexample to the advertised black-box criterion. In the reduced model, block rewiring alone does not justify the fixed-remainder transfer. Precise witnesses and bounded repairs are provided, together with five groups of local corrections. The Rips normality step remains a request for evidence under the full hypotheses, rather than a claimed counterexample to that corollary. No counterexample to the final fixed-ambient-dimension random-group theorems is established. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# P1. Major: the asserted simultaneous probability is zero

## Exact location and witness

Definition 3.2, pp. 10–11, calls an automaton \(\lambda\)-large when some \(K>0\) makes every start path of length at least \(K\) terminate at a vertex with at least \(\lambda q\) outgoing edges, where \(q=|S^\pm|\). It does not require such a path to exist.

Let \(\Sigma_0\) have one vertex, designated as its start, and no edges. It is finite and deterministically labelled. Taking \(K=1\) satisfies the definition for every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\). Its language is only the empty word; at every positive length it accepts zero words. Thus the growth-to-density assertion before Lemma 5.3, p. 27, and the automaton consequence of that lemma are false.

The effect in the main proof is exact. On p. 29, \(\boldsymbol\Sigma\) is the set of all \(\lambda\)-large automata over the block alphabet of size \(Q=q^m\), with at most \((Qn+1)^n\) states. The one-state \(\Sigma_0\) belongs to this family. For \(L=m\widehat L+r\), \(0\le r<m\), \(L\ge m\), every block relator in \(\widehat R_{m,L}\) has formal block length \(2\widehat L>0\). Formal concatenation is not free reduction. Consequently, \[\widehat R_{m,L}\cap\mathcal L_{\Sigma_0}=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P\!\left\{\forall\Sigma\in\boldsymbol\Sigma,\
       \widehat R_{m,L}\cap\mathcal L_\Sigma\ne\varnothing\right\}=0.\] The displayed event in the proof is therefore impossible, rather than overwhelmingly probable. Excluding the empty word from accepted languages does not repair this issue: the language then becomes empty.

## The arbitrary-vertex assertion and a precise repair

Remark 5.9, p. 29, also needs a restriction. Over \(\{a,A,b,B\}\), take start edges \(s\to u\) labelled \(a\) and \(s\to v\) labelled \(b\); put all four loops at \(u\) and no edge at \(v\). This automaton has infinite start paths and is \(1\)-large with \(K=2\). Yet the accessible vertex \(v\) has no positive-length language. Removing unreachable vertices alone is insufficient.

Require deterministic labelling and a start path of length \(K\), in addition to the eventual branching condition. Then, for \(L\ge K\), \[\#\mathcal L_\Sigma(L)\ge
 \lceil\lambda q\rceil^{L-K}\ge(\lambda q)^{L-K}.\] Every vertex reached by a start path of length at least \(K\) has the same lower bound for all its continuations, and every outgoing endpoint remains in this eventual set. These are precisely the vertices required after the long prefix \(R_1\) in the source’s concatenation argument.

If a length-\(L\) language has at least \(c q^{d'L}\) words and \(d+d'>1\), independent uniform sampling of \(\lfloor q^{dL}\rfloor\) relators gives miss probability at most \[\exp\{-c' q^{(d+d'-1)L}\}\] for sufficiently large \(L\). For fixed alphabet and state bound there are finitely many deterministic automata up to state renaming: with at most \(B\) states their number is at most \(B^2(B+1)^{qB}\). A finite union bound, the eventual-state restriction, and the finitely many residue classes then justify this probabilistic step for a productive family.

This is a Major defect in a central deduction, not a counterexample to the final random-group conclusion. A repaired proof must show that its geometric construction always supplies members of the restricted productive family; it cannot infer that fact from the current definition.

# P2. Major: the required uniform visible-clique bound fails

## The quantifier distinction is essential

Lemma 4.8, p. 20, introduces \(\epsilon(n)>0\) and \(\alpha(\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1,n)>0\), using a bound on pairwise crossings only in the visible set \(\mathcal H\). Its proof, pp. 21–23, proposes \[\alpha(\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1,n)
   =\epsilon_0(1-\epsilon_1)D_n(n-1).\] Theorem 4.16, p. 26, explicitly needs a growth constant depending only on ambient dimension and its specified epsilon parameter. Its induction imports \(\alpha\) with the visible-clique index.

There is a genuine logical qualification. Lemma 4.8 begins by fixing the action and basepoint. If its functions are allowed to depend on those previously fixed data and on the alphabet, that weaker reading is true. Choose any nonbackward letter \(s\). The path \(ss^{-1}\) has safe prefix orbit points \(x,sx,x\) and terminal witness \(H\). Taking such paths gives a checkpoint tree with growth at least \(1/|S^\pm|\). The finding below concerns the uniform reading needed by the subsequent quantitative proof, not this weak existence assertion.

## A complete family of examples

Fix an integer \(h\ge3\), let \(M\) vary, and put \(D=hM+1\). Let \(X=[0,1]^D\), \(x=0\). Use \(M\) distinct formal generators \(b_\ell\), all acting by the coordinate-one flip, and \(D-1=hM\) generators \(t_j\), \(2\le j\le D\), acting by the transposition of coordinates \(1,j\). Their inverse symbols are distinct, although the assigned automorphisms are involutions. Faithfulness and distinct generator images are not hypotheses of the lemma.

Only the flip letters move \(x\). The visible set is the singleton \(\{H_1\}\), with \(H_1=\{z_1=1/2\}\), while every pair of distinct coordinate hyperplanes crosses. Writing \(N=|S^\pm|=2(h+1)M\), we have \[|\mathcal B(H_1)|=2M,\qquad
 |\mathcal B(H_1)|/N=r=\frac1{h+1}.\] The visible crossing bound is \(n=1\), independent of \(M\). If a uniform \(\epsilon(1)>0\) exists, choose \(h\) large enough that \(r\le\epsilon(1)\), and set \(\epsilon_0=\epsilon_1=r\).

Every word \(v\) acts on vertices as \(v(z)=a\mathbin{\oplus}Pz\), with \(a=v(x)\) and \(Pe_j=e_{\pi(j)}\). A progressing terminal word \(w\) must keep every prefix orbit point on the \(x\)-side of \(H_1\). Its only possible visible witness is \(H_1\). Since distinct coordinate hyperplanes cross, the terminal alternative reduces to \(\pi_w(1)=1\).

Consider the parent word \(v\) of a terminal leaf. Prefix safety gives \(a_1=0\). If \(\pi_v(1)=1\), a flip gives terminal first coordinate one and violates safety, whereas every swap sends \(H_1\) to a different coordinate hyperplane. There is no terminal child. If \(\pi_v(1)\ne1\), a flip cannot restore that index. Only the swap with \(j=\pi_v^{-1}(1)\), and its formal inverse, can terminate. Thus every possible terminal parent has at most two terminal children.

There is no one-letter progressing terminal word. Any checkpoint tree of positive growth therefore has a nonroot internal vertex of maximal depth, all of whose children are terminal. Its degree is at least two even under the source’s undirected-leaf convention. It follows that every such tree satisfies \[\alpha N\le2,\qquad
 \alpha\le\frac1{(h+1)M}\longrightarrow0.\] The bound is attained by taking swaps at the root and the two inverse-image swap symbols at each child. Thus the problem is not nonexistence of individual trees; their best growth tends to zero with fixed visible and epsilon parameters.

## Counterevidence, actual impact, and repair boundary

The same example works without hyperplane inversions. Subdivide every coordinate interval at \(1/2\) and use \(H=\{z_1=1/4\}\). It remains the only visible hyperplane. A signed permutation stabilizing a quarter-level hyperplane preserves its sides. A terminal image at level \(3/4\) would force first orbit coordinate one, contrary to safety; an image in another coordinate crosses \(H\). The preceding two-child bound is unchanged.

The example has a global fixed center and its corner is not a displacement minimizer. Neither exclusion appears in Lemma 4.8. Its ambient dimension also grows. It does not refute Theorem 4.16 under that theorem’s additional hypotheses, or the final fixed-dimension result. Both the epsilon threshold and alpha must be uniform for the family argument; allowing an action-dependent threshold can also protect the displayed formula on a shrinking domain.

The exact failed step on p. 23 is applying the visible crossing bound to the translates \(uH\), which need not be visible. Here \(t_jH_1=H_j\) already demonstrates this. The weak bound \(1/N\) cannot supply the later sufficient condition \(\lambda>N^{-d}\), \(0<d<1\).

A sufficient geometric repair uses a global crossing bound, such as ambient dimension, throughout the checkpoint lemma. Keep that ambient parameter fixed while the later induction decreases the visible-clique index. With the local corrections below, each relevant finite array entry can be made at least \(1/2\), giving growth \(\epsilon_0(1-\epsilon_1)/2>0\). A continuing path of length \(k\) gives \(k+1\) pairwise crossing translates, so continuing depth is at most \(n-1\). The final easy extension may add two edges; it must not be subjected to the continuing-tree bound. This gives a finite depth bound \(n+1\). It supports a revised uniform construction, but does not by itself verify every exact state bound or graph form printed later.

# P3. Major at the black-box statement: empty words

Section 2.1, p. 7, explicitly includes the empty word among every word’s prefixes. Section 3.1, p. 10, permits trivial paths, and Definition 3.1 accepts every path from the start. Thus every automaton accepts the empty word.

Definition 3.4(1), pp. 11–12, applied to the trivial start path requires \(x\ne x\). Definition 3.6, p. 13, similarly makes a starting progressing pair impossible by testing its empty prefix. The nontriviality conclusions in Lemmas 3.5 and 3.13 need corresponding restrictions.

This has a direct theorem-level witness, beyond notation. For any proposed family in Theorem 5.10, p. 30, take \(R=\{\varnothing\}\), the singleton containing the empty formal word. It meets every accepted language. Nevertheless, \[\langle S\mid\varnothing\rangle=F_S.\] For nonempty \(S\), its Cayley-tree action has no global fixed point. Taking the product with an \((n-1)\)-cube on which it acts trivially gives exactly dimension \(n\). The advertised arbitrary-presentation criterion is therefore false under the explicit conventions.

The repair is to use accepted positive-length words in the nontriviality criterion, require positive start paths, and omit the empty prefix only in conditions requiring movement of \(x\). The same-halfspace condition at an \(H\)-checkpoint may retain it. Positive random relator lengths make this convention repair compatible with the intended asymptotic argument. That counterevidence does not remove the actual counterexample to Theorem 5.10, so the final assessment is Major at that core statement. This differs from the bounded helper’s initial Minor proposal; the original proposal and the reason for the primary adjudication are preserved. P1 remains even after this repair, so the two findings are not duplicates.

# P4. Major: fixed remainders need endpoint control

## The missing reduced-model step

Theorem 1.3’s proof sketch, pp. 31–32, rewires states to prevent cancellation between blocks, retaining growth \(\lambda-1/q\). It then transfers the remainder of Theorem 1.2’s proof under the condition \(\lambda-1/q>0\). That proof, p. 29, uses a fixed word \(w\) of remainder length and the two languages \(\mathcal L_\Sigma^{s}w^{-1}\) and \(w\mathcal L_\Sigma^v\). Their seams are additional constraints.

Here is a finite witness satisfying more than merely positive abstract growth. Let \[\mathcal A=\{a,A,b,B,c,C,d,D\},\quad
 A=a^{-1},\ B=b^{-1},\ C=c^{-1},\ D=d^{-1},
 \quad m=2.\] There are \(N_m=8\cdot7=56\) reduced blocks. Put \(P=\{xa:x\in\mathcal A,\ x\ne A\}\) and \(E=P\sqcup P^{-1}\), so \(|E|=14\). Use one start edge labelled \(aa\), and fourteen states indexed by \(e\in E\). At state \(e\), put an \(f\)-edge to state \(f\) for every \(f\in E\setminus\{e^{-1}\}\). Every nonstart state has thirteen outgoing edges, so \(\lambda=1/6\) works. The original accepted block words are freely reduced. There are fifteen states, below the \(n=1\) bound \(N_m+1=57\), and \(\lambda-1/q=1/24>0\).

After rewiring, the reachable states are exactly the clones \((e,a)\), \(e\in P\). At each, the surviving labels are all seven members of \(P\), and each ends in \(a\). Induction gives \(7^{\ell-1}\) positive accepted words of block length \(\ell\), all ending in \(a\). For the permitted remainder \(w=a\), every \(R_1w^{-1}=R_1A\) has a terminal cancellation. At every length \(2\ell+1\), this fixed-remainder language contains zero freely reduced relators.

## Progression, limitations, and repair

After P3’s positive-word correction, this automaton is also progressing for an explicit action. Map the seven \(P\)-symbols of the block free group to a free basis of \(F_7\), map their inverses accordingly, and send the other 42 block symbols to the identity. Let \(X\) be the Cayley tree of \(F_7\). Declare every nonstart state \(e\) a checkpoint labelled by the visible midpoint \(H_{e^{-1}}\). For \(f\ne e^{-1}\), \(x\) and \(fx\) lie on the same side of \(H_{e^{-1}}\), whereas \(fH_{f^{-1}}\) is a distinct midpoint separating them. All relevant checkpoint tests reduce to these single edges. The start edge has the required separating witness. The identity vertex minimizes total displacement: fourteen nontrivial letters each move every vertex at least one edge, and equality is attained there.

This refutes the unrestricted fixed-remainder inference from the printed growth and rewiring assumptions. It does not say that \(q=8\) exceeds the theorem’s unspecified \(2k(1)\), or that every automaton the source might choose for this action fails. Indeed, its many fixed block letters permit a different fix-set construction, and other remainders may work. No counterexample to the final existential-\(k(n)\) theorem is asserted. The missing transfer is nevertheless part of the argument for a principal result, and requires a proof rather than an unchanged repetition of the plain model.

One sufficient repair retains the theorem’s quantifiers. For general \(q\), write \[N_m=q(q-1)^{m-1},\qquad \gamma=\lambda-\frac1q.\] A specified first or last letter occupies exactly \(N_m/q\) reduced blocks. Choose \(q>2/\lambda\). In addition to rewiring, impose the final-letter restriction on \(R_1\) and the first-letter restriction on \(R_2\) needed by the fixed remainder. Each costs at most \(N_m/q\) candidates at the affected block, leaving positive choices. Apply these restrictions at eventual productive states; the two paths still concatenate in the rewired automaton. Only fixed endpoint factors are lost.

The correct original-letter growth base is \[(\gamma N_m)^{1/m}
   =(q-1)\left(\frac{\gamma q}{q-1}\right)^{1/m}.\] Its exponent relative to \(q-1\) tends to one as \(m\to\infty\). For each fixed \(d>0\), choose \(m\) large enough to exceed the density threshold. The stronger lower bound on \(q\) depends only on the dimension-dependent \(\lambda\), while \(m\) may depend on \(d\). This repairs this step without changing the intended order of quantifiers.

# Local corrections with bounded impact

## P5. Minor: prefix safety in easy extensions

Lemma 4.3, pp. 17–18, and Corollary 4.5, pp. 18–19, need safety for every existing prefix, not just the endpoint. On the cubical line, let \(x=0\), \(a(t)=t+1\), \(H=\{1/2\}\), \(w=aA\), and \(s=A\). Then \(wx=0\) is in the carrier on the \(x\)-side, and \(wsx=-1\) is on that side outside the carrier, so \(s\in\mathcal A_w(H)\). But the prefix \(a\) crosses \(H\), and \((ws,H)\) is not progressing.

For the full corollary, let \(a,b\) both translate by one. Take root edges \(a,b\), followed respectively by \(A,B\). Every nonleaf has at least one outgoing edge, giving \(\lambda=1/4\) over four letters. At each terminal return to zero, \(|\mathcal A_w(H)|\ge2\). No extension can remove the earlier crossing, so the claimed general extension conclusion fails.

The actual continuing trees in the main construction use the crossing-parallel sets and do preserve prefix safety. State that hypothesis explicitly. The two-step branch must test \(|\mathcal B(H)|\), as in the corollary’s hypothesis, rather than \(|\mathcal B_w(H)|\), which can be empty there. The end of Lemma 4.3(3) must refer to \((wst,H)\), not \((ws,H)\), and the indexed parallel set is required in item (2). With these corrections the nearest-separator witnesses are valid. This is Minor because the intended application has the missing safety property; the general lemma’s printed scope is too broad.

## P6. Minor: rooted leaves and mixed-depth lifts

The leaf convention on p. 10 uses undirected degree one. A one-edge \(H\)-tree then has no nonleaf vertices, so Definition 3.8’s branching requirement is vacuous at its root. Lemma 3.11, pp. 14–15, uses that root’s branching.

After the positive-start convention is repaired, let \(a\) translate the line by one, and take \(H_\pm=\{\pm1/2\}\). Use start edges \(a,A\) with respective witnesses \(H_-,H_+\). Give \(T_{H_-}\) one \(a\)-edge and \(T_{H_+}\) one \(A\)-edge, each retaining its own hyperplane label. They satisfy the printed hyperplane progression conditions and are vacuously \(1\)-large under the undirected convention. Their realization accepts only \(a^L,A^L\) at positive length \(L\), and its long paths have one continuation instead of two. This is a conditional counterexample to the realization lemma after P3’s repair, not an example satisfying the unrepaired contradictory start definition.

Use rooted terminal vertices as leaves and require branching at every \(H\)-root and internal vertex. The Section 4 constructions have additional structure; the example does not establish their failure.

A separate scope problem occurs in Lemma 4.7, pp. 19–20. Let the prefix-closed vertex words be \[\{\varnothing,a,c,aa,ab,ac,aac\}.\] The subtree \(T_0\) on nonleaves is the path \(\{\varnothing,a,aa\}\), with the rooted-lift property. At its terminal vertex \(aa\), the outgoing set \(\{c\}\) is contained in all suffix-vertex outgoing sets, namely \(\{a,c\},\{a,b,c\},\{c\}\). Any root condition from the undirected convention is tautological. Nevertheless the edge from \(a\) to \(ab\), labelled \(b\), has no rooted lift. Require equal leaf depths, as in the intended continuing trees, or test every old vertex receiving a terminal edge. These hypotheses preserve the use made in the main construction, so no additional Major is inferred.

## P7. Minor: the finite array and parameter domains

Remark 4.9, p. 20, claims total-index monotonicity for the triangular array. At \(\epsilon_0=1/100\), its exact recursion gives \[D_4(0)=\frac{94119003}{100000000}
       >D_2(1)=\frac{9303}{10000},
 \qquad 4+0>2+1.\] The stronger monotonicity claim is false. Every defined entry is, however, a polynomial with value one at zero. For a fixed dimension, choose \(\epsilon(n)\) so every entry in the finite relevant window is at least \(1/2\). This suffices for the lower bounds in P2’s repaired induction without the asserted total ordering. Lemma 4.10’s endpoint \(j=i\) is outside the defined domain \(0\le j<i\). Use proper suffixes in the filtration formula and lower inequalities in place of the ensuing counting equalities.

On p. 26, the definition of \(\epsilon_0\) uses \(\epsilon_0\) inside the set whose supremum defines it, rather than the dummy variable \(x\). Writing \(z=\min\{1/5,\epsilon(n)\}\), choose \(\epsilon_1=(1+(1-4z)^n)/2\). If the defining set is nonempty, its supremum must be \(z\), where its displayed inequality is false. If it is empty it has no positive supremum in the specified range. Thus the literal prescription fails.

Even correcting the dummy variable leaves a domain issue. The resulting choice is \(\min\{z,(1-\epsilon_1^{1/n})/4\}\), which can exceed \(\epsilon_1\); take \(n=2,\epsilon_1=z/2\). Some alpha evaluations then lie outside the declared range \(\epsilon_1\ge\epsilon_0\). A sufficient replacement is \[\epsilon_0=\min\left\{\frac15,\epsilon(n),\frac{\epsilon_1}{2},
                      \frac{1-\epsilon_1^{1/n}}8\right\},
 \quad \lambda=1-2\epsilon_0,\quad \rho=1-4\epsilon_0,
 \quad \beta_i=\frac{\epsilon_1}{\rho^{n-i}}.\] Then \(\beta_i\in[\epsilon_1,1)\) and \(\beta_i\ge2\epsilon_0\). Keep ambient \(n\) in alpha and take \[\gamma(1)=1-\beta_1,\qquad
 \gamma(i)=\min\{\epsilon_0,\alpha(\epsilon_0,\beta_i,n),
                         \rho\gamma(i-1)\}.\] All are positive. The restriction step has the correct normalization \(\beta_i/\rho=\beta_{i-1}\). Lemma 4.15 also needs \(\lambda>1/2\), as this choice provides. These numerical repairs do not resolve P2 by themselves; they are recorded separately from its uniform geometric defect.

## P8. Minor: inversion of plain blocks

Definition 5.4, p. 27, asserts fixed-point-free inversion on all plain words of every length \(m>0\). For \(m=2r\), exactly \(q^r\) words are fixed: they are \(uu^{-1}\), \(|u|=r\). The smallest example is \(aA\). Such words cannot be partitioned into a set and its disjoint inverse image. For odd \(m\), a fixed word would have a central letter equal to its formal inverse, which is impossible.

Choose sufficiently large odd \(m\). This preserves every needed growth inequality and the finite residue-class argument. Indeed each original generator is represented by \(a(bB)^{(m-1)/2}\), so in the plain model \(H_m=G\) for odd \(m\). A nonempty reduced word is never fixed by formal inversion, for either parity. This correction does not change the main asymptotic conclusion or supply a counterexample to it.

## P9. Minor: dimension of the median fixed-set realization

The proof of Lemma 1.7, pp. 28–29, says the median realization \(X_Y\) is \(n\)-dimensional because \(X\) is. Take \(K=\mathbb Z/2\) reflecting an interval, with \(G=K\) and trivial quotient \(N\). After cubical subdivision the fixed vertices consist only of its midpoint. Thus \(\dim X_Y=0<1=\dim X\), under the stated extension hypotheses.

The preceding source paragraph correctly gives \(\dim X_Y\le n\), and that is sufficient. If \(\mathrm{FW}_n\) is defined using exactly dimension \(n\), pad a lower-dimensional action by a trivially acted-on cube. A fixed point in \(X_Y\) provides a finite orbit in its stabilized cube, hence a finite \(G\)-orbit in \(X\), and the CAT(0) fixed-point argument applies. The quotient and finite-index permanence arguments also check directly. No counterexample to extension closure was found.

# Applications, proof dependencies, and evidence still needed

## Rips normality is not yet verified under the full hypotheses

On p. 33, the displayed relations \(a_i b_j a_i^{-1}=B_{ij}\) prove only \(a_iKa_i^{-1}\subseteq K\). The inverse inclusion is not supplied by inverting this statement. For the general inference, the affine maps \(a(t)=2t\), \(b(t)=t+1\) satisfy \(aba^{-1}=b^2\), while \(a^{-1}ba\) translates by \(1/2\) and is outside \(\langle b\rangle\).

This affine example does not satisfy the manuscript’s full fixed-point and small-cancellation hypotheses. It is therefore evidence of an insufficient algebraic justification, not a certified counterexample to Corollary 1.6. The status of normality under the complete construction remains NeedsEvidence.

An explicit algebraic repair reserves distinct words for both conjugation signs \(a_i^{\pm1}b_j a_i^{\mp1}\). Then \(K\) is normal, the quotient is \(G\), and \(K\) is a quotient of the retained \(\mathrm{FW}_n\) presentation. Removing this larger but fixed finite number of random relators preserves density. To obtain the stipulated base \(C'(1/(2p))\) from Corollary 1.4, choose \(d<1/(4p)\); the earlier \(d<1/(2p)\) alone does not furnish that stronger condition. The existential application allows the smaller choice.

The p. 34 assertion that a new piece contains one old contiguous piece of length \(|w|-2C\) also requires a precise marker argument. Deleting short prefixes alone can leave multiple fragments. The actual distinction between the \(a\)-markers and long \(b\)-words, together with normalized distinct input relators, gives counterevidence to a generic fragmentation objection. No complete counterexample covering the actual cyclic, inverse, and symmetrized relator conditions was found. The revised two-sign construction needs this piece check explicitly; it is not silently certified here.

## The \(\mathrm{FW}_\infty\) construction and external imports

The ray construction on pp. 32–33 is locally finite: each vertex and edge meets only finitely many finite presentations and additional cells. Every adjacent group is meant to generate the same quotient, so a single resulting group inherits \(\mathrm{FW}_i\) for every \(i\). This does not exchange a probability limit with an infinite intersection.

The attachment \(s t^{-1}Qt\) gives the transported relation \(s=Q^{-1}\), while the text writes \(Q\). Inverting the selected word preserves the generation and quotient argument. At the first ray vertex the nonexistent left attachment must be omitted.

A sufficient way to make the piece estimates transparent is to choose the available stronger base \(C'(1/12)\) presentations and lengths \(L_i>36\). Use each reserved word once. A distinct new-cell piece cannot traverse the entire unique connector marker in both cells; otherwise it identifies the same occurrence. The remaining overlap has at most one old-word piece and at most three connector letters. When a long overlap exists, the common vertex alphabet has the same base length, and \[|{\rm piece}|<L_i/12+3<(L_i+3)/6.\] Other overlaps are bounded connector segments. Old/new overlaps obey the old perimeter bound. This describes a tightened construction, not an assertion that every original choice already has the needed slack.

Asphericity supplies geometric dimension at most two. Exact dimension additionally needs nontriviality and a lower-bound argument; it does not follow from asphericity alone. Small-cancellation normal-form reasoning and the nontrivial \(\mathrm{FW}_1\) condition are natural ways to supply these steps, with the appropriate dimension theorem stated explicitly. No full counterexample to Theorem 1.5 is established.

For a bounded citation check, the actual three-page Jankiewicz–Wise arXiv:2006.05297v1 [JW], 8 June 2020, was obtained, read, and visually inspected. Its p. 2 identifies Wise’s Theorem 10.5 and Corollary 10.7 as the local-finiteness and free-cubulation imports. This verifies the citation route, not those external theorems in full. The original Wise paper, the median-realization literature, and the general small-cancellation theorems were not independently reproved in this initial review.

## Positive checks and limits of the black-box repair

Checkpoint separation has a valid geometric mechanism after the stated conventions are fixed. If the new separator is parallel to the old one, visibility places the relevant point adjacent to the old hyperplane. The opposite adjacent vertex lies on the same side of the new separator. The old opposite halfspace is connected and disjoint from the new hyperplane, which gives the claimed continuing separation.
