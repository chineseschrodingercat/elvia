Abstract: The paper derives a continuous-time limit of aligned minimizing movements for inner-product Gromov–Wasserstein geometry and relates an intrinsic distance to a degenerate Riemannian action. The distinction between ambient and intrinsic distance, covariance nondegeneracy, horizontal velocities and the possible reflected endpoint is essential. One Major issue concerns the entropy numerical validation: the displayed functional and gradient are inconsistent, and the derivative used in the supplied snapshot can lie outside the domain of the advertised inverse. Six Minor issues concern operator scope, an atomic spectral boundary case, curve conventions, mesh weights, a quantitative time range and plotting code. These findings do not constitute a general counterexample to the conditional continuous-flow or dynamic-representation results.

# Mathematical structure and supported scope

The paper uses the uncentered second moment \(\Sigma_\mu=\int xx^T\,d\mu(x)\), despite calling it covariance. Nonzero means therefore do not invalidate the stated identities. Writing \(C_\pi=\int xy^T\,d\pi\), the quadratic expansion gives \[\mathop{\mathrm{IGW}}(\mu,\nu)^2
 =\|\Sigma_\mu\|_F^2+\|\Sigma_\nu\|_F^2
       -2\max_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\|C_\pi\|_F^2.\] The auxiliary quadratic minimization in \(A\) gives \(A=C_\pi/2\). The optimal-transport reduction and the PSD alignment by an orthogonal matrix are consistent with this normalization. The existence of a Monge map uses the source density and invertibility assumptions; it is not an unrestricted assertion for arbitrary discrete measures.

For Theorem 4.1, the hypotheses include orthogonal invariance, a lower-bounded weakly lower semicontinuous regular functional with absolutely continuous effective domain, the specified convexity along aligned generalized geodesics, and the subdifferential/first-variation assumption. The argument controls discrete energy and the smallest second-moment eigenvalue on a small neighborhood. It obtains velocity bounds, an IGW Cauchy estimate of order \(\sqrt{\tau}\), a subsequence converging uniformly in \(W_2\), and a limiting continuity equation. The passage of the nonlocal moment term uses uniform moment bounds and a Young-inequality argument; these steps were read through the auxiliary proofs. The cited subgradient-closure and optimal-transport results are used conditionally here, not independently reproved.

The mobility operator is \[\mathcal L_{A,\mu}v(x)=2Av(x)+2\int yv(y)^T\,d\mu(y)\,x.\] For \(A=\Sigma_\mu\), its quadratic form equals the action tensor and has a rotational nullspace. In particular, \(\mathcal L_{\Sigma_\mu,\mu}(Sx)=0\) for every skew matrix \(S\). The horizontal space \[\mathcal I_\mu=\{v\in L^2(\mu;\mathbb R^d):
                  \mathbb E[v(X)X^T]\ \hbox{is symmetric}\}\] is consequently substantive, rather than a notational convenience.

Theorem 5.2 concerns the intrinsic length distance \(d_{\mathop{\mathrm{IGW}}}\), not the original ambient discrepancy. It assumes the existence of a minimizing curve with a uniform positive lower bound on \(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{\rho_t})\). Its endpoint is either \(\mu_1\) or a fixed reflection of \(\mu_1\). The reflection alternative is justified by the two components of the orthogonal group; dropping it would change the theorem. The proof’s smoothing, horizontal alignment, continuous rotation correction, action invariance and time-rescaled concatenation were followed. The full quotient tangent theory is not thereby settled.

The entropy extension explicitly uses modified multiplicative generalized geodesics and a local lower bound. The manuscript does not hide the absence of a global entropy lower bound. Likewise, the neural flow-matching examples minimize a discretized action with an MMD penalty; a small penalty value is not an exact endpoint constraint or a certificate of a global minimizing curve.

# Problems requiring revision

Problem 1 (Major: entropy functional, derivative and inverse-domain consistency).  Section 6.1, p. 39, defines negative differential entropy \(H(\mu)=\int\rho\log\rho\), but prints the surrogate \[\widetilde H_+(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
 =\frac1{2n}\sum_i\log\bigl(\epsilon+\|x_i-x_{N(i)}\|^2\bigr),\] and its positive outgoing nearest-neighbor field. Here \(N(i)\) is the nearest neighbor other than \(i\). At \(\epsilon=0\), scaling every point by \(a\) changes this surrogate by \(+\log a\), whereas \(H(a_\#\mu)-H(\mu)=-d\log a\). Thus the printed sign is inconsistent with the stated entropy.

The plotting snapshot instead uses a negative sum of logarithms and a negative outgoing field. This is counterevidence to any assertion that the displayed numerical trajectories necessarily used the printed positive sign. However, even with the sign repaired, the outgoing field is not the derivative of the stated finite-particle functional. On a region with unique nearest neighbors, set \[q_i=\frac{x_i-x_{N(i)}}{\epsilon+\|x_i-x_{N(i)}\|^2}.\] For the negative mean surrogate, its empirical Wasserstein gradient is \[g_i=n\,\partial_{x_i}\widetilde H_-
       =-q_i+\sum_{k:N(k)=i}q_k.\] The incoming-neighbor terms cannot be omitted when differentiating the same particle functional.

This omission also matters to the inverse mobility formula. Take \[\epsilon=\tfrac15,\qquad x_1=(1,0),\quad x_2=(0,2),\quad x_3=(0,3).\] The nearest-neighbor indices are \(2,3,2\), all strict. For the snapshot field \(g_i^{\rm out}=-q_i\), \[\sum_i g_i^{\rm out}=\binom{-5/26}{5/13},\qquad
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&0\\0&13/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \frac13\sum_i g_i^{\rm out}x_i^T
 =\begin{pmatrix}-5/78&0\\5/39&-5/18\end{pmatrix}.\] The nonzero sum contradicts the translation invariance of the proposed particle functional; the nonsymmetric moment shows \(g^{\rm out}\notin\mathcal I_\mu\). Applying the printed principal inverse formula to \(w=-g^{\rm out}\) gives residuals \[(\mathcal L v-w)(x_1)=\binom0{5/182},\quad
 (\mathcal L v-w)(x_2)=\binom{-5/91}0,\quad
 (\mathcal L v-w)(x_3)=\binom{-15/182}0.\] These are exact rational values, despite the nonsingular second moment. For comparison, the correct negative-surrogate gradient is \[g_1=\binom{-5/26}{5/13},\quad
 g_2=\binom{5/26}{50/39},\quad
 g_3=\binom0{-5/3};\] its sum is zero, its moment is symmetric, and the same inverse formula has zero residual.

The entropy experiment therefore needs a coherent functional, normalization, gradient and inverse-domain justification. A different consistent score estimator is possible, but it must be identified and justified as such. The affected experiment should be revisited after that choice. This Major assessment concerns the numerical validation and its derivation; it does not disprove the continuous entropy theory or certify which commit generated a published figure.

Problem 2 (Minor: the inverse proposition needs compatible right-hand sides).  Proposition 10.1, pp. 54–55, quantifies over arbitrary \(w\in L^2(\mu;\mathbb R^d)\) and asserts a unique solution, while its derivation uses \(v\in\mathcal I_\mu\). For \(A=\Sigma_\mu=I_2\) and \(w(x)=Sx\), \(S\ne0\) skew, its formula gives \(v=Sx/4\), but \(\mathcal L v=0\ne w\). Full-space uniqueness also fails because of the rotational kernel.

Merely imposing \(w\in\mathcal I_\mu\) is insufficient for general \(A\). Take a centered Gaussian with \[A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\quad\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,3),
 \quad w(x)=Wx,\quad W=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/3\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.\] Then \(W\Sigma\) is symmetric, but the proof’s Sylvester equation gives \[AB+B\Sigma=\tfrac12W\Sigma,\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/8\\1/6&0\end{pmatrix}.\] This \(B\) is not symmetric. The proposed solution has \(V=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1/24\\1/6&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and \[2AV+2\Sigma V^T-W
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&1/12\\-1/12&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0.\] The correct general qualification is \(w\in\mathcal L_{A,\mu}(\mathcal I_\mu)\), with uniqueness within \(\mathcal I_\mu\); equivalently the Sylvester solution must satisfy the required symmetry. In the main case \(A=\Sigma_\mu\), restricting \(w\) to \(\mathcal I_\mu\) gives the intended inverse.

Section 5.3 should also retain orthogonal invariance when introducing a formal gradient for \(F\). A noninvariant functional can vary along \(Sx\), although \(g_\mu(u,Sx)=0\) for every \(u\), so no such gradient represents its differential. Main Assumption 1 already supplies this invariance. The finding is thus a correction of scope, not a refutation of the compatible main flow.

Problem 3 (Minor: a spectral branch may have zero multiplicity).  Proposition 10.2, pp. 55–56, claims \(\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(\mathcal L_{\Sigma_\mu,\mu}|_{\mathcal I_\mu})
 =2(\Lambda_\mu\cup\Gamma_\mu)\) under nonsingularity of \(\Sigma_\mu\). For \(d=1\), \(\mu=\delta_1\), the uncentered second moment is 1, so the assumption holds. But \(L^2(\delta_1)=\mathbb R\), and direct evaluation gives \(\mathcal L v=4v\). Its spectrum is \(\{4\}\), whereas the displayed set is \(\{2,4\}\).

The \(2\Lambda_\mu\) branch requires nonzero fields orthogonal to the coordinate functions. That complement can be empty for minimally supported atomic measures. The proposition should qualify the branches that occur, or add a sufficient non-atomicity condition. The absolutely continuous setting of the main theorem is unaffected by this example.

Problem 4 (Minor: metric derivative and curve-equivalence conventions).  On p. 31 the metric derivative uses denominator \(h\) in a two-sided limit. The curve \(\rho_t=N(0,1+t)\), with \(1+t\) denoting variance, satisfies \(\mathop{\mathrm{IGW}}(\rho_{t+h},\rho_t)=|h|\). The displayed quotient has limits \(1\) and \(-1\). Use \(|h|\), or explicitly take \(h\downarrow0\).

The definition before Lemma 5.3, p. 35, allows any nondecreasing \(h:[0,1]\to[0,1]\). Let \(\alpha_t=N(0,1)\), \(\beta_t=N(0,1+t)\), and \(h\equiv0\). They meet that definition, but their lengths are 0 and 1. Hence the length equality in part (2) of the lemma is not valid for the printed definition. Require an endpoint-preserving continuous surjective nondecreasing reparametrization, as needed to traverse the whole curve.

There are related local scaling slips in (54), p. 65: from \(\mathop{\mathrm{IGW}}(\gamma_i,\gamma_{i-1})^2=\tau^2g_i+R_i\), the sum is \[\tau\sum_i g_i
   =\tau^{-1}\sum_i\mathop{\mathrm{IGW}}(\gamma_i,\gamma_{i-1})^2
      -\tau^{-1}\sum_i R_i
   \le L^2+O(\tau)\] when \(\mathop{\mathrm{IGW}}(\gamma_i,\gamma_{i-1})\le L\tau\) and \(R_i=O(\tau^3)\). The printed remainder and final unsquared length should be corrected accordingly. These corrections are consistent with the surrounding intended argument; no counterexample to the dynamic formula is inferred.

Problem 5 (Minor: an interpolation weight belongs to the other grid).  In (26), p. 24, interpolation in the \(\eta\)-grid uses \((1-\ell_\eta(s))\) and \(\ell_\tau(s)\). For \(\tau=1/2\), \(\eta=1/4\), \(s=1/8\), these coefficients sum to \(3/4\), rather than 1. The corresponding expansion on p. 25 repeats the mismatch. The proof on p. 26 uses the intended \(\ell_\eta\). Replace the second weight and its occurrences in the expansion by \(\ell_\eta(s)\); this is a local correction to the two-grid estimate.

Problem 6 (Minor: the quoted time bound does not match its derivation).  Put \(c=\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{\rho_0})\) and \(E=F(\rho_0)-F^*>0\). Theorem 4.1, p. 14, permits \[\delta<\frac{(1-1/\sqrt2)c}{2^{5/4}E}.\] Proposition 4.1, p. 16, instead gives \(\delta\le\bar\delta^2/(2E)\), with \(\bar\delta=(1-1/\sqrt2)c/2^{1/4}\), hence \[\delta\le\frac{(1-1/\sqrt2)^2c^2}{2^{3/2}E}.\] At \(c=E=1\), the theorem’s upper endpoint is about 4.06 times the derived one. Use the derived bound or supply an additional argument giving the quantitative estimates on the larger interval. Section 10.3’s concatenated global-existence discussion is distinct from this explicit uniform range. This is a proof-range mismatch, not a demonstrated nonexistence result.

Problem 7 (Minor: the pinned plotting routine mixes trajectory and field data).  In `Plots.ipynb`, the flow-matching plotting function evaluates sample velocities at `source_samples` for every time, although the nonlocal term requires samples from the current distribution. The notebook also computes `global_velocities` for the IGW model, subsequently loads the Wasserstein model, and draws its contours/arrows without recomputing those grid velocities.

Evaluate sample velocities on the current trajectory and pass locally computed model-specific grid fields to the plotting routine. This is established by static reading of the supplied snapshot. It does not prove that the published figures were generated by this exact code, and no author code was run.

# Assessment and limits

The useful contribution is the explicit aligned-flow construction and the link between the nonlocal mobility and an intrinsic action. Its hypotheses and its reflection alternative should remain visible. The established numerical inconsistency in Problem 1 warrants Major revision of that validation. The remaining problems are individually calibrated as Minor because their demonstrated effect is a local scope, convention, quantitative statement or snapshot correction; they do not presently negate the main conditional architecture.

A candidate objection to the Gronwall lemma was rejected after actual image inspection: pp. 27 and 60 contain absolute values on \(b(t)\), which the text extraction omitted. The superseded native calculation is retained in the technical records, and the current check explicitly marks it rejected. No defect is counted on that basis.

The qualitative plots do not establish a universal speed comparison with Wasserstein flow. For example, at an isotropic moment \(\Sigma=cI\), the potential-gradient velocity scales as \(x/(4c)\), so its relative time scale depends on \(c\). The paper’s observed damping and its open convergence-rate question should be interpreted accordingly. External analytical inputs, exact figure-generating provenance and the journal version remain outside the verified scope. Scientific adjudication is pending; this is an initial technical review, not independent acceptance.
