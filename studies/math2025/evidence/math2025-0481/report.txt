Abstract: The review checks the small-transition covering argument, its product supermartingale, the selection bound used to close the exponential estimate, and the reversible-kernel estimate used later in the partition argument. No substantive failure is established in these inspected derivations. Two incorrectly printed auxiliary formulas are clarified; the corresponding applications use bounds that remain valid after the corrections. This conclusion concerns the acquired author preprint and is not a certification of the complete graph-covering proof or of the journal version.

# Quantifiers and the covering estimate

For a finite Markov chain with transition probabilities \(\varphi\), let \(W\subset V\), \(|V|=n\), \(|W|=m>\beta n\), and \(M\leq Cm\). Theorem 1.2 assumes that the probabilities of transitions into a different state in \(W\) are uniformly below a sufficiently small \(\delta(C,\beta)\). It does not assume reversibility and does not bound self-loop probabilities. Its conclusion is an exponentially small upper bound for covering \(W\) during the specified time interval. Constants depend on \(C,\beta\); the paper explicitly works for sufficiently large \(n\) (p. 4). Thus very small graphs or unrounded noninteger times do not provide counterexamples to the intended asymptotic statement.

Write, as on p. 5, \[H_v(t)=\prod_{i=0}^t(1-\varphi(X_i,v)),\qquad
 r_v=\min\{t:H_v(t)<\lambda/L\},\qquad
 \lambda=1-\max_{u\ne v,\ v\in W}\varphi(u,v),\] with \(L=e^K\), \(p=L^{-1}\), and \(\lambda\geq1/2\) in the applications. Before \(v\) is hit, the excluded diagonal transition cannot enter a nonzero martingale contribution. Conditional survival over the next step has probability \(1-\varphi(X_i,v)\), which cancels its reciprocal in \[\xi_s^v=\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{T_v>s\wedge r_v\}}}
                    {H_v(s\wedge r_v-1)}.\] Stopping preserves the martingale property. If the initial state belongs to \(W\), one may remove that single already visited state and adjust the fixed constants for large \(m\); this explains the initial-state convention on p. 5.

For \(\xi_s=\sum_{v\in W}\xi_s^v\), only one summand can be killed in a single step, while the total positive increment is bounded by the sum of the transition probabilities. The displayed estimate (9), \(|\xi_s-\xi_{s-1}|\leq L/\lambda^2\), therefore gives explicitly \[\mathbb P(\xi_M<m/2)\leq
 \exp\left(-\frac{m^2\lambda^4}{8ML^2}\right)
 \leq \exp\left(-\frac{m\lambda^4}{8CL^2}\right).\] This is an analytic bounded-increment argument, not an independence assumption about the visited vertices.

# Product supermartingale and selection bound

For \(I\subset W\), the process \(S_k^I\) of Lemma 3.1 (p. 6) is a product of the stopped reciprocal survival factors, with the common survival indicator. If \(J\) denotes its currently unstopped coordinates and \(a_v=\varphi(X_k,v)\), then \[1-\sum_{v\in J}a_v\leq\prod_{v\in J}(1-a_v).\] The left side is the actual one-step probability of avoiding \(J\). The right side cancels the new reciprocal factors, giving \(\mathbb E(S_{k+1}^I\mid\mathcal F_k)\leq S_k^I\). On \(Q_v=\{T_v>r_v\}\), threshold crossing and \(1-a_v\geq\lambda\) give \(H_v(r_v-1)<L^{-1}\). Nonnegativity and the stopped bound justify the limit inequality \(\mathbb ES_\infty^I\leq \mathbb ES_0^I=1\). Consequently, \[\mathbb P\left(\bigcap_{v\in I}Q_v\right)
 =\mathbb E\left[S_\infty^I\prod_{v\in I}H_v(r_v-1)\right]
 \leq p^{|I|}.\] In particular, the proof does not require the \(Q_v\) to be independent.

The sampling step in Lemma 3.2 (p. 7) is also quantitatively consistent. For the selected random weight \(\psi_z\), let \(\mu=\mathbb E\psi_z>8\delta\). The stated variance bound yields \[\mathbb P(\psi_z<\delta)
 \leq \frac{\delta\mu}{(\mu-\delta)^2}
 <\frac{8}{49}<\frac16.\] Together with the two Markov bounds and the large-\(m\) binomial bound, this leaves positive probability for the required selection. The entropy calculation on p. 8 gives a family \(\mathcal I\) of \(\varepsilon m\)-element sets with \(|\mathcal I|<(15\varepsilon)^{-\varepsilon m}\), where \(\varepsilon=\lambda p/5\); rounding is subject to the paper’s large-size convention. Its decisive combination with Lemma 3.1 is \[|\mathcal I|p^{\varepsilon m}
 <\left(\frac{p}{15\varepsilon}\right)^{\varepsilon m}
 =\left(\frac{1}{3\lambda}\right)^{\varepsilon m}
 \leq (2/3)^{\varepsilon m}.\] Thus the family size does not overwhelm the small probability. On the covering event, the surviving stopped contributions force enough \(Q_v^*\) events; the threshold on \(H_v\) supplies the accumulated transition mass required by Lemma 3.2. These are the two separate ingredients needed for Theorem 1.2.

The reduction in Corollary 1.4 is compatible with this scale: some induced block receives at most \((C/\gamma)|V_i|\) visits, while its target set has size greater than \(\gamma|V_i|\). Hence its time-to-target-size ratio is at most \(C/\gamma^2\), as used in the stated small-transition condition. The bounded number of blocks permits the concluding union bound.

# Auxiliary formulas and their actual impact

Two displayed formulas on p. 4 must not be used literally.

Equation reference: eq:correctkernel. First, detailed balance and Cauchy–Schwarz give \[
 \frac{\varphi^{t+s}(v,w)}{\pi_w}
 \leq
 \left(\frac{\varphi^{2t}(v,v)}{\pi_v}
             \frac{\varphi^{2s}(w,w)}{\pi_w}\right)^{1/2}.\] Lemma 2.1 instead prints \(\pi_v\) under its left-hand numerator. For a simple star with \(N\geq2\) leaves, take \(v\) a leaf, \(w\) the center, \(t=1\), and \(s=2\). Then \(\pi_v=1/(2N)\), \(\pi_w=1/2\), \(\varphi^3(v,w)=1\), \(\varphi^2(v,v)=1/N\), and \(\varphi^4(w,w)=1\). The printed inequality becomes \(2N\leq2\), whereas [eq:correctkernel] (#eq:correctkernel) gives equality \(2\leq2\). All of these chains are finite, connected, simple-graph random walks and reversible; \(N\) can be arbitrarily large.

This discrepancy does not invalidate the later bound (47). Indeed [eq:correctkernel] (#eq:correctkernel) implies \[\varphi^{t+s}(v,w)\leq
 \sqrt{\frac{d_w}{d_v}}\,
 \sqrt{\varphi^{2t}(v,v)\varphi^{2s}(w,w)}.\] For the vertices used there, \(1\leq d_v,d_w<\Delta\), so the ratio factor is at most \(\sqrt{\Delta}\). Summing and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives exactly the estimate needed on p. 13. A false printed normalization has therefore been distinguished from a failed downstream derivation.

Second, Theorem 2.3 prints \(\mathbb EX_s\leq \mathbb EX\) for a bounded supermartingale with limit \(X\). The correct direction is \(\mathbb EX\leq\mathbb EX_s\), by dominated convergence and decreasing expectations. The deterministic sequence \(X_s=1/(s+1)\) already distinguishes the two directions. Crucially, Lemma 3.1 on p. 6 actually uses the correct inequality \(\mathbb ES_\infty^I\leq\mathbb ES_0^I\).

These are formula-level clarifications with checked, surviving applications. Under the review’s substantive-error scope they are not promoted to separate defect findings or to a Major assessment. They also do not imply that every other proof step has been verified.

# Conclusion and limits

No substantive issue was established in the inspected covering argument and kernel application. The calculations explain why two misleading auxiliary displays do not by themselves damage the core result. The full proof of Theorem 1.1 also needs the high-degree reduction and partition construction; this review has not certified every step of those arguments. The tree, expander, and appendix results likewise remain outside the fully checked scope. No claim is made about whether the journal version already corrects the displays.
