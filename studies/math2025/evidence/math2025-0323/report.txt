Abstract: The proof of Theorem 4.4 identifies the positive cone of the Dixmier closure of an arbitrary ideal with the strongly invariant order ideal generated by its original positive elements. That identification is false, already for the non-self-adjoint principal ideal in the paper’s Example 3.5. The erroneous identity is used to establish the squeezing theorem on which the principal equivalence theorem depends. This supports a Major assessment of the written derivation. A replacement argument using moduli of all elements, rather than only the original positive elements, repairs this bridge under the preceding results. No false main theorem is claimed.

# Definitions that matter

An ideal in the paper is a two-sided ring ideal; it is not assumed closed, self-adjoint or, in the nonunital case, complex-linear. A Dixmier ideal is positively spanned, hereditary, and has a strongly invariant positive cone. The positive cone of an arbitrary ideal is \(I_+=I\cap A_+\), as explicitly defined on pp. 2 and 9.

Equation reference: eq:commutative. For positive elements, the relation \(a\lhd b\) is defined on p. 5 by finite decomposition into elements of the form \(x^*x\) and \(xx^*\), followed by domination by an integer multiple of \(b\). In a commutative \(C^*\)-algebra, \(x^*x=xx^*\); hence \[
 a\lhd b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\leq mb
 \quad\text{for some integer }m\geq1,\] apart from the harmless zero case. Definition 4.2, p. 14, defines \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)\) as the smallest Dixmier ideal containing the entire ideal \(I\), not merely its positive elements.

# A substantive problem in the squeezing argument

Problem 1 (The positive cone does not determine the Dixmier closure of an arbitrary ideal).  Assessment: Major.

## Exact claim and proof dependency

Equation reference: eq:false. In the proof of Theorem 4.4, p. 15, the first displayed formula after Let us now prove the second part of the statement asserts \[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)_+=\{a\in A_+:a\lhd b\text{ for some }b\in I_+\}.\] The next displayed formula applies powers to this identification to infer \[\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)^{1+\varepsilon}\subseteq\operatorname{int}_{\mathrm D}(I).\] Theorem 5.2, p. 17, begins by invoking this squeeze and uses it in the implications from semiprimeness to idempotency and from stabilization of powers to semiprimeness. This is a direct dependency in the principal argument.

## An ideal satisfying every stated hypothesis

Take the unital commutative \(C^*\)-algebra \(A=C([-1,1])\), with the usual supremum norm, and the continuous function \[f(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 t,&t\geq0,\\
 it,&t<0.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad I=fA.\] This is exactly the principal ideal used in Example 3.5, p. 10. It is an additive, complex-linear, two-sided ideal; in particular it satisfies the weaker ring-ideal convention in Theorem 4.4. Both branches tend to zero, so \(f\) is continuous at zero. No self-adjointness or closedness assumption is imposed in the theorem.

Set \[J=\{g\in A:g(t)=o(|t|)\text{ as }t\to0\},\qquad
 K=\{g\in A:|g(t)|\leq C|t|\text{ for some }C\geq0\}.\] Both are two-sided ideals: sums preserve the indicated bounds, and multiplication by a continuous function preserves them because that function is bounded. Both are positively spanned, since the positive and negative parts of the real and imaginary parts obey the same bound. Their positive cones are hereditary, and strong invariance is automatic in the commutative algebra. Thus \(J\) and \(K\) are Dixmier ideals.

## The original positive cone

Equation reference: eq:positive. Let \(g\in I_+\), so \(g=fh\) for a continuous function \(h\) and \(g\geq0\). For \(t>0\), \[h(t)=g(t)/t\in[0,\infty).\] For \(t<0\), \[h(t)=g(t)/(it)=i\,g(t)/|t|\in i[0,\infty).\] Continuity at zero forces \(h(0)\) to belong to both closed rays, whose intersection is \(\{0\}\). Consequently \(g(t)/|t|\to0\). Conversely, if \(g\in J_+\), the quotient \(g/f\) away from zero extends continuously by zero at zero, and hence \(g\in I_+\). We have proved \[
 I_+=J_+.\] Using [eq:commutative] (#eq:commutative) and heredity of \(J_+\), the right side of [eq:false] (#eq:false) is therefore exactly \(J_+\).

## The actual Dixmier closure

Equation reference: eq:closure. Since \(|f(t)|=|t|\), clearly \(I\subseteq K\); thus \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)\subseteq K\). Conversely, let \(L\) be any Dixmier ideal containing \(I\). Positive spanning gives a finite expression \(f=\sum_j c_j b_j\) with \(b_j\in L_+\). Pointwise, \[|f|\leq\sum_j|c_j|b_j.\] The right side lies in \(L_+\), so heredity gives \(|f|\in L_+\). Every element of \(K\) is a linear combination of positive functions dominated by a scalar multiple of \(|f|\); hence \(K\subseteq L\). It follows that \[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)=K.\]

Now take \(a(t)=|t|\). By [eq:closure] (#eq:closure), \(a\in\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)_+\). But \(a\notin J_+\), because \(a(t)/|t|=1\) away from zero. Thus \(a\) belongs to the left side of [eq:false] (#eq:false) and not to its right side. Equivalently, no \(b\in I_+\) can dominate \(a\) up to any fixed multiple: \(b(t)/|t|\to0\) makes such domination impossible. This is an exact function-space counterexample, not a finite numerical test.

## Alternative readings and surviving conclusions

The definition \(I_+=I\cap A_+\) rules out reading \(I_+\) as the set of moduli of arbitrary elements of \(I\). Indeed \(|f|\notin I\): the quotient \(|f|/f\) has one-sided values 1 and \(i\), respectively. Nor can self-adjointness be inserted as an unstated hypothesis, since the theorem explicitly concerns arbitrary ideals and is used to prove automatic self-adjointness later.

The example does not refute the squeezing theorem itself. In fact \(\operatorname{int}_{\mathrm D}(I)=J\): any Dixmier ideal inside \(I\) is spanned by positive elements belonging to \(I_+=J_+\), while \(J\subseteq I\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\), positive elements of \(K^{1+\varepsilon}\) are \(O(|t|^{1+\varepsilon})=o(|t|)\), so \(K^{1+\varepsilon}\subseteq J\) holds in this example. The defect is the claimed identification used in its proof.

# A replacement argument for the affected bridge

The following correction uses only the preceding power theory and comparison lemmas, and verifies the local repair in arbitrary \(C^*\)-algebras.

Let \(L\) be a Dixmier ideal. Theorem 3.9 and Proposition 3.10 imply \[x\in L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |x|\in L.\] Indeed, \(x\in L\) gives \(x^*x\in L^2\), and the positive square root of that element belongs to \((L^2)^{1/2}=L\). Conversely, \(|x|\in L\) gives \(x^*x=|x|^2\in L^2\), and Proposition 3.10 again gives \(x\in(L^2)^{1/2}=L\). This uses a Dixmier ideal \(L\), not the arbitrary ideal \(I\).

Equation reference: eq:correct. Consequently \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)_+\) is the strongly invariant order ideal generated by the collection \(\{|x|:x\in I\}\). By Proposition 3.6 and additivity, its correct description is \[
 \operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)_+
 =
 \left\{a\in A_+:
 a\lhd\sum_{j=1}^n|x_j|\text{ for some }n\geq1,\ x_j\in I
 \right\}.\] The finite sum is retained in this general statement.

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and put \(J=\operatorname{int}_{\mathrm D}(I)\). For \(a\in\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)_+\), choose \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) as in [eq:correct] (#eq:correct). Proposition 2.8 and Lemma 2.7 give \[a^{1+\varepsilon}
 \lhd\left(\sum_j|x_j|\right)^{1+\varepsilon}
 \lhd\sum_j|x_j|^{1+\varepsilon}.\] The first part of Theorem 4.4, proved before the erroneous formula using Lemma 4.3, gives \(|x_j|^{1+\varepsilon}\in J_+\). Because \(J_+\) is a strongly invariant order ideal, the last display implies \(a^{1+\varepsilon}\in J_+\). The positive-cone description of powers in Theorem 3.9 now yields \[\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)^{1+\varepsilon}\subseteq J.\] For \(0<\delta<1\), choosing \(\varepsilon=(1-\delta)^{-1}-1>0\) and taking the \((1-\delta)\)-power gives \(\operatorname{cl}_{\mathrm D}(I)\subseteq J^{1-\delta}\), as required.

This repairs the affected inference without strengthening Theorem 4.4’s hypotheses. It is conditional on the earlier comparison and power results, whose relevant uses were checked here. It does not replace an independent verification of every external polar-decomposition or Riesz-refinement result cited in those earlier proofs.

# Other checks and assessment

The comparison arguments correctly distinguish operator monotonicity for exponents at most one from comparison up to \(\lhd\) for larger exponents. The power-product proof in Theorem 3.9 first proves a restricted-exponent statement and then changes the base Dixmier ideal; it does not assert that \(t\mapsto t^s\) is operator monotone for all \(s\).

Theorem 5.2’s exponent squeezes, including the choices \(2/3\), \(n-1/2\), and \(1/8\), were checked with the corrected Theorem 4.4 available. The square-root-to-factorization implication retains the nonunital ring-ideal convention by multiplying elements with factors already in \(A\). The union and intersection arguments in Theorems 5.9 and 5.12 use Dixmier power calculus, rather than exchanging arbitrary ideal products with arbitrary intersections. The one-sided ideal argument in Corollary 5.10 uses \(x^*x\) on the left and \(xx^*\) on the right, as required. No further substantive error was established.

The supported assessment is Major for the false identity in a central proof bridge. The explicit repair and survival of the theorem’s conclusion are separate facts; neither changes what the written derivation asserts. No claim is made that the principal semiprime/idempotent equivalence, or the entire paper, is false.
