Abstract: The 45-page publisher-typeset online-first article of Du, Gárriz, and Quirós is examined. Explicit weak solutions contradict two parameter regimes of the stated pressure gradient estimate. Differentiating the pressure equation reveals an omitted source term in the wave-speed calculation; a concrete admissible reaction contradicts its universal derivative bound. A separate energy inequality rules out the static subsolution used in the hair-trigger proof. Further corrections concern one-dimensional scope, tail normalization, and a missing logarithmic phase in a comparison function. These results identify false auxiliary statements and gaps in major proof dependencies. They do not establish that the intended asymptotic theorem, formulated using the actual wave-speed derivative, is false after repair. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Substantive problems

Problem 1 (Major: the pressure-gradient estimate has false regimes).  Lemma 3.1, pp. 10–11, especially (3.2), asserts \[|v_r|\leq
 \begin{cases}
 C M^{(m-\alpha)/p}(t^{-1}+H)^{1/p},&p\geq2,\\
 C M^{(m-\alpha)/2}(t^{-1}+H)^{1/2},&1<p<2,
 \end{cases}\] where \(C\) depends only on the constant convection parameter \(\gamma\), \(m\), and \(p\). The pure-diffusion case is expressly included. Both a nonzero-convection large-time limit and a singular-exponent short-time scaling contradict this claim.

First take \(p=m=2\), \(h=0\), \(\gamma>0\), and \(M>0\). For \(z>0\) set \[F(z)=M(1-e^{-\gamma z/2}),\qquad F(z)=0\quad(z\leq0),
 \qquad u(r,t)=F(r+\gamma Mt-R).\] On the positive branch, \[(F^2)'+\gamma F^2=\gamma MF.\] Differentiating proves \(u_t=(u^2)_{rr}+\gamma(u^2)_r\). The density and diffusive flux are continuous at the free boundary, with zero flux there, so this is a bounded weak solution of the auxiliary convection equation. Its pressure is \(v=2u\) and \[v_r=\gamma M e^{-\gamma z/2}\qquad(z>0).\] Given an arbitrarily large \(T\), choose \(R=\gamma MT+1\); then the front lies at \(r=1\) at time \(T\). At a fixed positive distance behind that increasing front, \(|v_r|\) is a positive constant independent of \(T\). The printed upper bound is \(CM^{1/2}T^{-1/2}\). Since \(C\) cannot depend on \(R\), the estimates are incompatible. This example addresses the stated bounded-solution class; it is not compactly supported on the whole line.

There is also a compactly supported, even counterexample to the \(1<p<2\) time exponent, with zero convection. Take \(p=3/2\), \(m=3\), \(\alpha=2\), \(h=0\), and \[B(x,s)=\left(s^{-1/2}-\frac{|x|^3}{36s^2}\right)_+ .\] In the positive set its flux is \[|(B^3)_x|^{-1/2}(B^3)_x=-\frac{xB}{2s}.\] Direct differentiation gives \(B_s=(-xB/(2s))_x\); the flux vanishes at the interface. Thus \(B\) is a weak solution. For \(\lambda>0\) put \[u_\lambda(x,t)=B\left(\frac{x}{\lambda},
                 1+\frac{t}{\lambda^{3/2}}\right).\] The initial supremum is \(M=1\), the data are even and compactly supported, and \(v_\lambda=3u_\lambda\). At \(x=\lambda\), \(t=\lambda^{3/2}\), \[|(v_\lambda)_x|=\frac{1}{16\lambda},
 \qquad
 CM^{1/2}t^{-1/2}=C\lambda^{-3/4}.\] No fixed \(C\) can satisfy this as \(\lambda\downarrow0\). This is an exact scaling contradiction, not a numerical approximation.

The proof exposes the corresponding defects. The Young inequality after (3.16), p. 17, applied to the convection term containing \(+1\), leaves a term that does not vanish with the spatial cutoff. That remainder is absent from (3.17), p. 18. On p. 19 the assertion that one may always arrange \(c_2S^{1-p}(t^{-1}+H)\geq1\) is impossible for \(H=0\) and unbounded \(t\). Moreover the maximum of its first power and its \(2/p\) power cannot generally be replaced by the selected branch in (3.2).

A valid replacement must retain the convection contribution and correct the time/scaling dependence. Corollary 3.2, pp. 19–20, invokes this derivation and therefore needs its own corrected argument. The weaker conclusion of Theorem 2.6 is only a bound for each fixed \(\tau>0\) and \(r\geq\rho>0\); these examples do not disprove that weaker statement. Nor does a counterexample with \(p<2\) directly refute the main asymptotic theorem, which assumes \(p\geq2\). Nevertheless, the advertised central technical estimate and its proof require substantive repair.

Problem 2 (Major: wave-speed differentiation loses a source term).  Lemma 4.15, pp. 30–32, asserts \(c'(\gamma)\in(-m,0)\). The displayed integral formula on p. 31 and (4.14) on p. 32 omit the contribution from differentiating \(\gamma|\mathcal P|^{p-2}\).

Set \(A_0=m-\alpha\), \(Q=m/A_0\), \(W=-\mathcal P>0\), \(y=W^{p-1}\), and \(s(q)=(A_0q/m)^{1/A_0}\). The preceding equation on p. 30 is \[y'=\frac{c-y}{A_0q}+\gamma W^{p-2}
                  -\frac{h(s)}{sW}.\] For \(Y=\partial_\gamma y\), differentiation gives \[Y'-aY=\frac{c'}{A_0q}+W^{p-2},
 \quad
 a=-\frac1{A_0q}+
 \frac{\gamma(p-2)}{(p-1)W}
 +\frac{h(s)}{(p-1)sW^p}.\] With \(\Psi'=-a\Psi\) and the endpoint behavior claimed in the lemma, the necessary compatibility formula is \[c'(\gamma)=
 -A_0\,\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^Q\Psi W^{p-2}\,\mathrm dq}
                   {\displaystyle\int_0^Q\Psi q^{-1}\,\mathrm dq}.\] The printed numerator has \(\Psi\) alone. The two integrals in (4.14) likewise require the complete source \(c'/(A_0q)+W^{p-2}\). As printed, one of those expressions is negative and the other positive when \(c'<0\), so they cannot be equal. The corrected compatibility expression reduces to the printed speed formula when \(p=2\), but its universal \(-m\) bound does not persist for \(p>2\).

Here is a concrete counterexample to the joint differentiability-and-bound assertion. Take \(m=1\), \(p=3\), and, for \(a>0\), define \[h_a(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 a^3u^{3/2}(1-u)(4-3u),&0\leq u\leq1,\\
 -a^3(u-1),&u\geq1.
 \end{cases}\] This is \(C^1\), satisfies the monostable sign assumptions, and has \(h_a'(1)=-a^3\). At \(\gamma=0\) the finite profile is \[U(\xi)=\tanh^2(a\xi/2)\quad(\xi<0),\qquad
 U(\xi)=0\quad(\xi\geq0),\qquad c=a^2.\] Indeed \(U'=-a\sqrt U(1-U)\) and \(\phi(U)=a^2U(1-U)^2\) verify the wave equation exactly. Differentiate the trajectory equation (4.5) along the parameter branch whose regularity the lemma asserts. For \(Z=\partial_\gamma\phi\) this yields \[Z'=c'+a\sqrt U(1-U)
          +\frac{4-3U}{2(1-U)^2}Z .\] An integrating factor is \[\mu(U)=(1-U)^{3/2}
                  \exp\!\left(-\frac1{2(1-U)}\right).\] The asserted endpoint regularity implies vanishing boundary terms, and hence \[c'_a(0)=-a\,\frac{J}{I},\quad
 I=\int_0^1\mu(U)\,\mathrm dU,\quad
 J=\int_0^1\mu(U)\sqrt U(1-U)\,\mathrm dU.\] Using the whole interval for \(I\) and \([1/4,1/2]\) for \(J\), \[I\leq\frac25 e^{-1/2},\qquad
 J\geq\frac{e^{-1}}{32\sqrt2},\qquad
 \frac{J}{I}\geq\frac{5e^{-1/2}}{64\sqrt2}.\] Thus \(a=100\) forces \(c'_a(0)<-3\), contrary to \(c'(0)>-m=-1\). If the claimed regular parameter branch failed to exist, that would already violate the other part of the same lemma. The contradiction therefore does not presume the incorrect printed derivative formula.

The reaction scaling \(h\mapsto\lambda h\) provides a second check: \[c_\lambda(\gamma)=
 \lambda^{(p-1)/p}c_1(\gamma\lambda^{-1/p}),\qquad
 c'_\lambda(0)=\lambda^{(p-2)/p}c'_1(0).\] A nonzero negative derivative cannot have a universal lower bound \(-m\) for all \(\lambda\) when \(p>2\). The corrected formula can instead give a reaction-dependent bound involving \(\sup W^{p-2}\). The continuation estimate on p. 33 based on \(c(\gamma)\geq c(0)-m\gamma\) must also be revised.

This matters to the proof of the logarithmic shift, which uses parameter regularity and an inverse speed function. It does not by itself contradict the main asymptotic statement when \(c_\sharp\) is defined by the actual \(-c'(0)/c_*\). A corrected parameter analysis may still supply the local estimates that argument needs.

Problem 3 (Major: the hair-trigger proof uses an impossible static subsolution).  In Lemma 5.2, p. 34, the initial bump \(\underline u=\delta(1-|x|^\sigma)_+^\beta\) is claimed to satisfy \(-\Delta_p(\underline u^m)-\widehat h(\underline u)\leq0\) when \(\beta\) and \(\sigma\) are sufficiently large. The subsequent time-monotonicity argument depends on this assertion.

Take \(N=1\), \(p=m=2\), \(\delta=1/2\), so the Fujita exponent is \(q_F=4\). An explicit allowed reaction is \[\widehat h(u)=u^4\bigl[1-16(u-3/4)_+^2\bigr].\] It is \(C^1\), positive on \((0,1)\), negative on \((1,\infty)\), and \(\widehat h'(1)=-8\). It equals \(u^4\) on \([0,3/4]\). For every sufficiently regular choice of the proposed bump, put \(w=\underline u^2\). Then \(0\leq w\leq1/4\), \(w\not\equiv0\), and \(w\in H^1_0(-1,1)\). The alleged static-subsolution inequality would be \(-w''\leq w^2\). Testing by \(w\) gives \[\frac{\pi^2}{4}\int_{-1}^1w^2
 \leq\int_{-1}^1|w'|^2
 \leq\int_{-1}^1w^3
 \leq\frac14\int_{-1}^1w^2,\] an impossibility. Increasing \(\beta\) or \(\sigma\) cannot repair this fixed-support, fixed-amplitude construction.

The displayed \(C_2\geq C_3\) comparison overlooks the different powers of \(|x|\) in the positive and negative terms. An exact sample \(\sigma=4\), \(\beta=2\) gives \[-(\underline u^2)''(1/2)-\underline u(1/2)^4
 =\frac{122007938175}{68719476736}>0.\] The energy argument, rather than this one sample, rules out every proposed sufficiently regular large-exponent bump for the chosen parameters.

Lemma 5.1 only yields a limsup of the supremum. The invalid monotonicity step is what was intended to turn that information into the limit at the center and then into Theorem 2.2. An alternative hair-trigger argument is therefore required. The stated spreading conclusion may still be correct; this is a decisive failure of the present proof, not a counterexample to spreading itself.

# Scope, normalization, and comparison estimates

Problem 4 (Minor: Corollary 2.5(iii) needs a one-dimensional qualification).  The corollary on pp. 8–9 assumes \(p\geq2\) but not \(N\geq2\). For \(N=1\) its asserted region of uniform convergence to one is \(|x|\leq c_*t\), because the logarithmic retreat is multiplied by \(N-1\).

For any radial spreading solution covered by Theorem 2.4, that theorem gives \[u(c_*t,t)\longrightarrow U_{c_*}(-r_0)<1,\] since \(r_0\) is finite and the profile is strictly below one at every finite argument. Thus the two printed statements cannot both hold in dimension one. The Section 6.3 refinements do not impose an \(N\geq2\) restriction retroactively.

Restrict part (iii) to \(N\geq2\), or require an additional retreat \(w(t)\to\infty\) from the corrected front radius, with a nonempty comparison region. For \(N\geq2\), \((N-1)(c-c_\sharp)\log t\to\infty\) for \(c>c_\sharp\), which explains why the argument works. Parts (i)–(ii) are not refuted by this boundary case.

Problem 5 (Minor: a missing factor \(m\) changes the tail exponent).  Lemma 4.10(b), p. 28, and Lemma 4.14(b), p. 30, lose a factor \(1/m\) when converting the trajectory asymptotic to spatial or pressure-level coordinates.

For \(p\geq2\), (4.11) implies \[\phi(U)\sim C_{p,\gamma}(1-U)^{p-1},\qquad
 U'\sim-\frac{C_{p,\gamma}^{\alpha}}m(1-U).\] The exponential rate is therefore \(C_{p,\gamma}^{\alpha}/m\), not \(C_{p,\gamma}^{\alpha}\). Likewise, if \(Q=m/(m-\alpha)\), \[Q-q\sim m(1-U),\qquad
 \mathcal P(q;\gamma)\sim
 -\frac{C_{p,\gamma}^{\alpha}}m(Q-q).\] The later computation on p. 32 already uses the correctly normalized coefficient in the \(p>2\) case.

A concrete check is \(p=m=2\), \(\gamma=0\), \(h(u)=u(1-u)\): \[U(\xi)=1-e^{\xi/2}\quad(\xi<0),\qquad
 U(\xi)=0\quad(\xi\geq0),\qquad c_*=1.\] This is an exact finite wave. Here \(C_{2,0}=1\), but \(1-U(\xi)=e^{-|\xi|/2}\), which cannot be bounded by \(M e^{-(1-\varepsilon)|\xi|}\) for \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). Restoring \(1/m\) repairs the stated rate; arguments requiring merely some positive exponential rate may still proceed.

Problem 6 (Major: the comparison function misses its logarithmic phase).  In Theorem 6.3, p. 39, set \(a=(N-1)c_\sharp\) and \(b=m(p-1)-1\). The printed comparison function has phase \(r-g(t)+C\), but its residual includes the extra term \(a/t\) that would come from differentiating \(a\log t\).

The specified ODE is \[g'=f^bc_*+(1-f^b)\frac at
 -\frac{\lambda\log(t-t_0)}{(t-t_0)^2}
 +\frac{\lambda}{(t-t_0)^2}.\] Consequently \(g'-c_*=O(\log t/t^2)\) and \(g-c_*t\) approaches a constant. It cannot already contain the required logarithmic shift. With the printed phase, the \(fU'\) coefficient is \(f^b(c_*-a/t)-g'\). The displayed residual instead has \(f^b(c_*-a/t)-g'+a/t\). The consistent repair is \[w(r,t)=f(t)U\!\left(r-g(t)+a\log t+C;\
                    \gamma(c_*-a/t)\right).\] It restores both the chain rule and the intended front location \(c_*t-a\log t+O(1)\). For \(N>1\), the omitted term in the actual printed subsolution residual is of order \(1/t\) and cannot be absorbed by the displayed \(O(\log t/t^2)\) terms. The actual residual exceeds the printed residual by \(-afU'/t>0\) at a fixed interior profile point when \(N>1\). Thus the printed comparison function does not establish the subsolution inequality used for Theorem 6.3 and the central logarithmic front-location estimate. This is a major defect in that proof dependency, even though the missing phase can be written explicitly. Restoring it repairs the chain rule; the full barrier still depends on resolving the parameter estimates in Problem 2. The additional formula corrections below are local and are not individually classified as major.

There are additional local formula corrections: Lemma 6.2 (p. 38) and both bounds in Theorem 6.3 (p. 39) print \(c_*\) where \(c_*t\) is required. The surrounding text and the ODE for \(g\) identify the intended expression. Lemma 4.12, (4.13) on p. 29, places both zero and nonzero derivative data at \(0\); the terminal nonzero-flux datum belongs at \(b\), with its derivative/flux normalization stated consistently. Lemma 4.6 (p. 25) reverses the ordering of trajectories with respect to the speed. The forward ODE (4.5) gives the larger trajectory for larger speed on their common positive interval, not the printed smaller one. For \(m=p=2\), \(h(U)=U(1-U)\), \(\gamma=0\) and \(c=3<4=d\), the maximal branches have slopes \(c,d\) at zero, contradicting the printed inequality.

Finally, the limit \(U^{(m-1)(p-1)}|U'|^{p-2}\to0\) at \(U\to1\), used on pp. 38 and 40, is \(1\) when \(p=2\). The argument there needs boundedness rather than vanishing, so replacing that assertion by the required boundedness avoids creating an artificial obstruction to the comparison.

# Positive checks and remaining proof obligations

The weak jump condition in the expanding compactly supported subsolution of Lemma 5.3 has the correct sign in the distributional inequality. Passing \(c_1\uparrow c\) gives the stated expanding-ball lower bound. The radial upper and lower comparisons explain the \(O(1)\) nonradial location conclusions of Corollary 2.5(i)–(ii), provided the radial asymptotics are established.

The cap-shaped subsolution in Lemma 6.1 has the appropriate power balance for \(p>2\): \[(n-1)(p-1)-1>n
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n>\frac p{p-2}.\] This supports the stated choice of a large spatial exponent. The \(p=2\) treatment is separately referred to prior work; the restriction \(p\geq2\) in the main result should not be discarded. The asymptotic finite-front law (2.2) and the identity \((u^m)_r=u^\alpha v_r\) are consistent with the explicit profiles above.

The parameter derivative should remain negative if the corrected positive-weight compatibility formula and endpoint controls are justified. Its bound must depend on the actual profile. Similarly, uniform flux bounds away from the initial time may be recoverable even though the stronger formula in (3.2) is false. These observations identify plausible repair boundaries; they are not completed replacements for the missing proofs.

Theorem 6.4 and the final subsequence-to-full-time argument in Theorem 2.4 are stated by adaptation of earlier results. Their entire external proofs were not independently reconstructed here. No counterexample to the correctly normalized final radial convergence theorem has been established by this review. Problems 1–3 and the missing phase in Problem 6 nonetheless affect central dependencies and warrant major scientific revision. The formal decision is `pending`, with an empty human-review list.
