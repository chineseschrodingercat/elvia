Abstract: The reviewed source is arXiv:2312.00138v3, the author-declared accepted revision of 4 June 2025. The radial scalar-curvature identity, parameter selection, entropy scaling, and hypotheses of the two entropy comparisons were checked. The smooth cutoff bounds in equation (7) are inconsistent: the second-derivative constant \(16\) is an unattainable extremum. A concrete smoothing construction with bound \(36\) repairs this local defect while preserving the geometric estimates and theorem hypotheses. This is assessed as Minor. No defect in the checked central entropy conclusions is established. The cited source passages and figures have now been visually confirmed; the stated external-proof limitations remain.

# The drawstring metric and its estimates

In a tube about a simple closed geodesic, the source uses \[g=e^{-2u}dr^2+e^{-2u}h^2\sinh^2r\,d\theta^2
                    +e^{2u}\cosh^2r\,dt^2.\] The radial functions are constant or even to the required orders near the core: \(u\) is constant and \(h=1-c_1r^2/(2r_2)\) there. With radial arclength \(\rho=e^{-u(0)}r\), the angular warping function is \(\rho+O(\rho^3)\) and the axial coefficient is even. This supplies the smooth extension at the core. Radial invariance also makes the local formula compatible with the normal holonomy around the geodesic.

For an independent curvature calculation, set \[X=h'/h,\quad Y=u',\quad H_2=h''/h,\quad U_2=u'',\quad
 C=\coth r,\quad T=\tanh r.\] If \(A=e^{-u}h\sinh r\) and \(B=e^u\cosh r\), their logarithmic \(r\)-derivatives are \(\alpha=-Y+X+C\) and \(\beta=Y+T\). The doubly warped scalar-curvature formula, using \(d/d\rho=e^u d/dr\), gives \[R_g=-2e^{2u}
  \bigl(\alpha'+\beta'+\alpha^2+\beta^2+\alpha\beta
                                      +Y(\alpha+\beta)\bigr).\] The bracket is \[H_2+2CX+Y^2+TX+2YT+3,\] because \(CT=1\). An independently executed symbolic expansion gave residual \(CT-1\) before that identity and exactly zero afterwards. Thus equation (13), pp. 6–9, has the correct signs and normalization. For \(h=1,u=0\) it returns \(R_{g_0}=-6\).

The order of parameter choices is material: \(r_1\) is chosen below the normal radius and \(e^{-100}\); \(c_1\) is small relative to \(r_1\) and the curvature tolerance; then \(r_2\) is made small enough that \(u(0)=a<0\) can be achieved with \(0<c_2<c_1\). The divergence of \(\int ds/(s\log(1/s))\) at zero permits any fixed negative \(a\). On the inner region, the positive terms from \(h\) dominate \((u')^2\) because \(c_2^2\leq c_1\) and \(\zeta(r/(4r_2))\leq\zeta(r/r_2)\). The remaining terms are bounded by the small tolerance using \(\log(1/r)>100\).

For the outer annulus \(r\in[r_1/4,r_1]\), \(u=0\) and \(\zeta(r/r_2)=1\). Although \(\psi(r)\) itself retains an additive dependence on \(r_2\), the uniform bound \(\psi\leq1/2\) suffices: \[|h'|+|h''|\leq C(r_1)c_1\] uniformly for \(0<r_2<r_1/64\), for any fixed smooth cutoff with bounded first and second derivatives. This is the uniform estimate needed before choosing \(r_2\).

The radial crossing bound follows from \(e^{-u(r)}\leq(\log(1/r))^{r_1}\). Integration by parts yields an endpoint term at most \(2r_1\) and a remaining integral at most \(r_1^2\), hence \(\int_0^{r_1}e^{-u}\,dr\leq3r_1\). This estimate is independent of the large contraction along the core and is essential to keeping the second based loop short.

# The impossible smooth cutoff constant

Problem 1 (The bounds in equation (7) cannot all hold).  On p. 5 the source requires a smooth function \(\zeta\) with \(\zeta=0\) on \([0,1/2]\), \(\zeta=1\) on \([1,\infty)\), \(0\leq\zeta'\leq4\), and \(|\zeta''|\leq16\). The same bounds, with reversed monotonicity, are imposed on \(\eta\). There is no such \(C^2\) function.

## A decisive nonexistence proof

Let \(v(t)=\zeta'(1/2+t)\) for \(0\leq t\leq1/2\). Smooth matching to the constant pieces gives \(v(0)=v(1/2)=0\), while the total increment requires \(\int_0^{1/2}v(t)\,dt=1\). The derivative bound implies \[v(t)\leq16\min(t,1/2-t)=:\mathcal T(t).\] The triangular upper envelope has area \[\int_0^{1/2}\mathcal T(t)\,dt
 =\frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot4=1.\] Since \(\mathcal T-v\) is continuous and nonnegative and its integral is zero, \(v=\mathcal T\) everywhere. But \(\mathcal T\) is not differentiable at \(t=1/4\). This contradicts \(v\in C^1\). The contradiction concerns all functions satisfying the stated bounds, not just one failed candidate cutoff. Replacing \(\eta\) by \(1-\eta\) gives the same obstruction for \(\eta\).

## An explicit admissible repair

Define a nonnegative continuous trapezoid \(v_0\) supported in \([13/24,23/24]\). It rises with slope \(36\) from zero to \(3\) on \([13/24,15/24]\), stays equal to \(3\) on \([15/24,21/24]\), and falls with slope \(-36\) to zero on \([21/24,23/24]\). Then \[\int v_0
 =2\left(\frac12\cdot\frac1{12}\cdot3\right)
       +\frac14\cdot3=1,\qquad
 0\leq v_0\leq3,\qquad \|v_0'\|_\infty\leq36.\] Convolve with a nonnegative smooth unit-mass mollifier of support radius \(1/48\). The resulting \(v\) is smooth, supported strictly inside \((1/2,1)\), has integral one, and satisfies \(0\leq v\leq3\), \(|v'|\leq36\). Set \[\zeta(x)=\int_0^x v(t)\,dt,\qquad \eta=1-\zeta.\] All endpoint, support, range and monotonicity conditions in (7) now hold, including the original first-derivative bound \(4\). Only the second-derivative bound changes from \(16\) to \(36\). The trapezoid area, slope and strict support margins were also checked by exact rational arithmetic.

## Repair boundary and actual impact

In the inner-region estimate (15), the argument uses \(\zeta'\geq0\), the cutoff values between zero and one, and the parameter inequalities; the constant \(16\) is not used. The second derivative enters the outer-annulus estimate through a fixed finite bound on \(\eta''\). The uniform estimate established above remains valid with \(36\), and choosing \(c_1\) smaller gives the same prescribed curvature tolerance. All subsequent choices of \(r_2,c_2\) and the radial crossing estimate remain available. The original conclusions (i)–(iv) of Theorem 1.1 are thus preserved by this repair, with their original hypotheses.

The literal preliminary cutoff selection is false. Its demonstrated impact is Minor, because the needed construction and bounds are established here after replacing a numerical auxiliary bound, without weakening the geometric theorem. This assessment is based on the verified downstream estimates, not merely on the brevity of the correction. No author adoption or journal-version persistence is asserted.

# Entropy arguments and boundary cases

## Arbitrarily large entropy

A shortest nontrivial closed geodesic can be chosen simple. In the hyperbolic fundamental group, powers of suitable noncommuting elements generate a free subgroup of rank two. The core loop length tends to zero under the drawstring construction, while a transverse based representative of the second generator has uniformly bounded length by the radial crossing estimate.

The precise inequality needed from Balacheff–Merlin [BM], Corollary 2, is \[\frac1{1+\exp(h\ell_1)}+\frac1{1+\exp(h\ell_2)}\leq\frac12.\] If \(h\) had a bounded subsequence while \(\ell_1\to0\) and \(\ell_2\leq L\), the first summand would tend to \(1/2\) and the second would remain bounded away from zero. Therefore \(h\to\infty\). The loop-independence condition, rather than arbitrary nontriviality of the loops, is essential. From \(R_{g_i}\geq-7\), the rescaling \(\widehat g_i=(7/6)g_i\) gives \(R_{\widehat g_i}\geq-6\) and \(h(\widehat g_i)=\sqrt{6/7}\,h(g_i)\to\infty\).

## Metrics close to the hyperbolic metric

Fix \(\delta>0\), choose \(a=-\delta\) and curvature tolerance and tube radius tending to zero. The uniform comparison \(e^{-2\delta}g_0\leq g_i\leq e^{2\delta}g_0\) gives equicontinuity and a subsequential uniform limit of the distance functions. The limit is bi-Lipschitz to \(d_{g_0}\). For sufficiently close distinct points on the core geodesic, the shortened core segment is strictly shorter than their original minimizing segment, so the limiting identity is not an isometry.

For a minimizing \(g_0\)-geodesic, each passage through the thin tube can be replaced by radial legs and a core path. The added error is at most \(6/i\) per passage. Every intervening exterior segment has length at least \(s_I/2\) for sufficiently large \(i\). If there are \(n\) passages and \(D=\operatorname{diam}(M,g_0)\), the immediately justified count is \[(n-1)s_I/2\leq D,\qquad n\leq1+2D/s_I.\] Consequently the argument gives the sufficient bound \[d_{g_i}(x,y)\leq d_{g_0}(x,y)
             +(6+12D/s_I)i^{-1}.\] This verifies the limiting \(1\)-Lipschitz property. The omitted \(+1\) in the printed count and the repeated \(d_{g_i}\) in equation (31), p. 13, are local corrections; they do not change the \(O(i^{-1})\) conclusion. No independent geometric counterexample to the claimed main distance bound is asserted.

In Song’s Theorem 3.5 [Song], take the open set \(A_i=M\), the homotopy equivalence \(f_i=\mathrm{Id}\), and the limiting bi-Lipschitz map \(\Psi=\mathrm{Id}\). Uniform distance convergence supplies the required approximate isometry. The theorem therefore gives a strict limiting entropy gap above \(2\). Its orientation hypothesis can be handled for a nonorientable \(M\) by the oriented double cover: the lifted radial construction has the same distance comparisons and a strictly shortened local core segment, and volume entropy is unchanged by a finite cover. Finally \(\widehat g_i=(1+1/(6i))g_i\) imposes \(R_{\widehat g_i}\geq-6\); the factor tends to one, preserving the strict limiting gap. Taking \(\delta\) small and then \(i\) large proves the stated \(C^0\) approximation regime.
