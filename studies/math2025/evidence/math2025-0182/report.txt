Abstract: This report examines the acquired third arXiv version. Explicit admissible examples disprove a Cartesian assertion used in the NS5 proof and the moment-map compatibility of a printed D5 resolution table. A separate co-separated example disproves the stated unnormalized one-term coefficient. Two local specification problems concern an attracting correspondence and change of group after full coefficient-field localization. The report records the correct constructions already present elsewhere in the paper, checks several coefficient and induction steps, and limits its conclusions to the acquired version. It does not establish that the normalized mirror identity is false. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Findings on the geometric proof path

Problem 1 (The NS5 Cartesian square contains additional points).  Equation (46), p. 48, and the Cartesian assertion of Corollary 7.3 are false for an allowed elementary NS5 resolution.

Exact example and conventions. The source uses quotient flag dimensions (footnote 7, p. 47). Take \[X=T^*\operatorname{Fl}(1;3),\qquad \overline X=T^*\operatorname{Fl}(1,2;3).\] Thus the coarse flag is a two-dimensional kernel \(K\subset\mathbb C^3\); the refined flag consists of kernel subspaces \(F_1\subset F_2\subset\mathbb C^3\) of dimensions one and two. The separated bow is \(/1/3\backslash2\backslash1\backslash\). Splitting its final NS5 local charge \(2\) as \(1+1\) gives \(/1/2/3\backslash2\backslash1\backslash\). Every D5 local charge is one, so this is exactly the flag-variety case used in the proof of Proposition 7.2.

Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) be the standard basis and put \[F_1=\mathbb Ce_1,\qquad F_2=\mathbb Ce_1+\mathbb Ce_2,\qquad
 N=E_{12}.\] The refined cotangent conditions hold: \(N(F_1)=0\), \(N(F_2)\subset F_1\), and \(N(\mathbb C^3)\subset F_2\). Moreover \(N^2=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}N=1\). It lies in the coarse Springer image \(Z_1\), since \(K=\ker N=\mathbb Ce_1+\mathbb Ce_3\) satisfies \(\operatorname{im}N\subset K\subset\ker N\). Consequently \((F_\bullet,N)\) lies in \(\overline X\times_{Z_2}Z_1\).

The incidence correspondence \(L\), however, requires the cotangent condition for the coarsened flag of the same point: \(N(F_2)=0\). Here \(N(e_2)=e_1\ne0\). Thus this point does not lie in \(j(L)\). This disproves the asserted Cartesian equality by an explicit point satisfying all the relevant flag and nilpotence conditions.

The affine square also fails. Footnote 8 observes that the affine bow quotient need not equal the Springer image. The following check does not assume such equality. In the refined NS5 quiver, take \[C_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 D_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad D_1=0.\] The two choices \(C_1=(0,1)\) and \(C_1=(1,0)\) give, respectively, a bad point and a good point in the same flag-variety presentation; the remaining three-way data are unchanged. All moment equations hold: \(C_2D_2=0=D_1C_1\) and \(C_1D_1=0\). For both representatives \(D_2C_2=E_{12}\), so the unchanged three-way tail still satisfies the boundary moment equation. The surjective \(C\)-maps give the stable flag representatives. The condition for membership in \(j(L)\) is that \(D_2\) factor through \(C_1\). It fails for the bad point and holds for the good one with \(D=e_1\).

Scaling the first gauge space by \(t\ne0\) sends \(C_1\) to \(tC_1\) and fixes the other maps. Both representatives have the same limit \(C_1=0\) in the affine moment variety as \(t\to0\). Polynomial invariants therefore give them the same affine quotient point. Pairing the bad stable point with the \(L_0\)-image of the good point produces an element of the fibre product in Corollary 7.3 which does not come from \(L\). The issue persists with the actual affine quotient.

Dependence, counterevidence and repair boundary. Lemma 7.7 explicitly invokes Corollary 7.3, through the orbit argument of Lemma 6.11. Theorem 7.9 uses Lemma 7.7 for its support condition, equation (49); the nonresonance analysis in the added Lemma 7.11 also uses the lower-fixed-point containment. This is a demonstrated invalid premise on a core proof path and supports Major. No counterexample to the conclusion of Lemma 7.7 or the stable-envelope equality of Theorem 7.9 has been obtained.

The actual map \(j\) remains a closed incidence embedding in this example, and \(p\) remains a proper \(\mathbb P^1\) bundle. The extra linear condition on the coarsened flag proves closedness directly. Hence it would be incorrect to infer that Proposition 7.2’s properness or closedness is false. A replacement proof of the required orbit-chain containment may recover the argument. Neither the incorrect Cartesian statement nor its use in that containment proof is repaired merely by proving that \(j\) is closed.

Problem 2 (The printed D5 table does not preserve the moment equation).  The separated column of Table 1, p. 31, sends an admissible stable bow representation outside the target moment-zero locus. This contradicts the compatibility assertion in the proof of Proposition 6.3, pp. 32–33.

A full stable input. Use the separated diagram \(/1/2/3\backslash1\backslash\), with NS5 charges \((1,1,1)\) and D5 charges \((2,1)\), and split the first D5 charge as \(1+1\). For its first three-way part set \[B_-=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\-1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad B_+=0,\quad A=e_1,\quad a=e_2,\quad b=1.\] For the NS5 maps choose \[C_2=(1,0),\quad D_2=\binom01,\qquad
 C_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 D_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.\] The last D5 has \(W_-=\mathbb C\), \(W_+=0\), \(a=1\), and \(B_-=0\). Maps involving the zero end spaces are zero. Direct multiplication gives \[C_2D_2=0,\quad C_3D_3=D_2C_2,\quad -D_3C_3=B_-,
 \quad B_-A-AB_++ab=0.\] These are all the nontrivial moment equations. Condition (S1) holds because \(A\) is injective. Condition (S2) follows because \(A=e_1\), \(a=e_2\) and \(B_-a=e_3\) span \(\mathbb C^3\). The last framing vector generates the last one-dimensional space; \(A,a,B_-\) then generate \(\mathbb C^3\), and the surjective \(C\)-maps generate both remaining spaces. This verifies the stated graded-subspace criterion for GIT stability. The native evidence also records a semi-invariant frame check and exact integer products. A binary contingency table with the indicated row and column sums exists, so the paper’s nonempty-fixed-locus hypothesis is satisfied.

The nonzero target residual. For \(w''=1\), the printed right resolved triangle has \[B_{\mathrm{mid}}=\begin{pmatrix}B_+&0\\b&0\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 A''=\binom10,\quad a''=\binom01,\quad b''=1.\] Its moment equation evaluates to \[B_{\mathrm{mid}}A''-A''B_++a''b''=\binom02\ne0.\] This is an exact obstruction over \(\mathbb C\), not a numerical tolerance issue or a point outside the original stable locus.

Counterevidence and bounded repair. Example 6.1, p. 31, draws \(a''=-1\), which fixes this right-hand residual. Its displayed left triangle also uses the coherent signs \[A'=(A,-a),\quad a'=-B_-a,\quad b'=(0,-1).\] Together with the displayed \(+b\) block of \(B_{\mathrm{mid}}\), these give both resolved moment equations equal to zero whenever the original equation holds. The surrounding prose instead mentions a \(-b\) block. The source therefore contains incompatible prescriptions; it also contains a correct elementary prescription. This material counterevidence is retained.

The printed table is the stated general construction and is cited for moment compatibility before the embedding, fusion and mirror-reduction arguments. Its failure supports Major for that construction and derivation. The coherent elementary signs give a verified local repair. This review does not certify all general splitting formulas or all co-separated table entries after a silent sign replacement, and does not refute the existence of the intended geometric embedding.

# An explicit normalization failure

Problem 3 (The co-separated one-term coefficient has the wrong range).  The uniform product in Corollary 6.14, p. 39, is false for a co-separated split with \(w'=1\) and \(w''=2\).

Take the source bow \(X=\backslash3/2/1/\), a point, and resolve its sole D5 local charge \(3\) as \(1+2\). Its global charges are zero; the zero \(3\times1\) contingency table gives its unique fixed point. Section 6.1 and Corollary 6.14 do not exclude this case. Use the coherent co-separated elementary map displayed in Example 6.1; this isolates the scalar issue from Table 1’s incompatible entries. The resolved component is \(F=\backslash1\backslash3/2/1/\). The original framing torus acts trivially on \(F\), so \(N_j^-=0\) for the original chamber and \(\operatorname{Stab}_X=1\). At the distinguished resolution \(f_\sharp\), the vertex characters are \[W_{\mathrm{mid}}=\{a'h\},\quad
 W_3=\{a'h,a''h,a''h^2\},\quad
 W_2=\{a''h,a''h^2\},\quad W_1=\{a''h^2\}.\] Subtracting the moment-equation and gauge characters from the representation characters gives exactly \[T_{f_\sharp}F=\frac{a''}{a'}+\frac{a'}{a''}h.\] For the chamber \(a'>a''\) the negative character is \(a''/a'\). The change of group in (33) is \((a',a'',h)=(a,ah,h)\), so the diagonal stable-envelope value is \(\vartheta(h)\).

The pullback at the image of the source point can be identified with this diagonal value without assuming that tautological characters determine every elliptic class. An explicit stable affine line joins the two points. In the resolved second triangle take \[A=e_1^*,\quad a=u,\quad b=e_2^*,\quad
 B_-=0,\quad B_+=uE_{12}+E_{23},\] and in its NS5 tail take \[D_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 C_1=\begin{pmatrix}-u&0\\0&-1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad D_2=(0,1),\quad C_2=\binom{-1}{0}.\] The first triangle has \(b_0=1\) and \(B_0=0\). All moment equations hold for every \(u\). The three covectors \(b_0A,b,bB_+\) form the identity frame, which verifies (S1); the surjectivity of \(A\) verifies (S2). The tail and frame criterion verify stability throughout. The point \(u=1\) is the coherent resolution image and \(u=0\) is \(f_\sharp\). The parameter has character \((a'/a'')h\), which becomes one under \(\varphi\), so this affine line is fixed pointwise by the original \(T\). Equivariant homotopy invariance identifies the two restrictions. Thus the value \(\vartheta(h)\) is the relevant pullback.

The printed coefficient instead gives \(\vartheta(h)\vartheta(h^2)\). They are unequal: for an elliptic parameter \(0<q<1\), \(h=-1\) has \(\vartheta(h)\ne0\) and \(\vartheta(h^2)=\vartheta(1)=0\). The other parameters can be chosen generically. Equivalently the two explicit functions have different zero divisors, so this is not rescued by reading the formula meromorphically. The evidence records the admissible representative, its tangent-character cancellation, and the restriction to the distinguished point.

The source’s unprinted comment distinguishes the co-separated product through \(w'\). That comment is corroborating context; the tangent calculation and the stable-envelope diagonal axiom are the proof. In this example the product through \(w'=1\) is the correct value. A proof of a corrected formula for every split is not claimed here.

The one-term fusion identity is an advertised construction in the paper, and this is an explicit failure of its unnormalized statement. It supports Major at that scope. The normalized ratios used in the mirror identity can cancel a common factor. This counterevidence prevents promoting the example into a counterexample to Theorem 8.1.

# Two local specification problems

Problem 4 (The normalization correspondence is an attracting graph).  On p. 19 the paper identifies the stable-envelope kernel on \(F\times\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak C}(F)\) with the Thom line of the inclusion of that whole product. The claimed identification fails when \(F\) has positive dimension.

Take the bow \(X=T^*\mathbb P^1\) and let \(A\) be the diagonal framing torus, which acts trivially. Use \(T=A\times\mathbb C_h^\times\), as allowed in Section 5.3. There is one fixed component \(F=X\), and \(\operatorname{Attr}(F)=X\). The printed map \(j\) is the identity on \(X\times X\), so \(N_j=0\) and \(\Theta(N_j)=\mathcal O\).

Here \(\mathcal L_{A,F}=\mathcal L_X\). By the source’s dual-kernel convention, the line \(\mathcal L_{A,F}^{\triangledown}\boxtimes\mathcal L_X\), restricted at a diagonal point \((f,f)\), is \(\Theta(T_fX)\). At a point of the zero section the two \(T\)-weights are \(1\) and \(h\). Thus this line has degree one along the elliptic \(h\) parameter, while \(\mathcal O\) has degree zero. They cannot be isomorphic. This example uses no residual framing-ratio parameter outside the chosen \(T\).

The correct correspondence is the graph of the attracting projection \(x\mapsto\lim_{t\to0}\sigma(t)x\). When \(A\) acts trivially, this is \(\Delta_X\) and its normal bundle is \(TX\), exactly supplying the missing Thom factor. Remark 5.5, p. 20, already states the correct \(\vartheta(N^-)[\Delta]\) restriction. The triangle and D5 proofs later use this diagonal normalization and the \(\Theta(TF)\) factor. For that reason the supported assessment is Minor: the printed specification is inconsistent, while the inspected core formulas explicitly use the corrected correspondence. This assessment is based on that counterevidence and dependency check, not merely on the brevity of a repair.

Problem 5 (Full coefficient-field localization does not commute with restriction).  The unrestricted change-of-group map used in Appendix A, pp. 64–65, does not exist on the fully localized coefficient fields displayed there.

Consider the allowed separated D5 split \(2=1+1\). In additive equivariant coordinates, (33) gives \[\varphi^*(u')=u-\eta,\qquad
 \varphi^*(u'')=u,\qquad \varphi^*(\eta)=\eta.\] The nonzero polynomial \(t=u''-u'-\eta\) is killed by \(\varphi^*\). After tensoring the source with \(\operatorname{Frac}\mathbb Q[u',u'',\eta]\), \(t\) is invertible. A unital extension would therefore imply \[1=\varphi^*(t\,t^{-1})=\varphi^*(t)\varphi^*(t^{-1})=0,\] a contradiction in the nonzero target coefficient field. The bow \(/1/2\backslash\) and its resolution \(/1/2\backslash1\backslash\) realize this torus map.

One may first change group on unlocalized equivariant cohomology and then localize at target coefficients, or localize the source only at elements whose images are nonzero. Regular stable-envelope classes can lie in such a valid domain. The proof’s phrase allowing arbitrary fully localized \(\alpha\) requires this restriction. The finding is Minor at the level of that overly broad map; no counterexample to Proposition A.1’s intended image statement or to the mirror identity has been established. A complete rewritten proof of the image statement is not asserted.

# Positive checks, rejected candidates and overall assessment

For Theorem 3.1, an extra torus weight block without framing would admit scalar automorphisms, contrary to freeness at a stable point. On an unframed three-way block, (S1) and (S2) force its \(A\)-map to be invertible. These observations support the two-factor fixed-locus decomposition and the charge bookkeeping.

The local \(K\)-theory identity of Proposition 3.6 can be checked directly. Writing \(x=\xi_1\), \(y=\xi_2\), \(z=\xi_3\), \(c=C_A\) and \(y'=x+z+c-y\), the local expressions are \[\alpha_{\rm before}=h y'y^*,\qquad
 \alpha_{\rm after}=h y'(y^*-c^*)+cx^*.\] Their difference is the stated \(\operatorname{Hom}(x,c)-h\operatorname{Hom}(c,y')\). The quadratic change \(-2h\sum t_i\) cancels the dynamical change \(+2h\sum t_i\), including the boundary interpretation modulo parameter-only terms.

The induction in Theorem 8.1 is finite: fully splitting one brane of charge \(w>1\) takes \(w-1\) elementary operations and reduces the number of nonunit branes by one. It is not necessary for every intermediate operation to reduce that number. The zero-charge step and the full-flag base result remain explicit dependencies.

The coefficient calculation in Lemma 8.5 also has a correct reconstruction. For the projective-space stable envelopes of Proposition 5.12, put \(Q=(z_1/z_2)h^{w-2}\). Their normalized restriction is \[\frac{S_w|_{f_i}}{S_i|_{f_i}}=
 \left(\prod_{j=i+1}^{w}
 \frac{\vartheta(a_jh/a_i)}{\vartheta(a_i/a_j)}\right)
 \frac{\vartheta(h)}{\vartheta(a_wh/a_i)}
 \frac{\vartheta((a_i/a_w)Q)}{\vartheta(Q)}.\] At \(Q=h^{-1}\) the last two factors multiply to one. The additional substitution \(a\mapsto a^{-1}\) in (62) gives \(\prod_{j>i}\vartheta(a_ih/a_j)/\vartheta(a_j/a_i)\). The crossing sign is \((-1)^{w-i}\), as required by Lemma 8.6. At \(i=w\) the empty product is one. Thus inconsistent indices and intermediate \(z\)-ratios on p. 62 do not supply an additional false-coefficient finding. The conflicting order display in (57) and the cyclic-frame index on p. 12 were likewise retained as editorial clarification points rather than promoted into unsupported failures of the main conclusions.

The supported overall assessment is Major, with the separate scope and counterevidence stated for each item. The first three findings concern explicit geometric or fusion assertions; the last two concern local specifications with viable restricted interpretations. No counterexample to the normalized mirror formula has been established. The repairs verified here are bounded calculations, not certification of the complete revised paper.
