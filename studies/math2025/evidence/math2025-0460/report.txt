Abstract: The paper’s conditional approximation framework connects local spectral spaces, thin-layer weak approximation, and separable approximation of solution operators. Its exponent improvement has a coherent underlying dimension balance. However, the verification for Maxwell problems uses a scalar-potential decomposition that fails even for smooth fields with constant coefficients. Explicit fields below satisfy the relevant local equations, including the impedance condition in the wave case, but their tangential traces cannot be scalar gradients. This is a Major failure in an essential application proof, without a counterexample to the final exponential rates themselves. A separate Minor normalization error changes the stated elastic spectral forms; its correction is verified. The assessment is confined to the acquired preprint. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Source and mathematical scope

The reviewed source is C. Ma, A Unified Framework for Multiscale Spectral Generalized FEMs and Low-Rank Approximations to Multiscale PDEs, arXiv:2311.08761v3, 17 December 2024 [source]. The acquired PDF has 66 pages; all page references below are its one-based physical pages, which agree with the printed numbering. The assigned journal DOI is https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-025-09711-z. The publisher identifies online publication on 29 April 2025 and volume 26 (2026), pp. 1699–1758 [publisher]. The 60-page journal text was not mathematically compared with these 66-page preprint bytes. No equivalence or persistence of a finding into that text is asserted.

The general setting is a bounded form \(B\) on \(\mathcal H(\Omega)\), continuously embedded in \(\mathcal L(\Omega)\), with a Gårding estimate, well-posed primal and adjoint problems, compatible local restrictions, and partition-of-unity operators. The generalized harmonic space on a patch consists of functions annihilating all local zero-boundary tests. It can therefore contain functions with nonzero trace on an artificial patch boundary. The positive local form \(B^+\) can have a finite-dimensional kernel \(\mathcal K\); the construction retains these modes and takes widths on the corresponding quotient.

The central result, Theorem 3.8, is conditional on the Caccioppoli estimate and one of the weak approximation assumptions. For the thin-layer case with exponent \(\alpha=1/d\), it gives exponential decay with exponent \(1/d\). The wave case also has a parameter-dependent starting dimension. Theorems 4.3 and 4.5 transfer the local approximation mechanism to Green functions, when these are available, and to solution operators on separated domains. Verification of those hypotheses for each PDE is therefore a substantive part of the scientific claim. Problem 1 concerns that verification, principally Theorems 6.17 and 6.28.

# Problem 1: scalar potentials cannot absorb general Maxwell traces

## Location and precise claim

On p. 46, (6.70)–(6.72) assert that every \(u\in H_B(\operatorname{curl};D^\delta)\) has a decomposition \[u=u^0+\nabla\phi,\qquad
 u^0\in X(\kappa;D^\delta)
 :=\{v\in H_0(\operatorname{curl};D^\delta):\operatorname{div}(\kappa v)=0\},\] with \(\operatorname{div}(\kappa\nabla\phi)=0\) and \(n\times\nabla\phi=n\times u\) on \(\partial D^\delta\). The proof uses this assertion to apply the spectral estimates (6.73)–(6.80) to \(u^0\), and scalar harmonic approximation to \(\phi\) in (6.81)–(6.90).

The same obstruction occurs in the impedance argument. Equation (6.120), p. 53, prescribes \(\nabla_Tp_n=u_{n,T}\) on the physical boundary. Equation (6.126), p. 55, prescribes \[i\omega\lambda\nabla_T\psi=(\mu^{-1}\operatorname{curl}u)\times n
 \quad\hbox{on }\Gamma^{**}.\] By (6.125), its right-hand side is \(i\omega\lambda u_T\). Membership in a tangential trace space does not imply membership in the range of the tangential scalar gradient.

## A counterexample within the positive curl–curl setting

Choose \[\Omega=(-2,2)^3,\quad D^\delta=(-1,1)^3,\quad
 D=(-1/4,1/4)^3,\quad \nu=\kappa=1,
 \qquad u(x,y,z)=(0,e^x,0).\] The intended distance from \(D\) to the artificial outer boundary is \(\delta=3/4\), and \(\operatorname{diam}(D^\delta)=2\sqrt3<6\delta\). These are convex Lipschitz domains satisfying the geometric conditions of the patch argument. The coefficient bounds hold, and (6.42) holds with \(C_1=1\) for the usual squared \(H(\operatorname{curl})\) norm. All functions are real, as required in this subsection.

Direct differentiation gives \[\operatorname{div}u=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{curl}u=(0,0,e^x),\qquad
 \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}u=(0,-e^x,0)=-u.\] For every \(v\in H_0(\operatorname{curl};D^\delta)\), integration by parts thus gives \[(\operatorname{curl}u,\operatorname{curl}v)_{D^\delta}+(u,v)_{D^\delta}=0.\] There is no physical boundary on this interior patch. Consequently \(u\) belongs to exactly the local space defined in (6.46); it need not satisfy the global perfectly conducting condition on \(\partial\Omega\).

Suppose the asserted decomposition existed. On the open face \(z=1\), the zero tangential trace of \(u^0\) would imply, in trace distributions, \[\partial_x(\phi|_{z=1})=0,\qquad
 \partial_y(\phi|_{z=1})=e^x.\] Distributional mixed derivatives commute, whereas these two identities give respectively \(\partial_y\partial_x\phi=0\) and \(\partial_x\partial_y\phi=e^x\). This is impossible. Equivalently, the tangential field has surface curl \(e^x\ne0\). For the unit-height rectangle with \(-1/2\le x,y\le1/2\), its circulation is \(e^{1/2}-e^{-1/2}>0\), while any scalar gradient has zero circulation. The distributional argument applies to the stated \(H^1\) potential; it does not impose extra smoothness on a hypothetical \(\phi\).

This example also addresses the most natural alternative reading: a standard Helmholtz decomposition may exist with a different boundary condition on the vector component, but it does not give the claimed \(u^0\in H_0(\operatorname{curl})\). The estimates subsequently applied on \(X(\kappa;D^\delta)\) rely on that boundary condition.

## The impedance condition does not remove the obstruction

Let \[\Omega=(-2,2)^2\times(0,2),\qquad
 D^{**}=(-1,1)^2\times(0,1),\qquad
 \mu=\varepsilon=\lambda=1.\] Its only physical face is \(z=0\), with \(n=-e_z\). It is a cube truncated by the physical boundary. Nested truncated cubes with half-sides \(1/4,1/2,3/4,1,5/4\) supply the surrounding patches and positive artificial-boundary gaps required in the argument. For any \(\omega>1\), put \[\beta=\sqrt{\omega^2-1},\qquad
 h(z)=\cos(\beta z)-\frac{i\omega}{\beta}\sin(\beta z),
 \qquad u=(0,e^{ix}h(z),0).\] Since \(h''+(\omega^2-1)h=0\), this smooth field satisfies \[\operatorname{div}u=0,\qquad \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}u-\omega^2u=0.\] At \(z=0\), \(h(0)=1\) and \(h'(0)=-i\omega\). Moreover, \[\operatorname{curl}u=(-e^{ix}h',0,ie^{ix}h),\qquad
 (\operatorname{curl}u)\times(-e_z)=(0,-e^{ix}h',0)=i\omega u_T.\] Thus the exact homogeneous impedance condition in (6.111) and (6.125) holds. The field and its tangential trace have finite energy on the patch. Integration by parts against tests with zero tangential trace on the artificial faces establishes \(u\in H_B^{\mathrm{imp}}(\operatorname{curl};D^{**})\), as defined in (6.116).

Nevertheless, on \(z=0\), \[u_T=(0,e^{ix},0),\qquad
 \partial_x(u_T)_y-\partial_y(u_T)_x=ie^{ix}\ne0.\] No scalar potential can have this tangential gradient, even locally in the interior of that face. Hence (6.126) is unsolvable for this admissible \(u\), regardless of its additional conditions on the other faces. Taking the constant bounded sequence \(u_n=u\) also invalidates the asserted existence of \(p_n\) in (6.120).

## Impact, calibration and repair status

This is a Major finding: a false auxiliary assertion is used at an essential step in the advertised Maxwell application. The positive example interrupts the proof of (6.68)/(6.90) and Theorem 6.17. The impedance example interrupts Lemma 6.27’s supplied compactness proof and the boundary approximation (6.128)–(6.139) used for Theorem 6.28. The interior impedance argument on p. 54 also invokes the same zero-tangential decomposition. The resulting Maxwell-specific low-rank conclusions cannot be certified through the presented verification.

Neither compactness itself nor the final \(e^{-cn^{1/d}}\) rates have been disproved here. The abstract theorem, conditional on its hypotheses, is not refuted. A replacement proof must control the nongradient part of the tangential boundary field, for example through an appropriate vector lifting or surface decomposition, with the required stability and approximation bounds. No complete repair of that application proof is claimed. Merely assuming tangential-gradient data would exclude the explicit admissible fields above and would narrow the stated application.

# Problem 2: elastic spectral forms have a normalization error

## Location and exact checks

The strain is defined on p. 39 and p. 51 by \(\epsilon(u)=(\nabla u+\nabla u^\top)/2\). Example 5.2, p. 31, correctly includes the factor \(1/2\) in the cutoff commutator. In contrast, (6.33) and (6.36), p. 40, and (6.107) and (6.110), pp. 51–52, use \[\Theta_\eta(u)=u(\nabla\eta)^\top+(\nabla\eta)u^\top\] without the corresponding factor \(1/4\) in its quadratic form.

Here is an admissible direct test of (6.33). On the interior square \(D^*=(-1,1)^2\), take \(C\) to be the identity on symmetric matrices, \(u=v=e_1\), and \(\eta=(1-x^2)^2(1-y^2)^2\). This is a \(C^1\) cutoff vanishing on the patch boundary, as required by Lemma 6.1. The constant field is generalized harmonic because its strain vanishes. But \[\epsilon(\eta e_1)=\tfrac12\Theta_\eta(e_1),\qquad
 B_{D^*}(\eta e_1,\eta e_1)=\frac{32768}{11025},\] whereas the right-hand side printed in (6.33) is \(131072/11025\). Both are strictly positive. The nonkernel generalized harmonic field \(u=(x,0)\) gives the same factor four: the two values are \(32768/43659\) and \(131072/43659\).

For an independent wave check on the same interior patch, take \(\omega=\rho=1\) and \(u=(0,e^{i\sqrt2 x})\). Then \(-\operatorname{div}\epsilon(u)-u=0\), so this field belongs to the local wave harmonic space. Exact integration gives \[\int|\epsilon(\eta u)|^2=\frac{360448}{99225},\quad
 \int|\Theta_\eta(u)|^2=\frac{131072}{11025},\quad
 \int\eta^2|u|^2=\frac{65536}{99225}.\] The first quantity equals one quarter of the second plus the third, as the corrected (6.107) requires. In (6.110), the corresponding weighted form has an additional mass term; its correct coefficient is \(2\omega^2\). The printed form exceeds the correct value on this test by \(32768/3675\).

## Verified correction and limited impact

Replace every such \(\Theta_\eta\) by \(\tfrac12(u(\nabla\eta)^\top+(\nabla\eta)u^\top)\), or multiply the strain quadratic terms in those four formulas by \(1/4\). Keep the wave mass term in (6.110) unchanged. The product rule and the harmonic identities then give the corrected equalities, consistently with Example 5.2.

The significance is the false equivalence of the stated spectral problems, rather than the appearance of a missing symbol alone. For static elasticity the erroneous numerator rescales the finite generalized eigenvalues by four; the same eigenspaces remain available. In the wave form only the strain part is misweighted, so a common rescaling of the whole eigenproblem is not the repair. The conservative static Caccioppoli estimate (6.34) and the qualitative approximation exponent are not refuted. This is a Minor normalization defect with a verified local correction, separate from the uncorrected Maxwell proof gap.

# Checks of the framework and boundaries of the conclusions

## Spectral construction and the exponent improvement

The quotient/kernel bookkeeping in Section 2 and Lemmas 3.13–3.14 was checked at the level of the main argument. Weak approximation forces the restricted kernel into the finite-dimensional approximating space. Adding it back after the quotient yields the dimension count \[\dim\mathcal K+(m-\dim\mathcal K)+(N-1)m=mN.\] For a compact Hilbert-space restriction, the width is the \((n+1)\)-st singular value, so the generalized eigenproblem constructs the optimal local range space after the kernel treatment. Appendix B’s compactness argument uses weak compact approximation on an intermediate patch followed by Caccioppoli control on the smaller patch.

The thin-layer volume is proportional to its thickness. With \(N\) layers and \(m\) modes per layer, the leading contraction factor in (3.18) scales as \(m^{-\alpha}N^{1-\alpha}\). Taking \(m\) proportional to \(N^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}\) makes this factor less than one; \(n=mN\) is proportional to \(N^{1/\alpha}\). Iteration therefore explains \(e^{-c n^\alpha}\). With only the bulk weak norm, the factor scales as \(m^{-\alpha}N\), giving instead \(e^{-c n^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}}\). This verifies the mechanism for the improvement from \(1/(d+1)\) to \(1/d\); it does not independently certify every printed sharp constant or rounding step for every integer \(n\).

Theorem 3.8(iii)’s starting dimension depends on \(\sigma^*\). For fixed geometry, its stated power is \((\sigma^*)^{1/[\alpha(1-\alpha)]}\); for \(\alpha=1/d\) this is \(d^2/(d-1)\). Thus a wavenumber-independent exponential constant alone is not a uniform small-rank assertion for all frequencies and all \(n\). Remark 3.10 explains the separate wavelength-scale patch regime. The FE mesh condition in Remarks 3.12 and 3.16, arising from \(h_K\lesssim\delta^*/N\), must likewise be retained.

## Other application and global arguments

The piecewise-constant construction of Lemma 5.5 gives \(m\simeq |V|H^{-d}\) and the \(H^1\)-to-\(L^2\) error \(H\|\nabla u\|\), explaining the volume-sensitive \(m^{-1/d}\) estimate. The elastic variant replaces constants by local rigid motions and uses the Korn estimate of Lemma 6.13. The discrete scalar Caccioppoli argument was checked for its use of \(\eta^2u-I_h(\eta^2u)\), superapproximation, inverse estimates and absorption. Discrete harmonicity is not silently identified with continuous harmonicity.

For the Gårding-based global argument, adjoint approximation gives \(\|e\|_{\mathcal L}\le C_b\eta(S_n)\|e\|_{\mathcal H}\). The stated smallness condition then absorbs the lower-order term and Galerkin orthogonality yields the usual \(2C_b/C_1\) quasi-optimal factor. The nonconjugate branch of the local-to-adjoint construction in Lemma 3.25 was followed. Its complex-conjugate branch requires further checking of approximation in the actual trial space; this review does not certify that branch or convert the unresolved question into a counterexample. The complex mixed implementation formulas in Section 2.3 were read but were not fully independently certified.

The reduction from local harmonic approximation to separated Green-function and solution-operator approximation in Section 4 was checked conditionally on the local result and the relevant existence and separation hypotheses. No Green function is assumed to exist merely from a formal symbol. The plate extension and its higher-order approximation structure were read, but every regularity estimate, external compactness theorem and sharp constant was not reproved.

The paper explicitly leaves the Nédélec weak approximation extension on p. 48 and global Maxwell quasi-optimality on p. 57 to further work. Their absence is not an additional defect. Likewise, the proposed \(1/(d-1)\) rate on p. 60 is identified as a conjectural improvement. No failure is inferred from that conjecture or from the lack of a released code repository.
