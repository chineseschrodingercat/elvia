Abstract: The general hierarchy criteria admit an explicit compact zero-dimensional counterexample. In the Brownian application, the natural measure is almost surely not doubling, and the bad-tile covering argument contains an almost-sure counterexample to an actual intermediate assertion. The false all-generation flatness assertion in the carpet proof is also a Major defect in a core derivation. These issues do not establish that the main Brownian conformal-dimension conclusion is false. A replacement modulus argument removes the doubling requirement, but further gaps in the good-set, compact-core, and weighted-averaging arguments remain. The uniform-fiber carpet and continuous-graph results have explicit repairs, which are distinguished from the unresolved Brownian proof. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The general hierarchy theorems are false as stated

Problem 1 (Major: Theorems 3.4, 3.6 and 3.7, pages 16–23).  The definitions do not require hierarchy cells to shrink. All their printed hypotheses can hold on a compact metric space of conformal dimension zero.

Let \(E=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\). For distinct \(a,b\), let \(k(a,b)\) be the first differing coordinate and set \[d(a,b)=2^{-k(a,b)^2}.\] This compact ultrametric space has diameter \(1/2\). Its ordinary length-\(n\) prefix cylinders give a cover with total \(s\)-content at most \[2^n\bigl(2^{-(n+1)^2}\bigr)^s
 =2^{n-s(n+1)^2}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(s>0).\] Thus \(\dim_H E=\dim_C E=0\).

The hierarchy used for the counterexample is different from these ordinary cylinders. Let \(c(b)=\sum_{j\ge1}2b_j3^{-j}\), the continuous bijection onto the ternary Cantor set. If \(a\) has an asymptotic frequency of ones \(p(a)\) in that Cantor set, put \(q(a)=c^{-1}(p(a))\); otherwise put \(q(a)=0^\infty\). The map \(q\) is Borel: the convergence set of the Cesàro means is Borel, their limit is Borel there, and \(c^{-1}\) is continuous. Every fiber \(q^{-1}(b)\) is dense and nonempty. Indeed, after any prescribed finite prefix, append the sequence \(\lfloor np\rfloor-\lfloor(n-1)p\rfloor\), with \(p=c(b)\).

For a binary word \(w\) of length \(i\), define \[E_{i,w}=q^{-1}([w]).\] Each generation is a Borel partition of \(E\); each cell has exactly two children, one parent and one sibling. Every infinite branch has nonempty intersection \(q^{-1}(b)\). Every cell is dense and has diameter \(1/2\), and every pair of siblings has distance zero. Therefore (3.6) and (3.7) hold with relative distance zero and \(L=1\).

Every proper ball in this ultrametric is clopen and contains no dense hierarchy cell. A ball equal to \(E\) contains each cell together with its parent. Hence the cardinality in the printed flatness condition on page 16 is always zero. In particular a uniform flatness constant exists. The conclusion \(\dim_C E\ge1\) of Theorem 3.4 is nevertheless false. Taking the constant family \(E^n=E\), with the same cells for every \(n\), also satisfies the nesting, boundedness, uniform-flatness, ratio and iterated-limit conditions of Theorems 3.6 and 3.7 and refutes their conclusions.

The stopping-cell cover on page 19 implicitly assumes that each point eventually lies in a cell contained in a given small ball. That is exactly what fails here. A vanishing-mesh hypothesis excludes this example, but is not by itself a complete repair: the proof also transfers original-metric flatness to image-space balls without proof, uses an inclusion of a closure into the original set, and reverses the inside/outside directions of a decreasing sequence. The limiting arguments in 3.6–3.7 require further localization and mass retention. For example, finite measures on a bounded noncompact space need not have a nonzero weak limit there.

The cylinder-weight algebra of Lemma 3.5 was checked separately. It does not supply the missing ball cover, and the example above is not offered as a counterexample to its intended normalized cylinder bound. A complete corrected general hierarchy theorem is not certified. Nor does this example describe the particular carpets or Brownian sets.

# The Brownian measure is not doubling

Problem 2 (Major proof-level issue: page 38 and the use of Theorem 3.2).  The measure defined by (6.1) is almost surely not doubling, even on the compact set used in the application.

Let \[X=\Gamma(W)\cap([0,T_6]\times[0,1]),\qquad z_0=(0,0).\] The space-time occupation identity identifies the integral of level local times in (6.1) with the pushforward of \(dt\) to the graph. One may use, for example, (2.5) of [occupation]; a common local-time normalization constant cancels in all ratios below.

For \(c=1,2\), define, before the terminal-time truncation, \[m_c(r)=\int_0^{cr}
 \chi_{\{0<W_t<r\sqrt{c^2-(t/r)^2}\}}\,dt .\] For all sufficiently small \(r\), almost surely, \(m_c(r)=\mu_X(B(z_0,cr))\), since \(2r<T_6\), \(2r<1\), and the times at level zero have zero Lebesgue measure. Brownian scaling, with \(\varepsilon=\sqrt r\), gives the joint law \[r^{-3/2}m_c(r)\ \stackrel{\rm law}{=}\
 \varepsilon^{-1}\int_0^c
 \chi_{\{0<B_u<\varepsilon f_c(u)\}}\,du,
 \qquad f_c(u)=\sqrt{c^2-u^2}.\] On one fixed Brownian path \(B\), joint continuity of local time and the occupation formula imply \[\varepsilon^{-1}\int_0^c
 \chi_{\{0<B_u<\varepsilon f_c(u)\}}\,du
 \longrightarrow V_c:=\int_0^c f_c(u)\,dL_u^0 .
 \tag{1}\] For completeness, a constant step height \(a\) on \((u,v]\) gives \(\varepsilon^{-1}\int_0^{\varepsilon a}
(L_v^x-L_u^x)\,dx\to a(L_v^0-L_u^0)\). Uniform upper and lower step approximations of the continuous \(f_c\) then prove (1).

We have \(V_1\ge(\sqrt3/2)L_{1/2}^0>0\) almost surely. Moreover \(V_2/V_1\) has unbounded support. For every \(\epsilon>0\), \[\mathbb P\{L_1^0\le\epsilon,\ L_{3/2}^0-L_1^0\ge1\}>0.\] The first condition has positive probability. Conditional on the finite position \(B_1\), there is positive probability to reach zero by time \(5/4\) and then accumulate at least one unit of local time by \(3/2\). On this event, \(V_1\le\epsilon\) and \(V_2\ge\sqrt7/2\).

Consequently \(R_r=m_2(r)/m_1(r)\) converges in law to \(V_2/V_1\). For any \(M<\infty\), Portmanteau gives \[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb P(R_{2^{-n}}>M)
 \ge \mathbb P(V_2/V_1>M)>0.\] The event \(R_{2^{-n}}>M\) infinitely often belongs to the Brownian germ sigma-field. Its probability is positive, hence one by the germ zero-one law (see [Retained latex: \cite[Corollary 2.2]{sznitman}]). Intersecting over positive integer \(M\) proves \[\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\mu_X(B(z_0,2^{1-n}))}
      {\mu_X(B(z_0,2^{-n}))}=\infty
 \quad\hbox{almost surely}.
 \tag{2}\] The eventual identification with \(m_c\) justifies the random terminal-time truncation. This proves failure of the actual measure hypothesis, not merely a missing citation.

Equality of a level local time with a gauge Hausdorff measure does not imply measure doubling, nor does approximating levels by dyadics. The upper mass estimate (6.2) is different: joint local-time Hölder continuity on a fixed compact time/height region does yield \(\mu_X(B(x,r))\le C_{\epsilon,X}r^{3/2-\epsilon}\) uniformly in \(x\). Thus the upper bound survives (2). A replacement for the modulus step is provided in Section 6 below.

# The bad-tile cover fails on actual Brownian paths

Problem 3 (Major: Proposition 6.2 proof, pages 41–43).  Assertion (1) in the proof, and the resulting covering estimate (6.5), are false almost surely for all sufficiently fine generations.

Put \[a_n=2^{-n},\qquad
 \delta_n=\frac{a_n}{2(n+1)\log2}.\] The set \(S'_n\) on page 42 consists of times \(t\le T_6\) for which the path stays within \(a_n\) of \(W_t\) for longer than \(\delta_n\).

In fact \(S'_n\) is eventually empty almost surely. Fix a deterministic \(T<\infty\). If such a time occurs in \([0,T]\), some deterministic grid interval of length \(\delta_n/2\) lies inside \([t,t+\delta_n]\). Relative to its left endpoint, the Brownian path on that grid interval stays within \(2a_n\). Split it into steps of length \(16a_n^2\). Each complete step must have increment at most \(4a_n\) in absolute value. These Gaussian increments are independent, so with \(p_0=\mathbb P(|N(0,1)|\le1)<1\), \[\mathbb P\{\exists t\le T:T_{\pm a_n}(t)>\delta_n\}
 \le
 \left(\frac{2T}{\delta_n}+3\right)
 p_0^{\lfloor\delta_n/(32a_n^2)\rfloor}
 \le C_T n2^n e^{-c2^n/n}.
 \tag{3}\] This is summable. Borel–Cantelli and a countable intersection over integer \(T\) cover the finite random \(T_6\).

Nevertheless bad tiles exist at every sufficiently fine level. Almost surely, Brownian maxima on all rational compact intervals are unique, interior, and have non-dyadic heights. After the first hit of \(1/2\), choose a rational interval before the path leaves \((0,1)\); it lies before \(T_6\). Its maximizer \(\tau\) has height \(h\in(0,1)\), not dyadic, and some \(\delta>0\) satisfies \[W_s<h\quad(0<|s-\tau|\le\delta).\] The tile \(E_n\) containing \((\tau,h)\) has a dyadic height band \((a_n^*,b_n^*]\) with \(b_n^*>h\). The construction on pages 39–41 makes each tile project onto its whole band, so it contains a point \((s_n,b_n^*)\). Necessarily \(|s_n-\tau|>\delta\). Hence \[\operatorname{diam}\pi_x(E_n)>\delta>
 \frac{2^{-n}}{n\log2}\] for all sufficiently large \(n\): these are bad tiles. They cannot meet the empty \(S'_n\). Likewise the left side of (6.5) counts squares meeting a nonempty bad tile, while its right side is zero. This is an actual counterexample to the covering step.

There is also a direct geometric error on page 43. Already the first upcrossing from zero to \(1/2\), after the height restriction, has time projection \(\{0<t\le T_{1/2}:W_t>0\}\). Immediate positive and negative oscillations give infinitely many components. It is not one interval or a union of two intervals; appending the final crossing piece does not remove the initial oscillations.

These facts do not disprove \(\dim_H A'\le1/2\) or \(\mu(A')=0\). The strict local maxima used above form a countable set, compatible with both estimates. They demonstrate that a new proof of those estimates is needed before the subsequent lemmas can use them. The range in Lemma 5.6 is \(2/3<\alpha\le2\), as confirmed on the source image; decreasing \(\alpha\) toward one is allowed. The difficulty here is the comparison of tiles with slow exit times, not that parameter range.

# Two further Brownian deductions need new arguments

## The compact-core selection

On page 46, compact subsets \(F_{n,j}\subset E_{n,j}\) are chosen only subject to \[\operatorname{diam}F_{n,j}\ge \operatorname{diam}E_{n,j}-128^{-n}.
 \tag{4}\] This does not ensure compatibility across generations, surviving branches, or the claimed almost-full vertical projection after taking infinite intersections.

A deterministic model shows the failure of this inference. Take \(E=(0,1]\) on a vertical line with its dyadic cells. In every level-\(n\) cell choose only the two points at distance \(\epsilon_n=2^{-(7n+2)}\) from its endpoints. Their diameter is \(2^{-n}-2\epsilon_n\ge2^{-n}-128^{-n}\), so (4) holds. Each such point has reduced dyadic denominator exactly \(2^{7n+2}\). The unions \(F_n\) for different \(n\) are therefore disjoint. Their liminf and all common tails are empty. This also defeats the intended tail version of \(K^N\), whose printed indexing on page 46 is inconsistent. It does not prove that no better Brownian compact-core construction exists.

## The averaging step uses unproved mass ratios

The proof on pages 48–49 copies the minimum-average selection used for uniform-fiber carpets, but Brownian child masses are not equal. The claimed monotonicity is not a formal consequence of minimum parent averages. Consider two parents in one height band: \[\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 &P_{\rm lower}&P_{\rm upper}&Q_{\rm lower}&Q_{\rm upper}\\
\hline
 \mu&1&99&9900&1\\
 \rho&100&0&2&2
\end{array}\] Parent \(P\) has average one, whereas \(Q\) has average two. Copying the selected \(P\)-average to the band gives integral \(10001\), below the original \(19902\). Refining \(P\) and copying its lower and upper child averages gives instead \[100(1+9900)+0(99+1)=990100,\] which exceeds the original integral. Multiplying all masses by a common constant does not repair this algebra. Adding one to the density and every copied average preserves the selection and the failed monotonicity, while ensuring admissibility when the fiber measures are probabilities.

This is a finite mass-tree countermodel to the inference, not an assertion that these four exact masses occur in the specified Brownian construction. A Brownian-specific relation between child masses, or a different selection/disintegration argument, is required. The carpet proof has exactly such an equal-mass relation; the source does not establish it here.

## Conditional steps that do work

If Proposition 6.2 were independently proved, (6.1) and \(\mu(A')=0\) would immediately give \(l^a(A')=0\) outside one Lebesgue-null set of heights. This is stronger than the half-mass claim in Remark 6.5 and avoids inferring a local-time estimate from an unweighted count of bad tiles.

For non-dyadic heights outside \(\pi_y(A')\), the selected nested fiber sections are compact and nonempty; their excluded time endpoints have dyadic heights. Their intersection gives a point of \(A\). At a point of \(A\), all sufficiently fine cells are good and have diameter at most \[2^{-n}\sqrt{1+(n\log2)^{-2}}\longrightarrow0.\] This supplies the eventual, rather than all-level, shrinking used for injectivity of \(\pi_y\) in Lemma 6.6. The Borel injective projection then has a Borel inverse on its image, so the pullback Lebesgue measure is meaningful. A good descendant with height \(h\in(r/3,2r/3]\) lies inside the radius-\(r\) ball and has \(\lambda_E\)-mass \(h\); this gives the local bound in Lemma 6.8. These conditional checks leave the bad-set estimate, compatible compact cores and positive modulus unresolved.

# A replacement modulus argument removes doubling

Remark 3.3 points toward [bhw]. Its Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.6 use upper regularity and a supremum density, without a doubling measure. The published global lower-radius hypotheses are not the pointwise hypotheses here. The following independent localization supplies the needed compact finite-measure version.

Assume \(X\) is compact, \(\mu(X)<\infty\), and \(\mu(B(x,r))\le C_0r^q\) uniformly for small \(r\). Suppose every \(E\) has conformal dimension at least one and, for each \(s>1\), a common \(c_s>0\) satisfies \(\lambda_E(B(x,r))\ge c_sr^s\) for \(x\in E\) at a possibly \(E,x\)-dependent small radius. If \(\operatorname{Mod}_p\{\lambda_E\}>0\) for \(1\le p<q\), then \(\dim_C X\ge q\).

Suppose otherwise and choose a quasisymmetry \(f\) and numbers \[\max(p,\dim_H f(X))<\alpha<q,\qquad
 0<t<1,\quad \dim_H f(X)<\alpha t,\qquad
 1<s<q/\alpha .\] Let \(E_N\) contain those points of \(E\) where the lower estimate holds for every \(r<1/N\). Since \(E=\bigcup_NE_N\) and \(\dim_H f(E)\ge1\), some \(E_N\) has dimension greater than \(t\). Stratify the family by pairs \((N,j)\) for which \(\mathcal H^t_{1/j}(f(E_N))\ge1\). It suffices to give each stratum zero \(p\)-modulus.

Fix such a stratum. Cover the union of its \(f(E_N)\) by image balls \(B_i'\), centered in that union, with radii \(\sigma_i\) and \(\sum_i\sigma_i^{\alpha t}<\varepsilon\). All radii may be arbitrarily small. If \(x_i\) is the preimage center and \(R_i=\sup\{d(x_i,x):f(x)\in B_i'\}\), take \(r_i=2R_i\) and \(B_i=B(x_i,r_i)\). A comparison with a point at distance at least \(R_i/2\) gives \[B_i'\subset f(B_i),\qquad
 f(10B_i)\subset B(f(x_i),\eta(40)\sigma_i).\] Isolated centers can instead use arbitrarily small singleton balls. Uniform continuity on the compact space makes \(2r_i<1/N\), \(r_i<1\), and \(2\eta(40)\sigma_i<1/j\). Define \[g=\sup_i\frac{\sigma_i^t}{r_i^s}\chi_{2B_i}.\] For a fixed \(E\), retain balls \(B_i\) meeting \(E_N\). A \(5r\) selection yields disjoint \(2B_j\) whose \(10B_j\) cover \(E_N\). At a good point in \(B_j\cap E_N\), \(B(x,r_j)\subset2B_j\), so \[\int g\,d\lambda_E
 \ge c_s\sum_j\sigma_j^t
 \ge c_s[2\eta(40)]^{-t}.\] Thus a fixed multiple of \(g\) is admissible. On the other hand, \[\int g^\alpha\,d\mu
 \le \sum_i\sigma_i^{\alpha t}r_i^{-\alpha s}\mu(2B_i)
 \le C_0 2^q\sum_i\sigma_i^{\alpha t}r_i^{q-\alpha s}
 \le C\varepsilon .\] Hölder on the finite measure space gives energy tending to zero also in exponent \(p\). Countable subadditivity over \((N,j)\) contradicts positive \(p\)-modulus.

The supporting checkpoint gives the additional finite-neighborhood localization for noncompact spaces. For the Brownian application, the compact upper mass bound is available. This repair therefore removes the doubling obstruction. It does not supply minimal vertical sets or positive modulus when the independent constructions of Sections 4–5 above remain unproved.

# The uniform-fiber carpet conclusions can be repaired

Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 1.4 have more structure than the general hierarchy theorem. The following direct checks provide a replacement for the false flatness step, together with several bounded local corrections.

For \(2\le\ell<m\) and \(k\) retained columns in each row, let \(q=1+\log_m k\). Give every child symbolic mass \(1/(k\ell)\). The vertical marginal is Lebesgue measure; at a non-\(\ell\)-adic height the horizontal conditional measure gives mass \(k^{-n}\) to each allowed \(n\)-prefix. Integrating its Frostman bound gives \(\mu(B(x,r))\le Cr^q\). For the reverse bound, fix an address of \(x\), its first \(n\) row digits, and its first \(p=\lfloor n\log\ell/\log m\rfloor\) horizontal choices. The resulting approximate cell has mass \(\ell^{-n}k^{-p}\) and diameter at most \(\sqrt{m^2+1}\ell^{-n}\). Choosing this scale below \(r\) gives \(\mu(B(x,r))\ge cr^q\). This repairs the axis alignment and boundary explanation on page 29, including \(k=1\), when mass may lie on a vertical grid boundary.

Each vertical set \(E\) has one child in every row. Its level-\(n\) cylinders have diameter between \(\ell^{-n}\) and \(\sqrt2\ell^{-n}\), and their adjacent relative gaps tend uniformly to zero. The cylinder-weight construction in Lemma 4.5 gives, for \(0<\alpha<1\), a probability \(\nu\) with \[\nu(f(E_{n,j}))\le C_\alpha
 \left(\frac{\operatorname{diam}f(E_{n,j})}{\operatorname{diam}f(E)}\right)^\alpha .
 \tag{5}\] The image scale factor must be retained. Symbolic probabilities give a rigorous measure: cylinder diameters shrink, individual codes have zero mass, and at most two codes meet at a boundary point.

Problem 4 (Major: the flatness step leading to Lemma 4.6, page 29).  The asserted flatness constant \(A\le2\) fails within the stated carpet class. Take \(\ell=2,m=3,k=2\), retaining the left and right columns in every row, and choose the left column at every stage. Then \(E=\{0\}\times[0,1]\) is an allowed vertical set with its dyadic hierarchy. In the ball whose height interval is \((1/3,1/2)\), infinitely many cylinders \[I_j=\left[\frac13+\frac1{6\cdot4^j},
           \frac13+\frac1{6\cdot4^{j-1}}\right],
 \quad j\ge2,\] lie inside the ball while their parents do not. Thus no finite flatness constant works. The page-29 deduction of (4.12) invokes this false premise and Theorem 3.4; it is then used in Lemma 4.6, Theorem 4.3 (the carpet conclusion of Theorem 1.2), and Corollary 1.4. This is a failure of that core derivation, not a numerical adjustment to the constant. A separate proof is required.

A fixed-generation cover supplies that replacement proof: for \(S=f^{-1}(B(f(x),r))\) put \(R=\sup_{y\in S}d(x,y)\), and choose \(R/\ell<\ell^{-n}\le R\). At most \(2\ell+2\) level-\(n\) cylinders meet \(S\). Comparison with a point of \(S\) at distance at least \(R/2\) shows that each selected image cylinder has diameter at most \(2\eta(2+2\sqrt2)r\). Summing (5) proves the required image ball bound. Singleton and large-radius cases follow from atomlessness and the root cylinder. Thus \(\dim_C E=1\), without Theorem 3.4.

There is also an exact positive-modulus argument. At every parent and row choose one of the \(k\) retained children uniformly and independently. This produces a random vertical set. For almost every height, its horizontal address has exactly the conditional law of \(\mu\), and hence \[\mathbb E_E\lambda_E=\mu,\qquad
 \int\rho\,d\mu=\mathbb E_E\int\rho\,d\lambda_E\ge1\] for every admissible nonnegative Borel density. The constant density one is admissible, so the modulus is exactly one; the printed strict inequality \(>1\) is false. The same calculation identifies why the minimum-average argument works here: all \(k\ell\) child masses are equal. Applying the repaired compact modulus criterion proves carpet minimality for \(k\ge2\). For \(k=1\), \(K=E\) and the preceding vertical-set proof handles the endpoint directly.

Finally, the three graph types on pages 31–33 have endpoint pairs \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\). The displayed blocks match adjacent column endpoints and define a contraction with vertical factor \(1/\ell\) on triples of continuous functions. Their fixed points are continuous graphs and retain \(k\) cells per row. Exact rational checks of both printed examples supplement this general argument.

The restriction \(1\le r<s\) in \(k=2^r,\ \ell=2^s\) yields only \(1+r/(r+s)<3/2\), contrary to the claimed density in \([1,2]\). Allow arbitrary positive \(r,s\); the construction still has \(m=k\ell>\ell\) and yields a dense subset of \((1,2)\). Use a constant function for dimension one. For dimension two, take \(r_i=i,s_i=1\). The final scaled union has domain \([0,1)\), so add \((1,0)\). Its piece heights are at most \(2^{-i}\), proving continuity at that endpoint. Hausdorff dimension is the supremum of the piece dimensions, while every quasisymmetry restricts to each minimal piece. This proves the corrected existence conclusion for every dimension in \([1,2]\), without assuming countable stability of conformal dimension. The false flatness step is classified separately as Major because it breaks the original core derivation. The scale, measure-boundary, strict-modulus, parameter-range and endpoint corrections are Minor with the explicit local repairs above. The replacement proof supports the specialized conclusions; it does not make the original flatness assertion true or complete the Brownian proof.

# Remaining evidentiary limits

The unresolved Brownian work is specific: replace the false bad-tile covering estimate; construct compatible compact cores with the claimed projection and geometric bounds; and prove positive modulus with the actual nonuniform local-time masses. A replacement for the doubling-dependent modulus step alone does not complete these tasks. No error is inferred from the conjectures in Section 7, which are presented as conjectures.
