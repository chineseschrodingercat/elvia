Abstract: The prescribed radial extension in Definition 4.15 need not be invertible under the stated amplitude restriction. An explicit smooth constant perturbation sends two distinct interior points to the same point, so the pullback used in Lemma 4.17 is undefined in part of its written range. A quantitative smallness condition repairs this auxiliary assertion and is compatible with the stronger local regime of the main proof. This is recorded as a Minor defect, without a disproof of the spectral conclusions. Independent calculations support the distinction between individual and complete-cluster stability, the cancellation of internal cluster denominators, and the stated scaling and heat-trace bounds.

Full reading is not a new proof of every cited compactness or free-boundary theorem. The explicit counterexample and the independent algebraic checks below delimit what was directly established.

For \(n\geq2\), the Dirichlet spectrum is defined for open sets of positive finite volume. Let \(B_\Omega\) be the ball of the same volume and \(T\) the torsional rigidity. Theorem 1.1, p. 5, controls each eigenvalue by the square root of \(T(\Omega)^{-1}-T(B_\Omega)^{-1}\). The linear estimate of Theorem 1.2, p. 6, requires that the ball eigenvalue be simple. Theorem 1.3, p. 6, instead controls the absolute value of the sum over a complete isolated ball cluster; it does not control the sum of absolute individual differences. The gap \(g_n(k)\) excludes equal ball eigenvalues and is capped at one. Its positivity for each fixed cluster is used; no uniform lower bound in \(k\) is supplied or assumed here.

# An overbroad invertibility assertion

Equation reference: eq:extension. Definition 4.15 and equation (32), p. 26, choose \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_+^*,[0,1])\) with \(\varphi=0\) on \([0,1/4]\), \(\varphi=1\) on \([1/2,3/2]\), and \(\varphi\) nondecreasing on \([0,1/2]\). They set \[
 \zeta^t(x)=x+t\varphi(|x|)h(x/|x|)\frac{x}{|x|}.\] The text declares this to be a diffeomorphism from \(B\) to \(B_{th}\) for every \(|t|\leq1\). Lemma 4.17, p. 27, assumes \(h\in C^{2,\gamma}(\partial B,[-1/2,1/2])\) and uses the corresponding vector field; its proof forms \((\zeta^t)^{-1}\), \((D\zeta^t)^{-1}\), and the Jacobian.

Problem 1 (P01: Minor; missing invertibility condition).  The amplitude bound on \(h\) does not make [eq:extension] (#eq:extension) injective. Consequently the stated diffeomorphism assertion is false, and this particular pullback is undefined for some allowed parameters. This does not itself refute the analytic spectral-branch conclusion of Lemma 4.17, which may use a valid extension.

Decisive example. Choose \(h\equiv-1/3\), \(t=9/10\), and any unit vector \(e\). Both \(e/5\) and \(e/2\) are interior points of \(B\). The prescribed values of \(\varphi\) give \[\zeta^{9/10}(e/5)=e/5
 =\left(\frac12-\frac9{10}\frac13\right)e
 =\zeta^{9/10}(e/2).\] The points are distinct. This calculation holds for every cutoff satisfying the written requirements, and \(h\) is smooth with values strictly inside the allowed interval. There is no endpoint ambiguity in \(t\). The geometric domains are nevertheless ordinary balls of radius \(1-t/3\), whose eigenvalues are \(\lambda_i(B)/(1-t/3)^2\). Thus this example specifically invalidates the chosen map, while providing counterevidence to any claim that spectral analyticity itself has failed. ◻

Even an implicit equal-volume and centering interpretation does not suffice to rescue the map. In dimension two take \[\rho(\theta)=\sqrt{17/18}+\frac13\cos(2\theta),
 \qquad h(\theta)=\rho(\theta)-1.\] The radial domain has area \(\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}\rho^2\,d\theta=\pi\), is centrally symmetric, and has \(-1/2<h<1/2\). At \(\theta=\pi/2\), write \(h_*=\sqrt{17/18}-4/3\). Then \(r_1=1/2+(9/10)h_*\in(0,1/4)\). The two points \(r_1e\) and \(e/2\) again have the same image under \(\zeta^{9/10}\). This example is not asserted to satisfy any additionally prescribed arbitrarily small \(L^1\) threshold or \(C^{3,\gamma}\) bound.

## Repair and impact

Equation reference: eq:repair. Let \(M=\|\varphi'\|_\infty\). Adding, for example, \[
 M\|h\|_\infty\leq\frac12\] gives a direct repair. Along each ray the new radius is \(R_t(r,\theta)=r+t\varphi(r)h(\theta)\), and \[\partial_rR_t=1+t\varphi'(r)h(\theta)\geq\frac12
 \quad (|t|\leq1).\] It is the identity near the origin, remains positive, preserves the angular direction, and is strictly increasing on every ray. Its Jacobian is \((R_t/r)^{n-1}\partial_rR_t>0\). These facts prove injectivity and the required regular inverse, with the boundary mapped to \(1+th\).

The dependency is equation (32), then the analytic pullback in Lemma 4.17, then the second-variation estimates in Propositions 4.18 and 5.20, and the local minimality arguments in Propositions 4.16 and 5.19. The actual main applications, pp. 29, 31 and 46–48, have both a fixed \(C^{3,\gamma}\) bound and a sufficiently small \(L^1\) norm. Interpolation then makes \(\|h\|_\infty\) small enough for [eq:repair] (#eq:repair) after reducing the dimensional constant. The cutoff is fixed, so this does not introduce a new \(k\)-dependent exponent. The examples above are not counterexamples in that arbitrarily small regime. This local repair and the stronger operative hypotheses justify the Minor classification.

# Independent mathematical checks

## Multiplicity and planar sharpness

The area-preserving ellipse with semiaxes \(a=e^\varepsilon\), \(b=e^{-\varepsilon}\) satisfies \[T(E_\varepsilon)=\frac{\pi}{8\cosh(2\varepsilon)},\qquad
 T(E_\varepsilon)^{-1}-T(B)^{-1}
 =\frac{16}{\pi}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4).\] This follows directly by integrating its quadratic torsion function. Its first boundary normal velocity is \(\cos(2\theta)\). For the first double disk eigenvalue \(\lambda=j_{1,1}^2\), normalization and radial dilation give squared normal-derivative factor \(2\lambda/\pi\). The Hadamard matrix is therefore \[-\frac{2\lambda}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(2\theta)
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \cos^2\theta&\cos\theta\sin\theta\\
 \cos\theta\sin\theta&\sin^2\theta
 \end{pmatrix}d\theta
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}-\lambda&0\\0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}.\] The individual branches have a nonzero linear splitting, whereas their full-cluster trace has zero first derivative. The angular integrals, torsion expansion and volume constraint were independently verified symbolically. This supports the need for a square-root estimate on individual multiple eigenvalues and a linear estimate only after complete-cluster cancellation. It is not a proof of all-index sharpness in every dimension. Proposition 6.1, pp. 48–49, makes its explicit sharpness statement in dimension two; that limitation is retained.

## Internal cluster cancellation

For the orthonormal analytic eigenbasis in Proposition 5.20, pp. 47–48, let \(C_{ij}=\langle u_i',u_j\rangle\). Differentiating orthonormality gives \(C_{ij}=-C_{ji}\). Hence, for a complete cluster \(I\), \[\sum_{i,j\in I}(\mu_j-\mu_i)C_{ij}^2=0.\] Writing \(v_i=u_i'\), \(z_i\) for its harmonic boundary extension and \(w_i=v_i-z_i\), the off-diagonal coefficient identity is \((\mu_j-\mu_i)v_{ij}=\mu_jz_{ij}\). It yields \[\mu_jw_{ij}^2-\mu_iv_{ij}^2
 =\mu_jz_{ij}^2-(\mu_j-\mu_i)v_{ij}^2.\] The last term cancels after summation. These identities were independently checked with a symbolic skew matrix, including coincident eigenvalues. No division by an internal cluster gap is required. Separation from the exterior spectrum remains necessary.

## Scaling, infinite sums and a nonlinear corollary

Under dilation by \(r\), eigenvalues scale by \(r^{-2}\) and torsional rigidity by \(r^{n+2}\). The factor \(\lambda_1^{1/2}|\Omega|^{1/2}
(T^{-1}-T(B_\Omega)^{-1})^{1/2}\) thus has exactly eigenvalue scaling. The linear estimates’ factor \(|\Omega|(T^{-1}-T(B_\Omega)^{-1})\) does too.

For the summability condition in Proposition 6.3, pp. 50–52, comparison with \(\sum i^a e^{-cti^{2/n}}\) gives small-\(t\) order \(t^{-(a+1)n/2}\). The first derivative weight for the heat trace has order \(t^{-4n-3}\); the second derivative weight has order \(t^{-7n/2-3}\). The latter is dominated by the former for \(n\geq2\) and \(0<t\leq1\); exponential decay handles large \(t\). These independently derived exponents support the final envelope without endorsing every intermediate printed simplification.

For Corollary 1.6 and its proof on p. 49, minimality of \(T^{-1}-\delta\lambda_1\) gives \(T(B)/T(\Omega)\geq1+\delta T(B)(\lambda_1(\Omega)-\lambda_1(B))\). Bernoulli’s inequality for \(p\geq1\) then gives the stated reverse Kohler–Jobin conclusion once \(p\delta T(B)\lambda_1(B)\geq1\). A sufficiently large dimensional \(p>1\) is consistent with the allowed small \(\delta\). No explicit optimal value of that exponent was established.

# Operative assumptions and remaining limits

The main cluster proof retains the smallness \(|\delta|\ll k^{-6-10/n}g_n(k)\). In particular it implies the stronger-than-coarse spectral-separation condition of Lemma 5.6, p. 34, when that lemma is invoked in the viscosity construction on p. 35. The latter is not read as dispensing with its own prerequisites. The vector regularity argument’s condition \(L^2m^2\sqrt{|\delta|}\leq1/8\) is likewise consistent with \(m\lesssim k\) and the main smallness after a dimensional constant is reduced. These checks trace conditions in the actual application; they do not assert a counterexample to an isolated lemma under weaker assumptions.

The revised regularity proof in Lemma 4.14, pp. 25–26, explicitly introduces a positive harmonic auxiliary denominator. In the acquired arXiv version of its reference [22], De Silva–Savin’s Theorem 2.4 [dss], p. 5, requires a positive homogeneous solution as denominator, a \(C^{1,\alpha}\) boundary (as set up on p. 2), and a numerator with \(C^\alpha\) right-hand side. The numerator need not be positive. The harmonic auxiliary construction matches this requirement; one may use signed ratios and square them in the free-boundary condition. The higher-order version on p. 6 provides the corresponding bootstrap framework. Reference pp. 1–6 were read, with the hypothesis and theorem pages visually checked. The 2015 journal PDF was not acquired: its official download returned an empty body, so the retained nine-page arXiv:1403.2588v1 is identified explicitly rather than certified equivalent to the journal text.

For Theorem 1.5, the equality of first derivatives of \(F\) is required within each complete ball cluster at the ball spectrum; global permutation symmetry is not silently assumed. The last index must end a cluster. For the infinite spectral applications, the weighted derivative summability is an essential hypothesis, not a consequence of arbitrary smoothness of \(f\).
