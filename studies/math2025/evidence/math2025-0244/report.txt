Abstract: The initial assessment is Major. The second branch of Proposition 5.8 admits a counterexample to its claimed uniform constant. Its use in Theorem 3.11 occurs in a truncation argument that also fails a direct feasibility check. Independently, Theorems 3.17 and 4.6 require an explicit choice of the numerical truncation tolerance: their literal inherited quantifiers admit estimators contradicting the stated rates. Local construction and calculation errors are documented separately. These findings do not establish that the general recovery theorem 3.5, or the intended Radon results after correction, are false. Scientific decision and adjudication remain pending; there are no human reviews in this package.

# F1. Truncation constants and feasibility: Major

## A counterexample to Proposition 5.8, second branch

On p. 34, condition (30) is \[m\ge \left\lVert G_1^{-1}\right\rVert^2\left\lVert G_2^{-1}\right\rVert^2\left\lVert G_2\right\rVert^2
       \log(1/\gamma).\] For the second branch, only \(\left\lVert G_1^{-1}\right\rVert\ge c\) is required. The conclusion claims a constant \(C_1\) depending only on \(C_F,c\): \[\left\lVert G_2^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert QAP_\Lambda^\perp x^\dagger\right\rVert
 \le C_1c_\nu^{-1/2}
 \bigl(\left\lVert F\Phi^*\iota_{\Gamma\setminus\Lambda}\right\rVert\left\lVert G_2^{-1}\right\rVert+1\bigr)r.
\tag{F1.1}\] Let \(H_1=\mathbb C^2\), \(H_2=\mathbb C\), \(\Phi=I\), \(\Lambda=\{1\}\), and \(D=\{0,1\}\). Give \(\mu=\nu\) mass \(\eta\) at \(1\), where \(0<\eta<1\), and define \[F_t(x_1,x_2)=x_1+t x_2.\] All relevant assumptions hold: \(f_\nu=c_\nu=1\), \(Q=I\), \(B=\omega_i=1\), \(\sup_t\left\lVert F_t\right\rVert\le\sqrt2\), \(\left\lVert F\right\rVert\le\sqrt2\), and the actual retained matrix is \(G=1\). Set \(x^\dagger=e_2\), \(r=1\), \(\beta=0\), and \[G_1=1,\qquad G_2=\sqrt\eta,\qquad c=1,\qquad
\gamma=\eta/2.\] The tail operator norm is \(\sqrt\eta\). Take \(m=\lceil L\log(2/\eta)\rceil\) for any fixed sufficiently large \(L\ge1\). This satisfies (30), and can also satisfy the sample condition of Theorem 3.5 if that condition is included in the phrase setting of Theorem 3.5.

For \(N=\sum_{k=1}^m t_k\), one has \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,\eta)\) and \(\left\lVert QAP_\Lambda^\perp x^\dagger\right\rVert=\sqrt{N/m}\). Thus (F1.1) is equivalent to \[\sqrt{N/m}\le 2C_1\sqrt\eta.
\tag{F1.2}\] For any fixed candidate \(C_1=C_1(\sqrt2,1)\), choose \(\eta\) so small that \(4C_1^2m\eta<1\). This is possible since \(m\eta\to0\). Every event \(N\ge1\) violates (F1.2), yet \[\Pr(N\ge1)=1-(1-\eta)^m\ge\eta>\gamma.\] Consequently no constant with the stated dependence exists.

For numerical corroboration, \(\eta=2^{-20}\) and \(m=21\) give \(\gamma=4.76837158203125\cdot10^{-7}\) and \(\Pr(N\ge1)=2.002696965179746\cdot10^{-5}\). This instance defeats \(C_1<111.72756\); the family above, rather than that one numerical threshold, rules out every finite uniform constant.

## Exact failure and repair boundary

The last absorption in the second branch on p. 36 is invalid. Write \(\kappa_i=\left\lVert G_i^{-1}\right\rVert\), \(c_F=\left\lVert F\Phi^*\iota_{\Gamma\setminus\Lambda}\right\rVert\), and \(d=\kappa_1\left\lVert G_2\right\rVert\). The preceding Bernstein estimate has terms of the form \[c_\nu^{-1}r^2\left[
(c_F\kappa_2)^2+\frac{CC_F^2}{d^2}
+\frac{\sqrt C\,C_Fc_F\kappa_2}{d}\right].\] A lower bound on \(\kappa_1\) alone does not control \(d^{-1}\). Replacing the second condition by \[\left\lVert G_1^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert G_2\right\rVert\ge c_*>0\] does allow the required absorption, with a constant depending on \(C_F,c_*\). The first branch \(G_1=G_2\) automatically satisfies the product bound with \(c_*=1\). This is decisive counterevidence against extending this counterexample to Theorem 3.5, which uses the first branch (p. 37).

Theorem 3.11 invokes the second branch with \(G_1=GD\) and \(G_2=GW^\zeta\) on p. 40. There are further, independently checkable issues in that application. Page 39 extends \(W\) by \(1\) outside \(\Lambda_{j_0}\), while p. 40 asserts \[\left\lVert P_{\Lambda_{j_0}}^\perp W^{-\zeta}x^\dagger\right\rVert
 \le 2^{-\zeta bj_0}r.\] A unit tail vector has left side \(1\) under that extension, so this inequality fails when \(r=1\), \(\zeta bj_0>0\). Also, bounding \(\left\lVert F\widetilde W^\zeta\right\rVert\) does not bound \(\sup_t\left\lVert F_t\widetilde W^\zeta\right\rVert\), which is required by the truncation argument.

A direct model shows that the feasibility concern is substantive. Let \(a=2^{-k}\), \(\eta=a^2/4\), and \[F_t(x_1,x_2)=a x_1+t x_2,\qquad \Pr(t=1)=\eta.\] Put the two basis vectors at scales \(k,k+1\), and take \(b=1\), \(j_0=k\). Relative to \(D_0=\operatorname{diag}(a,a/2)\), the Gram matrix is \[D_0^{-1}F^*FD_0^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&a/2\\a/2&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Thus uniform quasi-diagonalization constants \(1/2,3/2\) and \(C_F=\sqrt2\) suffice. Take \(d_{\rm retained}=2^k\), \(d_{\rm tail}=1\), \(\omega=1\), \(\zeta=1\), \(x^\dagger=e_2\), \(r=1\), \(\beta=0\). The stated sample condition permits \(m=O(k)\) for \(\gamma=\eta/2\). On \(N=1\), the smallest empirical residual over the retained coefficient is \[\min_x\left(\frac1m\sum_{\ell=1}^m|ax-t_\ell|^2\right)^{1/2}
 =\frac{\sqrt{m-1}}m.\] For any fixed \(C_3\), this eventually exceeds the allowed radius \(C_3a\). Moreover \(\Pr(N=1)=m\eta(1-\eta)^{m-1}>\gamma\) for large \(k\). One can add a deterministic orthogonal measurement channel at every other scale; using the harmless coherence bound \(B=2\) gives \(\tau=4\) and still \(m=O(k)\).

This disproves the high-probability feasibility step, not an error inequality conditional on an existing minimizer. It does not by itself disprove the particular Radon instance. Repair requires suitable pointwise tail control or altered sample/tolerance hypotheses, in addition to a corrected Proposition 5.8. The issue is Major because it breaks an actual proof dependency and the proposed reconstruction program.

# F2. The numerical truncation tolerance in the rate theorems: Major

Theorem 3.11 (p. 20) allows a supplied \(r\) with \(\left\lVert P_{\Lambda_{j_0}}^\perp x^\dagger\right\rVert\le r\). Program (12), p. 22, uses the numerical radius \(\beta+C_3 2^{-bj_0}r\). Theorem 3.17 (pp. 23–24) adds the existence of a sharper tail bound \(C2^{-aj}\) but does not prescribe replacing \(r\) in that program. A smaller available bound does not change an estimator already defined using a larger one.

Here is a deterministic counterexample to the literal inherited quantifiers. Let \(D\) be a singleton of probability one and \(H_1=H_2=\ell^2(\Gamma)\), with \(\Gamma=\{(j,n):j\ge0,\ 1\le n\le2^j\}\). Set \(\Phi=I\) and \[F_t=A,\qquad Ae_{j,n}=2^{-j}e_{j,n}.\] Choose \(b=d=1\), \(d_{j,n}=2^j\), \(B=\omega_{j,n}=f_\nu=c_\nu=1\). Quasi-diagonalization is exact, \(C_F=1\), and \(|\Lambda_j|=2^{j+1}-1\le2\,2^j\). The signal \(x^\dagger=e_{0,1}\) satisfies the added assumptions with \(a=2\), \(p=1\), \(c=1\), \(C=2\). All its tails and best \(s\)-term errors for \(s\ge1\) are zero. Nevertheless \(r=1\) is permitted. Use exact data; it satisfies the noise bound for every \(\beta>0\).

At scale \(J\), (12) reduces to \[\min\left\lVert x\right\rVert_1\quad\hbox{subject to}\quad
\left\lVert Ax-e_{0,1}\right\rVert_2\le\eta_J,\qquad
\eta_J=\beta+C_3 2^{-J}.\] For \(0<\eta_J<1\) the unique minimizer is \((1-\eta_J)e_{0,1}\). The first coordinate of the constraint forces \(|x_{0,1}|\ge1-\eta_J\); equality in the objective forces every other coordinate to vanish. Hence the error is exactly \(\eta_J\), independently of \(m\).

With dyadic logarithms, take \(\beta_J=2^{-3J}\), so \(j_0=J\). The prescribed sample scale is \(m\asymp\beta_J^{-4/3}\log^4(1/\beta_J)\), or one may take larger \(m\). The asserted rate is \(O(2^{-2J})\), whereas the actual error is at least \(C_3 2^{-J}\). No fixed constant reconciles them. For natural logarithms, take \(\beta_J=e^{-3J}\) instead: the asserted rate is \(O(e^{-2J})\), and the same lower bound still contradicts it. The objection therefore does not depend on choosing between these conventional logarithm bases.

The same problem occurs in Theorem 4.6 (p. 28), which inherits (16) from pp. 26–27. For a correctly constructed orthonormal wavelet basis, choose a normalized atom \(\phi_i\) supported strictly inside \(B_1\), and set \(u^\dagger=\phi_i\), \(a=p=1\), \(r=1\). A fixed \(C\) gives the required tail bounds at all scales. Let \(B_0=\sup_\theta\left\lVert F_\theta\phi_i\right\rVert>0\) and \[\delta=\frac{\beta+C_3 2^{-J/2}}{2B_0}<1.\] The competitor \((1-\delta)\phi_i\) is feasible for every angle sample set. Every minimizer has \(\ell^1\) coefficient norm at most \(1-\delta\), so its \(i\)th coefficient differs from \(1\) by at least \(\delta\). Its error is therefore at least \(\delta\). For dyadic \(\beta_J=2^{-3J/2}\), the claimed large-\(m\) rate is \(O(2^{-J})\), while this lower bound is of order \(2^{-J/2}\).

#### Counterevidence and repair.

The proof on p. 41 clearly uses \(r\) of order \(2^{-aj_0}\). Explicitly prescribing that numerical value in the program, or requiring the supplied \(r\) to satisfy that bound, removes these counterexamples. The earlier estimates retaining \(+r\) are not contradicted. The defect is Major because the stated rates fail for an admissible estimator under the inherited quantifier, even with exact data and arbitrarily many samples.

There is a separate algebraic discrepancy in the same rate statements. Page 41 gives \(\widetilde m\gtrsim m/\log^4(1/\beta)\) and an error term proportional to \(\widetilde m^{-p}\). Substitution yields \(\log^{4p}(1/\beta)\), whereas pp. 24 and 28 print \(\log^{4/p}(1/\beta)\). For \(p\le1\) the printed factor is no smaller; for \(p>1\) the stronger printed factor is not obtained by this proof. No estimator lower bound disproving that stronger logarithmic rate has been established here. The consistent correction is \(4p\), or a new proof is needed. The counterexamples above use \(p=1\) and are independent of this discrepancy.

# Further confirmed local issues

## F3. Wavelet scale indices: Minor

Page 12 defines \(\mathbb N\) as the positive integers. Page 25 defines \(\psi_{j,n}=D^1_{j-1}\tau_n\psi\) only for \(j\ge1\). The tensor prototypes on p. 26 use the undefined \(\psi_{0,n}\) and then apply \(D_j^2\).

Extending the preceding formula to zero does not repair the construction. For the regular compact orthonormal multiresolution system intended there, write \(\psi(t)=\sqrt2\sum_k g_k\chi(2t-k)\). That extension gives \[\psi_{0,m}(t)=\sum_k g_k\chi(t-2m-k).\] Some \(g_k\ne0\), hence \(\langle \chi_k,\psi_{0,0}\rangle\ne0\). The printed type-1 and type-3 tensors \(D_j^2(\psi_{0,0}\otimes\chi_k)\) and \(D_j^2(\psi_{0,0}\otimes\psi_{0,0})\) have nonzero inner product, despite both having norm one. For large \(j\), their compact supports lie in \(B_1\). This uses the regular refinement identity, not an inadmissible Haar substitution.

Use prototypes \(\tau_n\psi=\psi_{1,n}\) and dilations \(D_{j-1}^2\) for \(j\ge1\), keeping scaling atoms at level zero. The Minor classification reflects that the abstract hypothesis remains orthonormality, supplied by the standard corrected construction; it does not treat every locally repairable core defect as Minor.

## F4. Support area in Lemma 4.2: Minor

On p. 25, the uniform fixed-angle operator bound is said to depend only on \(|K|\). Under the usual area meaning this is false. Take \[K_L=[0,L]\times[0,L^{-1}],\qquad u_L=\mathbf1_{K_L}.\] Then \(|K_L|=\left\lVert u_L\right\rVert_2=1\), but \(R_{\pi/2}u_L=L\mathbf1_{[0,L^{-1}]}\) and \(\left\lVert R_{\pi/2}u_L\right\rVert_2=\sqrt L\). A maximal chord length, or \(\operatorname{diam}(K)\), supplies the bound by Cauchy–Schwarz. If diameter was intended by \(|K|\), that notation needs a definition. The application fixes its compact support through the wavelet basis, so this example does not refute its boundedness.

## F5. Fan-beam Jacobian: Minor

The calculation on p. 43 inserts \(t\) and \(1/t\) into an integral over \(t\in\mathbb R\). For \(x=\rho e_\theta+t e_{\theta+\alpha}\in B_d\), \(\rho>d\), and \(|\alpha|<\pi/2\), one has \(t=(x\cdot e_\theta-\rho)/\cos\alpha<0\). The Jacobian is \(|t|\); for a normalized atom the displayed integral with weight \(t\) equals \(-1\), not \(1\). The corrected proof is \[\left\lVert D_\theta\phi_{j,n}\right\rVert_2^2
\le C2^{-j}\int|\phi_{j,n}|^2\,dt\,d\alpha
\le \frac{C2^{-j}}{\rho-d}
       \int|\phi_{j,n}|^2|t|\,dt\,d\alpha
=\frac{C2^{-j}}{\rho-d}.\] Thus the stated coherence scale is unchanged. The defect is the displayed sign step, not the resulting bound.

## F6. One parameter display in Theorem 3.5’s proof: Minor

Page 36 prints \(\lambda=C_0\left\lVert G^{-1}\right\rVert^2\left\lVert G\right\rVert\,s\). Theorem 5.6 on p. 32, at \(\delta=1/2\), \(\rho'=1/3\), requires \(\lambda\ge135\left\lVert G^{-1}\right\rVert^2\left\lVert G\right\rVert^2s\). For \(G=aI\) this threshold is \(135s\), whereas the former with \(C_0=135\) is \(135s/a\). The omitted square must be restored. The final \(\tau=B^2\left\lVert G^{-1}\right\rVert^4\left\lVert G\right\rVert^2s\) in Theorem 3.5 is already consistent with the corrected choice, so this display mismatch does not disprove that theorem.

# Positive checks and remaining limits

The normalization of \(A\) includes \(m^{-1/2}\), and \(Q\) removes the sampling density: \(\mathbb E[(QA)^*(QA)]=G^2\). The checked restricted-isometry to null-space parameters produce the stated norm powers in Theorem 3.5. Its fixed-signal, nonuniform nature and uniform \(r=0\) exception are acknowledged on p. 17.

For Radon data, one angle returns an entire detector function, not a scalar. Page 11 identifies the semi-discrete model and leaves detector-coordinate sampling open. The exact-recovery assertion on p. 28 requires \(r=\beta=0\) and a sparse finite expansion. For a corrected orthonormal basis, substituting \(b=1/2\) and \(d_{j,n}=2^{j/2}\) gives \(\tau\asymp2^{j_0}s\) for the unweighted program and \(\tau\asymp s\) for the weighted program. These checks constrain the adverse findings.
