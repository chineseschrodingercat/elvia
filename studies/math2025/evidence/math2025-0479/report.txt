Abstract: The slicing argument for bounded regular sets has the expected dimension, radius, and component-count structure. A finite counterexample nevertheless disproves the uniform dependence on the sub-Gaussian parameter in the polynomial-sketching corollary. Independent corrections concern rational-map domains, normalized slices, a zero-inequality endpoint, tubular radii, and several application formulas. The assessment is Major because the distributional counterexample invalidates an advertised dimension-reduction guarantee. It does not refute the deterministic covering theorem or the fixed-Gaussian case.

Author: AI-assisted technical review

Title: Covering numbers, regularity hypotheses, and the distributional scope of sketching

# Source, scope, and the deterministic result

This review concerns Y. Zhang and J. Kileel, Covering Number of Real Algebraic Varieties and Beyond: Improved Bounds and Applications, arXiv:2311.05116v4, 6 June 2025 [paper]. The associated journal DOI is 10.1007/s10208-025-09735-5. The acquired source is the 38-page preprint, not a verified copy of the journal-final text. Page numbers below are one-based PDF pages, which coincide with its printed numbering. All pages were read in text; the proofs, counterexamples, and source passages discussed below received targeted inspection. This is not a verification of every cited external theorem or every generalization claim.

Definition 2.1, p. 5, requires at most \(K\) path components in almost every affine slice of codimension at most \(n\), and empty slices almost everywhere above that codimension. Codimension zero therefore bounds the number of components of \(V\) itself. Theorem 2.6, p. 6, asserts, for a bounded regular set with \([V]_N\le t\) and \(0<\varepsilon\le\operatorname{diam}V\), \[\mathcal N(V,\varepsilon)
 \le 2K\left(\frac{2tnN^{3/2}}{\varepsilon}\right)^n
 \qquad (n\ge1).\] For \(n=0\), Lemma 2.3 gives the direct bound \(\mathcal N\le K\).

The proof on pp. 7–9 was checked at its essential transitions. The closure bar in Definition 2.12 matters: a type-I cut component is already a component of \(V\), since a path leaving it would first meet a slicing plane in its closure and in \(V\). A type-II component lies within one cell diameter of an appropriate slice point. Slice nets can consequently be enlarged by that diameter. The induction product satisfies \[\prod_{j=1}^{n}(N-n+j+1)\le \tfrac32N^n
 \quad(1\le n\le N,\ N\ge2),\] and the auxiliary dilation can be chosen close enough to one. The interval test and the scaling \(t/\varepsilon\) are consistent. These checks supply no counterexample to the principal covering estimate.

Appendix A, p. 31, should use a second Fubini step to obtain almost every translation and almost every slice direction; the printed simultaneous assertion for all good directions is stronger than the preceding step establishes. The almost-everywhere statement is sufficient for Lemma 2.2. No stronger all-direction claim is needed in the covering argument.

# Failure of a uniform sub-Gaussian sketching guarantee

Problem 1 (Major: Corollary 3.13(26)).  The sufficient row count on p. 19 is false uniformly over the sub-Gaussian ensembles permitted by Definition 3.6, p. 15.

The paper defines \(S=G/\sqrt m\), with independent, mean-zero, unit-variance entries of \(G\) having \(\psi_2\)-norm at most \(\alpha\). Its convention on p. 5 makes \(c\) an absolute constant. Set \[n=N=d=2,\qquad p(x)=x,\qquad
 \varepsilon=\delta=\tfrac12.\] The identity satisfies the degree-at-most-two assumption, and \(N'=\min\{N,n^d\}=2\). Equation (26) then claims sufficiency of \(m\ge C\alpha^2\), where \(C=28c\log2\).

For any proposed absolute \(c>0\), choose the finite number \(q=\exp(-8C-8)\). Let every entry of \(G\) be an independent copy of \[Z=\begin{cases}
 q^{-1/2},&\text{with probability }q/2,\\
 -q^{-1/2},&\text{with probability }q/2,\\
 0,&\text{with probability }1-q.
 \end{cases}\] Its mean is zero and its variance is one. For the Orlicz norm specified in footnote 9, \[\alpha^2=\frac{1}{q\log(1+1/q)},\qquad
 \mathbb E\exp(Z^2/\alpha^2)
   =1-q+q(1+1/q)=2.\] Thus this is an allowed ensemble, including the exact norm normalization. Put \(m=\lceil C\alpha^2\rceil\). The printed row-count condition holds.

Uniform norm preservation on \(p(\mathbb R^2)=\mathbb R^2\) requires a nonzero first column: if that column vanishes, \(Se_1=0\) although \(\|e_1\|=1\). The union bound gives \[\begin{split}
 \mathbb P(\text{success})
 &\le \mathbb P(\text{some entry of the first column is nonzero})\\
 &\le mq
 \le \frac{C}{\log(1+1/q)}+q
 <\tfrac18+e^{-8}<\tfrac12.
 \end{split}\] This contradicts the stated success probability \(1-\delta=1/2\). The calculation is exact and finite; it uses no simulation.

The affected dependency is the passage from the geometric covering/width bound to a uniform probabilistic embedding bound with the displayed ensemble dependence. This corollary is a substantive application of the paper, so a failure of its stated distributional scope warrants Major even though the deterministic core survives. For the sparse family above, merely avoiding an empty column at fixed confidence requires order \(q^{-1}\) rows, which has order \(\alpha^2\log\alpha\) as \(q\) decreases. This is a necessary obstruction, not a complete replacement theorem.

A correction must either restrict the ensemble or justify a sufficient dependence on its distributional parameter. The witness does not refute a fixed-Gaussian or uniformly bounded \(\alpha\) formulation, nor does it address the separate SORS claim. The final bound in Theorem 3.12(18), p. 18, retains a logarithmic \(\alpha\)-dependence, so its failure does not follow from this example.

# Regularity lemmas: signs, domains, and an endpoint

Problem 2 (Minor: squared normalized-slice equations).  In Lemma 2.15(c), pp. 10–11, the polynomial equations describe a larger set than the asserted preimage of an affine slice.

Take \(p(t)=(t,1)\) and \(L=\{y:y_1=1/\sqrt2\}\). The squared equation and the nonzero constraint admit \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\), both with \(w=1/2\), whereas \(\pi(p(-1))=(-1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)\notin L\). The same sign loss occurs in Lemma 2.16(c), p. 32.

The component bound is repairable without increasing the stated constant. On each path component of the larger algebraic set, \(\langle p(x),e_i\rangle/\|p(x)\|\) is continuous and takes values in \(\{\langle u,e_i\rangle,-\langle u,e_i\rangle\}\). It is therefore constant in each required sign. The unsquared preimage is a union of entire path components. Its component count is no greater than the POMT bound for the larger set. The false equality needs this replacement; the example does not disprove the normalized-image regularity conclusion.

Problem 3 (Minor: arbitrary open dense rational domains).  Lemma 2.17, p. 11 and p. 32, is false for all domains allowed by Definition 3.15, p. 21.

Let \(n=N=d=1\), \[U=\mathbb R\setminus\{1,\ldots,9\},\qquad f(x)=x/1\quad(x\in U).\] The domain is open dense and even Zariski open. The indicated ratio has degree at most one. But \(\operatorname{Im}f=U\) has ten path components, exceeding the asserted \(K=(2Nd+1)^{n+1}=9\) already in the codimension-zero slice.

The proof’s denominator equation becomes \(w=1\), so its algebraic set includes every real \(x\) and does not encode the extra deleted points. Requiring the full domain on which the displayed denominators are nonzero repairs that identification; alternatively, additional domain restrictions need their own complexity bounds. This counterexample concerns the lemma’s quantifier. It does not establish failure of a neural-network entropy bound: a restricted evaluation image may instead be covered through a suitable larger image. Such a replacement must be stated and justified.

Problem 4 (Minor: zero inequality constraints).  The minimum in Lemma 2.18, pp. 11 and 32–33, contains \((cb^2)^N\), although \(b=0\) is explicitly used for varieties.

For \(U=\{x:x^2=0\}\subset\mathbb R\), the number of inequality constraints is zero and \(U\) has one component. The displayed minimum is zero and hence gives \(K=0\), which is impossible. Use the POMT branch at \(b=0\) and restrict the \((cb^2)^N\) branch to \(b\ge1\), or replace \(b\) by \(\max\{1,b\}\) there with an appropriate constant. This corrects the endpoint and leaves the ordinary variety bound available.

# Radius and distortion corrections

Problem 5 (Minor: radius range in Corollary 2.19).  The tube-volume bound on p. 11 omits the range \(0<\varepsilon\le\operatorname{diam}V\) needed for the invoked covering estimate.

For \(V=[-1,1]\subset\mathbb R\) and \(K=n=N=t=1\), the tube has length \(2+2\varepsilon\), while the final printed right-hand side is the constant \(4c\). It cannot hold for all positive radii. The first inequality, obtained by covering the tube with balls of radius \(2\varepsilon\), remains valid.

One may retain the theorem’s radius range. For a nonempty set and \(n\ge1\), a valid all-radius form is \[\operatorname{Vol}(T(V,\varepsilon))
 \le \operatorname{Vol}(B^N)(2\varepsilon)^N
 \max\!\left\{1,\,
 2K\left(\frac{2tnN^{3/2}}{\varepsilon}\right)^n\right\}.\] For radii larger than the diameter, the one-point net proves the first branch. This repair does not challenge Theorem 2.6; it is also not, by itself, a counterexample to the separate probability statement of Corollary 2.20.

Problem 6 (Minor: a vanishing logarithmic complexity term).  There is a separate low-dimensional endpoint failure in Corollary 3.13(26), p. 19.

Set \(n=d=1\), \(N=2\), and \(p(x)=(x,1)\). Then \(N'=1\), so \(n\log(ndN')=0\). Fix \(\varepsilon=1/2\) and any fixed allowed ensemble parameter \(\alpha\). For any proposed constant \(c>0\), choosing \(\delta=\exp[-1/(8c\alpha^2)]\) makes the stated lower bound on \(m\) equal to \(1/2\), so \(m=1\) is allowed. Every row \(S=(a,b)\) nevertheless fails: if \(a\ne0\), take \(x=-b/a\); if \(a=0\), take \(|x|\) sufficiently large. The success probability is zero, contrary to \(1-\delta>0\).

A positive floor such as \(\log(2ndN')\), together with a rechecked dimension argument, removes this particular endpoint. It does not repair Problem 1. The proof’s assertion \(\binom{n+d}{d}=O(n^d)\) also needs a stated uniformity range: for \(n=1\) it compares \(d+1\) with \(1\). No full corrected SORS theorem is inferred here.

Problem 7 (Minor: reversed factors in Remark 3.9).  The displayed approximate-minimizer chain on p. 17 uses the distortion factors in the wrong direction.

In the branch where the sketched minimum exceeds \(\tau\), the valid chain is \[\|f(x)\|\ge
 \frac{\|Sf(x)\|}{1+\varepsilon}\ge
 \frac{\|Sf(x^*)\|}{1+\varepsilon}\ge
 \frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\|f(x^*)\|.\] The printed chain instead ends with \((1+\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\|f(x^*)\|\); setting \(x=x^*\) contradicts it whenever the residual is positive. For example \(f\equiv1\), \(S=1\), and \(\tau<1\) already suffice. The intended near-optimality conclusion survives the corrected chain.

# Entropy notation and the remaining application checks

Problem 8 (Minor: the left side of Theorem 3.18).  Equations (34)–(35) and the intermediate proof display on p. 22 write \(\mathcal N\) where \(\log\mathcal N\) is required.

A literal covering-number interpretation is false. Take a one-layer fully connected network with \(d_0=d_L=2\), the fixed activation \(\rho(z)=z/(1+z^2)\), \(s=2\), \(t=1/2\), and a single sample \(X=0\). The six weights and biases are trainable. The two biases vary independently, so the evaluation image is exactly \([-1/2,1/2]^2\). Area comparison gives \(\mathcal N(\mathcal F(X),\varepsilon)\ge1/(\pi\varepsilon^2)\), whereas the displayed right side is \(6\log((1/2)/\varepsilon)\) plus a fixed constant. For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) the inequality fails. The logarithmic version agrees with Theorem 2.6 and with its explicit use in Corollary 3.19 on p. 23. This is a notation correction, not evidence against the intended entropy scaling.

The tensor construction on pp. 13–14 has degree \(d\) and \(r\sum_i n_i\) scalar parameters. In the improved low-rank case \(r\le\min_i n_i\), orthonormal factorization preserves the core norm; telescoping the Kronecker products gives the error allocation \(\varepsilon/2+d(\varepsilon/(2d))\). Lemma 3.3 should explicitly say orthonormal basis, as its proof on p. 33 and its use on p. 14 do. Without that normalization a basis vector of arbitrarily large length cannot satisfy the absolute bound. For example, for orthogonal lines \(E,F\), a basis vector \(2e_1\) of \(E\) has distance at least \(2>\sqrt2\) from every vector of \(F\), whereas the projection distance is one. This is an additional local hypothesis clarification; the actual tensor construction uses the needed normalized bases.

The degree calculation for rational networks in Appendix E, pp. 34–35, checks under its stated width and degree conditions. A preactivation has numerator degree at most \(d_i\theta+1\) and denominator degree at most \(d_i\theta\). Composition gives \(\Delta_{i+1}\le s(1+d_i\Delta_i)\); trainable activation coefficients add one. The convolutional count replaces \(d_i\) by \(c_i k^2\). The geometric-series factor two uses the explicit lower bounds in Lemma E.2.

For the ReLU application, pp. 24 and 35–38, the bounded input, layer norms, and Lipschitz loss are substantive hypotheses. The local error inequality \(\delta_i\le\varepsilon_i+\omega_i\delta_{i-1}\) follows when both preactivations lie in the prescribed approximation interval. This check is not a proof of every constant or of the external rational-approximation theorem.
