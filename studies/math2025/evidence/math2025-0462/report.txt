Abstract: This review examines the boundary arguments and the contrast between ordinary and normalized trace nonnegativity in the acquired journal version. Three central proof problems require revision: an invalid inference from positive eigenvalues to positivity of a quadratic form, a missing coordinate scaling in the undecidability reduction, and a circular connectedness argument. Exact examples establish the first two failures. A further example disproves an overbroad classification of critical points, while leaving the intended classification of boundary extrema open. These findings concern the supplied derivations; they do not establish that the main geometric or undecidability theorems are false. The report assessment is Major. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and substantive checks

The finite probability simplex is \(\Delta_{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^n:\sum_i x_i=1\}\). The paper studies the images of power sums \(p_j(x)=\sum_i x_i^j\), including generalized increasing integer exponents. In the ordered chamber, a multiplicity vector \((m_0,m_1,\ldots,m_\ell)\) records zero coordinates followed by increasing distinct positive coordinates. Type (1) requires \(m_{2i-1}=1\), while type (2) requires \(m_0=0\) and \(m_{2i}=1\). The sub-probability simplex replaces the equality by \(\sum_i x_i\leq1\). Squaring variables converts these domains to a sphere or ball and doubles the exponents. That transformation is useful, but it can introduce degenerate objective directions at zero coordinates, as Problem 3 demonstrates.

The elementary-symmetric convex-hull result in Section 5 has a direct supporting argument. For a linear combination of elementary symmetric polynomials, fixing all but two positive coordinates and their sum leaves an expression \(C x_1x_2+D\). A minimizer with maximal zero support either permits another zero coordinate when \(C\geq0\), or forces the two positive coordinates equal when \(C<0\). Thus some minimizer is uniform on its nonzero support. Applying this to every affine functional, and using separation, gives the claimed convex hull of these uniform-support images. This support-function argument also avoids any need to assume that every extreme point of an arbitrary compact convex set is uniquely exposed. The triangular polynomial change from \((t,\ldots,t^{d-1})\) to \[\left(\frac{(1-t)}{2!},\frac{(1-t)(1-2t)}{3!},
 \ldots,\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}(1-jt)}{d!}\right)\] has nonzero leading coefficients. It verifies the invertible affine correspondence used for the cyclic-polytope vertices. The hook-shaped test in Theorem 5.8 then follows from homogeneity and linearity on \(e_1=1\).

The trace-polynomial definition on p. 2043 uses products of traces of powers of individual matrices. It does not encompass arbitrary mixed noncommutative words. The normalized-trace proof on pp. 2048–2049 passes from rational multiplicity weights to all simplex weights by density and continuity, conditional on the stated half-degree principle. A common denominator accommodates several groups of rational weights if a common matrix size is required. This explains the change in quantifiers between fixed-size and all-size tests. No additional defect in that particular reduction was established here.

# Problems

Problem 1 (Major: an invalid matrix inference in Lemma 2.17).  The proof on p. 2019 argues that a totally positive matrix has positive simple eigenvalues and therefore \(v^TAv>0\) for every nonzero \(v\). The implication is false for a nonsymmetric matrix.

Consider \[A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\4&6\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad v=\begin{pmatrix}-5/2\\1\end{pmatrix}.\] All four entries are positive and \(\det A=2>0\), so every minor is positive. Its eigenvalues are \((7\pm\sqrt{41})/2\), both positive and distinct. Nevertheless, \[v^TAv=-\frac14,\qquad
 \frac{v^TAv}{v^Tv}=-\frac1{29}.\] Thus even the orthogonal compression to a one-dimensional subspace can be negative. This is not an unrelated matrix: \(A\) is the Jacobian of \[(x_1,x_2)\longmapsto(x_1+x_2,\;x_1^2+x_2^2)\] at \((2,3)\), with unit positive weights and increasing positive exponents \((1,2)\), within the hypotheses of the displayed lemma.

The example refutes the inference used in the proof, not the lemma itself. Indeed, in this two-variable unit-weight example the ordered coordinates can be recovered from their first two moments. The issue is that the manuscript has not obtained the asserted positive projected determinants for arbitrary subspaces by its stated reasoning. Lemma 2.17 is then used for boundary-preimage uniqueness in Lemma 2.18 (p. 2020) and for the curved-simplex parametrization in Lemma 3.5 (p. 2031). A replacement argument must establish the necessary injectivity and face regularity for the full weighted, general-exponent setting, including boundary faces. This is a Major gap in the supplied central derivation; it is not a counterexample to the main boundary theorem.

Problem 2 (Major: the displayed reduction map loses its scaling).  On p. 2046 the geometric variables are \((2e_2,6e_3)\), but the displayed homogenization map \(\tau\) substitutes \(e_2/e_1^2\) and \(e_3/e_1^3\). The equivalence asserted on p. 2047 then fails.

It suffices to take one group, \(k=1\), and the permitted integer polynomial \(p(Y)=1-2Y\). The prescribed coefficient bound is \(M=100\deg(p)(|1|+|-2|)=300\). With \(g(Y)=2Y^2-Y\), the auxiliary polynomial is \[q(Y,Z)=(1-2Y)(1-Y)^6+300(Z-2Y^2+Y).\] At the vertex corresponding to \(n=4\), \[(Y,Z)=\left(\frac34,\frac38\right),\qquad
 Z=g(Y)=L(Y),\qquad
 q(Y,Z)=-\frac1{8192}<0.\] This point belongs to the set \(R\) defined in the proof, so assertion (a) on p. 2047 is satisfied.

In contrast, on the infinite probability simplex \(\Delta\) of p. 2044, the displayed map gives \(\tau(q)=q(e_2,e_3)\), since \(e_1=1\). Every point of \(\Delta\) satisfies \[0\leq e_2=\frac{1-\sum_i x_i^2}{2}<\frac12,\qquad e_3\geq0.\] Consequently both terms in \[q(e_2,e_3)
   =(1-2e_2)(1-e_2)^6
     +300\bigl(e_3+e_2(1-2e_2)\bigr)\] are nonnegative. Assertion (b), which requires a negative value of \(\tau(q)\) somewhere on \(\Delta\), is false. This is a global argument on the stipulated domain, not merely a failure at one sampled preimage. At the uniform four-coordinate point itself, \((e_2,e_3)=(3/8,1/16)\) and the printed expression is \(49167625/1048576>0\).

The adjacent prose explicitly uses \(2e_{2,(i)}=x_i\) and \(6e_{3,(i)}=y_i\). It strongly identifies the intended correction: \[\tau_{\mathrm{corr}}(f)=
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1,(i)}^{3\deg f}\right)
 f\left(
 \frac{2e_{2,(1)}}{e_{1,(1)}^2},\ldots,
 \frac{2e_{2,(k)}}{e_{1,(k)}^2},
 \frac{6e_{3,(1)}}{e_{1,(1)}^3},\ldots,
 \frac{6e_{3,(k)}}{e_{1,(k)}^3}\right).\] The same homogenizing exponent still clears all denominators. On \(\Delta^k\) this gives precisely the scaled substitution used in the geometric argument; the example then has value \(-1/8192\). The correction is local and explicit. Its locality does not make the printed equivalence valid: that equivalence supplies the reduction for Theorem 6.6 and hence Theorem 6.2. The Major assessment concerns this broken core derivation, without alleging that undecidability itself is false or that all cited inputs have been independently reproved.

Problem 3 (Minor: the critical-point quantifier in Lemma 2.28 is too broad).  On pp. 2025–2026 the lemma classifies critical points, whereas its intended application concerns extrema. Under the intended constraints stated in the proof, a smooth admissible fiber has a critical saddle outside both listed types.

Choose \(n=d=3\), \(\alpha=(2,3)\), \(\beta=(4,6)\), and \[a=\frac{3}{\sqrt{20}},\qquad
 x=(0,a,a),\qquad
 c=\frac{81}{200}.\] The intended domain is \(p_2\leq1\), \(p_4=c\). Here \(p_2(x)=9/10<1\) and \(p_4(x)=c\). With inactive inequality multiplier \(\lambda_0=0\) and \(\lambda_1=-27/40\), \[\nabla p_6(x)+\lambda_1\nabla p_4(x)=0.\] The hypersurface \(p_4=c>0\) is smooth. The fiber \(p_2=1\), \(p_4=c\) is also smooth: dependence of these two gradients forces all nonzero absolute coordinates to be equal, giving \(p_4=1/r\) for \(r=1,2,3\), and \(81/200\) is none of these values. This fiber is nonempty; its squared coordinates can be \[\left(\frac13-\frac{\sqrt{43}}{60},
       \frac13-\frac{\sqrt{43}}{60},
       \frac13+\frac{\sqrt{43}}{30}\right).\] Thus the smoothness hypotheses are not evaded by an empty boundary fiber.

The multiplicity vector of \(x\) is \((m_0,m_1)=(1,2)\). It is not type (1), since \(m_1\neq1\), and not type (2), since \(m_0\neq0\). It is a saddle. Writing \(b=a^2=9/20\), a curve with first coordinate \(t\) and the other fourth powers equal to \(b^2-t^4/2\) gives \[p_6=\frac{729}{4000}-\frac{27}{40}t^4+t^6+O(t^8),\] which is smaller for sufficiently small nonzero \(t\). Keeping the first coordinate zero and taking the other fourth powers \(b^2+s\) and \(b^2-s\) gives \[p_6=\frac{729}{4000}+\frac53s^2+O(s^4),\] which is larger for sufficiently small nonzero \(s\). Both curves remain within \(p_2<1\) by continuity.

This disproves the all-critical-points formulation, not the claimed classification of boundary extrema. Accordingly its demonstrated impact is Minor. The statement should specify the intended extrema and supply the argument for zero-coordinate directions, where smoothness of the constraint alone does not make the objective Morse. The displayed \(\widetilde V\) index should also be reconciled with the number of constraints; the example above uses the explicit constraints in the proof, not an opportunistic reading of that index mismatch.

Problem 4 (Major: the proof of Lemma 2.29 is circular).  The connectedness argument on pp. 2026–2027 assumes that \(M\) is disconnected and concludes that \(M\) is disconnected, labeling that conclusion a contradiction.

The intervening argument establishes connectedness of the nonempty slices of \(M\) at fixed coordinate sum. This forces each such slice to belong to only one member of a proposed separation \(M=A\cup B\). It does not by itself prove that the set of attainable coordinate sums is an interval. In particular, the final empty intermediate slice does not contradict the initial assumption or any connectedness of \(M\) already established in that proof.

For the logical distinction, the compact set \[\bigl([0,1]\cup[2,3]\bigr)\times\{0\}\] is disconnected although each nonempty first-coordinate slice is a singleton. This elementary illustration is not offered as a counterexample to the specific Vandermonde assertion. It shows why connected slices alone cannot supply the missing conclusion.

An independent argument that the attainable sum range is an interval, together with compactness and connected fibers, would close this route: a separation would give two disjoint nonempty compact images partitioning that interval. Alternatively, a valid direct connectedness proof could be supplied. The argument must cover the stated singular values as well. Lemma 2.31 explicitly needs Lemma 2.29 to turn finitely many possible boundary preimages into a unique preimage; Lemma 2.32 and Theorem 2.27 then use that uniqueness. These dependencies give the missing argument Major significance within the sub-probability and limiting-boundary development. No counterexample to the connectedness statement or its downstream conclusions has been established here.

# Assessment and verification limits

The report assessment is Major: Problems 1, 2 and 4 require valid central derivations before the supplied proof chain can be relied upon. Problem 3 is deliberately classified as Minor because its verified saddle only defeats an overbroad auxiliary quantifier. The calculations do not support a claim that the principal results are false.

The exact matrix, rational substitution, multiplier and local-series calculations were independently implemented by this reviewer using existing native SymPy facilities, and completed successfully. Their script and literal outputs accompany the report. These checks supplement the displayed analytic arguments; neither finite sampling nor software assertions are used to claim the missing global theorems. Missing optional author code is not a mathematical defect.

A revision should replace the univalence argument, use the scaled homogenization consistently, distinguish critical points from extrema, and supply the missing connectedness step. The Section 5 test-set argument and the normalized-trace parameterization were checked within the limits stated above. All-page reading does not certify every external result, every singular-fiber assertion or every limiting-face incidence.
