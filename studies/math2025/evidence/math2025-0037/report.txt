# Claims and dependencies

The construction is affinization \(\mathrm U(X)=\operatorname{Spec}R\Gamma(X,\mathcal O)\) among higher fpqc stacks. Pointed cohomological connectedness makes the homotopy sheaves unipotent affine group schemes. Proposition 3.1.7 compares the first group with Nori’s unipotent fundamental group through pointed torsor universal properties.

Principal guarantees include profiniteness for proper pointed cohomologically connected schemes in positive characteristic, maximal pro-etale quotient comparison, Freudenthal suspension, recovery of formal Lie groups, and derived invariance for Calabi–Yau varieties. The suspension theorem is stated as Theorem 1.0.16 on page 7 for all integers \(n\ge0\), through Proposition 3.4.10 and Corollary 3.4.11 on pages 37–38. The defect is in the explicitly used \(n=0\) derivation.

For \(n\ge1\), the proof uses finite-dimensional cohomology, Postnikov-stage finiteness, preservation of certain pullbacks by affinization, and right exactness of commutative unipotent completion. Proposition 7.2.15 later uses the simply connected formal two-sphere, hence the \(n=1\) suspension case, followed by higher connectivity. It does not require the erroneous \(n=0\) group sequence.

# Problem 1: the EHP group sequence fails at zero connectivity

## Claim and exact location

Proposition 3.4.10, page 37, equation (3.4.4), asserts \[\pi_{2n+2}(X\wedge X)\longrightarrow
 \pi_{2n+1}(X)\longrightarrow
 \pi_{2n+2}(\Sigma X)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{R1}\] The following paragraph identifies the first arrow with a bilinear Whitehead product and, at \(n=0\), the commutator map. It explicitly uses this sequence to obtain the zero-connectivity surjection. The same text is in `main_revision.tex`, lines 1702–1709.

At \(n=0\), the first term is an abelian homotopy sheaf, whereas the kernel of the second arrow can be a nonabelian derived subgroup. Thus (R1) cannot be an exact sequence of group sheaves with the asserted arrows. The commutator also does not factor bilinearly through the abelianizations in Lemma 3.4.3.

## All hypotheses verified

Let \(k=\mathbf F_2\), let \(G\) be the constant group scheme of \(U_5(\mathbf F_2)\), the upper triangular \(5\) by \(5\) matrices with diagonal entries one, and set \(X=BG\). The group has order \(2^{10}\).

Every nonzero representation of a finite \(2\)-group in characteristic two has a nonzero invariant vector. Indeed, a central element \(z\) of order two has \((z-1)^2=0\), so its nonzero kernel is group-stable, and induction on the quotient group proves the assertion. Infinite-dimensional representations reduce to a nonzero finite-dimensional cyclic subrepresentation. Hence \(G\) is unipotent in the paper’s definition.

The stack \(BG\) is pointed, connected, and affine by Theorem 2.1.17. Every \(H^i(BG,\mathcal O)\) is finite-dimensional: the finite-group bar cochain complex has degree-\(i\) dimension \(|G|^i\). Both assumptions of Proposition 3.4.10 therefore hold for \(n=0\).

For a connected classifying space, the suspension map \[G=\pi_1(BG)\longrightarrow\pi_2(\Sigma BG)=G_{\mathrm{ab}}\] is abelianization, by degree-two Hurewicz and suspension homology. This persists for constant sheaves because the constant-object functor preserves finite limits and colimits. The kernel is the constant sheaf \(G'\).

Writing \(e_{ij}\) for elementary matrices and \([a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\), set \[A=I+e_{12},\quad B=I+e_{23},\quad
 C=I+e_{34},\quad D=I+e_{45}.\] Direct multiplication gives \[h=[A,B]=I+e_{13},\qquad v=[C,D]=I+e_{35},
 \qquad [h,v]=I+e_{15}\ne I. \tag{R2}\] Thus \(h,v\in G'\) do not commute. These identities were independently checked by original reviewer-written exact arithmetic modulo two.

But \(\pi_2(X\wedge X)\) is abelian. The image of a group-sheaf homomorphism from it must be abelian and cannot equal \(G'\). This disproves (R1) at \(n=0\). Also, \(h\) has zero class in \(G_{\mathrm{ab}}\) but \([h,v]\ne1\), independently ruling out the claimed bilinear commutator factorization.

## Impact and counterinterpretation

This is not only a label error: the ordinary EHP group sequence must be restricted to positive connectivity. Even interpreting the word sheaves as pointed sets would not validate the stated bilinear description. The manuscript expressly invokes the invalid sequence for the boundary part of a principal theorem, supporting the prescribed Major rating. No main theorem is claimed false, and no failure of the later Calabi–Yau applications follows from this example.

# Counterevidence and complete repair of the affected boundary

Let \(X\) be a pointed connected affine stack and \(G=\pi_1(X)\). For every commutative unipotent affine group scheme \(H\), adjunction gives \[\begin{split}
 [\mathrm U(\Sigma X),K(H,2)]_*
 &\simeq[\Sigma X,K(H,2)]_*\\
 &\simeq[X,BH]_*
 \simeq\operatorname{Hom}(G,H).
\end{split}\tag{R3}\] Brackets mean pointed homotopy classes. The last step uses one-truncation because \(BH\) is one-truncated.

The suspension is simply connected, and its first coherent cohomology vanishes. The paper’s Hurewicz criterion therefore gives \(\pi_1(\mathrm U(\Sigma X))=0\). The left side of (R3) is \(\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_2(\mathrm U(\Sigma X)),H)\), so the universal property yields \[\pi_2(\mathrm U(\Sigma X))\simeq G^{\mathrm{ab}}_{\mathrm{aff}}.\] Here the right side means abelianization in unipotent affine group schemes, not an assumption that an arbitrary sheaf quotient is representable. The suspension unit induces the quotient map, which is surjective by Remark 3.3.9. This proves the \(n=0\) conclusion without (R1), even without finite-dimensional cohomology.

The repair is complete within the established affine-stack framework. It explains why the conclusion survives the false auxiliary sequence. It does not certify the uninspected foundations or all positive-connectivity EHP inputs.

# Additional mathematical checks

## Definitions and universal properties

The Nori comparison on pages 24–26 uses the same pointed torsor functor on both sides, and the one-truncation step is appropriate. The Hurewicz statement on page 28 explicitly defines trivial degree-zero cohomology to mean \(H^0=k\); an objection based on \(H^0=0\) would be spurious. Its induction uses unipotence and vanishing of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_i,\mathbf G_a)\), conditional on the stated Postnikov comparison.

The product formula retains quasicompactness and quasiseparatedness for its Kunneth input. No extension to arbitrary schemes was inferred. The introductory suspension theorem’s more general wording is compatible with the affine version because the left adjoint satisfies \(\mathrm U(\Sigma\mathrm U(X))\simeq\mathrm U(\Sigma X)\).

## Spectral-sequence degree logic

In Lemma 3.4.7 and Proposition 4.3.7, fibre cohomology vanishes for \(0<j\le n\). The term \(E_2^{0,n+1}\) has no incoming differentials; outgoing differentials before \(d_{n+2}\) land in zero rows, and the sole possible later differential lands at \((n+2,0)\). Thus \(E_{n+3}^{0,n+1}=E_\infty^{0,n+1}\).

A map from an infinite-dimensional vector space to a finite-dimensional one has infinite-dimensional kernel. Similarly, torsion Frobenius modules are closed under extensions, so a non-torsion term cannot be killed by a torsion target. These yield the stated contradictions against the abutment. This checks the degree logic conditional on the base-change, Frobenius-representation, and profiniteness criteria; those entire foundational sections were not re-proved.

## Formal groups and sphere reconstruction

In Proposition 6.2.3, the symmetric algebra on degree two has \(H^1=H^3=0\). The first nonzero homotopy group lies in degree two, and its \(\operatorname{Ext}^1(-,\mathbf G_a)\) vanishes by injection into \(H^3\). The finite-dimensional formal-Lie criterion then applies. The final map is an isomorphism on algebras generated in degree two, not merely an equality of graded dimensions.

Lemma 6.1.18 proves vanishing only for \(i>2\). Its inverse-limit argument correctly uses vanishing of degree-three cohomology of successive Verschiebung kernels to obtain surjectivity on the degree-two tower and remove \(\lim^1\).

For Proposition 7.2.14 the obstruction is \(H^{r+3}(BE^\vee,\pi_{n+r+1})\), which vanishes for all \(r\ge0\). Later ambiguities involve \(H^{r+2}\) and vanish for \(r\ge1\), leaving the first-stage \(H^2\) torsor. The Milnor argument explicitly handles inverse-limit ambiguity. The nontwisted Postnikov construction uses \(n\ge2\).

For the algebraically closed Calabi–Yau applications, symmetrization \(x\otimes y\mapsto x\otimes y+y\otimes x\) explains the weak wedge. In the split height-one characteristic-two case the cokernel of multiplication by two on \(\mathbf Z_2\) is \(\mathbf Z/2\). In odd characteristic the symmetric bilinear functional is killed by the weak-wedge relation. The final uniqueness argument retains both \(n>2\) and \(p>2\).
