Abstract: The paper connects geometric canonical heights, analytic rigidity of entropy measures, and arithmetic equidistribution to obtain uniform and relative dynamical Bogomolov statements. This review establishes a counterexample to the all-points height inequality in Theorem 4.10, an invalid measure identity in the central proof of Lemma 5.2, and a counterexample to the unequal-degree extension in Theorem 3.1. A further local conjugacy inference requires correction. The height inequality has a sufficient dense-open replacement for its later generic-sequence use. A complete local replacement for Lemma 5.2 is supplied using the already cited Yuan–Zhang theorem. These findings do not disprove the principal uniform or relative Bogomolov theorems. The assessment is Major: revision of statements and proofs required; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Scope and principal dependencies

The review examined the definitions and quantifiers in the introduction, the normalization of heights and pulled-back measures, the ordinary and exceptional branches of Section 3, the geometric-height arguments of Section 4, the equidistribution and slicing chain of Section 5, Section 6, and the specialization appendix. They were not separate external referees or an independent acceptance panel.

The principal chain is geometric nondegeneracy, followed by equidistribution, equality of measures on a fibre product, slicing, and the equal-degree analytic rigidity theorem. The passage from relative non-density to uniform fibre counts permits deletion of finitely many base parameters. That permission resolves the apparent exceptional-fibre objection to the generically finite counting argument on pp. 29–30. The normalized pullback weights in Theorem 1.10 and the use of the fundamental inequality in Corollary 1.5 are consistent.

The height-coincidence input of Mavraki–Schmidt–Wilms was checked for its function-field hypotheses and exceptional set. # Problem 1: an isotrivial zero-height section survives the stated exceptional locus

Assessment: Major. Location: Theorem 4.10 and its preceding one-sentence deduction, p. 23; the exceptional-locus definition, p. 6; Definition 1.7, p. 4; the reused induction, pp. 22–23. The accepted manuscript retains the same Theorem 4.10 on physical p. 24.

Theorem 4.10 claims a positive lower bound \[\widehat h_\Phi(P)\ge c_1h_{\mathcal N}(\pi(P))-c_2
 \quad\text{for every }P\in\mathcal C^{\Phi,\star}(\overline{\mathbb Q})\] when at least one coordinate is isotrivial, with an additional non-isotriviality condition on the curve if both coordinates are isotrivial. The displayed exceptional locus does not remove every horizontal section of geometric canonical height zero.

Take \(B=\mathbb P^1\), \(K=\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\), \(B_0=\mathbb A^1\), and \[\Phi=(f_1,f_2)=(z^2,z^2+t),\qquad
 \mathbf C:\ y=\frac1{x-2},\qquad P_t=(2,\infty).\] Both maps have degree two. The first is isotrivial. The second is not: the two finite fixed-point multipliers satisfy \(u^2-2u+4t=0\), and the multiplier at infinity is zero. Their second elementary symmetric function \(4t\) is a nonconstant conjugacy invariant. Thus the additional both-isotrivial hypothesis is inapplicable.

The curve is the graph of a Möbius transformation, so both projections are isomorphisms. In coordinates \(x=[X:Z]\), \(y=[Y:W]\), its closure is \[Y(X-2Z)=ZW.\] Its only point above \(y=\infty\) is \((2,\infty)\); above \(x=\infty\) it has \((\infty,0)\). Since a polynomial has no finite preimage of infinity, the only point of \(\Phi^n(\mathbf C)\) above \(y=\infty\) is \((2^{2^n},\infty)\). These points are distinct, so \(\mathbf C\) is not preperiodic. The section \(P_t\) is not preperiodic either.

Under the paper’s stated preperiodic/torsion-coset convention this section belongs to \(\mathcal C^{\Phi,\star}\). In this pair \(f_2\) is ordinary: Remark 4.5 identifies a non-isotrivial exceptional map with a flexible Lattès map of square degree, excluding degree two. Thus the exceptional torsion-coset alternative is unavailable. The curve itself is not special; the non-preperiodic point is not a special point-component. The definition removes components of special subvarieties contained in \(\mathcal C\), not arbitrary intersections with an ambient preperiodic curve such as \(\mathbb P^1\times\{\infty\}\). No convention removing all non-preperiodic constant points of isotrivial coordinates is supplied in the manuscript.

For every algebraic \(t\), \[\widehat h_{\Phi_t}(P_t)
 =\widehat h_{z^2}(2)+\widehat h_{z^2+t}(\infty)=\log 2.\] With \(\mathcal N=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)\), positive integer parameters have unbounded base height. The claimed inequality is impossible for fixed \(c_1>0,c_2\). Shrinking \(B_0\) cannot remove this contradiction, and adding dominance of both projections cannot repair it.

Cause and impact. The generic section has geometric canonical height zero, since its first coordinate is constant and the second is fixed. In an isotrivial coordinate, geometric height zero does not imply preperiodicity. The base case of Theorem 1.11 uses non-isotriviality precisely to obtain that implication; it cannot be reused unchanged. Theorem 4.10 is a false height statement used in Proposition 4.13 and hence in the mixed-isotrivial route to the uniform theorem. The example does not violate the isotrivial-free hypotheses of Theorem 1.11.

Repair boundary. For the dominant curves in cases (2)–(3) of Proposition 4.12, Theorem 4.3 gives positive geometric curve height and Appendix A.1 gives the required inequality on a nonempty Zariski open subset \(U\). A generic sequence in the sense of Definition 4.11 meets the fixed proper closed complement only finitely often. The bound on base heights needed in Proposition 4.13 therefore follows after discarding finitely many terms. This repairs that downstream use, but does not restore the all-points assertion on the original \(\mathcal C^{\Phi,\star}\). A stronger corrected theorem must explicitly enlarge the removed locus or supply a different valid hypothesis. Dependence of the slope on the fixed choice of \(\mathcal N\) must also be allowed.

# Problem 2: finite pushforward does not determine its source measure

Assessment: Major; a local repair is verified below. Location: Lemma 5.2, p. 26, between (5.5) and the concluding display; diagram (5.2), p. 25. The same inference occurs on physical p. 27 of the accepted manuscript.

The proof asserts \[\pi_i^*\pi_{i*}\pi_{\mathcal C*}P
   =\deg(\pi_i)\pi_{\mathcal C*}P\] because \(\pi_i\) is finite. This is not a finite-map identity. For \(\pi(z)=z^2\), let \(m\) be Haar probability measure on the unit circle and put \(\mu=(1+\varepsilon\cos\theta)m\), where \(0<\varepsilon<1\). The two inverse branches cancel the cosine term under pushforward, giving \[\pi_*\mu=m,\qquad \pi^*\pi_*\mu=2m\ne2\mu.\] Testing against \(\cos\theta\) gives respectively \(0\) and \(\varepsilon\). These are positive nonatomic measures. The universally valid degree identity has the opposite order, \(\pi_*\pi^*\nu=\deg(\pi)\nu\). The same obstruction persists after taking a product with a base measure.

This disproves the stated justification, not Lemma 5.2 under all its dynamical hypotheses. Its particular limiting measure can have the required symmetry, but that symmetry must be proved. The step is central: Lemma 5.2 supplies the equality sliced in the proof of Theorem 1.8, which is then used for Theorem 1.4.

## A replacement using the cited equidistribution theorem

Write \[Y=\mathcal C\times_{B_0}\mathcal X,\quad
 Z=\mathbb P^1\times\mathcal X,\quad
 a_i=\deg(\widetilde\pi_i)=\deg(\pi_i),\quad r=\dim Y.\] After the permitted shrinking of \(B_0\), the coordinate projections are finite. Let \(\overline L_i\) be the invariant adelic line bundle for \(f_i\times_{B_0}\Psi\) restricted to \(Z\), and set \(\overline M_i=\widetilde\pi_i^*\overline L_i\). The following facts justify applying equidistribution on the source \(Y\).

First, \(\overline L_i\) is nef by Yuan–Zhang [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 6.1.1, pp.~191--192]{YZ}]; finite pullback preserves nefness. Second, the earlier nondegeneracy argument gives \(v_i=\int_Z\nu_i>0\), where \(\nu_i=\widehat T_{f_i\times\Psi}^{\,r}\wedge[Z]\). The finite projection formula [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition 4.1.2, p.~111]{YZ}] gives positive geometric top degree \(a_iv_i\) for \(\overline M_i\), with the compatible normalizations. Third, the original generic small sequence \(z_n\) on \(Y\) satisfies \[0\le h_{\overline M_i}(z_n)
 =\widehat h_{f_i\times\Psi}(\widetilde\pi_i(z_n))
 \le \widehat h_{\Phi\times\Psi}(z_n)\longrightarrow0.\] Finally, nefness and the fundamental inequality [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 5.3.3, p.~170]{YZ}] give \[0\le h_{\overline M_i}(Y)
 \le\lambda_1(Y,\overline M_i)=0.\] Thus smallness is convergence to the normalized variety height, as the general theorem requires.

Yuan–Zhang [Retained latex: \cite[Theorem 5.4.3, p.~177]{YZ}] now applies to each \((Y,\overline M_i)\). Uniqueness of the weak limit of the same Galois-orbit measures gives the stronger assertion \[P=\frac1{a_iv_i}\widetilde\pi_i^*\nu_i.\] Pushing forward through the Cartesian square yields \[\pi_{\mathcal C*}P
   =\frac1{a_iv_i}\pi_i^*p_*\nu_i,\qquad
 \pi_1^*p_*\nu_1
   =\frac{a_1v_1}{a_2v_2}\pi_2^*p_*\nu_2,\] which proves the desired lemma with the intended positive constant. For this base-change identity one may work off the branch locus, where the finite map is a union of local biholomorphisms. Products of Green currents with continuous local potentials charge no proper analytic subsets; the equality consequently extends across that locus. This avoids applying the separate submersion lemma outside its hypotheses.

The replacement is complete at this local step, conditional on the paper’s earlier nondegeneracy inputs and the cited theory. It does not claim an independent proof of that theory. The original invalid central derivation remains a Major correction even though its replacement is available; the corrected lemma and principal relative theorem have not been counterexampled.

# Problem 3: equal entropy measures need not imply commensurable degrees

Assessment: Minor, confined to the advertised unequal-degree extension. Location: Theorem 3.1, p. 11; the exceptional branch, pp. 15–16. The theorem’s statement is identical at the inspected accepted-manuscript physical p. 12.

Take \(f_1(z)=z^2\), \(f_2(z)=z^3\), and \(C=\Delta\subset(\mathbb P^1)^2\). The diagonal is smooth and irreducible, and both projections have degree one. Both entropy measures are the same Haar measure \(m\) on \(S^1\). Indeed \((z^d)^*m=dm\), and \(m\) is the atomless balanced probability measure. Thus the normalized pullback measures agree and their supports intersect. However, no positive integers \(\ell,k\) satisfy \(2^\ell=3^k\). The equivalence asserted in Theorem 3.1 is therefore false.

These are exceptional maps associated with \(\mathbb G_m\), and the diagonal is a subgroup. The proof obtains degree commensurability in its ordinary branch; the exceptional coset argument cannot imply it. The repair must distinguish an ordinary alternative involving suitable iterates from an exceptional coset alternative without a universal commensurability requirement. Since the paper defines weak specialness for polarized pairs, the latter alternative must be stated explicitly or accompanied by an extended definition. A full rewritten generalized theorem has not been certified here.

A related local algebra error occurs in the torus converse on p. 16: \(x^{k_1}y^{k_2}=\theta\), \(|\theta|=1\), does not imply \(k_1\log^+|x|=k_2\log^+|y|\). For \(xy=1\), \(x=2\), \(y=1/2\), the two sides are \(\log2\) and \(0\). The desired measure conclusion is nevertheless correct in this example: \[\log^+|t|-\log^+|t^{-1}|=\log|t|\] is harmonic on \(\mathbb C^*\), so applying \(dd^c\) gives equality. More generally a torus coset parametrized by \((at^m,bt^n)\) has pulled-back circle measures of masses \(|m|\), \(|n|\); intersecting supports force the same support radius, and normalization by projection degree gives equality.

Impact boundary. Theorem 1.10 assumes equal degrees. Proposition 3.6 and the arithmetic proof invoke that theorem, not the false extra unequal-degree conclusion. The example therefore does not refute the equal-degree rigidity theorem or the principal arithmetic results. The local classification here reflects that verified dependency boundary, not a claim that a false theorem is merely a typographical issue.

# Problem 4: nonconstant conjugacies can preserve the diagonal

Assessment: Minor. Location: Section 6, p. 31, the isotrivial case of the proof of Theorem 1.3 (source lines 1478–1483).

The proof infers that \((M_1,M_2)(\Delta)\) is not constant from nonconstancy of at least one \(M_i\). Let \[M_t(z)=z+t,\qquad f_t(z)=(z+t)^2-t,\qquad
 g_t(z)=(z+t)^2+1-t.\] Both maps vary, both are conjugate to fixed maps through \(M_t\), and \((M_t,M_t)(\Delta)=\Delta\). Hence the inference fails.

This is not confined to a weakly special diagonal. The fixed map \(g_0=z^2+1\) has an infinite critical orbit of \(0\), so it is ordinary; moreover \(\Delta\) is not preperiodic for \((z^2,z^2+1)\): if its image curves were eventually periodic, infinitely many orbit points of \((1,1)\) would lie on one image curve above first coordinate \(1\). That curve has finite projection to the first factor, whereas \(g_0^n(1)\) is strictly increasing. This is impossible.

The uniform conclusion still follows in this case because simultaneous conjugacy identifies the common-preperiodic sets with those of a fixed pair, so their intersection cardinality is independent of \(t\). The proof should distinguish a constant transformed diagonal from a varying one. This is a local logical correction, not a counterexample to Theorem 1.3.
