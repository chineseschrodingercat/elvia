# Claims, assumptions and scope

Theorem 1.2 (PDF p. 5, printed p. 5347) concerns invariant tempered Gibbs measures on an infinite vertex-transitive graph of polynomial growth, with \(g>0\), \(a\in\mathbb R\) and an admissible ferromagnetic interaction satisfying (C1)–(C4) on PDF p. 4. Its first conclusion describes invariant states as mixtures of the plus and minus states. Its second conclusion gives uniqueness among all tempered Gibbs states under the free-state correlation criterion \[\liminf_{d_G(o,x)\to\infty}\langle\varphi_o\varphi_x\rangle_\beta^0=0.\] These conclusions have different scopes.

The one-dimensional discussion is expressly qualified by the existence of a nontrivial transition in Remark 1.4 (PDF p. 9); this review does not establish that existence in every long-range case.

# Finite-volume expansion and structural reduction

Proposition 3.1 and its proof (PDF pp. 23–24, printed pp. 5365–5366) expand the interaction exponential and retain precisely the terms with the required parity of sources. This expansion has a direct analytic justification. In finite volume the sum of absolute Taylor terms is dominated by the integral of \[\exp\!\left(\beta\sum_{\{x,y\}}J_{x,y}|\varphi_x\varphi_y|
       +\beta\sum_x|h_x||\varphi_x|\right)
 \prod_x \rho_{g,a}(d\varphi_x).\] For \(g>0\), the quartic tails dominate every finite quadratic and linear term. Thus the integral is finite and the exchange of summation and integration is justified absolutely. Evenness of the single-site law gives the parity restriction. At \(\beta=0\), this reduces to the product single-site moments. A signed expansion for real \(h\) is not a positive current probability representation unless the required nonnegativity conditions are imposed.

Proposition 2.15 (PDF pp. 19–22) reduces invariant-state structure to equality of free and plus even correlations. Its final extremal-measure argument is consistent with spin-flip symmetrization and uniqueness of the extremal decomposition. The intermediate moment and coupling arguments require the stated uniform regularity estimates; those estimates were not re-proved in full.

Section 6 assumes a positive plus/free edge-correlation gap and derives bridges. Lemma 6.5 (PDF p. 62) yields a bound of two disjoint infinite paths and decay of the probability of two disjoint connections to a distant vertex. The two cases on PDF pp. 63–64 contradict these respective properties. In the nonpercolating-free-current case, the localization radius and lower volume threshold may depend on the chosen pair of vertices; the positive constants in the final comparison must remain uniform in the distant vertex. The displayed argument preserves this distinction. The switching construction, positivity of admissible tanglings and all local modification estimates remain substantive dependencies of this structural proof, not results independently certified by the present audit.

# Independent verification of the critical deduction

## Temperature limit

Write \(C_\Lambda(\beta)=\langle\varphi_x\varphi_y\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^0\) and \(C(\beta)=\langle\varphi_x\varphi_y\rangle_\beta^0\). Finite-volume continuity, together with monotonicity in volume and \(\beta\), gives \[C(\beta_c)
 =\sup_\Lambda\sup_{\beta<\beta_c}C_\Lambda(\beta)
 =\sup_{\beta<\beta_c}\sup_\Lambda C_\Lambda(\beta)
 =\lim_{\beta\uparrow\beta_c}C(\beta).\] The exchange is of two suprema. For fixed finitely supported \(v\), the quadratic form in (7.2), PDF p. 68, contains only finitely many correlations; it therefore passes to \(\beta_c\) entry by entry. This is done before taking the spatial averaging limit. The factor \((2\beta|J|)^{-1}\) has a finite limit when \(0<\beta_c<\infty\).

The derivation of (7.2) itself invokes an external reflection-positive infrared bound and the regularity estimates needed to pass unbounded moments from torus measures to infinite-volume measures. Weak convergence alone would not suffice. These are explicit dependencies.

## Spatial average and liminf

By transience, the Fourier kernel \((1-\widehat p(k))^{-1}\) is integrable. Its Fourier coefficients \(G(z)\) tend to zero and satisfy \(|G(z)|\le G(0)\). For \(N=|B_n|\) and any fixed radius \(R\), \[\frac1{N^2}\sum_{x,y\in B_n}|G(y-x)|
 \le \frac{|B_R|G(0)}N+\sup_{|z|>R}|G(z)|.\] First letting \(n\to\infty\) and then \(R\to\infty\) gives zero. The critical infrared inequality (7.3), PDF p. 68, implies the same zero limit for the nonnegative correlation average. If instead \(\liminf_{|z|\to\infty}C(z)>0\), some \(c>0\) would satisfy \(C(z)\ge c\) outside a fixed ball, forcing that average to be at least \[c\left(1-\frac{|B_R|}{N}\right).\] This contradiction proves the required liminf criterion. It does not assert pointwise correlation decay along every sequence.

For nearest-neighbour walk, \[\widehat p(k)=d^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^d\cos k_j,\qquad
 \frac{2|k|^2}{d\pi^2}\le1-\widehat p(k)\le\frac{|k|^2}{2d}
 \quad(k\in[-\pi,\pi]^d).\] Thus integrability near the origin holds exactly when \(d>2\). The walk has period two, but the frequency where \(\widehat p=-1\) is harmless: the denominator there is \(2\). The Fourier identity also follows by Abel resolvents and dominated convergence, since for \(r\ge1/2\), \[(1-r\widehat p)^{-1}\le 2(1-\widehat p)^{-1}.\] Consequently, the incidental reference to aperiodicity after (7.1) does not furnish a counterexample to the nearest-neighbour conclusion.

## Uniqueness and the direction of continuity

Theorem 1.2 now gives uniqueness at criticality. Equivalently, the plus and minus states coincide, and spin-flip symmetry forces \(m^*(\beta_c)=0\). The implication to uniqueness of all Gibbs states also agrees with Proposition C.2 (PDF p. 72): stochastic domination provides ordered couplings, while equal coordinate means force coordinate equality almost surely. Countability then yields equality of the laws.

Appendix A (PDF p. 69) proves the needed right continuity of \(m^*\) in \(\beta\). Its argument first uses finite-volume continuity with a tail error uniform over \(\delta\in[0,1]\), and only then sends the volume to infinity. Monotonicity supplies the reverse inequality. This is the correct direction for approaching criticality from the ordered phase. It is distinct from the left continuity of the free two-point function used above.
