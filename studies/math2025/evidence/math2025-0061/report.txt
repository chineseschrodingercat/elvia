Abstract: The reviewed publisher version contains an invalid closed-radius analytic step in Lemma 5.7. For odd \(p\), the exponential does not belong to the displayed Tate algebra, and the asserted unit \(\log(1+T)/T\) has a zero on its boundary. This affects the proof of the cohomological comparison, Theorem 5.5 (Theorem 1.4), without providing a counterexample to that theorem or to the categorical correspondence. A strictly smaller closed disc repairs the analytic assertion under explicit additional local smallness conditions. One Major finding is supported. Focused independent acceptance supports this finding and its stated local repair boundary; human adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions, assumptions and dependency check

The paper works over a complete algebraically closed extension \(K/\mathbb Q_p\), with \(X\) smooth and proper. A choice of lift to \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+/\xi^2\) and a continuous group splitting \(\mathrm{Exp}\) of the scalar logarithm enters the correspondence. The logarithmic differential bundle is Tate-twisted, and a Higgs field is integrable: \(\theta\wedge\theta=0\). Thus its symmetric-algebra action has commutative image \[B_\theta=\operatorname{im}\bigl(T_X\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_X}(E)\bigr),
 \qquad T_X=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_X}^{\bullet}\widetilde\Omega_X^\vee.\] This image is coherent and torsion free as an \(\mathcal O_X\)-module. Those conditions matter when applying Theorem 3.22; they must not be discarded for arbitrary coherent quotients.

The construction of the relevant \(p\)-divisible group in Theorem 3.2 does not require assuming representability of the whole Picard functor in every case. The additional representability conditions in parts of Theorem 2.4 should not be silently imposed on Theorem 5.1. Section 3.2 uses finite collections of lifted points to rigidify invertible modules. The uniqueness and common-refinement arguments, rather than an unmentioned universal single base point, support their naturality. These distinctions were checked against the actual text and diagrams.

Theorem 5.1 constructs mutually inverse exact tensor equivalences by twisting with invertible \(B\)-modules. Faithfulness of such twists and the cancellation of the tautological Higgs field explain the reconstruction of the spectral algebra. Theorem 5.5 subsequently constructs a derived comparison morphism and reduces its being a quasi-isomorphism to Lemma 5.7. The latter is a separate required step, not a consequence of the category equivalence alone. Its proof invokes local toric charts, small Higgs bundles and small pro-étale bundles, continuous cohomology, and an analytic change of coordinates between Koszul complexes.

The review treats the external foundational results on perfectoid/pro-étale cohomology, Picard functors and the local small correspondence as cited inputs; it does not claim to have independently reproved them. The full functoriality of the completed derived construction, its inverse-limit choices and every cited result remain outside independent certification.

# Problem 1: the critical closed radius in Lemma 5.7

## Claim and exact locations

Equation reference: eq:alpha. At the beginning of §3.3, p. 293 (PDF 33), the paper sets \[
 \alpha=\frac{1}{p-1}\quad(p\geq3),\qquad \alpha=2\quad(p=2)\] and correctly defines the power-series exponential on the open set \(p^\alpha\mathfrak m_K\).

Equation reference: eq:tate, eq:phi. In the proof of Lemma 5.7, p. 309 (PDF 49), the same \(\alpha\) is explicitly imported to define the closed Tate algebra \[
 \mathcal T_U=\mathcal O_U\langle p^{-\alpha}\partial_1,\ldots,p^{-\alpha}\partial_d\rangle.\] The proof then defines a continuous cocycle by \(\gamma_i\mapsto\exp(\partial_i)\). On p. 310 (PDF 50), it asserts that \[
 \varphi:R\langle p^{-\alpha}\partial\rangle
 \longrightarrow R\langle p^{-\alpha}T\rangle,\qquad
 \partial\longmapsto\log(1+T)\] is an isomorphism with inverse \(T\mapsto\exp(\partial)-1\). The next step asserts that \(\log(1+T)/T\) is a unit. These assertions supply the claimed equivariant isomorphism of the two Koszul complexes and complete Lemma 5.7 and Theorem 5.5. The original images confirm both the angle brackets and the explicit reference to §3.3; this is not an extraction ambiguity.

## Complete nonconvergence calculation

Equation reference: eq:factorial, eq:nonzero. Normalize \(v_p(p)=1\) and let \(p\) be odd. Choose \(\pi\in K\) with \(v_p(\pi)=\alpha=1/(p-1)\). The notation in [eq:tate] (#eq:tate) means, in one variable, \[A=R\langle\pi^{-1}\partial\rangle=R\langle Y\rangle,\qquad \partial=\pi Y.\] Membership in a Tate algebra requires that the coefficients in \(Y\) tend to zero. The purported exponential has coefficients \(a_n=\pi^n/n!\). For \(n=p^k\), the elementary factorial valuation gives \[
 v_p((p^k)!)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}p^{k-j}=\frac{p^k-1}{p-1}.\] Consequently, \[
 v_p(a_{p^k})=\frac{p^k}{p-1}-\frac{p^k-1}{p-1}
 =\frac1{p-1}
 \quad\text{for every }k\geq0.\] This subsequence has fixed nonzero norm, so \(a_n\) does not tend to zero. Therefore \[\exp(\partial)\notin R\langle\pi^{-1}\partial\rangle .\] The proposed cocycle is not defined in the stated algebra, and the stated inverse of [eq:phi] (#eq:phi) is not an element of that algebra. This is an infinite-subsequence proof, not an extrapolation from finite computation.

## An independent disproof of the asserted unit

Equation reference: eq:zero. Let \(\zeta_p\in K\) be a primitive \(p\)th root of unity and put \(t_0=\zeta_p-1\). The cyclotomic equation gives \(v_p(t_0)=1/(p-1)\), so \(t_0\) belongs to the closed disc in question. The logarithm converges at \(t_0\) because \(v_p(t_0)>0\). Its group law gives \[p\log(\zeta_p)=\log(\zeta_p^p)=0,\qquad
 \log(\zeta_p)=0.\] The analytic function \(u(T)=\log(1+T)/T\), with value \(1\) at \(T=0\), therefore satisfies \[
 u(t_0)=0,\qquad t_0\ne0.\] Bounded evaluation \(R\langle\pi^{-1}T\rangle\to R\), \(T\mapsto t_0\), would send a unit to a unit. Equation [eq:zero] (#eq:zero) proves that \(u\) is not a unit. Likewise the logarithm identifies the two distinct scalar points \(0\) and \(t_0\), excluding the claimed analytic coordinate isomorphism on this closed disc.

For a completely explicit parameter choice take \(p=3\). Then \(\alpha=1/2\), \(\pi^2=3\) may be chosen, and \(t_0\) is a root of \[t_0^2+3t_0+3=0.\] Its valuation is \(1/2\): the three terms can cancel only if the minimal valuations \(2v_3(t_0)\) and \(1\) agree. Equations [eq:nonzero] (#eq:nonzero) and [eq:zero] (#eq:zero) then give the two failures directly.

## Verification of the ambient assumptions

These algebraic failures occur inside the permitted geometric setting. Take \[p=3,\quad K=\mathbb C_3,\quad X=(\mathbf P^1_K)^{\mathrm{an}},
 \quad (E,\theta)=(\mathcal O_X,0).\] The field is complete and algebraically closed. The rigid space is smooth and proper and has a lift supplied by its model over \(\mathbb Q_3\). Choose any exponential splitting allowed in Definition 3.19. The unit object corresponds to the pro-étale structure bundle \(V=\mathcal O_X\).

Use the nonempty toric affinoid annulus \(U=\operatorname{Spa}(K\langle x,x^{-1}\rangle,\ldots)\) in \(X\) and its standard toric chart. Here \(d=1\), \(R=K\langle x,x^{-1}\rangle\), \(B=\mathcal O_U\), and \(J=(\partial)\). The Higgs field is zero, the corresponding action is trivial, and both objects satisfy every smallness condition. The coherence, torsion-freeness and zero-section condition on \(J\) used in Lemma 5.7 hold.

The auxiliary algebra nevertheless remains \(R\langle\pi^{-1}\partial\rangle\): smallness of the action on \(E\) does not remove its boundary. The claimed outcome is an exponential element, an analytic isomorphism and an invertible \(u\). The actual outcomes are nonconvergence, failure of injectivity on scalar points and a zero of \(u\). This is a counterexample to these auxiliary assertions. For the unit object it is not a counterexample to the final comparison, which is already expected from Hodge–Tate theory.

## Alternative readings and counterevidence

The preceding diagnosis survives the following possible readings.

1. The open exponential domain in §3.3 is correct. Replacing the later closed algebra by a suitably strict radius would avoid the problem, but that is a change to the displayed argument.

2. Smallness of \(E\) and \(V\) may ensure convergence after applying their actions. It does not imply convergence in the universal algebra [eq:tate] (#eq:tate). The zero-Higgs example already separates these assertions.

3. Lemma 4.6 and further étale localization provide room to impose stronger local bounds. They do not, without an explicit change of radius, make [eq:nonzero] (#eq:nonzero) false.

4. The global group splitting \(\mathrm{Exp}\) is not an analytic replacement for the power series in a Tate variable. It cannot turn the scalar logarithm, which has the above torsion kernel on the closed disc, into the asserted analytic isomorphism.

5. For \(p=2\) the specified \(\alpha=2\) is strictly inside the exponential convergence domain. This finding asserts no analogous failure there. With no toric variables (\(d=0\)), this particular obstruction is absent.

## Severity and precise impact

The finding is Major: a demonstrated invalid derivation is directly used to finish the core cohomological comparison (Lemma 5.7 \(\Rightarrow\) Theorem 5.5 \(\Rightarrow\) Theorem 1.4). The classification does not depend on proving that the statement is false or that repair is difficult.

The evidence does not refute Theorem 5.1, the overall research program, or Theorem 5.5 as a mathematical statement. The issue is the proof as written, for odd \(p\). No empirical experiment or author-code reproduction is needed for this analytic diagnosis.

# A verified local repair and its boundary

Proposition 1.  Let \(K\) be as above and \(R\) a \(K\)-affinoid algebra. Choose a rational \(\beta>1/(p-1)\) and an element of valuation \(\beta\) to interpret \(p^\beta\). On the closed disc of radius \(p^{-\beta}\), the power series \(\exp(z)-1\) and \(\log(1+T)\) are mutually inverse analytic coordinates, and \(\log(1+T)/T\) is a unit. The analogous coordinatewise assertion holds in any finite number of variables.

Proof. The coefficients of \(\exp(p^\beta Y)\) satisfy \[v_p(p^{\beta n}/n!)\geq
 n\bigl(\beta-1/(p-1)\bigr)\longrightarrow+\infty.\] For \(u(T)=1+\sum_{n\geq2}(-1)^{n+1}T^{n-1}/n\), the nonconstant coefficients after rescaling have valuations \[\beta(n-1)-v_p(n)
 \geq (n-1)\bigl(\beta-1/(p-1)\bigr)>0.\] Here \(v_p(n)\leq(n-1)/(p-1)\) follows, for example, from \(n\geq p^{v_p(n)}\geq1+(p-1)v_p(n)\). The valuations tend to infinity. Thus \(u=1+h\) with Gauss norm \(\|h\|<1\), and the geometric series gives its inverse. The same strict bounds show that the logarithm and exponential preserve the specified disc. Their formal inverse identities hold as convergent identities there. Applying the one-variable arguments in each coordinate proves the last assertion. ◻

For odd \(p\), \(\beta=1\) is sufficient; \(\beta=2\) works for every \(p\). Multiplying each degree-one Koszul generator by \(u_i=\log(1+T_i)/T_i\) gives the required isomorphism between the logarithm and linear-coordinate Koszul complexes at this stricter radius.

Applying this repair to Lemma 5.7 requires explicitly ensuring that both local actions extend to the algebras with \(\beta\) in place of \(\alpha\), and that the chosen lift of the invertible module agrees with this presentation. Further finite étale toric covers supply a plausible route: under \(x=(x')^{p^m}\), logarithmic differentials acquire a factor \(p^m\), while a continuous action becomes closer to the identity upon passage to an open subgroup. The paper already uses related localization in Lemma 4.6 (p. 300, PDF 40).

The proposition verifies the analytic repair itself, including the unit used in the Koszul step. It does not certify the complete global reconstruction, its choices and naturality after this substitution. That remaining argument should be checked explicitly before claiming a complete repaired proof of Theorem 5.5. No author adoption has been requested or observed.
