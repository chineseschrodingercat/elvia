This is recorded as a scope clarification, with an exact counterexample to a literal all-parameter reading, rather than a demonstrated failure of the intended nonround classification. The remaining rigidity and classification steps are not certified by these checks. Scientific adjudication is pending.

# Checks that support the nonround argument

The metric is obtained by multiplying the vertical part of the round metric tensor by \(\tau>0\) (p. 2). Thus \(\tau\) is a squared-length factor. From the radial Jacobi fields in a compact rank-one symmetric space, the vertical and horizontal squared-length factors are \(\frac14\sin^2(2t)\) and \(\sin^2t\). Their ratio is \[\frac{\sin^2(2t)}{4\sin^2t}=\cos^2t=\tau.\] The hyperbolic calculation gives \(\cosh^2t=\tau\). This agrees with (7), p. 14, and the derivation on pp. 22–23. The common length homothety is \(\sin t=\sqrt{1-\tau}\) in the compact case, and \(\sinh t=\sqrt{\tau-1}\) in the noncompact case. The round point is a limiting normalization, not a nonzero-radius member of these two formulas.

Write \(f=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathbb F\), and let the tangent decomposition be \(\mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_1\oplus\mathfrak p_2\), with vertical dimension \(f-1\). For a unit vertical \(X\), Table 5 (p. 18) gives spectra \(\{0,1/\tau\}\) on \(\mathfrak p_1\) and \(\{\tau\}\) on \(\mathfrak p_2\), omitting zero-multiplicity entries. For a unit horizontal \(Y\), it gives \(\{\tau\}\) on \(\mathfrak p_1\) and \(\{0,1,4-3\tau\}\) on \(\mathfrak p_2\), again with the stated multiplicities. For \(\tau>0\), overlap occurs only at \(\tau=1\). In particular, \(\tau=4-3\tau\) and \(\tau=1/\tau\) have no other positive solutions.

If a totally geodesic tangent subspace contains such an \(X\) or \(Y\), it is invariant under the corresponding self-adjoint Jacobi operator. With disjoint spectra, the projections onto \(\mathfrak p_1\) and \(\mathfrak p_2\) are polynomials in that operator. They therefore preserve the subspace, proving the splitting used in Lemma 4.1 (p. 21). This is a full linear-algebra justification of that step, rather than a check on sample vectors. Also, if its dimension is at least \(f\), its intersection with \(\mathfrak p_2\) has dimension at least \(d-(f-1)>0\). This validates the dimension argument on p. 24 within the nonround domain.

The outer-normal shape eigenvalues on p. 13 are \(-2\cot(2t)\) and \(-\cot t\) in the compact case. They are distinct for \(0<t<\pi/2\); the first vanishes at \(t=\pi/4\), corresponding to \(\tau=1/2\). Hence the half-parameter boundary cannot be folded into a nondegenerate argument without a separate step. Theorem 5.7 explicitly treats that boundary on pp. 30–32. Its polynomial covariant-derivative eliminations and its converse integration step have not all been independently verified here. Curvature invariance alone is not silently substituted for the complete analytic criterion in Theorem 2.1.

# Scope clarification at the round metric

Theorem A on p. 3 explicitly assumes \(\tau\ne1\), as do Lemmas 4.1–4.2, Theorem 4.4 and Theorem 5.7. Remark 1.1 (p. 4) and the discussion on p. 14 separately identify \(\tau=1\) as round. By contrast, Theorems B and C on pp. 5–6 do not repeat this exclusion; the paragraph before them on p. 4 discusses each \(\tau>0\). A literal reading that applies their tables at \(\tau=1\) fails.

For Theorem B, take \(n=1\), \(\tau=1\), and \[S^4=S^7\cap(\mathbb R^5\times\{0\}^3)
       \ \subset\ S^7_{\mathbb H,1}.\] This is a connected, complete, embedded proper submanifold of dimension \(4\ge2\). It is totally geodesic: for \(x\) in this intersection and a unit tangent vector \(v\), the ambient geodesic \(\gamma(s)=\cos(s)x+\sin(s)v\) stays in the same linear subspace. Table 1 for \(n=1\) has only dimensions \(2\) and \(3\) among its nonempty entries; Table 2 is restricted to \(\tau<1/2\). The great \(S^4\) is absent, and no ambient congruence can change dimension.

Similarly, \[S^8=S^{15}\cap(\mathbb R^9\times\{0\}^7)
       \ \subset\ S^{15}_{\mathbb O,1}\] satisfies every explicitly written completeness and dimension hypothesis of Theorem C. It is totally geodesic by the same formula. Table 1 lists no octonionic case of dimension \(8\) (its largest listed dimension is \(7\)), and Table 3 has \(\tau<1/2\). This example is absent from that literal classification as well.

The neighboring explicit exclusions, the separately discussed round case, and the spectral collision at \(\tau=1\) support the intended nonround reading. Accordingly the recorded outcome is `scope_clarification`. These examples establish the need to state that boundary accurately; they do not demonstrate an error in the nonround classification or in the exceptional projective and nonhomogeneous examples. A precise presentation is to state Theorems B and C for \(\tau>0\), \(\tau\ne1\), and give the usual round-sphere classification separately: complete connected totally geodesic images are great spheres, one congruence class per dimension. The geodesic formula proves this boundary assertion; it does not prove the remaining nonround tables.
