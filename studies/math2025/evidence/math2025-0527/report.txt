Abstract: The frozen 2023 preprint was read in full. Three substantive proof defects concern the hydrodynamic profiles, the constrained variational argument, and the passage to the global upper bound. Explicit calculations distinguish these defects from the final large-deviation formula: corrected profiles have the stated entropy cost, and a direct convexity certificate supports the intended optimizer. Five further local issues are identified. This is a review of the acquired preprint, not a claim that every item persists in the later journal article.

Author: AI-assisted technical review

Title: Technical Review of Atypical Behaviors of a Tagged Particle in Asymmetric Simple Exclusion

# Identity, hypotheses, and scope

The reviewed source is Sunder Sethuraman and S.R.S. Varadhan, Atypical behaviors of a tagged particle in asymmetric simple exclusion, arXiv:2311.07800v1, submitted 13 November 2023, 39 PDF pages [sv]. The first page carries the internal date 15 November 2023. The assignment associates this work with DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1752; that bibliographic association is not a certificate of equality with the journal text. All page locators below refer to the frozen preprint, whose printed and PDF page numbers agree.

The process is nearest-neighbor exclusion on \(\mathbb Z\), with \(p\in(1/2,1]\), \(q=1-p\), \(\gamma=p-q>0\), density \(\rho\in(0,1)\), and a particle pinned at the origin. The observation time is \(N\), and the deviation threshold is \(A\). Results for negative \(A\) require \(q>0\); the nonentropic lower-tail construction is proved only for TASEP. The main explicit upper-tail rate is \[I_\gamma(A)=
 \begin{cases}
 A\log\!\dfrac{A}{\gamma(1-\rho)}-A+\gamma(1-\rho),
       &\gamma(1-\rho)<A<\gamma,\\[3pt]
 I^Z(A)-A\log(1-\rho)-\gamma\rho,&A\geq\gamma,
 \end{cases}
\quad
 I^Z(A)=A\log c-pc-q/c+1,\] where \(c=(A+\sqrt{A^2+4pq})/(2p)\). The boundary value at the typical speed is understood by continuity.

For profiles vanishing at the origin, conditioning on \(\eta(0)=1\) must mean the pinned product law \[\delta_1(d\eta(0))\bigotimes_{x\ne0}\operatorname{Bern}
       \bigl(\rho_0(x/N)\bigr).\] Literal conditioning on a null event does not define that law. Under this intended interpretation, the Radon–Nikodym product on p. 22 omits \(x=0\); its printed extra factor is an order-one normalization error and does not change an \(N\)-scale entropy. Compact truncations, integer threshold conventions, and the order \(N\to\infty\) before the truncation radius tends to infinity are also operative.

# The hydrodynamic lower-bound profiles

Equation reference: burgers. Problem 1 (Major: several printed rarefaction profiles do not solve the conservation law).  Equation (4.5), p. 18, and the displayed evolutions in Sections 4.4 and 4.5, p. 20, are asserted to be entropic solutions of \[
 \partial_t r+\gamma(1-2r)\partial_u r=0.\] Their rarefaction branches fail this equation even at smooth interior points. The first two profiles are used to identify the typical tagged position in the changed measure.

Proof. In (4.5), take \(\gamma=\rho=1/2\) and \(a=A_\epsilon=3/8\), for instance \(A=5/16\), \(\epsilon=1/16\). These satisfy Theorem 2.2’s strict parameter restrictions. The printed boundaries are \(m(t)=-t/4\), \(s(t)=3t/8\), and the intervening density is \[r(t,u)=\frac{2(t-u)}{5t}.\] Its residual in [burgers] (#burgers) is \[\partial_t r+\tfrac12(1-2r)\partial_u r
       =-\frac{t-6u}{25t^2}.\] At \((t,u)=(1/2,0)\), strictly inside the fan, this equals \(-2/25\). Thus the discrepancy is not a choice of shock convention or a value at a discontinuity.

For Section 4.4, choose \(\gamma=\rho=1/2\) and \(A_\epsilon=1/8\), realized by \(A=3/16\), \(\epsilon=1/3\). The printed fan is \[r(t,u)=\frac{3t-2u+1}{5t},\qquad
 \partial_t r+\tfrac12(1-2r)\partial_u r
       =\frac{t+6u-3}{25t^2}.\] At \((1/2,1/2)\) its residual is \(2/25\). For Section 4.5, take \(\gamma=\rho=1/2\), \(A=-1/4\), \(\epsilon=1/8\) and \(x_0=A-\gamma-\epsilon=-7/8\). The printed fan has residual \[-\frac{4t-8u-7}{36t^2},\] equal to \(-2/9\) at \((t,u)=(1/2,x_0)\). All three residuals were independently reproduced by symbolic differentiation. ◻

Repair and counterevidence. A rarefaction from left density \(L\) to right density \(R<L\), initially at \(x_0\), has boundaries \[x_0+\gamma(1-2L)t,\qquad x_0+\gamma(1-2R)t,
 \qquad
 r(t,u)=\frac12\left(1-\frac{u-x_0}{\gamma t}\right).\] Consequently, in (4.5) one should use \[m(t)=\gamma(1-2\rho)t,\qquad s(t)=(2a-\gamma)t,\] while retaining the printed compression boundaries \(\ell(t)=\gamma-a+(2a-\gamma)t\) and \(r_+(t)=a-\gamma+2\gamma\rho+\gamma(1-2\rho)t\). The trajectory \(u=at\) remains in the density \(1-a/\gamma\) plateau until its right endpoint at \(t=1\). The left fan edge is strictly behind it, and \(\ell(t)-at=(\gamma-a)(1-t)\geq0\).

In Section 4.4 the correct fan boundaries are \(\gamma+(2a-\gamma)t\) and \(\gamma+\gamma(1-2\rho)t\). The shock from the origin has speed \(a-\gamma\rho\). Before \(t=1\) it has not met the fan, and the path \(at\) is in the plateau. In Section 4.5 the left edge of the fan should be \(x_0+\gamma(1-2\rho)t\); the right edge \(x_0+\gamma t\) is already correct. The separate particle comparison on p. 21 uses this right edge.

Equation reference: entropy. The corrected intermediate upper-tail profile has initial entropy \[\begin{aligned}
 &(\gamma-a)h_\rho(1-a/\gamma)
 +\int_{\gamma-a}^{a-\gamma+2\gamma\rho}
        h_\rho\!\left(\frac{u-a+\gamma}{2\gamma}\right)\,du \notag\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=a\log\frac{a}{\gamma(1-\rho)}-a+\gamma(1-\rho),\end{aligned}\] where \(h_\rho(z)=z\log(z/\rho)+(1-z)\log((1-z)/(1-\rho))\). The cumulative-mass calculation includes the left truncation’s vacuum-to-density shock; omitting that boundary flux would give an incorrect mass check. With that shock included, direct integration gives the tagged quantile \(a\). Three independent high-precision integrations of [entropy] (#entropy) agreed with its closed form. The error invalidates the printed invocation of the hydrodynamic limit, but these repairs support the intended conclusion of Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 2.4. They do not demonstrate a false final rate function.

# The variational proof

Problem 2 (Major: the Euler–Lagrange equation ignores active derivative constraints).  Section 6.2, pp. 26–29, imposes \(\epsilon\leq v'\leq1-\epsilon\), then derives an unconstrained Euler–Lagrange equality in Step 2 and (6.1) in Step 3. Under its stated assumptions that equality can be impossible for every admissible function.

Proof. Use the notation of Section 6.2 and take \[a=0,\quad b=1,\quad \delta=\epsilon=1/10,\quad \rho=1/2,\quad
 H(u)=u/10+u^2/1000,\quad \lambda=100.\] The function \(H\) is smooth, strictly increasing and strictly convex, with \(H(0)=0\) and \(H'(1)<\rho\). Also \(\epsilon\leq H'(\delta)=501/5000\), so this is within the third case of Proposition 6.1 and the specified regularization.

Every admissible \(v\) has \(v(\delta)\geq0\) and \(v(u)\geq(u-\delta)/10\). Hence \[H(u)-v(u)\leq 1/100+u^2/1000\leq11/1000.\] The asserted equality \((h_\rho'(v'))'=\lambda e^{-\lambda(H-v)}\) would imply \[h_\rho'(v'(1))-h_\rho'(v'(\delta))
 \geq 90e^{-1.1}>29.95.\] But \(v'\in[1/10,9/10]\) forces the left side to be at most \(h_\rho'(9/10)-h_\rho'(1/10)=2\log9<4.40\). The contradiction also applies to the claimed weak derivative, by integrating over interior subintervals and taking endpoints. The constrained minimizer exists by compactness and lower semicontinuity; it therefore cannot satisfy the printed equality. ◻

Dependency and repair. This is the foundation for the convexity and contact-set argument leading to Proposition 6.1, Proposition 2.9 and both upper bounds. A variational inequality or a proof that the constraints are inactive is required; the latter is false in the example. Thus the core derivation needs replacement.

Equation reference: certificate. There is direct counterevidence to a stronger allegation that the intended optimizer is wrong. For the corrected candidate \(v_*\), put \(\psi=h_\rho'(v_*')\). Its slope increases only while \(v_*=H\), and is constant on the initial tangent and final slope-\(\rho\) portions. For any admissible \(w\), convexity gives \[
 \int_a^b\!\bigl[h_\rho(w')-h_\rho(v_*')\bigr]\,du
 \geq [\psi(w-v_*)]_a^b-\int_{[a,b]}(w-v_*)\,d\psi\geq0.\] Here \(d\psi\geq0\) is supported on the contact set, where \(w-v_*\leq0\); the initial boundary difference is zero. At the right boundary either \(\psi=0\) or the candidate is still on \(H\) with \(\psi\leq0\), giving the same sign. For the quadratic obstacle used in Proposition 2.9, a zero initial slope is handled by truncation: the remaining boundary term is \(O(\delta|\log\delta|)\) and vanishes. Strict convexity gives uniqueness. This proves the required candidate optimality without the invalid regularization equation, after correcting the printed formulas below.

# The global upper-bound step

Problem 3 (Major: the displayed local bounds do not imply (5.7)).  Section 5.1, Step 2, p. 23, replaces a minimum containing an indicator by an infimum restricted to where that indicator is one. The preceding bound has already discarded the superexponential exclusion of the other cases. Its asserted compact cover is also not justified for the center-dependent neighborhoods used there.

Let \[F(\beta)=v_\beta(1,A)-v_\beta(0,0),\qquad
 J_i=\mathbf1_{\{F(\beta_i)<\epsilon+\delta\}}.\] The printed minimum is \(\min_i J_i(K(\beta_i)-2\delta)\). An inadmissible center contributes zero. Removing that center and taking a positive constrained infimum cannot follow from this expression: even the two values \(J_1=0\), \(J_2=1\), \(K(\beta_2)-2\delta=1\) give \(0\), not \(1\). The correct local conclusion for a strictly inadmissible center is a superexponential bound, or a logarithmic indicator equal to \(-\infty\), rather than the finite bound produced in (5.6).

The centers are continuous densities strictly between zero and one, while \(\mathcal M_R\) contains zero and saturated densities. Density of the centers does not make arbitrary center-dependent small balls a cover: choosing each radius less than half its distance from the zero measure excludes zero from every ball. Lemma 3.4 and (5.4) permit shrinking these radii, so the cover requires an additional construction. Further, the assertion on p. 22 that \(N\pi_t^N/(N+1)\in\mathcal M_R\) is false whenever the empirical measure has an atom: no positive atom satisfies \(\mu([a,b])\leq b-a\) on arbitrarily short intervals. A uniform neighborhood approximation by spreading each atom over its lattice cell would give the intended asymptotic compactness.

Repair route and impact. One can organize the argument around neighborhoods of every \(W\in\mathcal M_R\), using the Bernoulli entropy dual formula \[K(W)=\sup_{f\in C([-R,R])}
 \left\{\int f\,dW-\int_{-R}^R\log(1-\rho+\rho e^f)\,du\right\}.\] For each center choose a near-optimal bounded \(f\), then a neighborhood where its affine entropy lower bound persists. Keep the superexponential estimate for centers with \(F(W)>0\), with a separate tolerance near \(F=0\). The product Bernoulli moment generating function and the uniform quenched tagged-particle bound then yield the entropy cost on the remaining neighborhoods. A finite subcover of this actual cover, followed by the lower-semicontinuity argument in Step 3, is the appropriate route to (5.7). Extension of each local flux estimate to these neighborhoods must be stated.

This repairs the logic the proof needs; it is not a claim that the displayed chain already proves it. The defect is central to Theorems 2.7–2.8 and Corollary 2.10. No counterexample to those final conclusions is given. The step profile used in this repair is \(\mathbf1_{\{x\leq0\}}\), as in Proposition 3.3; the opposite inequality printed on p. 21 is a local orientation error. Also, the no-restriction assertion beyond \(A+\gamma\) follows directly by adding to \(\int_0^{A+\gamma}\rho_0\leq\gamma\), avoiding the unnecessary intermediate subtraction on p. 24.

# Local formula and hypothesis corrections

Problem 4 (Minor: the optimizer displays and the cost identity need normalization corrections).  Proposition 2.9, p. 12, has an incorrect translation in both terminal affine pieces. Theorem 2.7, p. 11, omits the initial empty-interval cost in its equality involving \(K_1\). The first case of Proposition 6.1, p. 26, omits the left-endpoint translation.

At \(z_\rho=A+\gamma(2\rho-1)\) the printed affine piece \(\rho(u-\gamma(2\rho-1)+A)+G(\gamma(2\rho-1))\) jumps upward by \(2\rho A\) from the obstacle branch. It is not even continuous and exceeds the obstacle near the join. The correct expression is \[\rho(u-z_\rho)+G(z_\rho-A).\] This expression already appears correctly in Section 6.1, p. 26, providing direct internal counterevidence to failure of the intended optimizer. The \(x\) in the initial branch of \(v_2\) should likewise be \(u\).

Even with the corrected optimizer, \[K_1(v_*)=-\gamma\log(1-\rho)-\gamma\rho,
\quad
 I_\gamma(A)=I^Z(A)+K_1(v_*)-(A-\gamma)\log(1-\rho)
 \quad(A\geq\gamma).\] For \(\gamma=\rho=1/2\), \(A=4/5\), the missing cost is \(0.20794415416798359\ldots>0\). Formula (5.11), p. 24, and Step 6, p. 25, already include it. Finally, the zero-cost candidate in Proposition 6.1 is \(\rho(u-a)\), with the corresponding obstacle condition. For example, \(a=1\), \(b=2\), \(\rho=1/2\), \(H(u)=1+u^2\) satisfies the printed assumptions but \(v(u)=\rho u\) violates \(v(a)=0\). These are false displayed assertions with local repairs, not a refutation of the rate after restoring the intended normalization.

Problem 5 (Minor: the preliminary exponential martingale has the wrong sign).  The last display of Section 3.3, p. 13, subtracts the left-jump contribution inside the compensator. For \(q>0\) this expression is not a martingale.

Writing \(a_\pm(s)=1-\eta_s(X_s\pm1)\), the correct compensator is \[p(e^\lambda-1)a_+(s)+q(e^{-\lambda}-1)a_-(s).\] For the printed expression, under the pinned equilibrium law its expectation has derivative at zero \(2q(e^{-\lambda}-1)(1-\rho)\). At \(p=3/4\), \(\rho=1/2\), \(\lambda=\log2\), this is \(-1/8\), whereas a martingale starting at one has constant expectation. Bounded jump rates justify the short-time generator calculation. Equation (4.3), p. 16, and the martingale actually used in Section 8, p. 33, have the correct sign. Thus the operative change-of-measure and step-profile upper bound survive this local correction.

Problem 6 (Minor: the stationary error in (1.3) is incompatible with the stated initialization).  Equation (1.3), p. 3, writes \(X_t=\operatorname{Poi}_t+\chi_t\), with \(\operatorname{Poi}\) a Poisson process and \(\chi\) stationary. Under the usual convention that the Poisson process starts at zero, and the paper’s \(X_0=0\), this cannot hold for genuine ASEP.

At zero the equality forces \(\chi_0=0\) almost surely; stationarity forces \(\chi_t=0\) almost surely at every fixed time. It would follow that \(X_t\) is nonnegative and Poisson. But for \(q>0\), the left neighbor is empty initially with probability \(1-\rho\), and \[\mathbb P_\rho(X_t=-1)=q(1-\rho)t+O(t^2)>0\] for sufficiently small \(t\). The claimed representation therefore needs the precise initialization/error formulation of its cited approximation result. A stationary coboundary or a separately specified random offset would avoid this elementary contradiction, but neither is printed or independently verified here. The authors explicitly develop the main upper-tail argument independently of this approximation; the present finding does not refute its LLN or CLT.

Problem 7 (Minor: the introductory path large-deviation formula has the wrong sign).  The display defining the path large-deviation cost on p. 5 has \(\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1}\log\mathbb P(B_\delta)=K(\rho_0)+I_{\rm JV}(\rho)\). Its right side must have a minus sign.

Both displayed costs are nonnegative, whereas the left side cannot exceed zero. Choose a nonconstant smooth compact perturbation of the background density, of sufficiently small amplitude and derivative that its entropic evolution is smooth up to time one. It is among the stated nice profiles, has strictly positive initial entropy and zero entropic dynamical cost. The printed equality would assign it a positive logarithmic probability. The negative-cost convention in the main theorem statements and the entropy argument on pp. 17–19 are consistent; this introductory sign error does not invalidate those calculations.

Problem 8 (Minor: the optional compactification proof uses an inadmissible generator test).  Section 7.3, p. 30, applies stationarity as \(0=\mathbb E_\mu L_{\rm ZR}\zeta(k)\) for every invariant measure on the compactified space allowing \(\zeta(k)=\infty\). This requires an additional domain and limiting argument.

The coordinate function \(\zeta(k)\) is neither bounded nor finite on that space. In particular, the point mass on the all-infinite configuration is invariant, but \(L_{\rm ZR}\zeta(k)\) is not defined there by differences of coordinate values. Thus stationarity alone cannot justify the printed equalities for every invariant measure. Bounded truncations give boundary terms at infinity which must be treated, rather than silently replaced by the finite-coordinate formula.

This is a gap in the optional self-contained LLN proof, not a demonstrated false invariant-measure classification or false speed \(\gamma\). The alternative cited by the paper was checked in the acquired Tracy–Widom preprint [tw], pp. 1–3. Its Theorem 2 assumes \(0<p_{\rm TW}<q_{\rm TW}\) and gives tight fluctuations of \(x_1(t/\gamma)+t\) on the square-root scale. Set \(p_{\rm TW}=q\), \(q_{\rm TW}=p\), and reflect by \(X_s=1-x_1(s)\). Its initial positive-integer step becomes the required step on \(x\leq0\), and \(X_s/s\to\gamma\) follows for \(0<q<p\). At \(q=0\) the leading TASEP particle has an unobstructed rate-one Poisson clock, so the same LLN follows directly. The bounded drift and vanishing scaled martingale in Lemma 7.1 upgrade fixed-time limits to the required uniform limit. This establishes the cited route’s applicability without re-proving its Fredholm asymptotics. A proof via compactification would still need bounded-test limits accounting for mass at infinity.

# Positive checks, boundaries, and review limits

The three lower-bound entropy mechanisms were traced separately. For \(A\geq\gamma\), the tilted birth–death rates \(pc,q/c\) have difference \(A\), and the empty interval contributes the cost isolated above. The signs of the blocking terms in (4.3)–(4.4) agree with the chosen direction of tilt. For the TASEP lower tail, the initial entropy in (4.7) plus the shock cost in (4.9) simplifies to \(A\log(A/(1-\rho))-A+1-\rho\); an independent numerical check agrees. The special \(A=0\) hole-clock construction yields \(1-\rho\) by cancellation of the two \(\rho\log\rho\) terms. The negative-\(A\) construction is restricted to \(q>0\).

The rate is continuous at \(A=\gamma\): both branches give \(-\gamma\log(1-\rho)-\gamma\rho\). Their first derivatives agree there, and the birth–death Legendre derivative is \(\log c\). At \(p=1\) the rate reduces to the Poisson formula throughout the nonnegative upper tail; the \(A<0\) construction is not extended to that endpoint.

The current/tagged-particle ordering, Hopf–Lax conjugate, and superexponential flux argument in Sections 3, 8, 9 and Appendix A were followed. Integer threshold discrepancies vanish after scaling. The LLN proof on p. 29 should compare levels strictly above and below the limiting quantile; convergence in probability does not determine a tail probability exactly at that quantile. In Section 9, the removable case \(b=L\) should be handled directly through the integral of \(F'\) rather than division by \(b-L\). In Section 8, attractiveness propagates monotonicity of the semigroup, which supplies the two-time correlation used in (8.1); equal-time association alone would not suffice. The exponential optimization is an infimum of \(-\lambda A+p(e^\lambda-1)+q(e^{-\lambda}-1)\), consistent with the final displayed rate despite the intervening typographical wording.

The one-block replacement, shock coupling, and construction of nonentropic process measures remain imported results; their complete proofs were not all independently reconstructed. The Tracy–Widom reference was examined at its definitions and Theorem 2, not read or re-proved in its entirety. No author code was executed, and lack of author code is not a mathematical finding. The independent symbolic calculations and high-precision integrations support the explicit certificates and do not replace the analytic proofs.

The official arXiv metadata inspected for this review still lists v1. The bounded correction search did not establish a formal resolving erratum; absence of a correction is not certified. The later journal version was not silently substituted for the acquired preprint. Three findings are Major because the printed derivations of central results fail; the other five are local corrections or a supplementary proof gap. No whole-paper failure, false final rate theorem or independent acceptance is asserted.
