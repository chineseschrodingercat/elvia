Abstract: This review concerns Spencer Leslie’s arXiv 1911.07907v3 author manuscript, 89 pages, bound to the supplied source manifest. One Major defect is identified in the support lemma used in globalization, and two Minor defects concern spherical normalization and a lattice equivalence. Explicit counterexamples satisfy the original local hypotheses. Corrected local statements are proved. These examples do not disprove the main endoscopic fundamental lemma. Equivalence with the journal Version of Record has not been established.

# Identity, scope, and principal assessment

The reviewed work is The endoscopic fundamental lemma for unitary Friedberg–Jacquet periods, by Spencer Leslie, associated by the supplied metadata with DOI 10.4007/annals.2025.201.3.1. All page references are to physical PDF pages 1–89, agreeing with printed pages, of arXiv 1911.07907v3. They are not asserted to describe the final journal article.

All 89 pages of extracted text were read and all 89 rendered source pages were visually inspected. The principal proof chain checked is Theorem 11.1, Theorem 5.3, Proposition 5.2, Proposition 4.8, Theorem 3.5, Proposition 3.12, and Theorem 2.12. The review checks definitions, regular semisimple restrictions, quantifiers, contraction maps, normalizations, central quotients, local spherical computations, and globalization. External results remain distinguished from independently checked arguments.

Lemma 11.5, p. 83, is false under the stated assumptions. Integral invertible points and \(Z\)-regularity do not imply that central orbital support consists only of units. The lemma is used to obtain nonzero central orbital factors in Proposition 11.4, then cancelled in proving Theorem 11.1. This is a Major defect in the core derivation under the requested criterion. A finite enlargement of the bad-place set provides a natural repair; its corrected local lemma is proved below.

The rational orbit quantified over in Lemma 10.6 is too large for its spherical normalization. The fixed-lattice equivalence after (3.8) is also false. Both have limited demonstrated impact, as their respective applications use a smaller orbit and a variable intermediate lattice.

For the split model \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&X\\Y&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), the characteristic polynomial is \(\det(t^2I-XY)\). A zero eigenvalue of \(XY\) would give a repeated zero root. Thus Lemma 2.4 correctly puts the regular semisimple locus inside the invertible locus, where Lemma 2.3’s torsor argument is used. The fibre integration and centralizer identification in Lemma 2.5 respect that domain. External cohomological and convergence inputs were not independently re-proved.

The minus sign in contraction (2.1) and the later positive Gram-matrix notation in density formula (3.8) were kept distinct. The \(q\) powers of Lemma 3.2 agree with \(q_E=q^2\) and the substitutions \(Z_i\mapsto q^{-b}X_i\) and \(Z_{a+j}\mapsto q^{-a}Y_j\). For \(n=a+b\) and \(d=d_a+d_b\), \[d(n-1)-d_ab-d_ba=d_a(a-1)+d_b(b-1).\] This checks the factorization in Proposition 3.12. Its unipotent volume \(q^{2\sum(i-1)\lambda_i}\) combines with \(\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle\) to give \(q^{d(n-1)}\), also correctly at \(d=0\).

The two Jacquet–Rallis transfers (4.3) and (4.7) were followed separately. The parabolic descent Jacobian in Proposition 4.8 is \(|\det m_1|_F^b\); combining it with the modular character gives the stated twist. Base change squares Satake variables, so the twist \(|\cdot|_F^{s/2}\) reproduces the twist over \(E\). This checks commutation directly, without relying on the explanatory support assertion on p. 39.

For Proposition 5.2, support gives vanishing for \(|q(a)|_F>1\), and the unit-norm reduction handles \(|q(a)|_F=1\). Fourier invariance of the self-dual lattice and upper-unipotent invariance for parameters in \(\mathfrak p_F^{-1}\) then give the conclusion using the standard Weil representation. The generation assertion on p. 43 is consistent: upper and lower elementary matrices with such parameters produce a diagonal uniformizer matrix, whose conjugations give elementary matrices at arbitrarily negative valuations. They generate \(\mathrm{SL}_2(F)\). Local constancy and density handle \(q(a)=0\). The cited Fourier-transfer theorem remains an external input.

The four central twists in Section 9 agree with the index-four norm subgroup in the two-dimensional centre. The factors \(4\) and \(4L(1,\eta)^2\) were traced through (9.4)–(9.5), including the idele-class volume ratio on p. 72. The restrictions to nice functions and two distinct split places are material. Lemma 11.2 uses the norm subgroup, whose even valuations explain the character period \(\pi/\log q\). Its Fourier uniqueness argument agrees with that quotient. The rank shift of Section 11.2, using \(\mathbf 1_{K_{n-1,E}}\otimes\varphi\), has the correct contraction and proves the stated dependence of Theorem 2.12 on Theorem 11.1.

# Problem 1: central support lemma

## Location and claim

Severity: Major. Lemma 11.5, p. 83, after the choice of \(S\) in Proposition 11.4 on p. 82, asserts \[\operatorname{Orb}(\mathbf 1_{K'_v},bz)\ne0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z\in Z_{G'}(\mathcal O_v). \tag{P1}\] Outside \(S\), the stated assumptions are \(a\in K_v\) and \(b\in K'_v\). The points are \(Z\)-regular semisimple: the two specified invariants are nonzero, not necessarily units. The supplied TeX has the choice and proof at lines 3304–3332, with the claim at 3315–3320.

## A counterexample in the original domain

Let \[F=\mathbb Q_3,\quad E=F(s),\quad s^2=2,\quad \bar s=-s,\quad
 a=1+s,\quad c=2as=4+2s.\] Since \(t^2-2\) is irreducible modulo 3 with unit discriminant, \(E/F\) is unramified quadratic. We have \(\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-1\) and \(\operatorname{Nm}(c)=8\); thus \(a,c,s,2\) are units. Write \(M^*=\bar M^t\) and set \[B=\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B'=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&c\end{pmatrix},\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(1/3,3).\] Both \(B,B'\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathcal O_E)\), and \[BB^*=\begin{pmatrix}9&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}=:T,\qquad
 B'B'^*=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&9\end{pmatrix}=DTD. \tag{1}\] Their determinants are \(-c,c\); \(\det T=8\). First use the Hermitian model \((x,y)=(1,I_2)\). Then \(b=(1,B)\in K'\) matches \(\gamma=(1,T)\in K\). Both off-diagonal coordinates of \(T\) are 1, so their nonzero product gives a closed orbit of maximal dimension under the rank-one Jacquet–Rallis action. Thus it is regular semisimple. Also \(\operatorname{Tr}(A)=9\ne0\) and \(d=1\ne0\), giving \(Z\)-regularity. All stated local integrality conditions hold.

Take \[z=(9,3I_2),\qquad h_1=9\in H'(F),\qquad u=(DB)^{-1}B'.\] Equation (1) implies \(uu^*=I_2\). With \(h_2=(1,u)\in U(1)\times U(I_2)\) the embedding of Section 6 gives \[h_1^{-1}bzh_2=(1,B')\in K'. \tag{2}\] The nonnegative integrand of \(\operatorname{Orb}(\mathbf 1_{K'},bz)\) is 1 on a nonempty open neighborhood of \((h_1,h_2)\), of positive Haar measure. Regular semisimplicity gives convergence, hence \[\operatorname{Orb}(\mathbf 1_{K'},bz)>0,\qquad z\notin Z_{G'}(\mathcal O_F), \tag{3}\] contradicting (P1).

To use exactly the Part 2 form, put \(w_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(\xi=s\), and \(y_0=sw_2\). Set \[P=\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\-a/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q=\operatorname{diag}(s,1)P.\] Then \(PP^*=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(QQ^*=y_0\), \(\det P=a\), and \(\det Q=sa\). In particular \(Q\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathcal O_E)\). Let \(B_0=BQ^{-1}\), \(B'_0=B'Q^{-1}\), and \(u_0=QuQ^{-1}\). We have \[B_0y_0B_0^*=T,\quad u_0y_0u_0^*=y_0,\quad
 \operatorname{diag}(1/9,1)B_0(3I_2)u_0=B'_0.\] Moreover \(\det B_0=-2\) and \(\det B'_0=2\). Thus no nonintegral change of compact subgroup is hidden in the example. These matrices are algebraic over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\).

For the trivial unramified central character the nonunit central coset of \(z\) contributes positively. The next displayed formula in Lemma 11.5, which restricts the central integral to units, omits this contribution. No numerical integration is needed.

## Diagnosis, counterevidence, and impact

The proof uses a nonzero integral invariant as if it were a unit. Also (7.8) gives the true scalings: \(\operatorname{Tr}(A)\) scales by \(\operatorname{Nm}(z_2/z_1)\) and \(d\) by \(\operatorname{Nm}(z_2)\). The linear scalings on p. 83 do not describe that action.

Counterevidence limiting the conclusion is the freedom to enlarge a finite set of bad places. The example has the nonunit \(\operatorname{Tr}(A)=9\) at precisely a place that such an enlargement can exclude. It does not refute Proposition 11.4 or the main theorem. Nevertheless the stated lemma is false and supplies the central nonvanishing/equality used in Proposition 11.4. The final paragraph on p. 85 cancels the other local factors to obtain (11.2), leading through Theorems 11.1, 5.3, and 3.5 to 2.12. This is a core derivation defect even though repair is possible.

## A verified local repair

Enlarge \(S\) to contain every place where \(\operatorname{Tr}(A(b))\) or \(d(b)\) is not a unit, and any bad places for forms and unimodular lattices. This is finite because the invariants are fixed nonzero global algebraic numbers.

Assume locally that \(b\in K'\), both these invariants are units, and the forms are integral unimodular. Put \(r=v_E(z_1)\), \(t=v_E(z_2)\). If the orbit of \(bz\) meets \(K'\), invariant integrality gives \(t-r\ge0\) and \(t\ge0\). The contracted determinant must be a unit; its central scaling is \(\operatorname{Nm}(z_2)^{n+1}\operatorname{Nm}(z_1)^{-n}\). Therefore \[(n+1)t-nr=0.\] For \(n\ge1\), \(0=(n+1)t-nr\ge t\ge0\), so \(r=t=0\). Conversely unit central elements preserve \(K'\), and \(b\in K'\) gives a positive orbital integral. This proves the corrected equivalence. An unramified character is trivial on unit central elements, so the stipulated unit-volume normalization also gives the corrected central integral equality.

This proves the local repair and shows why the finite global adjustment is available. It does not certify every remaining analytic step of a revised proof or assert author adoption.

# Problem 2: the orbit in the spherical normalization

## Location and claim

Severity: Minor. Lemma 10.6, p. 76, quantifies over \((x,y)\in G'(F)*(w_n,w_{n+1})\), with a fixed spherical test function, and asserts \[J^{x,y,\natural}_{\operatorname{BC}(\pi)}(f')=I^\natural_\pi(\operatorname{BC}(f')). \tag{P2}\] Its proof invokes Lemma 8.6, p. 61, whose normalization concerns the integral orbit \(K'*(w_n,w_{n+1})\) and odd residue characteristic. Relevant source TeX is at lines 3056–3067, with diagonal equivariance in Section 8.3 and the relative character in Definition 8.10.

## Counterexample and calculation

Use the same unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_3\), an unramified additive character, and \(n=1\). Let \(\chi\) be the unramified unitary character with \(\chi(3)=i\). Its base change \(\Pi_1=\chi\circ\operatorname{Nm}\) satisfies \(\Pi_1(3)=\chi(9)=-1\). Choose an irreducible tempered unramified generic \(\pi_2\) of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(F)\), for example normalized induction of \(1\) and the unramified quadratic character. Their ratio is not \(|\cdot|^{\pm1}\), so this principal series is irreducible; its base change is also generic. Set \(\pi=\chi\boxtimes\pi_2\) and \(f'=\mathbf 1_{K'}\). For these tempered parameters the normalizing local factors are finite and nonzero.

At \((x_0,y_0)=(1,sw_2)\), formulas (8.1), (8.3), and (8.7) give 1 for the normalized pairing, Rankin–Selberg functional, and FLO functionals on normalized spherical vectors. Thus \[J^{1,y_0,\natural}_{\operatorname{BC}(\pi)}(\mathbf 1_{K'})=1,\qquad
 I^\natural_\pi(\mathbf 1_K)=1.\] Replace only \(x_0\) by \(x'=9=3*x_0\). Then \((9,y_0)\) lies in the full rational orbit in (P2), but not in its integral orbit. The diagonal invariance defining the FLO functional says \[\alpha(3*x,\Pi_1(3)W)=\alpha(x,W).\] It follows that \(\alpha_9(W_0)=-\alpha_1(W_0)\), with the same normalizing \(L\)-factor. Definition 8.10 uses the contragredient functional; its scalar at 3 is also \(-1\), so the sign persists. The spherical projector has one contributing spherical line; the Rankin–Selberg factor, second FLO factor, and pairing are unchanged. Hence \[J^{9,y_0,\natural}_{\operatorname{BC}(\pi)}(\mathbf 1_{K'})
       =-1\ne1=I^\natural_\pi(\operatorname{BC}(\mathbf 1_{K'})).\] This refutes the quantifier with all original generic, unitary, and unramified conditions, already in odd residue characteristic.

## Impact, counterevidence, and repair

Restrict Lemma 10.6 to \(K'*(w_n,w_{n+1})\). Diagonal equivariance and \(K'\)-invariance of the spherical vector preserve the FLO value at the integral base point. This proves the orbit portion of the corrected assertion using the manuscript’s normalization theorem in odd residue characteristic.

The application on p. 85 uses the fixed base pair \((w_n,w_{n+1})\), not the false extension to every rationally equivalent form. This directly limits the demonstrated impact. Corollary 10.8 on p. 78 also invokes the overbroad computation and unit test functions, so its proof requires a scope check. The counterexample does not by itself disprove its comparison constant after test functions have been correctly transported. No such stronger conclusion is asserted. The residue-characteristic-two normalization remains a separate unchecked input.

# Problem 3: fixed and variable intermediate lattices

## Location and claim

Severity: Minor. The paragraph after (3.8), p. 28, asserts that integrality of \(T=xx^\tau\) is equivalent to \[L=x^\tau\Lambda\ \subset\ \Lambda\ \subset\
 L^\vee=x^{-1}\Lambda. \tag{P3}\] This substitutes a fixed lattice for the arbitrary intermediate self-dual lattice in the following argument. The claim occurs in the supplied Section 3.3 TeX paragraph describing the normalized local representation density.

## A regular counterexample

In the same extension set \(\Lambda=\mathcal O_E^2\) with form \(I_2\), retain \(a=1+s\), \(\operatorname{Nm}(a)=-1\), and define \[C=\begin{pmatrix}a&-a/2\\1&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 h=C\operatorname{diag}(3,1/3)C^{-1}
  =\begin{pmatrix}5/3&4a/3\\4/(3a)&5/3\end{pmatrix}.\] Here \(C^*C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(hh^*=I_2\), and \(\det C=a\) is a unit. Set \(y=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\) and \(x=yh\). Then \[xx^*=yy^*=\operatorname{diag}(1,4),\] an integral invertible matrix with distinct eigenvalues. Restriction to the regular semisimple locus does not remove this example.

Because \(y,y^*\) preserve \(\Lambda\), \[L=x^*\Lambda=h^{-1}\Lambda=L^\vee.\] But \(h^{-1}e_1=(5/3,-4/(3a))^t\) is not integral, so \(L\not\subset\Lambda\). Likewise \(he_1\) is not integral, giving \(\Lambda\not\subset L\). Both containments in (P3) fail. Nevertheless \(xh^{-1}=y\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathcal O_E)\), so the density integral in (3.8) is positive. The matrix identities were checked exactly.

## Correct statement and limited effect

Applying \(x^{-1}\) to \(xx^*\Lambda\subset\Lambda\) proves \[T\text{ integral}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\subset L^\vee.\] For a specified \(u\in U(I_2)\) the stronger condition is \[xu\in\operatorname{End}(\Lambda)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\subset u\Lambda\subset L^\vee.\] The right-hand inclusion is \(xu\Lambda\subset\Lambda\), and duality gives the other. In the example the intermediate lattice may be \(L\) itself.

The next paragraph on p. 28 already uses \(L'=u\Lambda\) and gives the correct bijection. Remark 3.9, p. 29, says that the density interpretation is not used in the paper. The subsequent integral/Hecke calculation does not require the false fixed-lattice equivalence. These are direct counterevidence to a main-theorem failure. The corrected equivalences repair the explanation without refuting the density identity.
