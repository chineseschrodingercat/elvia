# Definitions, hypotheses, and dependency audit

The CM type of \(L\) is lifted as specified in Section 1.1, and \(2d=[L:\mathbb Q]\). The base \(R\) is an \(\mathcal O_{L^{\rm Gal}}[1/d_L]\)-algebra contained in \(\mathbb C\) when integral specializations are used. A characteristic-zero domain need not contain the inverse of every integer. Critical Hecke type is \(\mu=\beta-\alpha\), with \(\alpha\geq\mathbf1\); derivative indices instead use \(\mu=\beta-\alpha-\mathbf1\), with \(\alpha,\beta\geq0\). These conventions must not be conflated.

The finite-index subgroup \(\Gamma\subset\mathcal O_L^\times\) is not everywhere torsion-free. Sections 3.2, 3.4 and the opening of Section 5 impose that condition; later congruence unit groups can have torsion. The original integral class requires the specified isogeny and its dual to be etale. Section 2.5 deliberately removes invertibility of \(N\mathfrak f\), which matters in Problem 2. Ordinary \(p\)-adic arguments require \(p\nmid d_L\), disjoint CM prime sets, and an initial point of order prime to \(p\). A generic-fiber identity alone does not establish integral descent.

Relevant dependencies in the manuscript are \[\begin{gathered}

# Substantive findings

## Problem 1 (Major): integral fundamental-cycle normalization fails for torsion

Location. Proposition 2.27, pages 22–23, the homology surjection and diagram (2.4.2); Corollary 2.29, page 24. See `Coherent-Eisenstein-classes.tex`, lines 792–852, especially 811–831.

The proof asserts that, for a free finite-index \(\Gamma'\), homological transfer \(H_{d-1}(\Gamma,\mathbb Z)\to H_{d-1}(\Gamma',\mathbb Z)\) is onto. It chooses an integral preimage of a generator and requires evaluation after cohomological restriction to be \([\Gamma:\Gamma']\) times evaluation before restriction.

Take \(L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), \(d=1\), \(\Gamma=\{1,-1\}\), and \(\Gamma'=1\). Both \(H_0\)’s are \(\mathbb Z\). Inclusion-induced corestriction is the identity, whereas transfer sends \([1]\mapsto[1]+[-1]=2[1]\). It has image of index two and no generator has an integral preimage. This directly contradicts the step used to choose \(\xi\).

The obstruction is integral, not an orientation choice. Let \(R=\mathbb Z[\zeta_3,1/3]\), so \(2\notin R^\times\), and take the critical weight \(\overline\sigma-\sigma\), i.e. \(\alpha=0,\beta=1\). The coefficient line is a trivial \(\Gamma\)-module, and restriction on \(H^0(\Gamma,R)=R\) is the identity. Free-group evaluation is the identity. Diagram (2.4.2) would require an \(R\)-linear evaluation \(a\mapsto ca\) with \(2c=1\), impossible in this \(R\). This base is compatible with CM geometry: \(y^2+y=x^3\) has discriminant \(-27\), admits the \(\zeta_3\)-action, and is smooth at two. The universal group-homology failure is independent of a chosen nonzero class.

Counterevidence. For torsion-free groups of rank \(d-1\), top transfer between finite-index lattices is an isomorphism. Over \(\mathbb Q\), or after inverting the torsion order, a rational fundamental cycle provides the intended normalization. Sections explicitly restricted to torsion-free groups avoid this obstruction.

Repair and impact. Restrict the integral proposition to torsion-free groups, or invert \(|\Gamma_{\rm tors}|\), and then prove the required integral descent for congruence units. Definition 2.28, Corollary 2.29 and Lemma 5.15 use this normalization. No counterexample to the final regularized \(L\)-value is established. The full norm-one subgroup of \((L\otimes\mathbb R)^\times\) on page 23 is also not the contractible positive-real model later supplied on page 33; correcting that notation does not remove the transfer obstruction.

## Problem 2 (Major): the refined comparison omits an isogeny-degree factor

Location. Theorem 2.30, pages 24–25; Proposition 2.31, pages 25–26; `Coherent-Eisenstein-classes.tex`, lines 873–993.

Work over \(\mathbb C\), with \(d=1,\Gamma=1\), and a square CM elliptic curve \(E\). Let \(\mathfrak f=(3)\), \(\mathfrak c=(2)\). Identify \(E(\mathfrak f^{-1})\) with \(E\) by multiplication by three on the fractional ideal. The isogeny \([\mathfrak f]\) and its dual are then \([3]\). Transport the chosen bases consistently and take \(\alpha=\beta=0\), of critical type \(-\sigma\).

At the zeroth moment the bundle map \([3]_\#\) is the identity. The construction on page 24 additionally applies \([3]^{\vee *}\) to the top differential, multiplying it by three. The left side of Proposition 2.31 then applies \([3]^*\), multiplying by three again. For a zeroth specialization \(s(x)\omega\), this gives \[[3]^*({}_{[3]}\operatorname{EK}^{0,0}(f,x))=9s(x)\omega,\qquad
 \operatorname{TSym}^0([3]_\#)\operatorname{EK}^{0,0}(f,x)=s(x)\omega.\]

A nonzero admissible specialization exists without numerical approximation. Use \(f=\delta_0-\delta_t\) on \(E[2]\), with \(t\neq0\) two-torsion. It is trace zero and \(\Gamma\)-invariant. The zeroth component is a meromorphic differential with residues \(1,-1\) at two distinct points. It has two simple poles and at most two zeros on an elliptic curve. Of the eight nonzero three-torsion points, none is a pole and some \(x\) is not a zero. This \(x\) meets the original hypotheses. The displayed comparison therefore requires \(9s(x)=s(x)\neq0\).

Counterevidence. The proof’s connection and moment diagrams do express functoriality over the localized base. They account for derivative and moment factors, but not the extra top differential inserted before differentiating. Transporting all structures under the curve isomorphism does not turn two multiplication-by-three maps into the identity.

Repair and impact. This comparison needs the factor \(\deg[3]=9\). The analogous determinant composition accounts for an isogeny degree in higher dimension. Dividing the refined construction by that degree can repair the rational comparison, but is not an integral repair when the degree is not a unit. Theorem 4.9 explicitly invokes Proposition 2.31 for denominator control, so that proof needs reconstruction. No counterexample to Theorem 4.10 is asserted. The non-etale pulled-back connection is a further question recorded below.

## Problem 3 (Major): the current does not satisfy its defining equation

Location. Definition 3.15 (3.4.1), page 37; Definition 3.18, page 39; Theorem 3.20 (3.5.1), pages 39–41; binomial expansion on page 41. See `Explicit-de-Rham-polylog.tex`, especially lines 536–545. Connection and character signs are fixed on pages 30–32 and 36.

Take \(d=1\), \(E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z)\), \(r=1\), \(H(z,w)=z\overline w\), \(\Gamma=1\), and \(f=\delta_0-\delta_{i/2}\) on \(E[2]\). Set \(n=1\), \(\ell=1\), and \(u=\overline u_1\). The paper’s character is \[\chi_\ell(z)=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{Im}(\ell\overline z)}
             =e^{\pi(\ell\overline z-\overline\ell z)}.\] Thus \(\partial_{\overline z}\chi_1=\pi\chi_1\), and the residue Fourier coefficient is \(F_\ell=1-\chi_1(-i/2)=2\). For \(d=1\) there is no \(r\)-variable and the \(j\geq1\) terms vanish. Equation (3.4.1) gives, through degree one, the mode \[F_\ell\left(\frac1a+\frac{u}{a^2}\right)
 \chi_\ell\,\iota_{\widetilde\ell}\operatorname{vol},\qquad a=\pi.\] Theorem 3.5 and (3.1.2) give \(d+\nu\) with \(\nu^{0,1}=u\,d\overline z\). Applying it gives \[(a+u)F_\ell\left(\frac1a+\frac{u}{a^2}\right)\operatorname{vol}
 =F_\ell\left(1+\frac{2u}{a}\right)\operatorname{vol}\pmod{u^2}.\] The prescribed right side has only degree zero. The residual \(4u\chi_\ell\operatorname{vol}/\pi\) is nonzero. Uniqueness of Fourier coefficients of distributions excludes cancellation with a different lattice mode.

The algebraic error is the missing \((-1)^b\) in \[(a+u)^{-j-1}=\sum_{b=0}^n
 (-1)^b\frac{(j+b)!}{j!}a^{-j-b-1}u^{[b]}.\] Divided powers absorb \(b!\), not a sign. The corrected inverse \(a^{-1}-ua^{-2}\) has product one modulo \(u^2\).

Counterevidence and repair. Degree zero does not detect this error. Inserting \((-1)^b\) fixes the local inverse, but the change must propagate through Corollary 3.22 and Theorem 3.27. There is a related derivative check: \(\partial_z\chi_\ell=-\pi\overline\ell\chi_\ell\), whereas page 43 uses a positive factor. Also \(dz\wedge d\overline z=-2i\,dx\wedge dy\). The Fourier and volume conventions need a full audit; a repaired final global sign has not been certified here. The abstract residue-defined class is not disproved. The printed explicit representative is.

## Problem 4 (Major): translation repeats the full residue pullback

Location. (5.5.1), page 66; Theorem 5.23, (5.5.2), page 67; the passage from (5.5.5) to the sum, page 68. See `p-adic-interpolation.tex`, lines 795–919.

Write \(\varphi:A'=A_{(n)}\to A\) for the etale isogeny. Equation (5.5.1) defines \(f'=\varphi^*f\) on the entire \(D'=\varphi^{-1}D\). At \(\mathfrak s=\mathfrak t=0\), the theorem states \[\vartheta_A(f,x)=\sum_{a\in\ker\varphi}
             \varphi_\#\vartheta_{A'}(f',x'+a).\] For this full pullback all summands have the same zeroth moment.

An admissible setting is \(L=\mathbb Q(i)\), \(p=5\), \(d=1\), \(\Gamma=1\), and \(E:y^2=x^3-x\), over a number-field base with good reduction at five and the required prime-to-five torsion sections. Since \(5=(2+i)(2-i)\), the ordinary condition holds. Take \(n=1\), hence \(\deg\varphi=5\). Use \(\mathfrak c=(2)\), \(f=\delta_0-\delta_t\) with \(t\neq0\) two-torsion, and a nonzero three-torsion \(x\) at which the zeroth differential is nonzero. Its existence follows from the two-pole argument in Problem 2. Thus \(\mathfrak f=(3)\), \(p\nmid\mathfrak f\), \(x\notin D\), and both translations are invariant.

Pulling back the residue-defined class along \(\varphi\), using Poincare functoriality, gives the class with residues \(f'\). Indeed pullback has those residues and residue uniqueness kills the completed-coherent global difference. In degree zero this is the ordinary differential pullback. Transport the \(\Gamma_{00}\)-structure through \(\widehat\varphi:\widehat A'\simeq\widehat A\), so \(\omega_{\rm can}(A')=\varphi^*\omega_{\rm can}(A)\). If the differential is \(h\omega_{\rm can}\), every summand’s zeroth coefficient is \[h(\varphi(x'+a))=h(x)=F\neq0.\] The map \(\varphi_\#\) on formal functions preserves constants, and its second-variable map does not affect a zeroth moment. The displayed formula requires \(F=5F\) in \(\mathbb C_5\), a contradiction.

Counterevidence and repair. The residue calculation on pages 68–70 yields \[\widehat f(c,a)=f'(c,a)\langle a,\mathfrak t\rangle.\] To express this as translates, use the restriction of \(f'\) to the single component \(A'[\mathfrak c]\times\{0\}\), extended by zero elsewhere. Call it \(f_0\). Translates of \(f_0\), rather than the full \(f'\), have the required residue decomposition. This support correction removes the zero-translation multiplicity. Weil-pairing signs and local factors must then be rechecked. This theorem feeds Corollary 5.25 and Theorem 5.27; the measure’s eventual existence is not disproved.

## Problem 5 (Major): the ray-class kernel uses the wrong units

Location. Theorem 5.27, Step 1, pages 74–75: exact sequence, \(G\), \(q\), and (5.7.1). See `p-adic-interpolation.tex`, lines 1217–1250.

For nontrivial tame modulus \(\mathfrak f\), the kernel of \[\operatorname{Gal}(L(p^\infty\mathfrak f)/L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L(\mathfrak f)/L)\] is \((\mathcal O_L\otimes\mathbb Z_p)^\times/\overline{\mathcal O_{\mathfrak f}^\times}\), where \(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak f}^\times=\{u:u\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak f\}\). The proof quotients by the closure of all \(\mathcal O_L^\times\).

Take \(L=\mathbb Q(i)\), \(\mathfrak f=(3)\), \(p=5\). The class number is one; the four units have distinct residues modulo three, so \(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak f}^\times=1\). Principal ideals modulo generators congruent to one give \[|\operatorname{Cl}(3)|=(9-1)/4=2.\] Since \(\mathcal O_L/(5^n)\simeq(\mathbb Z/5^n)^2\), \[|(\mathcal O_L/5^n)^\times|=16\,5^{2n-2},\quad
 |\operatorname{Cl}(3\cdot5^n)|=32\,5^{2n-2}.\] The onto map to \(\operatorname{Cl}(3)\) therefore has kernel of order \(16\,5^{2n-2}\). Quotienting the local units by all four global units instead gives \(4\,5^{2n-2}\). At \(n=1\) these are \(16\) and \(4\). The hypotheses hold: \(5\nmid4\) and \(x^2+1\) has distinct roots \(2,3\bmod5\).

Counterevidence and repair. For \(\mathfrak f=\mathcal O_L\), all units are correct, as in Step 3. Step 1 already uses \(\Gamma=\mathcal O_{\mathfrak f}^\times\) geometrically; use that group also in \(G,q\), pushforward and cosets. This changes the group and its finite characters, so it is not a harmless representative choice. Re-establish descent and interpolation with the corrected kernel. This disproves the displayed construction step, not a corrected measure-existence theorem.

## Problem 6 (Minor): the invariant-weight assertion needs a module hypothesis

Location. Proposition 1.5, page 8; Section 1.1 explicitly permits arbitrary algebraic modules, page 7. See `Preliminaries.tex`, lines 40–51 and 130–140. The claim is \(M^\Gamma=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\operatorname{HChar}_L(\Gamma)}
M(\mu)\) whenever the base \(R\) lies in a number field.

Let \(L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), \(R=\mathbb Z[\zeta_3,1/3]\), \(\Gamma=\mathcal O_L^\times=\{\pm1,\pm\zeta_3,\pm\zeta_3^2\}\), and \(M=R/2R=\mathbb F_4\), with algebraic torus weight \(3\sigma\). This is a valid comodule: \(m\mapsto m\otimes X^{3\sigma}\). Every unit acts by its cube, which is \(1\) or \(-1\), hence equals one on \(M\). Thus \(M^\Gamma=M\neq0\). But \(3\sigma(-1)=-1\neq1\) in \(R\), so this weight is not in \(\operatorname{HChar}_L(\Gamma)\). The claimed right side is zero.

Counterevidence, repair, impact. The later differential modules are locally free over a domain; they have no such torsion. For an \(R\)-torsion-free module, if \(\mu(\gamma)\neq1\), the equation \((\mu(\gamma)-1)m=0\) forces \(m=0\), giving the intended conclusion. Add that hypothesis, or state the weightwise annihilator condition. This is a false quantified auxiliary proposition, not a counterexample to the later locally free decompositions.

## Problem 7 (Minor): residues are those of the completed series

Location. Proposition 3.13, page 35; Mellin proof, page 36. Take \(d=1,\Lambda=\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z,H(z,z)=|z|^2,\mu=0,z=w=0\). The uncompleted series is \[K(s)=\sum_{(m,n)\neq(0,0)}(m^2+n^2)^{-s}=4\zeta(s)\beta(s).\] The equality follows by grouping representations as sums of two squares; \(r_2(N)=4\sum_{a\mid N}\chi_{-4}(a)\). Using \(\zeta(0)=-1/2\), \(\beta(0)=1/2\), and \(\beta(1)=\pi/4\), one gets \[K(0)=-1,\quad \operatorname{Res}_{s=0}K(s)=0,\quad \operatorname{Res}_{s=1}K(s)=\pi.\] For example \(\beta(s)=4^{-s}(\zeta(s,1/4)-\zeta(s,3/4))\) and \(\zeta(0,a)=1/2-a\) give \(\beta(0)=1/2\); the alternating odd reciprocal series gives \(\beta(1)=\pi/4\). The standard zeta residue at one is one.

The printed residues are instead \(-1\) at zero and \(1\) at one for this unit-volume example. They are exactly the residues of \(\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)K(s)\). Counterevidence and repair. The Mellin integral in the proof already represents the completed expression, and later current formulas contain \(\Gamma(s)K(s)\). State the residue assertion for that completed function and adjust wording on possible poles. This local analytic normalization error is separate from the missing current sign in Problem 3. No claim about the functional-equation phase in full generality is certified here.

## Problem 8 (Minor): subgroup change must track the torsion-coset stabilizer

Location. Definition 3.24 and Lemma 3.25, page 43; proof, page 44. The definition permits arbitrary torsion \(t'\), not only a \(\Gamma\)-fixed coset. It sums over \(\Gamma\backslash(\Lambda+\Gamma t')\). The lemma claims a factor \([\Gamma:\Gamma']\) under group change.

Take \(L=\mathbb Q(i),\Lambda=\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z,\Gamma=\{\pm1\},\Gamma'=1\), \(t'=1/10,\alpha=2,\beta=0,s=2\). Type \(-2\sigma\) is critical and trivial on \(\Gamma\). Absolute convergence holds since \(2>(0-2)/2+1=0\). The sets \(\Lambda+t'\) and \(\Lambda-t'\) are disjoint. Every \(\Gamma\)-orbit in their union has exactly one representative in \(\Lambda+t'\), and \(g(\lambda)=1/(\lambda^2|\lambda|^4)\) is even. Consequently \(E_{\Gamma'}(t',2)=E_\Gamma(t',2)\), not twice it.

This value is nonzero: the \(\lambda=t'\) term is \(10^6\). For all other \(\lambda=t'+m+in\), use shells \(\max(|m|,|n|)=k\). There are \(8k\) points and \(|\lambda|\geq k-1/10\geq9k/10\), so their absolute sum is bounded by \[8(10/9)^6\zeta(5)<16.\] Thus the two unequal multipliers cannot accidentally give the same value.

Repair. For abelian \(\Gamma\), the orbitwise factor is \([\Gamma_{t'}:\Gamma_{t'}\cap\Gamma']\), with stabilizer taken modulo \(\Lambda\). Equivalently, account for the splitting of the entire \(\Gamma\)-orbit of \(t'\) into \(\Gamma'\)-orbits. When \(t'\) is \(\Gamma\)-fixed, the printed factor is correct. Weighted sums over all torsion orbits can also recover the aggregate factor needed later. These are counterevidence to a claim that every later transfer formula is false, but they do not prove Lemma 3.25 with its stated quantifiers.

## Problem 9 (Minor): a critical \(L\)-value need not be nonzero

Location. Corollary 4.15 and Remark 4.16, page 54; the repeated conclusion on page 55; corresponding introduction statement. The corollary puts \(L_{\mathfrak f}(\chi,0)/c^+_\tau\) in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^\times\), with no nonvanishing hypothesis.

Let \(E_5:y^2=x^3-25x\), which has CM by \(\mathbb Z[i]\). Let \(\psi_5\) be its CM Hecke character over \(\mathbb Q(i)\), with infinity type \(\sigma\) under the paper’s convention. One explicit description away from \(2,5\) is \(\psi_5(\mathfrak a)=\psi_1(\mathfrak a)(5/N\mathfrak a)\), where \(\psi_1\) chooses the primary Gaussian generator congruent to \(1\bmod(2+2i)\). The CM Euler-factor identity gives \(L(\psi_5,s)=L(E_5,s)\). Set \(\chi=\psi_5N^{-1}\). Its infinity type is \(\sigma-(\sigma+\overline\sigma)=-\overline\sigma\). Thus \(s=0\) is critical by Definition 1.2: choose the attached CM type \(\{\overline\sigma\}\), \(\alpha=1,\beta=0\). Taking the exact conductor, \[L_{\mathfrak f}(\chi,0)=L(E_5,1).\]

The functional equation of \(E_5\) has sign \(-1\) (the congruent-number root-number formula for \(5\bmod8\)). Hence its completed central value equals its negative, so \(L(E_5,1)=0\). This uses no BSD conjecture or numerical rank inference. For a separate arithmetic check, \(E_1:y^2=x^3-x\) has conductor \(32\) and sign \(+1\); the coprime quadratic twist by discriminant \(5\) changes the sign by \((5/-32)=-1\). The root-number statement was checked against Elkies’ author research page [Elkies]; Tian’s treatment [Tian], selected passages on printed page 1994, also gives central vanishing for square-free \(n\equiv5,6,7\bmod8\). Its CM discussion on printed page 2005 identifies the elliptic and Hecke \(L\)-functions. These external inputs are identified as such, not claimed as newly reproved here.

Deligne’s period is nonzero, so the ratio in the corollary is zero, which is not in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}^\times\). Counterevidence and repair. The period comparison itself is a comparison of nonzero periods and can remain in \(\mathbb C^\times/\overline{\mathbb Q}^\times\). The \(L\)-value conclusion should be in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), or explicitly assume nonvanishing. Theorem 4.10 permits zero, and its integrality assertion is not refuted by this example. This is a mathematical boundary-case correction, not a complaint about a missing reference.

## Problem 10 (Minor): the Borel comparison proof falsely asserts flabbiness

Location. Appendix A.2, page 81, proof of (A.2.1); `Relation-to-de-Rham-polylog.tex`, lines 517–558. The proof claims that an injective equivariant sheaf \(\mathcal I\) pulls/descent-transforms to a flabby sheaf \(\widetilde{\mathcal I}\) on \(E\Gamma\times_\Gamma X\). It identifies sections on an arbitrary open set with invariant sections over its projection to \(X\).

Take the smooth zero-dimensional manifold \(X=\{\ast\}\), \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z\), \(E\Gamma=\mathbb R\), and \(I=\operatorname{Map}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q)\) with the shift action. It is an injective \(\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z]\)-module: it is coinduced from the injective abelian group \(\mathbb Q\), and coinduction is right adjoint to the exact forgetful functor. The descended sheaf on \(S^1\) is the local system with fiber \(I\) and shift monodromy. Its global sections are \(I^\mathbb Z\), the constant sequences, isomorphic to \(\mathbb Q\). On a proper contractible arc \(U\), sections are all of \(I\). Restriction is the inclusion of constant sequences in all sequences, and is not onto; the sequence with a single \(1\) at index zero is not in its image. Thus the local system is not flabby.

Moreover \(p(q^{-1}U)=X\), so the specific second section isomorphism in the proof would identify \(I\) with \(I^\mathbb Z\) through restriction, which is false. Openness of \(p\) does not provide that isomorphism.

Counterevidence, repair, impact. The Borel/group-cohomology comparison itself is standard and is not contradicted by this example. For the circle, coinduction is group-cohomologically acyclic: the map shift minus identity on all rational sequences is onto (solve the difference equation recursively in both directions). Use acyclicity and a descent or Cartan–Leray argument, rather than flabbiness, to prove the comparison. This is an auxiliary proof error with a standard replacement, not a new obstruction to equivariant cohomology or to the corrected current model.

# Impact, unresolved checks, and counterevidence

Problems 1–5 concern core normalization, comparison, explicit-current, translation, or measure-construction steps, and meet the major threshold even if the intended repair is local. They block acceptance of the displayed proof chain as it stands. They do not collectively prove that the final Hecke \(L\)-value or interpolation theorem is false. Problems 6–10 concern broader auxiliary statements, normalization, or side consequences; their counterexamples do not automatically propagate to the locally free, invariant, or completed cases actually needed later.

Three questions remain Needs Evidence, not additional established theorem counterexamples:

1. In Theorem 2.30, page 24, the expression \([\mathfrak f]^*(\nabla^a)\) is written with target \([\mathfrak f]^*\operatorname{TSym}^a\Omega_B^1\) although \([\mathfrak f]\) need not be etale. A connection pulled back in the usual sense lands in \(\Omega_A^1\); an inverse differential or divisibility argument is needed for this target. The review has not established all higher-order integral divisibilities. Problem 2 is already a closed zeroth-order counterexample independent of this concern.

2. Lemma 5.11, page 61, needs a precise formal-connection calculation. The displayed equation \(p^2\eta_\Sigma=p\eta_\Sigma\) is attributed to multiplication by \(p\), while \([p]\) acts by \(p\) on both parts of characteristic-zero \(H^1\), and pullback also changes the coefficient functions. A Frobenius/unit-root argument would require its own definitions. The types in (5.2.6) should be reconciled with the Hodge splitting. No counterexample to the final commutative diagram has been established here.
