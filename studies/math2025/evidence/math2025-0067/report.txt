Abstract: One Minor finding is established in the acquired author manuscript: the directional derivative of the Stokes constant has the opposite sign to Proposition 11.4 under the paper’s fixed parameter and unstable-minus-stable conventions, and Lemma 11.5 equates a raw integral with the derivative without its normalization factor. An admissible real analytic direction gives an explicit disagreement. The corrected value is still nonzero; the generic nonvanishing argument and the stated single-bump nonexistence mechanism are not refuted. The audit read the full manuscript and checked selected proof transitions, all figure pages, and an independent exact symbolic calculation. Focused independent acceptance confirms this local finding; human adjudication remains pending.

# Claims, hypotheses and proof dependencies

The equation is \[u_{tt}-u_{xx}+u-\tfrac13u^3-f(u)=0,\] where \(f\) is real analytic and odd, of order at least five at zero. The space \(\mathcal F_r\) uses absolutely summable, radius-weighted Taylor coefficients. A breather is nontrivial, temporally periodic and spatially decaying in the stated energy sense. Theorem 1.2 excludes single-bump breathers, with Definition 1.1 and a fixed \(\sigma\in(0,1)\), in its frequency-dependent smallness range. It does not exclude all multi-bump configurations, nor the exceptional nonlinearities for which \(C_{\rm in}=0\).

The intervals \(I_k\) and \(J_k\) separate a weakly hyperbolic resonant mode from the strongly hyperbolic regime. The endpoint \(1/(k+1)\) belongs to \(J_k\), where the zero spectral parameter is handled by a limiting Green kernel. The inner equation fixes translation through its leading singular profile and \(O(z^{-3})\) remainder. Its unstable-minus-stable difference has leading mode \[e^{-i\mu_3z}C_{\rm in}\sin(3\tau),\qquad \mu_3=2\sqrt2.\] The first-resonance result passes to order \(k\) through \[\widetilde\epsilon=\epsilon/\sqrt{k},\quad
\widetilde\omega=k\omega,\quad y=\sqrt{k}\epsilon\omega x,\quad
u=\sqrt{k}\epsilon\omega v.\] The exponential factor is therefore \(e^{-\pi\sqrt{2k}/\epsilon}\). The frequency-independent Stokes coefficient, splitting at the prescribed first crossing, and stable-fibre classification underlie the single-bump conclusion. The tail construction uses conserved energy and intersecting centre-stable/centre-unstable manifolds.

Checks included the choice \(\gamma=1/2\) in matching, logarithmic \(\kappa\) in exponential extraction, elimination of the inner critical component by decay normalization, and the strongly versus weakly hyperbolic alternatives. No further substantive contradiction was established in these inspected transitions. The analytic estimates and external geometric machinery were not all independently reproved. Section 11.2 explicitly presents a conjectural computation formula, not a proved numerical algorithm.

# Problem F01: Stokes derivative sign and normalization

## Location, classification and claim

Minor; confirmed local quantitative error. The locations are (11.4), (11.6)–(11.7), p. 66; (11.8)–(11.9) and Proposition 11.4, especially (11.13), p. 67; and Lemma 11.5 with its residue computation, pp. 68–69. Equation (11.4) fixes unstable minus stable. Equation (11.6) adds \(+\mu\Delta(\phi)\) to the sine-Gordon inner equation. Equation (11.7) defines \(c_{\rm in}^{d}(\Delta)=\left.\partial_\mu C_{\rm in}(\mu\Delta)\right|_{\mu=0}\).

The printed linearized equation (11.9) instead places \(-\Delta\) on the left, and Proposition 11.4 gives the wrong sign for the normalized integral. Lemma 11.5 further identifies the raw integral with \(c_{\rm in}^{d}\) without the prefactor. An admissible direction produces distinct derivative values, so this affects a quantitative claim beyond typography.

## Complete sign calculation

Equation reference: correct. Set \[\phi_b=2\sqrt2\arctan(-i\sin\tau/z),\qquad
L=\partial_\tau^2-\partial_z^2+\cos(\sqrt2\phi_b).\] Differentiating (11.6), with \(\psi^\star=\partial_\mu\phi^{0,\star}|_0\), gives \[L\psi^\star=-\Delta(\phi_b).\] Let \(\xi=\xi_3^+\) be the homogeneous solution in Lemma 11.3, with \(\xi\sim e^{i\mu_3z}\sin(3\tau)\). Define \[W(\psi)=\int_0^{2\pi}(\psi\,\xi_z-\psi_z\,\xi)\,d\tau .\] Periodic integration by parts and \(L\xi=0\) give \[\partial_zW(\psi)=\int_0^{2\pi}\xi L\psi\,d\tau
=-\int_0^{2\pi}\xi\Delta(\phi_b)\,d\tau .\] Horizontal-end boundary terms vanish: \(\psi=O(|z|^{-3})\), its derivative has one additional decay power, and \(\xi,\xi_z\) are bounded on each horizontal end. Integrate the unstable identity from \(-\infty\) to \(z\), and the stable identity from \(+\infty\) to \(z\). Subtraction yields \[W(\psi^u-\psi^s)=-I(\Delta),\qquad
I(\Delta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_0^{2\pi}
 \xi(z+s,\tau)\Delta(\phi_b(z+s,\tau))\,d\tau\,ds .\] Moving a sufficiently low horizontal contour leaves \(I\) unchanged, by holomorphy and decay on the connecting segments. The differentiated asymptotic (11.4) gives \(W(\psi^u-\psi^s)\longrightarrow2\pi i\mu_3c_{\rm in}^{d}(\Delta)\) down the imaginary axis. Consequently \[
c_{\rm in}^{d}(\Delta)=-\frac{I(\Delta)}{2\pi i\mu_3}.\] The differentiation uses the locally uniform analytic dependence established in Section 5. This sign follows from the paper’s own fixed conventions.

## Admissible direction and exact disagreement

For \(r=1\), take the real direction \[D(u)=\tan(\sqrt2u/4)^5\bigl(1+\tan(\sqrt2u/4)^2\bigr).\] It is real, odd, starts at order five, and has nearest poles of modulus \(\sqrt2\pi>1\). Its Taylor coefficients are therefore absolutely summable with radius-one weights: \(D\in\mathcal F_1\). The family is \(f_\mu=f_{\rm sg}-\mu D\in\mathcal F_1\) for real \(\mu\). A sufficiently low contour, for example \(\kappa>100\), places \(\phi_b(s-i\kappa,\tau)\) inside the analytic disk.

The manuscript’s direction \(\Delta_0=-iD\) belongs to the complexification, rather than being a real direction. We use it to evaluate an integral and return to \(D\). For \(w=s+z\), the supplied solution is \[\xi(w,\tau)=\frac{e^{i2\sqrt2w}}{8(w^2-\sin^2\tau)}
\left[4\sin\tau+(8w^2+i4\sqrt2w-5)\sin3\tau+\sin5\tau\right].\] Using \[\Delta_0(\phi_b)=-\frac{\sin^5\tau}{w^5}
 \left(1-\frac{\sin^2\tau}{w^2}\right)\] and exact sine moments gives \[\int_0^{2\pi}\xi\,\Delta_0(\phi_b)\,d\tau
=\frac{\pi e^{i2\sqrt2w}}{64w^7}(-33+i10\sqrt2w+20w^2).\] Its residue at zero is \(13\pi/15\). Close the horizontal contour below zero in the upper half-plane. The large-arc bound is a constant times \(R^{-5}e^{-\mu_3R\sin\theta}\), so the arc integral tends to zero. Thus \[I(\Delta_0)=\frac{26\pi^2i}{15},\qquad
I(D)=iI(\Delta_0)=-\frac{26\pi^2}{15}.\] The raw integral agrees with the manuscript. But [correct] (#correct) yields \[c_{\rm in}^{d}(D)=-\frac{13\pi i}{30\sqrt2},\] whereas Proposition 11.4 gives \(+13\pi i/(30\sqrt2)\). Also the normalized complex-direction value is \(c_{\rm in}^{d}(\Delta_0)=-13\pi/(30\sqrt2)\), not \(26\pi^2i/15\).

## Independent check and counterevidence

An original reviewer script ran in existing Python 3.13.5 and SymPy 1.13.3. It checked \(L\xi=0\) exactly, using \(h=\sin\tau\), \(\partial_\tau^2=(1-h^2)\partial_h^2-h\partial_h\), and \[\cos(\sqrt2\phi_b)=
\frac{z^4+6z^2h^2+h^4}{(z^2-h^2)^2}.\] The simplified residual was zero. Exact sine-moment integration and a Laurent residue reproduced the formulas above. Resonance scaling identities and the stated spectral-gap inequality were also checked. These symbolic checks do not certify the full PDE proof.

Reversing the parameter or exchanging stable and unstable labels would change (11.6) or (11.4); it cannot repair the formulas while preserving those definitions. The raw residue is correct. The complex direction is not used as an invalid real counterexample: \(D\) satisfies the real hypotheses. Both signs give a nonzero derivative, which is decisive counterevidence against a failure of generic nonvanishing.

## Core impact and repair boundary

Write \(L\psi+\Delta=0\) in (11.9), insert the minus sign in (11.13), and distinguish \(I(\Delta_0)\) from its normalized derivative in Lemma 11.5. The corrected real-direction derivative is nonzero.

For any prescribed \(r>0\), choose \(0<q<\min(r,\sqrt2\pi)\). Taylor polynomials \(D_N\) converge to \(D\) in \(\mathcal F_q\). Continuity of the analytic derivative implies a nonzero derivative for some real odd polynomial \(D_N\) of order at least five. That polynomial belongs to every \(\mathcal F_r\). Uniqueness of the normalized inner solution on a common far complex domain identifies the derivatives in the two radius spaces. The nontriviality input to the analytic zero-set argument therefore survives, conditional on the Section 5 analytic construction.

No change to the leading splitting order or to the single-bump conclusion follows from this correction alone. The quantitative value needs correction; the main theorems are not disproved. The verified local repair preserves their needed nonvanishing input, supporting Minor severity.
