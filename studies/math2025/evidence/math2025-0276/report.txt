# F1: the boundary-area description excludes a permitted case (Minor)

Location. The paragraph after Definition 2.1 on p. 10 identifies \(V_{n-1}(K)\) with one half of the \((n-1)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure of \(\partial K\). The definition on p. 9 expressly permits compact convex sets with empty interior.

Take the permitted convex body \(K=[-1,1]\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb R^2\). Its parallel set consists of a rectangle and two semicircles, so \[\operatorname{Vol}_2(K+rB^2)=4r+\pi r^2.\] Comparison with the source’s Steiner formula (2.10) gives \(V_1(K)=2\), \(V_2(K)=0\) and \(V_0(K)=1\). Since the ambient topological boundary of \(K\) is \(K\) itself, \[\tfrac12\mathcal H^1(\partial K)=1\ne2=V_1(K).\] All hypotheses of the paper’s convex-body definition are met.

The counterevidence is the correct intrinsic-volume definition and Steiner expansion on the same pages. Theorem 2.1 uses those coefficients, not the unqualified boundary-area description. For this example the regret is correctly \(\log(1+2/\sqrt{2\pi})\). The actual damage is a false geometric interpretation for a permitted boundary case, so the grade is Minor. Restrict the half-boundary-area description to full-dimensional bodies; for codimension-one bodies the intrinsic volume equals the volume within their affine span. The segment calculation verifies this distinction directly.

# F2: the interpolation sign requires \(\beta\ge1\) (Minor)

Locations. The smoothing family is introduced for every \(\beta>0\) on p. 19; after (4.3), p. 20, its interpolation is asserted to decrease under contractions.

For an exact admissible counterexample to that intermediate assertion, take \(A=\{-a,a\}\subset\mathbb R\), \(a>0\), \(B=\{0\}\) and the constant contraction \(\varphi:A\to B\). Retain the two source indices, as in the proof, so that the two target coordinates coincide. At \(\beta=1/2\), with \(s=a^2(1-t)\), the interpolated Gaussian coordinates are \(\pm\sqrt{s}\,Z\), each with variance \(s\), where \(Z\sim N(0,1)\). Consequently, \[F_{1/2}(t,t)
 =\mathbb E\!\left(e^{(\sqrt{s}Z-s/2)/2}
            +e^{(-\sqrt{s}Z-s/2)/2}\right)^2
 =2+2e^{-s/2}.\] This is strictly increasing in \(t\), contrary to the stated direction. The map is surjective and contracts every pairwise distance; all Gaussian expectations are finite.

The correct derivative, independently recomputed and agreeing with (4.3), is \[\frac{d}{dt}F_\beta(t,t)
 =\frac{\beta-1}{4}
 \sum_{i,j}\mathbb E[f(t)p_i(t)p_j(t)]
 \bigl(\Delta_{ii}+\Delta_{jj}-2\Delta_{ij}\bigr),
 \qquad \Delta=\Sigma^B-\Sigma^A.\] The bracket is nonpositive by contraction. Thus decrease follows for \(\beta\ge1\), with equality of this derivative at \(\beta=1\). This restriction is sufficient for the limit \(\beta\to\infty\). It leaves Theorem 4.1 unchanged and supplies its complete finite-set comparison. The issue is graded Minor because the excessive range concerns an intermediate smoothing claim; it does not give a counterexample to the principal comparison theorem. The verified repair is to choose \(\beta>1\) throughout that argument.

# F3: Notation clarification for Lemma 4.1

The definition of \(X(t)\) in Lemma 4.1, p. 20, should read \[X(t)=\sqrt{1-t}\,X^A+\sqrt t\,X^B,\] using the stipulated independent Gaussian vectors, rather than their covariance matrices. The main comparison proof on p. 19 already uses this construction. Gaussian integration by parts then gives (4.4). No invalid step in that proof is attributed to this notation error. This clarification retains the original F3 identifier for traceability and is not counted as a substantive Minor finding.

# Independent checks of the main deductions

## Envelope integral, intrinsic volumes and degenerate bodies

For nonempty \(A\subset\mathbb R^n\), the usual continuous Gaussian densities have the measurable envelope \[h_A(x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-\operatorname{dist}(x,A)^2/2}.\] If a predictive density \(q\) has worst-case regret at most \(c\), then \(h_A\le e^c q\), so \(\int h_A\le e^c\). Conversely, if \(Z_A=\int h_A<\infty\), the normalized envelope \(h_A/Z_A\) has constant regret \(\log Z_A\). This checks (2.12), including the infinite-integral alternative, without interchanging a minimax operation.

For a convex compact \(K\), Tonelli and the layer-cake substitution give \[\int e^{-\operatorname{dist}(x,K)^2/2}\,dx
 =\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Vol}_n(K+rB^n)\,r e^{-r^2/2}\,dr.\] Inserting Steiner’s formula and using \[\kappa_m\int_0^\infty r^{m+1}e^{-r^2/2}\,dr=(2\pi)^{m/2}\] yields \(\sum_{j=0}^n V_j(K)(2\pi)^{-j/2}\) after division by \((2\pi)^{n/2}\). This confirms the scale in Theorem 2.1, pp. 11–12, including lower-dimensional bodies. A singleton gives regret zero; a segment of length \(L\) gives \(\log(1+L/\sqrt{2\pi})\), and adding orthogonal zero coordinates changes neither answer.

## Metric comparison and limits

For finite sets, the covariance drift \(-\Sigma_{ii}(t)/2\) is essential: its derivative cancels the extra diagonal term and leaves the factor \(\beta-1\) in F2. The computation above therefore also checks the sign of the variance correction. Degenerate covariance matrices are allowed, including the repeated target coordinates in the counterexample.

For \(\beta\ge1\), \[e^{\max_i z_i}\le\left(\sum_i e^{\beta z_i}\right)^{1/\beta}
 \le N^{1/\beta}e^{\max_i z_i}.\] The exponential maximum is bounded by a finite sum of Gaussian exponentials with finite expectation. Hence this sandwich passes to \(\beta\to\infty\), establishing the required comparison. For arbitrary subsets of \(\ell_2\), lift each finite target subset through the surjective contraction, apply the finite comparison, and take the supremum over finite subsets as in (3.1), p. 14. This avoids any assumption that the whole set lies in a finite-dimensional space. No independent infinite-dimensional Lebesgue density is required.

## Local width and covering estimates

The local width uses centers in \(A\), as do the covering numbers in (4.5)–(4.6), pp. 20–21. The mixture bound on p. 44 follows from \(\sup_i h_{A_i}\le\sum_i h_{A_i}\): \[R^*(A)\le\log\sum_i e^{R^*(A_i)}
 \le\max_iR^*(A_i)+\log N.\] Together with the stated McMullen bound, applying this to \(A_i=A\cap B(\theta_i,r)\) gives the upper bound in (4.10).

For the local lower bound, the Gaussian representation and Jensen give \(R^*(A)\ge w_A(r)-r^2/2\). If \(r<r_*(A)\), the definition as a supremum supplies \(r'\ge r\) with \(w_A(r')\ge r'^2\); it is not necessary to assume that \(r_*\) is an attained continuous fixed point. Thus \(R^*(A)\ge r_*^2/2\). Likewise, for \(r>\max(r_*,\widetilde r)\), both defining complexity functions are below \(r^2\), proving (8.10), p. 45. These implications remain valid for discontinuous nonconvex local widths.

The remaining covering-number lower bound in (4.9) depends on the minimax-redundancy result from Section 8.3. That proof was not independently audited here. Consequently the complete two-sided quantitative theorem is not certified by the checks above.

As an exact boundary check, a two-point set at distance \(d\) has \[R^*(A)=\log\bigl(2\Phi(d/2)\bigr),\] where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function: integrate the larger of the two densities on each side of their midpoint. The variational expression in (4.10) equals \(\min(\log2,d/\sqrt{2\pi})\) for this set. This matches the linear small-distance behavior and bounded large-distance behavior of the exact regret. It does not by itself establish the general theorem.
