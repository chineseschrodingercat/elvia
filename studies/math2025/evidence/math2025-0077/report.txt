Initial outcome: Major. A complete planar construction refutes the \(n=1\) case of Theorem B as printed. It satisfies the precise logarithmic capacity density condition, has \(\mathcal A=0\) at every point of a line, and has no true tangent at any of those points. This does not refute Theorem A, the qualitative large-piece rectifiability mechanism, or the \(n\ge2\) case of Theorem B. Two further core proof steps have separate problems: the scale uniformity asserted in Lemma 9.1 and the preservation of a \(99\%\) mass conclusion under Claim 13.1.

# Problem 1: a planar counterexample to Theorem B

Locations and claim. Theorem B, 6/212, explicitly assumes \(n\ge1\), two disjoint open subsets \(\Omega^\pm\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}\), and the CDC for their union. It asserts, up to an \(\mathcal H^n\)-null set, that \(\mathcal A(x)<\infty\) is equivalent to a true tangent. The definition on 3/209 tests the union \(\partial\Omega^+\cup\partial\Omega^-\) against every sufficiently narrow double cone. Definition 2.2, 11/217, specifies logarithmic capacity when \(n=1\): \[\operatorname{Cap}_L(B(z,r)\setminus(\Omega^+\cup\Omega^-))\ge c r
 \quad(z\in\partial(\Omega^+\cup\Omega^-),\ 0<r<\operatorname{diam}(\Omega^+\cup\Omega^-)).\]

## Construction and all geometric hypotheses

Let \(C\subset[0,1]\) be the middle-third Cantor set, and put \(d=\log2/\log3\in(0,1)\). Its standard probability measure has a bound \(\nu(B(t,\rho))\le A\rho^d\) for \(0<\rho<1\): choose basic Cantor intervals of length \(3^{-m}\) comparable to \(\rho\); an interval of length \(2\rho\) meets at most a fixed number, each of mass \(2^{-m}\). Consequently, \[\iint\log^+\frac1{|u-v|}\,d\nu(u)d\nu(v)
 =\int_0^\infty(\nu\times\nu)\{|u-v|<e^{-t}\}\,dt
 \le A\int_0^\infty e^{-dt}\,dt<\infty .\] Thus \(q:=\operatorname{Cap}_L(C)>0\). Translation and dilation give \(\operatorname{Cap}_L(a+\ell C)=\ell q\).

For \(j\ge1\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\), define \[h_j=2^{-j},\quad
 K_{j,k}=\bigl(kh_j+(h_j/4)C\bigr)\times\{h_j\},\quad
 F=(\mathbb R\times\{0\})\cup\bigcup_{j,k}K_{j,k}.\] Set \(\Omega^-=\{y<0\}\) and \(\Omega^+=\{y>0\}\setminus F\). Above any positive height, the family is locally finite; its only new accumulation points as \(j\to\infty\) lie on the included horizontal axis. Hence \(F\) is closed. It has empty interior, so the sets are open and disjoint, their union is \(\mathbb R^2\setminus F\), and its boundary is exactly \(F\). Every \(K_{j,k}\) belongs to \(\partial\Omega^+\), and the entire axis belongs to \(\partial\Omega^+\cap\partial\Omega^-\). The union has infinite diameter.

## Uniform CDC, including every center and scale

At an axis point, a closed horizontal segment of length \(r\) lies in \(F\cap B(z,r)\) and has logarithmic capacity \(r/4\). At \(z\in K_{j,k}\), write \(h=h_j\) and \(\ell=h/4\). For \(0<r<\ell\), choose the basic Cantor cylinder containing \(z\) whose length \(L=\ell3^{-m}\) first becomes at most \(r/2\). Then \(r/6<L\le r/2\), the entire cylinder lies in \(B(z,r)\), and its capacity is \(qL>qr/6\). For \(\ell\le r\le2h\), a first-generation cylinder containing \(z\) has length \(\ell/3<r\); its capacity is \(q h/12\ge qr/24\). For \(r>2h\), the axis segment of length \(r\) centered below \(z\) lies in \(B(z,r)\), since its maximum distance is \(\sqrt{h^2+(r/2)^2}<r\), and has capacity \(r/4\). Monotonicity proves the required CDC with \(c=\min(q/24,1/4)>0\), for every \(z\in F\) and \(r>0\). All comparison subsets lie strictly inside the balls.

## The square function vanishes

Fix \(x=(a,0)\). The radii for which \(S(x,r)\) hits an off-axis piece form \[D_x=\bigcup_{j,k}\{|y-x|:y\in K_{j,k}\}.\] Each distance map is Lipschitz, so each image has Hausdorff dimension at most \(d<1\). The countable union has Lebesgue measure zero. For \(r\notin D_x\), the spherical sections are complementary open semicircles. The unit semicircle has first Dirichlet eigenvalue \(1\); the relation \(\lambda=\alpha(n-1+\alpha)\) at \(n=1\) gives \(\alpha^+=\alpha^-=1\). Therefore \[\min(1,\alpha^+(x,r)+\alpha^-(x,r)-2)=0
 \ \text{for almost every }r>0,\qquad \mathcal A(x)^2=0.\] The exceptional set is also null for \(dr/r\) on \((0,1)\), by first restricting to \([1/m,1]\). The integrand is bounded by one even at exceptional radii.

## There is no true tangent

Since the complete axis lies in the boundary union, a normal with a nonzero horizontal component has axis points in some double cone at every scale. Any possible tangent must thus be horizontal, with vertical normal. Choose \(k_j\) nearest to \(a/h_j\). Since \(0\in C\), \(z_j=(k_jh_j,h_j)\in K_{j,k_j}\), \(z_j\to x\), and \[\frac{|(z_j-x)\cdot e_2|}{|z_j-x|}
 \ge\frac1{\sqrt{1+(1/2)^2}}=\frac2{\sqrt5}>\frac12.\] The cone \(X_{1/2}(x,e_2)\) contains boundary points arbitrarily close to \(x\). The true-tangent definition fails on the entire axis, not an \(\mathcal H^1\)-null set.

## Counterevidence, dependency, and repair limit

The example is compatible with the measure-theoretic results. The off-axis sets have area zero and \(\mathcal H^1\) measure zero. At axis centers \(\varepsilon_1\) and the smooth half-space coefficient are zero; the axis is rectifiable and has its expected \(L^1\) tangent. The failure is the upgrade to the topological true tangent.

The Jordan-domain theorem on 3/209 has stronger hypotheses. Our punctured upper domain is not Jordan, and Theorem B does not assume it is. Changing the sets on null sets is no response: the true-tangent definition sees these boundary points, and the added sets have positive logarithmic capacity.

The proof’s dimension obstruction is explicit. Proposition 15.1 requires \(s>1\) (89/295), whereas in dimension one the attempted application in Lemma 16.1 uses a CDC exponent \(s\in(0,1)\) (93/299 and 97/303). The spherical thick-point definition on 12/218 treats \(n\ge3\) and \(n=2\), not \(n=1\). Defining a negative-index quantity cannot remove the example.

A necessary correction is to remove \(n=1\) from this CDC-only claim, or to supply a stronger planar hypothesis and a separate proof. The construction does not decide the \(n\ge2\) theorem. Restricting dimension removes this counterexample only; it does not complete the verification of the remaining proof. Adoption and acceptance remain pending.

# Problem 2: a scale-dependent Gaussian translation

Locations. Lemma 9.1 and Remark 9.2, 50/256; the last estimate and (9.3), 51/257. The useful assertion is that, for each small \(\lambda>0\), one can choose a small \(\epsilon\) and then \(\delta\) so that \[\mathfrak a^\pm(x,r)<\lambda\quad
 (x\in N_{2\epsilon r_0}(L_{B_0})\cap B_0,\quad
 10\epsilon r_0\le r\le r_0).\] Its assumption is a small square function on \(E\). Remark 9.2 requires \(\epsilon\ll\lambda\), and the proof takes \(10\epsilon<\lambda\).

Complete check. Take \(n=1\), \(B_0=B(0,1)\), \(E=[-0.9,0.9]\times\{0\}\), \(\mu=\frac12\mathcal H^1|_E\), and \(c_0=0.1\). The growth bound is \(\mu(B(z,r))\le r\) and \(\mu(B_0)=0.9\ge c_0\). The center lies in \(E\), which also meets the outer annular region when required. Let \(\Omega^\pm\) be the complementary open upper and lower half-planes. Every coefficient in (9.1) vanishes on \(E\) at every radius, so the assumption holds for every \(\delta>0\). The minimizing line is the horizontal axis.

For any \(0<\epsilon\le0.1\), choose \(x=(0,\epsilon)\) and \(r=10\epsilon\). Both restrictions hold. Direct Gaussian integration in general dimension gives \[r^{-(n+1)}\int_{\{y_{n+1}>0\}}e^{-|x-y|^2/r^2}\,dy
 =\frac{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}2
 \left(1+\operatorname{erf}\frac{x_{n+1}}r\right).\] Hence, for both signs, \[\mathfrak a^\pm(x,r)=\frac{\pi^{(n+1)/2}}2\operatorname{erf}(1/10).\] At \(n=1\) this is \(0.176656335382\ldots\), independently of \(\epsilon\). Taking \(\lambda=0.05\) contradicts the asserted nonvacuous small-\(\epsilon\) conclusion, despite the zero input square function.

Definition convention. The calculation uses \(\mathfrak a^i=\mathfrak a_\psi^i\) with the constant \(c_\psi=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}\psi\) defined in Section 3.2 (16/222). The subsequent display on 18/224 also writes the reference integral over \(\mathbb R^{n+1}_+\) with a kernel centered at \(x\). If that half-space is read literally as the fixed coordinate half-space, the same example instead gives \(\mathfrak a^+(x,r)=0\) and \(\mathfrak a^-(x,r)=\pi^{(n+1)/2}\operatorname{erf}(1/10)\). The minus coefficient still violates (9.2). Thus neither reading removes the counterexample; the equal values for both signs above use the explicit constant-reference definition.

Alternative reading. The bare existential wording permits \(\epsilon>0.1\), making the radius interval empty. Such an isolated vacuous reading is not refuted, but it is incompatible with Remark 9.2, (9.3), and the stated useful application. The finding concerns that explicitly intended regime.

Fault and supported local correction. The limiting translation estimate on 51/257 omits the scale normalization. For \(K_r(y)=r^{-(n+1)}e^{-|y|^2/r^2}\), \[\|K_r(\cdot-x)-K_r(\cdot-x')\|_1
 \le\|\nabla K_r\|_1|x-x'|=C_n|x-x'|/r.\] Thus displacement \(O(\epsilon r_0)\) costs \(O(\epsilon r_0/r)\), not uniformly \(O(\epsilon)\). A separate fixed minimum scale \(r\ge\eta r_0\) gives \(C_n\epsilon/\eta\), small after \(\epsilon\ll\lambda\eta\). Alternatively the strip must shrink with \(\lambda\) relative to the minimum radius.

Section 10 uses this input for domain splitting in Main Lemma I, which feeds the rectifiability proof. Its macroscopic test radii suggest a repair by separating the parameters and revising their order. The full downstream replacement has not been verified. The half-plane example does not refute the main rectifiability conclusion.

# Problem 3: localization loses the \(99\%\) target

Locations and dependency. Theorem A*, 5/211, and Lemma 11.1, 59–60/265–266, require a single Lipschitz graph with \(\mu(\Gamma)\ge0.99\mu(B_0)\). Claim 13.1, 69/275, replaces \(\mu\) by \(\mu|_{B_1}\) and \(B_0\) by \(\widetilde B_1/100\). The selected \(B_1\) is only guaranteed to have a fixed positive fraction of the original mass. Section 14.3, 88/294, finishes with \(99\%\) of the new measure on the new \(E_0\).

Logical check. The restricted conclusion is \[(\mu|_{B_1})(\Gamma)\ge0.99\mu(B_1).\] From \(\mu(B_1)\ge c(c_0)\mu(B_0)\) this gives only \(\mu(\Gamma)\ge0.99c(c_0)\mu(B_0)\). Adjusting \(c_0\) does not change the denominator in the target.

An explicit witness satisfying the original assumptions illustrates the lost implication. Use the half-planes, \(B_0\), \(E\), \(\mu\), and \(c_0\) from Problem 2. Flatness and half-space splitting are exact. The reflection coefficient \(\mathfrak g\) in Lemma 11.1 vanishes for every center in \(E\) and every radius. Its assumptions hold for arbitrarily small positive \(\theta\) and \(\varepsilon\).

Take \(B_1=B((-1/2,0),10^{-4})\) and \(B_2=B((1/2,0),10^{-4})\). They are centered in \(E\), with equal positive radii, and \(\operatorname{dist}(B_1,B_2)=0.9998\ge1000\,10^{-4}\). Their radii and masses are fixed positive fractions of the original quantities, as in the claimed comparability conditions. The smallest ball concentric with \(B_1\) containing \(B_2\) has radius \(1.0001\), giving new base radius \(0.010001\). The restricted support lies in that base ball; the center of \(B_2\) lies in its \(99\)–\(100\) annulus. The new mass lower bound holds with \(c_0'=0.001\). All flatness and symmetry assumptions persist.

Let \[I=[-0.5002,-0.4998],\qquad
 \Gamma=\{(t,0.01\,\operatorname{dist}(t,I)):t\in\mathbb R\}.\] This graph has slope at most \(0.01<0.1\) and contains \(E\cap B_1\). It satisfies the restricted conclusion, whereas \[\mu(B_0)=0.9,\quad\mu(B_1)=10^{-4},\quad
 \mu(\Gamma)=\tfrac12|I|=0.0002<0.891=0.99\mu(B_0).\] These numbers illustrate the transfer failure; they are not asserted to be the unique balls selected by the cited selection lemma.

Counterevidence and repair boundary. The horizontal axis covers the entire measure in this example. This refutes the stated transfer of a graph’s restricted conclusion, not existence of a suitable global graph. A union of local graphs need not be one graph with the required slope.

A positive-mass graph suffices for the qualitative contradiction argument in Theorem A. The unsupported strength is the near-total mass statement in Theorem A* and Lemma 11.1. A repair must retain the original mass, or provide global selection/gluing that preserves both the fraction and the single-graph constraint. Weakening the target to a fixed positive fraction changes the statement. No complete repair of the stronger claim is supplied.

# Two local analytic problems

## Problem 4: spherical coarea and a complete measurability repair

Location. Remark 2.6(ii), 13/219, equates an unweighted integral of latitude-section measures to upper-hemisphere surface measure outside \(\Omega^+\). The tangential coarea Jacobian is missing.

On \(S^2\), take the allowed Borel set \(\Omega^+=\varnothing\). A latitude of height \(h\in(0,1)\) has length \(2\pi\sqrt{1-h^2}\), so \[\int_0^1 2\pi\sqrt{1-h^2}\,dh=\frac{\pi^2}{2}
 \ne2\pi=\mathcal H^2(S^2_+).\] The correct height coarea factor is \((1-h^2)^{-1/2}\). If a finite-measure set of centers is needed in the surrounding discussion, a compact patch containing this center suffices; the mismatch is pointwise.

The intended measurability assertion has a direct proof. For a unit vector \(u\), put \[A(x,r,u)=\int_{S^n}\!
 \left[{\bf1}_{\omega\cdot u>0}{\bf1}_{(\Omega^+)^c}(x+r\omega)
 +{\bf1}_{\omega\cdot u<0}{\bf1}_{(\Omega^-)^c}(x+r\omega)\right]d\sigma(\omega).\] The integrand and its parameter integral are jointly Borel. For fixed \((x,r)\), changing \(u\) to \(v\) changes this integral by at most the measure of the hemisphere symmetric differences. That bound tends to zero as \(v\to u\), independently of \(\Omega^\pm\). Thus \(A(x,r,\cdot)\) is continuous. If \((u_m)\) is countable and dense, \[\varepsilon_n(x,r)=\inf_m A(x,r,u_m).\] This gives joint Borel measurability. Integrating the nonnegative square against \(dr/r\) gives square-function measurability. The \(r^{-n}\) factor is already canceled by the unit-sphere parametrization. This is a complete local repair without the false coarea identity or a fixed-radius section argument. The defect is peripheral: Minor.

## Problem 5: the kernel tail has the wrong homogeneity

Location. Lemma 3.3, 17/223. Its first constant may depend on \(\psi\), but its displayed linear tail has a dimension-only coefficient. Write \[I=\int_0^R\mathfrak a_\psi(x,r)^2\frac{dr}{r},\quad
 J=\int_0^{MR}\varepsilon_n(x,r)^2\frac{dr}{r},\quad
 T_\phi=\int_M^\infty\phi(t)t^n\sqrt{\log^+(t/M)}\,dt.\] The proof gives \(\sqrt I\le A_\phi\sqrt J+C_nT_\phi\), which supports \[I\le2A_\phi^2J+2C_n^2T_\phi^2,\] not the claimed uniformly linear tail.

For a complete test, take \(x=0\), \(M=R=1\). Inside \(B(0,1)\) let \(\Omega^\pm\) be complementary half-balls; outside assign all points to \(\Omega^+\) and none to \(\Omega^-\). The sets are disjoint Borel. For \(r<1\), \(\varepsilon_n(0,r)=0\), so \(J=0\). Choose nonzero nonnegative \(\phi_0\in C_c^\infty((2,3))\) and put \(\phi=A\phi_0\), \(\psi(y)=\phi(|y|)\). Every stated kernel integrability condition holds for all \(A>0\). For \(1/2\le r\le1\) the kernel is supported outside the unit ball. With \(c_{\psi_0}=\frac12\int\psi_0>0\), both signs satisfy \(\mathfrak a_\psi^\pm(0,r)=A c_{\psi_0}\). Thus \[I\ge A^2c_{\psi_0}^2\log2,\qquad T_\phi=A T_{\phi_0}.\] No dimension-only constant bounds this quadratic lower bound by the printed linear tail as \(A\to\infty\). The first coefficient cannot help, since it multiplies \(J=0\).

The squared-tail estimate above is verified. A kernel-dependent linear coefficient also follows by bounding one factor of the squared tail by the fixed weighted kernel norm. The later fixed Gaussian and normalized kernel are not refuted by this amplitude test. Assessment: Minor.

# Two local scope problems

## Problem 6: spectral equality is insensitive to polar punctures

Location. The rigidity assertion after (1.5), 5/211, is made for arbitrary disjoint open subsets of \(S^n\) and identifies equality with exact complementary hemispheres.

Take \(n=3\), complementary open hemispheres \(H^\pm\subset S^3\), and north pole \(p\). Let \(V^+=H^+\setminus\{p\}\) and \(V^-=H^-\). They are open and disjoint, but not exact complementary hemispheres. A cutoff zero on \(B(p,\rho)\), one outside \(B(p,2\rho)\), with gradient \(O(\rho^{-1})\), has energy \(O(\rho^{3-2})=O(\rho)\). Multiplying smooth compactly supported test functions on \(H^+\) by this cutoff approximates them in \(H^1\). Density gives \[H_0^1(H^+\setminus\{p\})=H_0^1(H^+).\] The first eigenvalue remains \(3\) and hence \(\alpha^+=\alpha^-=1\) by \(\lambda=\alpha(2+\alpha)\). Equality holds despite the puncture.

This does not dispute the inequality. Equality must be described modulo sets of zero Sobolev capacity, or under extra regularity excluding such punctures. The capacity discussion in Allen–Kriventsov–Neumayer [3] supports this distinction. No core failure is inferred from this local rigidity overstatement. Assessment: Minor.

## Problem 7: unrestricted slicing centers

Locations. Proposition 15.1 and Lemma 15.2, 89/295; their proof, 90/296; the use in Lemma 16.1, 97/303. The proposition permits any compact \(G\subset\Gamma\), without restricting \(G\) to \(B(0,r_0)\), yet bounds an expression containing \(\mathcal H^n(G)^2/r_0^n\).

Fix \(n=2\), \(s=3/2\), \(r_0=1\), \(\tau=10^{-3}\), and \(\Gamma=\mathbb R^2\times\{0\}\). Take \(B=B((0,0,1/2),1/20)\) and \(F=\overline B((0,0,1/2),1/40)\). Then \(B\subset B(0,1)\), its radius is below \(1/10\), \(\operatorname{dist}(B,\Gamma)=0.45\ge100\tau\), and \(F\subset B\) is compact. Normalized volume measure on \(F\) has finite \(s\)-energy since \(s<3\), so \(\operatorname{Cap}_s(F)>0\). Let \(G_T\) be the radius-\(T\) closed disk in \(\Gamma\), allowed for every \(T\).

Write \(d=\operatorname{diam}F=1/20\). For \(K\subset F\), the energy definition gives \(\operatorname{Cap}_{s-1}(K)\le d^{s-1}\), since every pairwise distance is at most \(d\). For each \(z\), radii whose spheres hit \(F\) occupy an interval of length at most \(d\). Consequently \[\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cap}_{s-1}(F\cap S(z,r))\,dr\le d^s.\] The asserted inequality would give \[\operatorname{Cap}_s(F)(\pi T^2)^2\le C(\tau)d^s\pi T^2
 \quad\text{for every }T,\] impossible as \(T\to\infty\).

The missing restriction also enters the comparison \(|z-x|+|z-y|\asymp r_0\) and the bounded strip-measure claim for the graph. The eventual application uses \(G=E\cap\frac14B_0\) and therefore has a bounded center range. Adding an appropriate localization removes this obstruction and matches that use; every localized transversality constant has not been verified here. This is a Minor overscope of the auxiliary proposition, not a counterexample to its localized application.

## Positive checks and exclusions

For a radial smooth kernel, the multiplier scaling in Lemma 12.2 is \[\int_0^\infty|\widehat\phi(r\xi)-\widehat\phi(0)|^2\,\frac{dr}{r^3}
 =C_\phi|\xi|^2.\] Evenness gives a numerator \(O(r^4|\xi|^4)\) near zero, and the integrand is \(O(r^{-3})\) at infinity. The change of variables is legitimate; the zero frequency contributes zero.

With \(\kappa=\epsilon^{1/(2n+4)}\) the reflection-cube estimate has \(\epsilon\kappa^{-2n-2}=\kappa^2\). The growth integral in Lemma 14.13 has exponent \(n+2>n\), so annular summation against degree-\(n\) growth converges. Fixed earlier parameter losses can be absorbed by the final small \(\epsilon\). The printed factor \(\alpha^2\) at the end of the big-angle estimate should be an inverse power, but this does not supply a separate failure of the intended smallness conclusion when \(\alpha\) is fixed.

In Corollary 1.2, orientation can be stabilized across comparable small radii using the small symmetric-difference error: opposite labels would force a substantial overlap disagreement. This supports the measure-theoretic conclusion, which differs from Problem 1’s topological obstruction. Known Jordan results do not establish the weaker-hypothesis planar Theorem B printed here.
