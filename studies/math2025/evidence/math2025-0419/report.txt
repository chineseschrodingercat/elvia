Abstract: The acquired source is arXiv:2402.18983v3. Its critical free-energy formula uses an inconsistent matrix-size scaling in the Tracy–Widom argument. Its precritical characteristic-polynomial prefactor also disagrees with the stated free-energy expansion. Both discrepancies are established by exact transformations, with admissible parameter examples. A separate Laurent calculation finds an uncancelled pole in the displayed refined Riemann–Hilbert construction. The diagonal coefficient used for the fixed-phase free energy nevertheless agrees with the claimed expression. The assessment is Major at these stated scopes; no rejection of all results is inferred. Scientific adjudication and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Source, conventions and scope

Sung-Soo Byun, Seong-Mi Seo and Meng Yang, Free energy expansions of a conditional GinUE and large deviations of the smallest eigenvalue of the LUE, Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 78 (2025), 2247–2304, DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.70005. The reviewed document is the 43-page preprint arXiv:2402.18983v3 [BSY], submitted on 15 May 2025 and internally dated 16 May. Equality with the publisher’s version was not established. All source page and equation references below concern this acquired version; PDF and printed page numbers agree.

All 43 pages of source text were read. Selected formulas, proofs and boundary cases were checked in detail, with actual source-image inspection for the decisive evidence. Reading the bibliography does not constitute reading its external works. No author code or empirical simulation is used as a substitute for a mathematical argument.

The potential is \(Q(z)=|z|^2-2c\log|z-a|\), with \(c>0\), and \(dA=d^2z/\pi\). Rotation permits \(a\geq0\). Write \[b=\sqrt{c+1}-\sqrt c.\] The strict precritical phase is \(a>b\); the postcritical phase is \(a<b\). Critical scaling requires a separate argument. We write \(n\) for the GinUE particle number and \(m\) for the LUE matrix dimension whenever both occur. This distinction is essential.

# Problem 1: the critical Tracy–Widom argument

Location. Equation (1.20), p. 6; Proposition 2.5 and (2.17), p. 9; the duality and soft-edge formulas (6.3)–(6.9), pp. 38–39.

Equation reference: eq:acrit, eq:duality, eq:edge, eq:critical-limit. For the critical scaling in (1.20), write \[
 a_n=b-Ds\,n^{-2/3}+O(n^{-4/3}),\qquad
 D=\frac{b^{1/3}}{2c^{1/6}(c+1)^{1/6}} .\] The exact duality in the source identifies \[
 \mathbb P_{m=cn,\,\alpha=1/c}
 \{\lambda_{\min}>a_n^2/c\}
 =e^{-cn^2a_n^2}\frac{Z_n(a_n,c)}{Z_n(0,c)} .\] In particular, the dimension appearing in the soft-edge scale is \(m=cn\), not \(n\). The lower edge and the parameter in (6.7) become \[\lambda_-=\frac{b^2}{c},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{c^{3/4}(c+1)^{1/4}}{b^2}.\] Direct expansion of [eq:acrit] (#eq:acrit) gives \[\lambda_--a_n^2/c
 =B s\,n^{-2/3}+O(n^{-4/3}),\qquad
 B=\frac{b^{4/3}}{c^{7/6}(c+1)^{1/6}} .\] Although \(B\delta^{2/3}=c^{-2/3}\), the actual normalized variable is \[
 y_n=(\delta m)^{2/3}(\lambda_--a_n^2/c)
     =s+O(n^{-2/3}),
 \qquad B\delta^{2/3}c^{2/3}=1 .\] Thus the edge law quoted in (6.8), together with continuity of its limit, gives \[
 \log Z_n(a_n,c)-\log Z_n(0,c)-cn^2a_n^2
 \longrightarrow \log F_{\mathrm{TW}}(s).\] By contrast, subtracting the radial reference (3.6) from (2.17) gives \(\log F_{\mathrm{TW}}(c^{-2/3}s)+o(1)\). All other terms at order one coincide; an omitted radial term of order \(n^{-1}\) cannot remove the discrepancy.

An admissible integer sequence makes the conflict explicit. Take \[c=\frac9{16},\quad s=1,\quad n=16k,\quad m=9k,\quad
 a_{16k}=\frac12-15^{-1/3}(16k)^{-2/3}.\] Here \(b=1/2\), \(\lambda_-=4/9\), \(\lambda_+=64/9\), and \(\delta=3\sqrt{15}/4\). The rectangular dimensions are \(9k\) and \(25k\), so there is no integrality ambiguity. The actual limiting argument in [eq:edge] (#eq:edge) is \(1\), whereas the printed expression uses \((16/9)^{2/3}\). These produce distinct values: from (2.15), \[\frac{d}{ds}\log F_{\mathrm{TW}}(s)=\int_s^\infty q(x)^2\,dx>0,\] where \(q\) is the real Hastings–McLeod solution specified in (2.16). Its nonzero Airy asymptotic excludes an identically zero tail.

Counterchecks and correction. Equation (6.9) can be read consistently with its \(N\) denoting the LUE dimension; it must then be converted before use with (1.20). Changing that convention silently in Proposition 2.5 does not preserve the stated scaling. The special cases \(c=1\) or \(s=0\) conceal the error and do not settle the general statement. For unchanged (1.20), the consistent limiting term is \(\log F_{\mathrm{TW}}(s)\). This check establishes the order-one correction, conditional on the edge law explicitly invoked by the paper. It does not newly prove that external law or the stated \(O(n^{-2/3})\) remainder rate.

The large-negative-\(s\) comparison (2.22) supplies a related internal check. Substitution into the tail formula (2.19) yields \[\log F_{\mathrm{TW}}(c^{-2/3}s)
 =\frac{\log2}{24}+\zeta'(-1)-\frac18\log|s|
  +\frac{\log c}{12}-\frac{|s|^3}{12c^2}
  +O(|s|^{-3})\] for fixed \(c>0\); the \(c\)-dependent terms cannot simply be omitted. This is part of the same scaling issue, not a separate finding.

Severity: Major. The discrepancy changes the advertised critical order-one term by a nonvanishing amount on an admissible sequence.

# Problem 2: the precritical moment prefactor

Location. Theorem 2.2, (2.9), p. 7; Theorem 2.7, (2.32), p. 10; the exact partition-function ratio (1.8) and radial normalization (3.7), p. 15.

Equation reference: eq:prefactor. In the precritical phase the Euler characteristic is one. Dividing the expansion in Theorem 2.2 by the GinUE partition function in (3.7) cancels its logarithmic, linear and universal constant terms. The exact identity (1.8) therefore requires \[\log\mathcal G^{\mathrm{pre}}(a)=\mathcal F^{\mathrm{pre}}(a,c)\] for the prefactor in Theorem 2.7, conditional on Theorem 2.2. Put \(w=F(a)=1/q>1\), using the inverse conformal map in the source. Algebraic substitution into (2.9) gives \[
 e^{24\mathcal F^{\mathrm{pre}}}
 =\frac{w^4(w^4+a^2w^2-2a^2)^4}
 {(w^2+a^2)^4(w^2-1)^3(w^6-a^4)}.\] The corresponding denominator in (2.32) has \((w-1)^3\), rather than \((w^2-1)^3\). Consequently, \[\frac{\mathcal G^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\rm printed}}
      {\exp(\mathcal F^{\mathrm{pre}})}=(w+1)^{1/8}.\] This fixed factor cannot be absorbed into \(\exp(O(n^{-1}))\).

For an exact admissible example, take \[a=1,\quad q=\frac12,\quad c=\frac{27}{8},\quad
 R=\frac54,\quad \kappa=\frac9{16},\quad w=2 .\] These satisfy (1.16)–(1.18), including \[q^6-\frac{a^2+4c+2}{2a^2}q^4+\frac1{2a^4}=0,\] and \(f(2)=1\). The branch is strict precritical and \(\kappa>0\). Equation [eq:prefactor] (#eq:prefactor) equals \(6912/4375\), whereas the twenty-fourth power printed in (2.32) equals \(186624/4375\). Their prefactor ratio is \(3^{1/8}\). One may take \(n\) through multiples of eight if \(cn\) is required to be an integer.

Correction and scope. Replacing \((F(a)-1)^3\) by \((F(a)^2-1)^3\) gives exactly the constant implied by (2.9). This is a verified algebraic correction conditional on that free-energy formula. The computation proves an internal incompatibility of the two stated main asymptotics; it is not an independent derivation of the full nonradial moment limit. No claim that the authors adopted this correction, or that it occurs in the journal version, is made.

Severity: Major. The error concerns the explicitly advertised constant-accurate characteristic-polynomial asymptotic, not only its presentation.

# Problem 3: an uncancelled pole in the refined parametrix

Location. The global parametrix (5.5), p. 29; (5.11)–(5.12), p. 30; the Airy coefficient (5.21), p. 32; Definition 5.8 and (5.30)–(5.35), p. 34; the use of holomorphy on p. 35.

Equation reference: eq:conjugation, eq:h11. Let \(\lambda=\bar\beta\) be the lower endpoint, put \[\rho=\sqrt{R\kappa}>0,\quad u=Rq-\kappa/q,\quad
 x=z-u,\quad \sigma(z)=\sqrt{x^2+4\rho^2},\] and use the branch normalized at infinity. Direct conjugation of the displayed matrices gives \[
 \Phi\Pi_{11}\Phi^{-1}
 =\frac1{48\sigma(z)}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 x+12i\rho&6ix-2\rho\\
 6ix-2\rho&-x-12i\rho
 \end{pmatrix}.\] With \(d=z-\lambda\), the source local coordinate has expansion \[\zeta(z)=n^{2/3}\gamma_{11}d
       \bigl(1+\tfrac12\eta d+O(d^2)\bigr),
 \qquad \eta=\gamma_{12}/\gamma_{11}.\] Define \[C=\frac{(\lambda-a)\lambda}{a(\lambda-R/q)},\quad
 A=3-10i\rho\eta,\quad B=19+30i\rho\eta .\] Expanding [eq:conjugation] (#eq:conjugation) divided by \(\zeta^{3/2}\) gives the required simple-pole coefficient \[
 h_{11}^{\rm required}
 =\frac{iC}{128\rho n}
 \begin{pmatrix} A&iB/3\\ iB/3&-A\end{pmatrix}.\] The printed coefficient (5.32), expressed with the same prefactor, has \(B/3\) in both off-diagonal positions. The full double-pole coefficient \(h_{12}\) and the two diagonal entries of \(h_{11}\) agree with the direct expansion. This agreement fixes the overall phase convention: a change of square-root branch cannot repair only the two off-diagonal entries.

Equation reference: eq:residual. The matrix \(H_1\) in (5.30) thus has \[
 \operatorname{Res}_{z=\lambda}H_1(z)
 =\frac{(1+i)CB}{384\rho n}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.\] At the admissible parameters used in Problem 2, the off-diagonal entry is exactly \[\frac{(1-i)(3845+304\sqrt5\,i)}{35280n}\ne0 .\] Hence \(H_1\) is not holomorphic at \(\lambda\) as asserted. This pole is of order \(n^{-1}\); on a fixed punctured neighborhood it cannot be absorbed into the claimed \(O(n^{-2})\) matrix error.

There is a related, but smaller-order-on-contours, issue in the definition \(R_1=R_2 Q R_2^{-1}\) in (5.34), where \(Q=I+h_{11}/(z-\lambda)+h_{12}/(z-\lambda)^2\). The conjugation need not be analytic at the opposite endpoint \(\beta\), as the proof on p. 35 requires. Even after correcting the off-diagonals in [eq:h11] (#eq:h11), the same exact example gives \[n\operatorname{Res}_{z=\beta}(R_1)_{11}
 =\frac{835}{49392}+\frac{31\sqrt5}{6174}i\ne0 .\] This local Laurent residue must not be confused with the expansion on contours separated from both endpoints. Writing \(Q=I+\varepsilon A_0\), \(R_2=I+\varepsilon B_0\), \(\varepsilon=n^{-1}\), one has exactly \[(R_1-Q)R_2=\varepsilon^2[B_0,A_0],
 \qquad R_1-Q=O(n^{-2})\] on such fixed contours. An alternative first-order rational correction can therefore address this secondary issue within the allowed boundary error. That observation alone does not reconstruct the complete analytic error problem.

Impact, counterevidence and repair limit. Multiplying both printed off-diagonal entries of \(h_{11}\) by \(i\), with the corresponding conjugate change at the upper endpoint, restores the checked local principal part. A complete revised matching construction and global remainder proof were not supplied in this review. The literal holomorphic-parametrix argument for the fine polynomial asymptotic (3.44) is therefore incomplete as written.

Equation reference: eq:diag. There is substantial counterevidence against extending this finding to a false free-energy coefficient. The general identity \[
 n[z^{-1}]R_{11}(z)
 =-\frac{q^2(1-a^4q^4)^2}
 {16a(1-q^2)(1-a^4q^6)^2}\] agrees exactly with Proposition 3.10. The correction in [eq:h11] (#eq:h11) changes only off-diagonals; at infinity, the first diagonal coefficient is \((h_{11}+h_{21})_{11}\), and cross products start at \(z^{-2}\). Thus neither this calculation nor the secondary conjugation issue disproves Theorem 2.2. No counterexample to the actual orthogonal polynomial asymptotic is claimed.

Severity: Major, within the proof-construction scope. The order-\(n^{-1}\) pole invalidates the displayed analytic error problem at the precision used for a stated central refinement. The classification does not assert that the refinement is unrepairable or that every resulting scalar coefficient is wrong.

# Checks supporting the analysis

## Exact references and phase algebra

At \(a=0\), the norms \[h_j=\frac{\Gamma(j+cn+1)}{n^{j+cn+1}},\qquad
 Z_n(0,c)
 =\frac{n!\,G((c+1)n+1)}
        {G(cn+1)n^{(c+1/2)n^2+n/2}}\] give an exact radial reference. Expanding its logarithm reproduces (3.6), including all checked coefficients through \(n^{-6}\). For example, the \(n^{-1}\), \(n^{-2}\), \(n^{-3}\) coefficients are \[\frac1{12},\qquad
 \frac{c^{-2}-(c+1)^{-2}}{240},\qquad -\frac1{360}.\] The corresponding GinUE ratio was also checked. An initial computational display of coefficient extraction was corrected in a separately retained run; the symbolic identity and the final coefficient extraction agree.

The cubic constraint gives \[c=\frac{a^2q^2}{2}+\frac1{4a^2q^4}-\frac{a^2+2}{4},\qquad
 \frac{q'}q
 =\frac{1+a^4q^4-2a^4q^6}{2a(a^4q^6-1)} .\] Using this derivative, the displayed energy and constant derivatives in (3.17)–(3.18) agree exactly. The difference of the phase energies has derivative \[\frac{(1-q^2)^2(1-a^4q^4)}{2aq^4}>0\] on the admissible precritical branch, where \(\kappa>0\) implies \(aq<1\). The alternative phase wording using \(c_{\rm cri}=(1-a^2)^2/(4a^2)\) requires \(0<a<1\); for \(a\geq1\) the inequality \(a>b\) always applies. This domain qualification is kept separate from the three findings.

## Differential identity, energy and local asymptotics

The determinant/tau-function derivation in Lemma 3.8 and Proposition 3.9 was read with its finite-dimensional normalization and rotation factors. Explicit moment determinants for \(n=1,2\), \(c=1\) satisfy the checked differential identity. The initial determinant must be understood as the empty determinant \(D_0=1\), not as a single moment whose derivative is generally nonzero. This check does not establish all contour nonvanishing assumptions.

The Section 4 rational partial fractions, Green residues, the closed-form energy relation, and the expansion of \(g'\) at infinity were verified algebraically. Their potential-theoretic input from the cited external work remains an input. The local-coordinate ratio \(\gamma_{12}/\gamma_{11}\), the leading Airy conjugation, and [eq:diag] (#eq:diag) were also checked exactly.

For the postcritical construction, the nilpotence of the upper triangular correction gives the stated cancellation at leading order. On a fixed endpoint-circle, \(\zeta\) has size \(n^{1/2}\), so the remaining Gaussian Cauchy-transform term has order \(n^{-3/2}\). The first-column invariance is consistent with the displayed polynomial formula. The full all-order iteration, all sectorial estimates, and the global small-norm theorem were not reproved. The differing exponential normalization in (5.51) and (5.53) is retained as a local notation issue, not an additional theorem counterexample.

## LUE large deviations

For the fixed-phase LUE transformation, the substitution is \(n=\alpha m\), \(c=1/\alpha\), \(t=a^2/c\). It reproduces the rate \(\alpha^2(I^{\rm pre}-I^{\rm post})\), the term \(-\log(\alpha m)/12\), and the stated constant difference. The cubic edge-rate coefficient \(\sqrt{\alpha+1}/(12\lambda_-^2)\) was checked exactly. These statements concern fixed \(\alpha>0\); they are not a hard-edge result for \(\alpha=0\). Their compatibility does not remove the separate critical-size error in Problem 1.

# Coverage and disposition

The principal definitions, all advertised main asymptotics, and their dependencies were examined. The checks establish exact algebraic inconsistencies and a defect in the written local construction; they do not certify every theorem in the source. In particular, integrating a derivative remainder from \(a\) to infinity requires an integrable bound uniform in that parameter, or another controlled treatment of the tail. Theorem 3.11 states uniformity in the spatial variable for fixed parameters, whereas Remark 4.9 gives decay of displayed coefficients. This review did not establish the needed remainder bound and does not treat that unverified dependency as a separate proved defect.
