Abstract: This report examines the acquired author manuscript arXiv:2305.12284v2, dated 8 June 2024, by Ahmadi, Chaudhry, Sindhwani, and Tu. Three major concerns are established for that version: the low-rank semidefinite formulation admits an unsafe point; a printed uncertainty set is incompatible with its reported infinite-horizon certificate; and the disturbance extension can fail to describe the exact information-conditioned safe set. Five minor corrections concern an offline rank construction, the scope of horizon monotonicity, two local proof inequalities, and the existence of optimization selections. Explicit counterexamples, repairs, and supporting checks are given. The conditional invariant-set and generic-identification arguments retain substantial support. The journal-final article was not acquired, so none of these findings establishes an error in that later version. This is an initial scientific continuation; independent acceptance and human adjudication remain pending.

## Definitions and dependency structure

For a fixed but uncertain map \(f\), the manuscript defines \[S^T(f)=\{x\in S:f^j(x)\in S,\quad j=0,\ldots,T\},
 \qquad S_k^T=\bigcap_{f\in U_k}S^T(f),\] where \(U_k\) consists of dynamics consistent with the collected trajectories. The uncertainty decreases with observations, so \(S_k^T\) increases for a fixed \(T\). The general prediction objective on page 3 is evaluated over the true \(S^T(f_\star)\). The specialized linear Definition 4 instead requires \(U_m\) to be a singleton. These targets are materially different.

For linear dynamics, the main dependencies are as follows. Proposition 1 dualizes one-step robust safety to a linear program. Theorem 8 combines that representation with successive independent queries. Theorem 10 uses the S-lemma for two-step safety under an ellipsoidal prior; Theorem 13 adds a generic rank argument. For infinite-horizon safety, Proposition 15 reduces exact trajectory consistency to finitely many observations, Lemmas 20 and 24 provide invariant ellipsoids and polynomial Lyapunov matrices, and Theorem 18 supplies an SOS hierarchy. Theorem 25 then proves generic identification from one trajectory. The nonlinear counterparts are Theorem 26, Lemma 31, and Theorem 29. Sections 8 and 9 address specialized priors and controlled dynamics.

The findings below are local to these dependencies. In particular, an invalid numerical input is not a counterexample to a theorem whose stability premise that input violates.

# F1: an unsafe feasible point in the low-rank formulation

Equation reference: eq:wrong-spectral. Severity: Major. Theorem 34 and its proof [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~52--54, equations (47)--(50)]{source}] use \[U_0=\{A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}:\operatorname{rank}(A)\le k,\ \left\lVert A\right\rVert_2\le M\},
 \qquad M\ge0,\quad k\in\mathbb N.\] The proof replaces the spectral constraint by \[\begin{pmatrix}MI&A\\A^T&I\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
 \] Its Schur complement is \(AA^T\preceq MI\), which imposes \(\left\lVert A\right\rVert_2\le\sqrt M\), rather than \(M\). The error propagates to the coefficient \(M\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_1)\) in the displayed dual formulation (48).

## A complete feasible certificate and unsafe transition

Take \(n=k=1\), \(M=4\), \(S=[-1,1]\), and \(x=1/2\). For either safety inequality \(hx\le1\), \(h\in\{-1,1\}\), choose \[\alpha=0,\qquad
 \eta_1=\frac18,\qquad \eta_3=\frac12,\qquad
 \eta_2=-\frac{xh}{2}.\] Then \(2\eta_2+xh=0\), so the first PSD block in (48) is zero. The second block \[\begin{pmatrix}\eta_1&\eta_2\\\eta_2&\eta_3\end{pmatrix}\] has determinant zero and eigenvalues \(0,5/8\). Its scalar cost is \[M\eta_1+\eta_3+\alpha Mk=\frac48+\frac12=1.\] The initial-state inequalities also hold. Thus \(x=1/2\) belongs to the printed SDP projection.

However, \(A_\star=4\) belongs to the stated prior, and \(A_\star x=2\notin[-1,1]\). The true robust one-step safe interval is \([-1/4,1/4]\), whereas the printed scalar program gives \([-1/2,1/2]\). This refutes the asserted equality of feasible sets and demonstrates a loss of safety, not merely a conservative approximation. The malformed block [eq:wrong-spectral] (#eq:wrong-spectral) at \(A=4\) has determinant \(-12\). These rational identities and both dual certificates were checked in one actual bounded calculation.

## Supported corrections and boundary cases

Replacing \(MI\) by \(M^2I\), and correspondingly replacing \(M\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_1)\) by \(M^2\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_1)\), repairs this spectral scaling. The nuclear-norm bound \(Mk\) retains its original meaning. The proof’s strict primal feasibility argument should treat \(M=0\) separately, since then the prior is the singleton \(\{0\}\).

Equation reference: eq:rankone-support. There is also a simpler exact formulation. For \(k\ge1\), \[\sup_{\operatorname{rank}(A)\le k,\ \left\lVert A\right\rVert_2\le M}h^TAx
       =M\left\lVert h\right\rVert_2\left\lVert x\right\rVert_2.
 \] The upper bound follows from the operator norm. For nonzero \(h,x\), it is attained by \[A=M\frac{h}{\left\lVert h\right\rVert_2}\frac{x^T}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_2},\] which has rank one; the zero cases are immediate. Thus the exact initial safe set is described by \[h_i^Tx\le b_i,\qquad M\left\lVert h_i\right\rVert_2\left\lVert x\right\rVert_2\le b_i
 \quad\text{for every }i.\] This is an SOCP representation. The rank cap does not improve this particular rank-one support objective.

When \(M=1\), the erroneous and corrected scalings coincide, so examples restricted to that value would not detect the error. Remark 36’s closure-under-powers argument is valid for the actual prior when \(M\le1\), because \(\left\lVert A^t\right\rVert_2\le M^t\le M\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(A^t)\le\operatorname{rank}(A)\) for positive \(t\). It must be combined with a corrected safety formulation.

# F2: the printed infinite-horizon example has only the origin safe

Severity: Major, confined to the stated demonstration and its dependent claims. Section 5.5.1 [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~33--34]{source}] prints \[\begin{aligned}
 U_0&=\left\{
 \begin{pmatrix}1/2&a\\b&1/2\end{pmatrix}:
 a,b\ge0,\ a+b=\frac95\right\},\\
 S&=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:x_1+x_2\le1,\ x_1\ge-1,\ x_2\ge-1\}.\end{aligned}\] It asserts that every member of \(U_0\) is stable and reports a feasible, full-dimensional degree-four certificate from program (22). Figure 6(a) on page 36 depicts the corresponding positive-area region. The fraction \(9/5\) was confirmed in the actual page image.

## Exact contradiction

Equation reference: eq:origin. Both matrices \[A=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&9/10\\9/10&1/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&36/25\\9/25&1/2\end{pmatrix}\] satisfy the printed constraints. With \(v=(1,1)^T\), \(w=(1,2)^T\), \[A^Tv=\frac75v,\qquad B^Tw=\frac{61}{50}w.\] In particular, both are unstable. If \(x\in S_0^\infty\), its orbits under both matrices must remain in the compact triangle \(S\). Consequently \[(7/5)^t v^Tx,\qquad (61/50)^t w^Tx\] are bounded for all \(t\), forcing \(v^Tx=w^Tx=0\). These two independent linear equations give \(x=0\). Conversely, the origin is safe for every linear map in the prior. Hence \[S_0^\infty=\{0\}.
 \]

Program (22) is in fact infeasible at every degree for this input. At the admissible \(A\), any SOS matrix \(Q(A)\) is positive semidefinite, and \[v^T\bigl(Q(A)-AQ(A)A^T\bigr)v
 =\left(1-\frac{49}{25}\right)v^TQ(A)v\le0.\] At \(k=0\), the right side of (22a) is \(\varepsilon I\), with \(\varepsilon>0\), plus PSD multipliers of nonnegative prior constraints. Its quadratic form at \(v\) is strictly positive. This is a contradiction. It is independent of numerical tolerances or author-code availability.

The native exact-fraction calculation verified both memberships, both left-eigenvector equations, the independent constraints, and the factor \(-24/25\). The general bounded-orbit and SOS arguments above establish the claims beyond those finite arithmetic checks.

## Dependent nonlinear example and a bounded correction

Section 7.1 [Retained latex: \cite[p.~49 and Figure 9]{source}] explicitly imports this \(S\) and \(U_0\), and displays full-dimensional certified regions for several nonnegative values of \(\gamma\). Since \(g=0\) remains admissible for every such \(\gamma\), the linear obstruction [eq:origin] (#eq:origin) transfers to the imported uncertainty family. The same page also asserts membership of \[\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.45\\0.45&0.5\end{pmatrix},\] whose off-diagonal sum is \(9/10\), not the printed \(9/5\). This is additional evidence of inconsistent specified inputs.

The necessary correction is to identify the actual intended uncertainty set and supply a matching certificate and figures. The available evidence does not determine the authors’ executed input. One mathematically valid alternative family replaces \(9/5\) by \(c\) with \(3/4<c<1\). Each individual matrix then has spectral radius at most \(1/2+c/2<1\), while the product of the two triangular endpoint matrices has characteristic polynomial at \(1\) equal to \(9/16-c^2<0\), and therefore an eigenvalue greater than one. For \(c=9/10\), the individual bound is \(19/20\) and that polynomial value is \(-99/400\). This supports a possible replacement example, but does not reproduce degree-four feasibility or establish the intended correction.

Theorem 18’s conditional soundness and full-dimensionality conclusions are not refuted. The printed example violates the stability premise, and the soundness constraint itself proves that its claimed certificate is impossible. Nor does the finding automatically transfer to Section 5.5.2, which uses a different Frobenius-ball prior. For that separate ball, the determinant and trace bounds checked below support individual stability.

# F3: changing disturbances can produce a nonconvex exact safe set

Severity: Major for the information-conditioned exactness claim. Remark 27 [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~39--40]{source}] treats \(x^+=Ax+g(x)+w\), with bounds \(W_\ell\) on past disturbances and \(W_{k+1}\) on the next one. Its displayed SOCP retains the possible linear parts and adds the worst-case nonlinear and future-disturbance supports. That program is safe. The stronger claim that its projection is the exact set conditioned on all observations can fail.

## Safe observations and the exact next safe set

Let \[n=1,\quad S=[-1,1],\quad U_{0,A}=\{0\},\quad
 \gamma=\frac34,\quad d=0,\quad p=1.\] Thus the nonlinear prior is \(|g(t)|\le3/4\). Take \(g_\star=0\) and two observations \[x_1=-\frac12,\quad x_2=\frac12,\quad
 y_1=y_2=0,\qquad W_1=W_2=\frac14,\] with actual past disturbances zero. Both queries were robustly safe: the prior worst-case output magnitude was \(3/4+1/4=1\).

Now set \(W_3=1/2\). At either sampled state, the zero observation and past bound imply \(|g(x_\ell)|\le1/4\). Therefore every consistent map and every next disturbance satisfy \[|g(x_\ell)+w_3|\le\frac14+\frac12=\frac34<1.\] Both sampled states remain safe.

Equation reference: eq:nonconvex. Every other \(z\in[-1,1]\) can be unsafe. To see this without relying on discontinuous functions, put \[N(t)=(t^2-1/4)^2,\qquad
 g_z(t)=\frac34\,\frac{N(t)}{N(t)+(t-z)^2}.\] For \(z\notin\{-1/2,1/2\}\), the denominator never vanishes. The function is smooth, lies between \(0\) and \(3/4\), vanishes at the two observed states, and has \(g_z(z)=3/4\). It is consistent with the observations. With \(w_3=1/2\), the next state from \(z\) is \(5/4\), outside \(S\). Hence the exact information-conditioned next safe set is precisely \[\left\{-\frac12,\frac12\right\}.
 \]

## The displayed program and its correct interpretation

The displayed SOCP uses the future support \(\gamma+W_3=5/4\) at every state. In this scalar instance the original prior has zero right sides, the past residual-radius coefficients are \(\gamma+W_\ell=1\), and all their dual multipliers are nonnegative. Thus no auxiliary choice can reduce the safety left side below \(5/4>1\). The displayed program is empty, although [eq:nonconvex] (#eq:nonconvex) is nonempty. A projection of a convex SOCP feasible set is convex, so it cannot describe this exact conditioned set.

The additive sign before \(W_{k+1}\) was confirmed at original image detail; there is no missing-sign finding. The actual independent calculation checked the rational bounds and the smooth midpoint witness. The analytic family \(g_z\) proves the assertion for every unobserved state.

There is an important alternative reading. The program is exact for the deliberately rectangular outer model \(U_{k,A}\times U_{0,g}\), which forgets constraints on the already sampled values of \(g\). It remains a sufficient safe inner approximation to the set conditioned on the full history. The claim should identify that outer model explicitly.

For exact conditioned treatment, one can combine the program for unobserved states with finitely many checks at sampled states that retain their joint linear-part, nonlinear-value, and past-disturbance constraints. In the infinity-norm setting those fixed-state checks are linear programs. Constant or nonincreasing disturbance bounds remove this particular previously sampled-point obstruction for histories collected by the robust program. The example does not refute the noiseless Theorem 26, the noise-only formula, or the conservative safety of Remark 27.

# F4–F8: local algorithmic, definitional, and proof corrections

## F4: affine mixing need not preserve the offline basis

Severity: Minor; the secondary offline baseline is affected. Algorithm 2 [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~12--13]{source}] starts with a basis \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\in S_0^1\), selects a minimum-cost \(x_0^\star\), and sets \[x_j=(1-\epsilon)x_0^\star+\epsilon z_j,\qquad
 \epsilon\in(0,1].\] It then returns \(YX^{-1}\). A common translation need not preserve linear independence.

For \(n=1\), take \(S=[-1,1]\), \(U_0=[0,1]\), \(A_\star=1/2\), and the nonnegative affine cost \(c(x)=x+1\). The constant has no effect on the optimization. All of \(S\) is robustly one-step safe, \(x_0^\star=-1\), and \(z_1=1\) is an allowed basis. At \(\epsilon=1/2\), the query is zero, the observation is zero, the posterior remains \([0,1]\), and \(X\) is singular. The claimed sufficient assumption on \(S_0^1\) is satisfied.

For an invertible \(Z=[z_1,\ldots,z_n]\), let \(s=e^TZ^{-1}x_0^\star\), where \(e=(1,\ldots,1)^T\). The determinant lemma gives \[\det X=\epsilon^n\det Z
       \left(1+\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}s\right).\] There is at most one exceptional positive \(\epsilon\); if \(s<0\), it is \(-s/(1-s)\). A rank check and another positive choice repair the baseline. The exact scalar calculation also checked the successful choice \(\epsilon=1/4\).

Arbitrarily small nonexceptional values exist, preserving the limiting cost as an infimum. That value need not be an attained minimum or an upper bound for a fixed positive-\(\epsilon\) run. For example, with \(S=[0,1]\), \(U_0=[0,1]\), \(A_\star=1/2\), and \(c(x)=x\), every identifying query has positive cost, although its infimum is zero. Algorithm 1 avoids the basis-collapse mechanism: it perturbs a point already in the previous query span toward a point outside it. No failure of that online independence argument or of the reported fixed-\(\epsilon\) experiments is established here.

## F5: horizon monotonicity needs a fixed learning target

Severity: Minor; the scope of the general definition is affected. Pages 3–4 assert that learnability for horizon \(T\) implies learnability for \(T'<T\), using the prediction criterion over \(S^T(f_\star)\). That target changes with \(T\).

Take \[S=[0,1]^2,\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 f_\star(x)=Rx,\qquad U_0=\operatorname{conv}\{-I,R\},\qquad
 \bar\epsilon=\frac12.\] The true one-step safe set is \(\{(a,0):0\le a\le1\}\), whereas \(S^2(R)=\{0\}\), since \(R^2=-I\). Every candidate fixes zero. The two-step prediction goal therefore holds with no observations.

Because \(-I\in U_0\), the only common one-step safe query is zero. Every such observation leaves the uncertainty unchanged. At \(e_1\) in the true one-step target, the candidate outputs differ by \(\left\lVert Re_1-(-I)e_1\right\rVert_2=\sqrt2>1/2\). Thus one-step prediction learning is impossible, even though two-step prediction learning in the stated sense is possible.

A horizon-independent prediction domain repairs the inference. Alternatively, restrict it to global parameter identification, as in Definitions 4 and 39: a safe long trajectory can then be decomposed into safe one-step observations without changing the identification target. The counterexample does not claim that the full matrix is identifiable from two-step data, and it does not invalidate Theorem 8 under its singleton-uncertainty definition.

## F6: the entrywise norm is not invariant under Schur conjugation

Severity: Minor; a complete local repair preserves the lemma. Lemma 24 [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~29--30]{source}] sets \(M=\max_{A\in U}\max_{ij}|A_{ij}|\) and \(R=\max_{A\in U}\rho(A)\). After a unitary Schur decomposition, equation (25) replaces the entrywise maximum of the triangular matrix by \(M\). That norm is not unitarily invariant.

For a witness with \(R>0\), let \[A=\begin{pmatrix}11/10&1\\-1&-9/10\end{pmatrix},\qquad U=\{A\}.\] Then \(R=1/10\), \(M=11/10\). At exponent one the printed bound squared is \(2(R+M)^2=72/25\). But for \(v=(1,1)^T\), \[\frac{\left\lVert Av\right\rVert_2^2}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_2^2}
 =\frac{(21/10)^2+(-19/10)^2}{2}
 =\frac{401}{100}>\frac{72}{25}.\] The executed nilpotent companion \(N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\-1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has \(N^2=0\), operator norm \(2\), and printed bound \(\sqrt2\). The positive-\(R\) witness above was also checked analytically, without a duplicate program run.

Replace \(M\) by \(\max_{A\in U}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_2\), or by \(nM\), since \(\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\rm entry}\le\left\lVert T\right\rVert_2=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_2\le n\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\rm entry}\). The resulting bound decays uniformly when \(0<R<1\); if \(R=0\), every \(A^n=0\).

For completeness, uniform decay also follows directly from compactness. For each stable \(A\), choose \(p_A\) with \(\left\lVert A^{p_A}\right\rVert_2<1\). A finite neighborhood cover of \(U\) supplies powers \(p_i\) and factors \(q_i<1\). Put \(p_{\max}=\max p_i\), \(q=\max q_i<1\), and \[C=\max\{\left\lVert B^r\right\rVert_2:B\in U,\ 0\le r<p_{\max}\}<\infty.\] Writing \(N=kp_i+r\) in a neighborhood containing \(B\) gives \(\left\lVert B^N\right\rVert_2\le Cq^{N/p_{\max}-1}\) for large \(N\). The original SOS matrix \(P_N(A)=\sum_{j=0}^N(A^j)^TA^j\) consequently has the required positive Lyapunov difference for large \(N\). Lemma 24 and the dependent existence argument survive.

## F7: a reciprocal error in two positive-radius arguments

Severity: Minor; the full-dimensionality conclusions survive. The proofs of Theorems 18 and 29 [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~32 and 48]{source}] assert that \[\begin{pmatrix}\beta I&x\\x^T&1/2\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
 \quad\text{if}\quad \left\lVert x\right\rVert_2\le\sqrt{\frac1{2\beta}}.\] For \(\beta>0\), the Schur complement instead requires \[\left\lVert x\right\rVert_2^2\le\frac{\beta}{2}.\] With \(\beta=1/4\) and \(x=1\), the printed radius includes \(x\), but the block determinant is \(-7/8\). This is consistent with the preceding linear construction: \(Q=1\), \(\alpha=2\) give \(Q/\alpha=1/2>\beta\); the resulting membership block has determinant \(-1/2\). A single exact calculation supports both occurrences and retains its actual original attribution.

Replacing the radius by \(\sqrt{\beta/2}\) repairs the step. It is still strictly positive; the added constant PSD block is an SOS matrix without increasing degree. Thus the proofs still yield a full-dimensional certified set.

## F8: optimization selections require explicit existence handling

Severity: Minor; an implementation and hypothesis qualification. Algorithm 1, line 7, assumes a minimum-cost safe point exists, while Theorem 8 permits arbitrary \(c\) on a polyhedral safety set. If \(n=1\), \(S=\mathbb R\), \(U_0=[0,1]\), \(A_\star=1/2\), and \(c=1\), every point is safe but minimizing \(x\) is unbounded. The literal optimizer selection is undefined, although querying \(1\) identifies the system. Imposing the earlier nonnegative-cost convention as an actual assumption excludes this example and makes the arbitrary-\(c\) wording too broad.

An empty safe set also needs an explicit return-impossible branch when uncertainty is nonsingleton. For instance, \(S=[1,2]\), \(U_0=[2,3]\) has no common one-step safe state.

Independently of the cost convention, Lemma 12’s coordinate-extrema procedure [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~63--64]{source}], used by Algorithm 3, needs finite attained extrema. With \(S=\mathbb R\) and the ellipsoidal prior \(U_0=[-1,1]\), \(S_0^2=\mathbb R\) is full-dimensional but its coordinate maximum and minimum do not exist. Section 4 does not impose compact \(S\).

Compact safety sets are a sufficient repair. More generally, handle an empty feasible set explicitly, use a feasible-point/rank fallback when cost is unbounded, and construct the sampling polytope inside a bounded slice that retains an interior ball. On a nonempty polyhedron a linear cost bounded below attains its minimum. Measurable selection should also be stated in the randomized construction. These qualifications concern the literal procedures; they do not disprove the existence or generic rank of identifying trajectories.

# Supporting proof checks and counterevidence

## One-step linear safety and the information argument

Proposition 1’s dual signs and transposes agree with \(\operatorname{Tr}(xh_i^TA)=h_i^TAx\). Exact data leave the actual matrix in \(U_k\), so the inner feasible set is nonempty. Finite LP support values admit dual attainment; no Slater assumption is needed. The actual scalar check with \(U=[1/2,3/2]\), \(x=2/3\), \(h=\pm1\) gave matching primal and dual values \(1\) and \(-1/3\).

Lemma 7’s homogenized span construction [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~62--63]{source}] can make both homogenizing variables positive by adding the same known feasible lift to the two signed pieces. Their difference does not change, and at least one normalized point extends the current span. The explicit argument on page 63 resolves an earlier duplicated subscript.

For well-defined selections and the singleton-identification target, Theorem 8’s impossibility argument is sound. At a stalled stage, the measured points span every currently safe point, and the uncertainty remains nonsingleton. Inductively, every alternative safe history stays in this span and retains that uncertainty subset. It cannot identify the matrix.

The displayed matrix in Section 3.5 lies on a face of the entrywise prior, so the earlier full-interior sufficient condition for an \(n\)-query lower bound does not literally apply. Nevertheless, its other rows lie strictly inside their row boxes. Fewer than four independent queries leave a nonzero null direction in an interior row, preserving all data under a small perturbation. Four queries are therefore still necessary for that displayed instance. No false lower-bound finding is made.

## Two-step safety and generic identification

In Theorem 10, the two-step observation equations are equivalently \(Ax_j=y_j^{(1)}\), \(Ay_j^{(1)}=y_j^{(2)}\), so they are linear in the unknown matrix. The proof handles singleton uncertainty separately. For a nonsingleton intersection with a strictly convex ellipsoid, affine parameterization of the measurement space gives a strictly convex pulled-back quadratic. A midpoint of two distinct feasible points is strictly feasible. The S-lemma therefore has its needed relative strict-feasibility condition, even for a lower-dimensional ambient intersection. Its multiplier sign and the affine dependence of the resulting quadratic coefficients on \(x\) are correct.

With \(m=\lceil n/2\rceil\), full row rank of \([X,A_\star X]\) determines \(A_\star\) from \[A_\star[X,Y^{(1)}]=[Y^{(1)},Y^{(2)}].\] A nonzero determinant witness works for both parities: choose \(X=[e_1,\ldots,e_m]\), and map \(e_j\) to \(e_{m+j}\) for \(1\le j\le n-m\), killing the remaining basis vectors. The first \(n\) columns form the identity. The bad joint algebraic set is null, and Fubini gives an ambient measure-zero exceptional set of matrices. This does not promise genericity relative to every lower-dimensional prior.

Given the finite selections qualified in F8, Lemma 12’s coordinate slicing constructs an affinely spanning polytope. Fresh independent simplex draws have an absolutely continuous image on state space. Conditioning on the full history, with measurable choices, validates the density argument in Proposition 14. It is not a guarantee for every exceptional matrix or for well-conditioned numerical recovery.

## Infinite-horizon certificates

Proposition 15 correctly uses Cayley–Hamilton on \(A_\star\) to show that agreement on the first \(n\) transitions determines the entire trajectory. The zero state and a lower-dimensional Krylov span cause no exception. The source explicitly warns that this exact-data reduction does not automatically extend to noisy trajectories.

For \(Q>0\), the constraints in Lemma 20 imply that \(E_Q=\{x:x^TQ^{-1}x\le1\}\) lies in \(S\), contains the chosen point, and is invariant under the fixed uncertain \(A\). The Schur-complement implication does not require \(A\) to be invertible. Theorem 18’s soundness follows pointwise: equality multipliers vanish on the data set, inequality multipliers are PSD, and the positive \(\varepsilon\) margin forces \(Q(A)>0\). The construction uses a matrix-dependent \(Q(A)\); it does not assert invariance under arbitrary switching between different matrices.

After F6–F7 are repaired, compact individual stability gives a uniform finite Lyapunov sum. The compact polyhedral set, including both signs of measurement equalities, has the required Archimedean description. Scalar and matrix positivity representations supply finite-degree certificates. Uniform scaling and the corrected positive ball prove full-dimensionality. The degree-dependent SDP size is polynomial for fixed degree, as the proof specifies; no polynomial sufficient-degree bound is established.

Theorem 25’s Krylov determinant is nonzero at the nilpotent shift and \(e_1\). Its generic single-trajectory statement follows from a polynomial zero-set argument and Fubini. It is an exact-arithmetic result, not uniform recovery for every matrix.

For counterevidence concerning Section 5.5.2, write its separate prior as \(D=C+E\), where \[C=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\-1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \left\lVert E\right\rVert_F\le1/10.\] The determinant expansion and \(|\det E|\le\left\lVert E\right\rVert_F^2/2\) give \(0.595<\det D<0.905\), \(\operatorname{Tr}D>1.3\), and \(1-\operatorname{Tr}D+\det D>29/200\). Complex eigenvalues then have modulus \(\sqrt{\det D}<1\); real eigenvalues are positive, and these inequalities exclude an eigenvalue at or above one. Thus individual stability of this separate prior is supported. Its reported costs and random-instance statistics were not reproduced.

## Nonlinear certificates, noise, and fitting

For noiseless one-step data and an unobserved candidate, the nonlinear value can be chosen independently of finitely many sampled values under the stated pointwise growth prior. The support separates as \[\sup_{A\in U_{k,A}}h_i^TAx+
 \gamma\left\lVert h_i\right\rVert_1\left\lVert x\right\rVert_p^d.\] The LP signs and dual-norm factor in Theorem 26 are consistent. Previously sampled points remain in the program’s safe set under a valid noiseless robust history, because the linear uncertainty only shrinks. Thus the proof’s union with sampled points does not by itself refute that theorem.

An actual scalar check with \(S=[-1,1]\), \(A\in[-1,2]\), \(\gamma=1/2\), \(d=p=1\), and observation \(x_1=2/5\mapsto0\), for true zero dynamics, expanded the safe interval from \([-2/5,2/5]\) to all of \(S\). The data imply \(A\in[-1/2,1/2]\), and the support coefficients change from \(5/2\) to \(1\).

Lemma 31’s LMI implies \((A+B)Q(A+B)^T\preceq Q\) for every \(\left\lVert B\right\rVert_2\le\gamma\). For a nonzero state \(u\), the choice \(B_u=g(u)u^T/\left\lVert u\right\rVert_2^2\) realizes the nonlinear update. At zero, the relative bound forces \(g(0)=0\), so no division is needed. A concrete positive certificate used \[A=\gamma=\frac14,\quad Q=\frac12,\quad
 \lambda=1,\quad x=\frac12,\quad\varepsilon=\frac14.\] The robust LMI has determinant \(3/16\), and after subtracting the strict margin its determinant is \(3/128\); the membership determinant is \(1/4\). This supports the signs in the main nonlinear certificate.

Theorem 29’s sufficient small-\(\gamma\) argument uses a uniform Lyapunov margin on a compact stable prior, a common multiplier bound, strict containment scaling, and the Archimedean module. The local radius correction F7 preserves that argument. Remark 30’s noisy-trajectory residual bound follows from the triangle inequality when its supplied \(M_k\) is a valid bound on a set containing the true \(A_\star\). A scalar exact check gave residual \(1/20\) against the valid bound \(1/4\). A wrongly noise-free estimate of \(M_k\) would not satisfy that premise, but no such violation was established.

The nonlinear fitting example [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~41--42]{source}] places the displayed true map within its claimed growth bound: its four unscaled component magnitudes are at most \(2,\sqrt2,1,1\) on the box, before multiplication by \(\gamma/2\). The fitted quadratic/SOS constraints are sufficient for prior consistency at the chosen degree. They do not place the nonpolynomial truth inside the fitted class or establish completeness of that fixed-degree certificate. The reported test errors \(0.0851\) and \(0.2567\) remain experiment-specific observations; the data, coefficients, random seed, and solver output were not available for independent reproduction.

## Sparse, permutation, and controlled extensions

Theorem 32’s sparse convex hull and LP dual agree with the support equal to \(M\) times the sum of the \(k\) largest absolute objective coefficients. For coefficients \((2,6,-1,-3)\), \(M=1/2\), \(k=2\), an actual finite enumeration gave \(9/2\), matching the threshold dual. The zero bound and a support budget exceeding dimension are harmless boundary cases.

Theorem 37 is the assignment LP dual over the Birkhoff hull. An explicit six-permutation check for \(h=(2,-1,3)\), \(x=(1,4,-2)\) gave maximum support \(16\). The identity permutation already imposes \(x\in S\); there is no missing initial-state constraint in that formulation. Closure under powers justifies Remark 38 for the initial permutation family.

Proposition 40 keeps the joint data equalities \(Ax_\ell+Bu_\ell=y_\ell\) and uses the same multiplier for both matrix blocks. It does not replace joint uncertainty by independent posterior projections. Corollary 41’s augmentation \[\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&I\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{on }S\times C\] correctly represents a one-step controlled query. Singleton joint uncertainty is equivalent to singleton \(A\) and \(B\) projections. The at-most-\(n+\bar n\) measurement conclusion follows from the augmented span argument, with the selection qualification in F8.
