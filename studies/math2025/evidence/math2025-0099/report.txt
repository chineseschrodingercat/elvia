# Version, claim, and scope

Thomas Budzinski, Nicolas Curien and Bram Petri, On Cheeger constants of hyperbolic surfaces, Inventiones mathematicae 242 (2025), 511–530, DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01361-w. The reviewed object is the manifest-bound publisher Version of Record, 20 PDF pages; PDF page \(p\) has printed page \(510+p\). All 20 pages were read as text and actually inspected as page images. No arXiv text is substituted for this version.

For a closed orientable curvature \(-1\) surface \(S\) of genus \(g\geq2\), put \(V=|S|=4\pi(g-1)\). Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2, printed p. 512) asserts \[\limsup_{g\to\infty}\ \sup_{S\in\mathcal M_g}h(S)\leq \frac2\pi .\] This is an asymptotic supremum bound, not a bound \(h(S)\leq2/\pi\) for each finite genus and not a claim of sharpness. The definition on p. 1 takes the infimum of boundary length divided by positive area over smooth subsurfaces of area at most \(V/2\).

The essential dependencies are Lemma 2 (area concentration), Proposition 3 (uniform expected Voronoi boundary length), Lemmas 4–7 used in that proposition, and the coloring/Markov argument in Section 3.4. Definitions, quantifiers, degeneration of the injectivity radius, and limiting operations were checked along this chain. Background comparisons with random surfaces, spectral gaps, and graphs were read but their cited theorems were not independently reproved. Statements about ideal tessellations are motivation rather than a dependency of this proof.

# Area concentration: hypotheses and probability identities

The construction on pp. 7–8 uses a Poisson process of intensity \(\lambda\) relative to area and independent fair colors for its Voronoi cells. When there are no nuclei, the convention is the single cell \(S\). The chosen \(A_\lambda\) is the smaller of the unions of the two colors; it need not be a single connected component. This formal definition resolves the looser introductory component language.

Let \(X\) be the total black area and let the cell areas be \(v_i\). Conditional on the tessellation, \[\mathbb E(X\mid\mathrm{Vor})=\frac V2,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(X\mid\mathrm{Vor})=\frac14\sum_i v_i^2.\] The conditional mean is deterministic, including the zero-nucleus convention. Thus there is no missing between-tessellation variance term, and \[\operatorname{Var}X
 =\frac14\int_{S^2}\mathbb P(x,y\hbox{ lie in the same cell})\,dx\,dy .\] This verifies the normalization in Lemma 2, pp. 8–9. Exhaustive independent-color enumeration for four cells of areas \(1,2,3,4\) gives mean \(5\) and variance \(15/2\), exactly the formula above.

The lower ball-growth estimate uses \(h(S)\geq\delta\) only while \(|B_r(x)|\leq V/2\). Coarea gives \(B_r'\geq\delta B_r\) almost everywhere in that range. The wording on p. 9 should retain this restriction; its actual application chooses the genus large enough that the universal hyperbolic upper bound for \(|B_{r_1}|\) is below \(V/2\), so the required range is valid.

For clarity, the thin-area estimate concerns truncated thin collars, not maximal standard collars. Choose \(r_0\) smaller than the collar-disjointness threshold. For a core geodesic of length \(\ell\leq2r_0\), the normal displacement formula gives \[\cosh R\leq \frac{\sinh r_0}{\sinh(\ell/2)},\qquad
 2\ell\sinh R\leq 4\sinh r_0.\] At most \(3g-3\) disjoint short cores contribute. Hence the area with injectivity radius at most \(r_0\), divided by \(V\), is bounded by \(3\sinh(r_0)/\pi=O(r_0)\) uniformly in the surface. Taking a smaller \(r_0\) is allowed and supplies the desired \(\delta^3\) bound.

For thick \(x\), the ball of radius \(r_0\) has its full hyperbolic area. Choose \(r_1=r_1(\lambda,\delta)\) so its subsequent exponential growth makes \(\exp(-\lambda|B_{r_1}(x)|)<\delta^3/2\). If \(x,y\) are thick and \(d(x,y)>2r_1\), a common nearest nucleus cannot coexist with a nucleus in each of the two disjoint \(r_1\)-balls. The union bound therefore gives same-cell probability at most \(\delta^3\). Pairs with a thin endpoint occupy at most \(2\delta^3 V^2\), and pairs at distance at most \(2r_1\) occupy at most \(V\,2\pi(\cosh(2r_1)-1)\). Consequently \[\operatorname{Var}X\leq \tfrac34\delta^3 V^2+O_{\lambda,\delta}(V)
 <\delta^3 V^2\] for sufficiently large genus. Chebyshev gives the stated probability bound. The genus threshold may depend strongly on \(\lambda,\delta\); the theorem requires no quantitative control of that dependence.

# Perimeter estimate and uniformity

## Angular optimization and Poisson conditioning

Lemma 4 (pp. 10–11) bounds the angular kernel \(K(t)=|\sin(t/2)|\). With Fourier normalization \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}\), \[\widehat K(0)=\frac2\pi,\qquad
 \widehat K(n)=-\frac{2}{\pi(4n^2-1)}\quad(n\ne0).\] All nonconstant coefficients are negative, which proves the asserted maximum \(2/\pi\) for probability measures, attained by uniform measure. Smooth approximation extends the density calculation to arbitrary probability measures because the kernel is bounded and continuous. The coefficients for \(n=0,\ldots,8\) were independently integrated symbolically. For the test density \((1+t\cos(m\theta))/(2\pi)\) the integral is \(2/\pi-t^2/[\pi(4m^2-1)]\), consistent with the sign and normalization.

For fixed \(S\), Voronoi boundaries form finitely many geodesic segments almost surely. Their \(\varepsilon\)-tube area divided by \(2\varepsilon\) converges to total boundary length. Fatou’s lemma has the correct direction for the desired expectation upper bound. The nearest-nucleus integral in (11), p. 13, includes the empty-ball factor \(e^{-\lambda|B_r(x)|}\) and a second-nucleus factor \(1-e^{-\lambda|A^\varepsilon_S(x,y)|}\). The latter region is outside the open nearest-nucleus ball, so independent Poisson increments apply. Null distance ties do not change the integral. Replacing \(1-e^{-u}\) by \(u\) gives (12).

A bisector meeting the small ball need not itself be an exposed Voronoi face. Nevertheless, the segment from \(x\) to that bisector either stays in the nearest cell up to its boundary, or exits earlier through another cell boundary. This justifies the event interpretation used for the upper bound. The zero-nucleus event creates no boundary.

## Thin annuli and changes of lift

Lemma 5 (pp. 11–12) gives \(o(\varepsilon)\) intersection area for two thin annuli when their centers are separated by \(1/K\) and their radii are at most \(K\). Hyperbolic cosine law restricts the angle to an interval in the cosine coordinate of size \(O_K(\varepsilon)\), so the angular measure is \(O_K(\sqrt\varepsilon)\) even at tangency. Multiplication by radial thickness gives \(O_K(\varepsilon^{3/2})\).

The preliminary inference printed as \(a\geq1/(2K)\) from nonempty intersection is slightly too strong: it only gives \(a\geq1/(2K)-\varepsilon\). Requiring \(\varepsilon<1/(4K)\) gives \(a\geq1/(4K)\), which is all that is needed to bound the hyperbolic-law denominator away from zero. This repairs the argument with changed constants, not changed hypotheses or conclusion.

In Lemma 6 (pp. 13–14), competing deck translates give intersections of such annuli. For a fixed compact surface their centers are separated by its positive systole, their radii are bounded by its diameter, and only finitely many translates are relevant, uniformly in \(x,y\). Thus the accumulated error is \(o_S(\varepsilon)\). This is not an error uniform over moduli space and need not be: the proof first takes \(\varepsilon\to0\) for each fixed \(S\). The bound left after that limit has constants depending only on \(\delta,\lambda\). No positive uniform systole is assumed.

Lemma 6 is a one-sided estimate. Accordingly the second line of the last display on p. 16 can be read with \(\leq o_S(\varepsilon)+\cdots\), rather than the printed equality. Every subsequent step is an upper bound and remains valid.

## The angle-sensitive displacement estimate

The calculation in Lemma 7 (pp. 14–16) yields, with \(r'\geq r\), \[(\cosh r'-\cosh r)^2
 \leq \varepsilon^2(\sinh r'-\sinh r)^2+
 4\varepsilon^2\sin^2\!\frac{\theta'-\theta}{2}\sinh r\,\sinh r'.\] Here \((\sinh r'-\sinh r)/(\cosh r'-\cosh r)
=\coth((r+r')/2)\leq\coth r_0\), and \(r'\leq r+2\varepsilon\). Choose \(\varepsilon_0\) so \[\varepsilon_0^2\coth^2r_0
 <1-(1+\delta/2)^{-2},\qquad
 e^{\varepsilon_0\coth r_0}
 <\frac{1+\delta}{1+\delta/2}.\] These inequalities justify absorption and the remaining square-root ratio, giving \[r'-r\leq2(1+\delta)\varepsilon
 \left|\sin\frac{\theta'-\theta}{2}\right|.\] Thus the last step uses both a large \(r_0\) and a small \(\varepsilon_0\); increasing \(r_0\) alone would not make a fixed \(\varepsilon\) ratio tend to one. The lemma already allows both choices. Independent parameter checks gave a common sufficient choice for several \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\).

The Dirichlet domain is star-shaped at its center, so its allowed angle sets \(I_r\) decrease with \(r\). This gives the inclusion used in the polar-area estimate and \[B'_r=|I_r|\sinh r,\qquad
 B_r\geq |I_r|(\cosh r-1),\qquad
 B'_r\leq(1+\delta)B_r\quad(r\geq r_0).\] The derivative identities hold almost everywhere; ball area is absolutely continuous, which is sufficient for integration.

# A verified finite-volume correction, with its effect

The last evaluation on p. 17 (printed p. 527), immediately after (17), drops the terminal term at \(r=\infty\). On a compact surface \(B_r\to V\), not infinity. The exact value is \[\lambda^2\int_0^\infty B'_r B_r e^{-\lambda B_r}\,dr
 =\lambda^2\int_0^V u e^{-\lambda u}\,du
 =1-(1+\lambda V)e^{-\lambda V}\leq1.\] It is strictly less than \(1\) for finite positive \(\lambda V\). Independent symbolic integration verifies the identity; its derivative in \(x=\lambda V\) is \(xe^{-x}>0\), and its limits are \(0\) and \(1\).

The displayed equality can therefore be replaced by an inequality with exactly the upper bound printed afterward. This is decisive counterevidence to treating the missing tail as a failure of the perimeter theorem. In particular, \[\frac{\mathbb E|\partial\mathrm{Vor}_\lambda(S)|}{V}
 \leq\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac2\pi(1+\delta)^3
 \leq\frac2\pi(1+\delta)^4\] for sufficiently small \(\lambda\) depending only on \(\delta\). The surface-dependent \(o_S(\varepsilon)\) has already vanished. No interchange of a uniform moduli-space supremum with that error limit is necessary.

This is recorded as a verified proof clarification, with exact location, correction, and effect, rather than a new substantive Major or Minor defect. It neither falsifies the proposition nor requires an additional geometric hypothesis. Likewise, the introductory ball-growth display on p. 4 has an extraneous additive \(1\); the usual differential inequality gives \(B_r\geq B_{r_0}e^{h(S)(r-r_0)}\) over its permitted range. That background slip is not used in the new proof.

# Final implication, boundary cases, and disposition

Fix \(0<\delta<1/2\). Surfaces with \(h(S)\leq\delta\) need no further argument. For the rest choose \(\lambda=\lambda(\delta)>0\) so the expected total Voronoi boundary is at most \((2/\pi)(1+\delta)V\). A side separating distinct cells belongs to the two-color interface with probability \(1/2\) conditional on the tessellation. Therefore \[\mathbb E|\partial A_\lambda|\leq \frac{1+\delta}{\pi}V,\qquad
 \mathbb P\!\left(|\partial A_\lambda|\leq
 \frac{(1+\delta)^2}{\pi}V\right)\geq\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}.\] The sidewise expectations require no independence between distinct sides.

Apply Lemma 2 with tolerance \(\delta^2\); \(h(S)>\delta\) implies its required \(h(S)\geq\delta^2\). Its area event has probability greater than \(1-\delta^2\) and gives \(|A_\lambda|\geq V(1/2-\delta^2)\geq(1-\delta)V/2\). The intersection with the perimeter event has positive probability, since \[\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}-\delta^2
 =\frac{\delta(1-\delta-\delta^2)}{1+\delta}>0.\] No area/perimeter independence and no expectation-of-a-ratio identity is being used. The smaller-color choice ensures area at most \(V/2\). Finitely many polygonal corners can be rounded with changes tending to zero in area and length; taking the smaller complementary side if necessary respects the smooth-boundary definition. Hence \[h(S)\leq\frac2\pi\,\frac{(1+\delta)^2}{1-\delta}.\] For each fixed \(\delta\) the genus threshold is uniform in \(S\); letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves precisely the stated limsup bound.
