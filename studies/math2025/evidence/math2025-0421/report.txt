Abstract: One invalid estimate is established in the proof of the principal Lipschitz statement: the outer-factor estimate on page 32 is false even for the bounded, real, reflection-symmetric data required there. An explicit admissible pair also disproves the subsequent numerical coefficient. This is a major issue in a core derivation, with a limited and explicitly proved repair: retaining the squares in the cited estimate gives the advertised constant \(1.6\). The counterexample therefore does not refute the final theorem. The review also examines the existence and uniqueness argument, the algorithmic error chain, and several independent low-degree computations. It is an initial technical review, not independent acceptance.

# Claims, hypotheses, and dependency structure

In Theorem 1 (page 4), \(f\) is real and measurable, with an even extension to \([-1,1]\), \(|f|\leq1\), and \[\int_0^1\frac{\log(1-f(x)^2)}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx>-\infty.\] The claimed phases satisfy both the weighted \(L^2\) convergence and the nonlinear Plancherel equality. Uniqueness is subject to these simultaneous requirements, rather than to convergence alone. The norm is \[\|f\|_{\mathcal S}^2=\frac2\pi\int_0^1
       \frac{|f(x)|^2}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx.\] The Lipschitz assertion concerns \(0<\eta<1/2\) and \(f,f'\in\mathcal S_\eta=\{f:\|f\|_\infty\leq1-\eta\}\), with constant \(1.6\eta^{-3}\) from \(\mathcal S\) to \(\ell^\infty\).

Under \(x=\cos\theta\), \(z=e^{2i\theta}\), the circle datum \(b=if\) is purely imaginary and reflection symmetric. The complementary factor has \(a^*\) outer, \(a^*(0)>0\), and \(|a|^2+|b|^2=1\). Theorem 4 supplies this normalization. Theorem 5 and Lemmas 9–11 use a densely defined, potentially unbounded operator; the bounded polynomial algorithm cannot replace this endpoint argument. The self-adjointness of \(iM\), its resolvent bound, and the weak testing identity are the relevant steps. The proof of Theorem 12 uses the resolvent comparison and the outer normalization.

Theorem 2 concerns even polynomials of degree \(2d\), \(d\geq1\), a strict positive gap \(\eta\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Its numerical claims do not include \(\eta=0\). The error analysis uses the annular extension, Fourier alias estimates, invertibility of the Hankel block system, and a lower bound for the normalization denominator. These dependencies are separate from the defective Lipschitz estimate below.

# Problem and decisive evidence

Equation reference: eq:false, eq:false-delta. Problem 1 (Major: false outer-factor estimate in a core derivation).  On page 32, Section 4.5, the unnumbered display introduced by By [1, (8.5)] asserts \[
 \|a-a'\|_2\leq
 \big\|\sqrt{1-|b|^2}-\sqrt{1-|b'|^2}\big\|_2
 +\frac14\big\|\log(1-|b|^2)-\log(1-|b'|^2)\big\|_2.\] The subsequent display introduced by Thus we obtain asserts \[
 \|a-a'\|_2\leq(\delta^{-1}+\tfrac12\delta^{-2})\|b-b'\|_2,
 \qquad \delta^2=\tfrac32\eta .\] Both statements are false under the hypotheses used in that proof. The cited estimate has the squares of all three norms.

All circle integrals below use normalized measure \(d\phi/(2\pi)\). Set \[p=\frac1{10},\qquad r=\frac1{100},\qquad t=\frac1{100},\qquad
 \alpha=\pi p,\qquad
 q(e^{i\phi})=\mathbf1_{\{|\phi|<\alpha\}}.\] Let \[r'=re^t,\quad c=\sqrt{1-r^2},\quad c'=\sqrt{1-r'^2},\quad
 \eta=1-c,\quad b=icq,\quad b'=ic'q.\] Here \(0<c'<c=1-\eta<1\) and \(0<\eta<1/2\). In the paper’s interval variable these are \[f(x)=c\,\mathbf1_{\{x>\cos(\pi p/2)\}},\qquad
 f'(x)=c'\,\mathbf1_{\{x>\cos(\pi p/2)\}},
 \quad 0\leq x\leq1,\] with even extensions. They are measurable and belong to \(\mathcal S_\eta\); both Szegő logarithms are bounded. Endpoint choices for the indicator have measure zero.

Write \(h=Hq\) for the circle Hilbert transform. Up to the harmless choice of its sign, \[h(\phi)=\frac1\pi\log\left|
       \frac{\sin((\phi+\alpha)/2)}{\sin((\phi-\alpha)/2)}
                         \right|.\] The normalized complementary factors are \[a=\exp\big((\log r)(q-ih)\big),\qquad
 a'=\exp\big((\log r+t)(q-ih)\big).\] Their conjugates are outer, have positive value \(r^p\) and \((r')^p\) at the origin, and satisfy the required modulus identities. Thus the construction uses exactly the normalization of Theorem 4.

Equation reference: eq:lower. Lemma 1.  For this pair, \[
 \|a-a'\|_2^2\geq
 t^2p\left(\frac23-p-\frac{t^2}{2}\right).\] In particular, \(\|a-a'\|_2>(3/4)t\sqrt p\).

Proof. The Hilbert-transform isometry on mean-zero functions gives \(\int h^2=p(1-p)\). On \(0<\phi<\alpha\), monotonicity of \(\sin u/u\) gives \[0\leq\log\frac{\sin((\alpha+\phi)/2)}
                       {\sin((\alpha-\phi)/2)}
 \leq\log\frac{\alpha+\phi}{\alpha-\phi}.\] Consequently, with \(E=\{|\phi|<\alpha\}\), \[\int_E h^2\leq
 \frac{\alpha}{\pi^3}\int_0^1
       \log^2\frac{1+u}{1-u}\,du
 =\frac p3.\] For completeness, the integral is \(\pi^2/3\): substituting \(v=(1+u)/2\) and using symmetry reduces it to \(\int_0^1\log^2(v/(1-v))\,dv\). The two square terms each integrate to \(2\), while \(\int_0^1\log v\log(1-v)\,dv=2-\pi^2/6\), obtained by integrating the power series for \(-\log(1-v)\). It follows that \(\int_{E^c}h^2\geq p(2/3-p)\).

The logarithmic singularities of \(h\) give \(h\in L^4\). The function \(g=q-p+ih\) is the boundary value of an analytic function with zero constant coefficient. Hence \(\int g^4=0\) and its real part yields \[\int h^4=6\int(q-p)^2h^2-\int(q-p)^4\leq6p.\] On \(E^c\), both moduli equal one and \(|a-a'|^2=2(1-\cos(th))\). The elementary inequality \(2(1-\cos u)\geq u^2-u^4/12\), valid for every real \(u\), now proves [eq:lower] (#eq:lower). Finally \(2/3-p-t^2/2=17/30-1/20000>9/16\). ◻

The right-hand side of [eq:false] (#eq:false) is exactly \[\left[r(e^t-1)+\frac t2\right]\sqrt p
 <0.005101\sqrt p,\] using \(e^{0.01}-1<0.0101\). The lemma gives a left-hand side larger than \(0.0075\sqrt p\), proving the first failure.

The next coefficient is not rescued by reading the first display as a missing-square typographical error. Indeed, \[\delta^2=\frac{3r^2}{2(1+c)},\qquad
 c-c'=\frac{r^2(e^{2t}-1)}{c+c'}.\] Since \(\delta^{-1}<6/(5r)\), \(1/(2\delta^2)<2/(3r^2)\), \(c+c'>1.99\), and \(e^{0.02}-1<0.0203\), \[\begin{aligned}
 (\delta^{-1}+\tfrac12\delta^{-2})\|b-b'\|_2
 &<\left(\frac65r+\frac23\right)
                  \frac{0.0203}{1.99}\sqrt p\\
 &<0.007\sqrt p<\|a-a'\|_2.\end{aligned}\] The exponential inequalities follow directly from their convergent positive Taylor series with a geometric bound on the remaining tail. Thus neither contradiction depends on a numerical quadrature.

# Repair, counterevidence, and impact

Equation reference: eq:true. The correct squared estimate is \[
 \|a-a'\|_2^2\leq
 \big\|\sqrt{1-|b|^2}-\sqrt{1-|b'|^2}\big\|_2^2
 +\frac14\big\|\log(1-|b|^2)-\log(1-|b'|^2)\big\|_2^2 .\] It is visible as (8.5) on page 30 of the acquired arXiv version of [amt]. It also follows directly here: the pointwise cosine identity bounds the squared difference by the squared modulus difference plus the squared phase difference; the phase difference is the Hilbert transform of the difference of the logarithmic moduli, and \(H\) is an \(L^2\) contraction. This argument requires no small-signal hypothesis when the logarithms are in \(L^2\), as they are on \(\mathcal S_\eta\).

Equation reference: eq:repair. On \(0\leq x\leq1-\eta\), the derivative bounds used by the manuscript are valid: \(\delta^{-1}\) for \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\) and \(2\delta^{-2}\) for \(\log(1-x^2)\). Applying them to [eq:true] (#eq:true) gives \[\|a-a'\|_2\leq
       \sqrt{\delta^{-2}+\delta^{-4}}\,\|b-b'\|_2.\] Together with the quotient decomposition on page 32 this yields \[\left\|\frac b a-\frac{b'}{a'}\right\|_2
 \leq\left(\delta^{-1}
       +\delta^{-2}\sqrt{\delta^{-2}+\delta^{-4}}\right)\|b-b'\|_2.\] Theorem 12 contributes \(\sqrt2\,\delta^{-2}(1+\delta)\). The arctangent map has Lipschitz constant at most one, and the circle-to-interval substitution preserves the relevant \(L^2\) norm. The same shift argument applies to each phase. Therefore the corrected constant in the theorem is bounded by \[\begin{aligned}
 C_\eta
 &\leq \sqrt2\,\delta^{-6}(1+\delta)
                    \left(\delta^3+\sqrt{1+\delta^2}\right)\notag\\
 &\leq \eta^{-3}\frac{8\sqrt2}{27}
     \left(1+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)
     \left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}+\frac{\sqrt7}{2}\right)
 <1.6\eta^{-3}.                                      \end{aligned}\] The last strict inequality is elementary, not a floating-point assumption: replacing \(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt7\) by the rational upper bounds \(1.415,1.733,2.646\), respectively, already gives a number below \(1.55\). The sharper evaluated coefficient is approximately \(1.5422422668\).

Severity and boundary. The false estimates are used in the quantitative proof of a main theorem, and both are contradicted within its full hypotheses. This supports a Major classification for the core derivation. The local repair above proves that this particular defect does not invalidate the stated \(1.6\eta^{-3}\) conclusion. No failure of existence, uniqueness, or the separate algorithmic theorem is established. The report recommends replacing the two estimates and the ensuing constant calculation, not withdrawing the final theorem. Author adoption of this repair is unknown.

The most favorable alternate reading is that squares were omitted in copying the reference. It explains the origin of the first display, but cannot justify the second, disproved coefficient. Conversely, simply taking a triangle bound with coefficient \(1/2\) in the unsquared display loses unnecessary sharpness; the squared estimate retains enough margin to prove [eq:repair] (#eq:repair). A bounded exact-title search of the primary author and journal records did not locate a relevant correction. This is not a claim that no correction exists anywhere.

# Independent checks and findings not established

For a separate exact check, let \(f_\rho=\rho T_2\), \(0\leq\rho\leq1\), and put \(s=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\), \(A=(1+s)/2\), \(B=(1-s)/2\). Then \[a=A-Bz^{-2},\qquad b=\frac{i\rho}{2}(z+z^{-1})\] satisfy \(|a|^2+|b|^2=1\). The symmetric phase sequence \[\psi_0=0,\qquad \psi_1=\tfrac12\arcsin\rho,\qquad
 \psi_j=0\quad(j\geq2)\] gives the required polynomial. With \(u=\rho/(1+s)\), \(1/(1+u^2)=A\), so the nonlinear energy is \(2\log(1+u^2)=-2\log A\), in agreement with the outer-factor identity. At \(\rho=1\), the isolated endpoint zeros still have integrable logarithm and the phase is \(\pi/4\). This checks a genuine Szegő boundary case; it does not extend Theorem 2’s algorithm to zero gap.

A reviewer-written implementation of Algorithms 1–2, using NumPy float64/complex128 and no author imports, tested \(0.1T_2\), \(0.8T_2\), \(0.999T_2\), and \(0.2+0.3T_2-0.1T_4\). Fourier sample counts were 2048, 2048, 524288, and 2048, respectively. The maximum reconstruction discrepancy on 1001 equally spaced points of \([0,1]\) was at most \(3.34\times10^{-16}\); the linear-system residuals were at most \(1.12\times10^{-16}\). The first three phase sequences agreed with the exact family to \(2.23\times10^{-16}\). These are finite checks, not a proof of the asymptotic precision or work bounds.

In the manuscript’s proof of Theorem 8, the required \[N\geq\frac{8d}{\eta}\log\frac{576d^2}{\eta^4\varepsilon}\] provides a positive-gap Fourier error budget. The purely imaginary Hankel coefficients make the corresponding off-diagonal block skew-Hermitian, giving the singular-value lower bound used in Proposition 24. The normalization lower bound prevents division by a vanishing coefficient. These checks did not reveal another substantive defect.

Several isolated sign, logarithm, index, or projection-label inconsistencies were considered against their surrounding definitions and actual uses. They were not counted as additional Problems where the operative argument was unambiguous or the stated final bound retained sufficient margin. In particular, the resolvent-domain reasoning was checked as an unbounded-operator argument, not by assuming a uniformly positive modulus in the full Szegő existence theorem.
