Abstract: The exact journal version is examined. A two-torsion example disproves the stated pole classification and characteristic-function limit for the \(c\)-cost. A constant positive weight also disproves two uniform estimates used in the spectral argument. These are substantive defects in stated core ingredients, although they do not by themselves refute the near-zero Gaussian laws. Two additional local corrections concern the unweighted counting constant and a missing centering hypothesis. The overall assessment is Major; scientific adjudication remains pending.

Title: Technical review of dynamics of continued fractions and modular symbols

# Identity, scope and necessary assumptions

The reviewed paper is Jungwon Lee and Hae-Sang Sun, Dynamics of continued fractions and distribution of modular symbols, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 27 (2025), 3527–3582, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1665 (https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1665) [paper]. This is the 56-page publisher PDF, received 8 April 2020 and revised 3 February 2025, published 19 June 2025. Physical page \(k\) has printed page \(3526+k\). The distinct arXiv:1902.06277v3, dated 7 May 2025, was found in the metadata search; no textual equivalence to that manuscript is asserted.

All 56 pages were read as extracted text. Detailed checks addressed Definitions 1.1 and the costs \(b_\psi,c_\psi\), the hypotheses of Theorems A–E, the finite-coset coboundary arguments, the passage from operators to Dirichlet series, the real-weight estimates, the residue, and the centering used in the Hessian calculation. Selected source pages were actually viewed to verify every decisive formula below. Reading the entire text does not constitute an independent reconstruction of every cited theorem.

Throughout the paper, \(\Gamma\) has finite index in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\) and is normalized by \(j=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\). Rational numbers have their canonical continued fraction, with final digit greater than one. The sample space is \[\Omega_M=\bigcup_{2\le n\le M}
 \{a/n:1\le a<n,\ (a,n)=1\},\] with interval restriction and, where specified, a nonnegative nonzero endpoint weight. The Gaussian conclusions require nondegenerate covariance, expressed through real coboundaries. Residual conclusions require the stated conditions modulo the actual modulus; a real coboundary condition cannot silently substitute for them. The averaging is over denominators up to \(M\), not a resolution of the fixed-denominator conjecture.

# Confirmed problems

Problem 1 (Two-torsion produces cancellation, not the asserted pole).  Definition 1.1(2), p. 3530 (physical 4), permits a nonzero constant \(c\)-coboundary in a group with two-torsion. Proposition 2.6 and Corollary 2.7, p. 3542 (physical 16), omit this possibility. It propagates to Proposition 3.2(1), p. 3544 (physical 18), and Theorem 3.8, p. 3547 (physical 21).

Complete analytic counterexample. Take \[\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z),\quad d=1,\quad
 g=c,\quad \psi\equiv1,\quad J=(0,1),\quad \Psi\equiv1,\quad v=\pi.\] There is one coset, the index is finite, and \(j\) normalizes \(\Gamma\). All test functions are admissible and nonzero. The real-valued function \(v\psi\), reduced in \(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z\), is a \(c\)-coboundary associated with \(\beta=0\): the left side of Definition 1.1(2) is \(\pi+\pi=0\) in that group, for every pair of digits. Thus the hypothesis of Proposition 3.2(1) holds.

On the other hand \(c_\psi(r)=\ell(r)\), the canonical length. For \(0<r<1/2\), write \(r=[0;m_1,\ldots,m_\ell]\), with \(m_1\ge2\). Except for \(r=1/2\), \[1-r=[0;1,m_1-1,m_2,\ldots,m_\ell]\] is canonical; when \(\ell=1\), this means \([0;1,m_1-1]\), whose last digit is at least two. Consequently \(\ell(1-r)=\ell(r)+1\). The reverse identity applies when \(r>1/2\). The involution preserves the reduced denominator and has no fixed point for denominator \(n>2\). Hence \[\sum_{\substack{1\le a<n\\(a,n)=1}}(-1)^{\ell(a/n)}
 =\begin{cases}-1,&n=2,\\0,&n>2.\end{cases}\] The exact Dirichlet series of Section 3 is therefore \[L_{\Psi,J}(2s,i\pi)=-2^{-2s}.
 \tag{2.1}\] It is entire. In particular it has no pole at \(s=1=s(i\pi)\), contrary to Proposition 3.2(1). Similarly, \[\mathbb E[(-1)^\ell\mid\Omega_M]=-\frac1{|\Omega_M|}
 \longrightarrow0,\] whereas Theorem 3.8 gives \(R(\pi)=1\) from \(\beta=0\) and the single coset. The contradiction is order one and cannot be absorbed by its decaying error.

Dependency and impact. In the same example over \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), the constant function \(1\) is a nonzero \(c\)-coboundary with constant \(\beta=0\). This directly contradicts Proposition 2.6 and the asserted description in Corollary 2.7(1). The passage in Section 10.1, p. 3579 (physical 53), conjugates the final operator as if it had the same coboundary transformation as the even-step operator. For this example the even-step weight is \(e^{2i\pi}=1\), while the odd terminal part carries a minus sign; the leading contributions cancel. An eigenvalue of the even-step operator alone does not establish a pole of the evaluated series.

The impact is Major: an explicit admissible example disproves two principal analytic and distributional statements used in Section 3, not merely a line of exposition. The example does not disprove Theorem A near zero, nor does it establish failure of every modular-symbol specialization or of every sufficient condition in Theorem B.

Repair boundary. The torsion hypothesis and the final even/odd contributions must be handled explicitly. Restricting a finite coefficient group to odd order removes this particular two-torsion example, but is not a verified complete repair of the published general statements. The condition for a pole must include nonvanishing of the actual residue after the final operator is applied. A full replacement classification, especially for even moduli, is outside the completed repair here.

Problem 2 (Missing exponential weights in uniform spectral estimates).  Proposition 7.4(1), p. 3568 (physical 42), and Lemma 8.4(1), p. 3573 (physical 47), state bounds uniform in the iterate \(n\) without necessary weight factors.

First exact counterexample. Again take \(\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), now \(g=b\), \(\sigma=1\), and the constant real cost \(\eta=\varepsilon>0\). Choose \(\varepsilon\) arbitrarily small so that \((1,\varepsilon)\) lies in the claimed neighborhood \(B\). Then \[\mathcal H_{1,\varepsilon}=e^\varepsilon\mathcal H_{1,0},\qquad
 \Phi(x)=\frac1{\log2\,(1+x)},\qquad
 \mathcal H_{1,0}\Phi=\Phi.\] The last identity follows directly by telescoping the branch sum: \[\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{1}{(m+x)^2}
       \frac1{\log2\,(1+(m+x)^{-1})}
 =\frac1{\log2}\sum_{m\ge1}
       \frac1{(m+x)(m+x+1)}=\Phi(x).\] Thus \[\|\partial\mathcal H_{1,\varepsilon}^{\,n}\Phi\|_\infty
 =\frac{e^{n\varepsilon}}{\log2}.\] Proposition 7.4(1) bounds this by \(C_B(1+\rho^n)/\log2\), with \(\rho=1/2\). No fixed \(C_B\) can do so for all \(n\). All functions are in the stipulated \(C^1\) space.

Second exact counterexample. Scalar multiplication changes the dominant eigenvalue to \(\lambda_{1,\varepsilon}=e^\varepsilon\), but leaves the normalized invariant measure \(\nu_{1,\varepsilon}=\nu_{1,0}\) unchanged. This is the probability measure with density \(\Phi(x)\). For any Gauss inverse branch \(p\) of depth \(n\), bounded distortion and the positive upper and lower bounds of \(\Phi\) give constants independent of \(n,p\) such that \[\|p'\|_\infty\asymp |p(I)|
       \asymp \nu_{1,0}(p(I)).\] This can also be checked directly from \(|p'(x)|=(q_n+q_{n-1}x)^{-2}\), where \(0\le q_{n-1}/q_n\le1\); its distortion is at most four. Consequently \[\frac{\|p'\|_\infty/\lambda_{1,\varepsilon}^{\,n}}
      {\nu_{1,\varepsilon}(p(I))}
 \asymp e^{-n\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{2.2}\] This disproves the two-sided uniform comparison in Lemma 8.4(1).

Dependency and impact. Proposition 7.4 is used in the spectral-radius argument of Proposition 7.5. More seriously, Lemma 8.4(1) is explicitly used in the estimate for the close-pair contribution \(I^{(1)}\), immediately below (8.4), which feeds Proposition 8.3 and Theorem 8.5. The weight omission therefore invalidates a quantitative step of the central vertical-strip proof as printed. This problem is Major on that proof impact. It is not an assertion that the Dolgopyat-type conclusion is false.

Verified local repair and remaining work. Direct differentiation and bounded distortion yield the defensible bound \[\|\partial\mathcal H_{\sigma,\eta}^{\,n}\Psi\|_\infty
 \le C\,\|\mathcal H_{\sigma,\eta}^{\,n}1\|_\infty
 \bigl(|\sigma|\|\Psi\|_\infty+
              \rho^n\|\partial\Psi\|_\infty\bigr),
 \tag{2.3}\] because all real branch weights are positive. In the constant-cost example it retains exactly the missing \(e^{n\varepsilon}\). Likewise (2.2) is repaired in that example by inserting the cylinder weight \(e^{n\varepsilon}\) in the numerator. For nonconstant costs the corresponding weight depends on the branch and coset. Replacing the stated comparisons and propagating these weights through the full close-pair estimate has not been completed here. The normalized operator of (8.1), p. 3571, is relevant counterevidence to a claim that all later estimates must be false, but it does not make the two displayed unnormalized statements true.

Problem 3 (The unweighted counting constant contains an extra factor).  Equation (3.3), p. 3546 (physical 20), states the leading constant \(3|J|/(\pi^2\log2)\) for \(|\Omega_{M,J}|\). For \(J=(0,1)\) the constant is \(3/\pi^2\).

Independent derivation. The definition of the sample space gives exactly \(|\Omega_M|=\sum_{n=2}^M\varphi(n)\). Counting coprime ordered pairs by Möbius inversion gives \[\sum_{n=1}^M\varphi(n)
 =\frac12\left(1+\sum_{d=1}^M
               \mu(d)\lfloor M/d\rfloor^2\right)
 =\frac{3}{\pi^2}M^2+O(M\log M).\] Here \(\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/d^2=1/\zeta(2)=6/\pi^2\); the replacement of floors costs \(O(M\sum_{d\le M}d^{-1})\), and the tail costs \(O(M)\). Removing \(n=1\) affects only a constant. Thus the error in (3.3) has nonzero order \(M^2\), while the preceding Proposition 3.4 requires an error \(O(M^\delta)\) with \(\delta<2\). The paper itself uses the correct totient constant on p. 3555 (physical 29).

Impact and repair. This is Minor on the demonstrated scope: the raw counting and residue normalization must be corrected, including the calculation on p. 3579. A common multiplicative normalization cancels from the ratios forming a moment generating function, so this discrepancy alone does not disprove the normalized Gaussian or residual laws. An independent sieve calculation is retained as a finite diagnostic; the asymptotic derivation above is the decisive evidence.

Problem 4 (The variance identity needs a centered parameter direction).  Section 9 introduces an arbitrary fixed real \(h\), p. 3574 (physical 48). Proposition 9.2, p. 3575 (physical 49), then states an uncentered second-moment limit and its proof assumes \(\kappa'(0)=0\) without imposing it.

Exact counterexample and correction. Use the one-coset \(b\)-system, \(\psi=1\), and \(h=0\). Then \(\kappa(w)=\lambda_{1,w}=e^w\), so \(\kappa'(0)=\kappa''(0)=1\), and the cost \(\Upsilon=2h\log x+\psi\) is identically one. The right-hand side of Proposition 9.2 is \[\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
   \int\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1\right)^2\Phi\,dx
 =\lim_{n\to\infty}n=+\infty,\] whereas its left-hand side equals one. The hypothesis needed by the proof is \(\kappa'(0)=0\), equivalently zero mean for the cost in this normalized setting. One must restrict \(h\) to the corresponding centered direction, or use a correctly centered variance and logarithmic eigenvalue derivative.

The downstream use in (10.1)–(10.2), p. 3578 (physical 52), does select \(h=s'(0)\) from \(\lambda(s(w),w)=1\); differentiation then gives precisely \(\kappa'(0)=0\). This verified compatibility limits the demonstrated impact to Minor: the overbroad proposition is false, but this example does not invalidate its intended centered use in the Hessian argument.

# Assessment, coverage and limits

The overall assessment is Major, based on Problems 1 and 2. Problem 1 disproves an actual characteristic-function limit and pole assertion under all their stated assumptions. Problem 2 disproves uniform estimates at arbitrarily small admissible real weights and identifies the core estimate that uses them. Neither assessment requires the entire paper to be false, and possible repair is not used as a reason to lower the grade.

The continued-fraction conventions, finite-index and normalization assumptions, interval weighting, nondegeneracy requirements, and the distinction between \(b\)- and \(c\)-costs were tracked. The operator-series representation and terminal parity were checked in the explicit one-coset example. The totient asymptotic, scalar operator identities, and centering counterexample are independently derived above. The complex perturbation, full UNI/Dolgopyat argument, and modular-form cohomology/period results depend on cited work and were not independently reconstructed in full.

In particular, the modular-symbol specializations, all even-prime arithmetic cases, and the optimal-period identifications are not certified by this review. No elliptic-curve counterexample is claimed. The report does not infer that the unrestricted arXiv version contains the same issues. A bounded official-page and author-page search did not locate a separate target erratum, supplementary file or released implementation; this is a search limitation. No author code was run.
