# Definitions, assumptions, and checked correspondence

A den has a positive integer \(h\), an irrational real slope \(p\), and integer sequences \(d,e\) indexed by \(0,\ldots,h\). The endpoint inequalities, balance \(\sum d_i=\sum e_i\), and the two strict slope inequalities (21)–(23) are essential. The general den theorem does not assume \(p>0\). The positive coprime parameters \(m,n\) belong to the Loehr–Warrington specialization, not to every den. The path counts are \[g_k=\sum_{i<k}(d_i-e_i),\qquad 1\leq k\leq h.\] Sources are ordered from left to right and then bottom to top, and sinks from right to left and then bottom to top. Paths use south and east steps, end with an east step, and obey head bounds at non-sink vertices. Nested paths may share south steps; replacing nesting by vertex-disjointness would change the theorem.

Combining (21) and (22) gives \(d_j-e_j\leq d_i-e_i+1\) for \(0<i<j<h\). Thus a negative interior difference cannot precede a positive one. Together with the endpoint conditions this places sources before sinks and gives the required positive, unimodal \(g\). In Lemma 3.2.1 (pp. 12–13), if \(y_{k,i}\) are increasing east-step heights, \[(\lambda_{(k)})_i=e_k-g_k+i-y_{k,i}.\] The head bounds, strict nesting, and starting/ending intervals make these partitions of length at most \(\min(g_k,g_{k+1})\). The converse recovers the paths: inequality (30) is precisely the compatibility of the two successive east-step lists. The area is \(|\lambda|\). Empty intervals and paths that end at the current column must be retained when checking this correspondence.

The vertical-run construction in Section 3.4 gives skew rows and adjusted contents, including the strict inequality in the positive-label condition between adjacent paths. Its attack condition is \(0<\widetilde c(b)-\widetilde c(a)<1\). The choice of the reference line changes the presentation but not the resulting LLT expression. Empty rows matter later: the triples in Section 5.5 depend on the row presentation and its boundaries, not just the set of occupied boxes.

# Checks of the proof chain

## Polynomial part and LLT series

For Theorem 5.5.4 (pp. 39–41), the dominant regular weights and coordinatewise condition \(\alpha\leq\beta\) cannot be omitted. In Lemma 5.5.5 a nonidentity permutation of a strictly dominant \(\alpha\) has an ascent. Subtracting it from strictly dominant \(\beta\) then forces an adjacent difference at least two, incompatible with a vector of zeros and ones. The vertical-strip chain that remains gives the negative tableaux used in the proof. The inversion increments are between blocks and correspond to the specified triples, with the endpoint conventions for empty rows. This supports the polynomial-part formula used in Section 7.2; it is not a test of arbitrary weights outside its hypotheses.

## Cauchy identity and specialization

Theorem 6.1.3 requires the same block maxima, the prescribed almost-monotone block minima, and compatible artificial extrema for zero-length blocks. These conditions were tracked through Lemmas 6.1.5–6.1.16 (pp. 42–48). In the specialization proof, if the omitted coordinate is strictly above its minimal bound, the partition has full block length and vanishes on setting the variable to zero. At the bound, deleting the coordinate preserves the relevant permutation sign. This verifies the two cases in (166), including the reason that zero-length blocks are allowed.

For equal compositions, the dual-basis coefficient in (168) reduces to the reproducing identity (169). Taking the block constant term in the first alphabet uses ordinary Cauchy reproduction; after substitution, the remaining factors with that block cancel. Iterating gives the stated result. For unequal compositions, the common enlargement \(r'_i=M_i-N+1\), with \(N\) below all minima, has no empty block. Specialization then uses the preceding lemma. These operations are coefficientwise in \(t\), where only finitely many partitions contribute at each degree.

## Winding permutations and the final matching

The operative signs in (179)–(181) agree with the Hecke normalization (77), and with the two possibilities for the difference of winding descent indicators. Intermediate comparison-word typos do not alter the correctly displayed operator identities or their applicable signs. They are not recorded as independent scientific findings.

In Theorem 7.1.1, the choices \(r=(0,\gamma_h,\ldots,\gamma_1)\) and \(s=(\gamma_h,\ldots,\gamma_1,0)\) meet the Cauchy hypotheses: the symbol \(\infty\) in (193) means a sufficiently large finite artificial minimum. The balance \(\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_i=u_i-v_{i+1}\) matches the block maxima. The winding identity then supplies (199).

For the final application (pp. 55–56), write \[f_i=\lfloor s-pi\rfloor,\quad
 u_i=f_i-e_i,\quad v_i=f_{i-1}-d_{i-1},\quad
 b_i=f_{i-1}-f_i.\] The almost-monotonicity inequalities (187) reduce exactly to the two den slope inequalities. Taking \(\gamma=g\) gives \(u_i-v_i+b_i=d_{i-1}-e_i\), the required Catalanimal weight. The condition \(\alpha\leq\beta\) in (203)–(204) becomes (30); the surviving terms therefore are exactly the nests. The row identifications in (205) match the previously defined \(\nu(\pi)\), while \(|\lambda|=a(\pi)\). Finally, the line-crossing condition \[0<c(b)-c(a)+c_i-c_j<1\] has precisely the two integer-content cases distinguished by \(c_i<c_j\) and \(c_i>c_j\). These are the triples counted in the LLT formula, so their number is \(\operatorname{dinv}_p(\pi)\). This checks the stated final statistic matching, including non-sink points on zero-length vertical runs.

The appearance of \(q^{-1}\) in the LLT argument alone would not prove nonnegative exponents. Here the formula first gives nonnegative \(t\) exponents; the \(q,t\) symmetry of this Catalanimal and the nonnegative combinatorial coefficients rule out negative \(q\) exponents. No contradictory boundary case was established in these checks.

# Independent exact finite checks

A reviewer-written Python program, saved with this report, enumerates south/east words directly. It does not use the partition parametrization to generate nests and does not import or execute author code. It imposes the source/sink order, last-east-step rule, head bounds, nesting inequalities, and positive-label conditions. Separate semistandard-tableau enumeration evaluates the Schur side.

For Example 3.5.5, it uses \[d=(3,2,2,1,-1),\quad e=(1,2,2,1,1),\quad
 p=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{100}.\] All 153 tested slope comparisons are certified by rational interval bounds \(51/100<p<52/100\), with no floating-point classification. Exactly six nests occur. Labels range from 1 to 4, enough to test the degree-four symmetric functions. \[\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
a&0&1&2&2&3&4\\
\operatorname{dinv}&5&3&2&3&2&0\\
\text{positive labelings}&36&96&80&36&80&20
\end{array}\] Each term agrees with (47). Their sum agrees with (46): \[\begin{aligned}
 &(q^3t+q^2t^2+qt^3)s_{31}\\
 &+(q^4+q^3t+2q^2t^2+qt^3+t^4)s_{22}\\
 &+(q^3+2q^2t+2qt^2+t^3)s_{211}
 +(q^2+qt+t^2)s_{1111}.\end{aligned}\] The result has 348 labeled configurations and 186 distinct keys \((\deg_q,\deg_t,z\text{-monomial})\); the latter is what the output field `distinct_q_t_monomials` counts. The calculation also checks \(q,t\) symmetry and nonnegative exponents. It does not independently expand the full eight-variable raising operator; it compares the directly enumerated LLT sum with the printed Schur expansion.

For \(d=(2,1,-1),e=(1,1,0)\), two nests give \(s_{11}+(q+t)s_2\). Analytically this also follows from \[\left.\sigma\left(
 \frac{z_1z_2}{(1-qz_1/z_2)(1-tz_1/z_2)}
 \right)\right|_{\operatorname{pol}}:\] only raising powers zero and one have polynomial highest weights. For \(h=1\), \(d=(2,-1),e=(0,1)\) has one nest and gives \(s_{11}\). The valid but abandoned den \(d=(0,2),e=(-1,3)\) has no path from its source to its higher sink, and its one-variable \(z^{-3}\) has zero polynomial part. Thus the empty-nest case is consistent, rather than an assumed existence condition.

There are also 75 exact checks of the Hecke quadratic/inverse identities on Laurent monomials of exponents \(-2,\ldots,2\), and 18 checks of \(T y T=q x\) and \(T^{-1}xT^{-1}=q^{-1}y\) on exponents \(-1,0,1\). All passed, with process exit code zero. These finite checks support the normalization and inspected examples; they are not a proof for arbitrary rank.

The present review checked its location and attribution, not every case of the original paper independently.
