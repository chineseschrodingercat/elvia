Abstract: The regularization argument controls incidence without establishing the directed extension property required of split paths. An explicit configuration for the multiplicative function \(g\equiv1\) satisfies all the properties listed in Lemma 3.4, including strong separation and a positive density of admissible prime pairs, but admits no split path of the length required in Lemma 5.4. This exposes a Major gap in the central proof chain. It is not a counterexample to Theorem 1.1. A separate exact calculation disproves the pointwise inequality (2.1); the uniform pyramid estimate can instead be obtained by propagating allocated error bounds. The review also records successful conditional checks and its version and external-dependency limits. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Major: incidence regularity does not provide long split paths

## Exact location and logical distinction

Equation reference: step. On p. 6, Definition 3.6 specifies the oriented physical relation \[
 x_{i+1}=\frac{q_i}{p_i}x_i+O(H/K).\] A path is split only when every \(p_i\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_1\) and every \(q_i\) belongs to \(\mathcal P_2\). These sets are disjoint and are fixed in Notation 3.5 on p. 5. Reversing a path, as in Notation 3.7, interchanges the two prime roles. A reversed split path is generally not split.

Definition 3.8 and the deletion argument in Lemma 3.9, pp. 6–7, use connection to other vertices. The deletion estimate subtracting twice the small degree is an incidence-degree argument. In the first paragraph of the proof of Lemma 5.4, p. 10, this is used to infer many split paths of length \(k_0+2\) with a prescribed initial vertex. Incidence degree alone does not imply this directed extension property.

The following construction verifies the obstruction within the paper’s actual numerical and multiplicative-function assumptions. It is not merely an abstract directed-graph example.

## An admissible configuration

Equation reference: scales. Fix \(\eta=1/2\) and \(0<\rho<1\). Choose \(0<\delta<1/10\) and then a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) as small as required in Lemmas 3.4 and 5.4, also with \(\varepsilon<1/100\). For arbitrarily large \(X\), set \[
 H=X^{1/2},\qquad P=H^{\varepsilon/3},\qquad
 P'=H^{\varepsilon/4},\qquad K=PP'=H^{7\varepsilon/12},
 \qquad R=H/K.\] Thus \(K\in[H^{\varepsilon^2},H^\varepsilon]\) and \(P,P'\ge H^{\varepsilon^2}\). The intervals \([P',2P']\) and \([P,2P]\) are disjoint for sufficiently large \(X\). The lower bound on \(H\) in Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 5.4 holds for every fixed required \(C\) once \(X\) is large enough.

Let \(g(n)=1\) for every positive integer \(n\). This is a 1-bounded multiplicative function, and (1.1) holds by taking \(\alpha=0\). Put \(S=500\) and define \[\mathcal J=\left\{(SRj,0):
    j\in\mathbb Z,\quad \frac{X}{10K}\le SRj\le\frac{2X}{K}\right\}.\] The first coordinates are \(500R\)-separated, with \(|\mathcal J|\asymp X/H\). For any sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\), this is a \((c_0,R)\)-configuration in the interval required in Lemma 3.4. Every associated length-\(R\) sum of \(g(n)e(0n)\) has size \(R+O(1)\), so the local correlation requirement holds.

Equation reference: classes. Take the prime classes \[
 \mathcal P_1=\{p\text{ prime}:P\le p\le 11P/10\},\qquad
 \mathcal P_2=\{q\text{ prime}:19P/10\le q\le2P\}.\] The prime number theorem gives \(|\mathcal P_1|,|\mathcal P_2|\asymp P/\log P\). They are disjoint, as required.

For each \(p\in\mathcal P_1\), \(q\in\mathcal P_2\), consider integers \[\frac{X}{10SH}\le j\le\frac{X}{2SH}.\] There are \(\asymp X/H\) such \(j\), and their number divided by \(p\) tends to infinity. In a complete block of \(p\) consecutive integers, the residues \(qj\bmod p\) run through all residues because \(p\ne q\). A fixed positive proportion therefore satisfy \[\operatorname{dist}(qj/p,\mathbb Z)\le 1/100.\] Consequently, \(\gg X/H\) of the indicated \(j\) have this property, uniformly in \(p,q\). Choose the nearest integer \(\ell\) to \(qj/p\), and set \(x=SRj\), \(y=SR\ell\). Both vertices lie in the displayed interval defining \(\mathcal J\) for large \(X\), and \[|y-(q/p)x|\le5R,\qquad
 |x/p-y/q|\le\frac{5R}{q}\le\frac{5H}{PK}.\] All phases are zero, so \(\|p\alpha_x-q\alpha_y\|_{p'}=0\) for every prime \(p'\in[P',2P']\). Hence this construction supplies \[\gg \frac XH\left(\frac P{\log P}\right)^2\] admissible ordered quadruples, each valid for \(\gg P'/\log P'\) auxiliary primes. It satisfies every property stated in Lemma 3.4, including its final restriction to \(\mathcal P_1\times\mathcal P_2\). It even retains the stronger separation used in the cited construction. For each edge the zero phases and zero intermediate phase give a pre-path with any positive permitted error bounds. Auxiliary prime products do not share primes with the path parameters.

## No required split path exists

Let \(M\) be any fixed constant implicit in the error in [step] (#step). Every split step for these data obeys \[x_{i+1}\ge \frac{19}{11}x_i-MR.\] Since \(x_i\ge X/(10K)\), \[\frac{MR}{x_i}\le 10M\frac HX\longrightarrow0.\] For large \(X\), every split step therefore satisfies \(x_{i+1}\ge(3/2)x_i\). Yet the ratio of the largest allowed first coordinate to the smallest is at most \(20\), whereas \[(3/2)^8=\frac{6561}{256}>20.\] Thus there is no split path of length \(8\) or greater, for any modulus. The requirement that all path primes be distinct can only reduce this set of paths.

On the other hand, for the paper’s fixed constant \(B\), \[\frac{\log(X/(HB\log X))}{2\log(2P)}
       \longrightarrow \frac{3}{2\varepsilon}.\] Our fixed small \(\varepsilon\) makes \(k_0+2>7\) for all sufficiently large \(X\). No vertex pair can have even one of the split paths demanded by Lemma 5.4, much less two with disjoint prime sets. Its lower bound \(|\mathcal J_0|\ge X/H^{1+\delta}=H^{1-\delta}\) is positive and tends to infinity. The long-path conclusion is therefore false for data allowed by all the stated configuration properties.

If connected in Definition 3.8 is read as an outgoing connection, then Lemma 3.9 already fails for these data: the largest-coordinate vertex of any nonempty finite subset has no outgoing split edge. If connection means incidence in either direction, the deletion argument can produce an undirected regular core, but that core supplies no such directed paths. Neither reading justifies the inference on p. 10.

## Impact and repair boundary

Lemma 5.4 is the input to Proposition 6.1, pp. 11–12, and to the global-frequency selection on p. 13. The missing directed extension property affects the central proof of Theorems 3.2 and 1.1; the assessment is Major. The construction does not contradict Theorem 1.1: \(g\equiv1\) is itself pretentious. Nor does it disprove the existence assertion of Lemma 3.4, or rule out a better choice of prime classes. It shows that the properties explicitly retained in Notation 3.5 are insufficient for the subsequent long-path assertion and its proof.

A repair must establish an appropriate directed minimum-degree condition for a suitable choice of data, or change the path scheme and redo the counting argument. Merely reversing edges is insufficient: the fixed prime roles are used in Corollary 5.3, p. 10, to place a shared prime in both products and factor it out. Allowing arbitrary changes of orientation requires a corresponding replacement for that counting step. No complete replacement is claimed in this review.

# Minor: the pointwise inequality (2.1) is false

Equation reference: printed. On p. 4, the proof of Lemma 2.4 asserts \[
 \|q_k\alpha_1^{(k+1)}-\alpha_1^{(k)}\|_Q
 \le \frac{\|p_1\alpha_1^{(k)}-\alpha_2^{(k-1)}\|_Q}{p_1}.\] Lemma 2.2 controls an allocated upper bound for a preceding residual. It does not give this inequality in terms of the actual preceding residual.

Here is an exact example, including the weighted interpolation prescribed in the proof of Lemma 2.1. Set \[k=2,\quad Q=101,\quad \epsilon=\epsilon_i=\epsilon_i'=1/10,\qquad
 (p_1,p_2)=(2,5),\quad(q_1,q_2)=(3,7),\] and take \[(\alpha_1^{(1)},\alpha_2^{(1)},\alpha_3^{(1)})
      =(0,0,1/140),\qquad
 (\alpha_2^{(0)},\alpha_3^{(0)})=(0,1/40).\] All four primes are distinct and none divides \(Q\). The two base residuals at the first edge are zero; those at the second edge are both \(1/40\), strictly less than \(\epsilon\). Equal allocated errors in the first lift give \[(\alpha_1^{(2)},\alpha_2^{(2)})=(0,1/1400).\] For the next lift, the allocated \(q_2\)- and \(p_1\)-side errors are \(\epsilon/p_2\) and \(\epsilon/q_1\). The same weighted interpolation gives \[\alpha_1^{(3)}
 =\frac{\epsilon/q_1}{\epsilon/q_1+\epsilon/p_2}
       \frac{\alpha_2^{(2)}}{p_1}
 =\frac1{4480}.\] The roots used here are the closest representatives, as required in that construction. All the strict output bounds of Lemma 2.2 hold. Nevertheless, the two sides of [printed] (#printed) are \[\left\|7\cdot\frac1{4480}-0\right\|_{101}
       =\frac1{640}>0
       =\frac{\|2\cdot0-0\|_{101}}2.\] This is a counterexample to the printed intermediate inequality, not to the uniform conclusion of Lemma 2.4: its bound for this edge is \(1/60\).

## A direct correction

Let \(E^p_{i,j}\) and \(E^q_{i,j}\) denote allocated bounds for the edges from \(\alpha_i^{(j+1)}\) to \(\alpha_{i+1}^{(j)}\) with multiplier \(p_i\), and to \(\alpha_i^{(j)}\) with multiplier \(q_{i+j-1}\), respectively. Propagating the bounds in Lemma 2.2 gives \[E^p_{i,1}=\epsilon/q_i,\quad E^q_{i,1}=\epsilon/p_i,\qquad
 E^p_{i,j}=\frac{E^q_{i+1,j-1}}{q_{i+j-1}},\quad
 E^q_{i,j}=\frac{E^p_{i,j-1}}{p_i}.\] Alternating these recurrences down to the first row gives \[E^q_{1,j}=
 \epsilon\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor(j-1)/2\rfloor+1}p_i\right)^{-1}
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lceil(j-1)/2\rceil}q_{j-i}\right)^{-1},\] which is precisely the asserted uniform bound. Thus one can replace [printed] (#printed) by a proof using the allocated bounds without changing Lemma 2.4 or the estimates in Section 4. This established preservation of the result, rather than the brevity of the correction alone, supports the Minor assessment of this separate issue.

# Other checks, evidence, and limitations

The following parts of the argument were checked without an additional confirmed core defect.

- The closest-root interpolation in Lemma 2.1 and the strict hypotheses in the preceding counterexample were checked exactly. The allocated-bound recurrence was derived algebraically and verified with rational arithmetic for lengths \(1\) through \(12\).

- The physical product estimates in Lemma 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 give bounded products along an existing path and displacement \(O(kH/K)\). Together with the corrected uniform pyramid bound, the telescoping estimates in Lemmas 4.3 and 4.4 have the stated scale. They do not create paths.

- The separation of unequal rational prime products in Lemma 5.1 and the factorial multiplicity in Lemma 5.2 were checked. The cited construction supplies stronger interval separation than the abbreviated configuration definition alone; no separate uniqueness defect is charged on that basis.

- Conditional on the required paths, the losses in Lemma 5.4 have logarithmic size \(O((L/h)\log L)\), with \(L=\log X\) and \(h=\log H\), apart from small powers of \(H\). The condition \(h^2\ge C^2L\log L\), after the stipulated choices of constants, has the appropriate scale to absorb them. This check does not resolve the directional obstruction.

- In Proposition 6.1, the two disjoint prime sets make the integer difference of products nonzero. The estimates for its small denominator, for \(T_y\), and for the rational comparison modulo a large common divisor were followed. The final interval shortening and Taylor approximation were checked subject to the missing path input. On p. 13 the final two correlation displays should retain the absolute values used in their hypotheses; they are otherwise comparisons of possibly complex sums.

The original calculation script uses exact fractions and independently generated primes. It checked the full-period residue count for \(208\) pairs from the separated prime intervals \([1000,1100]\) and \([1900,2000]\), as well as every hypothesis and residual in the counterexample to (2.1). Its exit code was zero. These finite computations support the exact identities and modular argument; the proof for all sufficiently large \(X\) is the analytic construction above, not an unverified extrapolation from a finite graph.

# Assessment

Major, because the directed long-path step in the main proof is not established by the stated assumptions and fails for an explicitly admissible configuration. The separate error in (2.1) has a verified local repair preserving the uniform estimate. Theorem 1.1 itself has not been disproved.
