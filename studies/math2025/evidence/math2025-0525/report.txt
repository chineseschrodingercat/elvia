Abstract: The transport application, with its printed coupling to the driving Brownian motion, has a zero-drift counterexample. A uniform localization lemma in the appendix is false under its stated assumptions. The principal strong-error proof also loses a nonzero diffusion-error forcing term and invokes diffusion regularity stronger than its formal hypothesis. These are distinct from a disproof of the main SDE convergence theorem. Further normalization, homogeneity, and change-of-measure errors admit explicit local repairs. The review concerns the frozen Accepted Version and compares relevant passages with arXiv version 6.

Author: AI-assisted technical review

Title: Technical Review of Taming Singular Stochastic Differential Equations: A Numerical Method

# Identity, hypotheses, and scope

The reviewed paper is Khoa Lê and Chengcheng Ling, Taming singular stochastic differential equations: A numerical method, DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1750. The frozen source is the White Rose Accepted Version, record 222738 [paper]: 62 PDF pages comprising a cover and printed pages 1–61. In this report, p. means a printed page; its PDF page is one greater. The complete source, proofs, appendix, and references were read.

Write \(L^q_tL^p_x=L^q([0,1];L^p(\mathbb R^d))\). The main assumptions include \(p,q\in[2,\infty)\), \(d/p+2/q<1\), bounded uniformly elliptic \(a=\sigma\sigma^*\), spatial \(\alpha\)-Hölder continuity of \(a\), and \[\nabla\sigma\in L^{q_0}_tL^{p_0}_x,\qquad
p_0\geq2,\quad q_0>2,\quad d/p_0+2/q_0<1.\] The approximations \(b^n\) have uniformly bounded \(L^q_tL^p_x\) norms and satisfy Condition B’s local \(L^q_tL^\infty_x\) control. The scheme freezes space at \(k_n(t)=\lfloor nt\rfloor/n\), but does not freeze the time argument. Theorem 2.2 claims an error bounded by the initial error, \(n^{-\alpha/2}+n^{-1/2}\log n\), and the transformed drift discrepancy \(\varpi_n(\bar p)\), for \[1<\bar p<p,\qquad \bar p<2(p\wedge p_0)/d,\qquad 0<\gamma<1,\] in \(L^{\gamma\bar p}\). Theorem 2.3 supplies stronger bounds for that discrepancy, with its additional restrictions on \(q_0,p_0,\nu\) and, in part (iii), \(d/p+4/q<1\). None of these strict inequalities is replaced by an endpoint assertion here.

The official arXiv record describes v6 as an update to the error formula. Its acquired PDF has 70 pages, an arXiv date of 31 January 2025 and a body date of 3 February 2025 [v6]. Relevant statements and proofs were compared directly; the two PDFs were not declared textually identical. In particular, the passages underlying Problems 1–6 below remain in the corresponding v6 passages. The journal typeset version was not acquired for comparison, and no claim is made that every version shares these defects. The bounded metadata search identified no separate formal correction resolving them; that is not proof that no correction exists.

# The transport application

Problem 1 (Major: the printed scheme in Theorem 8.1 does not converge to the stated solution).  Equations (8.3)–(8.5), pp. 47–48, change the sign of the Brownian increment while retaining the same driving path. Consequently the strong estimate (8.6) is false for the printed scheme.

Proof. The definition \(X_s^\tau=X_{\tau,\tau-s}\) and (8.3) give \[X_s^\tau=x-\int_0^s b(\tau-r,X_r^\tau)\,dr+W_{\tau-s}-W_\tau.\] The paper instead uses a plus sign before \(W_s^\tau=W_\tau-W_{\tau-s}\), in both (8.4) and the numerical scheme (8.5).

Equation reference: eq:transport. Take \(d=1\), \(b=b^n=0\), \(p=q=p_1=q_1=4\), \(\nu=0\), \(l=2\), and \(\bar r=8\). Then all restrictions in Theorem 8.1 hold: \(1/4+2/4<1\), \(\nu<7/8\), and \(1/8<1/2-1/4\). Zero drift satisfies Condition B with, for example, \(\mu_n(s,t)=t-s\) and any fixed positive \(\theta\). Set \(\rho(x)=e^{-(x-1)^2}\), which belongs to every required \(W^{1,r}\) space. The Stratonovich chain rule gives the exact solution \(u(t,x)=\rho(x-W_t)\). The printed scheme gives \(u^n(t,x)=\rho(x+W_t)\) for every \(n\). For \(Z=W_1\sim N(0,1)\), direct Gaussian integration yields \[
\mathbb E|\rho(Z)-\rho(-Z)|^2
=\frac{2}{\sqrt5}\left(e^{-2/5}-e^{-2}\right)>0.\] The corresponding \(L^2\) error is approximately \(0.6917405\). At \((t,x)=(1,0)\), the left side of (8.6) is therefore bounded away from zero, while its right side tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\). ◻

Counterevidence and repair. Both signs of a Brownian motion have the same marginal law. This does not repair an estimate of the difference of two solutions coupled to the same \(W\). Defining the reversed driver as \(W_{\tau-s}-W_\tau\), with the corresponding change to its extension (8.7), repairs this elementary sign obstruction. The issue concerns the stated transport algorithm and Theorem 8.1; it does not contradict the earlier SDE rate. The same signs occur on v6 pp. 54–55.

# Uniform localization in the appendix

Problem 2 (Major: Lemma A.8 requires uniform local integrability not supplied by Condition A’).  Lemma A.8, (A.19), p. 52, asserts that \[\sup_{t,z}\|(a-a(z))\psi_\rho^z\|_{W^{1,p_0}}\longrightarrow0
\quad(\rho\downarrow0)\] from the bound \(\sup_t\|\nabla a(t)\|_{p_0}<\infty\). This assertion is false.

Equation reference: eq:concentration. Proof. Use \(d=1\), \(p_0=4\), \(p=2\), \(q=4\), and zero forcing terms, so that \(p_0>d\) and \(1/p+1/p_0<1\). Let \(r_j=2^{-j}\) and \(\phi(u)=(1-u^2)^2\) on \([-1,1]\), zero outside. This compactly supported \(C^1\) function is sufficient for the weak differentiability required in Condition A’. On the disjoint intervals \(I_j=(2^{-j-1},2^{-j}]\), set \[
a(t,x)=2+r_j^{3/4}\phi(x/r_j),\qquad t\in I_j,\] and set \(a=2\) at all remaining times. Then \(2\leq a\leq3\), the coefficient is measurable, and \[\|\partial_xa(t,\cdot)\|_4^4
=r_j^{4(3/4-1)+1}\int_{-1}^1|\phi'(u)|^4\,du
=\frac{65536}{15015}>0.\] Thus its gradient norm is uniformly bounded in time, exactly as required.

Choose a smooth cutoff \(\psi_1\) supported in \([-1,1]\) and identically one on \([-1/2,1/2]\). For \(\rho_j=4r_j\), \(z=0\), and \(t\in I_j\), the cutoff \(\psi_{\rho_j}^0\) is identically one on the support of the bump. The average \(a(0)\) in the paper is spatially constant. Consequently \[\|\partial_x[(a-a(0))\psi_{\rho_j}^0]\|_4
\geq \|\partial_xa\|_{L^4([-r_j,r_j])}
=\|\phi'\|_4>0.\] The asserted limit fails, also with essential supremum in time because every \(I_j\) has positive measure. Equivalence of Bessel and classical first-order Sobolev norms gives the same contradiction in the paper’s notation. ◻

Dependency and impact. The false smallness is used in Lemma A.9, Step 1, p. 53, and absorbed in Step 2, p. 54. Lemma A.10 repeats that argument; Theorems A.5 and A.11 then feed Proposition 6.4 and the weak-topology estimates of Theorem 2.3. This is an invalid central derivation, not a demonstrated counterexample to those final PDE or SDE estimates.

Counterevidence and repair boundary. For a single time-independent gradient in \(L^{p_0}\), absolute continuity of its integral does give the needed local smallness uniformly over spatial centers. The missing point is uniformity over the time-indexed family. A uniform local equiintegrability assumption, such as \(\sup_{t,z}\int_{B_\rho(z)}|\nabla a(t,x)|^{p_0}\,dx\to0\), would support this step. Alternatively the localization proof must be replaced. Neither repair follows from the stated norm bound alone. The same assertion and inference occur as Lemma .8 on v6 pp. 59–60.

# The principal strong-error derivation

Problem 3 (Major: the diffusion forcing is lost in Proposition 7.3).  The proof on pp. 42–44 contains a false intermediate conclusion (7.7), as well as a use of \(\alpha\)-Hölder regularity of \(\sigma\) which is not implied by Condition A1.

Decisive check of (7.7). In the paper, \(V=|x_0-x_0^n|^{\bar p}+V^1+V^2+V^3\). Take \(d=1\), \(b=b^n=0\), \(x_0=x_0^n=0\), and \[\sigma(x)=1+\tfrac14\tanh x.\] This coefficient is smooth, bounded, uniformly elliptic and Lipschitz; its derivative belongs to every finite \(L^{p_0}\). Take \(p=q=p_0=4\), \(q_0=\infty\), \(\bar p=2\), and \(\alpha=1\). The assumptions hold. The PDE solution \(U\) is zero, so every term in the displayed \(V\) is zero.

For \(n=1\), the Euler process is \(X_t^1=B_t\), whereas the exact solution satisfies \(dX_t=\sigma(X_t)dB_t\). These paths cannot agree almost surely throughout \([0,1]\): equality would imply, by Itô isometry, \(\mathbb E\int_0^1(\sigma(B_s)-1)^2ds=0\), which is false. Thus \(\mathbb E\sup_t|X_t-X_t^1|^2>0\). The weight in (7.7) is strictly positive and bounded away from zero here, since \(\nabla\sigma\) and its maximal function are bounded. The claimed bound \[\mathbb Ee^{-c_{\bar p}|A_1^n|^{\max(\bar p/2,1)}}\xi_1\leq \mathbb EV_1=0\] is impossible. ◻

The preceding estimate of \(I^2\) on p. 44 replaces a random increment integral by a deterministic \(n^{-\bar p\alpha/2}\) using Lemma 7.2, which is only a moment estimate. Even for \(X^n=B\), \(\int_0^{1/n}|B_r|^2dr\) is not deterministically bounded by a fixed multiple of \(n^{-1}\). A random nondecreasing error term must remain in \(V\), with its expectation bounded at the required rate; the paper’s definition of \(V\) omits it.

There is also a separate hypothesis mismatch at that step. In \(d=2\), let \(\vartheta(x)=\chi(x)|x|^{3/4}\), with a smooth cutoff equal to one near zero, and let \(\sigma(x)\) be planar rotation by angle \(\vartheta(x)\). Then \[\sigma\sigma^*=I,\qquad \nabla\sigma\in L^4(\mathbb R^2),\qquad
\frac{\|\sigma(re_1)-\sigma(0)\|_{\rm HS}}r
=\frac{\sqrt8|\sin(r^{3/4}/2)|}r\longrightarrow\infty.\] The gradient is integrable to the fourth power near zero because its radial integral is proportional to \(\int_0^1 r^{4(-1/4)}r\,dr\). With \(p=q=8\), \(p_0=4\), \(q_0=\infty\), and zero drifts, all formal assumptions hold with \(\alpha=1\), but \(\sigma\) is not Lipschitz. Thus Hölder regularity of \(a\) cannot simply be substituted for the same regularity of \(\sigma\).

Finally, the covariance correction in (7.6) must be \[\frac12\int_0^t\nabla^2U(r,X_r^n):
 [a(r,X_{k_n(r)}^n)-a(r,X_r^n)]\,dr.\] The printed formula uses \(X_r\) and omits \(1/2\); Itô’s formula for \(U(t,X_t^n)\) evaluates its Hessian at \(X_t^n\). Its subsequent estimate therefore needs a discrete-path occupation estimate.

Counterevidence and repair boundary. The abstract describes the diffusion coefficient itself as Hölder continuous, which may reflect the intended hypothesis; the formal Condition A1 controls its covariance instead. Explicitly strengthening that condition and retaining the random diffusion forcing would address two identified obstacles. The existing strong-rate statement includes a diffusion-error term, so the counterexample to (7.7) does not refute Theorem 2.2. These proof passages persist on v6 pp. 48–51.

# Further local errors with explicit repairs

Problem 4 (Minor: the Gaussian envelope in Lemma 3.11 has the wrong width).  Equation (3.22), p. 16, bounds a difference of Gaussian densities by densities with half the covariance. It is false even in dimension one with positive covariances.

For \(\Sigma=1,\bar\Sigma=2,K=2\), all its scalar comparison hypotheses hold. With the paper’s variance convention, \[\frac{|p_1(x)-p_2(x)|}{|1-1/2|\,[p_{1/2}(x)+p_1(x)]}
\sim \sqrt2\,e^{x^2/4}\longrightarrow\infty.\] No constant \(N(d,K)\) can work. The proof of Lemma 5.2 on p. 25 and later uses of Lemma 3.11 inherit this invalid envelope. Wider Gaussian envelopes, obtained by differentiating the density along a covariance interpolation and absorbing its quadratic polynomial into a slower Gaussian, give the required type of estimate. This changes constants and Gaussian widths, not the displayed time exponents in (5.5)–(5.6). That local repair is why this is classified Minor. A precision-matrix interpretation is incompatible with the explicit variance convention on p. 2 and the density formula on p. 17. The same half-covariance envelope appears on v6 pp. 19 and 28.

Problem 5 (Minor: inconsistent covariance and generator normalizations in Section 5).  Equations (5.3)–(5.4), p. 23, and the Duhamel identity (5.10), p. 26, do not use a consistent factor \(1/2\).

The random variable in (5.3) has covariance \(\int_s^t a(r,y)dr\), whereas (5.4) prints \(\Sigma_{s,t}=\frac12\int_s^t a(r,y)dr\). For constant \(\sigma=1\) and \(f(x)=\cos x\), the actual adjoint in (5.3) gives \(e^{-(t-s)/2}\cos x\); the printed kernel gives \(e^{-(t-s)/4}\cos x\).

Even if \(T\) is defined by the actual adjoint (5.3), the \(H\) defined on p. 23 needs a factor \(1/2\) in (5.10). To see this without a convention ambiguity, take smooth time-independent \(a(x)=1+\frac12e^{-x^2}\), \(f=1\), \(s=0\), and \(0<t<1/n\). Then \(Q_{0,t}^n1=1\), while the actual adjoint has \(T_{0,t}1=1+\frac t2a''+o(t)\) and \(\int_0^tQ_{0,k_n(r)}^nH_{r,t}^n1\,dr=-ta''+o(t)\). At \(x=0\), the printed right side of (5.10) is \(1+t/2+o(t)\), not 1.

A consistent repair uses \(\Sigma=\int a\), the generator \(\frac12a^{ij}\partial_{ij}\), and \(\frac12H\) in the Duhamel formula. These factors can be absorbed in the subsequent norm estimates; the smoothing-rate conclusion is not refuted. The same definitions and (5.10) occur on v6 pp. 26–29.

Problem 6 (Minor: Proposition 4.6(b) has incorrect homogeneity).  The right side on p. 21 contains \(w_0(0,1)\), whereas it must scale as \(w_0(0,1)^{1/q}\).

Take \(d=1,p=q=4,\bar p=1\). Let \(f\geq0\) be smooth, nonzero, compactly supported and normalized by \(\|f\|_4=1\). Put \(g_\epsilon(t,x)=\epsilon f(x)\), \(w_0(s,t)=\epsilon^4(t-s)\), and \(\Gamma=1\). The Bessel potential operator of order \(-1\) is an \(L^4\) contraction, so both hypotheses of Proposition 4.6 hold. Its left side equals \(\epsilon\,\mathbb E\int_0^1 f(B_r)dr=c\epsilon\), with \(c>0\), whereas its right side is \(N\epsilon^4\). This fails as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Part (a) already has the correct power; Theorem 6.1(iii) and Theorem 2.3(iii) also print the correct power. Restoring it in part (b) repairs this localized statement. It does not establish a defect in their final rate. The same incorrect power is visible on v6 p. 24.

Problem 7 (Minor: the change-of-measure direction in (5.33) is reversed).  The density in the proof of Theorem 5.1, p. 36, introduces the negative of the desired drift into the driftless Euler process.

Let \(u_r=(\sigma^{-1}b^n)(r,\bar X_{k_n(r)}^n)\) and use the printed density \(\rho=\exp(-\int u\,dB-\frac12\int|u|^2dr)\). Under \(dQ=\rho\,dP\), \(\widetilde B=B+\int u\,dr\) is Brownian. Hence \[d\bar X_r^n
=\sigma(r,\bar X_{k_n(r)}^n)d\widetilde B_r
-b^n(r,\bar X_{k_n(r)}^n)dr,\] rather than the positive drift in (1.3). This distinction already occurs for a smooth compactly supported scalar drift equal to one near the initial point, which satisfies Condition B. The two laws have opposite initial drift generators.

Use the positive stochastic-integral sign to obtain the positive drift under the new measure. The exponential-moment argument depends on \(|u|^2\), so its estimates survive this correction. The continuum proof on p. 40 similarly evaluates its negative-sign density along \(\bar X\) while making a claim about the law of \(X\); it must consistently choose the process and direction of measure change. These are repairable proof identities, not counterexamples to the stated moment estimates. No independent comparison of these two passages with v6 is asserted here.

Problem 8 (Minor: the multiplier norm in Theorem 2.3(iii)’s proof has the wrong order).  On p. 46, the product estimate and the definition of \(w_1\) use \(\|g\|_{L^q_tL^{-1,p}_x}\), although Lemma A.3 requires the multiplier in the positive-order space \(L^{1,p}\).

There is no continuous multiplication map \(H^{-1}(\mathbb R)\times H^{-1}(\mathbb R)\to H^{-1}(\mathbb R)\). For a nonzero smooth compactly supported \(\psi\), take \(g_k=h_k=\psi(x)\cos(kx)\). Both \(H^{-1}\) norms are \(O(k^{-1})\), while \[g_kh_k=\tfrac12\psi^2+\tfrac12\psi^2\cos(2kx)
\longrightarrow\tfrac12\psi^2\ne0\quad\hbox{in }H^{-1}.\] Thus the printed negative-order product bound fails. This check applies already with \(d=1,p=2,q=10\), which obey \(d/p+4/q<1\); a constant time dependence supplies the temporal norms.

Here \(g=\nabla U\) actually has a uniformly bounded \(L^q_tL^{1,p}_x\) norm by (7.2), and \(p>d\). Replacing the negative-order norm of \(g\) by the positive-order norm repairs the multiplication step. The sentence choosing \(q_2=2\) must also be made consistent with \(q_3=q/2\): the available choice is \(q_2=q\). With these repairs, the product control has the required homogeneity. This local error does not refute (2.11). The same multiplier sign occurs on v6 p. 53.

# Coverage, counterchecks, and limits

The review followed the route from Conditions A–B through the conditional sewing and Khasminskii estimates, the driftless discrete transition estimates, Girsanov transfer, the continuum occupation estimates, the Zvonkin transformation, and the exponential-weight removal. The appendix’s multiplication, localization, duality, and terminal-time bounds were read, including their stated ranges.

Several checks support parts of this architecture. Condition B gives the \(L^m\) Euler increment estimate of Lemma 7.2. The three truncation and mollification calculations in Corollary 2.4 have the stated scaling under their respective strict parameter restrictions. The sixteen singularity differences arising from (5.29) are positive: their four base differences are \[1-\frac dp-\frac2q,\quad
\frac{1-\alpha/2-1/q-d/(2p)}{1-\alpha/2},\quad
\frac{1-d/p-2/q}{1-d/(2p)},\quad
1-\frac1q-\frac d{2p},\] and the four subsequent powers are positive. Independent exact arithmetic checked 168 admissible parameter cases, or 2688 differences, in addition to this algebraic reasoning. The moment restriction in Theorem 2.2 is compatible with the stretched-exponential exponent of Lemma 7.4 when the preceding occupation and error bounds are valid. The endpoint \(\nu=0\) of Theorem 2.3(ii) is covered directly by part (i), even though its interpolation proof is phrased for positive order.

The source has additional local notational inconsistencies, including the component \(b^{n,h}\) in the drift term of (2.3), where the vector \(b^n\cdot\nabla U^h\) is needed, and the unweighted definition of \(\mathcal A_t\) in the proof of Proposition 5.12 despite a weighted \(A_{s,t}\). These have transparent intended readings and are not counted as additional substantive findings.

The decisive examples above are analytic. A primary-authored Sympy script independently checks the Gaussian integral, concentration scaling, Gaussian-tail ratios, generator normalization, and admissible exponent grid; it runs no author code. An initial symbolic equality check failed to normalize \(\operatorname{erfc}=1-\operatorname{erf}\); that failed script and the diagnostic are preserved, and the corrected symbolic normalization is recorded separately. This was a verification-script simplification issue, not contrary mathematical evidence.

Relevant passages of v6 were read and visually inspected, not the entire second version. Imported results in the cited SDE, PDE, sewing, maximal-function, and pathwise BDG literature were tracked as dependencies but not all independently re-proved or acquired. No unavailable author program is treated as a mathematical defect. The proven failures are the printed transport estimate, the localization lemma, and specified intermediate identities or bounds. No whole-paper-failure conclusion or independent acceptance is claimed.
