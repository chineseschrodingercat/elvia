Abstract: The acquired version of Coiculescu and Palasek’s construction is examined through its critical initial data, alternating principal flows, residual correction and final solution class. Two core derivations require correction: the support and cutoff construction does not provide the containments it uses, and the stated linearized derivative estimate is contradicted by an admissible Fourier family. Explicit replacement arguments are supplied. Three further findings concern local heat estimates, a time-integral comparison and the auxiliary energy inequality. The overall assessment is Major for these specified derivations. The evidence does not establish that the main non-uniqueness conclusion is false.

# Problem 1: support and cutoff assertions in the critical-data construction

## Locations and consequence

Definition 3.1, pp. 14–15; Lemma 3.2(3), p. 15; Lemma 3.3, pp. 15–17; Definition 3.4, p. 17; Proposition 3.7 and (3.17), pp. 19–21; Remark 3.12, p. 23. These assertions feed the amplitude recursion, Proposition 3.8’s \(\operatorname{BMO}\) estimate, and the equality of the two initial traces.

## Decisive discrepancies

Let \(S_{j,k}=\operatorname{supp}\Psi_{j,k}\). It is nonempty and invariant under translations by \((2\pi/M_k)\mathbb Z^3\), since \(N_k/M_k\) is integral. Translates of any one point of \(S_{j,k}\) have covering radius at most \(\sqrt3\pi/M_k\). The literal fixed \(\delta_0\)-neighborhood in Definition 3.1 is therefore all of \(\mathbb T^3\) once \[M_k>\sqrt3\pi/\delta_0.\] The admissible choice of arbitrarily large \(A\) makes this true already at \(k=0\). Both intersections in that definition then equal the torus, whereas Lemma 3.3 at \(k=1\) requires \(|\widetilde\Omega_1|\leq|\mathbb T^3|/2\). This is a direct contradiction. The \(M_k^{-1}\) factor in Lemma 3.2’s separation formula is strong internal evidence for the intended radii \[\rho_k=\delta_0/M_k,\qquad 2\rho_k.\]

Restoring that factor is necessary but insufficient. The bump on p. 16 is one only on the base supports and zero outside their \(\delta_0\)-neighborhood. It cannot majorize the indicator of their \(2\delta_0\)-neighborhood: a point in the intervening annulus has indicator one and bump zero. In addition, the plateau required of \(\chi_k\) is \(\Omega_{k-1}\), not the entire new pipe \(\Omega_{j,k}\) used on p. 20. The choice \(x=x_j\) does not make \(\widetilde\varphi_j(M_kx)=1\).

Equation reference: trace-identity. Most consequentially, the definitions and the Nash identity give exactly \[
 \overline v_k(0)=\mathbb P\operatorname{div}\!\left(\chi_{k+1}^{\,2}\mathcal D\psi_k^0\right).\] The scalar multiple of \(\operatorname{Id}\) disappears under \(\mathbb P\operatorname{div}\). The desired value \(\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}\psi_k^0\) follows if \(\chi_{k+1}=1\) on \(\operatorname{supp}\mathcal D\psi_k^0\). Definition 3.4 does not supply this property after transition regions and convolution have enlarged the support. The first power of \(\chi_{k+1}\) printed on p. 23 is an additional algebraic slip; replacing it by its square does not supply the missing containment.

## An explicit compatible repair

Use the corrected radii and put \(S_m=\bigcup_jS_{j,m}\). Write \[\ell_m=N_{m+1}^{-1/3}N_m^{-2/3}\] for the mollifier support radius. Increase \(A\) so that, for every \(m\), \[\ell_m<\rho_m/4,\qquad
 \sum_{r\geq m+1}\ell_r\leq\rho_m/4,\qquad
 M_{m+1}/M_m\geq4.\] These conditions are compatible with the stated superlacunary scales. For \(k>m\), set \[d_{m,k}=\rho_m2^{-(k-m+2)},\qquad
 a_{m,k}=\rho_m+\sum_{r=m+1}^{k-1}(d_{m,r}+\ell_r).\] Choose \(0\leq h_{m,k}\leq1\), equal to one on the closed \(a_{m,k}\)-neighborhood of \(S_m\) and zero outside the \((a_{m,k}+d_{m,k})\)-neighborhood, with derivatives bounded by \(C_qd_{m,k}^{-q}\). Define \(\chi_k=\prod_{m<k}h_{m,k}\).

The sums of transition widths and later mollifier radii are each at most \(\rho_m/4\). Thus \(\chi_k=1\) on \(\Omega_{k-1}\) and is supported inside \(\widetilde\Omega_{k-1}\). Moreover, \[\sum_{m<k}d_{m,k}^{-1}
 =\frac4{\delta_0}\sum_{m<k}2^{k-m}M_m
 \leq C M_{k-1},
 \qquad
 \|\nabla^q\chi_k\|_\infty\leq C_qM_{k-1}^q.\] Hence the stated derivative scale (3.9) is retained, with constants independent of \(A\).

This choice also accounts for convolution. For every old index \(m<k\), the support of the unmollified \(k\)th potential lies in the \((a_{m,k}+d_{m,k})\)-neighborhood of \(S_m\). After convolution its radius is at most \[a_{m,k}+d_{m,k}+\ell_k=a_{m,k+1}.\] For the new index \(m=k\), its distance from \(S_k\) is at most \(\ell_k<\rho_k\). Consequently \(\chi_{k+1}=1\) on the entire support of \(\psi_k\), including its derivatives, and \(\operatorname{supp}\psi_k\subset\widetilde\Omega_k\). The base step follows from \(\operatorname{supp}\psi_0\subset N_{\ell_0}(S_0)\). Formula [trace-identity] (#trace-identity) now gives the required common initial data.

For the volume argument, choose a base bump \(\Phi\) equal to one on the \(2\delta_0\)-neighborhood and supported in the \(3\delta_0\)-neighborhood. Its integral is at most \(C\delta\); choose the fixed \(\delta\) small enough that its normalized mean \(\mu\) is at most \(1/4\). Retain the factor \(\delta_0^{-1}\) in its derivative bound. A bounded periodic corrector for \(\Phi-\mu\), followed by integration by parts on a cube \(Q\), gives \[\left|\int_Q f\bigl(\Phi(M_kx)-\mu\bigr)\right|
 \leq \frac C{M_k}\left(\int_Q|\nabla f|+\int_{\partial Q}|f|\right).\] For \(K=k(Q)\) and \(f=\prod_{m=K}^{k-1}\Phi(M_mx)\), the error is at most \[\frac{C|Q|}{M_k}
 \left(\ell(Q)^{-1}+\delta_0^{-1}\sum_{m=K}^{k-1}M_m\right).\] The scale ratios make this at most \(2^{-(k-K+1)}|Q|\), uniformly for \(k>K\), after increasing \(A\). Starting with the trivial bound at \(k=K\), induction proves the corrected local estimate \(|Q\cap\widetilde\Omega_k|\leq2^{-(k-K)}|Q|\). The torus-volume estimate follows without a boundary term.

Finally, the amplitude lower bound can be recovered by choosing a point at the correct scale. Inductively, \(\Omega_{k-1}\) contains a ball of radius \(\rho_{k-1}/2\). A point \(y=(x_j+2\pi z)/M_k\) lies within \(\sqrt3\pi/M_k\) of its center. For large \(A\), the ball of radius \(\rho_k/2\) around \(y\) remains in the old ball and in the new pipe neighborhood. Moving by at most \(\pi/(N_k|\eta_j|)\) in the \(\eta_j\) direction sets the cosine to one, keeps \(\chi_k=1\), and leaves \(\widetilde\varphi_j(M_kx)\geq1/2\). The positive Nash coefficient then gives the needed lower bound for \(I_1\). The mollifier and profile errors are smaller by positive powers of the scale ratios. The same argument at \(k=0\) makes the base upper and lower bounds uniform in \(A\).

## Assessment

Major. The printed construction fails at claims directly used to obtain critical data and equal initial traces. The repair above supplies the missing choices and estimates for this bounded part of the proof; it is more than a typographical replacement. It does not show the final non-uniqueness theorem false. Author adoption is not established. The monotonicity of the actual supports asserted in Remark 1.6 is unnecessary: the corrected nested enclosing sets suffice for smoothness outside their measure-zero intersection.

# Problem 2: the linearized derivative estimate is false uniformly in the scale

## Locations

Equation reference: false-semigroup. Section 4.2’s space \(Y\) and evolution family, p. 34; Proposition 4.2, p. 35; its derivative proof, p. 36; its use in Proposition 4.3, pp. 37–38. Here \[\|a\|_Y=\sup_{0<r\leq1}
 \left(r^{1-\alpha}\|a(r)\|_\infty
       +r^{3/2-\alpha}\|\nabla a(r)\|_{C^\kappa}\right).\] The proposition asserts \[
 \|S(t,s)a\|_\infty+(t-s)^{1/2}\|\nabla S(t,s)a\|_{C^\kappa}
 \lesssim s^{-1+\alpha-\epsilon}t^{-1/2+\epsilon}\|a\|_Y.\] Its own proof instead normalizes the derivative by \((t-s)^{1/2}t^{(1+\kappa)/2}\). The resulting estimate contains an additional \(t^{-\kappa/2}\).

## An admissible counterexample family

Equation reference: ratio. Fix \[\alpha=\frac1{32},\quad b=6,\quad \gamma=2,\quad
 \kappa=\frac1{16},\quad \epsilon=\frac1{64}.\] All printed restrictions hold. For integers \(L\to\infty\), choose \[A=L^{17},\qquad N_0=L^{34},\qquad
 n=L^{16},\qquad s=L^{-34}=n^{-2(1+\kappa)},\qquad
 t=L^{-32}=n^{-2}.\] Integer scales avoid any rounding issue. Let \(\eta\) be a fixed smooth bump supported in \((1/2,2)\), with \(\eta(1)=1\). Take the symmetric tensor whose only nonzero entries are \[a_{12}(r,x)=a_{21}(r,x)=s^{-1+\alpha}\eta(r/s)\sin(nx_1).\] It has \(\|a\|_Y\leq C\) uniformly in \(L\): the gradient term contributes \(s^{1/2}n^{1+\kappa}=1\). Also \[\mathbb P\operatorname{div}a(s)=s^{-1+\alpha}n\cos(nx_1)e_2.\] For the heat evolution, since \(n^2(t-s)=1-L^{-2}\), the weighted derivative norm is at least \(c\,s^{-1+\alpha}n^{1+\kappa}\). The right side of [false-semigroup] (#false-semigroup) is at most \(C\,s^{-1+\alpha}n^{1+2\kappa\epsilon}\). Their ratio tends to infinity as \[
 n^{\kappa(1-2\epsilon)}=L^{31/32}.\]

This also tests the actual drifts of the construction. On \([s,t]\), the preceding estimate (3.19) bounds every fixed derivative of \(v^{(i)}\) by a polynomial in \(L\) times \(\exp(-c_mL^{34})\), because \(N_0^2s=L^{34}\). Let \(B_L\) be the supremum of its inhomogeneous \(C^{1,\kappa}\) norm on that interval. In the \(C^{1,\kappa}\) Duhamel estimate, \[M_L\leq\|\mathbb P\operatorname{div}a(s)\|_{C^{1,\kappa}}
             +C\sqrt{t-s}\,B_LM_L,
 \qquad M_L=\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}\|S(r,s)a\|_{C^{1,\kappa}}.\] For large \(L\) the second coefficient is below \(1/2\). The difference between \(S(t,s)a\) and its heat evolution is therefore exponentially smaller than the heat norm, whose decay factor approaches \(e^{-1}\). The lower bound and [ratio] (#ratio) persist. This uses the paper’s preceding late-time bounds, which are compatible with Problem 1’s repair; it is not a numerical simulation or an unauthorized replacement of its drift by zero.

## Repair and impact on the fixed point

Retain the derivative estimate actually obtained: \[\|\nabla S(t,s)a\|_{C^\kappa}
 \lesssim(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1+\alpha-\epsilon}
             t^{-(1+\kappa)/2+\epsilon}\|a\|_Y.\] Choose, before \(A\), \[0<\kappa<\min\!\left\{\alpha,\frac12-\frac1{2\gamma}-2\alpha\right\},
 \qquad 0<\epsilon<\alpha.\] The interval is nonempty. The beta integral then yields \[\|\nabla T(w)(t)\|_{C^\kappa}
 \leq C B(\alpha-\epsilon,1/2)\,
      t^{-1+\alpha-\kappa/2}(\|w\|_X^2+\epsilon_0).\] Multiplying by the existing \(X\) weight \(t^{1-\alpha/2}\) leaves \(t^{(\alpha-\kappa)/2}\leq1\). The unchanged velocity estimate leaves \(t^{\alpha/2}\leq1\). The product bound from \(X\) to \(Y\) and the same estimate for differences consequently give the contraction after choosing \(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_0\) small. For example, keeping the other rational parameters above and taking \(\kappa=1/64\) gives the positive gradient exponent \(1/128\).

Major. A false uniform operator bound is used directly in the core perturbation argument. The weaker bound plus the additional parameter restriction repairs this step. No counterexample to the main theorem is claimed. At the time diagonal, arbitrary \(Y\) data give only \(C^\kappa\) initial vector fields; the derivative statement should be made for \(0<s<t\), using smoothing approximation for nonsmooth initial vectors.

# Problem 3: two local heat assertions need corrected scope and scaling

## The zero-gap endpoint, p. 13 after (2.7)

The homogeneous Hölder–Zygmund estimate itself is consistent. The subsequent blanket replacement of an output \(\mathcal C^0\) norm by \(L^\infty\) excludes too few endpoints. For \[f_J(x)=\sum_{j=1}^J2^{-j}\sin(2^jx_1),\qquad t_J=4^{-J},\] dyadic finite overlap gives \(\|f_J\|_{\mathcal C^1}\leq C\), whereas \[\partial_1e^{t_J\Delta}f_J(0)
 =\sum_{j=1}^Je^{-4^{j-J}}\geq J/e.\] Thus the advertised case \(m=1,s_1=0,s_2=1\) cannot have a uniform constant. The examples are smooth and mean zero; \(f_Je_2\) is divergence free and fixed by \(\mathbb P\). This is not the single Leray endpoint already excluded in the text.

Keep (2.7), and require a strictly positive smoothing gap for this general output-\(L^\infty\) replacement, or a summable Besov input at zero gap. Classical valid \(L^\infty\) heat endpoints can be stated separately. The inspected core applications have positive gaps or positive Hölder regularity.

## The pointwise kernel, p. 49, Appendix C.3

At \(t=0\), the inverse transform of \(m_n(\xi)=\pi(\xi/n)\) is \[\check m_n(x)=n^3\check\pi(nx).\] Choose \(x_*\) with \(\check\pi(x_*)\neq0\) and put \(x=x_*/n\). The left side grows as \(n^3\), while the printed bound is uniformly bounded by its minimum with \(1\). A compatible corrected estimate is \[|\check m_n(x)|
 \leq Cn^3\min\!\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{1+n^2t}{n|x|}\right)^{10}\right\}e^{-cn^2t}.\] Integration cancels the spatial factor \(n^3\); the remaining polynomial in \(1+n^2t\) is absorbed by a weaker exponential. This restores the uniform \(L^1\) kernel estimate.

Minor. These are false local assertions with explicit counterexamples. They do not refute Lemma 2.2’s operator bounds or the main construction. The following multiplier argument on p. 50 supplies further counterevidence to any claim that the heat theory itself fails.

# Problem 4: the final time-integral comparison in Proposition 3.13

On pp. 25–26, the quantity denoted \(III\) is \[III=\sum_k\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
          N_k^{1+\beta_1}e^{-N_{k+1}^2r}r^{-1/2}\,dr.\] Page 26 bounds it by a constant times \(t_1^{1/2-(1+\beta_1)/(2b)}\). Fix \(A\) and \(t_2=1\).                 \int_0^\infty e^{-N_{k+1}^2r}r^{-1/2}\,dr

The integration domains in \(I\) and \(II\) are correct: an initial suspicion about discarded tails was withdrawn after checking that \(N_k<t_2^{-1/2}\) implies \(N_k^{-2}>t_2\).

# Problem 5: the auxiliary energy bound loses dependence on the initial data

Proposition B.1, p. 44, gives after Grönwall an upper bound \[\|w^0\|_2^2+8\exp\!\left(\|v\|_{L^2_tL^\infty_x}^2\right)\] for the squared energy supremum plus a nonnegative gradient term. The additive allowance cannot control energy growth for a homogeneous linear equation under arbitrary scaling of \(w^0\).

On \([0,1]\times\mathbb T^3\), take \[v=\sin(x_2)e_1,\qquad
 w^0=c e_2,\qquad
 w(t)=c e_2-c(1-e^{-t})\cos(x_2)e_1.\] Both fields are smooth and divergence free. Direct differentiation verifies (B.1), with pressure zero. The span of \(e_2\) and \(\cos(x_2)e_1\) is invariant, so this is also an exact Fourier Galerkin example. At time one, \[\|w(1)\|_2^2-\|w^0\|_2^2
 =\frac{c^2(2\pi)^3}{2}(1-e^{-1})^2,
 \qquad \|v\|_{L^2_tL^\infty_x}^2=1.\] Taking \(c=100\) contradicts the allowance \(8e\) even if spatial volume is normalized; in that convention the gain is approximately \(1997.882\), versus \(21.746\).

A valid bound, with inessential constants, is \[\sup_{t_0\leq t\leq T}\|w(t)\|_2^2
       +\frac12\int_{t_0}^T\|\nabla w\|_2^2
 \leq2\|w^0\|_2^2
       \exp\!\left(8\int_{t_0}^T\|v\|_\infty^2\right).\] This is uniform in the Galerkin cutoff. It restores existence, and its application to a difference supplies uniqueness. One should use an \(L^2\)-orthonormal divergence-free basis complete in the relevant Sobolev space, and correct the factor \(2\) and sign in the later weak and forced-Stokes formulas. Smoothness of \(v\) on each finite interval is sufficient; integrability on the entire half-line is unnecessary.

Minor. The displayed bound is refuted, but a standard energy argument repairs the auxiliary linear existence proof. This does not furnish a counterexample to the final solutions.

## Uniform critical bounds require an explicit bridge

The following is a reviewer-supplied completion, conditional on the corrected support and perturbation estimates. It is not represented as an argument written out in Section 5.

For the heat component \(H=\sum v_k\) over its parity class, put \(E_k=\widetilde\Omega_k\). The pointwise bounds and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz imply \[|H(t,x)|^2\leq Ct^{-1/2}
          \sum_kN_ke^{-cN_k^2t}\mathbf1_{E_k}(x).\] For a cube \(Q\) of side \(R\leq1\), integration up to \(R^2\) bounds its local energy by \[C\sum_k\min(1,RN_k)|Q\cap E_k|.\] Let \(q=k(Q)\). For \(k\leq q-2\), the sum is bounded by \(CRN_{q-2}|Q|\leq C|Q|\), since \(\gamma<b\) and \(M_{q-1}<C_0/R\). The index \(q-1\) contributes one bounded term, and \(k\geq q\) is a geometric tail by the corrected local volume estimate. Empty low blocks when \(q=0,1\) cause no problem. This proves the local Carleson bound for \(H\). The inverse-cascade part is bounded by \(Ct^{-(1+\beta_1)/(2b)}\), with \((1+\beta_1)/b<1\), and \(w\) by \(\epsilon_1t^{-1/2+\alpha/2}\), so both have integrable squared \(L^\infty\) norms near zero. Periodicity handles large balls on \([0,1]\); the zero-mean smooth small solution has finite subsequent spacetime energy by the energy identity and Poincaré inequality. The pointwise bound and subsequent small-data decay give the other term in \(X_{KT}\).

The uniform \(\operatorname{BMO}^{-1}\) bound follows from the same cube split applied to the heat potentials and to the tensors \(N_{k+1}^{-2}a_{j,k+1}^2\) with their bounded exponential factors. Their amplitudes are bounded, their derivatives cost \(N_k\), and their supports lie in \(E_k\). The periodic Leray multiplier is bounded on \(\operatorname{BMO}\) modulo constants, and divergence removes the zero mode. Finally \(\mathcal C^{-1+\alpha/2}\) embeds into \(\operatorname{BMO}^{-1}\) by taking a positive-Hölder potential. This proves the required boundedness after the specified repairs; it does not assert norm continuity in \(\operatorname{BMO}^{-1}\) at zero.

The unnormalized introductory heat formula on p. 4 needs the missing spatial-volume factor. The standard definition on p. 12 and the normalized path norm on p. 2 identify the intended space. The author-hosted Koch–Tataru source was inspected: its theorem gives uniqueness of a small solution in its path space, not unrestricted uniqueness among all positive-time smooth solutions [KT]. Periodic comparisons with local \(bmo^{-1}\) also require the zero-mode qualification, satisfied by this construction. These clarifications are not additional Major findings.
