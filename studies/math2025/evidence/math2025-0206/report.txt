Abstract: The assigned author preprint proves uniqueness through a spectral ample–narrow decomposition. An explicit smooth epigraph construction shows that the basepoint-component eigenvalue used to define the preliminary narrow region need not be continuous, and that this region need not be open even under the stated spectral gap. This is a substantive gap in the written decomposition proof, not a counterexample to the uniqueness theorem. Three further local problems concern positive eigenfunctions on disconnected truncations, a Robin energy coefficient, and an upper parabolic envelope. The latter admit direct repairs. The assessment is Major; scientific adjudication and human review remain pending.

# Identity and exact mathematical scope

The authors are Henri Berestycki and Cole Graham. The journal association is DOI https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1578. The assigned primary input is the 40-page arXiv:2212.06611v1, submitted 13 December 2022. This is an author preprint, not a verified copy of the publisher Version of Record. The publisher landing page separately records submission on 13 December 2022, acceptance on 21 April 2024, and publication on 13 January 2025, with an online-first 2025 citation. Findings below apply to the exact assigned author version; equivalence with the later publisher PDF has not been established. Printed and PDF page numbers agree.

The standing hypotheses (pp. 2–3) are a nonempty connected uniformly \(C^{2,\gamma}\) domain \(\Omega\), \(f\in C^{1,\gamma}([0,\infty))\), \(f(0)=f(1)=0\), \(f>0\) on \((0,1)\), \(f'(0)>0\), and \(f<0\) above 1. Strong KPP additionally requires \(s\mapsto f(s)/s\) strictly decreasing on \((0,1]\). Weak KPP only requires \(f(s)\le f'(0)s\). The equations and boundary convention are \[-\Delta u=f(u),\qquad
 \mathcal N_\rho u=\rho\partial_\nu u+(1-\rho)u=0,\quad 0\le\rho\le1.\] The conclusions concern positive bounded solutions. Dirichlet, Robin, and Neumann conditions correspond to \(\rho=0\), \(0<\rho<1\), and \(\rho=1\).

Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.1 (p. 4) use the closure of the principal limit spectrum: \[f'(0)\notin\overline{\Sigma(\Omega,\rho)},\qquad
 \Sigma(\Omega,\rho)=\{\lambda(\Omega^*,\rho):\Omega^*\text{ is a connected limit}\}.\] Avoiding each isolated equality is not enough. Theorem 1.5 (p. 6) separates strict subcritical existence, weak-KPP strict supercritical nonexistence, and critical nonexistence in bounded or full-rank periodic domains under the stronger assumption. The general critical case and unconditional strong-KPP uniqueness remain conjectures in this version. Proposition 5.3 addresses a particular planar bulb with a critical half-strip, with the additional expansion \(f(s)=f'(0)s-Bs^2+O(s^3)\), \(B>0\).

# Established problems

Problem 1 (Major: the preliminary narrow region need not be open).  Location. Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 4.4, p. 21; (4.7), p. 22; definition of \(\Omega_+^0\), continuity claim, and application of Lemma 4.4, p. 23; connected-component convergence and (4.14), p. 24. Theorem 1.1 invokes this decomposition on p. 25.

Claim being tested. With \(R\) satisfying \(\lambda(B_1,0)R^{-2}<\delta\), the proof defines \[\Omega_+^0=
 \{z\in\Omega:\lambda(\operatorname{comp}_z(\Omega\cap B_{2R}(z)),\sigma)>\mu+\delta\}.
                                                                    \tag{2.1}\] It asserts continuity of the eigenvalue as a function of \(z\), infers openness, and applies the truncation lemma for an open set. Smoothness of the original domain does not prevent components of its ball intersection from joining through a newly opened passage.

A construction satisfying the stated gap. Work in dimension two with Dirichlet boundary. Put \(R=10\) and \(g(x)=\sqrt{400-x^2}\) for \(|x|<20\). Choose a compactly supported \(C^\infty\) function \(h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) such that \[\begin{gathered}
 h(0)=-1,\qquad h(-1)>g(-1),\\
 h(x)<g(x)\quad(0\le x\le7,\ x\ne1),\\
 h(x)=g(x)-(x-1)^2\quad\text{near }x=1,\qquad
 h(x)=-3\quad(3\le x\le7).                                  \tag{2.2}
 \end{gathered}\] These conditions are compatible: on \([0,7]\) choose \(h=g-F\), where \(F\) is smooth, positive away from 1, equals \((x-1)^2\) near 1, has \(F(0)=21\), and equals \(g+3\) on \([3,7]\). Smooth positive interpolations between these prescriptions preserve positivity; extend smoothly to the left with the stated value at \(-1\), and then to zero outside a compact interval.

Let \(\Omega=\{(x,y):y>h(x)\}\). It is connected and uniformly \(C^\infty\), a bounded perturbation of the upper half-plane. Let \[z_0=(0,0),\quad z_\varepsilon=(0,\varepsilon),\quad
 C_\varepsilon=\operatorname{comp}_{z_\varepsilon}
                  (\Omega\cap B_{20}(z_\varepsilon)).\] For \(\varepsilon=0\), the vertical sections at \(x=-1\) and \(x=1\) are empty. Hence \(C_0\subset(-1,1)\times\mathbb R\). The one-dimensional Dirichlet Poincaré inequality, applied to zero extensions and integrated in \(y\), gives \[\lambda(C_0,0)\ge\frac{\pi^2}{4}.                            \tag{2.3}\] For every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the upper circle arc is \(g(x)+\varepsilon\); the former contact at \(x=1\) now has positive gap \(\varepsilon+(x-1)^2\). All vertical fibers for \(0\le x\le7\) are nonempty intervals, and their union connects \(z_\varepsilon\) to the fixed square \[Q=(3,7)\times(-2,2)\subset C_\varepsilon.\] The square lies strictly inside the ball for small \(\varepsilon\), and \(h=-3\) there. Domain monotonicity and a square test function yield \[\lambda(C_\varepsilon,0)\le\lambda(Q,0)
 =\frac{\pi^2}{8}.                                          \tag{2.4}\] Thus the claimed continuity fails. No numerical eigenvalue computation or assertion of a full thin-passage spectral limit is needed.

The example also meets the hypotheses used immediately before (2.1). Every connected translation limit of \(\Omega\) is a translate of \(\Omega\), a half-plane, or \(\mathbb R^2\); each contains arbitrarily large balls and has principal eigenvalue zero. Thus \(\Sigma(\Omega,0)=\{0\}\). Set \[\mu=\frac{3\pi^2}{16},\qquad \delta=\frac{\pi^2}{64}.\] Then (4.7)’s gap holds, while the unit disk contains a square of side \(\sqrt2\), so \[\lambda(B_1,0)R^{-2}\le\frac{\pi^2}{100}<\delta .\] Nevertheless \[\lambda(C_\varepsilon,0)\le\frac{8\pi^2}{64}
 <\mu+\delta=\frac{13\pi^2}{64}
 <\frac{16\pi^2}{64}\le\lambda(C_0,0).\] Therefore \(z_0\in\Omega_+^0\), but \(z_\varepsilon\notin\Omega_+^0\) for arbitrarily small positive \(\varepsilon\). The set in (2.1) is not open.

Impact and repair boundary. Lemma 4.4 cannot be applied to this set as written. The same component-switching phenomenon prevents the unqualified connected-component convergence asserted before (4.14); convergence of the smooth original domains does not supply convergence of these selected ball-intersection components. This invalidates indispensable steps in the written proof of Theorem 4.3, on which the general uniqueness argument depends.

A repair needs a stable localization construction, for example with appropriately proved radius buffers and open spectral labels, followed by new verification of the narrow-side Lieb bound, the ample-side coverage, and the limit inequalities. Merely citing continuity of eigenvalues on smoothly convergent bounded domains does not address the failed component convergence. A larger ball can retain some limiting connecting paths, but changing one fixed radius alone does not establish a general repair.

This is a counterexample to the auxiliary continuity/openness claim under its actual hypotheses, not to Theorems 4.3 or 1.1 themselves. Indeed this particular epigraph admits the trivial ample decomposition \(\Omega_-=\Omega,\Omega_+=\varnothing\). The Major classification concerns a central proof gap with a demonstrated failure, not a claim that the desired theorem is false or unrepairable.

Problem 2 (Minor: a disconnected truncation need not have a strictly positive principal eigenfunction).  Location. Disconnected-domain convention, p. 10; Proposition 2.1(iii)–(iv) and the normalized \(\varphi_R\) argument, pp. 11–12; related eigenfunction selection in the hair-trigger proof, p. 31.

Evidence. The source correctly defines the principal eigenvalue of a disconnected domain as the infimum of its component eigenvalues. It then takes \(\varphi_R>0\) on the whole \(\Omega\cap B_R\) at that eigenvalue and normalizes it at an arbitrarily fixed basepoint. These requirements can conflict. If two bounded Dirichlet components have eigenvalues \(\lambda_1>\lambda_2\), a positive eigenfunction at \(\lambda_2\) cannot exist on the first component. Testing against its positive first eigenfunction \(\psi_1\) gives \[(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)\int_{D_1}\varphi_R\psi_1=0,\] which contradicts positivity. Smooth connected domains with unequal components after a ball cut are allowed. The domain in Problem 1 already provides a basepoint component with eigenvalue at least \(\pi^2/4\) and another component containing \(Q\), whose eigenvalue is at most \(\pi^2/8\).

Repair. Let \(D_R\) be the connected component of \(\Omega\cap B_R(x)\) containing a fixed interior point, for large \(R\). These components increase and exhaust \(\Omega\): each compact subset can be connected to the point by finitely many interior paths, all contained in a sufficiently large ball. Normalize the positive eigenfunction on \(D_R\), not on all components. Its decreasing eigenvalues \(\mu_R\) have a limit. The local Harnack/compactness argument produces a positive generalized eigenfunction and gives \(\lambda(\Omega,\sigma)\ge\lim_R\mu_R\); domain monotonicity gives the reverse inequality. Finally, \[\lambda(\Omega,\sigma)\le\lambda(\Omega\mid B_R(x),\sigma)
 \le\mu_R\longrightarrow\lambda(\Omega,\sigma).\] This supplies the intended exhaustion conclusion. For a compactly supported nonlinear subsolution one can instead select a component with a sufficiently small eigenvalue and extend by zero, as the proof on p. 18 already does. The finding is a local proof-selection error, not a counterexample to Proposition 2.1’s intended connected-domain conclusions.

Problem 3 (Minor: the Robin energy is missing a factor one-half).  Location. Proof of Proposition 1.4, p. 29, energy display; comparison with the boundary operator (1.2), p. 3.

Evidence. For \(0<\rho<1\), put \(a=(1-\rho)/\rho\). The displayed functional is \[E[v]=\int_\Omega\left(\frac12|\nabla v|^2-H(v)\right)
       +a\int_{\partial\Omega}v^2.\] Its first variation in direction \(\eta\) is \[DE[v]\eta
 =\int_\Omega(-\Delta v-h(v))\eta
   +\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_\nu v+2av)\eta .\] Thus its critical points satisfy \(\partial_\nu v+2av=0\), whereas (1.2) requires \(\partial_\nu v+av=0\).

Repair and consequence. Replace the boundary coefficient by \(a/2\). The negative-energy construction can still be obtained by making the ignition reaction large; truncation to \([0,1]\), minimization, and subsequent sub/supersolution comparison retain their purpose. The error affects the displayed Robin variational argument. The Dirichlet case has no such boundary term, and this calculation does not refute the weak-KPP multiplicity proposition after the correction.

Problem 4 (Minor: the upper parabolic envelope must start at least at one).  Location. Proof of Proposition 1.8, p. 31, the choice \(M=\sup v(0,\cdot)\) and (4.24); positive-reaction versus strong-KPP hypotheses, pp. 7, 31–32; multiplicity construction, pp. 29–30.

Evidence. At this stage only positivity of \(f\), not uniqueness, has been assumed. The asserted identity \(\mathcal P_\infty M=\mathcal P_\infty1=u_+\) need not hold when \(M<1\).

For a concrete bounded setting within this stage’s assumptions, use a Robin example with \(0<\rho<1\) from the multiplicity construction, with its energy normalized as in Problem 3. It has a low solution \(u_L\), \(\sup u_L<1/2\), and a high solution \(u_H\), \(\sup u_H>1/2\). Hopf’s lemma and the Robin condition give \(m=\min_{\overline\Omega}u_L>0\). Take \[v(0,\cdot)=\epsilon u_L,\qquad
 0<\epsilon<\frac{m}{\|u_L\|_\infty}.\] Then the printed \(M=\epsilon\|u_L\|_\infty\) satisfies \(M<m\), so comparison gives \(\mathcal P_t M\le u_L\) for all \(t>0\). But \(\mathcal P_\infty1=u_+\ge u_H\), which exceeds \(u_L\) somewhere. Therefore the asserted equality is impossible, even without presuming existence of a limit for \(\mathcal P_tM\).

Repair and consequence. Use \(M=\max\{1,\sup v(0,\cdot)\}\). This constant is a supersolution, so its flow decreases to a bounded steady state. The latter is at most 1, while comparison with the flow from 1 gives equality with \(u_+\). The intended upper bound and, when uniqueness is available, the convergence conclusion survive. This is not a counterexample to the hair-trigger statement.

# Positive checks and remaining dependencies

The generalized eigenvalue and boundary conventions were checked against the Rayleigh formulation. Domain monotonicity is used with the partial order of Definition 2.1, which imposes Dirichlet conditions on artificial boundaries; ordinary Robin domain monotonicity is not assumed. In Lieb’s localization calculation (p. 14), integration in the translation variable cancels the cross gradient term and retains the correct Robin boundary contribution. Its actual error is \(O(R^{-2})\); the nearby informal scale \(\varepsilon^{-2}\) should be read as \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\), without affecting the displayed theorem.

The weighted negative-part proof of the maximum principle (pp. 17–18) has the correct sign. With \(\zeta_\delta=\operatorname{sech}(\delta|x|)\), its key inequality is \[\bigl(\delta^2-\lambda(-\mathcal L,\Omega,\sigma)\bigr)
       \|\zeta_\delta w_-\|_2^2\ge0.\] Choosing \(\delta^2<\lambda\) forces \(w_-=0\). The bounded/periodic critical nonexistence argument correctly uses \(\int u f(u)<f'(0)\int u^2\). The Neumann zero eigenvalue argument (pp. 16–17) uses polynomial volume growth to rule out a uniformly positive shell-to-volume ratio.

Conditional on a valid ample–narrow decomposition, the sliding/comparison mechanism in pp. 25–27 was checked, including boundary approach and the difference quotient \(q\le f'(0)\). The last bound still holds when \(\mu u_2>1\): then \(f(\mu u_2)<0\le f(u_1)\), so the quotient is negative. There is no need to extend the strict ratio condition above 1. Quotients involving \(\sigma/\rho\) should of course be treated separately in the Dirichlet case.

The critical half-strip coefficient was independently recomputed. For \(\varphi_1(y)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(\pi y/L)\), \[\int_0^L\varphi_1^3\,dy=\frac{8\sqrt2}{3\pi\sqrt L},\qquad
 \frac{6\varphi_1(y)}{B\int\varphi_1^3}
 =\frac{9\pi}{4B}\sin(\pi y/L).\] The projected equation \(\alpha_1''=\zeta\alpha_1^2(1+o(1))\) with decay to zero gives \(x^2\alpha_1(x)\to6/\zeta\), agreeing with (5.2) (pp. 33–35). The cylinder lemma’s projection is a positive affine function on the entire line and hence constant; the subsequent comparison is consistent. These are positive checks of this special critical mechanism, not a solution of the general critical conjecture.

Some auxiliary regularity steps remain dependencies. In particular, the normalized compactness assertion in Lemma 4.5 is not supplied merely by the usual Harnack inequality for an arbitrary supersolution. A finite-domain eigenvalue test offers a direct way to exclude vanishing supersolutions when the limit eigenvalue is strictly below \(f'(0)\). No additional counterexample to that lemma’s conclusion is established here. Nor is the informal uniform smoothing step of Lemma 4.4 fully rebuilt. These limitations are recorded separately from the demonstrated problems.
