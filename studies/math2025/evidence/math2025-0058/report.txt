The setting is over \(\mathbb C\) with rational Chow coefficients. Stable data satisfy \(2g-2+n>0\), and the construction under consideration uses \(n\ge1\) after the introductory treatment of the unmarked case. Integer weights satisfy \[\sum_i a_i=k(2g-2+n).\] Thus \(\mathcal L=(\omega^{\log})^k(-\sum_i a_i x_i)\) has total degree zero. At a vertex \(v\), its multidegree is \[\deg_{k,A}(v)=k(2g(v)-2+n(v))-\sum_{i\text{ at }v}a_i,\] where incident half-edges count in \(n(v)\).

Theorems A and B require a small nondegenerate degree-zero stability condition \(\theta\) (pp. 15–16, 23). Theorem B identifies only the degree-\(g\) part of the mixed-degree class. Theorem C(i) compares this part for two small nondegenerate conditions; C(ii) allows any nondegenerate condition, but concerns degrees \(h>g\) (p. 24). The text explicitly excludes degenerate \(\theta\) from the formula. These restrictions prevent interpreting the full power series as a pure codimension-\(g\) cycle or extending the logarithmic DR interpretation to every large stability condition.

The logarithmic Chow ring is a direct limit under pullback over log modifications. Ordinary Chow injects into it, but proper pushforward back to the original space is generally only a vector-space map, not a ring map (p. 9). Equality of pushforwards therefore does not identify products upstairs.

# Checks of the formula and its proof dependencies

Equation reference: eq:eta, eq:formula. Using incoming divergence, the flow and potential conventions are \[\operatorname{div}(I)=\deg_{k,A}-D,\qquad
 \alpha(\operatorname{head}e)-\alpha(\operatorname{tail}e)=I(e)\ell_e.\] The defining cycle constraints make \(\alpha\) path independent. Equations (17)–(19), p. 22, read \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak L&=\sum_v(D+\deg_{k,A})(v)\alpha(v),&
 \eta&=k^2\kappa_1-\sum_i a_i^2\psi_i,\\
 P^\theta_{g,A}
 &=\exp\!\left[-\tfrac12\bigl(\eta+\Phi(\mathfrak L)\bigr)\right]
       \Phi(\mathfrak P).\end{aligned}\] Adding a constant to \(\alpha\) leaves \(\mathfrak L\) unchanged because both divisors have total degree zero.

## Chern algebra and discrete integration by parts

Theorem 67 (p. 81) writes \(c_1(\widehat{\mathcal L})=a+b\), with \(a=c_1(\mathcal L)\) and \(b=c_1(\mathcal O(\alpha))\). Then \[(a+b)^2-a^2=2ab+b^2=(a+(a+b))b.\] This is exactly the tensor-product Chern factor appearing in equation (45). Its pushforward gives \(\widehat\eta=\eta+\Phi(\mathfrak L)\), with the factor \(1/2\) and the sign in [eq:formula] (#eq:formula) intact. The algebra is verified; the full operational intersection-theoretic justification of (45) remains a dependency.

In Lemma 70 (pp. 84–85), choose \(J\) with \(\operatorname{div}(J)=D\) and set \(I_0=I+J\). Discrete integration by parts gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \sum_v\operatorname{div}(I_0+J)(v)\alpha(v)
 &=\sum_e(I_0(e)+J(e))(\alpha(\operatorname{head}e)-\alpha(\operatorname{tail}e))\\
 &=\sum_e(I_0(e)+J(e))(I_0(e)-J(e))\ell_e\\
 &=\sum_e(I_0(e)^2-J(e)^2)\ell_e .\end{aligned}\] There is one chosen orientation per unoriented edge. Reversing it reverses both flow values and leaves the result unchanged.

Equation reference: eq:residue. For a one-cycle graph, the degree-one residue contribution is \[
 \frac1{2r}\sum_{w=1}^{r-1}w(r-w)=\frac{r^2-1}{12}.\] The standard formulas for \(\sum w\) and \(\sum w^2\) prove this identity. The constant term is \(-1/12\), matching the nonseparating-edge coefficient on pp. 82–84. The operation takes the constant term of the polynomial; it does not evaluate the original residue sum at the undefined value \(r=0\). Independent exact-rational checks for \(1\le r\le20\) agree.

# An explicit genus-zero boundary check

Take \(g=0\), \(n=4\), \(k=0\), \(A=(1,-1,0,0)\). The stability and weight constraints hold. Define, compatibly with contractions, \[\theta(v)=\frac{2g(v)-2+n(v)}4
           -\frac12\mathbf 1_{\{\text{mark }1\text{ lies at }v\}}.\] Its total is \(2/4-1/2=0\). A stable graph of type \((0,4)\) is the no-edge graph or one of three one-edge trees. On a tree orient left to right with mark 1 on the left. Then \(\theta=(-1/4,1/4)\). The strict stability intervals are \[-\tfrac34<D_{\rm left}<\tfrac14,\qquad
 -\tfrac14<D_{\rm right}<\tfrac34,\] so \(D=(0,0)\) is the only integral stable multidegree. Subdividing the edge inserts an exceptional vertex with \(\theta=0\) and required degree \(1\). The two original vertex inequalities still force both degrees to zero, contradicting total degree zero. Thus no extra semistable admissible divisor arises. This proves smallness and nondegeneracy for these data, without relying on a finite search range.

For a partition \(S|S^c\) with \(1\in S\), let \(s=\sum_{i\in S}a_i\) and let the edge length be \(\ell\). The original multidegree is \((-s,s)\); the flow \(I=s\) has that incoming divergence. With \(\alpha_{\rm left}=0\) and \(\alpha_{\rm right}=s\ell\), equation [eq:eta] (#eq:eta) gives \[\mathfrak L=s^2\ell.\] The mod-\(r\) tree weighting with \(D=0\) is zero, so \(\mathfrak P=1\). The coefficients \(s^2\) are respectively \(0,1,1\) on \(12|34\), \(13|24\), and \(14|23\). Consequently the degree-zero part of [eq:formula] (#eq:formula) is \(1\), as required: multidegree-zero bundles on a rational tree are trivial.

There is also a nontrivial first-degree check. On \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}\simeq\mathbb P^1\), each \(\psi_i\) and boundary point has class \(H\) and \(H^2=0\). To see \(\deg\psi_i=1\), start with the family \(\mathbb P^1_x\times\mathbb P^1_t\) and sections \(0,1,\infty,t\), then blow up the three collisions. Every constant section changes self-intersection from \(0\) to \(-1\), and the diagonal from \(2\) to \(-1\). The cotangent-line degree is the negative section self-intersection.

Equation reference: eq:genus0. Thus \[\eta=-\psi_1-\psi_2,\qquad
 \Phi(\mathfrak L)=\delta_{13|24}+\delta_{14|23},\] and \[
 [P^\theta_{0,A}]_1
 =\tfrac12(\psi_1+\psi_2-\delta_{13|24}-\delta_{14|23})=0.\] This agrees with C(ii), since \(1>g=0\), and tests both the sign and the factor \(1/2\).

# A genus-one calculation on chambers and their boundaries

Take \(g=1\), \(n=2\), \(k=0\), \(A=(3,-3)\). Use two rational vertices joined by two edges, one marking on each vertex. Each vertex has three special points; the graph genus is one and the original multidegree is \((-3,3)\).

For clarity, an explicit global small nondegenerate condition for this type is \[\theta(v)=-\frac2{23}(2g(v)-2+n(v))
 +\frac{43}{230}\mathbf1_{\{1\text{ at }v\}}
 -\frac3{230}\mathbf1_{\{2\text{ at }v\}}.\] The total is zero and the terms are additive under contraction. Its values on the two-vertex cycle are \(1/10,-1/10\); on a separating rational tail carrying both markings they are \(2/23,-2/23\). These magnitudes are below half the relevant cut valence, so multidegree zero is stable. Every cut containing a proper nonempty selection of original vertices has a nonintegral stability endpoint. Exceptional-vertex cuts have exactly the equality exceptions permitted by Definition 30. The stable graphs of type \((1,2)\) and their contractions exhaust these cases, giving nondegeneracy here.

Equation reference: eq:interval, eq:chambers. Orient the edges around the cycle, with lengths \(x,y\ge0\) and cycle flow \(C=(1,1)\). All integral flows of the required divergence are \(F=(t+3,t)\), \(t\in\mathbb Z\). Equation (48), p. 86, becomes \[
 0\le(t+3)x+ty\le x+y.\] For \(x+y>0\), setting \(q=3x/(x+y)\) gives \(-q\le t\le1-q\). The generic choice is unique; two choices can occur on a wall. Substitution in Proposition 71 and Theorem 72 yields \[
 \mathfrak L'=
 \begin{cases}
 6x,&0\le x\le y/2,\\
 2(x+y),&y/2\le x\le2y,\\
 6y,&x\ge2y.
 \end{cases}\] Indeed the formula before choosing \(t\) is \(((t+3)^2-(t+3))x+(t^2-t)y\).

At \(x=y/2\), choices \(t=0,-1\) agree; at \(x=2y\), choices \(t=-1,-2\) agree. On either axis the value is zero for both admissible choices; at the origin it is zero for every integer \(t\). Thus generic uniqueness does not conceal an ambiguity in the resulting function.

Equation reference: eq:min. The only acyclic flows with this divergence are \((2,-1)\) and \((1,-2)\). Remark 73 independently gives the identity \[
 \mathfrak L'=2\min(2x,y)+2\min(x,2y),\] which agrees with [eq:chambers] (#eq:chambers) on the entire closed first quadrant. This is an analytic identity, not a conclusion inferred from a sampled grid.

Combining the ordinary nonseparating term with the logarithmic correction gives \(-(x+y)/12-\mathfrak L'/2\). On the half-cone \(x\ge y\), it is \[\begin{cases}
 -13x/12-13y/12,&y\le x\le2y,\\
 -x/12-37y/12,&x\ge2y.
 \end{cases}\] These are the actual coefficients in Figure 4 (p. 24). The figure explicitly draws the coarse space of the stacky cone; the symmetric chamber is accounted for by that convention. Independent exact arithmetic at chamber interiors, both walls, both axes, and the origin agrees with the analytic calculation.
