# Confirmed corrections

## P1. Minor: the avoidance definition uses the base field

Location. Page 5, the opening paragraph of Section 2. The displayed definition says that a \(K_0\)-field \(K\) avoids \(C\) when \(C(K_0)=\varnothing\). With the standing hypothesis this is independent of \(K\).

For a concrete check, let \(K_0=\mathbb Q\) and let \(C\) be \[x^4+y^4+1=0.\] Its smooth projective closure is the plane quartic \(X^4+Y^4+Z^4=0\), which is geometrically integral of genus three. Smoothness follows because its three partial derivatives cannot vanish at a projective point in characteristic zero. It has no rational point, since a sum of fourth powers is positive unless all coordinates vanish. On the other hand, with \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\), the point \((\zeta_8,0)\) lies on \(C(K)\). The printed condition therefore calls this field avoiding although it contains a point of the excluded curve.

Impact, counterevidence and repair. Replace \(C(K_0)\) by \(C(K)\) in this definition. The abstract, the definition of \(T_{C,K_0}\) on p. 2, condition \((\mathrm{III}_C)\) on p. 10, and the opening of Section 4 already use the latter condition. The subsequent proof of Proposition 2.5 also uses it. Thus the isolated incorrect definition must be repaired, but it does not expose a missing step in the construction under the consistently stated intended avoidance condition.

## P2. Minor: the effective claim needs an effective base

Location. Theorem 1.6, p. 2, and Remark 2.16, p. 10. Theorem 1.6 allows an arbitrary characteristic-zero \(K_0\) and asserts a computably axiomatized theory in the language containing a constant for every element of \(K_0\).

Take \(K_0=\mathbb R\) and the same smooth plane quartic, which has no real point and satisfies the geometric assumptions. The full language \(L(\mathbb R)\) is uncountable. More concretely, suppose an ordinary Turing procedure enumerates axioms for the claimed class. Only countably many real constant names occur among its finite output strings. In any model of the claimed companion, select a nonzero \(r\in\mathbb R\) whose name does not occur, and reinterpret \(c_r\) as zero while leaving the field and all mentioned names unchanged. All enumerated sentences retain their truth values, but the required base-field sentence \(c_r\ne0\) is false. This is a direct obstruction to an absolute computable axiomatization of this full class.

Impact, counterevidence and repair. The existence of the model companion over arbitrary \(K_0\) is not contradicted. The effective arguments are valid over \(\mathbb Q\), or over a base with a specified effective presentation and an effectively available field diagram. Over a general base one should state an axiom scheme relative to that diagram. The actual decidable completion in Theorem 4.19 and the number-field oracle equivalence in Theorem 4.21 use effective bases and are unaffected by this boundary correction. A relative scheme must not be called an absolute Turing enumeration without the additional data.

## P3. Minor: bounded top-dimensional degree is insufficient

Location. Fact 2.10, p. 7; the conventions on pp. 4–5. Varieties are expressly allowed to be reduced and nonintegral, and \(\operatorname{pdeg}(X)\) is defined by intersecting with a generic linear subspace of codimension \(\dim X\). This degree ignores lower-dimensional components. Fact 2.10 nevertheless claims a single definable family containing every projective variety of bounded such degree.

Work over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. Fix a line \(\ell\subset\mathbb P^2\) and distinct points \(p_1,\ldots,p_N\) outside it, and give \[X_N=\ell\cup\{p_1,\ldots,p_N\}\] the reduced structure. This is a projective variety permitted by the conventions, and a generic line meets it in precisely one point. Hence \(\operatorname{pdeg}(X_N)=1\) for every \(N\). If the asserted single definable family \(\{V_a\}_{a\in Y}\) existed for \(n=2,m=1\), the definable family \(V_a\setminus\ell\) would have finite fibers of every size \(N\). This contradicts the uniform bound on finite fibers in an algebraically closed field. One may see that bound directly from quantifier elimination: a fixed formula has a fixed finite algebraic complexity, so the number of points in its zero-dimensional fibers is uniformly bounded.

Impact, counterevidence and repair. Restrict Fact 2.10 to pure-dimensional varieties, or bound the sum of the degrees of all irreducible components. Chow varieties then provide the required finite union of parameter families. The graph \(\Gamma\) in Fact 2.11 and in the axiom scheme on p. 9 is irreducible, so this corrected version covers the actual use. The counterexample does not refute the degree bound for graphs or the first-order axiomatization.

## P4. Minor: Weil restriction is indexed by embeddings

Location. Lemma 4.13, p. 17, the displayed decomposition of \(W_F\), where \(F\) is a splitting field and \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)\). The product is indexed by every \(g\in G\). For a non-Galois extension \(L/K\), that repeats the conjugate factors.

Let \(K_0=K=\mathbb Q\), retain the negative Fermat quartic from P1, let \(L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2})\), and let \(V=\mathbb A^1_L\). Thus \(K\models C\mathrm{XF}_\forall\), \(L/K\) is a proper finite extension, and \(V\) is geometrically integral and affine, as required. The irreducible polynomial \(T^3-2\) has discriminant \(-108\), so its splitting field has group \(S_3\) of order six. But \[\operatorname{Res}_{L/K}\mathbb A^1_L\simeq\mathbb A^3_K.\] Consequently \(W_F\) has dimension three, whereas the printed product of six conjugate affine lines has dimension six.

Impact, counterevidence and repair. The correct decomposition is \[W_F\simeq\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Hom}_K(L,F)} V^\sigma_F,\] equivalently a product indexed by \(G/\operatorname{Gal}(F/L)\) after fixing an embedding. This correction preserves Lemma 4.13: a nonconstant map from the product to the hyperbolic curve depends on one factor by Lemma 4.3; that factor is unique, since a map factoring through two independent factors is constant. A map defined over \(K\) would make this factor \(G\)-invariant, but \(G\) acts transitively on more than one embedding. This is impossible. Hence the PAC conclusion in Theorem 4.14 retains its intended proof.

## P5. Minor: elementary extensions preserve avoidance

Location. Corollary 4.15(1), p. 18. For \(K\models C\mathrm{XF}'\) it asserts that a proper elementary extension \(L/K\) has \(C(L)\ne\varnothing\).

Choose any \(K\models C\mathrm{XF}\) for the curve in P1. Such a model exists by the construction, and it also satisfies \(C\mathrm{XF}'\). There is a proper elementary extension: compactness applied to the elementary diagram and a new constant distinct from every element of \(K\) supplies one, since \(K\) is infinite. But \(C(K)=\varnothing\) is a first-order statement over \(K_0\), so elementarity gives \(C(L)=\varnothing\). This satisfies every printed hypothesis and contradicts the stated conclusion.

Impact, counterevidence and repair. The intended word is algebraic. The preceding Theorem 4.14 proves that every proper algebraic extension is PAC, and hence has a point on the geometrically integral curve \(C\). That implication yields \((\mathrm I_C)\) and Corollary 4.15(2). Thus the incorrect displayed corollary needs correction; the intended redundancy of \((\mathrm I_C)\) does not depend on the false elementary-extension assertion.

## P6. Minor: a rational function field is not already a model

Location. The proof of Theorem 4.24, p. 20, states \(K(t)\models C\mathrm{XF}\). The stated argument only proves avoidance.

For any \(K\models C\mathrm{XF}\), let \(s\) be transcendental and set \(t=s^2\). The extension \(K(s)/K(t)\) is proper of degree two. There is no nonconstant rational map from \(\mathbb P^1_K\) to the smooth projective completion of \(C\): after algebraic closure, Riemann–Hurwitz contradicts genus at least two. Properness extends a rational map across the missing points, and a constant \(K\)-rational map into \(C\) would give a point in \(C(K)\). Thus \[C(K(s))=C(K)=\varnothing .\] This proper finite extension of \(K(t)\) violates \((\mathrm I_C)\), so \(K(t)\not\models C\mathrm{XF}\).

Impact, counterevidence and repair. The correct assertion is \(K(t)\models C\mathrm{XF}_\forall\). That is exactly the hypothesis needed for Theorem 4.16 to produce a regular extension \(K'/K(t)\) with \(K'\models C\mathrm{XF}\). Model completeness then gives \(K\preccurlyeq K'\), and regularity retains the desired relative algebraic closure of \(K(t)\). The Hilbertianity conclusion and its subsequent unboundedness application survive.

## P7. Minor: the recalled TP\(_2\) criterion omits inconsistency

Location. Page 21, the paragraph immediately before Corollary 4.29. It defines TP\(_2\) using only \[\varphi(a_\eta,b_{i,j})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
          \eta(i)=j\qquad(\eta:\omega\longrightarrow\omega).
          \tag{P7.1}\] This does not require the rows to be \(k\)-inconsistent.

In fact (P7.1) is exactly the independence property, IP. Given (P7.1), restrict \(\eta(i)\) to \(0\) or \(1\); the instances with \(b_{i,0}\) realize all subsets of \(\omega\). Conversely, an IP array indexed by the countable set \(\omega\times\omega\) realizes every subset of that set in a sufficiently saturated model. In particular it realizes the graph of every function \(\eta\), giving (P7.1).

A concrete example is the ordinary random graph with \(\varphi(x,y)=R(x,y)\). Its extension property and compactness produce all these path patterns. Yet every finite set of positive adjacency instances in a row has a common neighbor. Thus no row of this array is \(k\)-inconsistent for any finite \(k\). The random graph is a simple, hence NTP\(_2\), theory: quantifier elimination and free graph amalgamation show that forking is algebraic dependence, giving local character. This is the ordinary random graph, not the triangle-free Henson graph mentioned elsewhere in the paper.

Impact, counterevidence and repair. Require a fixed finite \(k\ge2\) such that \(\{\varphi(x,b_{i,j}):j<\omega\}\) is \(k\)-inconsistent for each \(i\), together with consistency of every path. Corollary 4.29 actually invokes the stronger PAC-field theorem, rather than deriving TP\(_2\) merely from (P7.1). The definition is wrong, but this example does not disprove TP\(_2\) for the fields constructed in the paper. The cited PAC result is retained as an external dependency.

# Positive checks and proof dependencies

## Axioms and the geometric complexity bound

With avoidance correctly formulated, Proposition 2.5 reduces existential closedness to finite extensions and regular function-field extensions. In characteristic zero, relative algebraic closure makes the function-field presentation geometrically integral. A rational point over that function field is a rational map to \(C\); absence of such a map gives the required Zariski density by existential closedness. Conversely, the finite-extension condition removes the algebraic part of a finitely generated extension.

For Fact 2.11, put \(d=\dim X\), \(e=\operatorname{pdeg}(X)\) and \(c=\operatorname{pdeg}(C)\). A general complete-intersection curve \(D=X\cap H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_{d-1}\) is smooth and avoids the codimension-at-least-two indeterminacy locus. It maps dominantly to \(C\): the hyperplane sections meet a general fiber properly in a nonempty zero-dimensional intersection, by ampleness. Thus its map to \(C\) is finite and separable. The calculation in Section 3 gives \[\operatorname{pdeg}(\Gamma)=e+d\,\deg(D/C)c,\qquad
       \deg(D/C)\le\frac{g(D)-1}{g(C)-1}\le g(D)-1.\] Here \(\beta^2=0\) on the curve factor accounts for the absence of higher mixed terms in the Segre degree. Together with the cited genus bound \(g(D)\le(e-1)^2\), this yields the displayed computable bound \(e+d(e^2-2e)c\). The apparently problematic small degrees do not provide examples: a curve of genus zero or one cannot dominate \(C\). In dimension zero no such dominant map exists at all. No counterexample to this core graph bound was found.

Resolution and the generic-graph criterion reduce the axiom scheme to smooth projective irreducible sources. These are the cases covered by the repair in P3. The characteristic-zero qualifications in the use of generic rational functions and separability are essential.

## Regular amalgamation and the field-theoretic applications

For a map from \(V_1\times V_2\) to a genus-at-least-two curve, finiteness of nonconstant maps from a fixed smooth projective source forces the map to depend on one factor. The factor descends to \(K\) when the original map does: regularity makes \[\overline K(V_i)\cap K(V_1\times V_2)=K(V_i)\] inside the common scalar extension. This supplies the descent implicit in the transition from Lemma 4.3 to 4.4. It justifies the regular tensor-product amalgam avoiding \(C\).

That amalgam gives elementary agreement over relatively algebraically closed subfields. It supports the classification by \(\operatorname{Abs}(M)\), the solvability-predicate quantifier elimination, and equality of model-theoretic and field-theoretic algebraic closure over \(K_0\). In Lemma 4.8 the perfectness hypothesis supplies a primitive element; the compactness embedding lies in the original relative algebraic closure because algebraic elements do not appear in an elementary extension. The two embeddings of algebraic extensions are isomorphisms by the finite root-set argument given there.

The corrected Weil product yields PAC proper algebraic extensions. The regular-extension construction realizes every allowable algebraic part. Over \(\mathbb Q\), excluding roots of irreducible polynomials of degree greater than one computably states \(\operatorname{Abs}(M)=\mathbb Q\). Completeness then makes the resulting consistent recursively axiomatized theory decidable. For number fields, the two searches in Theorem 4.21 halt on complementary alternatives; root tests are effective for the finitely many solvability predicates. No claim to have implemented a Mordell oracle is made.

The finite-group construction of Lemma 5.3 uses the action through \(\alpha:H\to G\), the regular permutation action on the transcendental variables, and the fixed field. The printed reference to \(\beta:H\to G\) is a symbol slip; the specified domains determine \(\alpha\). Taking invariants of \(C(L(\bar t))=C(L)\) proves avoidance. The compatible algebraic closures in Lemma 5.2 remove the inner-automorphism ambiguity in Theorem 5.4. The extensions used there are small enough for the stated monster saturation; no unrestricted cardinal-size embedding claim is needed.

For Example 4.30, Riemann–Hurwitz forces a dominant map between the two genus-two curves to have degree one. The six branch points give at most \(6!=720\) normalized parameter triples. Uniform finiteness gives a parameter-free definition of the finite-point locus: the numerical bound may be used in a pure ring formula. Thus naming the transcendental base does not invalidate the claimed pure-field algebraicity of its parameter triple.

## NSOP\(_4\), SOP\(_3\), and the limits of this check

The NSOP\(_4\) argument constructs the three initial edges in a regular \(\omega\)-free PAC extension, then reflects the middle vertex to obtain a fourth. Strong independence makes \(\operatorname{acl}(Ac_1)\operatorname{acl}(Ac_3)\) relatively algebraically closed. Consequently both two-edge composita are regular over that field and ACF-independent there. Lemma 4.4 applies to their compositum, which therefore avoids \(C\). Each adjacent pair retains its relative algebraic closure, so its \(\operatorname{Sol}_n\) predicates, and hence its quantifier-free type, are retained. A regular extension to a model of \(C\mathrm{XF}\) then realizes the forbidden cycle. The occurrence of \(\operatorname{acl}(Ac_3c_3)\) on p. 29 and the missing prime on \(L'\models C\mathrm{XF}\) in Claim 6.8 are local notation slips; the correct fields are present in the surrounding displays.

The stronger independence relation in Fact 6.6 can be reconstructed by taking a regular tensor-product amalgam over the relatively algebraically closed base and then a regular PAC extension; quantifier elimination supplies stationarity. The weaker-independence theorem in Fact 6.5 is a substantive cited input. Its original full proof was not independently reproduced or fully obtained in this review. The public publisher route provided access information, and attempted author-hosted routes failed. This access limitation is recorded in the auxiliary notes; a citation has not been substituted for an inspected external proof.

For the SOP\(_3\) instance, the real algebraic numbers avoid \(x^4+y^4+1=0\) and are an allowed algebraic part. Positive integers differ by a fourth power in that field. A directed triangle would yield three nonzero fourth powers with sum zero, and division by the third fourth power would produce a point on the excluded curve. The nonzero qualification prevents division by zero. This verifies the given obstruction and its pure-field formulation. Conjecture 7.3 and the proposals in Section 8 are kept as conjectures and questions.
