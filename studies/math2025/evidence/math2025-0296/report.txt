Abstract: This report examines Piotr Pstrągowski’s exact manuscript arXiv:2304.04685v2, associated with DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1669. Two substantive assertions are false as stated. The base-change surjectivity theorem fails for a connective rational square-zero extension, including with a connective even flat input module. The asserted strong monoidal structure on the module filtration fails for two suspensions of the rational Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectrum. Explicit computations establish both failures and isolate the affected conclusions. A narrower connective base-change statement is proved, and the distinction between lax and strong monoidality is essential. The supported assessment is Major for this exact manuscript; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# The construction and the dependencies used

Perfect even \(R\)-modules are generated by \(\Sigma^{2k}R\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\), under finite extensions and retracts (Definition 2.2, p. 7). Thus negative even suspensions are allowed. An even flat module is a filtered colimit of perfect even modules (Proposition 4.3 and Definition 4.4, pp. 30–31); it is homologically even by Remark 4.17. Homotopy-even modules are also homologically even, but the several notions must not be interchanged without their hypotheses.

Equation reference: eq:even, eq:post. Two consequences directly determine the examples: \[\begin{aligned}
 H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{p,q}(R,E)&=
 \begin{cases}\pi_{2q}E,&p=0,\\0,&p\ne0,\end{cases}
 &&\text{if $\pi_*E$ is even},                         \\
 \mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{q}(M)&\simeq\tau_{\ge 2q}M
 &&\text{if $\pi_*R$ is even}.                         \end{aligned}\] These are Corollary 2.37 and Proposition 2.40 on p. 16, respectively. The second statement applies to all \(R\)-modules; it does not require \(M\) to be homologically even.

Theorem 8.1, p. 51, assumes both a connective ring and a connective, homologically even module. It identifies \(H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{0,0}(R,M)\) with \(\pi_0M\) and gives vanishing for \(p>q\). Proposition 4.11, p. 32, makes the connective-envelope assertion only when both \(R\) and \(M\) are connective. These restrictions matter in Problem 1.

# Confirmed findings

Equation reference: eq:base. Problem 1 (Major: the base-change surjectivity region is false).  Theorem 1.10 on p. 6 and Theorem 8.8 on p. 55 assert that, for a right homologically even map \(f:R\to S\) of connective \(E_1\)-rings and an even flat \(R\)-module \(M\), the comparison \[\pi_0S\otimes_{\pi_0R}H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{p,q}(R,M)
 \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{p,q}(S,S\otimes_R M)             \] is surjective for \(p\ge q-1\).

## A connective counterexample satisfying every hypothesis

Let \(R=H\mathbb Q\) and let \[S=R\oplus\Sigma^2R\] be the split square-zero \(R\)-algebra. Equivalently, realize the differential graded rational algebra \(\mathbb Q[e]/(e^2)\), with \(|e|=2\) in homological grading and zero differential. The multiplication is unital and associative; the resulting ring spectrum is connective. The inclusion \(f:R\to S\) is a map of connective \(E_1\)-rings. As a right \(R\)-module, \(S\) is a finite direct sum of even suspensions of \(R\). Hence it is perfect even, even flat, and homologically even, as required. Also \(\pi_0R=\pi_0S=\mathbb Q\), with the identity map on these rings.

Take \(M=R\). This module is both connective and perfect even. At \((p,q)=(0,1)\), equation [eq:even] (#eq:even) identifies [eq:base] (#eq:base) with \[\mathbb Q\otimes_\mathbb Q\pi_2R=0
 \ \longrightarrow\ \pi_2S=\mathbb Q.\] The map is not surjective, although \(0\ge1-1\). The target class is the square-zero generator \(e\). Thus adding connectivity of \(M\) alone does not repair the stated range. This example also has an even flat cofiber \(\Sigma^2R\); a stronger faithful-even-flat assumption would not remove this particular obstruction.

## Why changing only the inequality is insufficient

Keep \(R,S,f\), but set \(M=\Sigma^{-2}R\). Definition 2.2 permits this perfect even, hence even flat, module. At \((p,q)=(0,0)\), the same comparison becomes \[\mathbb Q\otimes_\mathbb Q\pi_0(\Sigma^{-2}R)=0
 \ \longrightarrow\
 \pi_0(\Sigma^{-2}S)=\pi_2S=\mathbb Q.\] This fails even on the Milnor line \(p=q\). It satisfies every printed hypothesis but \(M\) is not connective, a condition absent from Theorems 1.10 and 8.8. Consequently, replacing \(p\ge q-1\) by \(p\ge q\) alone is also insufficient.

## Proof location and dependency

The first sentence of the proof on p. 55 invokes Theorem 8.1 for the \(q=0\) case without its connectivity hypothesis on \(M\). The subsequent choice of an envelope \(M\to E\) with \(C=\operatorname{cofib}(M\to E)\) 2-connective likewise invokes the connective part of Proposition 4.11 without assuming connectivity of \(M\).

There is a separate index issue. The displayed diagram on p. 55 has right column in cohomological degree \(p+1\), while the text assumes \(p\ge q-1\). Even when its other steps apply, this reaches degree \(r=p+1\ge q\), not the advertised degree \(q-1\). It does not reach the first counterexample at \(p=0,q=1\). Both issues are witnessed by actual non-surjective maps, so the finding does not depend merely on a missing explanatory sentence.

## A verified narrower statement and its boundary

A valid consequence of the earlier lemmas is obtained by imposing that \(M\) is connective and claiming surjectivity only for \(p\ge q\), with integer weights. Here is the corrected induction.

For \(q=0\), Theorem 8.1 identifies the only possible group on or above that line with \(\pi_0M\). Connectivity gives \(\pi_0(S\otimes_R M)\cong\pi_0S\otimes_{\pi_0R}\pi_0M\). For \(q>0\), choose the connective envelope \(M\to E\to C\) with \(C\) 2-connective. Both \(C\) and \(E\) are even flat, and \(E\) has even homotopy. The right-module form of Theorem 4.16 implies that \(S\otimes_R E\) also has even homotopy. For \(r\ge q>0\), the two long exact sequences therefore give surjections \[H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{r-1,q}(R,C)\twoheadrightarrow H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{r,q}(R,M),
 \qquad
 H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{r-1,q}(S,S\otimes_R C)
 \twoheadrightarrow H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{r,q}(S,S\otimes_R M).\] The same is true of the first row after tensoring by \(\pi_0S\), by right exactness. Put \(C'=\Sigma^{-2}C\). It is connective and even flat, and \(H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{r-1,q}(R,C)=H_{\mathrm{ev}}^{r-1,q-1}(R,C')\). The induction hypothesis applies because \(r-1\ge q-1\). Naturality and the two surjective rows prove the assertion in degree \(r\). Negative weights vanish for connective homologically even modules by Theorem 8.1 and vanishing in negative cohomological degrees.

This argument verifies a narrower statement, not an optimal replacement and not the originally claimed off-diagonal range. The advertised base-change theorem is itself a main result; its genuine loss of scope supports Major severity. The examples do not disprove the construction of the filtration, the descent theorem, or the connective convergence results.

Problem 2 (Major: the module filtration is not strongly monoidal).  Corollary 3.38, p. 29, claims that for an \(E_n\)-ring \(R\) the module filtration functor \[\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}:\operatorname{Mod}_R(\operatorname{Sp})
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(R)}(\mathrm{Fil}\operatorname{Sp})\] is canonically \(E_{n-1}\)-monoidal. The assertion is also advertised in item (2) after Theorem 1.3 on p. 3. Section 1.1, p. 7, explicitly distinguishes monoidal (strongly monoidal) from lax monoidal functors.

## An explicit failure of the tensor comparison

Use \(R=H\mathbb Q\), which is an \(E_\infty\)-ring, and let \(M=N=\Sigma R\). These are legitimate modules in the claimed domain; the statement imposes no evenness restriction on them. Write \(f_j(X)\) for the filtered object equal to \(X\) in weights \(k\le j\) and zero in weights \(k>j\), as in Notation 3.14, p. 21. Its tensor product is Day convolution. By [eq:post] (#eq:post), \[A:=\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(R)=f_0(R),\qquad
 \mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(\Sigma R)=f_0(\Sigma R)=\Sigma A .\] These last objects are free suspensions of the unit \(A\)-module. Their relative tensor product is therefore \[\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(M)\otimes_A\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(N)
 \simeq \Sigma^2 A=f_0(\Sigma^2R).\] In contrast, \[\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(M\otimes_R N)
 =\mathrm{Fil}_{\mathrm{ev}/R}^{*}(\Sigma^2R)=f_1(\Sigma^2R).\] At weight \(1\), the comparison has source zero and target \(\Sigma^2H\mathbb Q\), whose \(\pi_2\) is \(\mathbb Q\). It cannot be an equivalence. In fact the two filtered objects are inequivalent, so choosing a different comparison morphism cannot supply the asserted strong structure. The filtrations in this example are complete; completion does not eliminate the nonzero weight-\(1\) discrepancy.

## Impact and repair boundary

Theorem 3.37 on p. 28 asserts a lax symmetric monoidal functor on algebra–module pairs. The counterexample is consistent with that weaker assertion: a map \(0\to\Sigma^2H\mathbb Q\) exists, but is not invertible. The passage to the fixed-\(R\) fiber in the proof of Corollary 3.38 does not turn a lax comparison into an equivalence. The construction therefore does not justify the strong conclusion printed in the corollary.

A revision to lax \(E_{n-1}\)-monoidality, together with the corresponding correction in the introduction, is compatible with Theorem 3.37 and the example. A strong theorem would need additional restrictions or a different filtration; no maximal subcategory on which it holds is certified here. This is Major because an advertised structural conclusion fails on elementary objects in its stated domain, independently of the practical length of a textual correction. No failure of the lax multiplicative construction is claimed.
