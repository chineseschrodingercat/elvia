# Scope of the principal statements

The setting is a connected, simply connected, simple complex algebraic group \(G\), with a fixed maximal torus \(H\), finite Weyl group \(W\), weight lattice \(P\), and coroot lattice \(Q^\vee\). The coefficients are complexified. The localized affine-Grassmannian group carries the Pontryagin product, not the ordinary product on the affine flag \(K\)-group. Theorem 2.12 gives a dense embedding into the semi-infinite group whose image consists of finite Schubert combinations. Theorem 3.11 and Corollary 3.13 instead compare that semi-infinite group with the completed, localized quantum group.

This distinction is explicit on pages 2–4, 20–24, 29, and 60. Remark 1.28 further distinguishes the transported ring identity \([\mathcal O_{\mathbf Q_G}]\) from a hypothetical class of the entire ind-scheme’s structure sheaf. Consequently, the infinite positive-twist formulas below do not contradict the finite product statement for Schubert classes in Corollary 4.22. An isomorphism of the uncompleted source with the entire completed target is not what the precise statement asserts.

# Checked reasoning

## Localization and independence of choices

Write \(x_w=[\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Gr}_w}]\). Theorem 1.8, page 11, states \[x_w\odot x_{t_\beta}=x_{wt_\beta},
 \qquad w\in W_{\rm af}^{-},\quad
 \langle\beta,\alpha_i\rangle<0\ \text{for every }i.\] The proof uses the length-additivity and left \(W\)-invariance of the relevant Demazure element, then projects from the smash product to its translation subalgebra. The distinction between multiplication before projection and the commutative Pontryagin product is maintained in equation (1.8).

Conditional on this translation identity, the localization does not kill a nonzero finite Schubert combination: right translation of the distinct basis indices is injective. The independence of \[h_i=x_{s_it_\beta}\odot x_{t_\beta}^{-1}\] can be checked by comparing two choices \(\beta,\beta'\) with their common sum \(\beta+\beta'\), which remains strictly antidominant. Similarly, if \(\gamma=\beta_1-\beta_2=\beta'_1-\beta'_2\), then \(\beta_1+\beta'_2=\beta'_1+\beta_2\); multiplication of numerator and denominator by the corresponding translation classes proves choice-independence of \(t_\gamma\). Every coroot can be expressed as such a difference by choosing the subtracted coroot sufficiently antidominant. No additional boundary case with a merely weakly antidominant denominator was inserted into the theorem.

## Cyclic-module endomorphism comparison

Pages 26–30 give the transported Demazure action and its product compatibility. For \(i\in I\), \(D_i^\#\xi=\xi\) is equivalent to finite-reflection invariance of \(\xi\); this makes multiplication by \(\xi\) commute with \(D_i^\#\). Invariance under all finite simple reflections likewise gives commutation with \(D_0^\#\) through the highest-root reflection, as calculated in Lemma 2.4.

The comparison in Theorem 2.12 is consequently more than equality on one arbitrary vector. Both \[\Theta_i=e^{-\varpi_i}(1-h_i\odot)
 \quad\text{and}\quad \Xi(-\varpi_i)\] commute with the indicated Hecke and translation actions, and the module is cyclic by Lemma 2.8. Thus equality on its cyclic vector implies equality everywhere. The \(j=i\) calculation on page 30 uses \[D_i e^{-\varpi_i}
 =e^{-\varpi_i+\alpha_i}D_i+
   \frac{e^{-\varpi_i}-e^{-\varpi_i+\alpha_i}}{1-e^{\alpha_i}},\] whose second coefficient is \(e^{-\varpi_i}\). The first term vanishes when applied to the indicated difference of Schubert classes. This verifies the displayed local algebra, conditional on the stated geometric action and cyclicity inputs. No invalid cancellation was found in this comparison.

## Independent rank-one calculation

For \(G=SL(2)\), set \(z=e^\varpi\), \(t=t_{\alpha^\vee}\), and order the Schubert basis as \(e,s\). At \(q=1\), the positive and negative twists on pages 31–32 have matrices \[M=\frac1{1-t}
 \begin{pmatrix}z&zt\\z^{-1}&z^{-1}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 N=\begin{pmatrix}z^{-1}&-zt\\-z^{-1}&z\end{pmatrix}.\] Direct multiplication gives \(MN=NM=I\). Hence \[H=I-zN=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&z^2t\\1&1-z^2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad H^2-(1-z^2)H-z^2tI=0.\] These are rational identities over \(\mathbb C(z,t)\), and also identities in the formal completion because \(1-t\) is invertible there. The reviewer-authored SymPy calculation independently returned zero for all three matrix residuals.

In particular, at \(z=1\) the relation becomes \(H^2=tI\). This gives a useful nonequivariant boundary check on the signs. Applying negative translations recovers the two finite products on page 32. The same computation explains why \(N\) is finite but \(M\) contains \(\sum_{m\geq0}t^m\); the latter belongs to the completed target. No numerical sampling or analytic convergence assumption is involved.

The duplicated argument in one intermediate display on page 31 is resolved by the immediately preceding two distinct twist formulas, the following two product formulas, and source lines 1277–1288. It is not treated as a separate substantive problem.

## Completion and the final finite-rank step

The definition \(qK_H(\mathcal B)=K_H(\mathcal B)[[Q^\vee_+]]\) on page 23 and the formal coefficient description on page 20 give the coefficientwise completed ring \(R=\mathbb C[P][[Q_1,\ldots,Q_r]]\), followed by localization in the Novikov monomials. Ordinary algebraic tensor products should not be silently interchanged with this completion.

The algebraic mechanism needed at the end of Theorem 3.11 can be made explicit. For a continuous \(R\)-linear map between finite free modules, if its reduction modulo \((Q_1,\ldots,Q_r)\) is invertible, write its matrix \(F=F_0+\Delta\). Then \[F^{-1}=\sum_{m\geq0}(-F_0^{-1}\Delta)^mF_0^{-1}.\] Each coefficient has finitely many contributing terms. This proves invertibility before localization, and localization preserves it. The classical quotient comparison in Proposition 1.29 is the relevant input; density alone, without the completed-module structure, would not suffice. This check explains the finite-rank step under that coefficientwise interpretation. It is not a complete verification of the \(q\)-dependent convergence and specialization arguments used to construct the map in the first place.

# Geometric dependencies and historical issues

Pages 45–46 identify the geometric inputs. Theorem 4.13, pages 56–57, supplies rational singularities; Corollary 4.15 and Proposition 4.16 then support the Schubert-class comparison in Theorem 4.17. Corollaries 4.20–4.22 combine that comparison with Theorem 2.12 to obtain the Peterson-type map and finite quantum products. This dependency was traced, but the full transversal-slice construction in Sections 3.2 and 4.2–4.3 was not independently audited.

The footnote on page 3 explicitly attributes gaps to earlier versions and explains the separate normality treatment in the author’s Frobenius splitting of Schubert varieties of semi-infinite flag manifolds, Forum of Mathematics, Pi 9 (2021), e5, cited as [34]. The current manuscript imports that result in Theorem 4.4. This is retained as a historical issue acknowledged by the author; this review neither relabels it a demonstrated current defect nor claims to have independently certified the entire repair.
