Abstract: Three defects are established in the proof as written in arXiv:2404.07138v1: the local nodal-stability corollary is false on its specified half-tube; the purported smoothed metric remains discontinuous; and the stated variational decomposition is not a direct sum and does not have its claimed orthogonality. These are Major defects in core dependencies, although explicit repairs preserve the proposed strategy. They do not constitute a counterexample to the main existence theorem. Findings are restricted to the exact preprint, with no claim about the uninspected publisher version. The recovery visually checked all 17 pages of this source.

Title: Steklov Nodal Constructions: Initial Technical Review

# Identity, version and scope

The reviewed work is Alberto Enciso, Angela Pistoia and Luigi Provenzano, Nonexistence of Courant-type nodal domain bounds for eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, arXiv:2404.07138v1, 10 April 2024; corresponding journal DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1722. The bound 17-page PDF has SHA256 prefix `11db23b7960a94fdd`. The publisher records acceptance on 27 May 2025 and publication on 9 October 2025. The v1 preprint is not asserted to be equivalent to that later version.

All extracted pages, both appendices and references were read. The primary checks cover the product sloshing spectrum, the spectral ordering as \(\beta\to0\), Corollary 2.2, the two min–max bounds, the smoothing step, and the transition from nodal hypersurfaces to boundary nodal counts. Formula locations were additionally retained through raw extraction of pp. 7–13. The recovery inspected all source page images, including Figure 1 and both appendices.

The counterexamples below use \(n=3\), a connected smooth rounded cylinder containing \([-2,2]\times\overline D^2\) with a cylindrical side near this product, and \(\Sigma=\{0\}\times\overline D^2\). The ends are rounded outside this product. This is a smooth compact manifold with boundary; \(\Sigma\) is connected, separating, has nonempty boundary, and meets the manifold boundary transversally. Use the smooth product metric \(dt^2+\beta^2|dy|^2\) on the cylindrical part, extended smoothly over the ends. These examples do not rely on disconnected manifolds or an empty-boundary convention.

# Problem 1: nodal stability fails on the specified half-tube (Major)

## Source claim and a counterexample satisfying its hypotheses

Corollary 2.2 on p. 7 claims that every sufficiently small \(C^1\) perturbation of any of the first \(K\) sloshing eigenfunctions, restricted to \(\Omega_{1/2}\), has a nodal component isotopic to \(\Sigma\). Its proof asserts \(w_k(0,y)=0\) for all these modes.

Use the separation label \(\ell\) from (2.7), avoiding the inconsistent \(k\) versus \(k-1\) indexing. The \(\ell=2\) ground mode is \[w(t,y)=c\,I_0(\pi\beta|y|)\cos(\pi(t+1))
       =-c\,I_0(\pi\beta|y|)\cos(\pi t),\qquad c>0.\] Here \(I_0\) is the modified Bessel function; its power series has positive coefficients, and the radial equation is \(\Delta_y I_0(\pi\beta r)=\pi^2\beta^2 I_0(\pi\beta r)\). Thus this is precisely a positive transverse ground state times the second Neumann cosine. Choose \(c\) to impose the paper’s boundary normalization.

This mode belongs to the first three relevant modes for \(\beta=1/100\). Indeed, the disk’s first nonconstant transverse Steklov eigenvalue is \(1/\beta=100\), and adding a nonnegative potential can only increase it. The constant test function bounds the first three ground branches by \[\mu_0^\ell(\beta)\leq\frac{\pi^2\ell^2\beta}{8}
 \leq\frac{9\pi^2}{800}<100,\qquad1\leq\ell\leq3 .\] The ground branches strictly increase with the potential, by applying the smaller-potential Rayleigh quotient to a normalized minimizer of the larger-potential problem. Hence the asserted simplicity and low-mode ordering hold. Taking \(K=3\) includes \(\ell=2\) under either zero-mode indexing convention appearing in the manuscript.

On the whole closed half-tube \(|t|\leq1/2\), \(w\leq0\). For any proposed stability radius \(\delta>0\), put \(u=w-\eta\) with \(0<\eta<\delta\). Then \(\|u-w\|_{C^1}=\eta\), but \(u<0\) throughout \(\Omega_{1/2}\). Its nodal set is empty. This directly refutes the quantified corollary. The cosine is nonzero at \(t=0\) for every positive even \(\ell\), and its derivative there vanishes; the proof’s displayed central-slice calculation is also false.

## Dependency, repair and limits

This is the nodal-persistence input used in Step 5 to conclude Theorem 1.2 for the first \(K\) modes. Its failure is therefore Major under a core-dependency criterion, without claiming the existence theorem is false.

A precise repair is to work on \(\Omega_\tau\) with, for example, \(\tau=3/4\). For every \(\ell\geq1\), choose a zero \(t_\ell=(2m+1)/\ell-1\) closest to zero. Then \(|t_\ell|\leq1/2<\tau\), and \(|\partial_t w_0^\ell(t_\ell,y)|
=(\pi\ell/2)v_0^\ell(y)\) is uniformly positive on the compact slice. For finitely many modes choose disjoint local collars as needed and a common positive stability tolerance. Boundary-preserving isotopy applies to these transverse slices, each isotopic to \(\Sigma\). The paper already states convergence in \(C^r(\Omega_\tau)\) for every fixed \(\tau<1\) on p. 13, so this repair has the needed convergence input. It changes the false corollary and its application; it is not a verification of the corollary as printed.

# Problem 2: the proposed smoothing does not produce a smooth metric (Major)

Step 4 on p. 8 defines a smooth factor \(\rho_{\varepsilon,h}\) tending to \(1\) on \(\Omega\) and \(\varepsilon\) on \(\Omega^c\), but then sets \[g_{\varepsilon,h}=\rho_{\varepsilon,h}^2g_\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 g_\varepsilon=\begin{cases}g&\text{on }\Omega,\\
                         \varepsilon^2g&\text{on }\Omega^c.
             \end{cases}\] For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), at an interior interface point the two traces are \(\rho^2g\) and \(\rho^2\varepsilon^2g\). Smoothness and the stated positive lower bound on \(\rho\) make these unequal. Multiplication by a smooth positive scalar cannot remove this jump. For example, \(\varepsilon=1/2\) and an interface value \(\rho=3/4\) give coefficients \(9/16\) and \(9/64\).

Away from the transition strip on \(\Omega^c\), the displayed family equals \(\varepsilon^4g\), and tends to that value, rather than the target \(\varepsilon^2g\). Thus both asserted properties of this family—smoothness and convergence to the stated discontinuous metric—fail. Proposition 2.4 cannot be invoked for the written family, and Step 5 has not produced the smooth metric required by the main theorem. This is a Major construction defect, even though its likely correction is short.

Replace the definition by \(\widetilde g_{\varepsilon,h}=\rho_{\varepsilon,h}^2g\), using the original smooth metric, and choose \(\varepsilon\leq\rho_{\varepsilon,h}\leq1\) globally. The resulting metrics are smooth, agree with \(g\) on an interior tube, and converge to \(g_\varepsilon\) for fixed \(\varepsilon\). Uniform ellipticity at fixed \(\varepsilon\), convergence of the volume and boundary weights, compact trace embedding, and elliptic estimates on the unchanged interior tube give the appropriate setting for the cited spectral-approximation argument. This verifies the corrected geometric construction; it does not establish that the authors adopted this correction in the later journal version.

# Problem 3: the lower-bound decomposition and auxiliary estimate are invalid (Major)

## Failure of the stated direct sum and orthogonality

On p. 11, \(H_1\) is defined by harmonicity on \(\Omega^c\) alone. The claimed direct sum (4.6) and orthogonality (4.8) then fail. On the rounded cylinder described in Section 1, take \[h(t,y)=(t-1)_+ .\] Use the flat product metric on the whole rounded domain. This function lies in \(H^1(M)\), is zero on \(\Omega\), and is harmonic separately on the two components of \(\Omega^c\). It has zero trace on \(\partial_L\Omega\) and is approximable in \(H^1(\Omega^c)\) by functions vanishing near that interface. Consequently \(h\in H_1\cap H_2\) under the printed definitions, although \(h\ne0\).

Taking \(\psi=\psi'=h\) in (4.8) gives \(\int_M|\nabla h|^2>0\), not zero. Already the subcylinder \((1,2)\times D^2\) contributes \(\pi\beta^2>0\). The missing condition is the homogeneous Neumann condition on \(\partial_V\Omega^c\), which is imposed in the construction (4.7) but omitted from the definition of \(H_1\).

## The unweighted norm bound cannot hold uniformly

Equation (4.11), p. 12, also bounds the full \(\|\psi\|_{H^1(\Omega^c)}^2\) by a constant times the inside boundary norm of \(\psi_1\). The low-Rayleigh restriction does not supply such an \(\varepsilon\)-uniform bound.

Let \(z\in C_c^\infty(\Omega^c)\) be nonzero and set \(\psi=1+c\varepsilon^{-(n-2)/2}z\). Its mixed harmonic extension part is \(\psi_1=1\). All boundary traces of \(\psi\) equal one, while its weighted energy is \(c^2\int|\nabla z|^2\), independent of \(\varepsilon\). Choosing \(c\) sufficiently small gives a uniformly bounded Rayleigh quotient below the stated threshold. It can also be included in the low-energy trial subspaces: adjoin the finitely many extended sloshing modes, use their positive spectral margin, and note that the compactly supported bump is energy-orthogonal to their harmonic exterior extensions. Nevertheless the unweighted exterior gradient norm diverges as \(\varepsilon^{-(n-2)}\), whereas the right side of (4.11) is fixed. Thus the displayed estimate cannot serve as the asserted uniform input.

## A replacement lower-bound argument

Define \(H_1\) instead by the weak mixed problem: harmonicity on \(\Omega^c\) together with zero exterior normal flux on \(\partial_V\Omega^c\). Write \(\psi=a+b\), where \(a\) is the mixed harmonic extension of \(\psi|_\Omega\) and \(b\in H_2\). Then energy orthogonality is valid.

For a low-Rayleigh vector let \(E_\varepsilon(\psi)\leq L B_\varepsilon(\psi)\), with fixed \(L\), and put \[A=\|a\|_{L^2(\partial_V\Omega)}^2,\qquad
 S=\|a\|_{L^2(\partial_V\Omega^c)}^2,\qquad
 T=\|b\|_{L^2(\partial_V\Omega^c)}^2.\] The valid extension estimate from Appendix B gives \[S\leq C\big(\|\nabla a\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2+A\big)
   \leq C(LB_\varepsilon+A).\] The Steklov–Dirichlet inequality for \(b\) and orthogonality give \(\varepsilon^{n-1}T\leq C\varepsilon B_\varepsilon\). The boundary triangle inequality now yields \[B_\varepsilon^{1/2}
 \leq (A+\varepsilon^{n-1}S)^{1/2}
           +C\varepsilon^{1/2}B_\varepsilon^{1/2}.\] Absorbing the small terms, for \(n\geq3\), \(B_\varepsilon\leq(1+C\sqrt\varepsilon)A\). The restriction map is injective on such a trial subspace (if \(A=0\), the same estimate forces zero boundary norm). The min–max principle therefore yields the desired lower bound \(\sigma_k^\varepsilon\geq(1-C\sqrt\varepsilon)\mu_k\).

This supplies a replacement for the broken estimates and uses the actual boundary condition. The printed decomposition, orthogonality and uniform estimate are not thereby made true. Their central role in Proposition 2.3 and then Theorem 1.2 supports Major severity; whole-paper falsity is not required.

# Other checks, counterevidence and outcome

The conformal factors in the Rayleigh quotient (2.3) are correct: energy scales by \(\varepsilon^{n-2}\) and boundary mass by \(\varepsilon^{n-1}\). This explains the \(n\geq3\) restriction. For the upper bound, use the span including the zero mode, \(\{w_0,\ldots,w_k\}\), with mixed exterior extensions. The exterior energy is \(O(\varepsilon^{n-2})\) and the boundary Gram matrix tends to the identity. The source alternates between zero-based and one-based labels; this needs consistent correction but is not counted as a separate Major finding.

Appendix A’s completeness argument is valid after normalizing the zero cosine: for each \(\ell\) the transverse traces are complete, and Fubini followed by the countability of the \(\ell\) set gives a common full-measure set on which all cosine coefficients vanish. Appendix B’s extension estimates use nonempty boundary pieces and connected components meeting the Dirichlet part. The construction requires \(\partial\Sigma\ne\varnothing\). The phrase with boundary is read in that intended sense here; no additional finding is based on allowing a closed separating hypersurface.

The distinction between nodal-set components and nodal domains is retained; in particular the exact-\(N\) wording in Theorem 1.1 is not treated as an assertion of a single sign domain for a nonconstant boundary eigenfunction. The finite-\(K\) construction also does not refute an asymptotic bound on one fixed metric, as the authors explicitly explain.

The three Major findings concern invalid core steps in the exact v1. Counterexamples and repair limits have been checked; no counterexample to the main existence theorem is asserted.
