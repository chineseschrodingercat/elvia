Title: Scientific initial review of
Growth in linear groups

# Identity, summary, and scope

The paper is by Sean Eberhard, Brendan Murphy, László Pyber, and Endre Szabó, associated with Duke Mathematical Journal (2025), DOI 10.1215/00127094-2024-0031 (https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2024-0031). The reviewed artifact is the 39-page author manuscript arXiv:2107.06674v3, dated 26 April 2024 [paper]; all page references below are its one-based PDF pages, which agree with its printed numbering. The journal typesetting was not independently obtained. The arXiv record identifies v3 as incorporating referees’ corrections; this does not establish byte-level agreement with the journal edition.

Assessment: Major. The most substantial finding is a counterexample to the prime-field tensor decomposition required by Theorem 9.6. It also occurs for the precise module used in the diameter proof. Consequently that proof has a false structural input, although no counterexample to the diameter conclusion is established. A separate rank-one counterexample contradicts the stated step bound in Theorem 1.2. Two local failures arise because symmetry is explicitly defined without requiring the identity: the affine conjugating lemma has an odd-power obstruction, and Proposition 8.5 can miss a singleton nontrivial root image.

The full manuscript text and supplied TeX were available. The review concentrated on definitions, the finite-field reduction, normality of the resulting subgroups, the soluble-to-nilpotent argument, and the diameter application. External classification, representation, and linearization theorems were not completely rederived. Positive checks and unresolved steps are separated below.

# Necessary conventions

A symmetric set means \(A=A^{-1}\), not \(1\in A\), by Section 4.1, p. 10. The notation \(A^r\) denotes products of exactly \(r\) elements, whereas \(A^{\pm r}\) allows the identity and inverses. A section is \(H/N\); its trace is the image of \(A\cap H\), not an arbitrary projection from a larger group (p. 7). Lower central series are indexed from \(\gamma_1\), and Remark 1.3(a), p. 3, permits \(H=\gamma_n(\Gamma)\). Quasirandomness is measured by the least dimension of a nontrivial complex representation. These distinctions are operative in the counterexamples.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Major: the prime-field bounded-chain assertion is false).  Definition 9.4(c), p. 34, requires every irreducible constituent \(W\) of a bounded chain to have the form \[W\simeq X_1\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}X_l,\] with \(X_i\) an irreducible \(\mathbb F_pS_i\)-module for the quasisimple factors of \(G/O_p(G)\). Theorem 9.6, on the same page, asserts the existence of such a chain for every \(G\)-invariant section of every normalized \(p\)-subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}_n(F)\), once \(\deg_{\mathbb C}G\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(n\).

#### Admissible family.

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, \(q=p^2\), and set \[L=\operatorname{SL}_2(q)\times\operatorname{SL}_2(q),\qquad
 P=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_2&X\\0&I_2\end{pmatrix}:X\in M_2(\mathbb F_q)\right\}.\] Embed \(L\) block diagonally in \(\operatorname{GL}_4(q)\), and put \(G=P\rtimes L\). Then \(P\) is an elementary abelian \(p\)-group of dimension \(8\) over \(\mathbb F_p\), and \(G\) acts on its additive matrix coordinate by \(X\mapsto aXb^{-1}\). Conjugation by \(P\) itself is trivial. Thus this is a normalized \(p\)-subgroup within exactly the ambient dimension \(n=4\) required by the theorem.

#### Irreducibility over the stated field.

The \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra generated by \(\operatorname{SL}_2(q)\) in \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) contains \[(I+tE_{12})-I=tE_{12},\qquad
 (I+tE_{21})-I=tE_{21}\quad(t\in\mathbb F_q).\] Products give \(tE_{11}\) and \(tE_{22}\), so it contains all of \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\). An invariant \(\mathbb F_p\)-subspace of \(P=M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) is therefore stable under all left and right multiplications by matrices over \(\mathbb F_q\). In particular it is \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear and a two-sided ideal of the simple matrix algebra. It is consequently either zero or \(P\). Both factors of \(L\) act nontrivially.

It follows that \(O_p(G)=P\), \(\operatorname{Sol}(G)=PZ(L)\), and \([G,\operatorname{Sol}(G)]=[P,L]=P\). Here \([P,L]\ne0\), and irreducibility makes it all of \(P\); \(L\) is perfect. In particular \(G\) is perfect and the section \(P/[P,P]\) used on p. 36 is precisely this module.

#### No tensor product of the prescribed dimensions.

Every nontrivial representation of \(\operatorname{SL}_2(p^2)\) over \(\mathbb F_p\) has dimension at least \(3\). One elementary verification in dimensions at most \(2\) is as follows. The upper unipotent subgroup \(U\simeq C_p^2\) has image in a \(p\)-subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(p)\), whose order is at most \(p\). Its kernel therefore contains a nonidentity \(u(t)\). Diagonal conjugation gives all \(u(s^2t)\), \(s\ne0\). Squares span \(\mathbb F_q\) additively when \(p\) is odd: for instance \(x=((x+1)/2)^2-((x-1)/2)^2\). Hence the kernel contains \(U\), then its opposite by normality, and hence all of \(\operatorname{SL}_2(q)\). The one-dimensional case also follows from perfectness.

If \(P\) had the decomposition in Definition 9.4(c), both tensor factors would be nontrivial, so \[8=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}P=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}X_1\,\dim_{\mathbb F_p}X_2\ge 3\cdot3=9,\] a contradiction. Because \(P\) is irreducible, refining the chain cannot remove the obstruction. It fails for every value of the proposed boundedness constant.

#### Arbitrarily strong quasirandomness.

The example cannot be excluded by increasing \(K(4)\). A nontrivial complex representation of \(\operatorname{SL}_2(q)\) is nontrivial on \(U\), since conjugates of \(U\) generate the group. A nontrivial \(U\)-character has a diagonal-torus orbit of size \((q-1)/2\), so the representation has dimension at least this large. Every nontrivial representation of \(G=P\rtimes L\) restricts nontrivially to \(L\): killing \(L\) would also kill \([P,L]=P\). Thus \(\deg_{\mathbb C}G\ge(q-1)/2\to\infty\), with \(n=4\) fixed.

#### Faulty step, impact, and repair boundary.

The proof on p. 35 passes from tensor products over an algebraic closure to tensor products of irreducible modules over \(\mathbb F_p\). Restriction of scalars does not preserve that asserted tensor factorization. The dimension calculation above is a direct obstruction, not an OCR issue: the rendered definition and proof both specify \(\mathbb F_p\). Theorem 9.6 is invoked on p. 36 for \(P/[P,P]\); the present semidirect family makes this dependency explicit. This is a demonstrated invalid derivation closely tied to the diameter contribution.

A reformulation over appropriate endomorphism fields or with coupled Frobenius constituents would need a new bounded-chain statement and a revised growth argument. Merely changing a tensor subscript does not verify the remaining steps. In particular the proof of Lemma 9.7 on p. 36 also asserts that each vector of an isotypic module generates one simple copy. For \(S=\operatorname{SL}_2(p)\), \(X=\mathbb F_p^2\), and \(a=(e_1,e_2)\in X\oplus X\), the generated module is all of \(X\oplus X\): the matrix algebra generated by \(S\) sends the ordered basis to arbitrary ordered pairs. This refutes that proof sentence, but does not itself refute Lemma 9.7. The trivial-factor branch also uses generators only of \(G/O_p(G)\) as though they generated \(G\). For the natural action on \(V=\mathbb F_p^3\) of \[G=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\v&1\end{pmatrix}:a\in\operatorname{SL}_2(p),\
 v\in\mathbb F_p^{1\times2}\right\},\] the chain \(0<\mathbb F_pe_3<V\) satisfies the definition with \(B=1\), and \(V=[V,G]\). Lifts chosen in \(\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{SL}_2(p),1)\) generate the quotient, but their commutator images lie in \(\mathbb F_pe_1+\mathbb F_pe_2\), so the displayed equality with \(V\) on p. 36 fails. This is another invalid proof step, not a counterexample to the lemma’s final bound. No replacement proof of the diameter theorem is certified here.

Problem 2 (Major, confined to the admitted rank-one boundary).  Theorem 1.2, pp. 2–3, allows \(n=1\), gives nilpotency step at most \(n-1\), and says \(H=1\) in characteristic zero. These requirements are incompatible with its uniform coset-covering bound.

Take \(F=\mathbb Q\) and \[A_N=\{2^j:-N\le j\le N\}\subset\operatorname{GL}_1(\mathbb Q).\] This set is finite, nonempty, symmetric, and even contains the identity. Its cardinalities are \[|A_N|=2N+1,\qquad |A_N^3|=6N+1<3|A_N|.\] Thus \(K=3\) is fixed. The characteristic-zero clause gives \(H=1\), while step at most zero gives \(\Gamma/H=1\), hence \(\Gamma=1\). Covering \(A_N\) requires \(2N+1\) singleton cosets, contradicting \(3^{O_1(1)}\) as \(N\to\infty\).

No \(n\ge2\) exclusion was located, and Remark 1.3(a) rules out interpreting a nontrivial abelian group as step zero. The finite-field reduction on p. 14 uses \(e_{12}\), and Section 8.3 uses \(\gamma_n(T)=1\) for an abelian torus; both also reveal the intended \(n\ge2\) regime. Restricting the theorem to \(n\ge2\), or replacing the step bound by \(\max(1,n-1)\), addresses this boundary. In rank one the latter correction is verified directly by taking \(\Gamma=\langle A\rangle\), \(H=1\). The counterexample does not disprove the asserted \(n\ge2\) structure theorem. Its Major classification concerns a false quantified guarantee in the main theorem, not an alleged failure throughout the intended range.

Problem 3 (Minor: exact odd powers in the affine conjugating lemma).  Lemma 1.8, p. 6, restated as Lemma 7.11, pp. 21–22, claims \(A^{7d}\supseteq[V,G]\) for a symmetric \(G\)-invariant generating set of an abelian \(V\), under the tripling and quasirandomness hypotheses.

Let \(V=C_2\) additively, \(G=1\), \(d=1\), \(K=2\), and \(A=\{v\}\) for the nonzero element \(v\). The trivial group is generated by its identity and has \(\deg_{\mathbb C}G=\infty\), as defined on p. 11. Every hypothesis holds, but \(A^7=\{v\}\) does not contain \([V,G]=\{0\}\).

In the p. 22 proof, \(B=AG\) need not contain \(G\). The inference placing a commutator in \(B^6B\) therefore uses an absent hypothesis. Since \(G\subseteq B^2\), the already established conjugate inclusion instead puts each commutator in \(B^6B^2\cap V=A^8\). Rhemtulla’s displayed identity then gives \(A^{8d}\supseteq[V,G]\). This is a verified local repair; alternatively \(1\in A\) restores the printed \(7d\) argument. Later adjustable exponents must be changed accordingly. No new main-theorem counterexample follows from this parity defect alone.

Problem 4 (Minor: singleton nontrivial root images evade Proposition 8.5).  Proposition 8.5, p. 26, assumes symmetry and \(\langle A^\pi\rangle=T\), but not \(1\in A\). It concludes either a nontrivial root image of cardinality strictly greater than one and at most a specified bound, or uniformly bounded-power coverage of \(\langle(A^2\cap V)^T\rangle\).

For odd primes \(p>7\), take the affine group \(\Sigma=\mathbb F_p\rtimes\{\pm1\}\subset\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\), with \(U=V=\mathbb F_p\) the translations and \(T=\{\pm1\}\). Then \([V,U]=1\) and \(C_V(T)=1\). Let \[A=\{x\mapsto -x+b:b=-1,0,1\}.\] Each element is an involution, so \(A\) is symmetric; its projection generates \(T\). Moreover \(|A|=3\), \(|A^3|=7\), so \(K=3\) is valid. The unique nontrivial root has \(\chi(A)=\{-1\}\), of cardinality one, so alternative (a) fails.

The set \(A^2\) contains translation by \(1\), whence \(\langle(A^2\cap V)^T\rangle=V\). An odd power contains only reflections; an even \(m\)-th power has at most \(2m+1\) translations. No uniform exponent can cover all \(p\) translations as \(p\to\infty\). Alternative (b) fails as well.

Adding \(1\in A\) prevents a singleton nontrivial root image when \(A^\pi\) generates \(T\), and restores the relevant initial inference in the proof. Proposition 8.8 already includes \(1\in A\), and its uses of this lemma therefore supply the missing condition. This is a local false auxiliary statement, without demonstrated damage to that downstream application.

# Other checks and remaining uncertainty

The finite-field reduction’s finite pigeonhole argument over the possible normal closures of \(A^6\cap\Gamma\) was checked; its role is to make the subgroup independent of the finite test set, rather than merely to obtain unrelated finite approximations. The distinction between an arbitrary soluble section and a section covered by a trigonalizable subgroup is used explicitly in Section 6. The characteristic-intersection construction and bounded Weyl action were traced, subject to the cited finite-linear-group theorems.

In Section 8, the pushforward of uniform measure under an invariant bilinear map gives \(\widehat\mu(\chi)=[U:U_\chi]^{-1}\). The large-composition-factor condition therefore supplies the Fourier gap needed in Proposition 8.7. The central, abelian, and lower-central-series stages of Proposition 8.8 were followed, as were the comparison \(H_1=H_2\), the subsequent normalizer argument, and the finite intersection of conjugates in Section 8.5. These checks do not certify every imported theorem.

Two other proof details merit separate treatment rather than an additional claimed theorem counterexample. On p. 21, projection of \(B^3\) onto retained simple factors is used to infer coverage of the section \(\Gamma_1/\operatorname{Sol}(\Gamma)\); the defined trace requires intersection with \(\Gamma_1\). A complete justification under the special hypotheses was not supplied by this review. On p. 31, the coprime-action identity is stated for arbitrary subgroups \(L\le V\), although it requires \(T\)-invariance. This local step is repaired by taking \(L_T=\langle\operatorname{tr}(A^2,V)^T\rangle\): the trace lies in the \(T\)-stable image of \(U_1\), and \(C_V(T)=1\) gives \(L_T=[L_T,T]\le\operatorname{im}[U_1,T]\), which is the inclusion actually needed. This local repair is not a complete independent proof of the theorem.

The high-rank and non-finitely-generated examples in Section 3 were used as counterevidence against imposing stronger conclusions than the paper claims. Missing computational code is not a mathematical defect here. No author correction addressing the findings was established by the version search; this is not a claim of first discovery.

# Conclusion and review state

The main rank-one bound and the bounded-chain input to the diameter proof are false as stated in the reviewed version. The remaining structural theorem for \(n\ge2\) and the diameter theorem itself are not refuted by the examples. A satisfactory revision should repair the rank domain, supply a valid replacement for the prime-field descent and the affected orbit argument, and make the identity hypotheses or exponents consistent.
