Abstract: Three Major findings concern the proof as printed in the 142-page version of record. The shooting transversality calculation identifies physical velocity with a momentum variable of the opposite sign. The weighted bootstrap asks for a stronger radial norm than the stated energy hierarchy supplies. Finally, an asserted equivalence of leaf volume forms fails on the unboosted hyperboloidal end; with the printed coordinate derivatives this also gives a counterexample to Lemma 8.14. These are core derivation or lemma failures, not a counterexample to the nonlinear asymptotic-stability conclusion. Local corrections and the remaining verification needed for a complete repaired proof are distinguished.

# Scientific scope, hypotheses and dependency chain

The geometric unknown is a timelike hypersurface in Minkowski space solving the hyperbolic vanishing mean curvature equation. The stationary profile is the Lorentzian product of the \(n\)-dimensional Riemannian catenoid. The theorem assumes \(n\geq5\), sufficiently smooth compactly supported small normal displacement and physical normal velocity, and a codimension-one condition, (5.25). The proof fixes \(n=5\) from Section 5 onward. It modulates the \(n\) transverse translations and boosts; it does not introduce a square-integrable axial-translation or scaling mode.

The Jacobi operator \(H=\Delta_{\underline{\mathcal C}}+|\mathrm{II}|^2\) has one positive eigenvalue \(\mu^2\). Its eigenfunction \(\varphi_\mu\) is exponentially decaying. The transverse zero modes decay as \(r^{1-n}\). Their square-integrability for \(n\geq3\) follows from the end integral \(\int^\infty r^{1-n}\,\mathrm dr\); the axial mode has a different asymptotic behavior and is not interchangeable with them.

The profile and momentum definitions yield localized, smoothed modulation equations. Projected product-catenoid LED feeds the variable-profile energy and LED estimates, then the \(r^p\) hierarchy. Elliptic estimates and pointwise decay close Propositions 5.1–5.2, which enter the shooting argument in Section 5.1. In particular the estimates must improve the same bootstrap norms, with constants independent of the assumed large bootstrap constants. A norm convention or power cannot be changed silently at the final implication.

# Problem F01: the stated shooting direction is stable

Rating: Major. The affected step is Section 5.1, Step 2a, printed p. 985 (PDF 83), especially (5.24) and the symplectic pairing used immediately after (5.25). The data are also stated in Theorem 1.5, p. 924 (PDF 22). The necessary definitions are (1.20), p. 923; (3.15)–(3.16), p. 947 (PDF 45); the symplectic form on p. 959 (PDF 57); and the modes on p. 966 (PDF 64).

## Exact calculation

Write \(w=\sqrt{|h|}|_{\ell=0}>0\) for the coordinate density in the flat interior. At the unperturbed profile, with zero modulation variation, (3.16) gives \[p:=\dot\psi=-w\,\partial_t\psi.\] Here the dot on \(\psi\) denotes the paper’s momentum, not physical velocity. On the \(\mu\)-eigenspace, its first-order matrix is \[M_\mu=\begin{pmatrix}0&-w^{-1}\\-w\mu^2&0\end{pmatrix}.\] Consequently \[M_\mu\binom{\varphi_\mu}{+\mu w\varphi_\mu}
=-\mu\binom{\varphi_\mu}{+\mu w\varphi_\mu},\qquad
M_\mu\binom{\varphi_\mu}{-\mu w\varphi_\mu}
=+\mu\binom{\varphi_\mu}{-\mu w\varphi_\mu}.\] This agrees with the paper’s \(Z_-=c_-\chi\varphi_\mu(1,+\mu w)^\intercal\) and \(Z_+=c_+\chi\varphi_\mu(1,-\mu w)^\intercal\). The symplectic form is \(\boldsymbol\Omega(U,V)=\int(U_1V_2-U_2V_1)\,\mathrm d\rho\,\mathrm d\omega\).

In (5.24), however, varying \(b\) changes the physical data by \[\delta_b(\psi,\partial_t\psi)
=\varepsilon(\widetilde\varphi_\mu,-\mu\widetilde\varphi_\mu).\] Its first-order phase-space variation is therefore \[\delta_b(\psi,p)
=\varepsilon(\widetilde\varphi_\mu,+\mu w\widetilde\varphi_\mu).\] It has the exact pointwise cancellation \[\boldsymbol\Omega\!\left(\varepsilon
  \binom{\widetilde\varphi_\mu}{+\mu w\widetilde\varphi_\mu},Z_-\right)=0.
\tag{F01.1}\] No cancellation between different spatial regions is needed; arbitrary cutoffs in the two factors preserve this zero. The displayed nonzero pairing in Step 2a instead inserts \((\widetilde\varphi_\mu,-\mu\widetilde\varphi_\mu)\) directly as displacement and momentum. It omits both the sign change and the density in the velocity-to-momentum map.

The elementary second-order check is the same: the eigenmode equation is \(q''=\mu^2q\), and data \((q(0),q'(0))=(1,-\mu)\) produce \(e^{-\mu t}\). They do not vary the growing coefficient to first order.

## Counterevidence and precise limit

The issue is tested at the zero profile with radial eigenfunction data. The translation and boost tests have angular factors \(\Theta_i\), whose spherical integrals against radial data vanish; their first-order parameter correction therefore does not supply the missing radial growing component. The theorem’s restriction \(\mathcal Z(\psi_0,\psi_1,0)=0\) gives \(q(0)=0\) at \(b=0\), but does not prove transversality in \(b\).

The full definition is \[a_+=\boldsymbol\Omega(\vec\psi,Z_-)-e^{\mu t}\widetilde S(e^{-\mu t}F_+),
\qquad q=\dot a_+=\mu a_+-e^{\mu t}S(e^{-\mu t}F_+).\] Thus (F01.1) alone is not a proof that the complete finite-cutoff derivative \(\partial_b q(0)\) vanishes exactly. This possible objection was checked against (3.49)–(3.52). At zero modulation the linear residuals in \(F_+\) come from the almost-eigenfunction errors, supported near the large cutoff radius \(R_1\). Their pairings with the exponentially decaying eigenfunction tend to zero as \(R_1\to\infty\). The kernels of \(S\) and \(\widetilde S\) have bounded support and bounded integrals, so they preserve this smallness, including the fixed bounded extension to \([-1,0]\). Nonlinear terms do not contribute at zero data. They cannot justify the nonzero leading pairing asserted in Step 2a. An accidental small finite-cutoff derivative would require a new quantitative range argument; no such lower bound is established here or in that step.

There is also an overall \(\varepsilon\) from (5.24): even with a correct transverse direction the natural derivative is of order \(\varepsilon\), with \(q_0\) also scaled by \(\varepsilon\). This bookkeeping issue is not the basis for the Major rating.

## Impact and repair boundary

Step 2b needs a family realizing all initial \(q_0\) in the trapping interval. Step 2a’s justification of that family is invalid with the printed physical sign. This breaks a core step connecting Propositions 5.1–5.2 to Theorem 1.5, whether or not the asymptotic-stability conclusion is true.

Changing the physical velocity correction to \(+\mu b\widetilde\varphi_\mu\) gives the locally verified nonzero leading pairing \[2\mu\varepsilon c_-\int
 \widetilde\varphi_\mu\chi\varphi_\mu w\,\mathrm d\rho\,\mathrm d\omega .\] For the usual nonnegative cutoffs and consistently normalized ground state it is nonzero and remains so under sufficiently small cutoff errors after division by \(\varepsilon\). This verifies the local direction correction. It does not finish a corrected theorem: the data family, functional \(\mathcal Z\), smoothing and trapping range must be propagated consistently. No nonlinear counterexample or complete repaired shooting proof is claimed.

# Problem F02: the weighted norm is stronger than the proved hierarchy

Rating: Major. The locations are (5.12), p. 982 (PDF 80), and the promised improvement (5.23), p. 983 (PDF 81), compared with (8.16), p. 1026 (PDF 124), Lemmas 8.10–8.11, pp. 1035–1037, and the final proof of Proposition 5.2, p. 1041 (PDF 139).

For \(0\leq p\leq2\), the printed bootstrap and conclusion contain \[\|\chi r^p D X^k\mathbf T^j\phi\|_{L^2(\Sigma_\tau)}
\lesssim \varepsilon\tau^{-1-j+p/2},
\qquad D=\partial_r+\frac{n-1}{2r}.
\tag{F02.1}\] The squared boundary energy actually defined in (8.16) is \[\mathcal E^p[\psi](\tau)
=\int_{\Sigma_\tau}\chi\widetilde r^p
 \bigl(L(\widetilde r^{(n-1)/2}\psi)\bigr)^2\,\mathrm d\theta\,\mathrm dr .
\tag{F02.2}\] In the unboosted far region, with the Euclidean spatial coordinate measure, its leading radial term controls \(r^{p/2}D\psi\) in \(L^2\), not \(r^pD\psi\). The distinction is explicit at the end of Section 7, p. 1016 (PDF 114), where the corresponding norm is correctly written with \(r^{p/2}\). Even using the induced leaf measure discussed in F03, the leading conversion would have power \((p+1)/2\), not \(p\); at \(p=2\) the asserted power is still too strong.

## An exact witness against the inference

Take \(n=5\) and a smooth compactly supported scalar function \(f\) that is identically one in a neighborhood of some \(\tau_0>0\). The radial free wave \[u(t,r)=\frac{f'(t-r)+f'(t+r)}{r^2}
       +\frac{f(t-r)-f(t+r)}{r^3}
\tag{F02.3}\] satisfies \(-u_{tt}+u_{rr}+4r^{-1}u_r=0\). This follows by differentiation, also checked symbolically. The apparent singularity at \(r=0\) is removable: Taylor expansion leaves \(2f'''(t)/3+O(r^2)\) with an even smooth expansion. Its data on every fixed ordinary time slice are smooth and compactly supported.

On the hyperboloidal leaf \(t=\tau_0+\sqrt{1+r^2}\), for all sufficiently large \(r\), \(t-r\) lies in the plateau of \(f\) and \(t+r\) lies outside its support. Hence \[u=r^{-3},\qquad D u=-r^{-4},\qquad
L(r^2u)=-r^{-2},\qquad L=\partial_t+\partial_r\] in the last expression, while \(D\) differentiates along the leaf. The allowed \(p=2\) energy tail is finite: \[\int^\infty r^2\bigl(L(r^2u)\bigr)^2\,\mathrm dr
=\int^\infty r^{-2}\,\mathrm dr<\infty .\] The claimed \(p=2\) norm in (F02.1) has, instead, squared tail \[\int^\infty (r^2Du)^2r^4\,\mathrm dr=\int^\infty1\,\mathrm dr=\infty\] for the coordinate measure. For the actual unboosted catenoid-hyperboloid induced measure, which is asymptotic to \(r^3\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\omega\), it is \(\int^\infty r^{-1}\,\mathrm dr\), again divergent. Smooth cutoffs or modifications in a fixed compact interior cannot change these tail statements.

## Counterevidence, impact and repair boundary

The witness is an exact flat-space wave, not a solution of nonlinear HVMC on the catenoid. It refutes the proposed energy-to-stronger-norm inference. It is not a counterexample to Proposition 5.2 under all its assumptions: that proposition already assumes finiteness of the stronger bootstrap norm. Nor is failure of a global nonlinear solution inferred from it.

Additional commuted energies do not automatically remove this obstruction. On the plateau, \(rL\) preserves the \(r^{-3}\) tail up to a constant, angular derivatives vanish, and time derivatives vanish. The leading \(r^p\) boundary energy remains finite for \(p\leq2\), while the stronger norm above remains infinite.

The final proof asserts that (5.23) was already proved by Lemmas 8.10–8.11. The cited hierarchy supplies no such strengthening, so the claimed strict improvement of the actual bootstrap norm is missing. This is a core closure gap, not a harmless change of notation.

A plausible local correction is to use \(r^{p/2}\) with the spatial coordinate/flux measure, matching the end of Section 7 and (8.16). The measure must first be made consistent with F03. Every invocation of (5.12), including the modulation estimates and continuity argument, must then be checked with the corrected norm. That full propagation is not verified here; alternatively a genuinely stronger estimate would have to be proved.

# Problem F03: leaf measure equivalence and Lemma 8.14

Rating: Major. Section 2.1.5, p. 929 (PDF 27), defines \(\,\mathrm dV\) as the induced ambient volume unless another measure is specified. The parenthetical passage immediately preceding (8.39), p. 1039 (PDF 137), expressly uses the coordinate derivatives \(\partial_\rho,\rho^{-1}\partial_\omega\) and asserts that Euclidean \(\mathbb R^5\) volume is comparable to the geometric volume on \(\Sigma_\tau\) at large \(\rho\). Lemma 8.14 and its use to prove (5.16)–(5.17) occur on p. 1041 (PDF 139).

## Exact geometric test

Set all modulation parameters to zero, a permitted case of the bootstrap geometry. On one exterior end the explicit hyperboloidal parametrization in Section 4.1, (4.5), is, up to an irrelevant time constant, \[X=(\tau+\sqrt{1+r^2},\,r\omega,\,Q(r)).\] The unboosted \(n=5\) catenoid satisfies \(Q'(r)^2=(r^8-1)^{-1}\). Pulling back the Minkowski metric to \(\tau=\mathrm{constant}\) gives \[h_{\Sigma,rr}=\frac1{1+r^2}+\frac1{r^8-1},\qquad
h_{\Sigma,\mathrm{ang}}=r^2g_{\mathbb S^4}.\] Thus \[\,\mathrm dV_{\Sigma_\tau}
=r^4\sqrt{\frac1{1+r^2}+\frac1{r^8-1}}\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\omega
\sim r^3\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\omega .
\tag{F03.1}\] The ratio to Euclidean spatial measure \(r^4\,\mathrm dr\,\mathrm d\omega\) is asymptotic to \(r^{-1}\), not bounded below by a positive constant. Replacing \(r\) with the paper’s asymptotically equivalent exterior \(\rho\) does not cure this. The claimed uniform comparability is therefore false even before introducing perturbations.

## A counterexample under the printed coordinate convention

Choose a smooth bump \(\zeta\) supported in the unit ball of \(\mathbb R^5\), with \(\zeta(0)=1\) and supremum norm one. On the upper exterior end with spatial coordinate \(y=r\omega\), set \(\psi_R(y)=\zeta(y-Re_1)\), extended by zero elsewhere. For \(R\) large this is a smooth compactly supported function on \(\Sigma_\tau\), and belongs to \(H^3(\Sigma_\tau)\) for each \(R\), even if membership is interpreted intrinsically.

On this support (F03.1) is uniformly comparable to \(R^{-1}\,\mathrm dy\). The coordinate derivatives specified before (8.39) are combinations of Cartesian derivatives with uniformly bounded coefficients and their derivatives. Hence, for \(j=2,3\), \[\|\partial_\Sigma^j\psi_R\|_{L^2(\,\mathrm dV_{\Sigma_\tau})}
\leq C_j R^{-1/2}.\] The right-hand side of the printed Lemma 8.14 is therefore at most \[\|\partial_\Sigma^2\psi_R\|_2^{1/2}
\|\partial_\Sigma^3\psi_R\|_2^{1/2}
\leq C R^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0,\] whereas \(\|\psi_R\|_\infty=1\). This is a complete family contradicting the lemma under the stated induced-measure and coordinate-derivative conventions.

## Counterevidence, impact and repair boundary

Spacetime volume forms for the nearly flat catenoid and Minkowski metric can be comparable. Equation (F03.1) concerns the induced spacelike leaf volume; it does not contradict a spacetime comparison. If \(L^2(\Sigma_\tau)\) were instead redefined using spatial coordinate or suitable flux measure, the translated-bump counterexample would no longer refute the usual Euclidean inequality. Likewise, using intrinsic unit derivatives would change the scaling of the radial derivative and invalidate this particular bump calculation. Neither reinterpretation validates the explicit leaf-volume comparison and coordinate derivatives printed before (8.39).

The false comparison is used in transferring Euclidean fractional elliptic estimates into the norms of Lemma 8.12. Lemma 8.14 then supplies the pointwise bounds in Proposition 5.2 and the main bootstrap. The problem thus reaches core estimates independently of the shooting sign and independently of the extra radial power in F02.

A correction must specify a single consistent measure/derivative convention and prove the required energy, elliptic and Sobolev estimates in it. The formula (F03.1) and the failure of the printed inference are verified here. No global norm replacement, corrected local well-posedness argument or complete nonlinear proof is certified.

# Positive checks and remaining limits

The causal smoothing construction on pp. 963–965 survives a direct check: for \(\widetilde k(s)=0\) for \(s<0\) and \(\widetilde k(s)=-\int_s^\infty k(v)\,\mathrm dv\) for \(s\geq0\), the derivative in distributions is \(\widetilde k'=k-\delta_0\). The jump at zero is \(-1\), so \(\partial_t\widetilde S=S-I\) has the stated sign. A polynomial-kernel computation illustrates the identity; the argument itself applies to the smooth kernel in the paper.

The asymptotic-affine translation conclusion in Remark 1.6 follows from the stated modulation decay: if \(\kappa<1/2\), then \(\ell-\ell_\infty=O(t^{-3/2+\kappa})\), and \(\xi'-\ell_\infty=O(t^{-3/2+\kappa})\) is integrable. One obtains \(\xi(t)-a_\infty-\ell_\infty t=O(t^{-1/2+\kappa})\). The integration requires the second time integral, not merely integrability of \(\ell'\).

The five-dimensional Euclidean interpolation orders in the advertised Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality are dimensionally consistent: the mean of derivative orders 2 and 3 is \(5/2\). F03 concerns its transferred norms, not that Euclidean scaling. The range \(2<s<5/2\) in Lemma 8.12 and the explicit decision to work in \(n=5\) were tracked; the same displayed exponents are not certified for every higher dimension without adaptation.

The complete tensor-coefficient expansions in Sections 3–4, every low/high-frequency commutator estimate, spectral inputs from the cited literature, and all nonlinear error bounds were not independently reconstructed. A separate literal regularity concern remains with the auxiliary potential on p. 994: a smooth function cannot satisfy \(\operatorname{sgn}(\rho)V_{\rm temp}'(\rho)\leq-v_0<0\) on both sides of a neighborhood of zero. Whether the intended, weaker weighted condition suffices throughout (7.6)–(7.19) was not settled. This is recorded as an unrated clarification requiring a complete multiplier repair check, and is not counted as a fourth Major finding. Other isolated notation/sign irregularities have not been promoted into theorem failures. No optional code absence is treated as a defect.
