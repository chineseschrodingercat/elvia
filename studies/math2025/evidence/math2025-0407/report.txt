Abstract: The retained 72-page author manuscript of Halpern and Rauch is examined through its stretched Maxwell, Helmholtz and split-system arguments. Four substantive failures are demonstrated: the boundary spectral identity has incorrect scaling and signs; a complex stretched Rellich identity fails for a constant field; the asserted \(H^2\) solvability is incompatible with the allowed \(L^2\) currents; and the split reconstruction omits the forcing term. A separate general stretching lemma assumes insufficient spatial regularity for holomorphic continuation. Exact counterexamples and bounded algebraic corrections are supplied where justified. These findings concern the acquired manuscript. They do not refute the principal weighted \(L^2\) well-posedness conclusion or establish equivalence with the journal-final version.

Write \(W=(E,B)\) and \[A(\xi)(E,B)=(-\xi\times B,\xi\times E),\qquad
 \widetilde\partial_j=\frac{\tau}{\tau+\sigma_j(x_j)}\partial_j .\] The absorptions are nonnegative smooth functions of one coordinate, vanish on the domain of interest \(Q_I\), and have bounded derivatives of every order [Retained latex: \cite[p.~7, Assumption 1.2]{HR}]. Zero absorption is allowed. For the examples with nonzero absorption, take \[Q=(-3,3)^3,\qquad Q_I=(-1,1)^3,\] and choose a smooth bump \(b:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\), supported in \((3/2,5/2)\), equal to one near \(2\). Set \(\sigma_2=b\), \(\sigma_1=\sigma_3=0\). These coefficients satisfy all the stated absorption hypotheses. Choose the smoothing \(Q^\delta\) sufficiently close to \(Q\) that \((0,2,0)\) is an interior point and a neighborhood of \((3,2,0)\) remains a flat boundary patch. This is permitted by Definition 3.1.

For the stretched normal define, as in source Definition 3.3, \[\Pi=\prod_{j=1}^3\frac{\tau+\sigma_j}{\tau},\qquad

For the sufficiently large complex parameters used by the source, the square root is the branch continued from positive real values. Spectral projectors \(\pi^\pm(m)\) refer to the eigenspaces of \(A(m)\) with eigenvalues \(\pm s\). Set \[P_{\mathrm{tan}}(E,B)=(\pi_{m^\perp}E,\pi_{m^\perp}B),\qquad
 C(W)=(-\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}B,\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}E).\] The bilinear dot product, rather than the Hermitian inner product, is used in these algebraic identities.

# The boundary identity and its cancellation

Problem 1 (The spectral formula for the boundary term is false).  Proposition 5.10 on source p. 38 asserts, for \(|\tau|\) sufficiently large and arbitrary \((E,B)\in H^2(Q^\delta)\), \[\pi^\pm(m)\mathcal D(W)=s\,\pi^\pm(m)\bigl(\tau W+C(W)\bigr).
 \tag{2.1}\] This is inconsistent with the definition of \(\mathcal D\) in (5.12), p. 37.

## Exact calculation and all hypotheses

The defined boundary term is \[\mathcal D(W)=\tau P_{\mathrm{tan}}W+
   \bigl((\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}E)\times m,(\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}B)\times m\bigr)
   +\bigl((\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}E)m,(\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}B)m\bigr).
 \tag{2.2}\] At the flat point \(x_0=(3,2,0)\) use the absorptions specified above, and let \(\tau=T>0\) be arbitrarily large. Then \[\nu=e_1,\qquad m=(1+T^{-1})e_1,\qquad s=1+T^{-1}.\] Take the constant fields \(E=e_2\), \(B=e_3\). They are smooth on the bounded \(Q^\delta\), hence belong to \(H^2(Q^\delta)\). Since \(e_3\times e_1=e_2\), \(W=(e_2,e_3)\) belongs to the positive spectral subspace, so \(\pi^+(m)W=W\). All derivatives vanish. Formula (2.2) therefore gives \[\pi^+(m)\mathcal D(W)=TW,\] whereas the claimed right-hand side of (2.1) is \[s\,\pi^+(m)(TW)=(T+1)W.
 \tag{2.3}\] The discrepancy is the nonzero vector \(W\), for every \(T>0\) and thus beyond any fixed large-parameter threshold. Proposition 5.10 imposes no Maxwell equation or boundary condition on this test field, so no hypothesis has been dropped.

## Correct algebra and limited repair

The source’s cross-product part can be checked without any coordinate approximation: \[\bigl((\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}E)\times m,(\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}B)\times m\bigr)=A(m)C(W).\] The normal terms in (2.2) are annihilated by \(\pi^\pm(m)\); the projector commutes with \(A(m)\). Consequently the exact identities are \[\boxed{\ \pi^\pm(m)\mathcal D(W)
       =\tau\pi^\pm(m)W\ \pm s\,\pi^\pm(m)C(W).\ }
 \tag{2.4}\] This proves both the missing scale in the mass term and the different sign on the negative eigenspace. Normalizing \(m\) inside the projector does not repair (2.1), since that leaves the projector unchanged while the cross products still contain the unnormalized \(m\).

For homogeneous stretched Maxwell equations near the boundary, \(C(W)=-\tau W\) and the divergences vanish. If the absorbing condition \(\pi^-(m)W=0\) holds, (2.4) yields \[\mathcal D(W)=\tau(1-s)\pi^+(m)W.
 \tag{2.5}\] Thus the zero boundary term asserted in Remark 5.2 is not an algebraic consequence of those local conditions. A compatible local illustration on the same flat patch is \[W(x)=e^{-Tx_1}(e_2,e_3).\] Here \(\widetilde\partial_1=\partial_1\), the other derivatives of the field vanish, the homogeneous stretched Maxwell equations and absorbing condition hold locally, and \(\mathcal D(W)=-W\) where \(b(x_2)=1\). This is a local boundary illustration, not a claimed global solution satisfying every face condition.

A precise local correction is available. Replace only the boundary mass part of (2.2) by \(\tau sP_{\mathrm{tan}}W\), calling the resulting vector \(\mathcal D_s(W)\). Then \[\pi^\pm(m)\mathcal D_s(W)=s\,\pi^\pm(m)\bigl(\tau W\pm C(W)\bigr).\] Together with zero divergences, \(C(W)=-\tau W\) and \(\pi^-(m)W=0\), this gives \(\mathcal D_s(W)=0\). In the bilinear form this corresponds to multiplying the boundary mass integrand by \(s\). This establishes a local cancellation for Maxwell solutions. It does not verify every Helmholtz boundary claim in Remark 5.2, nor all estimates for the modified variational form.

## Impact and counterevidence

The unmodified form is used at the start of Section 7 and explicitly in the proof of Theorem 7.6, p. 55, where Proposition 5.10 is invoked to discard the boundary integral. Theorem 7.6 feeds Theorem 8.1 and then the main results. This invalid derivation therefore has Major impact under the core-dependency criterion. The counterexample does not assert instability of the PML. The unstretched positive-projection special case \(s=1\) does not exhibit the scale error, and the local corrected cancellation is counterevidence against a false-main-theorem conclusion.

# A complex stretched Rellich identity

Problem 2 (Taking real parts prevents the stated analytic continuation).  Lemma 6.4, equation (6.6), p. 45, claims for every \(E\in C^1(Q^\delta)\) and \(\operatorname{Re}\tau>0\) that \[\begin{aligned}
 \widetilde{\operatorname{div}}\left\{\tfrac12|E|^2X-\operatorname{Re}\bigl((\bar E\cdot X)E\bigr)\right\}
 =\operatorname{Re}\left\{\tfrac12|E|^2-(\bar E\cdot X)\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}E
                  +(\bar E\times X)\cdot\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}E\right\}.
 \tag{3.1}\end{aligned}\] Its proof asserts that every term is holomorphic in \(\tau\). Both that assertion and (3.1) fail in general.

Use the same admissible absorptions, and evaluate near the interior point \((0,2,0)\). Put \(E=e_2\), a constant smooth real field. Let \[\tau=T+i,\quad T>0,\qquad a=\frac{\tau}{\tau+1},\qquad
 d=1+\frac1{\tau}=a^{-1}.\] The coordinate definition (1.13) gives \(\partial_2X_2=d\) near this point, while \(\widetilde\partial_jX_j=1\) for every \(j\). All stretched derivatives of \(E\) vanish. Thus the two sides of (3.1) are respectively \[L=\frac32-a\operatorname{Re}d,\qquad R=\frac12,\] and their exact difference is \[L-R=1-a\operatorname{Re}d=-\frac{i\,a}{T^2+1}\ne0 .
 \tag{3.2}\] For example, \(T=2\) gives \[L=\frac{13}{25}-\frac{7i}{50},\qquad
 R=\frac12,\qquad L-R=\frac{1-7i}{50}.
 \tag{3.3}\] The example meets the entire lemma: \(E\) is \(C^1\), the absorptions obey Assumption 1.2, the point lies in \(Q^\delta\), and \(\operatorname{Re}\tau>0\). Taking \(T\) arbitrarily large also defeats an implicit large-real-part restriction.

The failure is not explained by a complex choice of \(E\), since \(E\) is real. Applying a real part to the whole left side would still leave \(\operatorname{Re}(L-R)=1/50\) in (3.3). The unstretched and positive-real-\(\tau\) identities do hold in this example. The problem is the continuation of a formula containing \(\operatorname{Re}\) of a \(\tau\)-dependent quantity. The source’s valid holomorphic continuation of algebraic differential identities does not justify this step.

The proof of Proposition 6.3 begins by multiplying (6.6) by \(\Pi\) and integrating to obtain (6.9). Proposition 6.1 is then deduced and is explicitly used at (7.9) to close Proposition 7.3. These estimates lead to Theorem 7.6. The failure is therefore Major as a defect in the central estimate’s derivation. It is not a counterexample to the inequalities of Propositions 6.1 or 6.3. A fully justified complex Rellich estimate, with the extra conjugate-dependent terms controlled, remains to be established. No complete repair of that estimate is claimed here.

# The source class does not permit the asserted regularity

Problem 3 (Theorem 8.1 cannot provide \(H^2\) fields for every allowed \(L^2\) current).  On p. 57, Theorem 8.1 assumes that \(J,R\) are holomorphic with values in compactly supported \(L^2\) fields and that \(-\widetilde{\operatorname{div}}J=\tau R\). It concludes that \(W=(F,G)\) is holomorphic with values in \(H^2(Q^\delta)\) and satisfies \[\tau F-\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}G=-J,\qquad \tau G+\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}F=0 .
 \tag{4.1}\] The asserted \(H^2\) regularity is false for the stated source class.

Set every absorption equal to zero, which is allowed by Assumption 1.2. Choose \(\eta\in C^\infty_c(Q_I)\) equal to one on a neighborhood of the origin, and define \[\psi(x)=\eta(x)|x_1|,\qquad
 J=\operatorname{curl}(0,0,\psi)
       =(\partial_2\psi,-\partial_1\psi,0),\qquad R=0.
 \tag{4.2}\] The function \(\psi\) is compactly supported and Lipschitz, so \(J\in L^2\) and \(\operatorname{supp}J\Subset Q_I\). Mixed distributional derivatives commute, hence \(\operatorname{div}J=0\). Treat \(J\) as constant in \(\tau\). It is then an entire \(L^2\)-valued function, as is \(R\), and \(-\operatorname{div}J=\tau R\) holds throughout every required half-plane.

Near the origin, \[J=(0,-\operatorname{sgn}x_1,0).\] Therefore \(\partial_1J_2=-2\delta_{\{x_1=0\}}\) locally in the distributional sense. This nonzero surface measure is not an \(L^2\) function, so \(J\notin H^1\).

If \(F,G\in H^2(Q^\delta)\) satisfied (4.1), then \[J=-\tau F+\operatorname{curl}G\in H^1(Q^\delta),\] contradicting (4.2). The boundary condition cannot remove this interior contradiction. The counterexample works for every positive real \(\tau\), including arbitrarily large values.

The source itself notes on p. 25 that an \(H^2\) Helmholtz field forces additional regularity of the compact current. In the construction on p. 57, however, the \(L^2\to H^2\) Helmholtz inverse is applied to \[(-\tau J-\widetilde{\operatorname{grad}}R,\widetilde{\operatorname{curl}}J),\] whose derivative terms need only be distributions of order one for the allowed inputs. This is the precise regularity mismatch.

This has Major impact: Theorem 8.1 is the smooth-domain existence step used to obtain Theorem 1.15, and its \(H^2\) property is used to invoke the converse in Proposition 3.4. The weaker \(L^2\) conclusion of Theorem 1.15 is not contradicted by this example. Starting with sufficiently smooth compatible sources and then proving an appropriate density passage to a weaker solution space is a possible route; that passage and all its trace and parameter claims have not been completed here. Merely changing the notation \(L^2\) to a smoother class would change the theorem’s stated domain.

# The split reconstruction loses the forcing

Problem 4 (Equation (8.12) does not reconstruct the prescribed source).  In the existence proof for Theorem 1.16, source (8.12), p. 63, defines \[\widehat U^k=-(\tau+\sigma_k)^{-1}A_k\partial_kW,
 \qquad \widehat V=W.
 \tag{5.1}\] The subsequent equation (8.15) attributes a nonzero forcing to this formula and concludes that \(\sum_k\widehat U^k=W\). The displayed definition lacks the forcing term.

## A compatible current with an explicit solution

Let \(\sigma_k=0\), choose a nonconstant real \(\phi\in C^\infty_c(Q_I)\), and choose the allowed source weights \(c_1=1\), \(c_2=c_3=0\). For \(t\ge0\) set \[j(t,x)=e^{-t}\nabla\phi(x),\qquad
 \rho(t,x)=-(1-e^{-t})\Delta\phi(x),\] and set both to zero for \(t<0\). Then \(\partial_t\rho=-\operatorname{div}j\) distributionally, with no delta contribution at \(t=0\) because \(\rho(0)=0\). Both sources have the required spatial support and belong to \(e^{\mu t}L^2\) for every \(\mu>0\).

Their Laplace transforms are \[J=\frac{\nabla\phi}{\tau+1},\qquad
 R=-\frac{\Delta\phi}{\tau(\tau+1)}.\] The stretched equations, now unstretched, have the explicit smooth solution \[W=(F,G)=\left(-\frac{\nabla\phi}{\tau(\tau+1)},0\right).
 \tag{5.2}\] Indeed \(\tau F=-J\), \(\operatorname{curl}F=0\), \(\operatorname{div}F=R\), and \(G=0\). The field vanishes near every face, so the absorbing boundary condition holds.

Because \(A(\partial)W=(0,\operatorname{curl}F)=0\), formula (5.1) gives \[\sum_{k=1}^3\widehat U^k
       =-\tau^{-1}A(\partial)W=0\ne W.
 \tag{5.3}\] Thus the error affects the reconstruction of the output, even with smooth compact sources and no absorption. It is not a missing regularity assumption.

## A complete algebraic correction

With \(\widehat f_k=c_k(-J,0)\), the correct formula is \[\boxed{\ \widehat U^k
     =(\tau+\sigma_k)^{-1}
        \bigl(\widehat f_k-A_k\partial_kW\bigr).\ }
 \tag{5.4}\] It immediately satisfies each transformed split equation once \(\sum_k\widehat U^k=W\) is verified. The stretched equation gives \[W=\frac{\widehat f}{\tau}
           -\sum_k(\tau+\sigma_k)^{-1}A_k\partial_kW.\] Since \(\sigma_k=0\) on \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat f_k\) and \(\sum_k\widehat f_k=\widehat f\), summing (5.4) yields exactly \(W\). This proves the reconstruction identity and each transformed split equation. In example (5.2), the missing source term supplies precisely the nonzero sum in (5.2).

The source’s preceding transformed equation (8.11), p. 62, contains the forcing and agrees with (5.4). This is strong evidence for an omission in the displayed construction. Interchanging the electric and magnetic block order in nearby source formulas cannot repair the absence of the forcing. The discrepancy is assessed as Major for the invalid existence reconstruction leading directly to Theorem 1.16; the short correction does not change that dependency. The correction proves the local transformed reconstruction, conditional on a suitable stretched solution. It does not repair the separate boundary-estimate and source-regularity problems above.

# A broader holomorphic stretching claim

Problem 5 (Smooth spatial coefficients need not admit holomorphic stretching).  Lemma 1.11, p. 10, asserts that an operator with coefficients smooth in real space and holomorphic in \(\tau\) acquires a holomorphic stretched extension to \(\operatorname{Re}\tau>0\). Spatial smoothness alone is insufficient.

Use the admissible one-direction absorption already fixed, and write \[S=\int_0^2 b(u)\,du>0,\qquad
 h(z)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&z\le0,\\
  e^{-1/z^2},&z>0.
 \end{cases}\] The real function \(h\) is smooth, including at zero. Let the operator in \(X\) coordinates be multiplication by \[a(X)=h\bigl(X_2-(2+S)\bigr).\] Its coefficient is smooth in \(X\) and independent of \(\tau\), hence holomorphic in \(\tau\) as required. At the fixed point \(x=(0,2,0)\), real positive stretching gives \[X_2(\tau,2)=2+\frac S\tau,\qquad
 a(X(\tau,x))=h\bigl(S(\tau^{-1}-1)\bigr).
 \tag{6.1}\] This is zero for every real \(\tau\ge1\) and strictly positive for \(0<\tau<1\). If the coefficient had a holomorphic extension to the connected half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}\tau>0\), the identity theorem applied to the interval \((1,\infty)\) would make it identically zero, contradicting (6.1). This disproves the asserted extension under the exact coefficient hypotheses.

The source explicitly says at the bottom of p. 10 and top of p. 11 that its use of the correspondence is for spatially constant coefficients. In that restricted case the substitution \(\partial_{X_j}\mapsto\widetilde\partial_j\) has coefficients holomorphic in \(\operatorname{Re}\tau>0\): they are finite sums and products of smooth spatial derivatives of \(\tau/(\tau+\sigma_j(x_j))\) and the original holomorphic coefficients. That restricted construction is valid. The demonstrated overstatement is therefore assessed as Minor; it does not by itself invalidate those constant-coefficient applications. The real-part error in Problem 2 is a separate issue.

# Assessment and remaining scope

The highest supported impact is Major. Problems 1 and 2 invalidate displayed identities used to close the central estimate; Problem 3 disproves the stated \(H^2\) conclusion of a central intermediate theorem; Problem 4 invalidates the printed source-dependent split construction. None of these arguments provides a counterexample to the principal weighted \(L^2\) well-posedness conclusion after appropriate corrections.

The exact algebra also supported the source’s Maxwell symbol identity \(A(\xi)^3=(\xi\cdot\xi)A(\xi)\), the six-dimensional injectivity test for the reduced Lopatinski problem on p. 28, and the three cyclic Turkel–Yefet factorizations on p. 66. These checks do not compensate for the failures above, but delimit what was and was not challenged. The paper explicitly leaves uniform long-time stability and decay through the layer open; they are not treated as proved claims requiring a new counterexample.
