Abstract: The exact author manuscript arXiv:2305.08668v2 is examined. Four Major findings and eight Minor corrections are established. The printed index definition changes the central bounded-index hypothesis; a double cover of the Clifford torus has ordinary Morse index four but infinite printed index. Independent target normalization invalidates the unqualified Willmore-energy conversion in Appendix A, with an exact \(4\pi\) discrepancy. The global avoidance assertion in the mean-curvature gauge lemma also fails under its expressly local Willmore hypotheses. An uncentered Poincare estimate also fails in the core interior C2 convergence argument. The remaining corrections concern explicit models, formulas and local statements. The principal conformally invariant energy quantization theorem is not disproved by these examples. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Substantive findings

## P1. Major: the definition does not measure Morse index

Location. Page 5, (1), and page 8, (6); the bounded-index hypothesis of Theorem III, p. 4, and Theorem 4.3, p. 12.

The definitions use \[\dim\operatorname{Span}\{v:Q(v)<0\},\] where \(Q\) is the second variation, rather than the maximum dimension of a subspace on which \(Q\) is negative definite. If \(Q(v)<0\), then for every smooth admissible \(w\) and sufficiently small nonzero \(t\), \(Q(v+tw)<0\). Thus \(w=((v+tw)-v)/t\) belongs to that span. The printed quantity is zero in the absence of negative directions and infinite dimensional whenever a negative direction exists.

A complete geometric example is \[\Sigma=(\mathbb R/4\pi\mathbb Z)\times(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z),\qquad
 \Psi(u,v)=\frac{(\cos u,\sin u,\cos v,\sin v)}{\sqrt2}.\] This unbranched double cover of the Clifford torus is a closed, genus-one Willmore immersion without umbilics. With \(N=2^{-1/2}(\cos u,\sin u,-\cos v,-\sin v)\), its metric is \(g=(du^2+dv^2)/2\), principal curvatures are \(-1,1\), and \(H=0\). Its area is \(4\pi^2\) and \(\mathcal E=8\pi^2\). For a normal velocity \(fN\), direct differentiation gives \[\operatorname{Area}''=\int_\Sigma(|\nabla f|^2-4f^2)\,dA,\qquad
 H'=\tfrac12(\Delta+4)f.\] Since \(\mathcal E=2\int(1+H^2)\,dA-4\pi\chi(\Sigma)\), \[Q_\mathcal E(f)=\int_\Sigma f(\Delta+2)(\Delta+4)f\,dA.
 \tag{P1.1}\] The eigenvalues of \(-\Delta\) are \(2(m^2/4+n^2)\). Only \((m,n)=(\pm1,\pm1)\) lie strictly between two and four. Their Fourier modes give four real negative directions, while all other modes are nonnegative. The ordinary index is exactly four. For example, \[f=\cos(u/2)\cos v,\qquad
 \int_\Sigma f^2\,dA=\pi^2,\qquad Q_\mathcal E(f)=-3\pi^2/4.\] The printed index is infinite.

All these velocities are realized by immersion variations: \(\cos(sf)\Psi+\sin(sf)N\) is immersed for sufficiently small \(s\). The time change \(s=\varepsilon\tanh(t/\varepsilon)\), with derivative one at zero, realizes the same velocity on \((-1,1)\). Stereographic projection from a point outside the image gives \[\Phi(u,v)=\frac{(\cos u,\sin u,\cos v)}{\sqrt2-\sin v},\] a closed immersion in the paper’s Euclidean setting with the same normal index, by conformal invariance of \(\mathcal E\).

Impact and counterevidence. Its constant sequence has bounded energy and ordinary index four, but is excluded by the literal printed hypothesis. The central theorem’s formal coverage is reduced to stable sequences. This scope change warrants Major. The example does not refute quantization: a constant sequence trivially quantizes.

Repair boundary. Both definitions must use \[\operatorname{ind}(Q)=\sup\{\dim L:L\text{ is linear and }Q(v)<0
                           \text{ for every }0\ne v\in L\}.\] Section 7.3 supplies relevant counterevidence: its fields have disjoint compact supports, so their local Hessian has no mixed terms and their span is negative definite. The corrected definition is compatible with that intended mechanism.

## P2. Major: Appendix A loses an inversion contribution

Location. Page 47, the final display converting the \(\mathcal E\) identity into a \(W\) identity; Definition 3.1, pp. 8–9; the equivalence claim on p. 4.

Use the singly covered Clifford torus, with both periods \(2\pi\), and project stereographically from a point outside its image. The resulting embedded closed \(\Phi\) has \[\mathcal E(\Phi)=4\pi^2,\qquad W(\Phi)=2\pi^2,\qquad \int_\Sigma K\,dA=0.\] Take \(\Phi_k=\Phi\), with a fixed normalized flat domain metric. The conformal limit has one component and no nodes. Its index is zero even under the printed definition: the single-cover eigenvalues \(2(m^2+n^2)\) avoid \((2,4)\) in (P1.1). All bounded-energy and bounded-index assumptions hold.

Choose a regular point \(p=\Phi(x_0)\), and set \[I_p(x)=\frac{x-p}{|x-p|^2},\qquad \Phi_\infty=I_p\circ\Phi .\] This is an allowed fixed smooth conformal normalization in \(S^3\) under Definition 3.1. In the Euclidean representative, the limit is a Willmore immersion of the punctured torus with one complete planar end of multiplicity one. Appendix A explicitly allows finitely many ends. There are no bubbles, and convergence is smooth away from \(x_0\). Density invariance gives exactly \[\lim_k\mathcal E(\Phi_k)=\mathcal E(\Phi_\infty)=4\pi^2.\] If a connected orientation-preserving convention is imposed on the conformal group, compose inversion with a reflection; the energies are unchanged.

Set \(r=|\Phi-p|\), \(g=\Phi^*\xi\). The curvature transformation is \[g'=r^{-4}g,\qquad
 K_{g'}\,dA_{g'}=(K_g+2\Delta_g\log r)\,dA_g.\] Excise a small intrinsic disk about \(x_0\). Here \(r=\rho(1+O(\rho))\), and the outward conormal of the punctured domain points into the disk. Hence \[\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
 \int_{\partial(\Sigma\setminus B_\epsilon)}
       \partial_n\log r\,ds=-2\pi,\qquad
 \int_{\Sigma\setminus\{x_0\}}K_{g'}\,dA_{g'}=-4\pi.\] The pointwise Gauss identity \(W=\mathcal E/2+\int K\,dA\) gives \[W(\Phi_\infty)=2\pi^2-4\pi,\qquad
 \lim_kW(\Phi_k)-W(\Phi_\infty)=4\pi .
 \tag{P2.1}\] Thus the displayed \(W\) identity for these permitted representatives is false, although their \(\mathcal E\) identity holds exactly.

Impact and counterevidence. This affects the asserted energy conversion, not the main \(\mathcal E\) quantization theorem. There is no neck in this constant sequence, so the preceding neck-curvature estimate cannot control an end introduced by target normalization. The topology warning on p. 1 agrees with (P2.1).

Repair boundary. Require compatible normalizations avoiding inversion through the surfaces, or retain the signed \(4\pi\) multiplicity contributions. Here the correction is \(W(\Phi_k)=W(\Phi_\infty)+4\pi\). Existence of differently normalized representatives satisfying a \(W\) identity is not contradicted. Major concerns the printed unconditional conversion for the specified pieces.

## P3. Major: the global avoidance in Lemma 5.2 is unavailable

Location. Section 5.1, pp. 13–15, especially Lemma 5.2, (16), and the last paragraph of its proof.

Page 13 explicitly permits the closed immersion to be non-Willmore outside the annulus. Nevertheless, p. 15 uses a global umbilic-set argument to choose a zero-mean-curvature inversion centre outside the entire surface. The following construction satisfies the stated local hypotheses on a fixed closed genus-one surface and makes every such centre belong to its image.

Start with the ring torus \[F(u,v)=((R+\cos u)\cos v,(R+\cos u)\sin v,\sin u),\qquad R=\sqrt2.\] Its metric is \(du^2+(R+\cos u)^2dv^2\), and, up to orientation, \[H=1-\frac{R}{2(R+\cos u)},\quad
 K=\frac{\cos u}{R+\cos u},\quad
 \Delta H+2H(H^2-K)=\frac{R(R^2-2)}{4(R+\cos u)^3}=0.\] It is a nonumbilic Willmore torus. Retain a fixed isothermal disk \(\phi(z)\) about \(u=v=0\), where \(H\ne0\). For small \(\rho\), let \(\bar Y_\rho\) be the unweighted annular average of its conformal Gauss map on \(\rho<|z|<2\rho\), and \(\bar H_\rho=(\bar Y_\rho)_5-(\bar Y_\rho)_4\). The averages extend smoothly to \(\rho=0\), with \(\bar H_0\ne0\) and \(\langle \bar Y_0,\bar Y_0\rangle_\eta=1\).

The inversion formula on p. 14 shows that zero mean curvature after inversion holds precisely for poles on the round sphere \[S_\rho=\partial B(c_\rho,r_\rho),\qquad
 c_\rho=\frac{(\bar Y_\rho)_{123}}{\bar H_\rho},\qquad
 r_\rho=\frac{\sqrt{\langle \bar Y_\rho,\bar Y_\rho\rangle_\eta}}
               {|\bar H_\rho|}.
 \tag{P3.1}\] Its centres and radii form a smooth compact family, with radii bounded away from zero. A similarity alone has nonzero mean. Every other Mobius map is a similarity after an inversion at its finite pole; therefore its zero-mean condition still forces that pole onto \(S_\rho\).

Extend the retained torus patch as follows. Take two labelled copies of \(S_\rho\), join the torus to the first by a tube, and the first sphere to the second by another tube. All torus surgery is outside the retained disk. On the first sphere modify only two small outer caps \(C_1,C_2\); on the second modify a cap \(C_3\), chosen with closure disjoint from both. Include smoothing collars inside these caps. The unchanged pieces cover the whole sphere exactly: \[(S_\rho\setminus(C_1\cup C_2))
       \cup(S_\rho\setminus C_3)=S_\rho .\] Target self-intersections and coincident sheets are allowed for an immersion. The abstract connected sum \(T^2\#S^2\#S^2\) has genus one.

For completeness, the surgeries have uniform geometric bounds. Choose fixed angular cap sizes, regular connecting arcs with prescribed end tangencies, and tubes of a fixed positive radius less than half the reciprocal of their maximum curvature. Flatten small graph disks inside the surgery neighborhoods and attach regular surface-of-revolution collars. The nonzero meridian velocity and tube curvature margin ensure immersion. Move these choices smoothly with \(c_\rho,r_\rho\), including \(\rho=0\). After decreasing the parameter interval, this is a smooth compact family of immersions on a fixed source, with uniformly nondegenerate metrics and bounded second derivatives. Consequently \[\sup_\rho\int_\Sigma |A_{\Phi_\rho}|^2\,dA<\infty,\qquad
 S_\rho\subseteq\Phi_\rho(\Sigma).\]

Now set \(T_k=k+k_0\), \(\rho_k=e^{-2T_k}\), and \(\Phi_k=\Phi_{\rho_k}\). Embed the flat cylinder \([-T_k,T_k]\times S^1\) in the retained disk by \(z=\rho_ke^{t+i\theta}\). Its image is \(e^{-3T_k}\le|z|\le e^{-T_k}\). The immersion there is exactly the nonumbilic conformal Willmore patch. Since its Gauss map is smooth at the centre, \[\int_{C_k}|\nabla n_k|^2\le Ce^{-2T_k}\longrightarrow0.\] Every unit subcylinder and dyadic subannulus has no more energy. Taking \(k_0\) large meets the prescribed smallness threshold. The global curvature bound and fixed genus were already established. The middle dyadic annulus is precisely the one defining \(S_{\rho_k}\).

Any transformation satisfying (16) must now have a pole in \(S_{\rho_k}\subseteq\Phi_k(\Sigma)\); it cannot give a smooth closed \(\mathbb R^3\)-valued immersion of the same topology. This contradicts the existence assertion independently of estimate (15).

Impact and counterevidence. This invalidates the specific gauge lemma used to establish the core Section 5.1 estimates under their stated hypotheses, and warrants Major. It does not refute the weaker direct conclusion of Theorem 4.1, nor the main theorem for globally Willmore immersions.

Repair boundary. A global Willmore assumption rules out the inserted open spherical pieces on a connected nonumbilic surface, but strengthens the printed hypotheses. An approximate gauge with a new estimate is another possible route; that replacement has not been established here. The distinction between local and global Willmore assumptions must be propagated through the proof.

# Further findings and their limits

## P4. Minor: the \(S^3\) energy identity on p. 5

Write \(B=\int_\Sigma|dN|^2\,dA=\int_\Sigma|A|^2\,dA\). The Gauss equation in the unit three-sphere gives \[\mathcal E=\tfrac12B+\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma)-2\pi\chi(\Sigma).
 \tag{P4.1}\] The display following the reference to Gauss–Bonnet instead gives \(\mathcal E=B/2-\chi(\Sigma)\). For the singly covered Clifford torus, \(B=\mathcal E=4\pi^2\), whereas that right side is \(2\pi^2\). This is a closed genus-one example satisfying the stated setup. The subsequent boundedness equivalence remains correct: \[\mathcal E\le B,\qquad B=2\mathcal E-2\operatorname{Area}+4\pi\chi
                    \le2\mathcal E+4\pi\chi .\] The numerical identity requires correction, while the fixed-topology compactness input survives.

## P5. Minor: the collar models omit a twist and use \(\sinh\)

Location. Page 10, the torus paragraph and the hyperbolic metric display.

A general flat torus requires a rotated identification of the ends of its cut cylinder. For a degenerating example, use the lattice \(\Lambda_L=\mathbb Z+(\tfrac12+iL)\mathbb Z\), \(L>1\), and normalize its area. Before normalization the unique shortest vectors are \(\pm1\). Every perpendicular lattice vector is an integral multiple of \(2iL\); the pair \(1,2iL\) generates a sublattice of index two. No rectangular lattice basis can therefore represent this torus isometrically. The printed untwisted gluing misses this family, including \(L\to\infty\). A rotation in the endpoint gluing repairs the model and leaves the local cylinder analysis intact.

For the hyperbolic collar put \(z=re^{i\alpha}\), \(\log r=\ell\theta/(2\pi)\), and \(\alpha=\ell t/(2\pi)\). The half-plane metric pulls back to \[ds^2=\left(\frac{\ell}{2\pi\sin(\ell t/(2\pi))}\right)^2
                  (dt^2+d\theta^2).
 \tag{P5.1}\] The printed denominator has \(\sinh\). For that printed metric, the claimed central circle \(t=\pi^2/\ell\) has length \(\ell/\sinh(\pi/2)\), and the derivative of the logarithmic conformal factor there is \(-\ell\coth(\pi/2)/(2\pi)\ne0\). Thus it is not the claimed geodesic of length \(\ell\). Replacing \(\sinh\) by \(\sin\) repairs the explicit isometry. The underlying local conformal-cylinder description remains available.

## P6. Minor: the limiting straight line is null

Location. Theorem 4.1, p. 11, and Propositions 5.13, 5.15, and 5.19, pp. 26, 27, and 31.

These statements call the nonconstant affine limit time-like. If \(a+tv\) lies in the de Sitter quadric, coefficient comparison in \(\langle a+tv,a+tv\rangle_\eta=1\) gives \(\langle v,v\rangle_\eta=0\), \(\langle a,v\rangle_\eta=0\), and \(\langle a,a\rangle_\eta=1\). Its nonzero velocity is null. This is also the classification proved in Remark 5.14, p. 27, and used in the introduction and later argument.

The actual catenoid Gauss maps in P8 satisfy (26)–(28) and have a nonconstant affine null limit after reparametrization. Thus the causal-type discrepancy in the propositions is not based on an unrelated map. Replace time-like by light-like/null. This correction agrees with the actual convergence argument; it does not refute null-line convergence.

## P7. Minor: the inversion conformal factor has the wrong sign

Location. Page 15, the conformal-factor display in the proof of Lemma 5.2.

For \(I_a(x)=(x-a)/|x-a|^2\), \[(DI_a)^TDI_a=|x-a|^{-4}\operatorname{Id}.\] Consequently, wherever the immersion avoids \(a\), \[\lambda_{I_a\circ\phi}=\lambda_\phi-\log|\phi-a|^2,
 \tag{P7.1}\] not the printed plus sign. This calculation applies to every inversion considered there, without a further surface hypothesis. For example \(\phi(x,y)=(x,y,0)\), inverted about \(a=(0,0,1)\), has conformal factor \(-\log(1+x^2+y^2)\). The following two-sided distance requirement remains appropriate: a positive quantity and its reciprocal are both bounded under the same two-sided bound. This sign correction alone does not invalidate that estimate; the separate global avoidance failure is P3.

## P8. Major: a Poincare step loses the angular mean

Location. Page 29, proof of Lemma 5.17, the first Poincare step following the Cauchy–Schwarz estimate.

The replacement of the integral involving \(|\nabla Y|_\xi^2+|\nabla^2Y|_\xi^2\) by angularly differentiated quantities requires zero angular means that are not available. An actual conformal Gauss map detects the failure. Take \[\Phi(x,\theta)=\epsilon
           (\cosh x\cos\theta,\cosh x\sin\theta,x).\] This is a conformal minimal, hence Willmore, immersion, with \[Y=\left(\operatorname{sech}x\cos\theta,
          \operatorname{sech}x\sin\theta,-\tanh x,
          \epsilon h(x),\epsilon h(x)\right),\qquad
 h(x)=1-x\tanh x .
 \tag{P8.1}\] Restrict \(x=2L+t\), \(|t|\le L/2\), and choose \(\epsilon_L\asymp L^{-1}\) to give a fixed positive Euclidean oscillation \(\delta\). An exact choice exists by continuity: the angular oscillation tends to zero, and the change in \(h\) across the interval is asymptotic to \(L\). Then \(Y\) is uniformly bounded, \(\|\nabla Y\|_\infty=O(L^{-1})\), and \(\int|\nabla Y|_\xi^2=O(L^{-1})\). These are the boundedness, small-gradient and fixed-oscillation assumptions (26)–(28) used in the stated local lemma.

At the middle circle, \[\int_{S^1}|\nabla Y|_\xi^2
 =4\pi\bigl(\operatorname{sech}^2(2L)
                +\epsilon_L^2h'(2L)^2\bigr)\asymp L^{-2},\] whereas the integrals of \(|\partial_\theta\nabla Y|_\xi^2\) and \(|\partial_\theta\nabla^2Y|_\xi^2\) are \(O(e^{-4L})\). No uniform Poincare constant yields the printed replacement. After \(t=Lr\), (P8.1) converges to a nonconstant affine null line. This example is used for the local statements under (26)–(28); no unproved closed extension is assumed.

Core dependency and impact. The failed inequality is used in Lemma 5.17 to obtain \(\delta_k''\ge\delta_k\) and the maximum estimate (55). Page 30 explicitly combines (55) with (35) to obtain (57); that estimate controls (60)–(61) in Lemma 5.18. Proposition 5.19 on p. 31 then supplies the \(C^2\) conclusion of Theorem 4.1, used again for derivative control in Section 7. Thus this is an invalid step in a core convergence derivation and is assessed Major. The availability of a replacement does not change that dependency.

Counterevidence and repair. The conclusion \(\delta_k''\ge\delta_k\) is not refuted. On interior cylinders, first differentiate the harmonic-map equation in \(\theta\). For \(w=\partial_\theta Y\) and \(U=\nabla w\), both angular means vanish, and \[\Delta w=-|\nabla Y|_\eta^2w
                  -2\langle \nabla Y,\nabla w\rangle_\eta Y.\] Bounded \(Y\), small \(\nabla Y\), and interior elliptic estimates give small coefficients in the differentiated equation for \(U\). Integration by parts and Poincare applied to these zero-mean fields give \[\delta_k''\ge(2-o(1))\int|\nabla U|^2-o(1)\int|U|^2
                 \ge\delta_k .\] This supplies the same conclusion under the local hypotheses, with the interior domain losses made explicit. It replaces the invalid intermediate inequality and does not assert control at otherwise uncontrolled cylinder endpoints.

## P9. Minor: the displayed normal family is only locally immersed

Location. Page 35, final construction in Proposition 6.4.

The family \[\widetilde\Psi_t=\frac{\Psi+t fN}{\sqrt{1+t^2f^2}},
 \qquad f=-\langle Z,\nu \rangle_\eta,\] is asserted to remain an immersion throughout \((-1,1)\). For the ordinary Clifford torus and \(f=2\), its second circle factor vanishes at \(t=1/2\). The corresponding \(Z\) is the derivative of a genuine local normal variation and satisfies both constraints; the base surface has no umbilics. Compactness supplies a uniform small-time interval, and the compression \(t\mapsto\varepsilon\tanh(t/\varepsilon)\) preserves the initial derivative while keeping the family inside it. This repairs the construction without changing the proposition’s existence conclusion.

## P10. Minor: the Hessian mixes ambient and covariant derivatives

Location. Page 38, (82)–(83), and the ambient expansion on pp. 40–41.

For a harmonic map into the de Sitter quadric and a tangent field \(V\), the intrinsic integrand is \[|\nabla^{S^{3,1}}V|_\eta^2-|dY|_\eta^2|V|_\eta^2
                                  +|\langle V,dY\rangle_\eta|^2.\] As \(|dV|_\eta^2=|\nabla^{S^{3,1}}V|_\eta^2+|\langle V,dY\rangle_\eta|^2\), the ambient expression is \[Q_\mathcal D(V)=\int (|dV|_\eta^2-|dY|_\eta^2|V|_\eta^2).
 \tag{P10.1}\] Equation (83) retains the extra positive term, while the later expansion differentiates ambient vectors and a constant ambient \(E\).

For a compact-support check, take the Clifford conformal Gauss map \[Y(u,v)=\frac{(\cos u,\sin u,-\cos v,-\sin v,0)}{\sqrt2}.\] Let \(f\) be any nonzero smooth function supported in a coordinate cylinder. The variation \(Y_\tau(u,v)=Y(u+\tau f(u,v),v)\) has \(V=fY_u\), and direct differentiation gives \(\mathcal D''(0)=\tfrac12\int|df|^2\). The printed ambient expression adds \(\tfrac14\int f^2>0\). This tests the arbitrary compactly supported map variations specified before (82), not the normal-immersion constraint in (6).

Deleting the spurious nonnegative term improves a negative upper bound. This local derivative-convention correction does not by itself invalidate the instability argument.

## P11. Minor: the area conversion in Lemma 7.7 misses two

Location. Page 46, first line, and definition (78), p. 37.

By \(\alpha(t)^2=\int_{S^1}|\nabla Y|_\eta^2\,d\theta\) and \(ds=\alpha\,dt\), \[\int\alpha(t(s))\,ds
     =\int|\nabla Y|_\eta^2\,dt\,d\theta=2\mathcal A(Y).\] The source writes \(\mathcal A(Y)\). Its area hypothesis is at least \(\mu>0\), so the identity cannot agree accidentally at zero. Consistently restoring the factor two changes the constants, while preserving the negative sign and its required order.

## P12. Minor: the arclength chain rule has one missing factor

Location. Page 31, the last display in Proposition 5.19; compare the correctly normalized mean-curve formula on p. 26.

Put \(v=|\partial_tY^*|_\xi>0\), \(ds=v\,dt\), and \(W=Y-Y^*\). Direct application of \(\partial_s=v^{-1}\partial_t\) gives \[W_{ss}=\frac{W_{tt}}{v^2}
       -\frac{\langle Y_t^*,Y_{tt}^*\rangle_\xi}{v^4}\,W_t .
 \tag{P12.1}\] The source has \(v^3\) in the second denominator, although \(v_t=\langle Y_t^*,Y_{tt}^*\rangle_\xi/v\). This is not an identity that can hold for the actual local family in P8: there \(W_t\ne0\), \(v\ne1\), and \(\langle Y_t^*,Y_{tt}^*\rangle_\xi\ne0\) for large \(x\). Indeed \(Y_t^*=(0,0,-\operatorname{sech}^2x,
\epsilon h',\epsilon h')\); its scalar product with its derivative is negative for large \(x\), since \(h'<0<h''\). Thus that family also gives a nonvacuous check within (26)–(28).

The relative estimates \(W_t=o(v)\), \(W_{tt}=o(v^2)\), and \(Y_{tt}^*=o(v^2)\) still make the corrected second term \(o(1)\). The missing factor must be restored, but it does not defeat the intended interior \(C^2\) conclusion.

# Positive checks and unresolved proof dependencies

## The constrained variation problem

The signs in (10), (66), and (70)–(71) are consistent. A direct global argument verifies Proposition 4.2. Let \(\nu=(\Psi,1)\), \(q=|\nabla Y|_\eta^2\), and suppose \(Z\) is tangent to the quadric and satisfies \[\langle \nu,\Delta Z-qZ\rangle_\eta=0,\qquad \langle \nu,dZ\rangle_\eta=0.\] Put \(f=-\langle Z,\nu\rangle_\eta\). A real normal immersion variation with velocity \(fN\) produces a smooth linear differential operator \(T(f)\), the derivative of its conformal Gauss map. The necessary constraints hold for \(T(f)\), and \(\langle T(f),\nu\rangle_\eta=-f\).

For \(W=Z-T(f)\), one obtains \(\langle W,Y\rangle_\eta=\langle W,\nu\rangle_\eta=\langle W,d\nu\rangle_\eta=0\). The orthogonal complement of \(\operatorname{Span}\{\nu,Y,\partial_1\nu,\partial_2\nu\}\) is \(\mathbb R\nu\), since \(\Psi\) is an immersion. Thus \(W=\beta\nu\). Lemma 6.3 gives \(\langle \nu,\Delta(\beta\nu)-q\beta\nu\rangle_\eta=-2\beta\), forcing \(W=0\). This proof crosses umbilics without dividing by \(\mathring A\).

Along genuine conformal-Gauss-map variations, \(\mathcal A(Y_t)=\mathcal E(\Psi_t)/2\). This defines the constrained Hessian at umbilics without asserting differentiability of unrestricted area at every degenerate ambient map. The linear map \(T\) is bijective onto the two-constraint space and preserves negative definite subspaces up to the positive factor two. The intended ordinary-index equality is therefore supported.

## Negative fields, supports, and the final sign

Substitution in (79)–(81) verifies both linear constraints. The reconstructed velocity \(-\langle Z,\nu \rangle_\eta N\) retains compact support. Cutoffs vanish on full endpoint neighborhoods, permitting smooth extension by zero. Disjoint supports give zero mixed Hessian terms, as needed for the corrected index count.

The little-\(o\) error in Lemma 7.5 is not by itself a sign obstruction. Set \[A_k=\int|\nabla f_k|^2,\qquad
 B_k=\int f_k^2|\nabla Y_k|_\eta^2 .\] The area upper bound implies \(A_k=O(\ell_k^{-2})\), and the scalar estimate gives \(B_k-2A_k\ge c\ell_k^{-2}\), after cutoff errors. Hence \(\ell_k^{-2}+A_k+B_k\le C(B_k-2A_k)\), and the stated remainder can be absorbed by the negative main part. No contrary sign result is inferred merely from the presence of \(o(B_k)\).

The final subdivision is justified eventually in a selected subcylinder of area \(\lambda/2\), partitioned into \(J\) equal-area pieces. A liminf lower bound does not warrant, for every \(k\), the printed subdivision of the entire collar into pieces whose total area is at most \(\lambda\). The continuous cumulative-area construction supplies the required disjoint supports. This is a proof-selection clarification, not a separate geometric counterexample.

## Limits of the verification

This review has not supplied a complete replacement for the uniform long-collar estimates of Lemma 7.5. Equation (77) is derived using Euclidean arclength, while (78) introduces Lorentz-average arclength. Their rates need an explicit comparison. The estimates for a fixed global \(E\) under local choices of constant Lorentz gauges also require uniform control. The full corrected field can transform covariantly; failure of one normalized-lift summand to be invariant does not disprove covariance of the full field.

The Appendix C perturbation is generally nonconformal. Subsequent flat-coordinate calculations must track a pulled-back metric or estimate the perturbation errors. Arbitrarily small smooth perturbations are available, which is relevant counterevidence. No all-hypothesis Willmore sequence violating the instability conclusion has been established for these concerns. They remain proof-completeness limitations and are not additional confirmed adverse findings.

The review followed the harmonic-map and angular-energy equations, the comparison of \(\alpha_k,\beta_k,\gamma_k\), the differentiated estimates for \(C^2\) convergence, and the construction and support of constrained fields. The external regularity, compactness and umbilic-set theorems remain identified dependencies; an independent reproof of all cited literature is not claimed.
