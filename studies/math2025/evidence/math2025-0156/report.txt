Abstract: The reviewed source is the 25-page arXiv manuscript 2302.07069v1. An explicit smooth complete intersection refutes the prescribed-fiber lifting assertion in Claim 25.2. Its use in the gluing argument gives a Major defect in the displayed derivation of the main deformation theorem; this is not a counterexample to that theorem itself. A separate finite collection of disconnected handles refutes the unrestricted statement of Theorem 44 and supports a local Minor finding. The unrestricted union-of-free-curves assertion also fails, as shown by two transverse plane conics, giving a further local Minor finding. The degree/count strengthenings of the residue-field lemma have separate elementary counterexamples. The arithmetic arguments were checked with their geometric dependencies and field hypotheses kept explicit. No conclusion is asserted about an unavailable journal revision. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and operative hypotheses

Theorem 1 concerns finitely many maps from nodal curves to a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field, whose pushforward cycles are algebraically equivalent. It appends mutually isomorphic curve maps and asks for deformation equivalence of the resulting maps. Theorem 2 adds a perfect ground field and equality of Euler characteristics. Its endpoints descend to the specified extension field, and its common tail descends to the ground field; Remark 3 explicitly does not descend the entire connecting family.

In Paragraph 25, for \(n=\dim X\), an \(H^{ci}\)-curve is \[L=H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_{n-1}\cap H_n^*
 \subset X\times\mathbb P^1,\qquad
 H_i\in p_1^*|H|,\quad H_n^*\in|p_1^*H+p_2^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(2)|.\] It must be smooth, and a length-two subscheme \(T\subset L\) is marked. The linear system \(H=mA\), \(A\) very ample and \(m\geq2\), is explicitly allowed. Claim 25.2 purports to lift an arbitrary reduced base curve in the marked length-two Hilbert scheme with arbitrary prescribed values in this family at finitely many base points.

In Theorem 44 the ambient variety is smooth projective of dimension at least three over a perfect field. Its bounded locally closed set \(W\) of nodal handles is not required to parametrize connected curves. A comb with \(r\) teeth is balanced when every irreducible handle component has at least \(r/(2c)\) teeth, where \(c\) is the number of components. Definition 42 further requires freeness and the vanishing of \(H^1\) after a divisor of degree at most \(d\) on every handle subcurve.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (Prescribed-fiber lifting fails when projected marks coalesce).  Assessment: Major. The affected assertions are the projective-bundle description in Paragraph 25 and Claim 25.2, p. 11. Corollary 26 on the same page invokes that claim directly.

## A complete admissible input

Work over \(\mathbb C\), put \(X=\mathbb P^2\), and take \(H=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(2)\). On \(B=\mathbb A^1_t\) mark the two points \[((0,0),[1:0]),\qquad ((t,0),[0:1])\] in the affine chart \(Z=1\) of \(X\times\mathbb P^1\). They remain distinct even at \(t=0\), so they define an honest morphism \(g:B\to\operatorname{Hilb}_{(2)}(X\times\mathbb P^1)\); designate the first point as the marked one.

Equation reference: eq:L, eq:binary. At \(0\) prescribe the conic \(Q_0=(XZ+Y^2=0)\) and the intersection \(L_0\) of \(Q_0\times\mathbb P^1\) with the divisor \[
 X^2S^2+Z^2SW+(YZ+X^2)W^2=0.\] This divisor has bidegree \((2,2)\), as required. Parametrizing \(Q_0\) by \([X:Y:Z]=[-u^2:uv:v^2]\) gives \[
 u^4S^2+v^4SW+(uv^3+u^4)W^2=0.\] The coefficients do not vanish simultaneously, so the projection to \(Q_0\) is finite of degree two. Its discriminant is \(\Delta=v^8-4u^5v^3-4u^8\). It is nonzero at infinity and is squarefree: a common root of \(1-4z^5-4z^8\) and its derivative would satisfy \(z^3=-5/8\) and \(z^5=2/3\), giving incompatible values of \(z^{15}\). Thus \(L_0\) is smooth. At \([u:v]=[0:1]\), equation [eq:binary] (#eq:binary) is \(SW=0\), so \(L_0\) contains both prescribed marks. This verifies, rather than assumes, that the specified fiber belongs to the source’s \(H^{ci}\)-family.

## The obstruction

If the asserted lift existed, the pulled-back conics \(Q_t\) would form a family regular near \(0\), specializing to \(Q_0\), and passing through \((0,0)\) and \((t,0)\). Write its affine equation as \[q_t(x,y)=a_{20}(t)x^2+a_{11}(t)xy+a_{02}(t)y^2
              +a_{10}(t)x+a_{01}(t)y+a_{00}(t).\] Choose a local projective coefficient chart containing \(Q_0\). The incidence conditions imply \[a_{00}(t)=0,\qquad t a_{10}(t)+t^2a_{20}(t)=0.\] Over the punctured base, \(a_{10}(t)+t a_{20}(t)=0\), hence regularity forces \(a_{10}(0)=0\). But \(q_0=x+y^2\) has \(a_{10}(0)\neq0\). This contradiction rules out even a rational lift defined at the prescribed point. A dominant ramified base change does not remove it: replace \(t\) by the nonzero local base parameter function and cancel it in the same identity.

The source’s bundle argument overlooks the rank jump in conditions imposed on \(p_1^*H\): two distinct projections impose two value conditions, whereas two points over the same \(x\) impose only one. Their limiting incidence nevertheless remembers a tangent direction. The extra \(\mathbb P^1\)-coordinate keeps the marks distinct but does not make the pulled-back divisors separate them.

## Counterevidence, dependency and repair boundary

This uses the most favorable reading of the forgetful map in Claim 25.2; its notation is not the objection. No stated condition excludes two different points in a common \(p_1\)-fiber. Increasing the degree does not remove the same first-order restriction. On a locus where the projections impose independent constant-rank conditions, the projective-bundle interpolation argument can work.

The explicit dependency is Claim 25.2 \(\to\) Corollary 26 \(\to\) the proof of Theorem 29 (p. 12) \(\to\) Theorem 31 (p. 13) \(\to\) Proposition 34 (pp. 14–15) \(\to\) Paragraphs 35–36 proving Theorems 1–2. These proofs require a justified gluing construction in place of the false unrestricted lifting assertion. This demonstrated invalid step is closely tied to the central contribution, which supports Major severity. It does not show that Theorems 1–2, or their arithmetic consequences, are false.

Using sufficiently ample divisors on \(X\times\mathbb P^1\) in every factor would remove this particular failure of length-two separation. It also changes the curve family and its double-cover description; the interpolation, moving and later teeth properties would need to be re-established. Another possible repair is to allow additional components of the base contracted to a bad marked point, varying the connector first inside a fixed incidence fiber. Theorem 29 permits base changes that may leave room for this maneuver. Such components are absent from fixed-base Claim 25.2, and the required construction at arbitrary pivots and branches is not supplied by its proof. Neither repair route is claimed to be a complete replacement proof here.

Problem 2 (The comb theorem omits connectedness of the handles).  Assessment: Minor. Theorem 44, p. 19, asserts that a single \(r>0\) gives both connected fibers and surjectivity on extension-field points. Its proof in Paragraph 47 moves teeth across nodes, without a connected-handle hypothesis.

## A counterexample to the existential choice of \(r\)

Let \(Y=\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}\), \(d=1\), and let \(W\) consist of two points: one represents \(C_2\), a union of two disjoint lines; the other represents \(C_3\), a union of three disjoint lines. This is a bounded locally closed subset of \(\operatorname{Hilb}^{nodal}_1(Y)\). Take all pointed lines as the teeth. The tangent-direction map is the isomorphism with \(\mathbb P(TY)\); its fibers are geometrically irreducible. For a fixed marked point \(x\), evaluation away from it identifies the universal line with \(Y\setminus\{x\}\). Finally \(N_{\ell/Y}=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(1)^{\oplus2}\), so the vanishing required in (39.5) holds. All hypotheses on the teeth are satisfied.

For transverse teeth meeting just their designated handle and avoiding one another, these combs are free and 1-free along every handle subcurve. One can check this without assuming Theorem 44: the normal restriction on a handle line is a positive elementary modification of \(\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus2}\); on a tooth it is \(\mathcal O(1)\oplus\mathcal O(2)\). The latter evaluates surjectively at its unique node and remains globally generated after vanishing there. The normalization sequence, using the teeth independently at their nodes, gives \(H^1(N(-D))=0\) for every allowed divisor of degree at most one. The same argument proves global generation and applies after removing any handle components; detached teeth cause no obstruction.

If \(r=1\), there is no balanced comb on \(C_2\). If \(r=2\), there is no balanced comb on \(C_3\). Thus (44.3) fails in these cases. For every integer \(r\geq3\), balanced combs on \(C_2\) have allocations \[m_1+m_2=r,\qquad m_i\geq \lceil r/4\rceil .\] There are \(r-2\lceil r/4\rceil+1\geq2\) such allocations, each realized by the free combs just described. Within the fiber over the fixed disconnected handle, the number of attachment points on each open and closed component is locally constant. The distinct allocations therefore lie in disjoint nonempty open and closed pieces. Consequently (44.1) fails for every remaining \(r\). The two-element choice of \(W\) is essential: a singleton handle at its smallest feasible tooth number would not refute the existential quantifier.

## Impact and boundary

The manuscript does not globally define curves to be connected; it explicitly introduces connectedness when needed, for example in Lemma 37. Allowing triple nodes does not connect two disjoint handle components. A corrected connected-handle hypothesis removes the obstruction exhibited here and matches the tooth-sliding argument. The intended main applications first arrange connected handles. No central damage from this separate omission is established, hence Minor rather than Major severity for this problem. A complete re-proof of all uniformity and field-point statements of a corrected Theorem 44 is not asserted.

Problem 3 (A union of free curves need not be free).  Assessment: Minor. Claim 38.3, p. 17, says that if \(C_1,C_2\) are free and \(C_1+C_2\) is locally unobstructed, their union is free. There is no restriction on the number of intersection points.

In the plane \(W=0\) of \(\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}\), take \[C_1=(XZ-Y^2=0),\qquad C_2=(X^2+Y^2+Z^2=0).\] Both are smooth conics. Under the parametrization \([X:Y:Z]=[u^2:uv:v^2]\) of \(C_1\), the second equation becomes \(u^4+u^2v^2+v^4=0\). It has four distinct roots, so their union \(C\) is a connected nodal plane quartic and has locally unobstructed singularities. Each conic has \(N_{C_i/\mathbb P^3}=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(4)\oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(2)\), hence is free. But \(C\) is the complete intersection of degrees \(1,4\), and therefore \[N_{C/\mathbb P^3}=\mathcal O_C(1)\oplus\mathcal O_C(4),\qquad
 \omega_C=\mathcal O_C(1),\qquad
 H^1(C,\mathcal O_C(1))\simeq H^0(C,\mathcal O_C)^*\simeq\mathbb C.\] Thus \(H^1(C,N_{C/\mathbb P^3})\ne0\), contradicting freeness as defined immediately before the claim. Local unobstructedness of the nodes does not remove this global cohomology group.

This refutes the unrestricted union assertion, including its displayed normal-sheaf justification. It does not show failure of the specialized operation of attaching one tooth at one point; that operation can be checked using normal restrictions and the normalization sequence, as done independently in Problem 2. The full intended tooth-sliding and triple-node argument still needs its appropriate local justification. No additional central damage from this generalization alone is established, so the classification is Minor.

Problem 4 (The degree and counting strengthenings of Lemma 60 are false).  Assessment: Minor. In Lemma 60, p. 24, part (60.1) requires infinitely many residue degrees when the perfect field is infinite, and part (60.2) requires at least \(q^d/2\) closed points of degree \(d\) for every \(d\) in an infinite set when \(k=\mathbb F_q\). Both fail already for the identity morphism of the affine line.

For \(k=\mathbb C\), every closed point of \(\mathbb A^1_k\) has degree one. Thus no infinite subset of degrees can satisfy (60.1). For \(k=\mathbb F_q\), let \(N_d\) count degree-\(d\) closed points of \(\mathbb A^1_k\). Each contributes \(d\) distinct \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\)-points, so \(dN_d\leq q^d\). For every \(d>2\) this contradicts the asserted \(N_d\geq q^d/2\); an infinite permissible degree set is impossible. These inputs satisfy perfectness and the nonconstant-morphism hypothesis.

Poonen’s qualitative equal-residue-field assertion is compatible with both examples. In the descent construction, applying it on successive nonempty opens avoids finitely many forbidden attachment points. Thus the proof need not use either false strengthening. The infinite-degree assertion is not repaired merely by inserting a missing factor \(1/d\) into the finite-field count: the degree set itself must also be qualified for infinite fields. The statement requires correction, but the present evidence establishes only a local Minor defect. No replacement uniform counting theorem for all bounded families in (60.3) is certified.

# Other checks and limits

The normalization contribution to Euler characteristic in Paragraph 50 is supported on a zero-cycle and is divisible by the index. The canonical divisor gives \(\operatorname{ind}(X)\mid2\chi(C)\). These checks explain why the invariant \(2\chi(C)/\operatorname{ind}(X)\bmod2\), rather than an unqualified integer genus, controls the possible order-two kernel. The descent bookkeeping of Theorem 2 is conditional on the earlier geometric construction: equal-residue-field attachment points allow one to add Galois orbits of tails while retaining distinct nodes. Poonen’s Corollary 2 [Poo] supplies that qualitative point-existence input over a perfect field.

Several observations were not promoted to additional substantive Problems. The sign of the node term in Example 9 does not change its congruence obstruction. Lemma 52’s construction must be read for effective cycles; unrestricted negative coefficients cannot be pushforwards of curves. Positive/negative decomposition supplies effective inputs in its intended application. These local presentation corrections do not establish an additional failure of the principal conclusions.

Paragraph 55 also invokes the perfect-field criterion (6.2) over the fraction field of a Henselian DVR with perfect residue field. Such a fraction field need not be perfect in equal positive characteristic. An extension of the criterion or a descent reduction needs checking; this review has not supplied one and does not turn that unresolved point into a counterexample. The loop construction was checked as a dependency trace, not as an independent proof bypassing Problems 1–2. No missing author code was treated as a mathematical defect.
