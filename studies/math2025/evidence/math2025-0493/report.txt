Abstract: The acquired arXiv version 2001.11977v2 was reviewed. A finite-domain counterexample invalidates the area-to-length estimate used for the non-uniform-integrability clause of Corollary 1.2. A localized replacement, including a boundary argument and a uniform tail bound, repairs that implication conditional on the first clause. This is a Major issue in a specific derivation, not a counterexample to the final corollary or Theorems 1–3. A domain error in Lemma 4.5 is Minor. The XOR mechanism and finite expansion were independently checked; external percolation and compactness theorems remain identified dependencies.

Title: A Technical Review of

# Reviewed object and theorem ranges

The authors are Nicholas Crawford, Alexander Glazman, Matan Harel, and Ron Peled; the associated DOI is `10.1214/24-AOP1712`. The reviewed object is the 35-page PDF of arXiv:2001.11977v2, posted 13 June 2024, whose title page bears 17 June 2024. These dates are not conflated. Its SHA256 is

ae2b8bf449b72aca63ea277ce332a0fa7cff4c8402edaed3b066496a9e50305e

No equivalence with an unexamined journal-final PDF is asserted.

The body on source pages 1–32 was read; references on pages 32–35 were read as bibliographic entries, not as the cited papers. Theorem 1 (p. 5) concerns translation-invariant Gibbs measures for \(1\le n\le1+\delta\), \(1-\delta\le x\le1\), and gives a bi-infinite-path or infinitely-many-surrounding-loops alternative. Corollary 1.1 additionally needs \(x\le1/\sqrt n\) and an external dichotomy for RSW. Theorem 2 (p. 7) requires \(n=1\), \(1<x\le\sqrt3\), and boundary configurations without bi-infinite paths. Theorem 3 (p. 8) concerns the torus for \(1\le n\le2,x=1\). These restrictions are operative.

# Checks of the main mechanisms

In Lemma 1.5 (p. 9), the loops meeting a finite circuit form a finite configuration when bi-infinite paths are excluded. The parity spin at the origin changes under XOR with the surrounding circuit. Every relevant resulting component meets the circuit, giving the diameter conclusion. The no-bi-infinite-path hypothesis cannot simply be omitted.

For the antiferromagnetic proof, (2.5), p. 13, is wired FK at parameter \(1/x\), conditioned on a decreasing compatibility event. Positive association gives the correct direction for decreasing vacant-circuit events in (2.9), p. 14. The endpoint \(1/\sqrt3\) supplies the uniform parameter range. Conditional XOR preserves the joint weight and yields the factor \(1/2\). External FK crossing estimates were not independently reproved.

For the torus, the blue probability \((n-1)/n\le1/2\) gives domination of the blue marginal by the red marginal; it does not make the two colors independent. An independent check of Lemma 3.3 uses mod-two homology: an essential simple torus curve has primitive integral class and hence nonzero mod-two class; unions of contractible curves have zero class, and XOR adds the classes. The external rerouting argument invoked in Lemma 3.4 remains a dependency. Conditional on that lemma, the \(1/4\) probability calculation is consistent. The \(\rho\) subscript \(1\) in (3.2), p. 17, should be \(n\).

Summing vacant-edge defects in (4.1), p. 18, gives \(x^{-|E|+|\omega|}\), while summing colors gives \(n^{\ell(\omega)}\). The extra factor is constant in \(\omega\). An independently written exact-rational computation checked all eight even configurations on a chain of three hexagons at \(n=3/2,x=4/5\), evaluating 72,008 joint color/defect states through product sums. All marginals equaled \(x^{-|E|}x^{|\omega|}n^{\ell(\omega)}\); XOR closure and involutivity also held. This does not test asymptotic percolation.

The domination proof (pp. 21–24) uses planar five-degeneracy, loop clouds of at most six sites, edge clouds of at most three, and \[p(n,x)=\min\!\left\{3\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{1/6}+6(1-x)^{1/3},1\right\}.\] Underlying cloud coordinates are independent before taking their coordinatewise maximum. Each face meets at most three distinct loops and six edges. The graph used for the external planar-limit theorem is simple, after deleting multiple edges and self-loops.

Lemma 4.10 (p. 26) derives uniform integrability of degrees from translation invariance, long-loop densities and absence of bi-infinite paths, without assuming bounded maximum degree. In Proposition 4.2, subsequential compactness gives the full-sequence conclusion by application to every potentially bad subsequence. The change of root on p. 28 is valid after averaging with a bounded density ratio, not as a pointwise comparison of independently sampled roots. Proposition 4.11 (pp. 28–30) uses Følner recentering, finite energy, Burton–Keane uniqueness, and removal of wiring ambiguity when at most two crossings remain. Those logical uses were checked; the external uniqueness, compactness and planar percolation results were not independently re-established.

# Problem 1: exterior loops and the area estimate

Classification: Major, localized to the second clause of Corollary 1.2.

## Exact assertion and hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:false. Corollary 1.2 (p. 7) permits arbitrary boundary loop configurations \(\xi_k\). The face \(f_k\) is uniform in the faces \(F_k\) of \(D_k=\Lambda_k(0)\), independently of the configuration. As defined on p. 6, \(L(f)\) is the length of the longest loop bordering \(f\), with value infinity for a bordering bi-infinite path. On p. 31, \(V(f)\) uses the outermost loop of the entire configuration surrounding \(f\). The first display on p. 32, obtained from (4.10), asserts \[
\mathbb P[V(f_k)>s]\le6c\,\mathbb E[
 L(f_k)\mathbf1_{\{L(f_k)>\sqrt{s/c}\}}],\] where \(c>0\) is an absolute isoperimetric constant. The sum in (4.10) includes all outermost loops and all bordering faces. Dividing by \(|F_k|\) does not convert this into an average over interior faces.

## Counterexample with all required hypotheses

Fix \(D_k\), and let \(M=|E(D_k)|\). Set \(n=x=1\), in the allowed rectangle. Let \(\xi_k\) consist of one large finite simple loop \(\Gamma\) strictly outside \(D_k\), with an empty intervening region, surrounding \(D_k\) but bordering none of its faces. There are no bi-infinite paths. Every variable loop lies in \(D_k\) and has at most \(M\) edges. For every allowed interior configuration, \[V(f)=\operatorname{Area}(\Gamma),\qquad L(f)\le M\quad(f\in F_k).\] Given any proposed \(c>0\), take \(s=c(M+1)^2\) and choose \(\Gamma\) with area greater than \(s\). Equation [eq:false] (#eq:false) then says \(1\le0\). No change of its finite multiplicative constant fixes the counterexample.

A separate computation instantiated the geometry: the seven-face \(D=\Lambda_1\) has 24 vertices, 30 edges, and 128 even configurations. All were enumerated at \(n=x=1\). The boundary of \(\Lambda_{20}\) has length 246 and area 1261. With illustrative \(c=1,s=961\), the cutoff is 31, whereas the largest bordering-loop length over all configurations is 22. The computed left probability is one and the right tail expectation is zero. The analytic construction, rather than this illustrative value of \(c\), proves failure for every positive constant. No author code was executed.

## Alternate reading and a localized repair

Restricting the outermost loop to loops contained in \(D_k\) avoids this example, but is not the stated definition. That interpretation also requires boundary control and a lower bound uniform in the tail threshold. The following repair proves the affected implication conditional on the first clause of Corollary 1.2.

Equation reference: eq:fixed. Let \(\mathcal O_D\) be the inclusion-maximal loops contained in the hexagonal domain \(D\), and \(V_D(f)\) the area of its surrounding member, or zero. Their interiors are disjoint. A safe non-optimal pathwise bound is \[
\frac1{|F_D|}\sum_{f\in F_D}\mathbf1_{\{V_D(f)>s\}}
\le\frac{36c}{|F_D|}\sum_{f\in F_D}
L(f)\mathbf1_{\{L(f)>\sqrt{s/c}\}} .\] Indeed, area is at most \(c|\ell|^2\), each loop has at least \(|\ell|/6\) interior bordering faces, and each face borders at most six distinct loops. All such interior faces lie in \(D\), and \(L(f)\ge|\ell|\). Summing gives [eq:fixed] (#eq:fixed). The constant 36 is deliberately generous.

Assume uniform integrability of \(\{L(f_k)\}\). For fixed \(r\), a crossing in \(C_{r,R}(f_k)\) forces a nearby face, within distance \(r\), to border a loop of length at least a fixed multiple of \(R-r\), or a bi-infinite path. Summation over those nearby faces gives a constant depending on \(r\) times the tail probability of \(L(f_k)\), plus a width-\(r\) boundary-strip error \(O(r/k)\). Tightness yields \[\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mathbb P[C_{r,R}(f_k)]=0 .\] The first clause of the corollary therefore gives arbitrarily large \(r\), with fixed \(R=R(r)\), such that \(\liminf_k\mathbb P[S_{r,R}(f_k)]\ge1/2\). Discarding a width-\(R\) strip costs \(O(R/k)\); the witnessing loop is then contained in \(D_k\) and has area at least \(|F_{\Lambda_r}|\). Put \(s_r=|F_{\Lambda_r}|/2\), to respect strict inequalities. Then \(\liminf_k\mathbb P[V_{D_k}(f_k)>s_r]\ge1/2\). Averaging [eq:fixed] (#eq:fixed) implies \[\liminf_k\mathbb E[L(f_k)\mathbf1_{\{L(f_k)>\sqrt{s_r/c}\}}]
\ge(72c)^{-1}\] along arbitrarily large \(r\), contradicting uniform integrability. Positivity depending on \(r\) alone would not suffice.

## Dependency and impact boundary

The invalid step converts surrounding area into a local length tail, the decisive step for non-uniform-integrability. Repair requires localization, a boundary argument and uniform tail quantifiers, rather than one typographical change. The counterexample establishes an invalid derivation as printed. It does not disprove the corollary, whose affected implication is repaired above, or Theorems 1–3. No whole-paper failure is claimed.

# Problem 2: the domain of the bijection

Classification: Minor. Lemma 4.5, p. 22, claims a bijection from the full cube \(\{0,1\}^{V}\) to coherent triples for a fixed uncolored configuration. Immediately above, face coordinates are deterministic functions of edge and selected loop coordinates; the projection discards face coordinates.

For a single-hexagon domain and the empty uncolored configuration, there are six edge bits and one face bit. The full cube has \(2^7=128\) states, while the coherent triples are the \(2^6=64\) defect-edge sets. Every triple has two preimages. This enumeration was independently executed. At \(n=1\), \(0<x<1\), all 64 edge patterns have positive probability, and the admissible face bit is the OR of the six edge bits.

Restricting the domain to states satisfying the deterministic face rule gives the correct bijection. The significant counterevidence against a stronger defect is that \(\nu_{G,n,x}\) already has that support: its pushforward law and the use in Proposition 4.6 survive unchanged. The repair affects a statement’s domain, not a demonstrated final theorem.
