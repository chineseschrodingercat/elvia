Abstract: No substantive mathematical defect was confirmed in the audited definitions, main reductions, regularization limits, Gaussian constant, or application scopes. The report gives explicit checks of the noncompact Gaussian limit and of the passage to even log-concave majorants. A direct trace argument independently verifies the Kolesnikov–Werner Gaussian constant, including its distinct two-input and multiple-input equality cases. These are bounded positive checks, not a certification of every cited external result.

Title: A mathematical audit of
A generalized Legendre duality relation and Gaussian saturation

# Source, scope, and outcome

The reviewed article is Shohei Nakamura and Hiroshi Tsuji, A generalized Legendre duality relation and Gaussian saturation, DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01382-5 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01382-5) [NT]. The exact supplied publisher PDF has 49 pages, printed 607–655, and records online publication on 20 October 2025. PDF page \(r\) below corresponds to printed page \(606+r\). The primary source and source manifest were hash-verified. A targeted publisher/arXiv correction lookup found no separate correction notice; this is not an exhaustive absence claim.

Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 6–7) takes \(c_i>0\), an orthogonal block decomposition \(\mathbb R^N=\bigoplus_i\mathbb R^{n_i}\), an arbitrary symmetric matrix \(Q\), and nonnegative even integrable functions satisfying \[\prod_i f_i(x_i)^{c_i}\le e^{-x^TQx}\quad\hbox{for every }x.\] It bounds the product of their integrals by the supremum over centered Gaussians with the same constraint. This supremum may be infinite. Theorem 1.3 (PDF pp. 11–12), concerning the inverse Brascamp–Lieb functional, additionally requires log-concavity. It is proved for positive exponents and the stated coordinate projections; a theorem for arbitrary linear maps or negative exponents is not being asserted.

The outcome is no confirmed substantive finding. Gaussian saturation identifies the optimal constant; it does not, by itself, classify every functional equality case. The paper proves the exponential-kernel case \(\rho(t)=e^{-t/(m-1)}\) of the more general Kolesnikov–Werner functional conjecture, as explicitly qualified on PDF pp. 5–6. It does not claim the full arbitrary-\(\rho\) result. Independent scientific acceptance remains pending.

# Regularized inverse inequality: checks of the main mechanism

## Compactness and extended-valued cases

For normalized densities in the regularized class \(0<\lambda\le\Lambda<\infty\), Lemma 2.2 gives the fixed envelopes \[\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-\Lambda|x|^2/2}
 \le f(x)\le
 \left(\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}\right)^{n/2}e^{-\lambda|x|^2/2}.\] Evenness places the extremum of the convex/concave normalized potential at zero. The envelopes and local convexity estimates supply equicontinuity and local boundedness. A common diagonal subsequence over the finitely many inputs gives a positive limit in the same regularized class. Dominated convergence preserves each integral, and Fatou’s lemma gives the needed lower semicontinuity for arbitrary \(Q\), without requiring the weighted integrand to have a common integrable upper bound. This verifies the extremizer construction in Theorem 2.1 (PDF pp. 14–16).

The fixed lower envelope makes the regularized inverse constant strictly positive. In the convolution proof one should separate the case where it is infinite: then the Gaussian constant is also infinite because the Gaussian class is a subclass. Cancellation is needed only in the finite positive case. This supplies the harmless extended-value qualification to the argument.

## Self-convolution and the Gaussian limit

For fixed \(x_i\), the paired function \[y_i\longmapsto
 f_i((x_i+y_i)/\sqrt2)\,f_i((x_i-y_i)/\sqrt2)\] is even in \(y_i\), positive, and retains both curvature bounds. The \(45^\circ\) change of variables preserves \(x^TQx+y^TQy\) and the joint Jacobian. Integration of the paired function gives \(2^{n_i/2}(f_i*f_i)(\sqrt2x_i)\), a normalized density. These checks verify the factors and inequality direction in Proposition 2.4 (PDF pp. 17–19).

The rescaled convolution remains in the same curvature class, as shown in Appendix Lemma A.2 (PDF p. 46). The initial even density has mean zero, finite covariance, and nondegenerate covariance because of the Gaussian envelopes. The normalized repeated convolutions therefore have a centered Gaussian limit. The use of almost-everywhere convergence after \(L^1\) convergence can be made precise by taking one common subsequence for the finitely many inputs; convergence of the entire sequence almost everywhere is unnecessary. The same envelopes and curvature bounds persist, so the limit belongs to the permitted Gaussian class. Fatou’s lemma then has the correct direction for the infimum.

## Removing regularization

The order of limits in Lemma 2.7 is significant: first \(\Lambda\to\infty\), then the additional lower-curvature parameter tends to zero. The preliminary quartic cutoff \(f_i e^{-\varepsilon_0|x_i|^4}\) preserves evenness and log-concavity. Its removal uses monotone convergence and \(c_i>0\).

Gaussian mollification supplies positivity even when the original support has zeros. The resulting lower curvature approaches the target from below as \(\Lambda\to\infty\); monotonicity of the classes permits the fixed smaller target needed in the proof. The split estimate on PDF pp. 22–23 gives quartic and Gaussian tails. Mixed factors have a uniform dominator: for a fixed \(\Lambda_0\), \[e^{-c\varepsilon_0 r^4}
 \le \exp\!\left(\frac{c\Lambda_0^2}{4\varepsilon_0}\right)
       e^{-c\Lambda_0r^2}.\] Choose \(\Lambda_0\) large enough that the weighted block Gaussian dominates \(e^{x^TQx}\). This bounds every term in the mixed product, not just its all-Gaussian term. The resulting \(\Lambda\)-independent dominator justifies the limiting weighted integral. No failed limiting direction was found.

# The duality reduction and its boundary cases

## Uniformity in the Gaussian limit

Lemma 3.1 (PDF pp. 24–25) uses \(c_i(p)=(d_i+p)/p\), \(Q_p=Q/p\), and \(d_i>0\). Writing \(M=\sup_x e^{x^TQx}\prod_i f_i(x_i)^{d_i}\), the regularized bound gives \[e^{x^TQ_px}\prod_i f_i(x_i)^{c_i(p)}
 \le M^{1/p}\prod_i f_i(0)\,e^{-\lambda|x|^2/2}.\] Taking the \(p\)-th power of the integral removes the fixed Gaussian factor as \(p\downarrow0\). This avoids an unjustified \(L^p\)-to-\(L^\infty\) limit on an infinite-measure space.

The potentially noncompact case \(\Lambda=\infty\) in Lemma 3.2 also has a direct justification. Let \(A=\operatorname{diag}(A_i)\), \(A_i\ge\lambda I\), and \[C_p=\operatorname{diag}((d_i+p)I_{n_i})-2A^{-1/2}QA^{-1/2}.\] For the finite Gaussian integral, \(C_p>0\). Exact integration gives \[\mathrm{BL}(A)^p
 =(2\pi)^{-\sum_i d_i n_i/2}\,
 p^{Np/2}\prod_i(\det A_i)^{d_i/2}
 (\det C_p)^{-p/2}.\] For \(0<p\le1\), \(C_p\le CI\), with \(C\) independent of \(A,p\). Hence the final factor together with \(p^{Np/2}\) is bounded below by \((p/C)^{Np/2}\to1\), uniformly.

If the liminf under consideration is finite, near minimizers have bounded \(\prod_i(\det A_i)^{d_i/2}\). Because \(A_i\ge\lambda I\) and every \(d_i>0\), each \(A_i\) then has bounded eigenvalues. A subsequence converges, and (35) passes to \(\operatorname{diag}(d_iA_i)-2Q\ge0\). Continuity of the determinant yields the claimed limiting infimum. If no bounded near-minimizer values exist, the lower-bound statement is immediate. Thus the monotone feasible-set observation can be completed without assuming compactness when \(\Lambda=\infty\). The isolated inverse-matrix symbol in the final sentence on PDF p. 26 is inconsistent with (35); the consistent constraint is the one just displayed. It does not create a separate substantive finding.

## Even log-concave envelopes and damping

In the nontrivial case of Theorem 1.1, each diagonal block \(Q_{ii}\) can be taken positive semidefinite. If it has a negative direction, choose a Gaussian precision tending to zero in that direction and fixed sufficiently large precisions on the complementary directions and blocks. The matrix constraint still has slack, while the Gaussian integral product diverges. The claimed upper bound is then infinite.

For \(Q_{ii}\ge0\), taking the infimum of the ratio defining the \(i\)-th dual envelope amounts, after a negative logarithm, to \(x_i^TQ_{ii}x_i\) plus a supremum of affine functions of \(x_i\). It is therefore convex. Simultaneously reflecting the other coordinates verifies evenness. Each replacement enlarges that input and preserves the joint pointwise constraint; induction preserves both properties for the final tuple.

Assume the original integral product is nonzero, since otherwise the inequality is immediate. Choosing positive values of the other inputs and also their simultaneous reflections bounds each envelope above by a finite constant. It is measurable and log-concave; zero values outside its support are permitted. Multiplication by \(e^{-\varepsilon|x_i|^2}\) ensures integrability and preserves the constraint. Monotone convergence removes this damping before comparing with the original inputs. Thus integrability of an undamped envelope need not be assumed.

## Elementary scope tests

For \(m=1\) and \(Q>0\), the constraint is exactly \(f(x)\le e^{-x^TQx/c}\); its Gaussian bound is immediate. For \(Q=0\), the dual supremum is infinite, as the paper allows. The inverse functional equals one when every \(c_i=1\); if some \(c_i\ne1\), its Gaussian infimum is zero by changing that block’s scale.

Evenness cannot simply be discarded from the stated dual inequality. In dimension one, take \[f_1(x)=e^{-(x-a)^2/2},\qquad
 f_2(y)=e^{-y^2/2-ay},\quad a\ne0.\] The exact Legendre inequality gives \(f_1(x)f_2(y)\le e^{-xy}\), but their integral product is \(2\pi e^{a^2/2}>2\pi\). This is a check of an essential hypothesis, not a counterexample to the theorem as stated.

# Independent verification of the Gaussian constant

For the Kolesnikov–Werner datum, PDF p. 33 gives the constraint \(M(A)\ge0\), with diagonal blocks \(A_i>0\) and off-diagonal blocks \(-I/(m-1)\). The following direct calculation verifies the optimization target without assuming an extremizer exists.

Put \(B_i=A_i^{-1/2}\), and test the block quadratic form on \(x_i=B_i z\). Taking the trace of the resulting inequality yields \[\sum_{i\ne j}\operatorname{tr}(B_iB_j)\le mn(m-1).\] Although \(B_iB_j\) need not be symmetric, it is similar to a positive definite symmetric matrix. Applying \(s\ge1+\log s\) to its positive eigenvalues gives \[\operatorname{tr}(B_iB_j)\ge n+\log\det(B_iB_j).\] Summing and using \[\sum_{i\ne j}\log\det(B_iB_j)
   =-(m-1)\sum_i\log\det A_i\] therefore proves \(\sum_i\log\det A_i\ge0\). Equivalently \(\prod_i\det A_i^{-1}\le1\). The choice \(A_i=I\) is feasible, so the supremum is exactly one. This recovers the constant \((2\pi)^{mn/2}\) in Theorem 4.1.

If equality holds, every scalar eigenvalue inequality above is an equality, so \(B_iB_j=I\) for all \(i\ne j\). For \(m\ge3\), this forces \(A_i=I\) for every \(i\). For \(m=2\), it gives \(A_2=A_1^{-1}\), retaining the expected linear invariance. This classifies the equality cases of this Gaussian matrix problem only. It is not an assertion about all non-Gaussian functional extremizers.

The direct certificate above independently verifies their target constant and bypasses reliance on the external equality argument for that target. It does not certify every assertion made in the cited transport literature.

# Applications and limits of the audit

The Minkowski-functional substitution in Corollary 4.2 has the correct factor \(1/2\) and reproduces the claimed volume product. Theorem 4.3 (PDF p. 29) deliberately uses two different conditions: the condition on \(\sum_i V_i\) for \(0\le\lambda\le1/2\), and the condition on \(\sum_i V_i^*\) for \(1/2\le\lambda\le1\). This distinction is necessary. Taking \(V_i=a|x|^2/2\), \(a>1\), satisfies the first condition but gives product affine surface area \((2\pi)^{mn/2}a^{mn(\lambda-1/2)}\), which exceeds the claimed bound if one incorrectly extends that condition to \(\lambda>1/2\). The printed theorem avoids that extension. The range \(p\in[0,n]\) in Corollary 4.4 agrees with \(\lambda=p/(n+p)\in[0,1/2]\).

Theorem 4.5 concerns symmetric measures in \(\mathcal P_2\) and equal weights \(1/m\). If any relative entropy is infinite, the bound is immediate. Finite entropy gives absolute continuity, and finite second moments are already part of \(\mathcal P_2\). The multimarginal cost identity verifies the coefficient \((m-1)/m^2\). For finite entropy, symmetrized Kantorovich potentials with \[\sum_i\phi_i(x_i)\le
 \frac{1}{2(m-1)}\sum_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|^2\] give even inputs \(f_i=e^{\phi_i-|x_i|^2/2}\) for Theorem 4.1. Its conclusion and entropy duality yield the barycentric inequality. One can first truncate to symmetric balls, use compact dual potentials extended by \(-\infty\), and then pass to the limit using entropy convergence and \(W_2\) convergence. This handles potential integrability without assuming an unproved global bound. At \(m=2\), the coefficient reduces exactly to the symmetric two-measure Talagrand inequality.
