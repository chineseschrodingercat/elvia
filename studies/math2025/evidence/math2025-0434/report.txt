Abstract: The general profile formula in Corollary 2.32 is false as printed. In its upper one-sided branch, the volume integral in Lemma 2.31 omits the completely filled slices. An explicit parameter pair in the paper’s torus-base model then assigns an area below \(1/50\) to a volume above \(149/1500\), whereas an independent Gaussian contraction argument proves that the true profile there is larger than the volume. Adding the missing full-slice contribution repairs this particular defect. The positive-mean-curvature branch used in the three-dimensional cube argument and the two-sided Gaussian branch do not encounter it. Exact independent checks support the high-dimensional comparison and the stated \(T>\pi\) instability threshold. No headline theorem is disproved by this report.

# The missing contribution

Equation reference: eq:printedV. Write \(S_T(I_0)\) for the paper’s two-dimensional model slab with probability measure \(T^{-1}dt\,\varphi_{I_0}(s)\,ds\). Here \(I_0\) is a continuous symmetric concave profile, positive on \((0,1)\), and \(\Phi_{I_0}\) is the distribution function of its associated log-concave density. A downward set has slice volume \(v(t)=\Phi_{I_0}(f(t))\). For endpoints \(0\leq v_0<v_1\leq1\), let \(\ell\) be the chord of \(I_0\) through those endpoints and set \[\begin{aligned}
 L=T(v_0,v_1)
 &=\int_{v_0}^{v_1}
 \frac{dv}{I_0(v)\sqrt{(I_0(v)/\ell(v))^2-1}},\\
 \widetilde V_T(v_0,v_1)
 &=\frac1T\int_{v_0}^{v_1}
 \frac{v\,dv}{I_0(v)\sqrt{(I_0(v)/\ell(v))^2-1}},\\
 A_T(v_0,v_1)
 &=\frac1T\int_{v_0}^{v_1}
 \frac{dv}{\sqrt{1-(\ell(v)/I_0(v))^2}}.\end{aligned}\] The tilde distinguishes the printed volume formula from the actual volume in the exceptional branch.

Problem 1 (P01: Major; a false general profile formula).  Theorem 2.27(3), p. 25, puts the active interval at \([T-L,T]\) when \(v_1=1\) and \(v_0>0\). Thus \(v(t)=1\) on \([0,T-L]\). Nevertheless Lemma 2.31, equation (2.19), p. 26, identifies [eq:printedV] (#eq:printedV) with the whole volume. The first change of variables in its proof, p. 27, drops the constant-volume part of the interval. Corollary 2.32, p. 28, then uses \(\widetilde V_T=\bar v\) as the volume constraint in its third column, together with \(L\leq T\) and \(1=v_1>v_0>0\). This produces a false value for the asserted isoperimetric profile, not only an incorrect description of an auxiliary quantity.

Equation reference: eq:model. Claim 1.  For the model explicitly defined in (2.7), p. 16, \[
 T=1,\qquad I_0(v)=\min\{\sqrt{\pi v},1,\sqrt{\pi(1-v)}\},\] Corollary 2.32 is false at \(w=1/10-\pi/6000\).

Equation reference: eq:values. Proof. Take \[r=\frac1{10},\qquad v_0=1-\frac{\pi r^2}{4},\qquad
 v_1=1,\qquad \ell(v)=\frac{2(1-v)}r.\] These parameters are in the upper square-root zone of [eq:model] (#eq:model), and \(\ell<I_0\) throughout \((v_0,1)\). With \(v=v_0+\pi x^2/4\), \(0<x<r\), one has \[I_0(v)\sqrt{(I_0(v)/\ell(v))^2-1}=\frac{\pi x}{2}
 =\frac{dv}{dx},\qquad
 \sqrt{1-(\ell(v)/I_0(v))^2}=\frac{x}{r}.\] Consequently all integrals are finite and \[
 L=r\leq1,\qquad
 \widetilde V_1=r-\frac{\pi r^3}{6}=w,\qquad
 A_1=\frac{\pi r^2}{2}=\frac{\pi}{200}.\] Thus this pair satisfies every displayed condition in the third column of Corollary 2.32, which would imply \(I(S_1(I_0))(w)\leq\pi/200\).

We independently bound the true profile from below. Let \(I_\gamma(v)=\varphi_\gamma(\Phi_\gamma^{-1}(v))\) be the standard one-dimensional Gaussian profile. If \(0<v\leq1/2\) and \(z=\Phi_\gamma^{-1}(v)\leq0\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \[I_\gamma(v)=-\int_{-\infty}^{z}x\varphi_\gamma(x)\,dx
 \leq
 \left(\int_{-\infty}^{z}x^2\varphi_\gamma(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}
 \sqrt v\leq\sqrt v.\] On this interval, \(I_0(v)=\min\{\sqrt{\pi v},1\}
\geq\sqrt{2v}\); symmetry therefore gives \(I_0\geq\sqrt2\,I_\gamma\) on the whole unit interval. The monotone map \(\Phi_{I_0}^{-1}\circ\Phi_\gamma\) from the Gaussian line to the vertical model measure has derivative \(I_\gamma/I_0\leq1/\sqrt2\). The map \(\Phi_\gamma\) to uniform measure on \([0,1]\) has derivative at most \(1/\sqrt{2\pi}\). Their product is a measure-preserving \(1/\sqrt2\)-Lipschitz map from the standard Gaussian plane onto \(S_1(I_0)\).

Gaussian isoperimetry and Lipschitz transference now yield \[I(S_1(I_0))(v)\geq\sqrt2\,I_\gamma(v).\] This is the same type of transference used in the paper on pp. 8 and 17; it follows also by pulling back metric neighborhoods and then taking the perimeter limit. Concavity of \(I_\gamma\), together with \(I_\gamma(0)=0\) and \(I_\gamma(1/2)=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\), gives \[I(S_1(I_0))(v)\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}v>v,
 \qquad 0<v\leq\frac12.\] But \(\pi<4\) implies \[w>\frac{149}{1500}>\frac1{50}>\frac{\pi}{200}.\] This contradicts the value allowed by the printed formula. ◻

The geometry of the omitted volume is explicit. In the model [eq:model] (#eq:model), put \(R=1/2+1/\pi\). Its upper density is \(\varphi_{I_0}(s)=\pi(R-s)/2\) for \(R-2/\pi\leq s<R\). The corresponding downward set has \[f(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 R,&0\leq t\leq1-r,\\
 R-\sqrt{r^2-(1-t)^2},&1-r\leq t\leq1.
 \end{cases}\] Since \(r<2/\pi\), the entire curved boundary stays in that zone. Its actual volume is \(1-\pi r^3/6=1-\pi/6000\); the printed value is smaller by exactly \(1-r=9/10\). The finite weighted perimeter is the value in [eq:values] (#eq:values). No assertion that this set is itself a minimizer is needed or made: the counterexample uses an admissible parameter entry in the asserted infimum and a lower bound for the actual profile. The integral identities and all exact inequalities were also checked by a root-authored symbolic calculation, with actual exit code zero and retained inputs and outputs.

# Repair, counterevidence and impact

Equation reference: eq:repair. For \(v_1=1>v_0>0\), the correction is \[
 V_T^{\mathrm{correct}}(v_0,1)
 =\widetilde V_T(v_0,1)+\frac{T-T(v_0,1)}T.\] The third-column constraint in Corollary 2.32 must use this volume. There is no analogous added term when \(v_0=0\), because the inactive slices are empty, and none when both endpoints are interior, because \(L=T\). The area formula is not affected by the missing full slices.

A possible alternative formulation is to restrict explicitly to nonnegative mean curvature for volumes at most one half and extend by symmetry. Such a restriction would exclude the negative-curvature example above, whose chord slope is \(-2/r=-20\). It is not present in the printed corollary. Merely appealing to complementary sets does not repair a formula which already admits the wrong candidate at \(w<1/2\).

The dependency is direct: Theorem 2.27’s classification leads to Lemma 2.31’s volume calculation, which supplies the feasibility constraints in the general profile characterization of Corollary 2.32. The severity is Major because this central general characterization has an actual false quantitative conclusion. The available repair is local; the classification does not assert that the paper’s headline applications fail.

There is substantial counterevidence against a broader impact. For the small-volume cube argument, Section 4.2, pp. 38–39, establishes \[\lambda\geq
 \frac{1-2\pi/9}{1/2-4\pi/81}>0.8.\] Theorem 2.27 then gives \(v_1<1\), excluding the affected branch. Proposition 4.2 separately assumes \(v_0=0\). In the Gaussian application, Corollary 2.30, p. 26, excludes all one-sided unduloids because \[\frac{v}{I_\gamma(v)^2}\sim\frac{1}{2v\log(1/v)}
 \quad\hbox{as }v\downarrow0\] is nonintegrable. Section 6 invokes this exclusion explicitly on pp. 46–47. The high-dimensional argument in Section 5 does not use the defective one-sided volume constraint. Theorems 1.2, 1.3, 1.6 and 1.8 are therefore not disproved by P01. A correction should check every use of the general profile formula and distinguish its branches.

# Independent positive checks

## High-dimensional finite comparisons and induction

For Lemma 5.2, pp. 44–46, set \[\omega_k=\frac{\pi^{k/2}}{\Gamma(1+k/2)},\quad
 v_s(n)=\frac{\omega_n}{2^n},\quad
 I_m^{(k)}(v)=\frac{k\omega_k^{1/k}}2v^{1-1/k}.\] The finite tests at \(n=10\) and \(n=11\) were independently certified by exact rational bounds, rather than read from Figure 5. For each \(1\leq k<n\), the adjacent ratio raised to the positive power \(k(k+1)\) is \[Q_{k,n}
 =2^n\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k(k+1)}
 \frac{\omega_k^{k+1}}{\omega_{k+1}^{k}\omega_n}.\] All 19 lower bounds were strictly greater than one. Integer and half-integer Gamma identities reduce each check to a positive rational number times an integer power of \(\pi\). The retained certificate uses rational bounds obtained from Machin’s formula and alternating-series remainders.

The remaining induction condition at \(n=11\) was also certified: \[D=\log2+\frac12\psi(13/2)-\frac12\log\pi-1>0.\] The stored rational lower bound exceeds \(0.01454\). It uses the half-integer digamma identity, \(\gamma_{\mathrm E}\leq H_{100}-\log100\), and explicit positive-series bounds for logarithms. Log-convexity of \(\Gamma\) then preserves the needed derivative inequality for \(n\geq11\), as in the paper. This checks the numerical and induction component of the high-dimensional argument; the cited geometric exclusion of a tangentially self-touching ball remains an external dependency.

## The Gaussian instability threshold

Lemma 3.1, p. 31, and the last step of Theorem 1.8, p. 48, can be tested by an explicit admissible variation. At any finite height \(a\), put \[h_\varepsilon(t)=a+\varepsilon\cos(\pi t/T)+b(\varepsilon),\] where the implicit function theorem chooses \(b\) so that \(T^{-1}\int_0^T\Phi_\gamma(h_\varepsilon(t))\,dt=\Phi_\gamma(a)\). Then \(b(0)=b'(0)=0\), \(b''(0)=a/2\). The variation satisfies the Neumann endpoint condition. For its weighted area, \[A''(0)=\frac{\varphi_\gamma(a)}2
 \left(\frac{\pi^2}{T^2}-1\right).\] The cosine integrals and cancellations were independently verified symbolically. Thus every nontrivial horizontal half-plane is unstable for \(T>\pi\), as claimed. At \(T=\pi\) this second variation is zero and proves no instability. Nor does this calculation settle the intermediate range \(\sqrt{2\pi}<T\leq\pi\), which the paper leaves with transition parameters rather than an asserted explicit solution.
