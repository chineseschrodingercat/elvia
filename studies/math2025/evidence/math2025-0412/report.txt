Abstract: This review concerns the acquired 94-page arXiv version of the paper by Vadim Gorin and Karl Liechty, arXiv:2310.12735v1, submitted 19 October 2023 (the PDF is dated 20 October 2023), associated with DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.22254. Three demonstrated defects affect core proof steps: the uniform upper bound in Proposition 6.12, the asserted independence of the antiferroelectric model solution from \(n\), and the boundedness of the ferroelectric local Gamma factor at the origin. A separate normalization problem is peripheral to the normalized limit laws. These findings do not demonstrate that the principal limiting theorems are false. Repairs are proved or delimited below. Scientific adoption remains pending; this initial review is not independent acceptance.

Title: Initial Scientific Review
Boundary statistics for the six-vertex model with DWBC

# Version, scope, and principal claims

All 94 pages of the exact acquired source were read, including Appendix A and the bibliography. Page numbers below are the printed PDF pages. The publisher’s 2025 article was identified but was not acquired and compared line by line. Every finding is restricted to the specified arXiv version.

The model has positive homogeneous weights \(a,b,c\), domain wall boundary conditions on an \(n\)-square, and \[\Delta=\frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}.\] The coordinates \(\lambda_i^k\) form a strictly increasing row of length \(k\), with weak interlacing between adjacent rows. Theorem 1.3 (p. 6) fixes the weights with \(\Delta<1\) and asserts finite-dimensional convergence, after centering by \(mn\) and scaling by \(s\sqrt n\), to GUE corners. This includes \(\Delta=-1\), but not \(\Delta=1\) or weights varying with \(n\). Theorem 1.4 (p. 7) assumes \(\Delta>1\), \(a>b\), and gives an unscaled stochastic six-vertex limit with \(0<b_1<b_2<1\). Reflection handles the opposite ordering; \(a=b>0,c>0\) cannot satisfy \(\Delta>1\). Theorem 1.8 (p. 8) first takes \(c\downarrow0\) at fixed \(n\), then \(n\to\infty\), for fixed \(a>b>0\). It does not divide zero partition functions at literal \(c=0\). Proposition 1.10 (p. 9) instead assumes \(n\log q_n\to\theta\); its density at \(\theta=0\) has the uniform continuous extension.

The proof uses the Izergin–Korepin determinant and orthogonal polynomials for normalized partition-function asymptotics. Propositions 4.4 and 4.6 are the analytic inputs; Sections 4–5 pass from one inhomogeneity to fixed \(k\), and Section 6 identifies laws using symmetric functions, tightness and conditional interlacing measures. Section 7 treats \(c=0\) by a separate permutation argument. Section 3 explicitly presents a conditional alternative; its named conjectures alone are not defects in the separate Appendix A route.

The review examined positivity and convergence domains, fixed-\(k\) restrictions, lattice exclusions, analytic continuation at zero, coalescing inhomogeneities, tightness versus uniform integrability, and the order of limits. It does not certify every theorem in all cited external works. No author code was executed.

# A false uniform tail bound

Problem 1 (Major: Proposition 6.12).  Equation (6.28), p. 58, is false under its stated quantifiers, including the diffusive scaling used in Proposition 6.14.

## Claim and admissible parameters

Equation reference: eq:printed-tail. With \(\widetilde F_\nu\) defined in (6.19), p. 56, put \[r_j=\frac{b(t+\varepsilon\xi_j,\gamma)a(t,\gamma)}
 {b(t,\gamma)a(t+\varepsilon\xi_j,\gamma)}.\] The claimed bound is \[
 \widetilde F_\nu(\varepsilon\xi_1,\ldots,\varepsilon\xi_k)
 \le C_1\prod_{j=1}^k r_j^M
 \exp\left((k+o(1))\sum_{i=1}^k
       |\sigma\varepsilon(\nu_i-M)\xi_i|\right).\] Here \(k,t,\gamma\) and a compact set of real \(\xi\)’s are fixed, \(C_1\) is independent of \(\nu,M\), and the error is uniform. The absolute values in the actual page image are retained.

Take \(k=2\), \(t=0,\gamma=\pi/4\) in the disordered parametrization: \(a=b=1/\sqrt2,c=1,\Delta=0,\sigma=2\). Let the compact set be \(\{(1,0)\}\), and let \(\nu=(M,M+L)\), where \(M,L\) are positive integers and \(n\ge M+L\). These are admissible strict rows, extendable to a monotone triangle with top row \(1,\ldots,n\).

## Exact finite counterexample

Equation reference: eq:finite-tail. The lower-row entry \(u\) ranges from \(M\) to \(M+L\). Formula (6.3), p. 50, or (6.15), p. 55, gives weights \(1/2\) at both endpoints and weight \(1\) at each interior point, of total \(L\). Put \(a_\varepsilon=\sin(\pi/4-\varepsilon)\), \(a_0=1/\sqrt2\), and \[r=\frac{\sin(\pi/4+\varepsilon)}{\sin(\pi/4-\varepsilon)}>1
 \quad(0<\varepsilon<\pi/4).\] Only the first row is perturbed. Direct substitution gives \[
 \frac{\widetilde F_{(M,M+L)}(\varepsilon,0)}{r^M}
 =\frac{a_0}{a_\varepsilon L}
 \left(\frac{r^{-1}}2+\sum_{v=1}^{L-1}r^{v-1}
                   +\frac{r^{L-1}}2\right)
 \ge\frac{a_0r^{L-1}}{2a_\varepsilon L}.\] The exponent in [eq:printed-tail] (#eq:printed-tail) is zero because \((\nu_1-M)\xi_1=0\) and \(\xi_2=0\), so it would bound [eq:finite-tail] (#eq:finite-tail) by \(C_1\).

In particular, set \(n=2j^2\), \(M=L=j^2\) and \(\varepsilon=n^{-1/2}\). Then \(M=mn\), since \(m=1/2\), and \(\nu_2=n\). Since \(\log r=2\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^3)\), the logarithm of the lower bound is \[\sqrt2\,j-2\log j+O(1)\longrightarrow+\infty .\] A uniform \(o(1)\) cannot alter the zero exponent. Independent direct summation gives the following illustrations; the analytic divergence proves the defect.

50 | \(1.60891\times10^2\) | \(2.29240\times10^1\)

200 | \(9.60625\times10^4\) | \(6.80399\times10^3\)

## Core dependency and a sufficient replacement

On p. 60, (6.28) and (6.36) justify passage to the limit in (6.35) to obtain (6.38). This is a core uniform-integrability step for Proposition 6.14 and Theorem 1.3. Neither an omitted absolute value nor compactness rescues the claim. An ordering restriction on \(\xi_i\)’s is not stated and would require a new argument.

Equation reference: eq:replacement. A weaker estimate suffices. For fixed positive weights, fixed \(k\), and compact real \(\xi\), there are \(C_0,C>0\) such that, for small \(\varepsilon>0\), \[
 \frac{\widetilde F_\nu(\varepsilon\xi)}{\prod_j r_j^M}
 \le C_0e^{C\varepsilon R},\qquad R=\max_i|\nu_i-M|.\] Indeed, every interlacing entry lies in \([M-R,M+R]\). For \(T_j=\sum_{i=1}^j u_i^j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}u_i^{j-1}\), \[|T_j-M-j|\le(2j-1)R+j.\] In the finite weighted average from (6.3), the factors \(r_j^{T_j-M-j}\) have logarithms at most \(C\varepsilon(R+1)\), since \(|\log r_j|\le C\varepsilon\) uniformly on the compact set. There are only \(O(k^2)\) corner indicators. Their perturbed-to-original weight ratios, and the powers of \(a_0/a_\varepsilon\), are uniformly bounded by positivity. Averaging proves [eq:replacement] (#eq:replacement).

At \(\varepsilon=n^{-1/2},M=mn\), (6.36), conditional on its preceding inputs, controls a strictly larger exponential of \(R/\sqrt n\). It therefore makes the contribution from \(R/\sqrt n>K\) uniformly small as \(K\to\infty\). This repairs this particular limit step, not the other analytic inputs below. The classification is Major because an invalid bound is explicitly used in a core derivation, even though a local replacement is available.

# The antiferroelectric model depends on n

Problem 2 (Major: Proposition 4.4 and Appendix A.1).  The antiferroelectric model functions cannot have their asserted independence from \(n\).

Proposition 4.4, p. 29, equations (4.30)–(4.33), uses \(M_{\mathrm{AF}}(z),N_{\mathrm{AF}}(z)\). Equation (A.45) and footnote 20, p. 79, explicitly assert independence from \(n\) for the model quantities; p. 80 identifies \(M_{\mathrm{AF}}(z)=\mathbf M(\gamma z)_{22}\). The saddle prefactor on pp. 40–41 is treated as a fixed analytic function in Lemma 4.7 (p. 31), which requires that property.

## A parity obstruction in the cited source

The paper’s reference [15], here [BL], gives \[\Omega_n=\pi+n\pi(1+\zeta),\qquad \zeta=t/\gamma ,\] in (7.5), p. 28. By (8.1), its model jump on the nonempty saturated interval is \(\operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\Omega_n},e^{i\Omega_n})\). Take \(\gamma=1,t=0\), allowed positive weights \(a=b=\sinh1,c=\sinh2,\Delta=-\cosh2<-1\). Then \(\Omega_n=(n+1)\pi\): the jump is \(+I\) for odd \(n\) and \(-I\) for even \(n\). One normalized model matrix cannot satisfy both.

Equation reference: eq:parity-ratio. The scalar entry also changes. Equations (8.29)–(8.30), p. 31, and (13.4)–(13.5), p. 47, of [BL] imply \[M_{22,n}(z)=A(z)
 \frac{\vartheta_3(v+\Omega_n/2)\vartheta_3(0)}
      {\vartheta_3(v)\vartheta_3(\Omega_n/2)},\qquad
 v=\widetilde u_\infty-\widetilde u(z),\] with their Abel coordinate \(\widetilde u\) and nonzero algebraic prefactor \(A(z)\) near infinity. The odd subsequence gives \(A(z)\); the even-to-odd ratio is \[
 R(v)=\frac{\vartheta_4(v)\vartheta_3(0)}
            {\vartheta_3(v)\vartheta_4(0)}.\] For \(q=e^{-\pi^2/2}\) and real \(z>\beta\), \(v\) ranges between \(0\) and \(\pi/4\). At \(v=\pi/8\), \(R(v)>1\), because every factor in \[\frac{\vartheta_4(v)}{\vartheta_3(v)}
 =\prod_{m\ge1}
 \frac{1-2q^{2m-1}\cos(2v)+q^{4m-2}}
      {1+2q^{2m-1}\cos(2v)+q^{4m-2}}\] is strictly larger than at \(v=0\). The two analytic functions therefore differ, also at some fixed nonreal points. Those points satisfy the lattice-separation condition for large \(n\). An \(O(1/n)\) remainder cannot absorb the persistent parity difference.

Independent theta-series and product evaluations both gave \(R(\pi/8)=1.00846270596025\). An Abel-integral calculation at \(z=10+i\) gave a parity difference of magnitude approximately \(0.0014108651\). These ordinary floating-point calculations include quadrature refinement but no interval certification; the product proof is decisive.

## Impact, alternative reading, and repair boundary

This input feeds the saddle analysis, extension through \(\xi=0\), Theorem 2.6, and the antiferroelectric part of Theorem 1.3. Implicitly allowing \(M_n\) would contradict the footnote and require the uniformity arguments to be supplied. Fixed weights and the \(z\mapsto\gamma z\) rescaling do not remove the phase (\(\gamma=1\) in our example).

There is counterevidence against a false GUE limit: the relevant saddle tends to infinity as its argument tends to zero, where the model is normalized. A plausible repair keeps \(M_n,N_n\), proves uniform bounds on the required domains, and uses a prefactor \(\psi_n\) satisfying uniformly \[\psi_n(0)=1,\qquad\psi_n(\xi/\sqrt n)=1+O(n^{-1/2})\] for bounded \(\xi\). The Cauchy argument of Lemma 4.7 extends to analytic nonvanishing \(\psi_n\) under appropriate uniform bounds. For fixed nome, theta denominators with real periodic phase are bounded away from zero, supporting this route. That fact alone does not prove all contour and error estimates. The full uniform Riemann–Hilbert repair remains to be checked. The demonstrated invalid core premise is Major; the limit theorem has not been disproved.

# The ferroelectric local factor is unbounded at zero

Problem 3 (Major: Appendix A.2).  The function \(D(z)\) in (A.88), p. 87, is unbounded at the origin, contrary to its use in (A.95); the global estimate (A.98) fails for the error matrix as defined.

## Local calculation and matrix consequence

Equation reference: eq:D, eq:Dpole. Put \(w=nz/2\). For positive real \(z\), the printed definition is \[
 D_n(z)=\frac{\Gamma(w+3/2)e^w}
              {\sqrt{2\pi}\,w^{w+1}}.\] For every fixed \(n\ge1\), \(w^w\to1\) and \(\Gamma(w+3/2)\to\sqrt\pi/2\), so \[
 D_n(z)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,nz}\qquad(z\downarrow0).\] This does not refute (A.90), restricted to \(|z|\ge\varepsilon\). It refutes the boundedness used inside the origin disk in (A.95), p. 89.

Take \(t=2,\gamma=1\), so \(t>|\gamma|>0\). The shifted nodes in (A.50), p. 81, are \(-1/n,-3/n,\ldots\), with positive weights. The monic orthogonal polynomial \(q_{n,n}\) has only negative zeros, hence \(q_{n,n}(0)>0\). Equations (A.64) and (A.73), pp. 82–83, show that the first column of \(S_n\) has a finite nonzero limit on the positive axis at zero: its diagonal exponential factors are nonzero and \(g(0)\) is finite. The model \(\mathbf M\) in (A.84) is analytic and invertible there; its cut ends at \(\alpha<0\).

Inside the origin disk, (A.91) defines \(X_n=S_nD_n^{\sigma_3}\mathbf M^{-1}\). Writing the columns of \(S_n\) as \(s_1,s_2\), this equals \[D_n s_1(e_1^T\mathbf M^{-1})
 +D_n^{-1}s_2(e_2^T\mathbf M^{-1}).\] The first term has a nonzero \(z^{-1}\) leading coefficient by [eq:Dpole] (#eq:Dpole); the second tends to zero. Thus \(X_n\) is unbounded, contradicting the stated uniform \(X_n=I+O(1/n)\) on all of \(\mathbb C\) in (A.98), p. 89. The small-norm Cauchy representation invoked immediately before (A.98), via (A.34) on p. 76, likewise requires repaired origin behavior or an additional singular contribution.

## Verified scalar repair and unverified matrix repair

Equation reference: eq:D0. The Gamma recurrence suggests \[
 D_n^{(0)}(z)=\frac{nz}{nz+1}D_n(z).\] Directly, \[D_n^{(0)}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(w+1/2)e^w}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,w^w},
       &\Re z>0,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}e^w}
 {\Gamma(1/2-w)(-w)^w},&\Re z<0 .
 \end{cases}\] The one-sided origin limits are \(1/\sqrt2\) and \(\sqrt2\). The apparent pole at \(nz=-1\) cancels the zero in the original left-half-plane formula. The multiplier has equal boundary values on the imaginary axis, preserving the jump, and is \(1+O(1/n)\) for \(|z|\ge\varepsilon\). These scalar facts are proved; independent Gamma evaluations check the recurrence and \(nzD_n(z)\to1/\sqrt2\).

A complete repair must recheck the local matrix, analyticity, matching, all remaining jumps, and small-norm estimates. It is not established by these scalar facts alone. Proposition 4.6 is stated away from the origin, so its conclusion is not directly contradicted. Its proof nevertheless uses the invalid global estimate and feeds Theorem 2.4 and Theorem 1.4. This is a Major proof defect, not a demonstrated false stochastic limit.

# Normalization of the ferroelectric base measure

Problem 4 (Minor: Lemma 2.9 and Remark 2.10).  The measure in (2.15), p. 14, has the wrong Laplace normalization for (2.14), and its asserted normalization to a probability measure is unavailable.

Equation reference: eq:measure. For \(t>\gamma>0\), the printed measure is \(m=\sum_{k\ge1}2\sinh(2\gamma k)\delta_{-2k}\). A convergent geometric-series calculation gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \int e^{tx}m(dx)
 &=\frac{1}{e^{2(t-\gamma)}-1}
       -\frac{1}{e^{2(t+\gamma)}-1}\\
 &=\frac{\sinh(2\gamma)}
 {2\sinh(t-\gamma)\sinh(t+\gamma)}
 =\tfrac12\phi(t;\gamma).\end{aligned}\] Equation (2.14) fails by a factor two. For \(n=1\), (2.34), p. 18, then gives \(Z_1=c/2\), whereas the unique DWBC vertex has weight \(c\). The admissible choice \(t=2,\gamma=1\) verifies both calculations.

Moreover \(m(\mathbb R)=\infty\), since the positive atomic masses do not even tend to zero. The formal \(\phi(0;\gamma)=-2\coth\gamma<0\) is outside the Laplace convergence domain; it is not a finite positive mass. The normalization proposed in Remark 2.10 is therefore invalid in this phase.

Replacing \(2\sinh(2\gamma k)\) by \(4\sinh(2\gamma k)\) restores (2.14). A probability measure can be obtained by exponential tilting at \(t_0>\gamma\) and dividing by its finite Laplace integral. Alternatively retain the measure and correct its transform and absolute partition-function formulas.

Constant rescaling cancels in the normalized log-gas law, monic orthogonal polynomials, and Cauchy-transform-to-norm ratios. Footnote 11, p. 28, notes a related constant cancellation. This is counterevidence against an effect on the main normalized laws. No core dependency on this erroneous absolute normalization was demonstrated, so the finding is Minor. The infinite-mass normalization assertion is substantive; this is not a typographical finding alone.

# Additional independent checks and review limits

Put \(S=a^2+b^2-c^2>2ab\) and \(d=\sqrt{S^2-4a^2b^2}\). The stochastic parameters in Theorem 1.4 are \(b_1=(S-d)/(2a^2)\), \(b_2=(S+d)/(2a^2)\). For \(a>b>0,c>0,\Delta>1\), they are positive and distinct. Since \(2a^2-S=a^2-b^2+c^2>0\) and \[(2a^2-S)^2-d^2=4a^2c^2>0,\] we obtain \(b_2<1\), and directly \(b_1b_2=b^2/a^2\). Thus their probability range does not rest on numerical examples.

At \(t=0\), (6.6) and (6.8), p. 51, give \[(m,s^2)=(1/2,1/4)\ (\gamma=\pi/4),\qquad
 (m,s^2)=(1/2,3/8)\ (\gamma=\pi/3),\] and \(s^2=(\pi^2/6-1)/4>0\) in the boundary phase at \(\gamma=1\). These checks do not prove positivity of the full antiferroelectric theta expression throughout its parameter range.

For the permutation limit, choosing the \(r\)-th smallest remaining symbol with probability proportional to \(q^{r-1}\) creates \(r-1\) new inversions. Multiplication gives the Mallows law \[\frac{q^{\operatorname{inv}(\pi)}}{\prod_{m=1}^n(1+\cdots+q^{m-1})}.\] Exact rational enumeration for \(q=1/2\), \(1\le n\le7\), checked all 5,913 permutations and mass one. This uses the ascending-word convention of Definition 1.7. The conflicting word largest in Definition 7.1 is read as an orientation typo, not a separate finding. At fixed \(k\), deleting finitely many symbols changes a rank by at most \(k\); convergence of the truncated geometric laws therefore also explains the macroscopic density in Proposition 1.10.

Other textual discrepancies were not promoted into Problems. The boundary derivative of \(\log(b/a)\) is \(1/(\gamma+t)+1/(\gamma-t)\), consistent with p. 51; the minus sign in (6.21) gives no viable scaling at \(t=0\). The intended derivative was used when tracing the argument. Lattice exclusions and the separate \(\xi=0\) argument were respected. Full reading does not mean that every inherited analytic estimate has been independently reproved: the uniform repairs of Problems 2–3 still need complete verification.

# Disposition and version-sensitive follow-up

The acquired version contains three demonstrated invalid core proof steps and a peripheral normalization defect. No counterexample to Theorems 1.3, 1.4 or 1.8 was established. Problem 1 has a proved replacement bound. Problem 2 requires a uniformity argument retaining the \(n\)-phase. Problem 3 has a verified scalar candidate but needs a complete matrix repair. Problem 4’s correction preserves normalized laws.

The arXiv history, publisher landing page and both authors’ publication pages were checked for newer public versions and relevant corrections. No paper-specific erratum was found in that bounded search. The public four-page errata sheet for the cited 2014 book was read and viewed. It concerns that book, not the reviewed paper, and does not remove the explicit phase in [BL]. The journal text was not scientifically compared, so these findings must not be projected onto an unseen revision.

Before adoption, compare the actual journal text or an author correction at the exact locators above, verify complete repairs for Problems 2–3, and recheck the analytic-to-probabilistic dependency chain. The scientific decision remains pending; no human reviews are recorded.
