Abstract: The acquired 47-page publisher-formatted paper by Antonelli, Pozzetta and Semola is reviewed at the level of its hypotheses, proof dependencies and selected central calculations. A translated-unit-disk example gives the opposite sign in the printed inward-normal mean-curvature formula. The same formula is used in the graph-estimate proof underlying uniqueness, so this is a Major defect in that derivation. Two Minor local defects concern a metric estimate on an unrestricted Fermi tube and an assertion of Sobolev compactness for every finite exponent. Explicit local repairs are proved. Positive checks cover the exceptional-volume selection and the warped-surface example. None of these examples refutes the main uniqueness or strict-stability theorem. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Paper identity, claims and scope

The reviewed paper is Gioacchino Antonelli, Marco Pozzetta and Daniele Semola, Uniqueness on average of large isoperimetric sets in noncompact manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature, Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics (2025), DOI 10.1002/cpa.22252 (https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.22252) [APS]. The exact institutional copy is publisher-formatted, says Accepted: 21 February 2025, and prints pages 1–47. The acquisition metadata identifies volume 78, issue 9, pages 1656–1702. This report uses the acquired early-online pagination: PDF page and printed page coincide. A separately repaginated issue version has not been checked.

For a smooth complete manifold \(M^n\), the principal hypotheses are \[\begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Ric}\geq0,\qquad
 \operatorname{AVR}(M)=
 \lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_r(p))}{\omega_n r^n}>0,\\
 |\operatorname{Riem}(x)|\leq C\,d(p,x)^{-2},
 \qquad M\text{ not isometric to }\mathbb R^n .
 \end{gathered}\] Theorem 1.1 asserts uniqueness and strict volume-preserving stability of isoperimetric sets for volumes in a measurable set \(\mathcal G\) satisfying \[\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal G\cap(r,2r)|}{r}=1 .\] Theorem 1.2 constructs a surface showing why all sufficiently large volumes cannot replace this density-one statement.

All source text, including both appendices and the bibliography, was read. The detailed checks below concern the normal-graph formula and estimates in Section 4.2, their use in Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.1, the compactness step of Theorem 3.1, and selected estimates in Sections 2, 3 and 4.4. The source’s external existence, rigidity, regularity and metric-measure convergence theorems were not independently reproved. # Problem 1: the inward-normal graph formula has the opposite sign

Problem 1 (Major: Lemma 4.5 and the derivation of Lemma 4.8).  In Section 4.2, Item 2 explicitly uses the unit inner normal to parametrize the graph. Item 1 gives the two isoperimetric boundaries the same positive mean curvature. With \(g_z\) the metric in these Fermi coordinates and \(\dot g_z=\partial_z g_z\), formula (4.37), on source page 26, has the opposite sign from the mean curvature specified by those conventions. The incompatible sign is then used in (4.47), on page 28.

## Conventions and a fully admissible example

Equation reference: eq:printedH. The convention is fixed independently on source page 20: \(H_{\partial E}=\Delta d^s\), where \(d^s\) is positive outside \(E\). Thus a Euclidean unit circle has \(H=1\), while the Fermi coordinate \(z\) increases inward. Write \[q=|\nabla^w w|_{g_w}^{\,2},\qquad Q=\sqrt{1+q},\qquad
 Y=\frac{\nabla^w w}{Q}.\] The printed formula is, in this notation, \[
 H_{\Sigma'}=-\operatorname{div}_{g_w}Y+
 \frac Q2\operatorname{tr}_{g_w}\dot g_w-
 \frac{\dot g_w(\nabla^w w,\nabla^w w)}{2Q}.\]

Equation reference: eq:diskw. Take \(M=\mathbb R^2\), \(E=B_1(0)\), and \(E'=B_1(d e_1)\), with \(0<d<1\). Both sets are smooth isoperimetric sets of area \(\pi\); their mean curvatures are equal to 1. The auxiliary assumptions of Section 4.2 do not exclude the Euclidean ambient manifold. In particular, this is an example against the auxiliary formula, not against the non-Euclidean main theorem. For \(\Sigma=\partial E\), set \[F(z,\theta)=(1-z)(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\qquad -1<z<1 .\] This is a diffeomorphism onto the punctured disk \(0<|x|<2\); \(a=1\) is a valid tube parameter and \(g_z=(1-z)^2d\theta^2\). The curvature bounds in Item 4 hold with fixed \(K=2\). The translated circle is the normal graph of \[
 \rho_d(\theta)=d\cos\theta+\sqrt{1-d^2\sin^2\theta},
 \qquad w_d(\theta)=1-\rho_d(\theta).\] It has \(\|w_d\|_\infty=d<1\), and \(w_d\to0\) in \(C^\infty\) as \(d\downarrow0\). Thus any stipulated sufficiently small positive height and gradient bounds in Item 5 can also be met. The boundaries are distinct for every \(d>0\); the example does not rely on allowing the same set twice.

At \(\theta=0\), direct differentiation gives \[w_d=-d,\qquad w_d'=0,\qquad w_d''=d+d^2 .\] Consequently \(Q=1\), the last term of [eq:printedH] (#eq:printedH) vanishes, and \[\operatorname{div}_{g_w}Y=\frac{d+d^2}{(1+d)^2}=\frac d{1+d},
 \qquad
 \frac12\operatorname{tr}_{g_w}\dot g_w=-\frac1{1+d}.\] The printed right-hand side is therefore \(-1\), whereas the actual positive mean curvature of \(\partial B_1(d e_1)\) is \(+1\). This exact discrepancy persists for arbitrarily small \(d\). The accompanying independent symbolic calculation reproduces these identities; the analytic argument supplies the quantifiers and hypothesis verification.

## The corrected formula and its derivation

Equation reference: eq:correctH. Proposition 1.  For the inward Fermi coordinate and positive mean-curvature convention above, the graph formula is \[
 H_{\Sigma'}=\operatorname{div}_{g_w}Y-
 \frac Q2\operatorname{tr}_{g_w}\dot g_w+
 \frac{\dot g_w(\nabla^w w,\nabla^w w)}{2Q}.\]

Proof. The graph metric is \(g_w+dw\otimes dw\), so its area is \(A(w)=\int_\Sigma Q\,d\mu_{g_w}\). Differentiating \(A(w+tv)\) at \(t=0\), including the dependence of \(g_w^{-1}\) on \(w\), gives \[A'(w)[v]=\int_\Sigma
 \left\{\frac{g_w(\nabla^w w,\nabla^w v)}Q
 -\frac{\dot g_w(\nabla^w w,\nabla^w w)}{2Q}\,v
 +\frac Q2\operatorname{tr}_{g_w}\dot g_w\,v\right\}d\mu_{g_w}.\] The inward graph unit normal is \(N_w=(\partial_z-\nabla^w w)/Q\). The velocity \(v\partial_z\) has normal speed \(v/Q\) and \(d\mu_{\Sigma'}=Q\,d\mu_{g_w}\). The first variation with this positive \(H\) convention is therefore \(A'(w)[v]=-\int_\Sigma H_{\Sigma'}v\,d\mu_{g_w}\). Integration by parts yields [eq:correctH] (#eq:correctH). ◻

The source cites Pacard–Sun, Proposition 4.1. In the actually consulted author preprint [PS], pages 7–9 define \(h=-g(\nabla N,\cdot)\), \(H=\operatorname{tr}h\), and print precisely the signs in [eq:correctH] (#eq:correctH). This comparison is restricted to that formula and its local derivation. The independent proof above does not depend on every normalization in the cited preprint.

An outward-coordinate convention would make [eq:printedH] (#eq:printedH) appropriate, but that would change the explicitly stated inner normal. Changing \(H\) to a negative value would conflict with Item 1 and with \(H=\Delta d^s\). Direct inspection of source pages 20, 25–26 and 28 rules out an extraction ambiguity. In particular, \(\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}_{g_0}\dot g_0=-H_\Sigma\), whereas (4.47) substitutes \(+H_\Sigma\).

## A complete local repair of the differential estimate

Equation reference: eq:divrepair. The intended estimate (4.44) nevertheless follows with the correct signs on a uniformly controlled small tube. Put \[\beta(w)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}_{g_w}\dot g_w,\qquad
 \gamma(w)=\frac{\dot g_w(\nabla^w w,\nabla^w w)}{2Q}.\] Suppose \(H_{\Sigma'}=H_\Sigma\), \(|H_\Sigma|\leq\eta\), \(q\leq1\), \[|\beta(w)-\beta(0)|\leq C|w|,\qquad
 |\gamma(w)|\leq Cq .\] The small-tube bounds proved in Section 3 (#sec:tube) provide these estimates under the original curvature hypotheses, after restricting the graph height. Since \(\beta(0)=-H_\Sigma\), formula [eq:correctH] (#eq:correctH) gives exactly \[\operatorname{div}_{g_w}Y
 =Q\bigl(\beta(w)-\beta(0)\bigr)+(1-Q)H_\Sigma-\gamma(w).\] Using \(Q\leq\sqrt2\) and \(0\leq Q-1\leq q/2\) proves \[
 |\operatorname{div}_{g_w}Y|\leq C'(|w|+q).\] No sign assumption on \(\beta(w)\) is needed.

For completeness, this repair supplies the energy input to the graph estimates. Multiply [eq:divrepair] (#eq:divrepair) by \(w_+^p\), \(p\geq1\), and integrate by parts in \(g_w\). Uniform metric equivalence and \(Q\leq\sqrt2\) imply \[p\int_\Sigma w_+^{p-1}q\,d\mu_{g_w}
 \leq C\int_\Sigma w_+^{p+1}\,d\mu_{g_w}
     +C\|w\|_\infty\int_\Sigma w_+^{p-1}q\,d\mu_{g_w}.\] Choosing \(\|w\|_\infty\) small enough absorbs the last term for all \(p\geq1\). The same argument applies to \(w_-\). For \(s=p+1\geq2\) it gives \[\|\nabla(w_\pm^{s/2})\|_2^2
 \leq C\,\frac{s^2}{s-1}\|w_\pm\|_s^s .\] With the uniform Sobolev inequality used in the source, choose a fixed finite gain \(\chi>1\). Then \[\|w_\pm\|_{s\chi}
 \leq \left(C\frac{s^2}{s-1}\right)^{1/s}\|w_\pm\|_s .\] For \(s_k=2\chi^k\), the product converges because \(\sum_k(1+\log s_k)/s_k<\infty\). This yields the desired \(L^\infty\)–\(L^2\) estimate; \(p=1\) gives the gradient estimate. For \(n\geq3\), the source’s choice \(\chi=n/(n-2)\) is available on the \((n-1)\)-dimensional hypersurface. When \(n=2\), its written exponent is undefined, but a finite choice such as \(\chi=2\) on the one-dimensional hypersurface gives the same iteration. This is a dimension clarification, not a counterexample to the graph estimates.

## Central dependency and limits

The affected chain is \[\begin{gathered}
 (4.37),\ (4.47)\ \longrightarrow\
 \text{Lemma 4.8}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{Lemma 4.7}\\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{Proposition 4.3}\ 
 \longrightarrow\ \text{Theorem 4.1}.
 \end{gathered}\] On source page 31, Lemma 4.7 supplies (4.65)–(4.66); on page 24, the \(L^\infty\)–\(L^2\) estimate controls the tubular Ricci remainder in (4.27). Thus the discrepancy changes an identity actually used in the central uniqueness derivation, supporting the Major assessment. # Problem 2: the metric estimate needs a smaller tube

Equation reference: eq:claimedmetric. Problem 2 (Minor: the unrestricted quantifier in Lemma 4.6).  On source page 27, Lemma 4.6 assumes only Items 2, 3 and 4, omitting Item 5’s small-height condition. It claims a constant \(C_1=C_1(K,a)\) such that, in particular, \[
 |\nabla^w w|_{g_w}^2
 \leq |\nabla w|_{g_0}^2(1+C_1|w|).\] That assertion fails over the full allowed tube even for the translated unit disks above.

At \(\theta=\pi/2\), formula [eq:diskw] (#eq:diskw) gives \[\rho_d=\sqrt{1-d^2},\qquad
 w_d=1-\sqrt{1-d^2},\qquad w_d'=d\ne0 .\] Therefore \[\frac{|\nabla^w w_d|_{g_{w_d}}^2}
      {|\nabla w_d|_{g_0}^2}
 =\frac1{1-d^2}.\] Choose \(d=d_N=\sqrt{1-N^{-2}}\) for integers \(N\geq2\). The original tube \(a=1\), \(K=2\), equal enclosed volume and both isoperimetric boundaries remain fixed in the relevant sense. Every graph satisfies \(\|w_{d_N}\|_\infty=d_N<a\). Inequality [eq:claimedmetric] (#eq:claimedmetric) would require \[N^2\leq1+C_1(1-N^{-1})\leq1+C_1 ,\] which is impossible as \(N\to\infty\). Equivalently, the source proof’s claimed bound for all parallel second fundamental forms fails here: \(|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_z}|=(1-z)^{-1}\) tends to infinity as \(z\uparrow1\). The tube is nonsingular at every interior point; the problem is a uniform estimate up to its excluded endpoint.

These examples do not satisfy Item 5 with one fixed sufficiently small height. That is why they disprove Lemma 4.6 as written, but do not disprove its later use after the height is made sufficiently small.

Proposition 2 (Sufficient local replacement).  Assume Items 2–4 and the uniform bounds \(\|\mathrm{II}_\Sigma\|_\infty\leq K\), \(\|\operatorname{Riem}\|_\infty\leq K\) on the given tube. Set \[a_*=\min\{a/2,\,[8(K+1)]^{-1}\}.\] For \(|z|\leq a_*\), the metrics \(g_z\) are uniformly equivalent to \(g_0\), and \[\|g_z-g_0\|_{g_0}+\|\dot g_z-\dot g_0\|_{g_0}\leq C(K)|z|.\] In particular, for \(\|w\|_\infty\leq a_*\), \[\left||\nabla^w w|_{g_w}^2-|\nabla w|_{g_0}^2\right|
 \leq C(K)|w|\,|\nabla w|_{g_0}^2 .\] The volume-density ratio differs from 1 by at most \(C(n,K)|w|\).

Proof. Parallel-transport the differential of the normal exponential map to \(T_xM\). For \(v\in T_x\Sigma\), its Jacobi field \(J\) satisfies \(J''=-R_zJ\), \(J(0)=v\), \(|J'(0)|\leq K|v|\). The first-order system and Gronwall’s inequality give \[|J(z)|+|J'(z)|
 \leq (K+1)e^{(K+1)|z|}|v|
 \leq 2(K+1)|v|
 \quad (|z|\leq a_*).\] Hence \(|J(z)-v|\leq2(K+1)|z||v|\leq|v|/4\). It follows that \(\tfrac9{16}g_0\leq g_z\leq\tfrac{25}{16}g_0\). Since \(g_z(v,v)=|J(z)|^2\), the same estimates and the Jacobi equation bound both \(\dot g_z\) and \(\ddot g_z\). Polarization and integration give the displayed Lipschitz bounds. Uniform invertibility gives the inverse-metric estimate; applying the smooth determinant map on this uniformly positive set of matrices gives the volume-density bound. ◻

Shrinking the height once more makes all comparison factors positive with any required fixed margin. This supplies the estimates actually needed in Section 4.2 and in Section 2 of this report. The supported defect is the lemma’s unrestricted domain. The main application already permits arbitrarily small graph height, so central damage from this domain overstatement has not been demonstrated. The assessment is Minor.

# Problem 3: an unnecessary assertion of unrestricted \(L^p\) compactness

Problem 3 (Minor: Theorem 3.1, Step 3, source page 13).  After obtaining uniform \(H^{1,2}\) bounds on \(\bar f_i\), the text asserts a uniform \((p,2)\)-Sobolev inequality and strong \(L^p\) subsequence convergence for every \(p>1\). In hypersurface dimension \(m=n-1\geq3\), that general compactness assertion is false. The argument needs substantially less.

Here is a complete example against the stated compactness implication. Fix a compact smooth \(m\)-manifold, \(m\geq3\), a coordinate chart and a nonzero smooth bump \(\psi\) supported in its interior. For small \(\delta>0\), let \[b_\delta(x)=\delta^{-(m-2)/2}\psi(x/\delta)\] inside the chart and zero outside. Change of variables and smooth uniform metric bounds show \[\|b_\delta\|_2\asymp\delta,\qquad
 \|\nabla b_\delta\|_2\asymp1,\qquad
 \|b_\delta\|_p\asymp
 \delta^{\,m/p-(m-2)/2}.\] For \(m=3,p=8\), the last exponent is \(-1/8\). Subtract the mean of \(b_\delta\), add a fixed smooth mean-zero function \(u\) with \(\|u\|_2=1\), and divide by the resulting \(L^2\) norm. The denominator tends to 1. This produces smooth mean-zero, \(L^2\)-normalized functions with bounded \(H^1\) norm and unbounded \(L^8\) norm. The construction applies on any such compact link, including a scaled three-sphere.

This example addresses the claimed Sobolev/compactness implication. It is not asserted to satisfy the contradictory Rayleigh inequality (3.12) for the paper’s actual sequence, and is not a counterexample to Theorem 3.1. For \(m\geq3\), finite exponents strictly below \(2m/(m-2)\) give the usual compact embedding; in particular an exponent slightly above 2 would already be enough.

There is an even simpler repair that uses only strong \(L^2\) convergence. Pull the metrics back to the limiting compact manifold using the \(C^{1,\alpha}\) convergence obtained in Step 2. Rellich compactness gives \(f_i\to f\) in \(L^2\); the uniformly convergent volume densities preserve the mean-zero condition and \(\|f\|_2=1\). In the source’s notation, \[h_i=\operatorname{Ric}(\nu_{\Sigma_i},\nu_{\Sigma_i})
       +|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_i}|^2+\tfrac32\varepsilon_M,
 \qquad
 h_i\longrightarrow c:=n-1+\tfrac32\varepsilon_M
 \quad\hbox{in }L^1 ,\] and \(\|h_i\|_\infty\leq M\) uniformly, with \(M\geq|c|\). On a common fixed measure space, \[\left|\int h_i(f_i^2-f^2)\right|
 \leq M\|f_i-f\|_2(\|f_i\|_2+\|f\|_2)\longrightarrow0 .\] For \(L>0\), the other difference satisfies \[\left|\int(h_i-c)f^2\right|
 \leq L\|h_i-c\|_1+
      2M\int_{\{f^2>L\}}f^2 .\] First let \(i\to\infty\), then \(L\to\infty\). Uniform convergence of the volume densities contributes only vanishing terms of the same type. Thus \[\int h_i f_i^2\,d\mu_i\longrightarrow
 \left(n-1+\tfrac32\varepsilon_M\right)\int f^2\,d\mu
 =n-1+\tfrac32\varepsilon_M .\] Lower semicontinuity of Dirichlet energy now gives precisely the bound in (3.17), which contradicts the spectral gap \(\lambda_1(N)\geq n-1+2\varepsilon_M\) established in Step 1. The term \(3\varepsilon_M/2\) is missing in the intervening printed (3.16), but is already present in (3.13) and (3.17); it is not counted as an additional substantive problem. The unnecessary all-\(p\) assertion can be removed without changing this contradiction, so the supported impact is local and Minor.

## The warped-surface example

The hypotheses of source Lemma 2.2 are nonempty. For example, \[a_j=2^{3j+10},\qquad b_j=2^j,\qquad j\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{b_j}{a_j}=\frac1{768}<\frac1{16}.\] These sequences have \(a_{j+1}>a_j+b_j\), \(a_j\geq b_j\), and \(b_j\to\infty\). Choosing \(J_j\) to be the middle half of \([a_j,a_j+b_j]\) permits the smooth concave warping construction described in the paper. Its stated inequalities imply on \(J_j\) \[\varphi\varphi''-(\varphi')^2+1
 \leq -\frac78-\left(\frac78\right)^2+1
 =-\frac{41}{64}<0 .\] The first angular Fourier mode therefore gives negative constrained second variation for the centered circle. Also, on \([a_j,a_j+b_j]\), \[-\frac{\varphi''}{\varphi}
 \leq\frac{16}{7a_jr}
 \leq\frac{32}{7r^2}\leq\frac{16}{r^2}.\] For the chosen central intervals, the corresponding volume interval has length at least \(7\pi a_jb_j/8\), tending to infinity.

The rotation argument has a useful geometric qualification. A smooth finite-volume rotationally invariant constant-mean-curvature region cannot have an inner boundary circle when \(\varphi'>0\): the inner and outer boundary circles would have opposite signs of the oriented mean curvature. Since \(\varphi(r)\geq7r/8\), a finite-volume such region cannot extend to infinity. Thus a rotationally invariant isoperimetric region must be a centered disk. When that disk is unstable, an isoperimetric region is not rotationally invariant; its nontrivial rotation orbit supplies distinct minimizers and a nonzero volume-preserving Jacobi field. This explains the failure of uniqueness and strict stability for those volumes, conditional on the isoperimetric existence result invoked by the source.

## Supporting checks

For the concave normalized profile \(J=I^{n/(n-1)}\), its derivative has a finite limit at infinity. Therefore the tail variation of \(-D^2J\) tends to zero. At twice differentiable points, let \(B_\delta=\{v:-vJ''(v)>\delta\}\). Then \[\frac{|B_\delta\cap(r,2r)|}{r}
 \leq \frac2\delta\int_r^{2r}(-J''(v))\,dv\longrightarrow0 .\] This checks the selection in (3.32)–(3.33). In Section 4.4 the paper appropriately uses the full distributional measure \(|D^2J|\), rather than discarding a singular part. The weak-type maximal estimate and its dyadic assembly in (4.79)–(4.81) give \[\frac{|\text{excluded volumes}\cap(z,2z)|}{z}
 \leq20\sqrt{|D^2J|((z/8,\infty))}\longrightarrow0 .\] These measure estimates and the scale exponents in the averaged Ricci bounds were checked. The complete external comparison, regularity, convergence and existence inputs to those arguments are not certified by these computations.

## Remaining geometric scope

Both appendices were read, including the almost-minimizer regularity input and Proposition B.1’s distinction between a local Jacobi-field estimate and a global first-collision argument. A separate concern was considered at (4.63): a curvature-decay bound is asserted on a unit tube around a rescaled boundary, whereas curvature decay directly controls annuli away from the rescaled basepoint. A smaller annular tube is the natural domain to check. No complete counterexample satisfying all of the isoperimetric hypotheses was established, and no additional severity is assigned to this point. It remains an explicit limit of the global repair claimed here.

# Overall impact and recorded state

Problem 1 establishes an invalid identity and subsequent sign substitution on the proof path to the principal uniqueness result. The corrected formula and local graph estimates support a repair; they do not establish independent acceptance of the entire paper. Problem 2 concerns a domain larger than the later small-graph application needs. Problem 3 concerns stronger compactness than the actual spectral contradiction needs. The latter two findings do not demonstrate failure of the intended central conclusions.
