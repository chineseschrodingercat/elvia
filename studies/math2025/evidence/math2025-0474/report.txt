Abstract: The acquired article develops Trotter estimates using Favard regularity and, separately, energy constraints. Its identification of Favard spaces with fractional-power domains is false for the stated supremum-based definition. A diagonal self-adjoint operator gives an explicit counterexample, and finite truncations also disprove the claimed norm equivalence. This identification is used in the derivation of the Hilbert-space and singular-potential applications. The resulting assessment is Major, restricted to that proof dependency. The counterexample does not disprove all stated convergence rates. A separate omitted case in an increment lemma admits a direct repair.

# The abstract estimate and its actual hypotheses

Let \(A,L\) generate strongly continuous contraction semigroups on a Banach space. For \(L\) relatively \(A\)-bounded with bound less than one, Lemma 2.1 (PDF pp. 9–10) reduces the splitting error on \(D(A)\) to \[t\sup_{s,\tau\in[0,t]}
 \left\lVert[L,e^{sA/n}]e^{\tau(A+L)}x\right\rVert.\] The telescoping sum and the differentiated interpolation in (2.2) have the required order of factors. In particular, both endpoint products are evaluated on the common generator domain; this is not an unrestricted formal manipulation of unbounded operators.

Equation reference: eq:def. For \(0<\alpha<1\), the article defines (PDF p. 12) \[F_\alpha(A)=
 \left\{x\in X:\sup_{\lambda>0}
 \left\lVert\lambda^\alpha A(\lambda-A)^{-1}x\right\rVert<\infty\right\}.
 \tag{2.1}\] For higher orders it uses graph spaces and writes \(\alpha=k_\alpha+r_\alpha\), \(k_\alpha\in\mathbb N_0\), \(0<r_\alpha\le1\). The semigroup-increment characterization (2.14)–(2.16), PDF p. 14, is also a supremum norm.

Theorem 2.8 (PDF pp. 18–20) assumes \[F_\alpha\subset D(L),\qquad LF_\gamma\subset F_\beta,\qquad
 \alpha<\min\{1,\gamma\},\qquad
 k_\gamma<\beta\le\gamma<2,\] with positive exponents. The resulting rate has \(\delta=\min\{1,\beta,\gamma-\alpha\}\) and is \(O(t^{1+\delta}n^{-\delta})\) from \(F_\gamma\) to \(X\). The two terms in \[[L,T_h]=L(T_h-I)-(T_h-I)L\] explain the appearances of \(\gamma-\alpha\) and \(\beta\). The perturbation and domain-invariance steps are substantive hypotheses in this argument. In particular, the later replacement of \(k_\gamma<\beta\) by \(\gamma-1<\beta\) is not already part of this abstract theorem.

# A false identification used in the applications

Problem 1 (Lemma 2.16, PDF pp. 22–23).  For the definition [eq:def] (#eq:def), the asserted equality \(F_\alpha(A)=D(|A|^\alpha)\), including equivalence of norms, is false even for negative self-adjoint generators on a Hilbert space. The asserted unitary analogue is false as well.

Equation reference: eq:dyadic. Let \(X=\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), with indices beginning at one, and set \[Ke_k=2^ke_k,\qquad
 D(K)=\left\{y:\sum_{k\ge1}4^k|y_k|^2<\infty\right\},\qquad A=-K .\] Then \(K\) is positive self-adjoint, \(A\) generates the strongly continuous contraction semigroup \((T_ty)_k=e^{-t2^k}y_k\), and \(0\in\rho(A)\). These meet, and even strengthen, the stated assumptions. Take \(\alpha=1/2\) and \(x_k=2^{-k/2}\). Then \[\left\lVert x\right\rVert^2=\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}=1,\qquad
 \left\lVert K^{1/2}x\right\rVert^2=\sum_{k\ge1}1=\infty .\] On the other hand, for every \(\lambda>0\), \[\left\lVert\lambda^{1/2}A(\lambda-A)^{-1}x\right\rVert^2
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\lambda2^k}{(\lambda+2^k)^2}\le4.
 \tag{3.1}\] Indeed, on \(2^k\le\lambda\) the summand is at most \(2^k/\lambda\), whose geometric sum is at most two. On \(2^k>\lambda\) it is at most \(\lambda2^{-k}\), again with sum at most two. Empty low-frequency sums cause no difficulty. Thus \(x\in F_{1/2}(A)\setminus D(K^{1/2})\).

The same vector disproves the second assertion. The operator \(iK\) generates a strongly continuous unitary group, and \[\left\lVert\lambda^{1/2}iK(\lambda-iK)^{-1}x\right\rVert^2
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\lambda2^k}{\lambda^2+4^k}\le4\] by the same split. There is no domain or sign exception that excludes this example.

A finite-dimensional approximation makes the failure of norm equivalence particularly explicit. Let \(x^{(N)}\) agree with \(x\) in its first \(N\) coordinates and vanish elsewhere. Each vector belongs to every power domain, its \(X\)-norm is at most one, and the supremum in [eq:dyadic] (#eq:dyadic) is at most two after taking the square root, uniformly in \(N\). Yet \(\left\lVert K^{1/2}x^{(N)}\right\rVert=\sqrt N\). No uniform graph-norm bound in the claimed reverse direction is possible.

## The invalid step

On PDF p. 23 the proof optimizes a scalar spectral integrand at \(\lambda=r t/(1-r)\). This optimizing value depends on the spectral variable \(t\), which is subsequently integrated. In general \[\sup_{\lambda>0}\int g(\lambda,t)\,d\nu(t)
 \ \le\ \int\sup_{\lambda>0}g(\lambda,t)\,d\nu(t),\] and equality does not follow. In the example above, with \(r=1/2\), the left side is finite whereas the right side is \(\tfrac14\sum_{k\ge1}1=\infty\). The analogous optimization for \(iK\) has the same defect. This is not a missing multiplicative constant.

## Dependency and severity

Remark 2.17, PDF p. 22, uses the identification to assert (2.32) and to relax the exponent restriction in the subsequent corollaries. Corollaries 2.19 and 2.22, PDF pp. 24–25, explicitly invoke Lemma 2.16 and that remark. Examples 2.20 and 2.21 identify the corresponding Favard spaces with Sobolev spaces. The discussion of singular potentials on PDF pp. 25–26 and the derivation of Theorem 2.24 on PDF p. 27 rely on this identification, including its combination with perturbation stability in Lemma 2.7. Proposition 2.18, PDF p. 23, also cannot hold as stated: choosing the normal operator \(N=A\) above already contradicts its equality with \(D(|N|^{1/2})\).

The assessment is Major. A false regularity lemma is used to pass from the abstract hypotheses to important stated applications. The issue therefore concerns a central derivation rather than only an incidental description.

## Counterevidence and limits of correction

The forward inclusion \(D(|A|^\alpha)\subset F_\alpha(A)\), \(0<\alpha<1\), survives: the scalar spectral bound used in the proof does establish that direction. It does not establish the stronger assumption \(F_\alpha(A)\subset D(L)\) from a domain assumption involving only \(D(|A|^\alpha)\). Retaining the actual supremum definition is essential; changing it silently would change the theorem.

Nevertheless, some application estimates can be approached directly. For the \(\gamma=1\) setting of Corollary 2.22, let \(H,K\) be self-adjoint, \(0<\alpha,\beta<1\), with \(D(|K|^\alpha)\subset D(H)\) and \(HD(K)\subset D(|K|^\beta)\). Closed-graph boundedness and spectral calculus give, for \(0<|h|\le1\), \[\left\lVert(e^{ihK}-I)y\right\rVert_{D(|K|^\alpha)}
 \lesssim |h|^{1-\alpha}\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{D(K)},\qquad
 \left\lVert(e^{ihK}-I)Hy\right\rVert
 \lesssim |h|^\beta\left\lVert Hy\right\rVert_{D(|K|^\beta)} .\] The first estimate follows by splitting the spectral variable at \(|k|=|h|^{-1}\). The assumption \(\alpha<1\) also makes \(H\) infinitesimally \(K\)-bounded, so the self-adjoint sum has domain \(D(K)\), with equivalent graph norms and invariant graph evolution. The commutator argument on that domain therefore yields \(\delta=\min\{\beta,1-\alpha\}\) without the false equality.

This is a verified restricted alternative, not a repair of all fractional-\(\gamma\) statements or all singular-potential hypotheses. In particular, no counterexample to the published Coulomb rate has been established here, and no unavoidable loss of an extra power of \(n\) is inferred.

# A repairable omitted regularity case

Problem 2 (Lemma 2.6, PDF pp. 14–15).  The proof asserts that \(\gamma-\alpha<1\) implies \(k_\gamma=k_\alpha\). This implication is false.

For example, \(\alpha=3/4\), \(\gamma=5/4\) gives integer parts zero and one in the article’s convention. The omitted case can occur in the range of Theorem 2.8.

There is a direct correction. Write \(\alpha=k+a\), \(\gamma=k+1+b\), where \(0<b<a\le1\), and put \(Y=D(A^k)\) with its graph norm. For \(x\in F_\gamma\), the increment of \(Ax\) in \(Y\) satisfies \(\left\lVert(T_u-I)Ax\right\rVert_Y\lesssim u^b\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{F_\gamma}\). Commutativity and integration of the semigroup derivative yield \[\left\lVert(T_s-I)(T_t-I)x\right\rVert_Y
 \lesssim
 \begin{cases}
 s\,t^b\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{F_\gamma},&s\le t,\\
 t\,s^b\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{F_\gamma},&s>t.
 \end{cases}\] Multiplication by \(s^{-a}\) and taking the supremum gives \(O(t^{1+b-a})\). The remaining graph-norm term is \(O(t)\), hence also \(O(t^{1+b-a})\) for \(0<t\le1\). This is precisely the required \(t^{\gamma-\alpha}\) estimate.

The assessment of this omission is Minor: the printed case division is incomplete, but the missing case has the stated rate. It does not independently invalidate the abstract result.

# Separate checks and interpretation

The energy-constrained argument in Section 3 has distinct hypotheses. Theorem 3.6, PDF pp. 32–33, requires self-adjoint generators with joint energy-stability constants, a core for \(G^{1/2}\), twice continuously differentiable two-parameter products on that core, and a \(G^{1/2}\)-bounded commutator. Its energy-restricted norm estimate is not a claim of convergence in the unrestricted operator norm. The telescoping estimate in Lemma 3.7 correctly accounts for growth of the energy constraint. This part does not invoke the false Favard identification.

For the Schrödinger application, the hypotheses on PDF pp. 37 and 40 include \(V\in C^4\), bounded second derivative, polynomial growth of the third and fourth derivatives, and initial data in \(D(N)\), where \(N=P^2+Q^2\). Equation (3.45), PDF p. 41, visibly omits \(i\) from the potential exponential. The proof on the same page uses \(e^{itP^2/n}e^{itQ^2/n}\), which is the unitary product to which the displayed bound applies. Likewise the potential exponential in (3.43)–(3.44), PDF p. 40, must carry the unitary phase matching the sign of the joint evolution. These are correctable formula errors; a real growing exponential would not define the stated operation on every vector of \(D(N)\).

An exact bounded check of the intended harmonic product is available. For \(\psi_0(x)=\pi^{-1/4}e^{-x^2/2}\), \(N\psi_0=\psi_0\). A Gaussian calculation, with square-root branch continuous from \(h=0\), gives \[\left\lVert e^{ihP^2}e^{ihQ^2}\psi_0-e^{ihN}\psi_0\right\rVert^2
 =2-2\operatorname{Re}
       \frac{e^{-ih}}{\sqrt{1-2ih-2h^2}} .\] Its leading norm error is \(\sqrt2\,h^2+O(h^4)\), consistent with an \(O(h^2)\) local error and the stated upper-bound scale. This checks one smooth vector, not all unbounded-domain steps. No published numerical experiment was reproduced.

Overall assessment: Major, because of Lemma 2.16 and its material use in the application derivations. The corrected omitted case of Lemma 2.6, the surviving one-way domain inclusion, and the restricted direct commutator proof are counterevidence against a claim that all results fail. A full replacement of the fractional regularity argument remains to be supplied. Author adoption is unknown.
