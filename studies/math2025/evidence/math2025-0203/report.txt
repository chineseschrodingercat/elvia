Theorem 1.4, pp. 5475–5476 (PDF 7–8), concerns an \(m\)-equivariant \(H_m^3\) solution with \(m\ge1\) that blows up at a finite time \(T\). It gives an \(L^2\) decomposition \[u(t)-Q_{\ell(T-t),\gamma^*}\longrightarrow z^*,\qquad
\ell>0,\quad \gamma^*\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z),\] and a prescribed local structure for \(z^*\). # Time-variable clarification

Location. Proof of Corollary 4.17, p. 5546 (PDF 78), the sentence immediately after (4.80); compare the definition of \(s\) on p. 5481 (PDF 13), the definition \(\mu=\lambda\sqrt{E[u]}\) on p. 5526 (PDF 58), and (4.74), (4.78)–(4.81), pp. 5545–5546 (PDF 77–78).

Printed claim and decisive evidence. The sentence says that (4.77) follows by integrating (4.80) using \[ds=\lambda^2\,dt.\] The defining relation is instead \(ds/dt=1/\lambda^2(t)\), so the correct differential is \[ds=\lambda^{-2}\,dt,\qquad
\mu^2\,ds=E[u]\,dt.                                      \tag{2.1}\] Both the defining page and the incorrect sentence were actually inspected as images, including a separate high-resolution rendering of PDF 78. This is not an inference from a lost minus sign in extracted text.

More explicitly, put \[G=\sqrt{1+\mathcal F}+\frac{b}{\mu^{3/4}}.\] Equation (4.80) gives \(G_s\le K''\mu^2G\). Equation (2.1) yields \[G(t)\le e^{K''E[u](t-t_0)}G(t_0),\] which is exactly the dependence displayed on the next line of the proof. In contrast, the printed positive power would give \(\mu^2ds=E[u]\lambda^4dt\), inconsistent with that change of variables. The spacetime integrals in (4.74) and (4.81) also correctly use \(dt/\lambda^2\).

Impact, counterevidence, and repair boundary. The written time-change identity is false for the paper’s time variable. Replace \(\lambda^2\) by \(\lambda^{-2}\) in that sentence. The surrounding estimate, its physical-time exponential, and the stated spacetime measure already use the corrected identity. The demonstrated discrepancy is therefore an isolated typesetting error, with no failed calculation established downstream. Under the substantive-error scope of this review, it is a clarification and is not counted as a confirmed Minor problem. No alteration to the hypotheses, energy bound, or blow-up classification is justified.

# Positive checks and rejected defect candidates

## Soliton normalization and the regularity boundary

From (1.9), p. 5472 (PDF 4), write \(n=m+1\) and \(z=r^{2n}\). Direct integration and differentiation give \[Q(r)=\sqrt8\,n\,\frac{r^{n-1}}{1+r^{2n}},\qquad
A_\theta[Q](r)=-\frac{2nz}{1+z},\qquad M[Q]=8\pi n.\] Indeed, \(Q'/Q=(m+A_\theta[Q])/r\), so the Bogomol’nyi equation holds, and \(m+A_\theta[Q](\infty)=-m-2\), consistent with the exterior effective negative equivariance used in Remark 4.4 (PDF 59). The self-dual energy has the nonnegative form \(\frac12\left\|\mathbf D_u u\right\|_2^2\). Its scaling is \(E[u_\lambda]=\lambda^{-2}E[u]\), while mass is invariant. Thus \(\mu=\lambda\sqrt{E[u]}\) has the stated scaling.

The pseudoconformal transform on PDF 4 contains \(-1/t\) and the phase \(e^{ir^2/(4t)}\); for the static soliton at \(t<0\) its phase is \(e^{-ir^2/(4|t|)}\). At fixed \(t\ne0\), the leading tail of the \(k\)-th radial derivative is proportional to \(r^kQ(r)\). Its squared radial integral behaves as \[\int^\infty r^{2k-2m-3}\,dr,\] which converges exactly when \(k<m+1\). Consequently the explicit solution is in \(H^1\) for \(m\ge1\) and in \(H^3\) only for \(m\ge3\). The zero-radiation explicit solutions for \(m=1,2\) therefore do not contradict the nonzero coefficient imposed on \(H_m^3\) blow-up solutions; this agrees with Remark 1.9 (PDF 10).

## Why the energy argument can precede the blow-up rate

Lemma 4.2 (PDF 58–62) gives \[\beta+\left\|\epsilon\right\|_{\dot H_m^1}
+\left\|\epsilon_1\right\|_2\lesssim_M\mu,\qquad
\beta=(b^2+\eta^2)^{1/2}.\] The smallness threshold depends on the mass bound \(M\); the radiation’s \(L^2\) norm is bounded, not required to be small. In the proof, localized orthogonality first controls \(\beta\) and the inner part of \(\epsilon_1\); the global triangle inequality then controls all of \(\epsilon_1\). The nonlinear Hardy inequality (4.10) retains the nonperturbative exterior term. Thus the argument is not a small-\(L^2\) perturbation proof applied outside its assumptions. The cited proof of that Hardy inequality in the earlier soliton-resolution paper remains an external dependency.

For the conjugated operator, direct substitution into (2.21)–(2.22) gives \[\widetilde V_Q=m^2+\frac{4(m+1)}{1+r^{2m+2}},
\qquad -r\partial_r\widetilde V_Q=r^2Q^2\ge0 .\] Hence \(\widetilde V_Q\ge m^2\), and \(m\ge1\) supplies the positive Hardy term in Lemma 2.1 (PDF 23–24). This calculation supports the actual restriction on \(m\), not an extension to zero equivariance.

Starting from Proposition 4.12’s structural estimate, the integration in Corollary 4.17 is not circular. For small \(\mu\), \(|b|/\mu^{3/4}\lesssim_M\mu^{1/4}\ll1\), so \(G\) above is positive and comparable to \(\sqrt{1+\mathcal F}\), with no assumed sign of \(b\). The ratio of the troublesome forcing to the negative term in (4.79) is \[\frac{\beta^{9/2}|\log\beta|^2/\mu^3}
     {\beta^2/\mu^{3/4}}
=\frac{\beta^{5/2}|\log\beta|^2}{\mu^{9/4}}
\lesssim_M\mu^{1/4}|\log\mu|^2\longrightarrow0.\] The bound is uniform for \(0<\beta\lesssim_M\mu\), since \(x^{5/2}|\log x|^2\) is increasing sufficiently near zero; the case \(\beta=0\) follows by continuity. With the corrected time conversion, \(\int\mu^2ds=E[u]|I|\) is finite on a bounded physical-time interval. Thus this integration does not presuppose \(\lambda\asymp T-t\), \(b>0\), or monotone contraction. This check takes the structural remainder bounds as inputs; it does not independently prove every term in Lemma 4.15.

## Nondegeneracy, phase convergence, and parameter replacement

The refined variables in Lemma 5.2 (PDF 88) satisfy \[|\widehat b-b|+|\widehat\eta-\eta|\lesssim\mu^2,\quad
\left|\frac{\lambda_s}{\lambda}+\widehat b\right|
+|\gamma_s-(m+1)\widehat\eta|\lesssim\mu^2,\] and \[|\widehat b_s+\widehat b^2+\widehat\eta^2|
+|\widehat\eta_s|\lesssim\beta^3+\beta\mu^2+\mu^4.\] For a fixed blow-up solution, \(\mu\asymp_u\lambda\). With \(a=(\widehat b^2+\widehat\eta^2)^{1/2}/\lambda\), these estimates imply \[|a_t|\lesssim a+\lambda,\qquad
|\lambda_t|\lesssim a+\lambda.\] Thus \(h=a+\lambda>0\) satisfies \(|h_t|\lesssim h\). It is bounded by the coercivity estimates; its derivative is integrable up to \(T\), and two-sided Grönwall gives a strictly positive limiting value. Since \(\lambda\to0\), \(a\to\ell>0\). At a zero of the numerator one may use the absolute continuity of the vector norm; division by that norm is not essential.

There is no missing absolute-value hypothesis in Proposition 5.3 or Remark 5.4: the paper explicitly defines \(A\lesssim B\) to mean \(|A|\le CB\) for \(B\ge0\), on PDF 16–17. Next, in the order used on PDF 90–91, integration gives \(b\sim\ell^2(T-t)\), then almost decreasing \(\lambda\), then \(|\eta|\lesssim\lambda(T-t)\), and finally \(\lambda\sim\ell(T-t)\) and bounded \(\gamma_t\). Positivity of \(b\) is obtained at this stage. Theorem 1.4’s exact linear scale and fixed phase are permitted in the limiting \(L^2\) decomposition by strong continuity of the dilation and phase actions; they are not claims of exact finite-time modulation trajectories.

## The low-index profile and the origin

In (5.22)–(5.23), PDF 93–94, the leading profile terms rescale as \[\lambda^{-1}b^2(r/\lambda)=(b/\lambda)^2r,\qquad
\lambda^{-1}b^3(r/\lambda)^2=(b/\lambda)^3r^2 .\] The quoted modified-profile coefficients therefore give respectively \(-e^{i\gamma^*}\ell^2\sqrt2\,\chi(\ell r)r/8\) for \(m=1\), and \(ie^{i\gamma^*}\ell^3\sqrt2\,\chi(\ell r)r^2/64\) for \(m=2\). This verifies the rescaling of those coefficients, rather than independently reconstructing the entire profile expansion.

The change from \(\chi(\ell r)\) in the proof to \(\chi(r)\) in the theorem is harmless: their difference is smooth, compactly supported, and zero near the origin, so it can be absorbed into the regular part. Also, (1.16) does imply \(z_{\rm rg}^*=o(r^2)\). Put \(z_{\rm rg}^*(r)=r^2f(\log r)\). The weighted \(k=0,1\) conditions give \(f,2f+f'\in L^2((-\infty,c))\), hence \(f\in H^1\cap L^2\) and \(f(x)\to0\) as \(x\to-\infty\). The term singular here concerns the stronger weighted/exterior regularity; it should not be read as a claim that \(\chi(r)r^me^{im\theta}\) itself is nonsmooth in the original equivariance class.
