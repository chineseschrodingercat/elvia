# The cylinder criterion

Write \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal M)\) and let \(h\) be its homological dimension: the maximum dimension of the span of horizontal cylinder core curves over horizontally periodic surfaces. The rank is half the complex dimension of the absolute tangent space \(p(T\mathcal M)\). Minimal homological dimension means \(h=r\), not merely \(h\geq r\). Cylinder equivalence means persistence of parallelism throughout a neighborhood in \(\mathcal M\), not just parallelism on a single surface (Definition 2.1, p. 6).

In the forward implication of Theorem 1.7, the reduction to a cylindrically stable surface uses Lemma 2.7, p. 8, as an input. Let \(W\subset H_1(X;\mathbb R)\) be the span of the horizontal core curves there. Restriction of homology functionals to the absolute tangent space gives a map \(\varphi\). The stated stability property supplies \[\dim\varphi(W)=r,\qquad \dim W\leq h=r.\] Therefore \(\dim W=r\) and \(\varphi|_W\) is injective. Collinearity of the functionals of equivalent cylinders consequently implies collinearity of their homology classes.

The final conversion from collinearity to equality also has a topological justification. A cylinder core is an embedded simple closed curve with nonzero period, so it is nonseparating and its integral homology class is primitive. Collinear primitive integral classes are equal up to sign. Orienting parallel cores in the common positive direction fixes the sign through their positive periods. This checks the homologous-core conclusion; arbitrary proportional integral classes would not suffice.

Conversely, when cores within an equivalence class \(\mathcal C\) are homologous, the absolute image of the standard deformation is \[p(\sigma_{\mathcal C})=
 \left(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}h_C\right)\operatorname{PD}(\gamma),
 \qquad \sum_{C\in\mathcal C}h_C>0 .\] The cylinder deformation theorem, used here as stated in the source, places these classes in \(p(T\mathcal M)\). Thus the complexification of \(\operatorname{PD}(W)\) lies in that \(2r\)-dimensional complex symplectic space. Disjoint horizontal cores have zero pairwise intersection, so this subspace is isotropic and has dimension at most \(r\). Together with the source’s lower bound \(h\geq r\), this yields \(h=r\). The proof’s use of the word Lagrangian on p. 13 can be weakened to isotropic for this dimension estimate; maximality is not needed. No failure of the claimed implication follows from that wording.

# The final arithmetic in Section 9

Theorem 1.8 asserts a classification under exactly \(2g-2r\) zero exponents, the maximum allowed at rank \(r\). It does not assert that every minimal-homological-dimension locus has this many zero exponents. Section 9 first excludes \(r=1\) by an explicitly cited external classification, then assumes \(r>1\), non-full rank, and maximal vanishing.

For the remaining audit, take as inputs the preceding classification, Lemma 9.1’s non-varying sum, the optimal-cover property, and Corollary 6.13. Let \(d\geq2\) be the cover degree, \(g'\geq2\) the base genus, \(R\) total ramification, and \(P\) the number of regular Weierstrass points on the base. Goodness of the cover (Definition 6.9, p. 27) is essential to the single-preimage-cylinder property: a cylinder has unchanged height and circumference multiplied by \(d\). Consequently the finite-coset average in (9.0.2)–(9.0.3) acquires a factor \(1/d\); it is not a claim for arbitrary covers.

Equation reference: eq:sum, eq:finite. Put \[w(m)=\frac{m(m+2)}{m+1},\qquad
 A=\sum_iw(m_i),\quad A'=\sum_iw(m_i'),\quad
 S=\sum_{d_i\ \mathrm{odd}}\frac{1}{d_i+2}.\] The positive zero orders \(m_i\geq1\) must be distinguished from regular marked points. Equation (9.0.6), multiplied by 12, is \[
 3(1-d^{-1})S=A-d^{-1}A'.\] Since \(1<(m+2)/(m+1)\leq3/2\), the strict inequality in (9.0.7) is justified by at least one positive zero. Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(2g-2=d(2g'-2)+R\). The inputs \(R\geq P-1\) and \(S\leq P+(2g'+2-P)/3\), followed by \(P\leq2g'+2\), yield exactly \[
 18d-24>(2d^2-4d+3)(2g'-2).\] The coefficient of \(P\) at the substitution step is \(2d-4\geq0\), so its direction is correct even at \(d=2\).

This really excludes all larger degrees, not just tested examples. At \(g'=2\), the right side minus the left in [eq:finite] (#eq:finite) is \(2(2d-3)(d-5)\), nonnegative for \(d\geq5\). For \(g'\geq3\), it is at least \(2(4d-9)(d-2)\), nonnegative for every integer \(d\geq2\). Hence only \(g'=2\) and \(d\in\{2,3,4\}\) remain.

## Ramification cases and a bounded clarification

For \(P=6\), the exact strict upper bounds on \(R\), for \(d=2,3,4\), are \(13/2,7,25/4\); since \(R\geq5\) and \(R\) is even, \(R=6\). For \(P=5\) the bounds are \(11/2,17/3,19/4\), and \(R\geq4\) forces \(R=4\). Parity follows directly from Riemann–Hurwitz.

Equation reference: eq:weight. There is a safer direct check of the \(P=6\) contradiction than the count of order-one zeros stated on p. 45. A ramification index \(e\) over a base simple zero produces a zero of order \(2e-1\), and \[
 w(2e-1)-e\,w(1)=\frac{e^2-1}{2e}\geq0.\] The two base simple zeros therefore contribute at least \(3d\) to \(A\). Each branched regular point contributes at least \(w(1)=3/2\). Branching over at least five such points gives \(A\geq3d+15/2\), which is stronger than the lower bound \(3d+6\) used in the printed contradiction.

For precision, the conditional profile with simple branching over one base simple zero and five regular points has \(2d+3\) order-one zeros and one order-three zero, rather than \(2d+4\) order-one zeros. Its weighted contribution is \(3d+33/4\), so it too satisfies the needed lower bound. This is local pullback bookkeeping, not a construction of an invariant variety satisfying all the contradictory hypotheses. No counterexample to Theorem 1.8 is asserted.

Using even the weaker valid bound, [eq:sum] (#eq:sum) would require \[18(1-d^{-1})\geq3d+6-3d^{-1},
 \quad\text{hence}\quad
 6d^2-24d+30\leq0.\] But \(6d^2-24d+30=6(d-2)^2+6>0\). The printed strict sign at this final step is unnecessary; the non-strict inequality already contradicts positivity. For \(P=5\), four simple regular branch points give \(A=4(3/2)+d(8/3)\) and \(A'=8/3\). The resulting equation is \[8d^2-30d+40=8(d-15/8)^2+95/8=0,\] which has no real solution. These checks preserve the terminal contradictions conditional on the stated inputs.
