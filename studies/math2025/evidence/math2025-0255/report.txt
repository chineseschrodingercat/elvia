Abstract: The exact author manuscript arXiv:2303.00245v2 is examined. One Major proof defect and six Minor corrections are established. The Major issue concerns the passage from a compatible product of flag spaces to a join in the proof of Lemma 4.22: the stated filtration and hemisphere cover do not have the asserted properties. An explicit finite example disproves the intermediate assertions, while supporting the lemma’s conclusion. A separate counterexample distinguishes ordinary bar homology from absolute Tor in the generality of Proposition 4.9. The remaining corrections concern rank zero, two inclusion–exclusion displays, the definition of splittings and local Morse-filtration claims. The field-connectivity and Koszul-dual conclusions are not disproved by these examples. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# An essential model-comparison proof

Problem 1 (Major; Lemma 4.22, pp. 29–30).  The filtration used to identify \(D^{a,b}(M)\) with an iterated suspension of \(T^{a,b}(M)\) has a product as its final term, and the proposed two hemispheres do not cover the preceding term.

The lemma asserts \[D^{a,b}(M)\simeq \Sigma^{a+b+1}T^{a,b}(M).\] After establishing \(D^{a,b}\simeq\Sigma\mathring L^{a,b}\), the proof defines \(F_k\subset\mathring L^{a,b}\) by requiring the first \(k\) factors to lie in \(T(M)\) or \(ST(M)\). It then identifies \(F_{a+b}\) with the compatible join \(T^{a,b}(M)\) and claims \(N_k\cup S_k=F_{k-1}\), with both pieces contractible.

Take the field \(\mathbb F_2\), \(M=\mathbb F_2^2\), \(a=2\), \(b=0\). There are exactly three nonzero proper subspaces, \[L_1=\langle e_1\rangle,\qquad L_2=\langle e_2\rangle,
 \qquad L_3=\langle e_1+e_2\rangle.\] Any pair of flags has a common basis. Definition 4.15 therefore gives \(L^{2,0}=L(M)\times L(M)\), and the defined final term is \[F_2=T(M)\times T(M),\] a discrete space with nine points. On the other hand, \[T^{2,0}(M)=T(M)*T(M)=K_{3,3}.\] The graph has six vertices, nine edges and a rank-five incidence matrix. Consequently, \[\widetilde H_0(F_2)=\mathbb Z^8,\qquad
 \widetilde H_0(K_{3,3})=0,\qquad H_1(K_{3,3})=\mathbb Z^4.\] The two spaces are not even homotopy equivalent. This example satisfies the PID and common-basis hypotheses without any degenerate rank convention.

The covering assertion fails independently. In the diagonal simplicial set take the 1-simplex with first flag \(0\subset M\) and second flag \(L_1\subseteq L_1\). It lies in \(\mathring L^{2,0}=F_0\), because the second flag is not full. An interior point has positive weights on both \(0\) and \(M\) in its first factor. It lies in neither \(N_1\) nor \(S_1\). Moreover, with the same example, \[F_1=T(M)\times L(M),\qquad
 N_2=T(M)\times L_{\text{missing }M}(M).\] The second factor is contractible, but the first has three components. Thus \(N_2\) is not contractible either. These are structural failures of the proposed induction, not an indexing change in the suspension exponent.

Counterevidence and impact. The conclusion survives this test. Since \(D^{1,0}(M)\simeq\bigvee^2S^2\), the actual quotient is \[D^{2,0}(M)=D^{1,0}(M)\wedge D^{1,0}(M)
 \simeq\bigvee^4S^4
 \simeq\Sigma^3K_{3,3}.\] No counterexample to the lemma’s conclusion is asserted. The defective proof nevertheless lies on the central chain: Lemma 4.22 is used in Corollary 4.23, the iterated-bar Lemma 1.3, Proposition 5.8 and the comparison in Lemma 6.2. A valid natural comparison is needed before those deductions follow from the written argument. This substantive effect on the core proof warrants Major assessment even though a replacement argument is available.

Repair boundary. For positive rank, the augmented flag spaces have the form \(\Delta^1*T(M)\) and \(\Delta^1*ST(M)\), with nonfull boundary \(\partial\Delta^1*T(M)\) or \(\partial\Delta^1*ST(M)\). Compatibility restricts the interior supports. A correct quotient argument should keep these boundary conditions in all factors. Writing the endpoint and interior weights in factor \(i\) as \((u_i,v_i,s_i)\), put \[t_i=\frac{u_i}{u_i+v_i},\qquad
 y_i=\frac{s_i}{1-s_i},\qquad
 r=\sum_i y_i,\qquad w_i=y_i/r.\] Off the collapsed boundary, \(t_i\in(0,1)\), and the compatible weighted supports \((w_i,x_i)\) describe the join. The radial compactification and the collapsed cube boundary yield \(\Sigma^{a+b+1}T^{a,b}\). At \(r=0\) the support coordinates disappear; at \(r=\infty\) or a cube endpoint one reaches the collapsed boundary. This gives a replacement through finite subcomplexes, extended in the CW topology. Naturality under isomorphisms and forgetting complements must be included. The detailed auxiliary notes record this construction; it does not validate the printed \(F_k\) proof. The cited single-factor comparison in [Retained latex: \cite[Lemma 6.1, p.~25]{gkrw}] uses two genuine cone subspaces. Its actual source page was checked; it does not establish the false multifactor filtration assertions. Rank zero must be treated separately, as in Problem 3.

# Additional confirmed corrections

Problem 2 (Minor; Proposition 4.9, p. 27).  Freeness of one module over \(\mathbb Z\) alone does not make the ordinary bar construction compute absolute Tor for an arbitrary \(\operatorname{VB}\)-ring.

Concentrate all objects in \(\operatorname{VB}\)-degree zero, where the monoidal product is the ordinary tensor product over \(\mathbb Z\), and set \[A=\mathbb Z[\epsilon]/(2\epsilon,\epsilon^2),\qquad M=N=\mathbb Z,\] with the augmentation action \(\epsilon\mapsto0\). Both modules are free abelian in every \(\operatorname{VB}\)-degree, including the zero groups in positive degrees. Thus the printed freeness hypothesis holds on both sides.

The augmentation ideal \(I=(\epsilon)\) is \(\mathbb Z/2\). The normalized bar construction has \(I^{\otimes p}\cong\mathbb Z/2\) in every positive degree, and all differentials vanish because \(I^2=0\). In particular \(H_3(B(M,A,N))=\mathbb Z/2\). A free \(A\)-resolution through the degrees needed for \(\operatorname{Tor}_3\) has differentials \[d_1=(\epsilon),\qquad d_2=(2\ \epsilon),\qquad
 d_3=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0&0\\0&2&\epsilon\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 d_4=\begin{pmatrix}
 2&\epsilon&0&0&0\\0&0&\epsilon&0&0\\0&0&0&2&\epsilon
 \end{pmatrix}.\] Exactness follows directly by writing every element of \(A\) uniquely as \(x+\bar y\epsilon\), with \(x\in\mathbb Z\) and \(\bar y\in\mathbb Z/2\): \(\ker d_1=(2,\epsilon)\); \(\ker d_2\) is generated by \((\epsilon,0),(0,2),(0,\epsilon)\); and \(\ker d_3\) is generated by the five displayed columns of \(d_4\). These free modules can be continued to a resolution.

After tensoring with \(\mathbb Z\), the kernel of \(d_3\) is \(\mathbb Ze_1\oplus\mathbb Ze_3\), while the image of \(d_4\) is \(2\mathbb Ze_1\oplus2\mathbb Ze_3\). Hence \[\operatorname{Tor}^A_3(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)=(\mathbb Z/2)^2
 \not\cong H_3(B(\mathbb Z,A,\mathbb Z)).\] This is an exact integral calculation, not a numerical test.

Counterevidence, impact and repair. The general statement needs a flatness hypothesis on the underlying algebra, or a derived bar construction with suitable flat replacements. The Steinberg groups actually used later are free abelian, and the simplicial chain groups used in Lemma 6.2 are free. The above torsion algebra is not their algebra. Thus this defect limits the general proposition; it does not by itself invalidate the paper’s specific Koszul calculation. The repair must preserve the distinction between absolute derived functors and a relative bar resolution. The cited [Retained latex: \cite[Proposition 2.33(ii), pp.~18--19]{mpp}] was also checked in its exact source: it requires both its algebra \(R_n\) and its left module \(B_n\) to be free abelian. The algebra hypothesis is therefore present in the original reference and absent from the statement under review.

Problem 3 (Minor; Lemmas 4.12 and 4.22, Proposition 5.8, and Theorem 6.3, pp. 27–34).  Rank zero is included in several formulas that require positive rank.

The paper explicitly sets \(D^{a,b}(0)=S^0\) on p. 29. For Lemma 4.12 take \(A=\mathbf1\), the unit in \(\operatorname{VB}\)-based spaces, and \(\alpha=0\), \(\beta=-1\). Then \(A(0)=S^0\) and \(A(R^n)=*\) for \(n>0\). Every value is nonempty, so the required \((-1)\)-connectivity holds for every \(n\). The bar simplicial spaces are constant and proper. Nevertheless \(BA(0)=S^0\) is not connected, contrary to the conclusion at \(n=0\). The proof’s assertion about degree-zero bar simplices being contractible also only applies at positive rank.

Similarly, for \((a,b)=(1,0)\) the right side of Lemma 4.22 at \(M=0\) is \(\Sigma^2T(0)\). The building is empty. With the usual augmented convention \(\Sigma\varnothing=S^0\), this is \(S^1\), whereas the left side is \(S^0\). With an ordinary unaugmented convention it still cannot give the stated unit. Proposition 5.8 at \(n=0,k=3\) would likewise make \(D^{3,0}_0=S^0\) connected.

Theorem 6.3 explicitly says \(n,i\ge0\). At \(n=i=0\), \[\operatorname{Tor}^{\operatorname{St}(R)}_0(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)_0=\mathbb Z,\qquad
 \widetilde H_{-3}(T^2_0(R))=0,\] because \(T^2_0\) is empty and its augmented simplicial chains have no degree \(-3\) term. The first isomorphism is false at that endpoint. The later field formula \(\operatorname{St}_0\otimes\operatorname{St}_0=\mathbb Z\) is itself consistent with the unit.

Counterevidence, impact and repair. The introduction already restricts Theorem 1.5 to \(n\ge1\), and Theorem 5.9 treats the small ranks separately. State the suspension and connectivity bounds for positive rank, retain \(D_0=S^0\), and record the rank-zero Tor value separately. The positive-rank theorems are not contradicted by these unit examples. In applying the bar connectivity argument, zero-rank factors contribute units and should not be treated as extra highly connected factors.

Problem 4 (Minor; last display of the proof of Theorem 2.25, p. 11).  The summation order is reversed in the displayed Möbius-inversion formula.

Take \(R=\mathbb Q\), \(n=2\), \(k=1\), \(U_1=\mathbb Qe_1\) and \(S=\varnothing\). The module is split and has the common-basis property. By Definition 2.15, \(F_{\mathcal U}(\varnothing)=2-1=1\). The printed sum over \(S\supseteq T\) includes only \(T=\varnothing\) and gives \(2\). The correct inversion of the preceding display is \[F_{\mathcal U}(S)=\sum_{T\supseteq S}
 (-1)^{|S|-|T|}\operatorname{rank}(U_T).\] For the example this is \(2-1=1\). Definition 2.24 already has the correct direction, and the corrected formula proves the stated criterion. Thus the counterexample concerns the displayed inversion, not Theorem 2.25.

Problem 5 (Minor; Lemma 2.27, case (i), p. 13).  Two successive terms replace the running intersection \(U_T\) by \(U_A\).

Use \(R=\mathbb Z\), \(n=4\), \(\ell=1\), \(k=3\) and \[U_1=\langle e_1\rangle,\quad
 V_1=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle,\quad
 V_2=\langle e_1,e_2,e_3\rangle,\quad A=\varnothing.\] The flag condition and both required common-basis hypotheses hold using the standard basis. Set \(S=\{2,3\}\). The rank on the left is \(\operatorname{rank}(V_1\cap U_1)=1\). In the offending sum the only running subset is \(T=\{1\}\); replacing \(U_T\) by \(U_A=\mathbb Z^4\) makes its term \(\operatorname{rank}(U_A\cap V_1\cap V_2)=2\). Restoring \(U_T\) in both lines gives the correct value \(1\).

The surrounding cancellation and modular-law argument works with this correction. The compatibility citations on p. 15 should also refer to \(U_1,\ldots,U_{k-2},V_i\), the collections assumed compatible, rather than using \(U_k\) in those lists. These are local substitutions; an independent construction of a compatible basis verifies the lemma itself. They do not supply a counterexample to its conclusion.

Problem 6 (Minor; Definition 3.2 and Proposition 3.3, p. 16).  The simplicial-complex definition of a splitting omits the requirement that its components be nonzero.

The displayed definition only requires \(M=V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_k\). Literally it permits \((0,M)\) and \((M,0)\) as vertices of \(ST(M)\). For \(M=\mathbb F\), these form an edge, whereas \(T(M)\) is empty. The proposed map forgetting the complement would take its vertices to \(0\) and \(M\), neither of which belongs to \(T(M)\). At rank two, the allowed size-three tuple \((\mathbb Fe_1,0,\mathbb Fe_2)\) maps in Proposition 3.3 to a repeated vertex, not a 1-simplex.

Counterevidence, impact and repair. Definition 3.5 on the same page explicitly uses proper nonzero components, and the comparison arguments use that interpretation. Add the nonzero requirement in Definitions 3.2 and the statement of Proposition 3.3. Keep the different convention of Definition 4.14: its simplicial set intentionally permits zero components and uses them for degeneracies. The example exposes a definition mismatch; it does not refute the comparison theorem with the intended nonzero splittings.

Problem 7 (Minor; Proposition 3.12 proof, pp. 20–21).  Two descriptions of the Morse filtration are stronger than the properties that hold and are needed.

Let \(M=\mathbb Q^4\), \(V_0=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\), \(\tau=\sigma=\{V_0\}\), and choose two distinct preimages with complements \[C_1=\langle e_3,e_4\rangle,\qquad
 C_2=\langle e_3,e_4+e_2\rangle.\] These meet all hypotheses of Proposition 3.12: \(r=2\), \(\tau\) is a nonempty flag, and each \(\{V_0,C_i\}\) has a common basis. The bases \((e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)\) and \((e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4+e_2)\) also show that \(W=\langle e_1,e_3\rangle\) is a vertex of the intersection \(X\). It is not comparable to \(V_0\), and \(W+V_0\ne M\). Yet the printed bound is \[\operatorname{rank}W\le n-\operatorname{rank}V_0-1=1,\] whereas \(\operatorname{rank}W=2\). The valid bound is \(\operatorname{rank}W\le n-2\): the proper split module \(W+V_0\) strictly contains \(W\). This is exactly what the \(A_1,\ldots,A_{n-2}\) filtration requires.

For the assertion about \(G_{k-1}\) on p. 21, retain the same data and put \[W_B=\langle e_2,e_3,e_4\rangle,\quad U=\langle e_3\rangle,
 \quad d=2,\quad k=1.\] Then \(W_B\in B_1\), \(k<d\), and \(U\in\operatorname{Link}^{<}_X(W_B)\cap G_0\). The vertex \(W_B\) has rank at least \(\operatorname{rank}U\) but is not in \(G_0\). Thus \(G_{k-1}\) does not contain every vertex of rank at least \(\operatorname{rank}U\) as claimed.

The actual image \(f(U)=U+(W_B\cap V_0)=\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\) does lie in \(G_0\). In general the required argument is \(f(U)+V_0=U+V_0\ne M\). Also, the homotopy assertion about the order-preserving map uses \(\mathrm{id}\le f\) and \(f^2=f\); order preservation alone would not suffice. These repairs preserve the inductive contractibility argument. The examples do not refute Proposition 3.12.

# Coverage and proof dependencies

The algebraic preliminaries were checked using saturation over a PID. Intersections of summands are saturated, while their sums need not be; the index-two example over \(\mathbb Z\) correctly separates these facts. For the common-basis criterion, proceeding upward through the intersection poset produces complements of the sums of strict lower intersections. Their ranks are the corank function values. When these sums split, the construction gives a minimal common spanning set. Its size is \(n\) exactly when it is a basis. Möbius inversion then yields Theorem 2.25 with the corrected summation direction. The two-step flag lemma was checked through the four membership cases and an independent basis construction.

The comparison in Section 3 was examined at the level of its actual hypotheses. Fullness follows from the flag lemma. In Proposition 3.12 the successive links have the stated cone or retraction after the local corrections above; the case of complements to \(V_0\) reduces the rank and preserves the required distinctness of lifted flags. The higher-building induction removes one join factor. Theorem 3.15 uses a cover obtained by compatible choices of complements; intersections of at least two distinct lifts are contractible and contain the original flag. No additional counterexample to this homology comparison was established.

The direct-sum multiplication and bar identification in Section 4 were checked by restricting compatible flags to the summands of an ordered decomposition. Zero summands encode degeneracies; augmentation faces agree with the nonfull-lattice quotient. The conversion between the product and join models requires the replacement argument in Problem 1. The comparison in Corollary 4.23 must use the suspensions of the natural forgetful maps, not merely unrelated equivalences of the source and target models. The suspended CW comparisons and the small-rank conventions then justify the passage from homology to homotopy.

For Section 5, the fiber of the map to \(\operatorname{CB}_n\) becomes contractible by coning a finite image to a vertex in a fresh join factor. The equality \(T_n^2(F)=T_n(F)*T_n(F)\) uses the field hypothesis; it is not asserted for general PIDs. The bad-edge and bad-vertex modifications prove simple connectivity in rank at least three. After the valid suspended-model comparison and a positive-rank bar-connectivity statement, homology vanishing and Hurewicz give the claimed range. For the conditional PID statements, the hypotheses needed at all ranks occurring in the bar factors should be stated explicitly; no counterexample to the intended uniform reading was established.

Finally, the building has dimension \(n-2\) and is \((n-3)\)-connected, so its top reduced homology is free abelian and there is no other reduced homology. This supplies the concentration used in Lemma 6.2. In the hypertor spectral sequence, for fixed \(\operatorname{VB}\)-degree \(n\), the internal homological degree is \(n\); nonzero higher differentials therefore have no target on that row. The bar length is bounded by \(n\) after normalization in positive rank, supplying convergence. For a field, the join formula and the torsion-free Künneth calculation produce \(\operatorname{St}_n(F)\otimes\operatorname{St}_n(F)\) in the stated degree. Question 1.6 asks for additional algebra and coalgebra structure and is not a proved claim. No-code availability was not used as an adverse criterion.
