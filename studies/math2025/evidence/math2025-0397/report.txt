Sharpness and Locality for Percolation
on Finite Transitive Graphs

Author. Philip Easo. Frozen identity. Geometric and Functional Analysis 35 (2025), 1526–1586; DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-025-00726-w. Reviewed object. # Assessment and scope

The manuscript connects sharpness and locality for bounded-degree finite transitive graphs through four quantitative steps: large clusters give local connection bounds; a multiscale argument extends these bounds; global connections give approximate uniqueness; a coupling then produces a giant cluster. The coarse geometric alternative is particularly interesting.

Major revision is required for this exact version. The finite-graph cost used in the central induction diverges after volume saturation. This gives explicit counterexamples to Lemmas 3.5 and 3.6 under all their printed hypotheses, not merely a missing estimate. It affects a core dependency of Proposition 3.1 and the proof of the main theorem. A piecewise zero-cost convention repairs the saturated branch, as explained below, but it must be inserted consistently. No counterexample to Theorem 1.1 is established here.

Eight additional Minor issues concern local counting, rounding, parameter domains, and proof algebra. Their severity reflects the bounded repairs available and their actual role, without a quota. The complete 47-page source, including the appendix and references, was read. Relevant complete proof units in three retained arXiv references and a further published primary reference were also examined. This is an initial scientific review, with independent acceptance still pending.

# Major issue

## P1. Finite-volume saturation invalidates the stated cost lemmas

Location. Definition of \(\Delta_t(n)\) and Lemma 3.5, p. 20; Lemma 3.6, p. 21; its finite-limit case, p. 24; appendix proof of Lemma 3.5, pp. 42–43. The dependencies run through Lemma 3.2, pp. 21–22, Proposition 3.1, pp. 17–19, and Claim 6.1, pp. 38–39.

Definitions and scope. Section 3.1 fixes an arbitrary finite transitive graph of degree \(d\). It uses \[\phi(t)=1-2^{-e^t},\quad
 \delta(n)=e^{-\sqrt{\log\log n}},\quad
 R(n)=e^{(\log n)^9},\quad L(n)=\sqrt{\log n},\] and \(\mathcal L=\{n\ge3:\operatorname{Gr}(n)\le e^{(\log n)^{100}}\}\). The integer \(U_t(n)\) is the largest \(b\le n^{1/3}/8\) for which \(\mathbb P_{\phi(t)}(\mathrm{Piv}[4b,n^{1/3}])\le1/\log n\), and \[\Delta_t(n)=
 \left(\frac{\log\log n}{(\log n)\wedge\log\operatorname{Gr}(U_t(n))}\right)^{1/4}.\] There is no upper restriction on \(n\) relative to the diameter or volume. Real radii are rounded down as specified in footnote 9, p. 5.

Exact witness. Take \(G=C_3\), \(d=2\), \(t=0\), so \(\phi(t)=1/2\). Distinct vertices are connected if the direct edge is open or the other two edges are both open. Hence \[\min_{u,v}\mathbb P_{1/2}(u\leftrightarrow v)
 =\frac12+\frac12\left(\frac12\right)^2=\frac58.\] Once \(\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor>1\), the outer sphere is empty. The pivotal event is therefore impossible, independently of the percolation parameter. For sufficiently large \(n\), \[U_s(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{1/3}}8\right\rfloor,\quad
 \operatorname{Gr}(U_s(n))=3,\quad
 \Delta_s(n)=\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log3}\right)^{1/4}
 \longrightarrow\infty\] for every real \(s\). Thus the finite-limit argument on p. 24 has the limit in the wrong direction.

Counterexample to all quantifiers in Lemma 3.6. Choose \(\varepsilon=1/2\) and \(n_1=e^{24}\). Every \(n\ge n_1\) belongs to \(\mathcal L\) and is orange at time \(0\), since \[\delta(n)\le e^{-\sqrt{\log24}}<\frac12<\frac58.\] The restriction \(t\le1/\varepsilon\) is satisfied. Yet, for every \(n\ge n_1\), \[\Delta_{t+\varepsilon}(n)
 \ge\left(\frac{\log24}{\log3}\right)^{1/4}>1>\varepsilon.\] Consequently no proposed \(n_2(2,\varepsilon,n_1)\) can supply an interval \([L(m),m]\subset[n_1,n_2]\) satisfying the conclusion. Every point in any such interval lies in \(\mathcal L\) and violates the asserted bound.

Counterexample to Lemma 3.5 at arbitrarily large scales. Set \(n=\exp(\exp k)\), with \(k\ge48\) arbitrarily large. Here \[L(n)=e^{k/2}\ge e^{24}.\] The scale \(L(n)\) is orange and belongs to \(\mathcal L\). Every tube of radius \(L(n)\) contains the whole triangle, so its endpoint connection probability is at least \(5/8\). Since \(\delta(R(L(n)))\le\delta(L(n))<1/2\), this scale is green. All hypotheses of Lemma 3.5 are satisfied, however large its proposed \(n_0(2)\), whereas \[\Delta_0(n)=\left(\frac{k}{\log3}\right)^{1/4}>1>\frac1k
 =\frac1{\log\log n}.\] The appendix’s claim that the case above the diameter is trivial because \(\Delta_t(n)\le(\log n)^{-1/5}\) is false for the same reason.

Impact and verified repair boundary. These are false core auxiliary assertions in the finite-graph argument. The triangle is not a counterexample to the asymptotic Theorem 1.1: a fixed finite graph does not satisfy its infinite-family hypothesis.

A concrete repair to the saturated branch is to replace the definition by \[\Delta_t^*(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor>\operatorname{diam}G,\\
 \Delta_t(n),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}\] In the first case \(B_n=G\) and every tube of radius \(n\) is \(G\). Orange already implies green because \(\delta(R(n))\le\delta(n)\). Thus the zero-cost conclusion required in Lemma 3.4 is valid, and the saturated case of Lemma 3.5 becomes automatic. For a fixed finite graph \(\Delta_t^*(n)\) is eventually zero, repairing the finite-limit branch of Lemma 3.6. In a local limit that is finite, connected regular graphs are eventually that same graph once a sufficiently large ball is matched. For an infinite local limit and each fixed scale, the approximating diameters eventually exceed that scale, so this modification does not change the infinite-limit estimates.

This checks the proposed correction exactly where the displayed failure occurs. It does not make the printed definition correct, or by itself constitute acceptance of every imported estimate. The definition, all cost statements, and the induction must be updated together; the domain issue in P7 also remains necessary. An alternative is a carefully proved scale restriction and an early stopping rule, not the unqualified limit on p. 24.

# Minor issues and bounded corrections

## P2. The packing in Lemma 2.7 permits overlapping closed balls

Location. Lemma 2.7 and its proof, p. 13. The centers have distance at least \(2r\), but the proof adds the sizes of closed \(r\)-balls as if disjoint.

All-hypothesis witness to the proof implication. Let \(G=C_L\square K_{10,10}\), where \(L\) is a sufficiently large even integer; take \(p=\delta=9/10\), \(r=1\), and \(n=55\). The graph is connected, finite, vertex-transitive, and \(12\)-regular, with \(|V|=20L\) and \(|B_1|=13\). Every neighbor is connected to the origin with probability at least \(p=\delta\). For any proposed positive constant \(c(\delta)\), choose even \(L\) so large that \(55\le c(\delta)20L/13\).

Use the bipartition consisting of the first side of \(K_{10,10}\) at even layers and the second side at odd layers as \(W\). It is a maximal set with mutual distances at least two. In the auxiliary graph \(H\), choose a spanning tree containing the star through the ten first-side vertices in layer zero, rooted at one of them. Let \(U\) be these ten vertices. It is tree-connected and has precisely the required size: \[|U|=10=\left\lceil\frac{2\cdot55}{(9/10)13}\right\rceil.\] Consider the configuration with all \(100\) edges of that single \(K_{10,10}\) fibre open and every other edge closed. Its probability is \(p^{100}(1-p)^{|E|-100}>0\). Every \(u\in U\) is connected to its tree parent, and \[|K_u\cap B_1(u)|=11\ge\frac{\delta|B_1|}{2}=\frac{117}{20}.\] Thus every event \(A_u\) used in the proof holds. Nevertheless \(|K_o|=20<55\). The claimed deterministic implication is false. This does not disprove the final probability inequality in Lemma 2.7.

Repair. Put \(R=\lfloor r\rfloor\ge1\) and use a maximal net with distances strictly greater than \(2R\). Then the closed \(R\)-balls are disjoint and the \(2R\)-balls cover \(V\). The auxiliary graph using distance at most \(5R\) is connected: neighboring vertices can be assigned centers at distance at most \(4R+1\le5R\). The volume and Harris estimates, including the five-link bound, now work with the same qualitative constants. The support computation constructs the witness explicitly, rather than merely noting that two balls may overlap.

## P3. Lemma 2.8 needs an integer threshold or a ceiling

Location. Lemma 2.8, p. 14, and its application with \(n=c|B_r|\log|V|\), p. 15.

The stated quantifier is for all \(n\ge0\). Taken literally for real \(n\), the bound \(\operatorname{Var}_p|X|\le n^2\mathbb E_p|X|\) is false. Take \(G=C_3\), \(p=1/100\), and \(n=6/5\). Here \(X\) is the set of nonisolated vertices. Exact enumeration of the eight configurations gives \[\mathbb E|X|=6p-3p^2=\frac{597}{10000},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}|X|=12p-33p^2+30p^3-9p^4
 =\frac{11672991}{10^8}.\] But \[n^2\mathbb E|X|=\frac{21492}{250000}
 <\frac{11672991}{10^8}.\] The exploration may reveal \(\lceil n\rceil\) vertices, so the last bound \(|A_i|\le n\) is the failing step.

For integer \(n\) the stated proof gives the claimed bound. For real \(n\), replace \(n^2\) by \(\lceil n\rceil^2\). This explicitly resolves the ambiguity even if an integer convention was intended. In Proposition 2.6 use an integer threshold or the ceiling bound and absorb the bounded rounding error into the constant, treating the finitely many small sizes separately. The real threshold in that application is why the convention should not remain implicit.

## P4. The first implication in Proposition 2.9 uses Corollary 2.3 outside its domain

Location. Proof of \(3\Rightarrow2\), pp. 15–16, compared with Corollary 2.3, p. 11.

The substitution \(\lambda=n/\log|V|\) is asserted for every \(n\ge1\), although the corollary requires \(\lambda\ge1\). It fails this hypothesis whenever \(n<\log|V|\). The excluded range grows with the graph, so a graph-independent constant cannot be justified just by calling it a finite range of \(n\).

A direct application of Lemma 2.2 repairs the proof. Write \(p_0=(1-\varepsilon/2)p_c(H)\) and use the chosen radius \(r\) with \(\mathbb P_{p_0}(|K_o|\ge r)\le\varepsilon/4\). For \(0<\varepsilon<1\), set \[h=\frac1r\log\frac1{1-\varepsilon/4}.\] The same union bound as in Corollary 2.3 gives \(\mu_{p_0,h}\le\varepsilon/2\). Hence, for every \(n\ge1\), \[\mathbb P_{(1-\varepsilon/2)p_0}(|K_o|\ge n)
 \le(1-\varepsilon/2)^{-1}e^{-hn}.\] Because \((1-\varepsilon/2)^2\ge1-\varepsilon\), monotonicity yields the desired estimate at \((1-\varepsilon)p_c(H)\) with a uniform constant. The quantitative assertion is therefore locally repairable.

## P5. The radius in the last implication of Proposition 2.9 is too large for the displayed error estimate

Location. Bottom of p. 16 and top of p. 17, Case 2.

The proof has error \(Cr d^{r+1}/\sqrt n\) and chooses \[r=\log_d\left(\frac{\delta^2\sqrt n}{2Cd^2}\right).\] Substitution gives exactly \(r\delta^2/(2d)\), not a quantity bounded by \(\delta^2/2\) for all large \(n\). It exceeds that target once \(r>d\). For example, \(d=4\), \(C=1\), \(\delta=1/2\), and \(n=2^{46}\) give \(r=8\), error \(1/4\), and target \(1/8\).

Replace \(r\) by one half of the displayed logarithm, rounded down if needed. With \(A=\delta^2\sqrt n/(2Cd^2)\) and \(r\le\frac12\log_d A\), the inequality \(r\le d^r\) gives \[\frac{Cr d^{r+1}}{\sqrt n}
 \le\frac{Cd^{2r+1}}{\sqrt n}
 \le\frac{\delta^2}{2d}\le\frac{\delta^2}{2}.\] The repaired radius still diverges. The numerical example checks the algebraic implication; it is not claimed to be an actual graph in the contradiction case, or a counterexample to Proposition 2.9.

## P6. Proposition 2.1 cannot allow arbitrary positive sprinkling size

Location. Proposition 2.1, p. 11, and the initial parameter reduction in its proof.

The conclusion is a connection probability at least \(\eta^2/20\) for every \(\eta>0\). For \(\eta=5\) it exceeds one. The premise is not vacuous: for any proposed \(c=c(2,5)>0\), choose \(\lambda=\exp(1/c+1/c^2)\), so the displayed ball radius \(c\log\lambda-1/c\) equals one. Take \(G=C_N\), \(p=1-N^{-2}\), and \(N\) sufficiently large that \[\lambda\log N\le N,\qquad
 (cN^c)^{-1}\le\frac12,\qquad N\ge3.\] All edges are open with probability at least \(1-N(1-p)=1-1/N\ge1/2\) by a union bound, giving the premise. Under the paper’s clamping convention \(p+\eta\) means probability one, while the required lower bound is \(25/20>1\).

Restrict \(\eta\) to the small range actually used, for example \(0<\eta\le1/2\), or cap the asserted lower bound by the value obtained from a fixed small sprinkling. Merely saying that one may assume \(\eta\le1/2\) does not preserve the printed right-hand side for larger \(\eta\).

## P7. Proposition 3.1 has an undefined expression on allowed finite graphs

Location. Definition of \(\gamma,\gamma^+\) and statement, pp. 17–18; proof’s choice of \(\alpha\), p. 18.

Here \[\gamma=\operatorname{diam}(G)d_{\mathrm{GH}}\bigl((\pi/\operatorname{diam}G)G,S^1\bigr),\qquad
 \gamma^+=\exp((\log\gamma)^9),\] and the conclusion uses \(\exp(-\sqrt{\log\log\gamma^+})\). For \(G=C_{2m}\), embed its diameter-normalized vertices as equally spaced points on the unit circle with its intrinsic metric. This is isometric, with Hausdorff distance at most \(\pi/(2m)\). Since \(\operatorname{diam}G=m\), one has \(0<\gamma\le\pi/2<e\). If \(\gamma\le1\), \(\log\log\gamma^+\) is undefined; if \(1<\gamma<e\), it is negative, so its square root is not real. In addition, the coloring process is defined only at scales at least three.

The proposition and its deduction should explicitly handle small \(\gamma\) separately, or restrict \(\gamma\) to a sufficiently large domain including the relevant starting scale. Simply flooring \(\gamma\) at three does not by itself prove the new quantitative lower bound. The main application in Section 6 has \(\gamma>\exp((\log\operatorname{diam}G)^{1/9})\) and hence eventually lies in the large domain. This explains the bounded effect on that application. Also the asserted positive \(\alpha\) solving \(\phi(1/\alpha)=1-\varepsilon\) exists only when \(\varepsilon<1/2\); state that harmless small-\(\varepsilon\) reduction explicitly.

## P8. Individual bounded cycles cannot converge to a bi-infinite geodesic

Location. First paragraph under Cycles, p. 28.

The text describes a sequence of individual cycles, each of extrinsic diameter at most \(\delta(G)\), converging pointwise to a bi-infinite geodesic, and calls this equivalent to one-endedness. On the one-ended graph \(\mathbb Z^2\), unit plaquettes generate the finite cycle space and \(\delta(G)=2\). Choose two edges of the horizontal axis farther apart than two. Pointwise convergence would put both in every sufficiently late cycle, contradicting that diameter bound.

The intended statement concerns finite sums of bounded generators. For example, boundaries of the rectangles \([-N,N]\times[0,N]\) converge pointwise to the horizontal axis. Each is a sum of unit plaquettes, but its own diameter is unbounded. The actual proof of Lemma 3.17, p. 45, uses a finite cycle-space decomposition and remains valid; it does not require the false sentence. Replace that sentence with the correct formulation or omit it.

## P9. The density quantiles require a convention for certain events

Location. Definition of \(p_c(\alpha,\delta)\), p. 33.

The text asserts a unique \(p_c(\alpha,\delta)\in(0,1)\) satisfying \(\mathbb P_p(\|K_1\|\ge\alpha)=\delta\) for every \(\alpha,\delta\in(0,1)\). If \(\alpha\le1/|V|\), the event is certain for every configuration. On \(C_3\), take \(\alpha=1/4\), \(\delta=1/2\): no such parameter exists.

Use the generalized infimum over \(p\in[0,1]\), as in the retained reference’s Definition 3.1, and write the sharp-density condition multiplicatively, \[p_c(\alpha,1-\delta)\le e^\delta p_c(\alpha,\delta),\] to avoid an undefined \(0/0\) quotient when both thresholds are zero. Alternatively, restrict the equality definition to the nontrivial range \(\alpha>1/|V|\). The proof of Proposition 4.1 uses densities well above this microscopic range for large \(|V|\), so this is a definition and endpoint correction, not a refutation of its conclusion.

# Proof dependencies, counterevidence, and coverage

The review distinguishes an incorrect intermediate assertion from an incorrect main result. The following checks provide that distinction.

#### The four-step conclusion.

With \(\delta_N=(\log N)^{-1/20}\), the second-largest-cluster bound in Proposition 4.1 can be made at most \(\delta_N^2\) with failure probability at most \(\delta_N^4\): choose the tail parameter of order \(\delta_N^6(\log N)^{1/2}\), which diverges. Indeed the threshold becomes of order \(\delta_N^2\) and the failure bound of order \((\log N)^{-1/4}\le\delta_N^4\). The final coupling hypotheses \(\delta_N^2<\delta_N/2\) and \(\delta_N^4<\delta_N^2\) hold eventually. Thus these logarithmic exponents do not supply an additional contradiction. The obstruction in P1 lies earlier in the finite-graph induction.

#### The geometric constant is not refuted.

The conversion from the rough cycle parametrization on p. 31 can be made precise without changing the final constant \(200\). The parametrization has distortion at most \(4n\) and image \(10n\)-dense, giving a correspondence of distortion at most \(24n\), hence \(d_{\mathrm{GH}}(G,\mathbb Z_l)\le12n\). Retaining the paper’s bound \(|D-l/2|\le20n\), with \(D=\operatorname{diam}G\), the identity correspondence between differently rescaled copies of \(G\) yields \[d_{\mathrm{GH}}((\pi/D)G,(2\pi/l)G)\le20\pi n/l.\] Together with the isometric discretization of the circle, the total is at most \(46\pi n/l\). If \(D\ge40n\), then \(l\ge2(D-20n)\ge D\), and \(46\pi<200\). If \(D<40n\), the trivial bound \(d_{\mathrm{GH}}\le\pi/2\) suffices. This supplies a precise constant calculation rather than reporting the conclusion as false.

#### Ball matching in the appendix.

On p. 44, the text states matching of \(50n\)-balls but applies Lemma 3.16 at \(m_2\le3m\), requiring a larger ball. The relation plateau in its own definition of the exceptional set can supply it: for \(s=m/k\), excluded scales prevent a relation change at any dyadic \(a\) from \(s/2\) through \(m\), and the \(i+10\) buffer extends equality to at least \(512m\). The quotient therefore matches balls of radius at least \(256m-1\), exceeding \(150m\) in the stipulated large-scale range. This stronger matching should be written out. The missing scale factor in the tube thickness on p. 45 and the reversed negative interval on p. 31 are local notation corrections. They are not counted as additional independent mathematical findings.

#### Primary references actually inspected.

The retained version of Easo–Hutchcroft’s locality paper is arXiv:2310.10983v1 [EHloc]. Reading covered the cluster-repulsion and two-ghost statements, the complete ghost-field snowballing proof in Section 4.1, the structure statements and proof of Proposition 5.3, the random-walk estimates and proof of Proposition 5.4, and the complete Section 6 induction estimates. These units were checked for finite-volume applicability and use of infinity. The early random-walk estimates explicitly allow finite graphs; later heat-kernel statements need the finite-scale replacement described in the target’s appendix. The bounded growth regime and the local uniqueness hypotheses remain essential.

This is not a claim that every line of that older preprint is literally correct. The entropy estimate needs a radius of order \(n\log[t\operatorname{Gr}(n)]\), not a radius missing the factor \(n\); the path-selection argument must greedily exclude conflicts with previously selected paths; and the conditional sprinkling probability uses \((p_2-p_1)/(1-p_1)\), not a uniform unnormalized increment as \(p_1\uparrow1\). A ghost intensity required to be at least \(\operatorname{Gr}(b)^{-1}\) can be chosen as \(\max\{\operatorname{Gr}(b)^{-1},n^{-1/15}\}\), absorbing fixed constants after the lower bound on \(\operatorname{Gr}(b)\). These local readings preserve the intended estimates; they do not excuse the separate finite-volume error P1.

In arXiv:2112.12778v2 [EHgiant], the complete Section 3 sandcastles argument, including its concentration step and sharp-density estimate, was read. In the target, starting with a point-to-point bound replaces the much worse general lower bound denoted by \(\tau(\varepsilon)\) there; its later uses are quantitative in precisely that bound. In arXiv:2208.09501v2 [Eas], Lemma 8 and its complete coupling proof were read, including the conditional product law outside the exploration and the failure event. They support the target’s Lemma 5.2 with the stated hypotheses.

The additionally retrieved Vanneuville article [Van] was read in full, all twelve pages. It is the 2025 published version, whose Theorem 1.2 has the ghost-field inequality used as Lemma 2.2 in the target; it is not silently identified with the target’s cited earlier version or theorem numbering. Its exploration lemma is formulated on a finite graph. Applying that argument to the whole finite transitive graph, with the magnetization bound supplied by transitivity, establishes the needed finite analogue directly. No limiting infinite-volume step is required for that analogue.
