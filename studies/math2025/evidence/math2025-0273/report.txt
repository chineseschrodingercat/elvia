Abstract: An admissible identical-curve construction contradicts the final assertion of Lemma 2.10 at a fixed endpoint. Changing the selector on a null singleton leaves the permuton unchanged but changes the asserted support coordinate. The supported impact is Minor; an almost-everywhere replacement is verified. The deterministic compactness argument for sublinear longest increasing subsequences withstands inspection. The endpoint example does not refute the core LIS theorem or the independent-SLE re-rooting distributional law.

# The confirmed endpoint concern

Equation reference: eq:def. Section 1.2 allows \(\gamma\in(0,2)\), \(\kappa_1,\kappa_2>4\) and an arbitrary coupling of two whole-plane space-filling SLE curves \(\eta_1,\eta_2\), sampled independently of a unit-area LQG sphere field \(h\). They are then parametrized by \(\mu_h\)-mass on \([0,1]\). A Lebesgue-measurable selector satisfies \[\eta_1(t)=\eta_2(\psi(t))\quad(t\in[0,1]),\qquad
 \pi(B)=\int_0^1\mathbf 1_B(t,\psi(t))\,dt. \] These are (1.1)–(1.2), PDF p. 4. No endpoint normalization of \(\psi\) is imposed.

Problem 1 (Minor: a fixed-endpoint assertion is false).  The last assertion of Lemma 2.10, PDF p. 24 (printed 4916), says that for every admissible selector and each fixed \(t\in[0,1]\), almost surely \(\psi(t)\) is the unique \(s\) with \((t,s)\in\operatorname{supp}\pi\). It fails at \(t=0\) under the permitted identical-curve coupling.

Complete admissible counterexample. Take \(\gamma=1\), \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2=8\). Sample \(h\) and a whole-plane space-filling \(\mathrm{SLE}_8\) curve \(\eta\) independently and put \(\eta_1=\eta_2=\eta\). Both curve marginals have the required law, the pair is independent of \(h\), and identical coupling is expressly allowed. Their common area parametrization gives \(\eta(0)=\eta(1)=\infty\); the closed-loop endpoint convention also appears in Definition 5.1, p. 37.

Define the Borel function \[\psi(t)=\begin{cases}1,&t=0,\\t,&0<t\leq1.\end{cases}\] At zero, [eq:def] (#eq:def) holds because both endpoints map to infinity; elsewhere it holds because the curves coincide. Thus the parameter ranges, curve laws, independence from \(h\), allowed coupling, parametrization and selector requirements all hold.

For every Borel \(B\), \[\pi(B)=\int_0^1\mathbf 1_B(t,t)\,dt,\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}\pi=\{(u,u):0\leq u\leq1\}.\] The integral is unchanged by a singleton modification. Every relative neighbourhood of a diagonal point has positive diagonal Lebesgue mass, whereas every off-diagonal point has a disjoint neighbourhood. Hence the support fibre at \(t=0\) is \(\{0\}\). The lemma identifies it with \(\psi(0)=1\), but \((0,1)\) is not in the support. This contradiction holds on every realization where the specified objects are defined. ◻

## Source step, counterevidence and impact

The final proof paragraph of Lemma 2.10 invokes property (vi) of Section 2.4 (PDF p. 21). That hitting property concerns each fixed \(z\in\mathbb C\), and does not include infinity, attained at both interval endpoints. The example needs no claim about independence at deterministic interior times.

The first conditional assertion of Lemma 2.10 survives: it assumes that \(\eta_2\) hits \(\eta_1(t)\) only once, a premise that fails at the chosen endpoint. Lemma 2.8’s selector independence is preserved exactly. The discussion above Lemma 2.10 already notes diagonal support for identical curves and selector-dependent extra graph points.

Theorem 1.13 has stricter coupling alternatives (p. 9); the example does not refute its LIS conclusion. Theorem 1.22 requires independent SLE curves and uses a support coordinate for its shift. The example does not meet that independence hypothesis. Its footnote 3 (p. 14) and proof (p. 40) nevertheless need care when invoking unique hitting at endpoints. Shifts by \(0\) and \(1\) agree modulo one; no failure of the re-rooting distributional identity is established. Minor reflects this demonstrated local damage, not merely the ease of repair.

## Verified repair boundary

Keep the conditional unique-hit assertion. A supported further statement is: almost surely there is a Lebesgue-null set \(N\) such that for every admissible selector and every \(t\notin N\), \(\psi(t)\) is the unique support coordinate over \(t\). The proof of Lemma 2.8 (p. 23) supplies the null set of times at which \(\eta_1(t)\) is a multiple point of \(\eta_2\). Outside it, the first assertion of Lemma 2.10 applies to all selectors. An independently sampled uniform time avoids \(N\) almost surely.

This verifies only the almost-everywhere/uniform-time replacement. Deleting the endpoints alone does not prove a replacement at every deterministic interior time under arbitrary coupling. No such stronger repair or author adoption is claimed.

# Checks of the core LIS route

Proposition 3.2 (pp. 25–26) asserts that \(\pi^{\sigma_n}\Rightarrow\pi\), \(|\sigma_n|\to\infty\), and zero \(\pi\)-mass for every monotone set imply \(\operatorname{LIS}(\sigma_n)/|\sigma_n|\to0\).

An independent compactness check is as follows. If increasing subsequences occupy a fixed fraction \(c>0\), let \(A_n\) be the union of their closed permutation cells. They are nonempty compact sets with \(\pi^{\sigma_n}(A_n)\geq c\). Pass to a Hausdorff limit \(A\) and a weak limit \(\nu\) of \(\nu_n=\pi^{\sigma_n}|_{A_n}\). For each nonnegative continuous \(f\), \(\int f\,d\nu\leq\int f\,d\pi\), so \(\nu\leq\pi\). Testing \(1\) gives \(\nu([0,1]^2)\geq c\); Hausdorff convergence gives \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\subseteq A\). Thus \(\pi(A)\geq c\).

The two-dimensional cells need not themselves be monotone. Their diameters vanish, however. Relative to a selected cell’s upper-right corner, earlier selected cells lie southwest and later cells lie northeast. The order relation is closed under limits, so \(A\) is monotone, contradicting the premise. Only a closed monotone limit is needed. Actual image inspection confirms closure bars on both neighbourhoods in (3.3): the displayed Portmanteau inequality is valid.

The scale-invariant positive mass ratio in Lemma 3.4 yields a positive limsup without independence between scales. If every event \(\{R_{r_n}\geq c\}\) has probability at least \(p\), its limsup event has probability at least \(p\); letting \(p\uparrow1\) gives the assertion. The SLE geometric input giving positive interior and the LQG absolute-continuity input remain external dependencies of this check.

For Proposition 3.5 (p. 30), positive limsup density of the disjoint set \(Q_z\) gives a strict deficit in the density of \(X\) along a sequence: \[\liminf_{r\downarrow0}
 \frac{\mu_h(B_r(z)\cap X)}{\mu_h(B_r(z))}<1.\] This contradicts the full density limit \(1\). The displayed (3.15) uses a stronger limsup formulation. The sufficient liminf calculation validates the intended contradiction; this is a proof-reading clarification, not another confirmed defect in the main conclusion.

The simultaneous statement for every monotone set in Proposition 3.6 is needed, including sets depending on the realized permuton. A subsequence/Skorokhod argument then transfers Proposition 3.2 to the convergence-in-law setting and yields convergence in probability of normalized LIS. It does not give almost-sure convergence under an arbitrary original coupling. The theorem’s coupling restriction remains essential.
