Abstract: The reviewed paper is by Keshav Aggarwal, Wing Hong Leung and Ritabrata Munshi, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1667. An elementary infinite family contradicts the generic square-free-modulus bound in Lemma B.1, even with both coefficient parameters invertible modulo the modulus. Its local degeneracy at one prime is not detected by the stated congruences modulo the entire modulus. This estimate enters the main Cauchy–Schwarz iteration. The supported assessment is Major for this invalid core proof input; the final subconvexity theorem is not disproved here. The same claim and its use remain in the separately checked arXiv v3. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Supporting checks

Equation reference: eq:moment. The main theorem fixes a Hecke–Maass cusp form \(\pi\) for \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb Z)\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(t^{1/2}<M<t^{1-\varepsilon}\), Theorem 1.1, printed p. 4800, claims \[
 \int_{t-M}^{t+M}|L(1/2+iv,\pi)|^2\,dv
 \ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}t^\varepsilon
 \left(\frac{t^{9/4}}{M^{3/2}}+
 \frac{M^3}{t^{21/20}}+M^{7/4}t^{3/40}
 +M^{15/14}t^{15/28}\right).\] Together with the short-interval pointwise reduction, \(M=t^{2/3}\) gives the claimed \(5/8+\varepsilon\) pointwise exponent in Theorem 1.2, printed p. 4801. Direct exponent checking gives \(5/4,19/20,149/120,5/4\) for the four second-moment terms. This final substitution is consistent; the concern is an earlier uniform arithmetic estimate.

Equation reference: eq:C. Put \(e_c(u)=\exp(2\pi i u/c)\) and use the unnormalized Kloosterman sum \[S(A,B;c)=\sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z)^\times}
             e_c(Ax+B\bar x),\] where a bar denotes a multiplicative inverse modulo the current modulus. Appendix B.1, printed pp. 4861–4862 (PDF pp. 65–66), defines \[\begin{aligned}

 \mathcal C(a,b_1,b_2,b_3,c,q_1,q_2)
 ={}&\sum_{\gamma\bmod c}^{*} e_c(a\gamma)\nonumber\\
 &{}\times S(b_1-\bar q_1b_2+\bar\gamma b_2,\,
                  b_3(\gamma-q_1);c)\nonumber\\
 &{}\times S(b_1-\bar q_2b_2+\bar\gamma b_2,\,
                  b_3(\gamma-q_2);c).\end{aligned}\] The inverses on \(q_j\), as well as on \(\gamma\), are part of the original formula and were checked on the actual PDF.

Equation reference: eq:exception. Problem 1 (Partial prime-factor degeneracy is omitted).  Lemma B.1 assumes integer parameters, square-free \(c\), \((q_1q_2,c)=1\), and \(c\nmid b_2,b_3\). It claims \(\mathcal C\ll c^{2+\varepsilon}\) if at least one of \[
 q_1=q_2,\quad
 b_1\pm b_2(\bar q_1-\bar q_2)=0,\quad
 b_1-\bar q_1b_2=0,\quad b_1-\bar q_2b_2=0
 \pmod c\] holds, and otherwise claims \(\mathcal C\ll c^{3/2+\varepsilon}\). The latter estimate is false uniformly in the stated parameters.

## A complete family satisfying the hypotheses

For any prime \(p>7\), write \[S_p(z)=S(1,z;p),\qquad
 z_p=\min\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\le z<p} S_p(z)^2.\] This specifies \(z_p\) exactly: the least residue attaining the minimum of a finite collection of real algebraic numbers. Indeed, \(S(A,B;p)\) is real, since complex conjugation followed by \(x\mapsto-x\) leaves the sum unchanged. Let \(d_p=49z_p\pmod p\); this is nonzero.

Equation reference: eq:parameters. Set \(c=7p\), \(a=0\), and choose the unique residues modulo \(c\) specified by the Chinese remainder theorem: \[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \bmod 7 & \bmod p\\ \hline
 q_1 &1&1\\
 q_2 &2&1\\
 b_1 &2p&1\\
 b_2 &p&1\\
 b_3 &p&d_p
\end{array}\] All entries are interpreted in the indicated residue field. The modulus is square-free; \(q_1,q_2,b_2,b_3\) are units modulo \(7p\). Thus the example satisfies even the stronger condition \((b_2b_3,c)=1\), not merely \(c\nmid b_2,b_3\).

Equation reference: eq:nonexceptions. None of the exceptions [eq:exception] (#eq:exception) holds. It suffices to examine them modulo 7. There \(\bar q_1=1\), \(\bar q_2=4\), and their respective left sides are \[
 -1,\qquad -p,\quad 5p,\qquad p,\qquad -2p .\] All five residues are nonzero because \(p>7\) is prime. There are no restrictions on \(a\) excluding zero. In particular, every original hypothesis and the claimed otherwise branch have been verified.

## Exact independent evaluation

Equation reference: eq:factor, eq:D. For coprime moduli \(m,n\), the elementary factorization \[S(A,B;mn)=
 S(A\bar n,B\bar n;m)\,S(A\bar m,B\bar m;n)\] follows by applying CRT to the summation variable and to the additive character. Applying it to [eq:C] (#eq:C), the coefficient choices [eq:parameters] (#eq:parameters) give \[
 \mathcal C(0,b_1,b_2,b_3,7p,q_1,q_2)=K D_p,
 \qquad K=\mathcal C(0,2,1,1,7,1,2),\] where, with \(k=7^{-1}\pmod p\), \[D_p=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
         S(k\bar\gamma,kd_p(\gamma-1);p)^2.\] Because the first Kloosterman argument is nonzero, a change of variable gives \[S(k\bar\gamma,kd_p(\gamma-1);p)
       =S_p\bigl(k^2d_p(1-\bar\gamma)\bigr)
       =S_p\bigl(z_p(1-\bar\gamma)\bigr).\] As \(\gamma\) runs through \(\mathbb F_p^\times\), its last argument runs bijectively through \(\mathbb F_p\setminus\{z_p\}\). Consequently \[
 D_p=\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p}S_p(z)^2-S_p(z_p)^2
     =p(p-1)-S_p(z_p)^2 .\] For completeness, the orthogonality identity used here has the following direct proof: \[\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p} S_p(z)^2
 &=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb F_p^\times}e_p(x+y)
                 \sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p}e_p\bigl(z(x^{-1}+y^{-1})\bigr)\\
 &=p\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p^\times}e_p(x-x)=p(p-1).\end{aligned}\] Also \(S_p(0)=-1\). By the definition of \(z_p\), \[S_p(z_p)^2\le
 \frac{p(p-1)-1}{p-1}
 =p-\frac1{p-1}<p,
 \qquad D_p>p^2-2p.\] Thus the lower bound does not require a numerical approximation or an appeal to a deep bound for Kloosterman sums.

Equation reference: eq:hist. It remains to check that the fixed factor \(K\) is nonzero. Expanding its two sums expresses it as a sum of \(6^3=216\) seventh roots of unity, each with integer multiplicity. If such a sum vanished, irreducibility of \(\Phi_7(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^6\) would force all seven multiplicities to be equal. This is impossible since \(7\nmid216\). Independently enumerating the exponents gives, in order \(0,\ldots,6\), the exact multiplicities \[
 (34,28,29,34,34,29,28).\] Reduction modulo \(\Phi_7\) gives the nonzero polynomial \(6+X^2+6X^3+6X^4+X^5\). (The standard irreducibility here also follows immediately by applying Eisenstein at 7 to \(\Phi_7(X+1)\).)

Combining [eq:factor] (#eq:factor)–[eq:hist] (#eq:hist) yields \[|\mathcal C|\ge |K|(p^2-2p).\] For every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), \[\frac{|\mathcal C|}{(7p)^{3/2+\varepsilon}}
 \ge
 \frac{|K|}{7^{3/2+\varepsilon}}
 p^{1/2-\varepsilon}(1-2/p)\longrightarrow\infty .\] This disproves the uniform big-\(O\) assertion itself, rather than comparing one computed value with an unspecified implied constant.

## Finite computation and alternative readings

The primary reviewer independently wrote and ran `independent_check.py`; it is not author code. The modulus-7 histogram, inverses, CRT residues and hypothesis checks are exact integer calculations. Direct complex evaluations at \(p=11,29,101,503,1009\) are explicitly floating diagnostics. For \(p=11,29,101\), direct sums modulo \(7p\) agreed with the CRT product to within \(2.4\cdot10^{-11}\) in the computed arithmetic. At \(p=101\), one diagnostic parameter choice is \[(a,b_1,b_2,b_3,c,q_1,q_2)=(0,405,304,255,707,1,506),\] with \(z=32\). The diagnostic value of \(\mathcal C\) is approximately \(-53092.1320\). The infinite contradiction is the exact argument above; finite floating results do not prove it.

The global congruences in the lemma cannot be reinterpreted as nondegeneracy at every prime without changing the statement. Our \(q_1,q_2\) agree modulo \(p\) but differ modulo 7. The coefficients \(b_1-\bar q_jb_2\) likewise vanish at \(p\) without vanishing modulo \(7p\). Restricting \(c\) to primes would exclude the family, but the paper explicitly uses square-free composite moduli. Strengthening the divisibility assumptions to unit conditions does not exclude this family.

The same definition, inverse notation and global exceptions appear in v3 Lemma B.1, p. 51. Its Lemma B.2 on p. 52 again uses this base case. The v3 revision notice therefore does not repair the specific claim demonstrated here. This is a targeted comparison of the affected passages, not a statement that the two complete versions are identical.

# Core dependence, severity and repair boundary

The dependency is explicit in the publisher source. In Section 9.5.3, printed p. 4842 (PDF p. 46), equation (9.21) defines the relevant sum \(\mathcal D_3\) with square-free modulus \(n'h'_1\). The next displayed estimate, (9.22), invokes Lemma B.1 and uses precisely the two global branches. The bound feeds the small-frequency contribution and the iteration of Lemma 9.4, printed pp. 4846–4847 (PDF pp. 50–51). Appendix B.2, printed p. 4866 (PDF p. 70), identifies the iterated character sum with the family of Lemma B.2. The resulting character bound is inserted before (9.28), printed p. 4848 (PDF p. 52); Section 10 then derives [eq:moment] (#eq:moment). The corresponding direct use remains in v3 (56)–(57), p. 36.

This is a demonstrated false arithmetic estimate used as a core proof input, so the assessment is Major. No example of a Hecke–Maass form violating Theorem 1.1 or 1.2 is claimed. Nor has this review embedded the entire residue family into every dyadic size restriction of the original analytic sums. The precise supported consequence is that the stated uniform lemma, and its invocation as a uniform justification, are invalid; the current chain does not supply the advertised justification without further arithmetic analysis.

A repair would have to handle degeneracy prime by prime and propagate its cost through the actual summations and iterations. Even a valid generic prime-modulus estimate alone cannot justify the displayed composite-modulus branch. A factor depending on bad prime divisors may be necessary, but neither a sufficient replacement bound nor its effect on the final exponent has been proved in this review. Consequently no corrected theorem is proposed or marked verified.

The reading also considered fixed versus growing iteration length and the final optimization. Constants in Lemma 9.5 are allowed to depend on the iteration count. Choosing a sufficiently large count depending only on \(\varepsilon\) is a natural way to address the isolated \(99/2^\nu\) loss. This observation does not repair Problem Problem 1 (#prob:crt), and no separate confirmed finding is assigned to that bookkeeping point. Likewise, the checked exponent substitution at \(M=t^{2/3}\) does not validate the preceding arithmetic argument.
