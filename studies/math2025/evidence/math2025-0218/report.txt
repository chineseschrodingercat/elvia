Abstract: The 15 February 2024 author version of Kehle and Unger’s black-hole gluing paper is examined. A substantive gap occurs in the subsidiary interior-gluing argument: real characteristic pulses on a charged background generate complex transverse scalar derivatives, whereas the stated Borsuk–Ulam construction uses the dimension appropriate to a neutral real field. An exact covariant transport identity identifies the missing condition. Additional, locally repairable problems concern global seed-data existence, constrained electromagnetic jets, the printed gauge-group law, and several formula and parameter normalizations. The principal horizon-gluing construction uses the correct complex dimension and is not refuted by these observations. The finite-regularity, apparent-horizon, and trapped-surface quantifiers are retained throughout. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

The system is the spherically symmetric Einstein–Maxwell charged massless scalar field system, with fixed nonzero coupling \(\mathfrak e\), complex scalar \(\phi\), and electromagnetic gauge \(A_v=0\). The black-hole charge \(e\) and the dimensionless ratio \(\mathfrak q=e/M\) must be distinguished from \(\mathfrak e\). Theorem 2A concerns neutral Minkowski-to-Schwarzschild gluing; Theorem 2B concerns gluing to an RN event horizon; Theorems 2C and \(2\mathrm C'\) concern general RN spheres and ingoing gluing. Theorem 1 uses 2A and 2B. Corollaries 2 and 3 use the more general construction.

The quantifier is that for each finite \(k\) there is a corresponding \(C^k\) example. It is not the existence of one \(C^\infty\) example; Remarks 1.6 and 1.8 explicitly discuss this limitation. # The substantive proof gap

Problem 1 (Major: the interior-gluing parameter count).  Section 4.3, p. 35, Gluing 3, uses \(k+1\) real pulses as in (4.1), imposes a mass condition, and invokes the Borsuk–Ulam argument. At nonzero background charge \(Q_f\), this does not supply the dimension needed to eliminate \(k\) complex transverse scalar jets. The static source confirms this wording (`final.tex`, lines 1663 and 1671).

Equation reference: covariant, imag. Here is an exact obstruction to treating the transverse jets as real. Along the gluing cone put \(\Omega^2=1\) and let \(\phi\) be real. Maxwell’s equation then makes \(Q=Q_f\) constant along that cone. Define \[\psi=r\phi,\qquad Z=D_u\psi=\partial_u\psi+i\mathfrak eA_u\psi .\] Combining (2.13), p. 18, with \(\partial_v A_u=-Q_f/(2r^2)\), equation (2.9), gives \[
 \partial_v Z=
 \left(-\frac{m}{2r^2}+\frac{Q_f^2}{4r^3}\right)\phi
       -\frac{i\mathfrak eQ_f}{4r}\phi .\] Indeed, the derivative of the gauge-potential term contributes \(-i\mathfrak eQ_f\phi/(2r)\); together with the positive imaginary term in (2.13), its net coefficient is the negative one in [covariant] (#covariant). If the scalar and its transverse derivative vanish at the initial endpoint, and the scalar vanishes at both endpoints, then \[
 \operatorname{Im}\!\left[r(1)\partial_u\phi(1)\right]
   =-\frac{\mathfrak eQ_f}{4}
       \int_0^1\frac{\phi(v)}{r(v)}\,\mathrm dv .\] The same obstruction holds with the transport initialized backwards at the final RN sphere. A nonnegative nonzero real pulse makes the right-hand side nonzero. Thus reality of the prescribed scalar along one characteristic does not preserve reality of its transverse derivative. An allowed residual gauge transformation cannot turn a nonzero endpoint scalar derivative into zero: where \(\phi=0\), that derivative is multiplied by a nonzero scale and phase.

The real and imaginary conditions are not generically the same moment. Linearizing [covariant] (#covariant) on RN of modified mass \(M\) gives real and imaginary weights \[a(r)=\frac{-Mr+Q_f^2}{2r^3},\qquad
 b(r)=-\frac{\mathfrak eQ_f}{4r}.\] For \(M=Q_f=\mathfrak e=1\), their values at \(r=2,3\) have determinant \[\det\begin{pmatrix}a(2)&a(3)\\b(2)&b(3)\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{1}{1728}\ne0.\] Smooth pulses supported near these distinct radii therefore give independent linearized endpoint moments. This is a diagnostic of the missing real condition, not a claimed counterexample to the final nonlinear gluing theorem.

There is also a strict counterexample to the printed two-pulse procedure when \(k=1\). Choose \(Q_f=R_f=M_f=M>0\), an allowed case for \(0<\mathfrak r\leq1\), and nonnegative, nonzero \(\chi_1,\chi_2\) with separated supports in that order. Let \(\phi=\alpha_1\chi_1+\alpha_2\chi_2\). Assume the claimed construction retains \(r>0\) and \(r_u<0\). The endpoint conditions give \(r_v(1)=0\); Raychaudhuri implies \(r_v\geq0\) and \(r\leq M\). The mass identity gives \(\varpi_v\geq0\) and \(\varpi\leq\varpi(1)=M\). Define \[H=\frac{2m}{r}-\frac{M^2}{r^2}.\] Using (2.14), direct differentiation yields \[H_v=4r(-r_u)|\phi_v|^2+
       \frac{3M^2-2mr}{r^3}r_v,\qquad
 3M^2-2mr=4M^2-2\varpi r\geq2M^2.\] If both amplitudes are nonzero, \(H\) is strictly increasing between the supports because the second pulse forces \(r_v>0\) there. Set \[J_j=\int_0^1\frac{\chi_j}{r}\,\mathrm dv>0,\qquad
 K_j=\int_0^1\frac{H\chi_j}{r}\,\mathrm dv .\] Then \(K_1/J_1<K_2/J_2\). Equation [covariant] (#covariant), with zero initial scalar jet, gives \[r(1)\phi_u(1)
   =-\tfrac14(\alpha_1K_1+\alpha_2K_2)
     -\frac{i\mathfrak eM}{4}(\alpha_1J_1+\alpha_2J_2).\] Vanishing imaginary part forces \(\alpha_1J_1=-\alpha_2J_2\); strict separation of the weighted averages then makes the real part nonzero. If only one amplitude is nonzero, the imaginary part already cannot vanish. The zero amplitude vector cannot supply the prescribed positive mass increment. Thus these allowed two nonnegative profiles admit no nonzero pulse parameters satisfying the advertised \(C^1\) matching within the construction’s positive-radius, negative-\(r_u\) class. This refutes that stated procedure for an allowed profile choice, not the existence assertion of Theorem 2C with a different family of pulses.

With \(k+1\) real amplitudes and one mass constraint, the described parameter surface has dimension \(k\). The required target is \(\mathbb C^k\simeq\mathbb R^{2k}\). Theorem 3 does not guarantee a zero for an odd map \(S^k\to\mathbb R^{2k}\); the coordinate inclusion itself is a zero-free odd example. Section 4.2 correctly uses \(2k+1\) amplitudes and a \(2k\)-dimensional target, so this problem does not apply to its horizon-gluing argument.

A concrete repair is available in principle: use \(2k+1\) real pulses in Gluing 3, construct the mass level as an antipodally invariant radial graph over \(S^{2k}\), and use all real and imaginary endpoint jets. Real pulses still keep the tangential charge constant. The relevant scale also appears compatible with this repair: for amplitude \(\beta\), \[\Delta\varpi
  =\beta^2\int_0^1 2r^2(-r_u)
       \left|\partial_v\sum_j\widehat\alpha_j\chi_j\right|^2\,\mathrm dv
  \asymp \beta^2 M_f^3.\] Since \(M_f-M'\asymp M_f\), this gives \(\beta^2\asymp M_f^{-2}\). A corrected proof must establish the positive radial derivative, smooth even radial graph, uniform bounds, and oddness of the full complex jet map. These are repair requirements, not a completed replacement proof supplied here. The affected dependencies are 2C, \(2\mathrm C'\), and Corollaries 2–3; no counterexample to their existence conclusions, or to Theorem 1, is established.

# Local statements and formal data requiring correction

Problem 2 (Minor: unrestricted seed data need not exist on the whole interval).  Proposition 2.4, p. 21, asserts a globally regular \(A_u\) for every compatible seed, although the Raychaudhuri solution can reach zero. Its seed construction therefore needs a positive-radius restriction on the prescribed interval. Proposition 2.3 should make that restriction explicit; a classical interpretation of its rational constraint equations may already exclude zeros of their denominators.

Take \(k=1\), \(\mathfrak e=1\), \(v\in[0,\pi]\), \(\Omega^2=1\), and \[\phi(v)=e^{-iv},\qquad
 r(0)=1,\quad r_v(0)=0,\quad Q(0)=A_u(0)=0.\] The initial sphere jets can be completed using the constraints near \(v=0\): for example \(r_{vv}(0)=-1\), \(Q_v(0)=1\), \((A_u)_v(0)=0\), and \((A_u)_{vv}(0)=-1/2\). The paper’s convention is \(Q_v=\mathfrak er^2\operatorname{Im}(\phi\overline{\phi_v})\). The unique tangential solutions are \[r(v)=\cos v,\qquad Q(v)=\frac v2+\frac{\sin2v}{4}.\] At \(v=\pi/2\), \(r=0\) but \(Q=\pi/4\). Consequently \[\lim_{v\uparrow\pi/2}(\pi/2-v)^2(A_u)_v=-\frac{\pi}{8},\] so \(A_u\) cannot be \(C^2\) on the stated interval. This explicit seed counterexample refutes Proposition 2.4. It is not a separate counterexample to Proposition 2.3 when that proposition’s constraints are understood classically throughout the interval, nor to the small-pulse construction.

Require \(r>0\) and \(\Omega^2>0\) in Proposition 2.3, and restrict Proposition 2.4 to seeds whose Raychaudhuri solution remains strictly positive, or to the interval before its first zero. On a compact positive-radius interval the transport equations have the required regular coefficients. Also replace the lower integration limits \(0\) by \(v_1\) in the general statement. Lemma 4.4 and the preceding small-amplitude construction independently provide positive radius where the main argument needs it.

Problem 3 (Minor: RN data must retain their constrained Maxwell jets).  Definitions 3.4–3.5, pp. 24–25, prescribe nonzero charge and make all other components zero. Read literally, these are not elements of the sphere-data space \(\mathcal D_k\). Lemma 4.1, p. 26, also omits the lapse in its mass condition.

Definition 2.3(v) and (2.9) impose \[a_v^1=-\frac{q\omega}{2\varrho^2}.\] For the normalized charged RN sphere this is \(-e/(2\varrho^2)\ne0\). Higher dependent \(v\)-jets must likewise be obtained from the constraints. It is not possible to remove the nonzero electromagnetic field by a gauge choice. The definitions should specify the free components and fill the dependent components by the RN equations. The phrase in Lemma 4.2 that the charge vanishes to all orders must mean its positive-order derivatives; the charge itself is prescribed nonzero.

For Lemma 4.1, the invariant mass is \(\varrho(1+4\varrho_u^1\varrho_v^1/\omega)/2\), whereas the displayed hypothesis omits \(\omega\). Sphere data with \[\varrho=1,\quad \varrho_u^1=-\tfrac12,\quad
 \varrho_v^1=\tfrac12,\quad \omega=4,\quad q=\phi=0\] and zero remaining free jets satisfy the printed condition with \(M=0\), but have Hawking mass \(3/8\). Gauge transformations preserve that mass, so they cannot be equivalent to the asserted mass-zero sphere. Either require lapse-normalized input or restore the denominator. The actual applications on pp. 28 and 33 already use \(\omega=1\). These are exact formal inconsistencies with direct local repairs; they do not rule out gluing to actual RN data.

Problem 4 (Minor: the displayed gauge multiplication is not associative).  Definition 2.4, p. 20, prints the composition law \[((f_2,g_2),\chi_2)\cdot((f_1,g_1),\chi_1)
 =((f_2\circ f_1,g_2\circ g_1),\chi_2\circ f_1^{-1}+\chi_1).\] This law is incompatible with associativity for noncommuting coordinate changes.

Set \(f_1(u)=2u\), \(f_2(u)=u+u^3\), \(f_3(u)=u\), all \(g_i=\mathrm{id}\), \(\chi_1=\chi_2=0\), and \(\chi_3(u)=u\). These are allowed global increasing diffeomorphisms and smooth phases. The two associations give phases \[f_2^{-1}\circ f_1^{-1}(u)
    =\tfrac u2-\tfrac{u^3}{8}+O(u^5),\qquad
 f_1^{-1}\circ f_2^{-1}(u)
    =\tfrac u2-\tfrac{u^3}{2}+O(u^5),\] which differ. For the pullback transformations (2.15)–(2.19), a consistent left-action convention uses \((f_1\circ f_2,g_1\circ g_2)\) in this product and retains the printed phase expression. Alternatively, change the action convention consistently.

The example following (2.19) also needs the chain rule: \[\tau(\phi_u^1)
 =f'(0)e^{-i\mathfrak e\chi(0)}
      \bigl(\phi_u^1-i\mathfrak e\chi'(0)\phi\bigr).\] The printed example even fails to act identically when \(f=\mathrm{id}\) and \(\chi=0\). In (2.19), the transformed potential must retain its second argument \(g(v)\). The ordinary coordinate and electromagnetic transformations remain available; the correction concerns their formal packaging, not a new obstruction to the geometric construction.

Problem 5 (Minor: local parameter, formula, and threshold corrections).  Several displayed formulas disagree with equations or quantifiers used elsewhere in the same proof.

On p. 32, the first two denominators of (4.18) should contain \(r\), not \(r^2\), as in (2.5). Lemma 4.8 actually uses the correct identity (2.12), which supports treating this as a local formula error. On p. 34, the listed final radius is \(2M_f\), although the construction imposes \(r_+=(1+\sqrt{1-\mathfrak q^2})M_f\); replace that copied Schwarzschild value.

Theorem 2C allows every real \(\mathfrak q\). Its proof’s reduction \(R_f\leq3M_f\) on p. 34 has no admissible radius if \((1+\mathfrak r)\mathfrak q^2>6\). In that regime it cannot be justified by extending from \(3M_f\), which can have nonpositive Hawking mass. Replace \(3\) by \(C(\mathfrak q,\mathfrak r)\geq
\max\{3,(1+\mathfrak r)\mathfrak q^2/2\}\). Beyond this admissible radius the RN lapse is positive, and the constants are already permitted to depend on these parameters. On p. 35, (4.25) gives \[M_f-M'\geq\frac{\mathfrak r}{2+2\mathfrak r}M_f,\] not the subsequently printed equality. This lower bound, with \(M_f-M'\leq M_f\), suffices for the intended scale.

Corollary 1, pp. 37–38, invokes Theorem 2B at regularity \(k+1\) to allow the loss at the center. Its mass threshold should be chosen for that increased index, and above the strict threshold of 2B. An enlarged threshold repairs this existential result. The same bookkeeping should be used in its later corollaries. Finally, the horizon calculation on p. 41 simplifies to \[\frac{r_u}{4M_f^2}
       -\frac{3M_f^2r_u}{4M_f^4}
       =-\frac{r_u}{2M_f^2},\] not \(-2r_u/M_f^2\). Its positive sign for \(r_u<0\) is unchanged.

# Proof-chain checks and counterevidence

The charge-surface construction in Section 4.2 has the right topological dimension. Disjointly supported pulses give a positive tangential charge density. The small amplitude bootstrap yields \(r\geq r_+/2\); the charge level is a smooth even radial graph, and the transverse scalar map is odd into \(\mathbb C^k\). The large \(|\mathfrak e|M/|\mathfrak q|\) condition also supplies the negative \(r_u\) margin. The neutral case is separated rather than dividing by \(\mathfrak q=0\). These checks explain why Problem 1 is confined to the subsidiary interior-gluing proof.

Section 5 constructs a spacetime first and then selects a spacelike, one-ended initial hypersurface with a regular center. It does not identify a characteristic cone with Cauchy data. The global conclusion relies on the cited maximal-development and future-boundary results, notably Kommemi’s theorem [kommemi]. Its repository metadata and abstract were checked, but its full proof, and the external local-existence and center-regularity proofs, were not independently reconstructed here. Uniform local-existence control when the final interior sphere approaches \(r_+\) remains part of the repair and verification required for Corollary 2.

The earlier Schwarzschild portion is an apparent-horizon segment behind the final global event horizon. Treating it as the earlier global event horizon would change the claim. Similarly, the theorem excludes strictly trapped closed surfaces wholly contained in a certain neighborhood \(U\) of the event horizon. It does not exclude every trapped surface that merely intersects \(U\); Remark B.1 explicitly makes that distinction. Persistent trapped spheres farther inside are therefore not contradictory evidence.

Appendix A gives a local stationary metric satisfying the dominant energy condition, not an asymptotically flat EMCSF collapse solution. For its explicit choice, \[g=u^3\,\mathrm dt^2-2\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm du+(1-u)^2g_{\mathbb S^2},\] the displayed curvature calculation gives \[\theta=\frac{1-3u^2+4u^3}{(1-u)^2},\qquad
 \zeta=\frac{3u(2u-1)}{1-u}.\] Thus \(\theta(0)=1\), \(\zeta(0)=0\), and \(\theta>|\zeta|\) on a sufficiently small neighborhood, which is the stated energy-condition argument. The null-expansion comparison in Appendix B has the appropriate inequality direction. At a regular center, a spacelike surface cannot attain a local minimum of \(t-r\) or a local maximum of \(t+r\) there, supplying the briefly stated exclusion of a central contact point.

No independent counterexample was found to the central finite-\(k\) collapse construction after the local formal corrections. This is a bounded review conclusion, not a certificate of the complete external PDE and global causal theory.
