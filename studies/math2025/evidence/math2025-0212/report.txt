Abstract: The accepted manuscript of Gray and Kambites is examined through its Schützenberger-graph constructions and recursive-presentation arguments. The principal classification proofs can be followed within the dependencies stated in the paper. A precise boundary failure occurs in the idempotent specified for Theorem 5.3 when the finite set is empty: for a concrete two-element group its maximal subgroup is infinite, whereas the desired stabilizer has order two. This disproves the specified construction at that boundary, not the existential theorem. The nonempty-set construction and the principal applications survive. The supported finding is Minor; scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and dependency chain

For an inverse monoid \(M\), the maximal subgroup at an idempotent \(e\) is its group \(\mathcal H\)-class \(H_e\). The group at \(1\) is the unit group \(U(M)\). These are distinct roles in the paper. An \(E\)-unitary inverse monoid has only idempotents in the kernel of its maximal group image; the paper uses the corresponding embedding of each Schützenberger graph into that group’s Cayley graph.

Theorem 4.1, pp. 10–15, characterizes the unit groups of finitely presented special inverse monoids as finitely generated recursively presented groups. The construction uses an embedding \(H\le G\), where \(G\) is finitely presented, and introduces \(p_i,z,d\). The maximum group image \(G*F(p_0,\ldots,p_k,z)\) proves that the \(G\)-Cayley graphs at \(p\)-vertices are genuinely embedded, rather than merely morphic images. The subgroup represented by \(zwz^{-1}\), \(w\in B^*\), is \(H\). The quotient to \(N\), followed by the bicyclic monoid, restricts the possible unit factors. The auxiliary graph \(\Omega\) then excludes the unwanted \(p_iap_i^{-1}\) factors by the absence of the required incoming \(a\)-edge. Only a morphism \(S\Gamma(1)\to\Omega\) is needed for that exclusion; an unproved isomorphism is not used.

Equation reference: eq:M. Theorem 5.3, pp. 16–20, starts from a group presentation \(G=\langle A\mid R\rangle\), a subgroup \(H\), and words \(B\) generating \(H\) as a monoid. It defines \[\begin{aligned}
 M=\operatorname{Inv}\langle A,y,z\mid {}&
 R,\ aa^{-1}=a^{-1}a=1\ (a\in A), \notag\\
 &zbz^{-1}zb^{-1}z^{-1}=1,\quad
 yby^{-1}yb^{-1}y^{-1}=1\ (b\in B)\rangle .
 \end{aligned}\] The maximum group image is \(G*F(y,z)\). The fundamental-idempotent relations preserve that group image. Lemma 3.5 consequently transfers \(E\)-unitarity from \(G*\operatorname{Inv}\langle y,z\rangle\) to \(M\); this is not an assertion that arbitrary quotients preserve \(E\)-unitarity.

Equation reference: eq:e. For a finite set \(X\subseteq G\), the proof specifies \[
 e_X=\prod_{x\in X}xz^{-1}zy^{-1}yx^{-1}\] and claims \(H_{e_X}\cong\operatorname{Stab}_G(XH)\). For nonempty \(X\), the vertices with both an incoming \(y\)-edge and an incoming \(z\)-edge identify the distinguished \(G\)-component. Its marked set is \(XH\). Lemma 3.1 extends its automorphisms uniquely; conversely the marked component forces every automorphism to arise this way.

# A verified local finding

Problem 1 (The empty-set idempotent does not realize the claimed stabilizer).  The statement of Theorem 5.3 on p. 16 permits every finite \(X\), including the empty set. The specific assertion about [eq:e] (#eq:e) on p. 17 and the final automorphism identification on p. 20 fail at this boundary.

Take \[G=H=C_2=\langle a\mid a^2=1\rangle,\qquad B=\{a\},\qquad X=\varnothing .\] All are allowed inputs to [eq:M] (#eq:M): \(G\) is finitely presented, \(H\) is finitely generated, and \(\{a\}\) is a monoid generating set for \(C_2\). The empty product in [eq:e] (#eq:e) is \(e_X=1\), whereas \[XH=\varnothing,\qquad \operatorname{Stab}_G(XH)=G=C_2.\] In \(M\), \(a\) is a unit and \(a^{-1}=a\). Put \(u=yay^{-1}\). One defining relation in [eq:M] (#eq:M) is \[yay^{-1}ya^{-1}y^{-1}=1,\] so \(u^2=1\). Thus \(u\), and hence \(au\), is a unit of \(M\). Under the homomorphism to the maximum group image \(C_2*F(y,z)\), \[au\longmapsto ayay^{-1}.\] This is a cyclically reduced word of free-product syllable length four. For every positive integer \(n\), its \(n\)th power has syllable length \(4n\), and therefore is not the identity. Consequently \(au\) has infinite order in \(M\). In particular, \[|H_{e_X}|=|H_1|=|U(M)|=\infty
 \quad\hbox{but}\quad |\operatorname{Stab}_G(XH)|=2.\] This is an exact normal-form calculation, not a numerical experiment or a failure to locate a proof.

The logical cause is visible on pp. 18–20: preservation of an empty set of doubly marked vertices does not force preservation of a distinguished \(G\)-component. The extension direction alone supplies automorphisms from \(G\); it does not exclude the additional units just exhibited.

## Impact, counterevidence, and repair boundary

The example disproves the specified \(e_X\) construction, not the existential assertion that a suitable group \(\mathcal H\)-class exists somewhere in \(M\). Indeed, in this very example \(X=\{1\}\) in the nonempty construction gives a different idempotent realizing \(C_2\). This is decisive counterevidence to any stronger allegation that the whole theorem or the classification is false.

A verified restricted repair is to state and use the construction for nonempty finite \(X\). The nonempty marker restores the distinguished component argument. Theorem 5.4 uses \(\{1,t\}\); Corollary 5.8 can use \(\{1\}\); Corollary 5.12 uses a nonempty finite subgroup. Thus these applications do not require the failed empty-product case. If the original quantifier over all finite \(X\) is retained, a separate general argument realizing \(G=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\varnothing)\) in the same constructed monoid is still required. Such an argument has not been established here. The supported severity is Minor: a localized construction/proof boundary with a verified narrowing preserving the main applications. Author adoption and journal-final applicability are unknown.

# Additional checks and rejected apparent defects

The compressed prefix argument on p. 20 omits explicit mention of prefixes lying in \(G\). This need not be treated as a separate error: free-product normal forms provide the missing detail. Inside \(F=G*F(y,z)\), the relevant prefix monoid is \[P=\langle G,y,z,yHy^{-1},zHz^{-1}\rangle .\] For the homomorphism \(\phi:F\to\mathbb Z\), with \(\phi(G)=0\) and \(\phi(y)=\phi(z)=1\), its zero layer is \[P_0=L=G*(yHy^{-1})*(zHz^{-1}).\] If \(v\in G\) and \(vy^{-1}=xy^{-1}\ell\) for \(\ell\in L\), then \(\ell=yx^{-1}vy^{-1}\) lies in \(L\cap yGy^{-1}=yHy^{-1}\); hence \(v\in xH\). The alternative start \(xz^{-1}\) would require \(zx^{-1}vy^{-1}\in L\), excluded by reduced normal form. This explicitly accommodates the \(G\)-prefixes rather than incorrectly discarding them by the exponent map alone.

For Lemma 5.1, the two distinct cosets give an action into \(S_2\) with kernel \(K\cap tKt^{-1}\). If the cosets coincide, the stabilizer is directly \(K\), so no two-point action is needed. In Lemma 5.2 the HNN construction centralizing a recursively enumerable subgroup has an enumerable relation set. Britton’s lemma gives the intersection, and the stable-letter exponent excludes \(K\cap tKt\). The Higman embedding preserves both properties.

For Proposition 5.6, in \(F(a,b)\times\langle q\rangle\) the group \(U=\langle a,qb\rangle\) is the graph of the exponent homomorphism \(\operatorname{exp}_b\). Consequently \[\langle a,b\rangle\cap U=\ker(\operatorname{exp}_b)
 =\langle b^nab^{-n}:n\in\mathbb Z\rangle\] is free of infinite rank. This checks the claimed non-finite-generation mechanism without needing an undecidable membership computation. For Corollary 5.7, the equations \(vv^{-1}=e=v^{-1}v\) can be enumerated by positive derivations. Enumerating all resulting group relations supplies a recursive presentation; decidability is not asserted.

The parenthetical description of Higman’s universal group on p. 23 needs the recursive-presentation qualifier if phrased for finitely generated groups. The finite-group application only requires a finitely presented group containing every finite group. This scope clarification is recorded, without treating it as a second substantive problem. Questions 5.9–5.11 and 5.13 are explicitly questions, not unproved claims.
