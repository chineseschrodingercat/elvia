Title: Initial scientific review
The exact rank of sparse random graphs

Assignment. Authors. Margalit Glasgow, Matthew Kwan, Ashwin Sah and Mehtaab Sawhney. Exact source. arXiv:2303.05435v1, 9 March 2023, 17:27:44 UTC, 46 PDF pages. DOI: 10.4171/jems/1692 (https://doi.org/10.4171/jems/1692). The journal record was published on 2 September 2025; no equivalence of that record and this preprint is assumed.

# Assessment and scope

Major revision of the stated technical results. The robust random-walk theorem, a central tool introduced for the rank argument and for possible wider use, is false over its stated initial-condition range. A deterministic counterexample satisfies every hypothesis. Three further auxiliary statements omit necessary hypotheses. The principal sparse-graph rank conclusions have not been disproved: in particular, their random walk starts with a stronger negative margin that excludes the counterexample. This distinction determines the scope of the assessment.

All 46 pages were read as extracted text, including the appendix and references. Thirty-one source pages were also inspected as images. The review checked the theorem ranges, Karp–Sipser reductions, special-cycle kernel vectors, degree conditioning, rank-boosting hypotheses, random-walk application, stalk dependency chain and asymptotic distributions. Exact arithmetic and elementary linear algebra were independently computed in reviewer-written Python; no author code was run.

# Confirmed problems

Problem 1 (Major: Theorem 7.2 permits an insufficient initial margin).  Theorem 7.2, pp. 21–22, assumes \(X_N\le(1-\varepsilon)\delta N\), upward jumps at most \(C\), downward drift at good positive steps, and the prefix bound \[|R\cap\{0,\ldots,t\}|\le \delta t/(32C)\] with probability at least \(1-p\). It concludes \(\Pr(X_0>0)=O_{C,\delta,\varepsilon}(p)\).

Set \(C=\delta=1\), \(\varepsilon=1/100\), \(N=3200\), \(R=\{3101,\ldots,3199\}\), and \(X_N=3168\). Define the entire process deterministically, backwards, by \[X_t=X_{t+1}+1\quad(t\in R),\qquad
 X_t=X_{t+1}-1\quad(t\notin R).\] Take \(G_t\) deterministic and encoding \(X_t\) and membership in \(R\). Then W0 and W2 hold, W1 is equality, and W3 holds with probability one: every good positive step decreases by \(\delta\), and every other step belongs to \(R\). All \(X_t\) are positive. For \(t\le3100\) the prefix count is zero; for \(3101\le t\le3199\) it is \(t-3100\le t/32\); at \(t=3200\) it is \(99\le100\). Thus W4 also holds surely. Nevertheless \[X_0=3168-3200+2(99)=166>0.\] The same process satisfies the assumptions for every \(p>0\), so its probability one cannot be bounded by a fixed constant times \(p\) as \(p\downarrow0\). The family \(N=3200k\), \(|R|=100k-1\) gives \(X_0=168k-2\), excluding a large-\(N\) escape.

The proof on p. 22 reduces the effective downward drift to \(1/2\) when adding the debt process, but retains an initial bound sized for drift \(1\). This invalidates the appeal to Lemma 7.1. Theorem 7.2 is explicitly used in the proofs of Theorem 1.12(A1) and (B1), pp. 28–29 and 39–40. However, in (A1) the actual initial value is \(X_N\le N/8-N/4=-N/8\), and the bipartite application has a comparable negative margin. These facts are substantial counterevidence to any claim that the main rank formula fails.

A corrected theorem must either strengthen the initial bound to match the reduced drift or make the bad-step budget depend on \(\varepsilon\). Restricting the initial value to be nonpositive would cover the displayed applications. The deterministic budget and location of the failed parameter transfer are established here; a complete revised stochastic theorem and its rounding details still require proof.

Problem 2 (Minor: independence is absent from Lemma 3.3).  On p. 12, the bounded-difference hypothesis for \(Z=f(X_0,\ldots,X_n)\) is stated without independence or an appropriate martingale increment condition. Take sixteen variables all equal to a fair Bernoulli variable \(U\), and define \(f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{15}x_j\) on the whole Boolean cube. Changing one coordinate changes \(f\) by at most \(1\). Yet \(Z=16U\), \(\mathbb{E}Z=8\), and, at \(t=8\), \[\Pr(|Z-\mathbb{E}Z|\ge8)=1>2e^{-2}.\] This directly contradicts the printed bound. Add independence, or state a sufficient conditional martingale hypothesis. The application on pp. 18–19 uses independent truncated Poisson variables, so this omission does not establish a failure of that application.

Problem 3 (Minor: the simplicity bound lacks control of total degree).  Lemma 6.5(A2), p. 20, claims that \(\sum_i d_i^2\le Cn\) implies a positive configuration-model simplicity probability bounded below only in terms of \(C\). The convention on p. 9 explicitly allows zero in \(\mathbb N\). For \(k\ge2\), let \(n=k^2+k\), and take one degree \(k\), \(k\) degrees \(1\), and \(k^2-1\) degrees \(0\). This is graphical (a star and isolated vertices), and \(\sum d_i^2=n\), whereas \(\sum d_i=2k\). A simple pairing must match every central stub with a leaf stub. Therefore \[\Pr(\text{simple})
 =\frac{k!}{(2k-1)!!}
 =\frac{2^k}{\binom{2k}{k}}
 \sim \sqrt{\pi k}\,2^{-k}\longrightarrow0.\] Even \(C=1\) supplies no asserted lower bound. Control the second moment relative to total degree, or add a linear lower bound on total degree and invoke the corresponding simplicity theorem with its constants. The paper’s uses have minimum degree two or the linear total-degree condition T4 on p. 31; the counterexample does not satisfy those stronger application conditions. This finding concerns (A2); no separate counterexample to (B2) is claimed.

Problem 4 (Minor: the rank-boosting lemmas include a deterministic slice).  Lemmas 8.2 and 8.4(a), pp. 22–23, assume only \(d\ge1\), although their input Lemma 8.5 explicitly requires \(d\le n/2\). For even \(n\), put \(\eta=1/4\), \(E=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(d=n\), and \(B=A=J_n\), the all-ones matrix. The vector with half its entries \(1\) and half \(-1\) is an \(\eta\)-balanced kernel vector, as required; arbitrary real matrices and symmetric real matrices are allowed. The random slice vector is deterministically \(x=\mathbf1\). Thus \[\operatorname{rank}[J_n\mid\mathbf1]=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}J_n&\mathbf1\\
 \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}=2.\] The claimed rank increments, respectively one and two, fail with probability one for every even \(n\), contradicting an \(O_\eta(d^{-1/2})\) failure bound. Add \(1\le d\le |E|/2\), or formulate a symmetric slice bound that degenerates appropriately at both endpoints. In the actual graph applications, \(d=O(\log n)\) while \(|E|\) is linear in \(n\), so this range is available. No independent counterexample to part 8.4(b) is asserted.

# Positive checks and remaining limits

A leaf and its neighbour contribute an invertible \(2\)-by-\(2\) block; its Schur complement on the remaining graph is unchanged, proving the exact adjacency-rank drop of two. Alternating signs on the degree-two half of a special \(4k\)-cycle give the claimed kernel vector. An isolated such cycle gives two independent vectors. Their addition across cycles requires disjointness, supplied later in Claims 10.4 and Lemma 11.6, rather than assumed solely from the definition.

The restrictions \(c\ne e\), \(c>1\) for the giant 2-core, and \(n_1-n_2\to\infty\) in the degree-constrained bipartite theorem were retained. The critical-window assertion is existence of a sequence \(np_n\to e\); it is not a theorem for \(p=e/n\). The v1 CLT range is \(c<1\) or \(c>e\). Section 13 explicitly supplies sub-Gaussian concentration to control moments; the potential objection that an \(O_p(1)\) comparison alone is insufficient is therefore not a finding.

The Poisson factors for isolated and nonisolated special cycles and the positivity of the giant-core mean were checked. The extraction log-concavity calculation and the main conditioning dependencies were traced. The full combinatorial stalk enumeration, every external spectral and core-process theorem, and the complete bipartite proof sketch were not independently reconstructed. Consequently this report is not an exhaustive correctness certificate.

The publisher record, arXiv history and an author publication page were searched for supplementary material and corrections. No separate author code or supplement was located in that bounded search; absence of code is not a defect.
