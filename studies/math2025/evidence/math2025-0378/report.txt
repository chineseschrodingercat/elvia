Abstract: The energy sign, boundary cancellations, and final level-crossing argument are consistent within the scope checked. Two limited defects are identified in the assigned preprint: Lemma 3.2 claims an initial-time \(L^\infty\) bound independent of the initial peak, and the Neumann Parseval identity omits the weight of the zero vertical mode. Explicit smooth data and a single Fourier mode establish these failures. A data-dependent iteration restores the qualitative \(L^2\)-to-\(L^\infty\) implication needed by the blowup criterion, while the Fourier correction changes universal constants. Neither check supplies a counterexample to the main global-regularity theorem. Independent acceptance and scientific adjudication remain pending.

# Problem 1: the initial peak cannot be removed from Lemma 3.2

Classification: Minor. Location: Lemma 3.2, p. 9; its use in Corollary 3.3.

The lemma assumes \(\|\rho(t)-\rho_M\|_2\le2M\) on \([0,T]\), with \(M\ge\max(1,\sqrt{\rho_M})\), and concludes \(\|\rho(t)-\rho_M\|_\infty\le CM^2\) with universal \(C\), including \(t=0\).

Choose \(0\le\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_1(0))\), \(\phi(0)=1\), \(\phi\le1\), and an interior point \(x_*=(0,\pi/2)\). For \(A\ge1\), define \[\rho_{0,A}(x)=A\phi(A(x-x_*)).\] Its support stays away from the boundary and the periodic seam, so the smooth nonnegative data obey the boundary conditions. A change of variables in two dimensions gives \[\|\rho_{0,A}\|_2^2=\int_{\mathbb R^2}\phi^2\le\pi<4,\qquad
 \rho_{M,A}=\frac{1}{2\pi^2A}\int\phi\le\frac1{2\pi A}.\] Subtracting the mean only decreases the \(L^2\) norm. For any fixed nonzero \(g\), local existence and continuity provide a sufficiently short interval \([0,T_A]\) on which \(\|\rho_A(t)-\rho_{M,A}\|_2\le2\). Thus the lemma’s assumptions hold with \(M=1\). Yet \[\|\rho_{0,A}-\rho_{M,A}\|_\infty
 \ge A-\frac1{2\pi A}\longrightarrow\infty.\] No universal \(C\) can give the stated conclusion. This is a short-time estimate failure, not a singular solution of the coupled system.

## A sufficient qualitative repair

The blowup criterion needs a finite \(L^\infty\) bound for a fixed smooth initial datum when the \(L^2\) norm stays bounded. That weaker statement can retain the initial peak. Here is an independent iteration establishing this distinction.

Equation reference: lp. Suppose \(\sup_{t<T}\|\rho(t)\|_2\le K<\infty\). Set \(Y_p=\int_\Omega\rho^p\), \(w=\rho^{p/2}\), \(p\ge2\). Testing [system] (#system), using incompressibility and the boundary conditions, gives \[
 Y_p'+\frac{4(p-1)}p\|\nabla w\|_2^2
 =(p-1)\int\rho^{p+1}-(p-1)\rho_MY_p.\] The compact-domain two-dimensional interpolation inequality gives \[\int\rho w^2\le K\|w\|_4^2
 \le CK\bigl(\|w\|_2\|\nabla w\|_2+\|w\|_2^2\bigr).\] Young’s inequality in [lp] (#lp) yields \[Y_p'+\|\nabla w\|_2^2\le Cp^2(1+K^2)Y_p.\] The compact-domain Nash inequality gives \[\|\nabla w\|_2^2\ge cY_p^2/Y_{p/2}^2-CY_p.\] Set \(A_{p/2}=\sup_{t<T}\|\rho(t)\|_{p/2}\). The resulting differential inequality bounds \[A_p\le
 \max\left\{\|\rho_0\|_p,\,
 [Cp^2(1+K^2)]^{1/p}A_{p/2}\right\}.\] For \(p=2^j\), the products converge because \(\sum_{j\ge2}2^{-j}=1/2\) and \(\sum_{j\ge2}j2^{-j}=3/2\). This proves a finite bound depending on \(K\), \(\|\rho_0\|_\infty\), and the fixed domain, uniformly in \(T\). The zero-density case is immediate; otherwise all denominators can be treated by the positive lower norms or by the usual zero-case separation.

A bound on \(\|\rho-\rho_M\|_2\) bounds \(K\) because the mean is conserved. Thus the qualitative implication used to exclude finite-time blowup with bounded \(L^2\) remains available. The review does not restore the printed universal \(CM^2\) estimate at \(t=0\), or assert its optimal replacement. This verified distinction limits the impact to Minor.

For completeness, the Riccati step in Corollary 3.3 also has the correct direction: from \(Y'\le C(Y^2+Y)\), \(Z=1+Y^{-1}\) satisfies \(Z'\ge-CZ\). Letting \(Y(t_k)\to\infty\), \(t_k\uparrow T\), gives \(Y(t)\ge(e^{C(T-t)}-1)^{-1}\), upgrading the limsup to a limit.

# Problem 2: the Neumann zero mode needs its normalization weight

Classification: Minor. Location: Section 2.2, p. 7, final gradient Parseval identity; repeated in the discussion of (6.9), pp. 19–20.

The source defines \[\widehat f_N(k_1,k_2)=\frac1\pi\int_\Omega
 f(x)e^{-ik_1x_1}\cos(k_2x_2)\,dx,
 \quad
 f=\frac1\pi\sum_{k_1\in\mathbb Z,\ k_2\ge0}
 \frac{\widehat f_N(k)}{1+\delta_{k_2,0}}e^{ik_1x_1}\cos(k_2x_2).\] Under this convention the correct quadratic form is \[\|\nabla f\|_2^2
 =\sum_{k_1\in\mathbb Z,\ k_2\ge0}
 \frac{k_1^2+k_2^2}{1+\delta_{k_2,0}}|\widehat f_N(k)|^2.\] The printed identity omits the denominator. Take \(f(x)=\cos x_1\). This is smooth, mean zero, satisfies the Neumann condition and also equals its horizontal fluctuation \(\widetilde f\). Its only coefficients are \(\widehat f_N(\pm1,0)=\pi\). The true gradient integral is \(\pi^2\); the printed unweighted sum is \(2\pi^2\).

Inserting the same weight into the \(L^2\) Parseval sums repairs the identity. These weights lie between \(1/2\) and \(1\), so all affected norms remain uniformly equivalent. The cone argument can retain its universal-constant estimates after shrinking its auxiliary parameter and adjusting constants. This verifies the normalization repair and its bounded-factor character, not every lattice-counting or basis-transfer step of Proposition 6.1. No main-result counterexample follows from this defect.

# Checks of the buoyancy mechanism and final contradiction

## Boundary conditions and energy sign

For periodic pressure, the Darcy equation gives \(u=g\nabla^\perp(-\Delta_D)^{-1}\partial_{x_1}\rho\), with \(\nabla^\perp=(-\partial_{x_2},\partial_{x_1})\). Multiplying the density equation by \(x_2\), the boundary conditions eliminate flux terms but retain the vertical diffusion contribution. With \(E(t)=\int_\Omega x_2\rho\), \[E'=-g\|\partial_{x_1}\rho\|_{\dot H^{-1}_0}^2
       -\int_\Omega\partial_{x_2}\rho
       +\int_\Omega\rho\,\partial_{x_2}c,
 \qquad 0\le E\le\pi M_0.\] Horizontal integration by parts fixes the sign: \[\int_\Omega\rho u_2
 =-g\int_\Omega\partial_{x_1}\rho\,(-\Delta_D)^{-1}\partial_{x_1}\rho.\] The diffusion term is the difference of boundary densities and is not presumed nonnegative.

Reflection \(x_2\mapsto\pi-x_2\), with \(u_2\mapsto-u_2\), transforms parameter \(g\) to \(-g\) and preserves the density and no-flux conditions. The positive-\(g\) proof therefore transfers to negative \(g\). If \(M_0=0\), nonnegativity forces the trivial solution; divisions by \(\rho_M\) in nontrivial estimates are unnecessary in that case.

## Level crossing and the finite-energy budget

Write \(Y=\|\rho-\rho_M\|_2^2\). A good level-\(N\) interval goes from \(2^N\) to \(2^{N+1}\), and a bad one goes in the reverse direction. The intermediate-time bounds differ, as stated in Definition 5.1; they are not replaced by endpoint monotonicity. The differential estimate \(Y'\le CY^2\) yields a minimum duration \(c2^{-N}\) for an upward crossing.

At the first hit of level \(Q+1\), every edge between \(N\) and \(N+1\), \(N_0\le N\le Q\), has exactly one more upward crossing than downward crossing. This follows by telescoping the indicator of being above that edge. There are only finitely many crossings before that fixed time, by local smoothness or the upward duration bound. Thus the argument does not need to rearrange an uncontrolled infinite series.

Assume the established interval estimates (6.19) and (6.25): \[\Delta E_{\rm good,N}\le-\frac{c_1g}{N},\qquad
 \Delta E_{\rm bad,N}\le C_1(\rho_M)2^{-N/3}.\] For every fixed \(g>0\), \(N2^{-N/3}\to0\), so a sufficiently large \(N_0\) makes a good/bad pair nonpositive at each level. The unmatched upward crossing then contributes \[E(t_K)-E(t_1)\le-c_1g\sum_{N=N_0}^{Q}\frac1N.\] The harmonic sum can exceed \(\pi M_0/(c_1g)\), contradicting \(0\le E(t_K)\) and \(E(t_1)\le\pi M_0\). This verifies the terminal accounting for arbitrary fixed nonzero coupling, conditional on the technical interval estimates. It supplies no claim of a coupling-independent bound as \(g\to0\).
