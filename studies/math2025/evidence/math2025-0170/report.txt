Abstract: Two quantitative steps in the isoperimetric application fail under the printed definitions. A family of flat tori with truncated distances disproves uniform dependence on the listed local geometric data. A smooth ellipsoid disproves the fixed dilation asserted in the proof: local constancy need not give one multiplicity on a disconnected metric ball. An explicit global volume parameter and a larger dilation repair the argument. The fundamental-class and surface constructions survive the checks, with local endpoint and proof-writing repairs. These findings concern the supplied author version. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

The lead read the complete supplied text, including all definitions, proofs and references, and viewed 30 source pages. Combined actual image coverage is all 36 source pages. The highest supported impact is Major. The uniform dependence claimed after Theorem 1.5 is false for the printed Ahlfors definition. Independently, equation (7.1) is false and its local-constancy argument is invalid on general metric balls. Both are on the proof path to a principal application. The counterexamples do not disprove the existence of an isoperimetric constant for each fixed space, nor the fundamental-class or Lipschitz-volume-rigidity theorems. A corrected quantitative statement is proved below; its added datum must not be silently omitted.

# Definitions and dependencies that matter

Equation reference: eq:ahlfors, eq:iso. Write \(\mu=\mathcal H^n\), normalized to Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R^n\), and \(D=\operatorname{diam}X\). We write \(T\mathbin{\vert}E\) for restriction of a current \(T\) to a Borel set \(E\). All manifolds in the source are connected and without boundary. Balls are open. The definition on p.4 is \[
 \alpha^{-1}r^n\le\mu(B(x,r))\le\alpha r^n,
 \qquad 0<r<D.\] Linear local contractibility with constant \(\Lambda\ge1\) means that \(B(x,r)\) contracts inside \(B(x,\Lambda r)\) whenever \(0<r<D/\Lambda\). It does not say that arbitrary balls are connected. For a Borel set \(E\), let \(E_t=\{x:d(x,E)<t\}\). The source uses \[\mathcal M_-(E\mid U)=\liminf_{t\downarrow0}
       \frac{\mu(E_t\cap U)-\mu(E\cap U)}{t}.\] For \(n\ge2\), Theorem 1.5 states \[
 \min\{\mu(E\cap B),\mu(B\setminus E)\}
 \le C\,\mathcal M_-(E\mid\lambda B)^{n/(n-1)},\] with \(C,\lambda\) depending only on \(n,\alpha,\Lambda\), as specified immediately after its statement.

Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 construct a cycle \(T\) with \(\varphi_\#T=\deg(\varphi)[\![M]\!]\) for every Lipschitz \(\varphi:X\to M\). Since \(\mathcal H^{n+1}(X)=0\), top-dimensional integral homology is the group of cycles, without nonzero higher-dimensional boundaries. Theorem 1.1 supplies a quantitative lower mass density; the surface theorem obtains existence without LLC. The isoperimetric proof uses this cycle, minimal fillings in the compact injective hull, a controlled continuous retraction, and topological constancy after Lipschitz approximation. The rigidity application instead needs the Hausdorff/Busemann mass of a suitable cycle.

# The missing global scale in the advertised data

Problem 1 (Major; Theorem 1.5 and its following paragraph, p.4; the doubling inference, p.26).  Under definition [eq:ahlfors] (#eq:ahlfors), the constants in [eq:iso] (#eq:iso) cannot be bounded by \(n,\alpha,\Lambda\) alone.

## A family satisfying every printed hypothesis

For each \(L\ge4\), take the flat torus \[X_L=(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z)\times(\mathbb R/4\mathbb Z),\qquad
 d(x,y)=\min\{d_0(x,y),1\},\] where \(d_0\) is its Riemannian distance. This is a metric, since \(\min\{a+b,1\}\le\min\{a,1\}+\min\{b,1\}\). It induces the same topology and agrees with \(d_0\) at small distances. In particular, the Hausdorff measures agree: their covers of diameter less than any fixed \(\delta<1\) are identical. Thus \[D=1,\qquad \mathcal H^2_d(X_L)=4L.\] These are compact, connected, oriented smooth topological two-manifolds of finite measure.

The flat injectivity radius is at least \(2\). For every \(0<r<1\), \(B_d(x,r)\) is an embedded Euclidean disk, and \[\mathcal H^2_d(B_d(x,r))=\pi r^2.\] Consequently \(\alpha=\pi\) works in [eq:ahlfors] (#eq:ahlfors) for every \(L\). Each such ball contracts inside itself by Euclidean radial contraction, so \(\Lambda=1\) also works uniformly. These are even the smallest allowed upper Ahlfors and LLC constants in this example.

## The claimed and actual bounds

Let \(B=B_d(p,2)=X_L\), and take the closed half-cylinder \[E=[0,L/2]\times(\mathbb R/4\mathbb Z).\] Then \[\mu(E)=\mu(X_L\setminus E)=2L.\] For \(0<t<1\), the exterior \(t\)-neighborhood adds a strip of width \(t\) along each of the two boundary circles, each of length \(4\). Equivalently, its long-coordinate interval has length \(L/2+2t\). Hence \[\mu(E_t)=2L+8t,\qquad
 \mathcal M_-(E\mid\lambda B)=8\quad\hbox{for every }\lambda\ge1.\] The asserted uniform conclusion is therefore \[2L\le64\,C(2,\pi,1),\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 C(2,\pi,1)\ge L/32.\] As \(L\) is arbitrary, no such finite uniform constant exists. Increasing the dilation has no effect because \(\lambda B=X_L\).

The same family diagnoses the proof’s uniform-doubling inference. For example, \[\frac{\mu(B_d(p,3/2))}{\mu(B_d(p,3/4))}
       =\frac{64L}{9\pi}.\] Each individual torus is doubling; a bound from the three listed data does not follow.

## Alternative reading and precise impact

If Ahlfors regularity was intended to include a bound on the total measure at the diameter scale, this family would be excluded. The supplied definition explicitly restricts the upper bound to \(r<D\), while the theorem concerns every open ball. Interpreting data dependence as permitting an arbitrary larger Ahlfors constant chosen using total volume would hide the missing datum. The ordinary quantitative interpretation must apply to the valid common choice \(\alpha=\pi\).

This is a counterexample to the stated uniform dependence. It is not a counterexample to the existence of some finite \(C\) for a fixed \(X_L\). The source’s qualitative existence conclusion can be retained with the additional parameter described in Section 5 (#sec:repair).

# The fixed dilation and disconnected balls

Problem 2 (Major; equation (7.1), p.26; local constancy and Lemma 7.3, p.29; concluding inference, p.30).  The stronger claim with the fixed dilation \(\lambda=6\) is false, even for a smooth compact ellipsoid with explicit global geometric bounds. Local constancy gives one integer per connected component, which need not be one common integer on the target metric ball.

## An explicit metric two-sphere

Let \[X=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:(x^2+y^2)/10000+z^2=1\}\] with the restricted Euclidean distance. Put \(F(u)=\operatorname{diag}(100,100,1)u\) for \(u\in S^2\), where \(S^2\) has its chordal metric. Then \[|u-v|\le|F(u)-F(v)|\le100|u-v|,\qquad D=200.\] This is a compact connected oriented smooth metric sphere.

For a chordal cap on the unit sphere, the area is \(\pi s^2\) for \(0<s\le2\). The above distance comparisons give \[\frac{\pi r^2}{10000}\le\mathcal H^2(B_X(Fu,r))
       \le10000\pi r^2,\qquad 0<r<200.\] For the upper bound when \(r>2\), use \(\mathcal H^2(X)\le10000\mathcal H^2(S^2)=40000\pi\le10000\pi r^2\). Thus \(\alpha=10000\pi\) satisfies both sides of the source definition.

The LLC condition holds with \(\Lambda=200\). Indeed, if \(r<1\), the preimage of \(B_X(Fu,r)\) lies in the chordal cap \(B_{S^2}(u,r)\). Contract this cap to \(u\) by normalized straight-line contraction. The homotopy stays in that cap, so its image under \(F\) lies in \(B_X(Fu,100r)\subset B_X(Fu,200r)\). Since \(D/\Lambda=1\), this checks every required radius and center.

## A positive left side and a zero right side

Equation reference: eq:positive. Take \[p=(0,0,1),\qquad B=B_X(p,3),\qquad E=X\cap\{z\ge0\}.\] Both graphs \[z=\pm\sqrt{1-(x^2+y^2)/10000},\qquad x^2+y^2<1,\] lie in \(B\): their squared distance to \(p\) is less than \(5<9\). Orthogonal projection onto the planar unit disk is \(1\)-Lipschitz. It follows that \[
 \min\{\mathcal H^2(E\cap B),\mathcal H^2(B\setminus E)\}\ge\pi.\]

For \(q=(x,y,z)\in6B\) with \(z<0\), we have \(x^2+y^2<18^2\); therefore \[z^2>1-\frac{18^2}{10000}
       =\frac{2419}{2500}>
          \left(\frac{49}{50}\right)^2.\] For every \(e\in E\), \(|q-e|\ge|z|>49/50\). Consequently \[E_t\cap6B=E\cap6B\quad(0<t<49/50),\qquad
 \mathcal M_-(E\mid6B)=0.\] Equation (7.1) asserts that the positive quantity in [eq:positive] (#eq:positive) is at most zero, for every finite \(C\). The verification is exact and does not rely on a plot or numerical surface approximation.

## The invalid inference and its scope

The source obtains \(\operatorname{spt}(\partial Q)\subset\rho(X\setminus2B)\), then writes \[Q\mathbin{\vert}\rho(B)
       =c[\![M]\!]\mathbin{\vert}\rho(B)\] with one integer \(c\). Constancy only provides such a constant on each connected component of \(\rho(2B)\). In this example, \(B\) meets two caps in distinct components of \(2B\), and even of \(6B\). The northern-hemisphere current has multiplicity \(1\) on the upper cap and \(0\) on the lower cap, with its boundary near the remote equator. The two alternatives used in Lemma 7.3 cannot be selected by a single global choice of \(c=0\) or \(c\ne0\).

This issue survives adding a global volume bound. Conversely, it does not disprove an estimate with a dilation depending on LLC: the statement of Theorem 1.5 already permits that dependence. The correct local-to-global step is given next.

# A corrected quantitative statement and proof

Equation reference: eq:global. Retain the hypotheses of Theorem 1.5 and make explicit the additional bound \[
 \mathcal H^n(X)\le A_0D^n,\qquad D>0.\] For a fixed compact \(X\), \(A_0=\mathcal H^n(X)/D^n\) is finite: finitely many balls of radius less than \(D\) cover \(X\). Problem 1 shows that \(A_0\) cannot be bounded by the printed local data alone.

Proposition 1.  With [eq:global] (#eq:global), the source’s construction yields [eq:iso] (#eq:iso) with \(\lambda=9\Lambda\) and \(C=C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,A_0)\). If [eq:global] (#eq:global) is required with \(A_0\le\alpha\), the advertised dependence on \(n,\alpha,\Lambda\) is recovered.

Proof. First, the missing global control now follows quantitatively. At radii \(r<D/2\), the measure-doubling ratio is at most \(2^n\alpha^2\). For \(D/2\le r<D\), total mass and the local lower bound give a ratio at most \(2^nA_0\alpha\). The endpoint \(r=D\) follows by comparison with \(D/2\), and \(r>D\) is trivial. Thus a bound \[2^n\max\{\alpha^2,A_0\alpha,1\}\] suffices. The usual disjoint-ball packing argument gives a uniform metric doubling constant and hence the finite Nagata data needed in the source. Moreover, \(\mu(B(x,r))\le\max\{\alpha,A_0\}r^n\) at every positive radius.

Fix a target ball \(B_0=B(x_0,R)\). Define a separate working radius \[q=
 \begin{cases}
   \Lambda R,&R<D/\Lambda,\\
   3D/2,&R\ge D/\Lambda.
 \end{cases}\] Then \(q<2D\), \(q\le3\Lambda R/2\), and \(B_0\subset B(x_0,q)\). In the first case the LLC contraction places all of \(B_0\) in one connected component \(O\) of \(B(x_0,2q)\). In the second case this larger ball is \(X\), so take \(O=X\). These components are open because \(X\) is a topological manifold. Also, \[B(x_0,6q)\subset B(x_0,9\Lambda R).\]

Run the filling construction on pp.27–30 with working radius \(q\) and \(P_0=\mathcal M_-(E\mid B(x_0,6q))\). Keep \(B_0\) as the target of the final two volume terms. If \(P_0>\varepsilon_0 q^{n-1}\), the desired estimate follows immediately from the global upper bound on \(\mu(B(x_0,q))\). Otherwise choose \(0<\delta<\min\{C_2\varepsilon_0q^{n-1},1\}\) and a regular distance level \(r>0\) with \[V=\partial(T\mathbin{\vert}E_r),\qquad
 \|V\|(B(x_0,6q))\le C_2P_0+\delta,\qquad
 \mu(B_0\setminus E)\le2\mu(B_0\setminus E_{3r}).\] Closure of \(E\) changes no relevant measure when \(P_0<\infty\). Slicing and the defining liminf allow these simultaneous choices with \(r\) arbitrarily small.

Choose \(R'\in(5q,6q)\), fill the boundary of \(V\mathbin{\vert}B(x_0,R')\) minimally by \(U\), and then fill \(W=V\mathbin{\vert}B(x_0,R')-U\) minimally by \(S\). Exactly as in (7.4)–(7.8), \[\operatorname{M}(U)\le C_2P_0+\delta,\quad
 \operatorname{M}(W)\le2(C_2P_0+\delta),\quad
 \operatorname{M}(S)\le D_*\operatorname{M}(W)^{n/(n-1)}.\] Here \(D_*\) is the filling constant, distinct from the diameter. The source’s smallness choice for \(\varepsilon_0\) keeps the fillings in the controlled retraction neighborhood because \(q<2D\), and keeps \(U\) outside \(B(x_0,4q)\). The zero-dimensional filling used when \(n=2\) is covered by Theorem 3.3’s explicit convention.

Let \(Q=\varphi_\#(T\mathbin{\vert}E_r-S)\) as in the source. Its boundary is outside \(\rho(B(x_0,2q))\). Apply constancy on \(\rho(O)\), which contains \(\rho(B_0)\). There is now legitimately one integer \(c\) on the target. The proof of Lemma 7.3, restricted to \(B_0\), gives \[B_0\cap E_{r-h}\subset N_h(\pi(\operatorname{spt}S))
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 B_0\setminus E_{3r}\subset N_h(\pi(\operatorname{spt}S)).\]

The source’s covering and minimal-filling density argument in Lemmas 7.2 and 7.4 is used at radius \(q\). For completeness, with \(r_j=d(y_j,A)\), the relevant indices satisfy \[r_j\le d(y_j,\operatorname{spt}\partial S),\qquad
 \sum_{d(y_j,x_0)\le3q}r_j^n
   \le L_*(D_*/a)^n\operatorname{M}(S).\] The small balls have multiplicity at most \(L_*\); the remaining cover of \(A\) has sum of \(n\)-th powers of radii at most \(\delta\). The controlled retraction and the global upper bound on ball measure therefore give \[\mu\bigl(N_h(\pi(\operatorname{spt}S))\cap2B_q\bigr)
       \le C_3\bigl(P_0^{n/(n-1)}+\delta\bigr)\] through the finite covering set \(A^*\) of the source. Since \(B_0\subset2B_q\), the preceding alternative implies \[\min\{\mu(B_0\cap E),\mu(B_0\setminus E)\}
       \le2C_3\bigl(P_0^{n/(n-1)}+\delta\bigr).\] Let \(\delta\downarrow0\), choosing \(r,h\) as above. This includes \(P_0=0\), without dividing by it.

Finally, \(\mathcal M_-(E\mid U)\) is monotone in \(U\): its numerator is \(\mu((E_t\setminus E)\cap U)\). Thus \(P_0\le\mathcal M_-(E\mid9\Lambda B_0)\), proving the proposition. ◻

This repair preserves the qualitative existence assertion for each \(X\) by allowing its finite \(A_0\), and proves a quantitative version with the stated added datum. It does not establish the false original uniform dependence or the fixed dilation \(6\). Combining the repaired estimate with the ball-volume upper bound gives the strong relative inequality \(\min\{\mu(E\cap B),\mu(B\setminus E)\}\le C'R\mathcal M_-(E\mid\lambda B)\). The source’s cited doubling-space characterization then gives its qualitative weak \(1\)-Poincare conclusion. That external characterization is retained as a dependency.

# A local zero-measure endpoint

Problem 3 (Minor; Lemma 4.2, equation (4.1), p.15).  The asserted approximation by a finite complex with zero displacement when \(\mathcal H^{n-1}(S)=0\) is not available in that literal form. The lemma itself has a direct repair.

Take \(n=2\), a round two-sphere \(X\), and an infinite middle-thirds Cantor set \(S\) embedded in a short smooth arc. It is closed and has \(\mathcal H^1(S)=0\), so it satisfies the small-measure hypothesis for every positive \(c,r\). Equation (4.1) would give a finite zero-dimensional simplicial complex \(K\) and a map \(f:S\to K\) with \(\|x-f(x)\|\le c_1\mathcal H^1(S)=0\). Hence \(f(x)=x\) and \(S\subset K\), impossible because a finite zero-complex has finitely many points.

The cited filling result explicitly retains a positive tolerance at zero Hausdorff content [llnr]. More directly, handle this case before the finite-complex construction: \(\mathcal H^{n-1}(S)=0\) implies \(\dim_{\rm top}S\le n-2\). Take \(Z=S\), \(f=\mathrm{id}_S\), and \(g\) the inclusion into \(X\); the constant homotopy proves Lemma 4.2 in this case. For positive measure the approximation argument proceeds. Thus neither Lemma 4.2 nor Theorem 4.1 is refuted. For nonorientable manifolds in Theorem 4.1, the cited separator lemma uses mod-\(2\) cohomology and requires no doubling hypothesis [kinneberg].

# Verified constructions and boundary repairs

## Fundamental class and uniqueness

The degree construction in Section 5 is consistent after the routine choices are made explicit. A controlled extension of each bi-Lipschitz chart has local degree \(+1\) or \(-1\) almost everywhere: composition with a Lipschitz extension of the inverse has derivative the identity at density points. The finite-fiber area formula then turns the oriented current into the sum of local degrees. Bate approximation eliminates the purely unrectifiable image, so the total degree of a map to \(\mathbb R^n\) is zero and \(\partial T=0\). The same calculation and uniform Lipschitz approximation into \(M\) give the degree identity. Finite mass justifies summation and continuity in the first test function.

The thin-filling uniqueness argument of Lemma 5.6 uses a finite-dimensional linear contraction \(\psi\) approximating the identity on compact \(C\). Deformation gives \(\psi_\#S=0\). A straight-line homotopy, with orientation chosen to have boundary \(S\), gives the correct estimate \[\operatorname{M}(R)\le(n+1)\varepsilon\operatorname{M}(S).\] The intervening \(\operatorname{M}(S')\) in the printed p.20 display is incorrect after \(S'=\psi_\#S=0\). Deleting it restores the argument: evaluation of \(S=\partial R\) against a fixed Lipschitz test tuple tends to zero with \(\varepsilon\). This is a verified local correction, not evidence that uniqueness fails.

The proof of Proposition 5.7 uses \(1/(n-1)\) and should separate \(n=1\). Here is the missing endpoint. Proposition 5.9 independently gives \(\operatorname{spt}T=X\). For almost every \(r<D/8\), if \(\partial(T\mathbin{\vert}B(x,r))=0\), Proposition 5.5 makes this restriction \(mT\). Its proper support forces \(m=0\), contradicting full support. The slice is therefore a nonzero integral zero-cycle with total coefficient zero, and has mass at least \(2\). Slicing yields \(\|T\|(B(x,r))\ge2r\) in this range. Monotonicity extends the bound to \(r\le D\), for example with constant \(1/4\). No singular exponent is needed. For \(n\ge2\), the small-ball proof extends to all scales by decreasing its constant by a factor \(8^n\).

The density-point estimate in Lemma 5.3 is valid if the nearby point \(w'\in K\) is chosen so that \[|w-w'|\le
   \frac{|w-z|}{2L(C+L^{-1})}.\] Then \(L^{-1}|z-w'|-C|w-w'|\ge|w-z|/(2L)\). Density permits this stronger choice. These proof-writing corrections do not constitute additional substantive Problem sections.

## Sobolev pushforward and the surface theorem

For \(n>1\), Proposition 6.1’s uniform convergence is justified even at the critical exponent. Put \(A_k=\{h\le k\}\) and \(\varepsilon_k=\int_{M\setminus A_k}h^n\). The source has \(\mathcal H^n(M\setminus A_k)\le\varepsilon_k/k^n\) and \(\varepsilon_k\to0\). A uniform lower volume bound for small balls on the compact Riemannian domain gives \[k\,\sup_{z\in M}d(z,A_k)\le C_M\varepsilon_k^{1/n}\longrightarrow0.\] Indeed, a ball of radius less than \(d(z,A_k)\) is contained in the bad set. The \(2k\)-Lipschitz extensions and uniform continuity of \(\rho\) now imply \[\sup_M d(\rho_k,\rho)
 \le 4k\,s_k+\omega_\rho(2s_k)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad s_k=\sup_M d(\,\cdot\,,A_k).\] This uses the controlled bad-set estimate; it is not an untrue general continuous embedding of \(W^{1,n}\). If \(s_k=0\), the two continuous maps agree on the dense good set, so they agree everywhere. For \(n=1\), strong \(L^1\) boundedness of a maximal function must not be assumed. Instead, if \(g\in L^1\) is the curve gradient and \(h=C\mathcal{M}g\), weak type \((1,1)\) and truncating \(g\) give \(k\mathcal H^1(\{h>k\})\to0\). Use \(\eta_k=k\mathcal H^1(M\setminus A_k)\) in place of \(\varepsilon_k\); the remainder bound is \(2\eta_k\), and the same uniform-convergence argument works. Integrability of \(g\) controls the omitted-domain volume. The surface theorem uses \(n=2\), where the displayed \(L^n\)-tail argument applies directly.

The remainder mass is at most \(2^n\varepsilon_k\). Agreement on the nested good sets makes the pushforward cycles Cauchy in mass; integral closure and shrinking support place the limit in \(X\), with zero boundary. The uniformization theorem used on p.25 applies to non-length metric surfaces [nrnonlength]. Its monotone map has singleton fibers for target-almost every point by the separately applicable Lemmas 7.5 and 7.8 of [nrpoly]. Applying the area formula on the Lipschitz good sets suffices; a global Lusin \(N\) assumption is not silently needed. The uniqueness argument uses compactness and \(\mathcal H^3(X)=0\), without importing LLC back into Theorem 1.3.

## Lipschitz-volume rigidity

In Lemma 8.2, surjectivity, the \(1\)-Lipschitz bound and equality of total finite Hausdorff measures force measure preservation and multiplicity one almost everywhere. On the rectifiable charts of a nonzero cycle \(S\), the area formula and \(\varphi_\#S=k[\![M]\!]\) give \(|\theta_i|=|k|\) almost everywhere. Degenerate Jacobians cannot carry positive chart measure under measure preservation. Thus \(k\ne0\), \(T=S/k\) remains an integral cycle, and \[\operatorname{spt}T=\operatorname{spt}S,\qquad \varphi_\#T=[\![M]\!],\qquad
 \operatorname{M}^{b}(T)\le\mathcal H^n(X).\] This is the required Busemann mass comparison, rather than the weaker ordinary-mass upper bound of Theorem 1.1.

The hypotheses of Züst’s Theorem 1.2 were checked in the later external version [zuest]: a closed Riemannian target is an infinitesimally Euclidean essential length space; its boundary condition is empty; and Busemann volume is Euclidean rigid. Together with full support, these hypotheses match the application. The alternative surface proof’s compactness and degree argument prevents a zero limiting cycle. At the \(n=1\) endpoint of Theorem 1.4, a finite-length metric circle has an oriented arclength parametrization giving the needed cycle directly; this avoids invoking the LLC theorem without LLC.

# Dependence, limits and conclusion

Problems 1 and 2 affect the quantitative isoperimetric application and its printed proof. Problem 1 requires either a global hypothesis or an explicit extra parameter. Problem 2 requires a connected region for constancy and a compatible enlargement of the localization ball. The corrected proposition addresses both. Problem 3 has a local zero-measure repair and does not damage the lower-measure or fundamental-class result.
