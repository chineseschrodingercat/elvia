Abstract: This report examines the abstract openness criterion, weighted variational formulas, and Fano-type application in Boucksom, Jonsson, and Trusiani’s arXiv manuscript 2412.06096v2. A complete example disproves the abstract criterion when its limiting base value is allowed to be infinite. A separate auxiliary interpolation step has an explicit integrability counterexample and a corrected proof. The concrete main application has a finite base value and is not refuted by the first example. A log-concavity hypothesis is retained as a scope question, not a demonstrated nonexistence result.

# The main claims and the inspected dependency

Theorem A (p. 2), expanded as Theorem 4.11 (p. 48), assumes a compact Kähler klt space \(X\), a compact torus \(T\subset\operatorname{LAut}(X)\), positive smooth weights \(v,w\) on the base moment polytope, and a \(T\)-equivariant resolution \(\pi:Y\to X\) of Fano type. The equivariant Kähler forms \(\Omega_j=(\omega_j,m_j)\) converge smoothly to \(\pi^*\Omega_X\), with \[\omega_j\ge(1-\varepsilon_j)\pi^*\omega_X,\qquad
 0\le\varepsilon_j<1,\qquad \varepsilon_j\longrightarrow0.\] Coercivity of the relative weighted Mabuchi functional on \(X\) modulo \(T_{\mathbb C}\) implies uniform eventual coercivity on \(Y\). Quantitatively, a slope \(\sigma\) on the base yields every fixed \(\sigma'<\sigma\), with a single eventual additive constant. The result does not assert preservation of the endpoint slope. It uses unweighted energy normalization \(E_j=0\). Maximality of \(T\) and log-concavity of \(v\) enter the metric-existence corollary, not this coercivity-transfer statement.

The strong-convergence proof in pp. 13–21 retains the needed lower bound on the forms. Proposition 2.9 compares truncations and rooftop envelopes to establish continuity of \(d_{1,j}\). Theorem 2.10 uses bounded entropy to upgrade weak convergence to strong convergence. In the finite-base-value case, the proof of Theorem 2.15 then chooses group-minimal endpoints and fixed-time points on long geodesics. Strong compactness, Proposition 2.9, and Lemma 2.17 preserve the quotient-distance minimum in the limit. The last contradiction is \[\sigma t-C\le M(\varphi_t)\le \sigma't,\qquad
 t>C/(\sigma-\sigma').\] The normalization at the beginning of this chain is the issue examined next.

# Problem 1: an infinite base value invalidates the abstract criterion

Problem 1 (Theorem 2.15 under its literal extended-value hypotheses).  The setup in §2.6, pp. 19–20, allows \(M:\mathcal F\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\) and \(M_j:\mathcal F_j\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\). Its normalization hypothesis says \(0\in\mathcal F,\mathcal F_j\) and \(M_j(0)\to M(0)\), without stating \(M(0)<\infty\). The proof on p. 20 immediately subtracts these base values. With extended-real convergence permitted by the displayed codomains, the conclusion is false.

## A geometric example satisfying every listed condition

Equation reference: eq:B. Take \(X=\mathbb P^1\) with \[\omega=dd^c\!\left(\tfrac12\log(1+|z|^2)\right),\qquad
 d^c=i(\bar\partial-\partial),\qquad
 V=\int_X\omega=2\pi,\qquad \operatorname{Ric}(\omega)=2\omega.\] Set \(\omega_j=\omega\), \(\varepsilon_j=0\), the reference measure \(\nu=\omega\), \(G=\{1\}\), and \(\mathcal F_j=\mathcal F=\mathcal E^1(\omega)\). Write \(E\) for the unnormalized Monge–Ampère energy, \(E(0)=0\), and \[J(\varphi)=\int_X\varphi\,\omega-E(\varphi),\qquad
 H(\varphi)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Ent}(\operatorname{MA}(\varphi)\mid\omega).\] The ordinary Mabuchi functional normalized at zero is \[
 B(\varphi)=H(\varphi)-2J(\varphi).\] Indeed, in complex dimension one the Ricci-energy term is \(-2\int_X\varphi\,\omega\) and the average-scalar-curvature term is \(2E\). This is the relative functional for the trivial torus with \(v=w=1\); it is not the unadjusted functional with target scalar curvature \(1\).

Equation reference: eq:counter. Define \[
 M_j(\varphi)=B(\varphi)+j,\qquad M(\varphi)\equiv+\infty,\qquad j\ge1.\] The six conditions preceding Theorem 2.15 hold as follows. Both domains and functionals are translation invariant, and \(G\)-invariance and properness of the group action are immediate. The domain \(\mathcal E^1\) is geodesically convex, and \(B\) is convex along all finite-energy psh geodesics, as recalled on p. 39 and in Proposition 3.50 on p. 40. Thus the required convexity of \(M_j\) holds. This uses the source’s stated finite-energy convexity theorem, not an assertion that \(M\) is a proper convex functional.

Equation reference: eq:growth. The energy–metric comparison in §1.2, (1.9)/(1.12), gives \(J(\varphi)\le C d_1(\varphi,0)\) for a fixed constant. Relative entropy is nonnegative here because both measures have mass \(V\). Consequently \[
 M_j(\varphi)\ge H(\varphi)-C d_1(\varphi,0)+j,\] which implies uniform entropy growth with coefficient \(\delta=1\). For any strongly convergent \(\varphi_j\to\varphi\), the distances \(d_1(\varphi_j,0)\) are bounded. Equation [eq:growth] (#eq:growth) therefore gives \(\liminf_jM_j(\varphi_j)=+\infty=M(\varphi)\), as required. Finally, \(M_j(0)=j\to+\infty=M(0)\) satisfies the stated normalization when understood in the declared extended-real codomain. The limiting functional satisfies every finite coercivity slope vacuously.

## The conclusion fails on an explicit normalized orbit

Equation reference: eq:orbit. For \(t>0\), set \(a=e^{2t}\) and \(y=|z|^2/(1+|z|^2)\in[0,1]\). The map \(g_t:z\mapsto e^tz\) has potential and density \[\psi_t=\tfrac12\log(1+(a-1)y),\qquad
 \rho_t=\frac{g_t^*\omega}{\omega}
       =\frac{a}{(1+(a-1)y)^2}.\] After angular integration, \(\omega/V=dy\). Direct integration gives \[\begin{aligned}
 F:=\int_0^1\psi_t\,dy&=\frac{a\log a-a+1}{2(a-1)},&
 G_0:=\int_0^1\psi_t\rho_t\,dy&=\frac{a-\log a-1}{2(a-1)},\\
 \int_0^1\rho_t\,dy&=1,&
 E(\psi_t)/V&=(F+G_0)/2=\tfrac14\log a=t/2.\end{aligned}\] Thus \(\tau_t=\psi_t-t/2\) satisfies \(E(\tau_t)=0\). Since \(\log\rho_t=\log a-4\psi_t\), the same integrals yield \[
 \frac{J(\tau_t)}{V}=\frac{t\coth t-1}{2},\qquad
 H(\tau_t)=2J(\tau_t),\qquad B(\tau_t)=0.\] In particular \(J(\tau_t)\to\infty\), and Lemma 1.6 gives \(d_1(\tau_t,0)\to\infty\). For every fixed \(j\), however, \(M_j(\tau_t)=j\). Taking \(\sigma=1\) and \(\sigma'=1/2\) in Theorem 2.15 therefore makes its conclusion impossible, even for a single eventual \(j\).

These identities were also checked by an independently authored, actually executed SymPy calculation: derivatives of all three integration primitives have zero residual, the density has mass one, [eq:orbit] (#eq:orbit) has zero residual, \(J(\tau_t)/(Vt)\to1/2\), and \(J(\tau_t)/(Vt^2)\to1/6\) as \(t\downarrow0\). The analytic formula proves divergence; the saved finite samples do not substitute for that proof.

## Correction, competing reading, and precise impact

A sufficient precise correction is to add \(M(0)\in\mathbb R\) to §2.6. Then \(M_j(0)\to M(0)\) makes the eventual base values finite and uniformly bounded. Subtracting them preserves the assumptions after changing a uniform additive constant, and the finite-case argument in Section 2 applies. No additional uniform upper bound on all values of \(M_j\) is needed.

One may reasonably read the intended normalization as convergence to a finite number. Under that reading the counterexample is excluded and the proof works; the written extended-value statement should say so. The earlier §2.5 assumptions on different functionals \(F_j,F\) do not supply this condition for the new \(M_j,M\) in §2.6. The properness bullet concerns only the group action.

The same boundary occurs in Lemma 1.11 (p. 12). Let \(\mathcal F=\mathcal E^1(\mathbb P^1)\), \(M\equiv+\infty\), \(G=\operatorname{Aut}_0(\mathbb P^1)=\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)\), and \(H=\{1\}\). Proposition 1.8 gives a proper \(G\)-action; \(M\) is \(H\)-coercive in Definition 1.10, but \(G/H\) is noncompact. The escaping diagonal transformations above witness this. Adding \(0\in\mathcal F\) and \(M(0)\in\mathbb R\) makes the proof’s orbit bound legitimate. This is the same issue family, not an additional severity count.

Theorem 2.15 is the paper’s general openness guarantee and is invoked in the proof of Theorem 4.11 (pp. 56–59). Its false literal generality is therefore closely tied to a central contribution, supporting Major under the stated review standard. This does not imply that the concrete main theorem is false. Lemma 3.59 (p. 44) shows that the smooth base weighted measure has bounded density relative to a finite adapted measure; its entropy is finite because \(x\log x\) is bounded below and is bounded above on a bounded density range. The other energy terms vanish at zero. Thus \(M_X^{\rm rel}(0)\in\mathbb R\), and Proposition 4.16 (p. 52) gives the required finite convergence \(M_j^{\rm rel}(0)\to M_X^{\rm rel}(0)\). The actual application lies in the corrected finite case.

# Problem 2: the Fano-type interpolation uses the wrong singular endpoint

Problem 2 (Lemma 4.6, p. 47).  For \(L=-(K_Y+B)\), the proof chooses a singular metric \(\phi_0\) with a strictly positive vertical curvature margin and a smooth nef approximation \(\phi_1\). It sets \(\phi_t=\phi_B+(1-t)\phi_0+t\phi_1\) and infers a trivial multiplier ideal for \(0<t\ll1\) from that of \(\phi_B\). The permitted \(\phi_0\) need not have a trivial multiplier ideal, so that inference is false.

## All hypotheses and the actual integrability threshold

Let \(X=Y=\mathbb P^1\), \(\pi=\mathrm{id}\), \(B=0\), and \(L=\mathcal O(2)\). This is a compact Kähler klt pair with effective boundary and a \(\pi\)-nef \(L\). Let \(h\) be a positive smooth metric on \(\mathcal O(1)\), with curvature \(\omega_h\), and let \(s_p\) vanish simply at \(p\). With the paper’s normalization, \(dd^c\log|s_p|_h=2\pi[p]-\omega_h\). Choose \[\phi_0=2h+\tfrac32\log|s_p|_h,\qquad \phi_1=2h.\] Then \(dd^c\phi_0=\tfrac12\omega_h+3\pi[p]\), meeting the required Kähler-current lower bound, and \(\phi_1\) is an allowed smooth nef approximation. Near \(p\) the singular part of \(\phi_t\) is \(\tfrac32(1-t)\log|z|\). Local integrability requires \[\int_0^r s^{\,1-3(1-t)}\,ds<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t>1/3.\] It fails for every \(0<t\le1/3\), with logarithmic divergence at equality. Thus the claimed small-\(t\) inference fails even when the particular interpolating curvatures are positive. The conclusion that this \(\pi\) is of Fano type is not false: the smooth metric \(2h\) already proves it.

## A corrected proof preserving the lemma’s conclusion

Fix the source’s \(\phi_0\) and \(\epsilon>0\) with \(dd^c\phi_0\ge\epsilon\omega_Y-C_0\pi^*\omega_X\). Because \((Y,B)\) is klt, choose \(q>1\) for which \(e^{-2q\phi_B}\) is locally integrable. Local exponential integrability of the quasi-psh weight \(\phi_0\), Hölder’s inequality, and a finite cover of compact \(Y\) give a sufficiently small \(0<\delta<1\) such that \(e^{-2(\phi_B+\delta\phi_0)}\) is locally integrable. These choices are made in local frames; smooth changes of frame do not affect integrability.

Only after choosing \(\delta\), use \(\pi\)-nefness to select a smooth metric \(\phi_{1,\eta}\) with \[dd^c\phi_{1,\eta}\ge-\eta\omega_Y-C_\eta\pi^*\omega_X,
 \qquad \eta=\frac{\delta\epsilon}{2(1-\delta)}.\] The metric \(\Phi=\phi_B+\delta\phi_0+(1-\delta)\phi_{1,\eta}\) on \(-K_Y\) then has a trivial multiplier ideal and satisfies \[dd^c\Phi\ge \frac{\delta\epsilon}{2}\omega_Y
 -\bigl(\delta C_0+(1-\delta)C_\eta\bigr)\pi^*\omega_X.\] Here effectiveness of \(B\) gives a nonnegative divisor current. This is the required Fano-type metric. No uniform bound on \(C_\eta\) as \(\eta\downarrow0\) is needed.

Simply exchanging small \(t\) with \(t\) near one is insufficient as a proof repair: the displayed original curvature estimates only give \(\epsilon(1-2t)\omega_Y\) minus a pullback bound. The ordered two-parameter choice above controls both integrability and curvature.

Lemma 4.6 is used in Example 4.8 for minimal resolutions of klt surfaces. Theorem 4.11 instead assumes Fano type and starts directly from Definition 4.4. Smooth single blowups use relative ampleness in Examples 4.3/4.5, supplying a smooth metric without this interpolation. Thus this is a Minor auxiliary defect with a proved correction; no failure of the main transfer or the smooth-blowup consequence has been established. The classification follows this dependency boundary, not the ease of repair.

# Positive checks and unresolved scope

## A nonconstant-weight test of the formalism

On the unit round sphere, take \(y\in[-1,1]\), angular period \(2\pi\), \(A=1-y^2\), \(\omega=dy\wedge d\theta\), \(m=y\), and \(g=A^{-1}dy^2+A\,d\theta^2\). The expressions extend over both poles. Here \(S=\operatorname{tr}_\omega\operatorname{Ric}(\omega)=1\), \(\Delta f=(Af')'\), and \(m_f=Af'\). Direct weighted Ricci calculation gives \[S_v=1-\frac{A'v'}{v}-\frac{Av''}{2v},\] which agrees with (3.34) on p. 29. Exact rational polynomial checks for \(v=1+y/2\) and \(v=2+y+y^2/2\) give zero residual; the second includes a nonzero Hessian term.

For \(v=1+y/2\), \(w=1\), the affine pairing, with the angular factor omitted, is \[G=\begin{pmatrix}2&1/3\\1/3&2/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det G=11/9,\qquad \ell_{\rm ext}(y)=(9+12y)/11.\] The relative derivative density is \((-2+6y^2)/11\), whose integrals against \(1\) and \(y\) vanish exactly. In particular the negative value \(\ell_{\rm ext}(-1)=-3/11\) is allowed; positivity is imposed on the pairing weights \(v,w\), not on this affine projection. For \(f=y^2,g=y^3\), the weighted integration-by-parts expressions all equal \(-12/35\), before multiplying by \(2\pi\); the endpoint fluxes vanish. This tests formulas and invariance, not existence of an extremal metric for these chosen weights.

## Main curvature, entropy, and mass checks

The Fano-type definition supplies a quasi-psh \(f\) with finite \(\widehat\nu_Y=e^{-2f}\nu_Y\) and a one-sided Ricci lower bound. Averaging over \(T\) preserves finiteness by Jensen’s inequality. Strong openness supplies \(p>1\); the proof on p. 57 obtains an entropy coefficient \(1-1/p>0\). Weighted masses converge to a positive mass, and positivity of \(v\) persists uniformly on the converging polytopes. Thus neither a collapsing volume nor a sign reversal is required.

Lemma 4.19 identifies the weak limit of weighted Monge–Ampère measures off the exceptional locus; equality of total masses and the absence of exceptional mass for the nonpluripolar limit then exclude an additional exceptional component. Lemma 4.22 has the upper semicontinuity needed for the negative twisted Ricci energy to be lower semicontinuous. Lemma 4.26 supplies the comparison in the direction used by the final liminf, without assuming entropy-recovery approximation on every singular space. These checks support the stated dependency under its finite-base-value condition, subject to the cited analytic inputs.

The intermediate factors \(1/2\) and \(e^{2\rho}\) in the entropy computation on p. 50 should be retained. With these conventions its final Lemma 4.15 identity is unchanged. Likewise the final decomposition on p. 58 uses \(-c_j\) from (4.14)/(4.18), but \(c_j\to0\) leaves the liminf unchanged. These notation corrections are not counted as separate scientific defects.

## Dependence on the full equivariant lift

Lemma 3.44 (pp. 36–37) says its compact set \(K\) depends only on \(\theta\). Read uniformly across all lifts \(\Theta=(\theta,m_\Theta)\), this is too strong. Fix the sphere above and \(\theta=0\). Given a nonempty proposed compact \(K\), set \(a=\min K\), \(\Theta=(0,a-1)\), \(f=0\), \(v=2+y\), and \(w=-a\). The tested inequality \(v'\beta+w=\beta-a\ge0\) holds on \(K\), but \(\operatorname{MA}_v^\Theta+\operatorname{MA}_w=-\omega\), so the functional decreases along increasing constant potentials. For empty \(K\) the condition is vacuous. Allowing dependence on full \(\Theta\) restores the source’s moment-image argument. Its inspected application \(\Theta=A\Omega\), \(K=AP\), survives. This is retained as a local dependence clarification, plausibly a \(\theta\)-for-\(\Theta\) notation error, outside the substantive issue count.

## Log-concavity in Corollary 4.12: Needs Evidence

The metric-existence direction of Theorem 3.51 requires \(v\) log-concave on the moment polytope of the target class. Corollary 4.12 (p. 49) uses \(P\), while §4 uses \(P_X,P_j\) and assumes only \(P_j\to P_X\). If \(P\) means only \(P_X\), that hypothesis does not transfer automatically.

For example, on \(\mathbb P^1\) with \(\pi=\mathrm{id}\), choose a circle moment map with \(P_X=[-1,0]\) and set \[\omega_j=(1+1/j)\omega_X,\qquad
 m_j=(1+1/j)m_X+1/j,\qquad P_j=[-1,1/j].\] These equivariant forms satisfy the required convergence and lower bound. The smooth positive weight \(v(x)=e^{\lambda x^3}\), \(\lambda>0\), has \((\log v)''=6\lambda x\le0\) on \(P_X\), but is not log-concave on any \(P_j\). Even a smooth concave extension agreeing with this weight on \(P_X\) cannot remove that boundary behavior: its second derivative vanishes at zero while its third derivative there is positive.

This example disproves only the implication about weight domains. It is not a counterexample to metric existence satisfying all the corollary’s assumptions. Corollary B on p. 3 uses an unqualified log-concavity hypothesis; a global interpretation avoids the question. Requiring log-concavity on a neighborhood of \(P_X\), or directly on all sufficiently late \(P_j\), is sufficient for the displayed derivation. Clarification of the intended \(P\) remains necessary before calling the corollary false. The maximal-torus assumption is separately retained; the nonmaximal centralizer obstruction is explicitly discussed in Remark 4.13 and is not silently ignored.
