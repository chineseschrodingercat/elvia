Abstract: This initial review concerns the supplied 84-page publisher PDF of the article by Arul Shankar, Anders Södergren and Nicolas Templier. It establishes a prime-level hypothesis missing from the cited subconvexity argument, an incorrect extension of secondary residue weights to prime powers, a noncongruence obstruction for the stated all-order local-specification indicator, and a counterexample to the border-range estimate within the parameter range actually used in its proof. These are substantial defects in essential inputs; they do not constitute counterexamples to the article’s main nonvanishing conclusions. A separate normalization error admits a complete local correction preserving the analytic assertion and the implicitly defined main-term constants. Two further local errors concern unweighted automorphism counts and an omitted scalar in a uniform error estimate; their verified downstream effects are limited. The report gives exact witnesses, counterevidence, dependency paths and bounded repair statements. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Mathematical framework and checked dependencies

The article studies \(L(s,\rho_K)=\zeta_K(s)/\zeta_{\mathbb Q}(s)\) for cubic fields, where \(\rho_K\) is the two-dimensional standard Artin representation. For a non-Galois cubic field it is irreducible, the finite conductor is \(D_K=|\operatorname{Disc}(K)|\), and \(L(1/2,\rho_K)\) is real. Since \(\zeta_{\mathbb Q}(1/2)<0\), positive \(L(1/2,\rho_K)\) corresponds to negative \(\zeta_K(1/2)\). The sign follows, for example, from \(\zeta(s)=\eta(s)/(1-2^{1-s})\) and \(\eta(s)>0\) for real \(0<s<1\). This sign conversion was checked rather than inferred from a numerical sample.

Binary cubic forms are acted on by the twisted action \(g\cdot f(x,y)=(\det g)^{-1}f((x,y)g)\). Discriminants are homogeneous of degree four in the coefficients. Integral orbits parametrize cubic orders; maximality, the generic \(\mathbb Q_p\)-algebra, and the splitting type of a possibly nonmaximal reduction are different conditions. The distinction is essential in Problems 2 and 3.

The finite calculations supporting the positive local checks included all 144 entries of the relevant Fourier-table tests at \(p=2,5,7,11\), and direct maximality/lift enumerations modulo \(p^2\) at \(p=2,3,5\). They agreed with the tested entries and the raw first two maximal-locus moments. The corresponding reviewer-written scripts, exact inputs and output receipts are preserved; no author-supplied code was executed. These finite checks support specific identities and do not prove unrestricted prime statements. The symbolic arguments, the local Artin factors and the contour-shift identities were separately checked in the bounded notes.

For the approximate functional equation, the operative body normalization is \(2S(f)=L(1/2,\rho_K)\) on maximal orders; the introductory schematic display omits a factor two that is retained in the body. This does not generate an additional finding. Similarly, the explicit finite-strip choice of smoothing function is adequate for a fixed finite collection of contour shifts; a literal claim of decay faster than every power as \(y\to\infty\) for one fixed finite-strip choice can be replaced by an allowed entire choice such as \(G(u)=e^{u^2}\). No substantive failure is inferred solely from that presentation.

The main dependency chain is: local coefficients and residues \(\longrightarrow\) congruence counts and sieve \(\longrightarrow\) small- and border-range moment estimates \(\longrightarrow\) the Section 10 sign/counting argument. The individual defects below are located on this chain. A correction of one input does not certify the other inputs.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (P01: the cited subconvexity theorem has an unmet prime-level hypothesis).  Severity: Major.

## Original claim and exact location

Equation reference: eq:subconv. Theorem 4.1, p. 57 (PDF 25), asserts, for every cubic field \(K\), \[
 |L(1/2+it,\rho_K)|
 \ll_{\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^{O(1)}D_K^{1/4-1/128+\varepsilon}.\] For the \(S_3\) case its proof identifies a weight-zero or weight-one automorphic form of level \(D_K\) and invokes Blomer–Khan, Theorem 1. The inspected theorem in the Blomer–Khan arXiv version [BK] assumes a prime level \(q\) and a primitive central character. The article gives no reduction from arbitrary cubic discriminants to prime level. The claimed numerical savings are then used in the later \(\delta\)-dependent bounds and the numerical lower bounds of Theorem 3, pp. 35–36 (PDF 3–4).

## A concrete failure of the invocation

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\theta)\), where \[f(T)=T^3-5T+1.\] The only possible rational roots are \(\pm1\), and \(f(1)=-3\), \(f(-1)=5\). Thus \(f\) is irreducible. Its discriminant is \[-4(-5)^3-27=473=11\cdot43.\] It is squarefree, so \(\mathbb Z[\theta]\) is maximal and \(D_K=473\). It is positive and nonsquare, so \(K\) is a totally real non-Galois cubic field. The associated Artin conductor is therefore the composite integer 473. It does not satisfy the prime-level hypothesis of the theorem invoked.

This example also lies in the type of \(S_3\) family used in the article: modulo 2, \(T^3+T+1\) has no root and is irreducible; modulo 3, \[T^3-5T+1=(T-1)(T^2+T+2),\] and the quadratic factor has nonsquare discriminant 2. Consequently the field has inert type \((3)\) at 2 and mixed type \((12)\) at 3. The obstruction is not removed by the later use of two auxiliary splitting conditions.

This proves a failure of the cited theorem’s applicability. It does not disprove the estimate [eq:subconv] (#eq:subconv): one field cannot by itself contradict a uniform asymptotic bound with an unspecified constant. Nor does the check establish that no alternative theorem could supply the requested saving. It establishes that the proof given has not supplied it. The cyclic case, handled separately by a Burgess-type estimate, does not repair the missing \(S_3\) argument.

## A verified weaker substitution and its limits

As a precisely delimited external alternative, Corollary 1.5 of the inspected version of Wu [Wu] states a bound of the form \[L(1/2,\pi)\ll_{\varepsilon}
 C(\pi)^{1/4+\varepsilon}
 \left(\frac{C(\pi)}{C(\omega)}\right)^{-\alpha}
 C(\omega)^{-1/224},
 \qquad
 \alpha=\frac{1-2\theta}{56}.\] With \(\theta=7/64\), one has \(\alpha=25/1792>1/224\). For the Artin representation and its unitary imaginary twists, \(C(\omega)\ll C(\pi)\) and \(C(\pi)\asymp D_K(1+|t|)^2\), up to the fixed archimedean conventions. Writing \(\beta=1/224\) gives \[(C(\pi)/C(\omega))^{-\alpha}C(\omega)^{-\beta}
 =C(\pi)^{-\beta}
  (C(\pi)/C(\omega))^{-(\alpha-\beta)}
 \ll C(\pi)^{-\beta}.\] Thus this stated external input gives a polynomial-in-\(t\) bound with \(\delta=1/224\) in place of \(1/128\). This is a conditional use of the cited external theorem, not a new proof of that theorem.

The Section 10 exponent algebra, considered conditionally on its other inputs, then gives the following exact values:

\(0\) | \(2/3\) | \(3/4\)

\(1/224\) | \(112/167\) | \(169/224\)

The formulas are \(2/(3-4\delta)\) and \(3/4+\delta\). Only the last row is the article’s numerical choice. This local substitution does not restore the residue, congruence or border-range inputs treated below.

## Impact and repair boundary

The issue is Major because a stronger uniform subconvex input is used to deduce the article’s quantitative improvements, and the named theorem does not establish that input for the family at hand. A valid unrestricted discriminant theorem, with its exact hypotheses and uniformity checked, would be a repair. Otherwise the numerical conclusions must be weakened and all dependent exponents revisited. No conclusion that the main nonvanishing theorem is false is drawn.

Problem 2 (P02: prime-level secondary residue weights are incorrectly pulled back to prime powers).  Severity: Major.

## Original claim and exact location

Equation (35), p. 66 (PDF 34), defines \[\mathcal C_{p^\beta}(\phi)
   =p^{-4\beta}\sum_{f\in V(\mathbb Z/p^\beta\mathbb Z)}
      \phi(f)c_p(f\bmod p),\] with \(c_p\) factoring through reduction modulo \(p\). Table 1, p. 67 (PDF 35), has \(c_p(0)=p^{2/3}\). Proposition 5.2 uses \(\gamma^\pm\mathcal C_n(\phi)\) as the residue at \(5/6\) for arbitrary permitted moduli, and Theorem 5.6 uses that residue in its smoothed count. The same weights enter the maximality sieve in Section 6.

## An exact scaling counterexample

Take \(p=5\), \(n=p^2\), and \(\phi(f)=\mathbf1_{\{f\equiv0\pmod{p^2}\}}\). This is a permitted invariant function. The printed definition gives \[\mathcal C_{p^2}(\phi)=p^{-8}p^{2/3}=p^{-22/3}.\] On the other hand, multiplication by \(p^2\) is an exact bijection from integral cubic forms to the forms in its support, compatible with \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\)-orbits and preserving stabilizers. Since \(\operatorname{Disc}(p^2g)=p^8\operatorname{Disc}(g)\), the actual Shintani series satisfies \[\xi^\pm(\phi,s)=p^{-8s}\xi^\pm(1,s).\] Its residue at \(5/6\) is therefore \[\gamma^\pm p^{-20/3},\] which differs from the asserted \(\gamma^\pm p^{-22/3}\). Here \(\gamma^\pm\ne0\): the gamma factor is nonzero, and \(\zeta(1/3)<0\) by the alternating-series identity already used above.

The contradiction is exact and does not depend on fitting a finite numerical count. For a fixed smooth nonnegative test function whose Mellin transform at \(5/6\) is nonzero, the coefficient discrepancy has order \(X^{5/6}\). It cannot be absorbed into Theorem 5.6’s error for this fixed modulus. To stay on an \(S_3\) support, multiply the indicator by an inert condition and a mixed condition at two different primes other than \(p\). Scaling by \(p^2\) is an invertible scalar at those primes and preserves their types. Their secondary factors are positive. The same discrepancy then occurs on an \(S_3\) support in the \(q=1\) case of Theorem 6.6.

## The defect persists in the maximality sieve

Equation reference: eq:true-nm. Let \(p\ge5\) and \(t=p^{-1/3}\). The six mod-\(p\) types \((0),(1^3),(1^21),(111),(12),(3)\) have normalized masses \[t^{12},\quad t^6-t^{12},\quad t^3-t^9,\quad
 \frac{(1-t^3)(1-t^6)}6,\quad
 \frac{(1-t^3)(1-t^6)}2,\quad
 \frac{(1-t^3)(1-t^6)}3.\] Their projective root counts are \(p+1,1,2,3,1,0\). Substitution in Table 1 gives \(\mathcal C_p(1)=1\) and \(\mathcal C_p(\omega_p)=1+t\). For clarity, the valid weighted switching identity can be derived without using the article’s unweighted version. Let \[P(s)=\sum_{[R\subset R'],\, [R':R]=p}
       \frac{|\operatorname{Disc}R|^{-s}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(R,R')|}.\] For a finite group acting on a set, the sum of reciprocal stabilizers over its orbits equals the size of the set divided by the group order. Counting the pairs by \(R'\) therefore gives \(P(s)=p^{-2s}\xi(\omega_p,s)\). Counting them by \(R\), a primitive nonmaximal order has one overorder; a form \(f=pg\) has \(\omega_p(g)\) overorders. Thus \[P(s)=\xi_{W_p}(s)-p^{-4s}\xi(1,s)+p^{-4s}\xi(\omega_p,s).\] These equalities first hold in absolute convergence and then continue meromorphically. Equating them gives \[\xi_{W_p}(s)
 =(p^{-2s}-p^{-4s})\xi(\omega_p,s)+p^{-4s}\xi(1,s).\] Consequently the actual nonmaximal secondary factor is \[
 c_{\rm nm,true}
 =(p^{-5/3}-p^{-10/3})(1+p^{-1/3})+p^{-10/3}
 =t^5+t^6-t^{11}.\] This value is also stated independently in Taniguchi–Thorne, Corollary 8.15, p. 1371 (PDF 52 of the acquired publisher PDF) [TT].

The integral of the reviewed article’s pulled-back \(c_p\) over the nonmaximal locus, p. 76 (PDF 44), instead gives \[c_{\rm nm,table}
 =t^{10}+t^3(t^6-t^{12})c_p(1^3)
          +t^3(t^3-t^9)c_p(1^21)
 =t^6+t^7-t^{13}.\] The zero mod-\(p\) class is entirely nonmaximal; for each primitive repeated-root class exactly \(1/p\) of the lifts are nonmaximal; unramified classes are maximal. These are exactly the lift proportions in this calculation. The difference is \[c_{\rm nm,true}-c_{\rm nm,table}
   =t^5(1-t^2)(1-t^6)>0.\] Thus the true maximal factor is \((1-p^{-2})(1-p^{-5/3})\), whereas the printed integral gives \((1-p^{-2})(1-p^{-7/3})\). Restricting the auxiliary modulus \(n\) to be squarefree cannot fix the \(q=p\) maximality step.

## Impact, counterevidence and repair boundary

The prime-level table itself is not rejected: the failure is its unchanged use at higher level and on a locus whose content affects the residue. The scaling test is already decisive without any assumed formula for the entire corrected sieve. The correct one-prime value [eq:true-nm] (#eq:true-nm) supplies an additional independent comparison. A repair requires the actual level-dependent orbital residue functionals, followed by corrected nonmaximal and maximal factors and their uniform error estimates. Only the scaling identity and the one-prime comparisons above have been restored here. The error changes a secondary term and affects an essential counting input; it is Major even though it does not by itself disprove the final sign theorem.

Problem 3 (P03: the stated generic-algebra indicator is not a fixed congruence function on all orders).  Severity: Major.

## Original claim and exact location

On p. 78 (PDF 46), before Corollary 6.7, \(\chi_\Sigma\) is defined on all integral or \(p\)-adic forms by the condition \(R_f\otimes\mathbb Q_p\in\Sigma_p\). The text then treats it as a function factoring through \(V(\mathbb Z/r_\Sigma\mathbb Z)\) and asserts the requisite \(S_3\) support property. No maximality restriction at the specified primes is inserted in that definition. Corollary 6.7 and the subsequent counts apply finite-congruence estimates to this function.

## A counterexample to every fixed modulus

Take \(\Sigma_7\) to consist of the unramified cubic extension, \(\Sigma_{11}\) to consist of \(\mathbb Q_{11}\) times its unramified quadratic extension, and \(\Sigma_\infty=\{\mathbb R\times\mathbb C\}\); impose no other conditions. This is an \(S_3\)-collection. Let \[g(x,y)=x^3-2y^3,\qquad h(x,y)=x^3-15y^3.\] The first is Eisenstein at 2 and the second at 3. Their discriminants are \(-108\) and \(-6075\), respectively, both negative and nonsquare, so each defines a non-Galois cubic field. At 7, 2 is a noncube in \(\mathbb F_7^\times\), whereas \(15\equiv1\) and \(T^3-1\) has three distinct roots. At 11, cubing is bijective on \(\mathbb F_{11}^\times\), so each polynomial has one simple root and an irreducible quadratic factor. Both discriminants are units at 7 and 11. Thus \(\chi_\Sigma(g)=1\) and \(\chi_\Sigma(h)=0\).

For every positive integer \(M\), the twisted action of \(M I_2\) sends \(f\) to \(Mf\). This matrix is invertible over \(\mathbb Q\) and every \(\mathbb Q_p\); hence it preserves the generic algebra. Therefore \[\chi_\Sigma(Mg)=1,\qquad \chi_\Sigma(Mh)=0,
 \qquad Mg\equiv Mh\equiv0\pmod M.\] No fixed congruence modulus can distinguish them. The witnesses remain irreducible and nondegenerate, so exclusion of singular or reducible forms does not remove the obstruction. For a modulus containing 7, the zero reduction also contradicts the asserted inert finite-level support condition.

## Impact and precise limits of a repair

The statement is false on the domain on which it is defined. Its use affects Corollary 6.7, Theorem 6.10 and the weighted counts in Sections 8–9. One may define a different finite-level extension agreeing with the desired local algebra on the maximal locus, or impose maximality at the finitely specified primes and sieve only elsewhere. Either route changes the weights on nonmaximal orders. The switching argument, overlaps at the fixed primes and the corresponding residue functionals must then be checked explicitly.

Even on maximal orders, arbitrary ramified specifications cannot in general be encoded solely by the mod-\(p\) splitting type. For instance, over \(\mathbb Q_7\) the Eisenstein cubics \(T^3-7\) and \(T^3-14\) give distinct tame cubic extensions but the same reduction \(T^3\). The field \(\mathbb Q_7\) contains the cube roots of unity, and the Kummer classes of 7 and 14 generate different lines since 2 is not a cube. Both local orders are maximal. This observation limits a proposed repair; it is not counted as an additional finding.

The ability to choose a corrected extension on the maximal locus is counterevidence against declaring the main field-counting conclusion false. It is not a proof of the printed all-order congruence assertion, or a completed repair of its downstream uses.

Problem 4 (P04: Proposition 9.11 fails inside the border range used later).  Severity: Major.

## Original claim and exact location

Proposition 9.11, pp. 100–102 (PDF 68–70), writes \(n=n_1\ell_1\), with \((\ell_1,q_1)=1\) and \(n_1\) supported on primes dividing \(q_1\), and defines \(\ell=\operatorname{rad}(\ell_1)\). It bounds the indicated weighted sum over \(\mathcal U_{m_1q_1}(\Sigma)\) by \(X^\varepsilon H(n,m_1,q_1;X)\). On p. 102 its proof uses \[\mathcal A_{\ell_1}(\lambda_{\ell_1})\ll \ell^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_{\ell_1}(\lambda_{\ell_1})\ll \ell^{-1/3},\] citing Lemma 5.9. That lemma concerns the first prime coefficient \(\lambda_p\). The coefficient \(\lambda_{p^2}\) instead has a positive mean of order one. The following counterexample verifies not only the literal proposition’s hypotheses but also the standing border range and the truncations in its subsequent application.

## Fixed family and admissible parameters

Equation reference: eq:border-parameters. Fix the totally real specification at infinity, inert type at 7, mixed type at 11, and no other restrictions. Let \(\Psi\ge0\) be a fixed smooth function supported in \([1,2]\) with integral one. Choose a sufficiently small fixed \(\kappa=\kappa_1\in(0,1/100)\) and put \[a=\kappa/10,\qquad \varepsilon=\kappa/100.\] Let \(Q\) run through primes other than 7 and 11, set \(X=Q^8\), and choose another prime \(p\asymp X^a\). Bertrand’s postulate suffices for this last choice; for large \(Q\) it is different from \(Q,7,11\). Set \[
\begin{aligned}
 q=q_1=Q,\quad m=m_1=1,\quad k=Q^2,\quad n=p^2,\\
 n_1=1,\quad\ell_1=p^2,\quad\ell=p,\quad Y=X/Q^2=X^{3/4}.
\end{aligned}\] The integer 1 is powerful; \(q_1\) is squarefree and all required coprimalities hold. The choice \(q=X^{1/8}\) is in every stated border interval. The integer \(k\) is cubefree, has the article’s \(k_2=1\), is supported on \(mq\), and is divisible by \(q_1\). Finally, \(n=p^2\le X^{1/2+\varepsilon}/k\) for all large \(X\) since \(a<1/8\). Thus this is a retained term of the later sum.

## The summands cannot cancel the selected positive subfamily

Every \(f\in\mathcal U_Q(\Sigma)\) has a maximal overorder of index \(Q\). Lemma 9.8 and (62), pp. 98–99 (PDF 66–67), give \(E_Q(s,f)\in\{1-Q^{-s},1+Q^{-s},1\}\). With \(x=Q^{-s}\), the local coefficient definition gives \[\sum_j e_{Q,j}(f)Q^{j/2}x^j
 =Qx^2\frac{E_Q(1/2-s,f)}{E_Q(1/2+s,f)}.\] For \(E_Q(s)=1\pm Q^{-s}\), the right-hand side is \(Qx(x\pm Q^{-1/2})/(1\pm Q^{-1/2}x)\), whose \(x^2\) coefficient is \(Q-1\). For \(E_Q=1\) the coefficient is \(Q\). Hence \[e_{Q,2}(f)\in\{1-1/Q,1\}>0.\] At the different prime \(p\), \(f\) is maximal. Expanding the five local Artin factors gives, in the order \((111),(12),(3),(1^21),(1^3)\), \[\lambda_{p^2}(f)=3,1,0,1,0.\] All are nonnegative. On the support of \(\Psi\) the kernel argument is \[\frac{p^2Q^2}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Disc}(f)}}=O(X^{2a-1/4})\longrightarrow0.\] The normalized kernel satisfies \(V^+(y)\to1\). Its real part is therefore at least \(1/2\) uniformly for sufficiently large \(X\). This also covers any initially permitted complex auxiliary smoothing choice; alternatively one can fix an allowed real choice. There is no negative real contribution from the remaining summands.

## An independent uniform counting input

Equation reference: eq:btt. Select those maximal overorders \(K\) for which both \(Q\) and \(p\) are totally split. Theorem 1.3 and Table 1 of the acquired 33-page author PDF of Bhargava–Taniguchi–Thorne [BTT], pp. 2–3, give after partial summation \[

 =c_{Q,p}Y+
 O\!\left(Y^{5/6}+(Qp)^{2/3}Y^{2/3+\tau}\right),
 \qquad c_{Q,p}\ge c>0.\] The fixed conditions at 7, 11 and infinity are absorbed into \(c\) and the implicit constant. The totally split local main density is \(Q^2/[6(Q^2+Q+1)]\), and similarly at \(p\); the secondary densities are bounded. The variable-prime error factors are \((Qp)^{2/3}\). The second error in [eq:btt] (#eq:btt) has \(X\)-exponent \(7/12+2a/3+3\tau/4<3/4\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(\tau>0\). The count is consequently \(\gg Y\).

Each such \(S_3\) field has three index-\(Q\) suborders: they are the preimages of the three pair-equality subalgebras of \(\mathbb F_Q^3\). They are distinct as isomorphism classes because \(\operatorname{Aut}(K)=1\). For each, \(\lambda_{p^2}=3\), \(e_{Q,2}=1-1/Q\), and \(\operatorname{Disc}(f)=Q^2D_K\). Writing \(\mathcal S\) for the left-hand side of Proposition 9.11, we obtain \[|\mathcal S|\ge\Re\mathcal S
 \ge \frac92(1-1/Q)\sum_K\Psi(D_K/Y)
 \gg X^{3/4}.\] The external counting theorem is an identified input whose statement, uniformity and local factors were checked. Its full proof is not claimed as a new derivation here. Crucially, this argument does not use the reviewed article’s disputed congruence counts or secondary residues.

## Contradiction with the printed bound

For [eq:border-parameters] (#eq:border-parameters), the printed \(H\) reduces to \[H=\frac{X^{3/4}}p+
    \frac{X^{5/8+\kappa/3}}{p^{1/3}}+
    pX^{1/4+9\kappa}+X^{3/4-\kappa}.\] After multiplication by \(X^\varepsilon\), the four exponents are \[\frac34-a+\varepsilon,\quad

This is an asymptotic counterexample satisfying the application range, not a numerical experiment or an unproved conjecture about central values.

## Impact, counterevidence and bounded repair

The estimate is used directly in (64), Proposition 9.13, Proposition 9.14, Theorem 9.15 and Corollary 9.16, and hence in the proof of Theorem 9.1 under either hypothesis and in the Section 10 argument. Its wrong savings are therefore essential to the proof as written.

There is meaningful counterevidence against inferring that the final moment theorem must be false. Write \(\ell_1=AB\), where \(A\) is the product of primes occurring exactly once, \(B\) is powerful and \((A,B)=1\). The appropriate first- and second-residue savings concern \(A^{-1}\) and \(A^{-1/3}\). The Fourier error must also change: the prime-square bound in Lemma 5.8 gives the weight \[W=A\operatorname{rad}(B)^2\le\ell_1,\] rather than \(\operatorname{rad}(\ell_1)\). The aggregate estimates \[\begin{aligned}

   &\ll_\varepsilon L^\varepsilon,\\

   &\ll L^{1/6},\\

   &\ll L^{3/2}\end{aligned}\] follow directly. For the first, \(\sum_A A^{-3/2}\) converges and \(\sum_{B\ {\rm powerful},\, B\le L}B^{-1/2}\ll_\varepsilon L^\varepsilon\). For the second, sum \(A^{-5/6}\) up to \(L/B\) and use the convergence of \(\sum_{B\ {\rm powerful}}B^{-2/3}\). For the third, use \(W\le\ell_1\). These observations permit the same polynomial summation exponents at this local stage, with an \(\varepsilon\) loss. They do not address overlaps with \(m_1\), the fixed local conditions, or the correct secondary residue functionals. No complete repaired proof of Theorem 9.1 is claimed. The demonstrated false estimate and its used dependency justify Major classification independently of whether a full repair is possible.

Problem 5 (P05: the Euler product uses global density coefficients as normalized local factors).  Severity: Minor, with the analytic conclusion locally restored.

## Original claim and exact location

The definition on p. 80 (PDF 48) uses additive Haar integrals over the maximal locus, with \(\operatorname{vol}(V(\mathbb Z_p))=1\). On p. 90 (PDF 58) the article sets \(t_\Sigma(n)=\mathcal A^{\max}(\lambda_n\chi_\Sigma)\). Proposition 8.4, p. 91 (PDF 59), then asserts multiplicativity, uses reciprocal finite-orbit cardinalities as local weights, and places \(t_\Sigma(p^j)\) inside every Euler factor. Under the actual definition, \[t_\Sigma(1)=D_\Sigma,\] the global density of the specified maximal locus, and generally \(0<D_\Sigma<1\). For the fixed inert/mixed family above this strict inequality holds. The displayed product over prime powers exactly dividing \(n\) is empty at \(n=1\) and equals 1, already contradicting the definition. Reciprocal orbit cardinality is not the additive Haar mass of the orbit.

## Correct normalization and complete analytic check

Define the genuinely local quantities \[a_p(j)=\int_{V(\mathbb Z_p)^{\max}}
       \chi_{\Sigma,p}(f)\lambda_{p^j}(f)\,df,\qquad
 m_p=a_p(0),\qquad D_\Sigma=\prod_p m_p.\] The local specification here is imposed only on the maximal locus; this definition does not assert the false all-order extension from Problem 3. Nonempty admissible local conditions have \(m_p>0\). Away from finitely many primes, \(m_p=(1-p^{-2})(1-p^{-3})\). The exact coefficient formula is \[t_\Sigma(n)=D_\Sigma\prod_{p^j\parallel n}\frac{a_p(j)}{m_p},
 \qquad
 T_\Sigma(s)=D_\Sigma\prod_p F_p(s),
 \quad
 F_p(s)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{a_p(j)}{m_p}p^{-js}.\] Thus \(t_\Sigma/D_\Sigma\), not \(t_\Sigma\), is normalized multiplicative.

For an unspecified prime \(p\), Proposition 3.9 supplies the raw moments \[a_p(1)=\frac{(p-1)(p^2-1)}{p^4},\qquad
 a_p(2)=\frac{(p^2-1)^2}{p^4}.\] Dividing by \(m_p\) gives \[\frac{a_p(1)}{m_p}=\frac{p}{p^2+p+1},
 \qquad
 \frac{a_p(2)}{m_p}=\frac{p(p+1)}{p^2+p+1}.\] The first is \(O(p^{-1})\) and the second is \(1+O(p^{-2})\). Since \(|\lambda_{p^j}|\le j+1\), for \(\sigma=\Re s>1/3\), \[(1-p^{-2s})F_p(s)
 =1+O\!\left(p^{-1-\sigma}+p^{-2-2\sigma}+p^{-3\sigma}\right),\] locally uniformly. Therefore \[T_\Sigma(s)=D_\Sigma\zeta(2s)H_\Sigma(s),\] where \(H_\Sigma\) is holomorphic for \(\Re s>1/3\). The finitely specified local factors are holomorphic there: their Artin denominators have no zero for \(|p^{-s}|<1\).

At \(s=1/2\), each \(F_p\) is positive. Indeed, putting \(x=p^{-1/2}\in(0,1)\), it is the normalized positive average of the local factors \[(1-x)^{-2},\quad(1-x^2)^{-1},\quad
 (1+x+x^2)^{-1},\quad(1-x)^{-1},\quad1.\] The absolutely convergent corrected product consequently gives \[R_\Sigma:=\operatorname{Res}_{s=1/2}T_\Sigma(s)
 =\frac{D_\Sigma}{2}\prod_p(1-p^{-1})F_p(1/2)>0.\] This restores Proposition 8.4’s analytic assertion. It preserves the implicitly defined \(C_\Sigma=\alpha^\pm R_\Sigma\) in (54) and the derivative constant in the Mellin expansion of Proposition 8.5, since it is a correct representation of the original Dirichlet series.

## Why the printed residue recipe is false

With the actual global coefficients, \[\sum_{j\ge0}t_\Sigma(p^j)p^{-j/2}=D_\Sigma F_p(1/2).\] The printed finite products would therefore be \[\frac12\prod_{p\le P}\left[(1-p^{-1})
      \sum_{j\ge0}t_\Sigma(p^j)p^{-j/2}\right]
 =\frac{D_\Sigma^{\pi(P)}}2
      \prod_{p\le P}(1-p^{-1})F_p(1/2).\] They tend to zero when \(0<D_\Sigma<1\), in contradiction with \(R_\Sigma>0\) above.

## Impact and repair boundary

The error affects a displayed multiplicativity claim and an explicit residue recipe. The complete normalization correction retains the global density once, uses \(a_p(j)/m_p\) in the Euler factors and uses actual Haar masses at the finitely specified primes. Minor classification rests on the proved preservation of the analytic assertion and the implicitly defined leading and derivative constants. It does not rest on the error being repairable or on its failure to overturn a whole theorem. The independent counting and border-range problems remain outside this correction.

Problem 6 (P06: unrestricted unweighted switching neglects automorphism orbits).  Severity: Minor on the verified downstream path.

## Original claim and exact location

Proposition 6.1, p. 73 (PDF 41), and Theorem 6.5, p. 75 (PDF 43), state unweighted sums over cubic-form orbit sets before the \(S_3\) restriction of Section 6.2. The intermediate bijection of Lemma 6.2 is a bijection of isomorphism classes of pairs. The roots of a representative must themselves be divided by its automorphism action when such pairs are counted as isomorphism classes.

## An exact discriminant-four counterexample

Set \(q=2\). Choose a nonnegative smooth \(\Psi\) supported in a sufficiently small interval about 4 containing no other positive integer, with \(\Psi(4)=1\). In the right-hand side of Proposition 6.1, the terms with the \(2^4\) scaling require a discriminant near \(1/4\) and vanish. The term with the \(2^2\) scaling comes from \(\mathbb Z^3\), of discriminant 1, represented by \(xy(x-y)\). It has three projective roots over \(\mathbb F_2\), so the right side is 3.

For completeness, every nonmaximal-at-2 ring \(R\) of discriminant 4 has index \(j\ge2\) in its maximal order \(O\) and \(4=j^2\operatorname{Disc}(O)\). Positivity and integrality force \(j=2\) and \(\operatorname{Disc}(O)=1\). A cubic field of discriminant 1 is impossible: Minkowski’s ideal bound would produce an integral ideal of norm at most \((3!/3^3)\sqrt1=2/9<1\). A product of \(\mathbb Q\) and a quadratic field cannot have discriminant 1 by the quadratic discriminant formula. Hence the only possible maximal algebra is \(\mathbb Q^3\) and \(O=\mathbb Z^3\).

An index-two subring of \(\mathbb Z^3\) contains \(2\mathbb Z^3\). It corresponds to a two-dimensional unital subalgebra of \(\mathbb F_2^3\). There are exactly three, defined by equality of one pair of coordinates. The group \(S_3=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z^3)\) permutes these transitively. There is therefore one isomorphism class on the left side, represented by \(xy(x-2y)\) of discriminant 4. The displayed identity would give \(1=3\).

The automorphism orders are 2 for this subring and 6 for \(\mathbb Z^3\). With reciprocal-automorphism weights both sides are \(1/2\). This agrees with the pair-mass proof in Problem 2 and is corroborated by a direct exact enumeration of the three subalgebras and their single \(S_3\) orbit.

## Impact, counterevidence and repair

Reciprocal stabilizer weights restore the identity. Alternatively, the statement can be restricted to invariant support on which all stabilizers are trivial. The later \(S_3\) applications have precisely that property, so this specific defect does not invalidate their switching step. The original cited switching literature uses its own weighted/irreducible counting convention [BST]; it does not justify the unrestricted unweighted extension. Minor severity follows from this verified downstream restriction, rather than from a general rule that repairable defects are minor.

Problem 7 (P07: the uniform error omits the norm of a removed local scalar).  Severity: Minor on the verified downstream path.

## Original claim and exact location

Theorem 6.6, p. 77 (PDF 45), permits complex-valued invariant tensor functions \(\phi\) with \(S_3\) support, simple at \(d=(q,n)\) and with \(\phi_p(0)=0\) for \(p\mid d\). There is no unit-norm hypothesis. The displayed error contains the Fourier norm of \(\phi_{n/d}\) but omits \(|\phi_d(1^21)|\), although the latter scalar is present in the switching formula in its proof. The implied constant is specified as depending on \(\varepsilon\), not on that missing scalar.

## A homogeneity counterexample with all hypotheses fixed

Take \(n=5\cdot7\cdot11\), \(q=d=5\). At 5 use the indicator of type \((1^21)\), at 7 the indicator of type \((3)\), and at 11 the indicator of type \((12)\). The function is simple at 5, vanishes at the zero type there, and is an \(S_3\)-function. Multiply only its 5-factor by a real number \(M>0\). The factor \(\phi_{n/d}\) stays fixed.

For the unscaled function define the positive constants \[a=\alpha^-\mathcal A_n^{(q)}(\phi)>0,\qquad
 c=-\gamma^-\mathcal C_n^{(q)}(\phi)>0.\] Their signs follow from the nonempty local type sets, their positive Haar masses and table values, and \(\gamma^-<0\). Choose a fixed number \[0<b<\min\{1,(c/(2a))^6\},\] and a nonzero smooth nonnegative \(\Psi\) supported in \((b/2,b)\). Set \(X=1\), which is allowed by the theorem. Every nonzero integral discriminant has absolute value at least 1, so the left side is identically zero for every \(M\). The stated main terms are \[M\left(a\int_0^\infty\Psi(x)\,dx
       -c\int_0^\infty x^{-1/6}\Psi(x)\,dx\right)
 <-Ma\int_0^\infty\Psi(x)\,dx.\] The asserted error is independent of \(M\), since \(n,q,d,\Psi\) and \(\phi_{n/d}\) are fixed. Letting \(M\to\infty\) is a contradiction. This proof tests the literal coefficients and uniformity. It does not assume that the secondary functional criticized in Problem 2 is a correct residue.

## Impact and exact repair boundary

The switching formula requires multiplication of the error by \(|\phi_d(1^21)|\), or an explicit bound on that scalar. This restores the missing homogeneity; it does not repair the independent residue issue. For the ordinary \(\lambda_{p^j}\) applications at type \((1^21)\), the removed factor is 1. Thus this omission, by itself, does not enlarge that application’s bound. The proved limitation of its downstream effect is the basis for Minor severity. General tensor functions still require the stated norm correction.
