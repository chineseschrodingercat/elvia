Abstract: The highest supported assessment is Major. Two displayed steps in the construction are invalid: the Harnack proof applies discrete energy to an admissible density instead of its potential, and the uniform capacity proof interchanges the coarse level with the refinement depth. A third problem is a false direction of covariance in the stated energy-measure theorem. Complete counterarguments and local repairs are given. These findings do not establish that the corrected Harnack theorem, the existence of the Sobolev space, or the conditional attainment results are false. A separate vertex-count normalization defect is Minor. The selected 93-page author manuscript was read in full; the later long manuscript was consulted only at specified passages. This is an initial scientific review. Scientific decision and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Conventions needed for the checks

For a graph \(G=(V,E)\), the paper uses \[\mathcal E_p^G(u)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\in E}|u(x)-u(y)|^p.\] A path \([x_0,\ldots,x_\ell]\) has length \(\ell\), while its weighted length is the sum of a nonnegative density over its vertices. Thus modulus is a vertex \(\ell^p\) minimization, not the discrete energy of the density [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~14--17]{short}].

For the carpet, \(V_n=\{1,\ldots,8\}^n\), and vertices are adjacent when the corresponding closed carpet cells meet. The word \(1\) denotes the lower-left subsquare and \(5\) the upper-right subsquare. For a word \(w\), \(S^k(w)\) is the family of its descendants \(k\) levels deeper. The probability measure \(m\) gives each level-\(n\) cell mass \(8^{-n}\). Put \[d_f=\frac{\log 8}{\log 3},\qquad
 d_w(p)=\frac{\log(8\rho(p))}{\log3}.\] The pushforward convention is the usual one: \((T_*\nu)(A)=\nu(T^{-1}(A))\). This is explicit in the setwise form of equation (8.16), page 60.

# Problem 1: the Harnack estimate uses the energy of the wrong function

## Claim, location, and impact

Equation reference: bad-harnack. In the proof of Theorem 5.4, page 25, write \[\begin{aligned}
 h_\epsilon&=h+\epsilon,\qquad D=\log M-\log m,\\
 u&=h_\epsilon^*=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{\log h_\epsilon-\log m}{D}\right),\\
 v(x)&=\widetilde h_\epsilon^*(x)=\max_{y\sim x}|u(x)-u(y)|.
\end{aligned}\] The density \(v\) is admissible for paths between the low and high level sets. The first inequality in the displayed equation (5.4) nevertheless uses \[
 \operatorname{Mod}_p^G(\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max};\delta B)
 \le C\,\mathcal E^G_{p,\delta B}(v).\] Here \(C\) is the constant of Lemma 2.12 and depends on \(p\) and the degree bound, not on the radius. An admissible density controls modulus through its vertex \(\ell^p\) mass. Its discrete energy, which differentiates it once more, need not control modulus.

The following counterexample satisfies the actual hypotheses of Theorem 5.4. It shows that no fixed constant can make [bad-harnack] (#bad-harnack) valid. The defect is Major as a proof step: the estimate is used to derive Harnack, then Hölder cutoff regularity in Theorem 5.6, the uniform cutoff condition in Proposition 6.12, and the regularity part of the Sobolev construction. A valid local replacement is given below; no false-Harnack conclusion is asserted.

## All structural hypotheses on one fixed graph

Take the nearest-neighbor graph on \(\mathbb Z\), \(p=2\), \(d_f=1\), and \(\beta=2\). It is connected, simple, locally finite, and has degree two. For \(r\ge1\), \[\#B(x,r)=2\lceil r\rceil-1,\qquad
 r\le \#B(x,r)\le3r.\] Thus \(\mathrm{AR}(1)\) holds with fixed constants.

For two radius-\(r\) balls separated by at most \(\kappa r\), a shortest connecting path has at most \((\kappa+3)r\) distinct vertices; if the balls overlap, a nontrivial path through the intersection can be used. Every admissible density has squared mass at least the reciprocal of this vertex count by Cauchy–Schwarz. The same path has diameter at most \((\kappa+2)r\). Hence the graph satisfies \(\mathrm{BCL}_2(-1)\) with fixed constants, and \(-1=1-p=d_f-\beta<1\).

For the capacity condition, take a function equal to one on \(B(x,r)\), zero outside \(B(x,2r)\), and linear in the intervening distance. Each edge difference is at most \(1/r\), and at most \(4r+2\) edges contribute. Its energy is at most \(6/r\), which is at most \(6\#B(x,r)/r^2\). Replacing this function by its complement matches the paper’s orientation of the condenser. Thus \(\operatorname{cap}_{2,\le}(2)\) holds uniformly. These verifications use no external theorem and no assumption peculiar to the carpet.

## The positive harmonic family and its exact modulus

Equation reference: mMq. Let \(L\ge2\) be any fixed integer, as may be chosen after increasing the constant used in the proof, and set \[\delta_H=\frac1{8L},\qquad R=8Ln,\qquad
 \delta=4\delta_HL=\frac12,\qquad n\ge2,\qquad \epsilon=1.\] Define, at every integer \(j\), \[h(j)=2R+\max(-R,\min(j,R)).\] This function is strictly positive everywhere and affine on the ball \(B=B(0,R)\). It is therefore \(2\)-harmonic on every vertex of that ball, including those whose neighbors meet its boundary. The small ball is \(B(0,n)\), so \[
 m=(16L-1)n+2,\qquad M=(16L+1)n,\qquad q=M-m=2n-2>0.\] Choose the low path from \(-n+1\) to \(-R+1\) and the high path from \(n-1\) to \(R-1\). They meet the specified inner and outer boundaries and lie respectively in \(\{h_\epsilon\le m\}\) and \(\{h_\epsilon\ge M\}\), exactly as required on page 25.

Equation reference: mod-exact. Interpret the notation for paths in \(\delta B\) in its natural, most favorable sense: their endpoints belong to the portions of those two paths inside \(\delta B\). Every such connecting path visits all \(q+1=2n-1\) vertices of \[I=\{-n+1,-n+2,\ldots,n-1\}.\] The path consisting of \(I\) itself is allowed. Cauchy–Schwarz gives a lower bound \(1/(q+1)\) for any admissible squared mass, and the density \(1/(q+1)\) on \(I\), zero elsewhere, attains it. Consequently, \[
 \operatorname{Mod}_2^G(\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max};\delta B)=\frac1{q+1}.\]

## The displayed energy is smaller by an unbounded factor

Equation reference: energy-density, energy-upper, ratio. Set \[a_j=\frac{\log((m+j+1)/(m+j))}{\log(M/m)},\qquad 0\le j\le q-1.\] These are the positive, decreasing successive increments of \(u\) across \(I\). The values of \(v\) on \(I\) are \[a_0,\ a_0,\ a_1,\ldots,a_{q-1},\] and \(v\) vanishes outside \(I\). All contributing edges are far inside \(\delta B\), so adding or removing its exterior graph boundary has no effect on this calculation. Direct evaluation gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E^G_{2,\delta B}(v)
 &=a_0^2+a_{q-1}^2+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}(a_j-a_{j-1})^2 \\
 &\le3a_0^2
 \le3\left(\frac{M}{mq}\right)^2. \end{aligned}\] The first bound follows because the nonnegative differences telescope; the second uses \(\log(1+t)\le t\) and \(\log(1+t)\ge t/(1+t)\) for \(t\ge0\). Since \(m/M\ge31/33\), equations [mod-exact] (#mod-exact)–[energy-upper] (#energy-upper) imply \[
 \frac{\operatorname{Mod}_2^G(\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max};\delta B)}
 {\mathcal E^G_{2,\delta B}(v)}
 \ge \left(\frac{31}{33}\right)^2\frac{q^2}{3(q+1)}
 \longrightarrow\infty.\] The graph, exponent, degree bound, and geometric constants are fixed. A larger constant \(C\) cannot repair the claimed inequality.

## Verified local repair and alternative readings

Equation reference: harnack-repair. Use \(u=h_\epsilon^*\) as a capacitary potential. It equals zero on the low set and one on the high set. These sets are disjoint because \(m<M\). The modulus–capacity comparison and the \(1\)-Lipschitz truncation give, on the relevant local graph, \[
 \operatorname{Mod}_p^G(\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max};\delta B)
 \le C\operatorname{cap}_p^G(\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max};\delta B)
 \le C\mathcal E^G_{p,\delta B}(u)
 \le C D^{-p}\mathcal E^G_{p,\delta B}(\log h_\epsilon).\] If the local-energy convention includes the exterior boundary, the same argument on that enlarged induced graph gives the corresponding inequality. This restores the estimate needed before the log-Caccioppoli bound (5.5), with harmless changes to fixed constants. It is a general analytic replacement, not merely a check on \(\mathbb Z\).

The symbol \(v\) cannot consistently be read as \(u\): page 25 defines both separately, and the actual PDF shows the tilde in (5.4). Nor can \(\mathcal E(v)\) be read as \(\sum v^p\), because Definition 2.5 explicitly specifies edge differences. A likely notation slip is a plausible explanation of how the error arose, but the displayed essential inequality is false. The later long manuscript retains the same expression in (5.12), page 48 [long]; it supplies no correction at that location. This review verifies only the local replacement [harnack-repair] (#harnack-repair), not a separately rewritten proof of every downstream result.

# Problem 2: the capacity cutoff is built at the wrong scale

## Claim and an admissible finite example

Proposition 8.8(ii), page 53, is the step that establishes the uniform capacity upper bound used to verify Assumption 6.15 for the carpet. The proof defines \(n(R)\) by \[2\cdot3^{n(R)}<R\le2\cdot3^{n(R)+1},\] then chooses \(w\in V_{n(R)}\) and functions \(\varphi_w\) equal to one on \(S^{n-n(R)}(w)\). It defines \[\mathcal N(x,R)=
 \{w\in V_{n(R)}:B_{d_n}(x,R)\cap S^{n-n(R)}(w)\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 \varphi=\sum_{w\in\mathcal N(x,R)}\varphi_w,\] and asserts \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset B_{d_n}(x,2R)\). This support assertion fails independently of the choice of the minimizers.

Take \[n=4,\qquad R=7,\qquad x=1111,\qquad y=1555.\] Then \(n(R)=1\). Encode a level-four cell by the integer coordinates of its lower-left corner in the \(81\) by \(81\) grid. The eight allowed ternary digits are \[(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2),(1,2),(0,2),(0,1),\] in the paper’s word order. Thus \(x\) has coordinates \((0,0)\), \(y\) has coordinates \((26,26)\), and \(5555\) has coordinates \((80,80)\). Adjacent closed cells change each grid coordinate by at most one. It follows that \[d_4(x,y)\ge26>14=2R,\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}G_4\ge d_4(x,5555)\ge80>R.\] Therefore \(R\in[2a_*+1,\operatorname{diam}G_4)\), as required in the proof, and every parameter is in its stated range.

Equation reference: support-failure. Because \(x\in S^3(1)\), the coarse word \(1\) belongs to \(\mathcal N(x,7)\). Also \(y\in S^3(1)\), so every permitted \(\varphi_1\) has \(\varphi_1(y)=1\). All the summands are nonnegative. Hence \[
 \varphi(y)\ge1,\qquad y\notin B_{d_4}(x,14).\] This contradicts the stated support property. Clipping the sum at one would leave \(\varphi(y)=1\) and would not fix the scale mismatch.

A separate reviewer-authored reconstruction of the 4096-vertex graph gives \(d_4(1111,1555)=35\) and \(d_4(1111,5555)=107\). These finite graph calculations corroborate [support-failure] (#support-failure); the coordinate lower bounds already prove it without graph enumeration.

## A complete local replacement for the capacity argument

The refinement depth must correspond to the graph radius. The coarse word length is the remaining depth. The following choice also leaves enough space for neighboring cells.

Equation reference: k-scale. Use the constants \(A_1=3\), \(A_2=4\) verified in Proposition 8.4(iv), page 52. Put \(C_0=2A_1+A_2=10\). For \(R>60\), choose \(k\ge1\) such that \[
 20\cdot3^k<R\le60\cdot3^k.\] Since \(R<\operatorname{diam}G_n\le2\cdot3^n\), we have \(k<n\). Put \(j=n-k\) and define the fibers \(Q_w=S^k(w)\) for \(w\in V_j\).

For each \(w\), take a capacitary cutoff \(\psi_w\) equal to one on \(Q_w\), zero outside \[S^k(B_{d_j}(w,2)),\] and between zero and one. The definition of \(C_p^{(k)}\) in (8.3), and (8.6), give \[\mathcal E_p^{G_n}(\psi_w)\le C_p^{(k)}
 \le C\rho(p)^{-k}.\] This uses a finite condenser, so a minimizer exists also when \(p=1\); uniqueness is not needed. Reversing zero and one does not change its energy.

Let \(\mathcal N\) be the set of \(w\in V_j\) for which \(Q_w\) meets \(B_{d_n}(x,R)\), and set \[\psi=\min\left(1,\sum_{w\in\mathcal N}\psi_w\right).\] Then \(\psi=1\) on \(B_{d_n}(x,R)\). If \(\psi_w(y)>0\), the point \(y\) lies in a fiber indexed by either \(w\) or a neighbor of \(w\). Choose \(z\in Q_w\cap B_{d_n}(x,R)\). The two fiber-radius bounds in (6.3) and the center-distance bound (6.4) imply \[d_n(z,y)\le(2A_1+A_2)3^k=C_0\,3^k<R/2.\] Thus \(\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset B_{d_n}(x,3R/2)\subset B_{d_n}(x,2R)\).

Equation reference: capacity-repair. For completeness, the number of summands is uniformly bounded. The centers of the fibers in \(\mathcal N\) are within \(R+A_1\,3^k\) of \(x\), and their disjoint fibers contain balls of radius \(3^k/A_1\). Uniform Ahlfors regularity and [k-scale] (#k-scale) bound the number of these disjoint balls by a constant independent of \(x,R,n\). Hence \(\#\mathcal N\le N_0\) with a fixed \(N_0\). Convexity on each edge and the \(1\)-Lipschitz truncation yield \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_p^{G_n}(\psi)
 &\le(\#\mathcal N)^{p-1}\sum_{w\in\mathcal N}
           \mathcal E_p^{G_n}(\psi_w)\\
 &\le C N_0^p\rho(p)^{-k}
 =C N_0^p(3^k)^{d_f-d_w(p)}
 \le C' R^{d_f-d_w(p)}
 \le C''\frac{\#B_{d_n}(x,R)}{R^{d_w(p)}}. \end{aligned}\] The constants may depend on \(p\) and the fixed geometry, as allowed. The comparison of powers is valid whether \(d_f-d_w(p)\) is positive, zero, or negative, since \(R/3^k\) lies in a fixed interval.

For \(1\le R\le60\), the indicator of \(B_{d_n}(x,R)\) is a cutoff supported in \(B_{d_n}(x,2R)\) and has energy at most \(L_*\#B_{d_n}(x,R)\). Increasing the constant by \(60^{d_w(p)}\) gives the same required estimate. Together these two cases prove the local replacement of Proposition 8.8(ii), using its stated geometric and capacity-scaling inputs.

## Counterevidence, version comparison, and severity

The words and descendants are explicitly defined on page 50, so \(S^{n-n(R)}(w)\) cannot be read as a fiber of depth \(n(R)\) without changing the displayed construction. The failure [support-failure] (#support-failure) also holds if one replaces the sum by a maximum or clips it. The later long version repeats the same construction on page 99. Its subsequent Loewner argument on page 103 correctly uses words in \(V_{n-n(R)}\) and descendants \(S^{n(R)}\), supporting the intended distinction between the two scales [long].

This is Major as a construction/proof defect. The selected cutoff is not admissible for the ball condenser, so its asserted properties cannot prove the uniform capacity bound. That bound is an input to the Harnack/cutoff results and to Theorem 8.3(b), which supplies the assumptions used in the core Sobolev construction. Equation [capacity-repair] (#capacity-repair) restores this particular argument. It does not refute the uniform capacity theorem or certify every subsequent proof. The distinction in the scale indices, and the replacement of \(\rho(p)^{-n}\) by the refinement-scale factor \(\rho(p)^{-k}\), must be made in the proof rather than silently assumed.

# Problem 3: the energy-measure covariance has the inverse in the wrong place

## The stated identity and the actual set transport

Equation reference: bad-covariance, right-covariance. Theorem 1.2(v), page 6, and Proposition 8.15(e), page 61, assert, for all \(f\in\mathcal F_p\) and all \(\Phi\in D_4\), \[
 \Phi_*\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle=\Gamma_p\langle f\circ\Phi\rangle.\] Definition 8.1(3), page 49, includes the quarter-turn rotations, so these maps need not be involutions. Under the pushforward convention explicitly used in (8.16), the correct identity is \[
 \Gamma_p\langle f\circ\Phi\rangle=(\Phi^{-1})_*\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 \Phi_*\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle=\Gamma_p\langle f\circ\Phi^{-1}\rangle.\]

On page 61, the proof defines \(C_{n,\Phi}\) through cells and their coding cylinders. The cylinders meet \(\chi^{-1}(\Phi(A))\). For closed \(A\), its displayed sums give \[m_p\langle f\circ\Phi\rangle(\Sigma_{C_n})
 =m_p\langle f\rangle(\Sigma_{C_{n,\Phi}}).\] Taking the decreasing-cylinder limits gives \[\Gamma_p\langle f\circ\Phi\rangle(A)=\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle(\Phi(A)),\] which is [right-covariance] (#right-covariance). In the final line the paper instead replaces the limiting set \(\chi^{-1}(\Phi(A))\) by \(\chi^{-1}(\Phi^{-1}(A))\). Regularity extends the correct equality from closed sets to all Borel sets. The scalar symmetry \(\mathcal E_p(f\circ\Phi)=\mathcal E_p(f)\) used earlier does not change this set-transport direction.

## A cutoff counterexample on the actual carpet

Fix any \(p\in(1,\infty)\) and let \(a=(-1,-1)\) be the lower-left corner. Let \(\Phi\) be the rotation through \(\pi/2\). In the normalized Euclidean metric of Definition 8.1, the points \(\Phi a\) and \(\Phi^{-1}a\) are distance one apart.

Take \(r=1/16\), let \(C=\overline{B_d(a,2r)}\), and choose the continuous cutoff \(f=\psi_{a,r}\in\mathcal F_p\cap C(K)\) supplied by Proposition 6.21, pages 38–39. It equals one on \(B_d(a,r)\) and vanishes outside \(C\). The ball is proper and there is a nonempty open region outside \(C\). Since \(m\) has full support, \(f\) is nonconstant even as an \(L^p(m)\) class. Thus \[\nu:=\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle,\qquad \nu(K)=\mathcal E_p(f)>0.\] Weak locality, Lemma 8.16, gives \(\nu(K\setminus C)=0\): on that open set \(f\) agrees with zero. No assumption that an ordinary coordinate function lies in \(\mathcal F_p\) is needed.

Equation reference: cov-counter. Now \(g=f\circ\Phi\) is continuous and belongs to \(\mathcal F_p\) by the scalar symmetry, and it vanishes outside \(\Phi^{-1}C\). The closed sets \(\Phi C\) and \(\Phi^{-1}C\) are disjoint: their centers are distance one apart and each has radius \(1/8\). The set \(\Phi C\) has an open neighborhood on which \(g=0\). Weak locality therefore yields \[\Gamma_p\langle g\rangle(\Phi C)=0.\] On the other hand, \[
 (\Phi_*\nu)(\Phi C)=\nu(C)=\nu(K)=\mathcal E_p(f)>0.\] Equations [bad-covariance] (#bad-covariance) and [cov-counter] (#cov-counter) are incompatible. This uses only the paper’s cutoff, nondegeneracy, invariance of \(\mathcal F_p\), and weak locality properties; it does not assume the disputed covariance formula.

## Repair, valid special cases, and impact

Replacing either occurrence of \(\Phi\) in [bad-covariance] (#bad-covariance) by its inverse gives [right-covariance] (#right-covariance), which follows from the cylinder construction as shown above. The printed formula is valid for reflections and the half-turn, since \(\Phi^{-1}=\Phi\) in those cases. Those special cases do not cover \(D_4\). A different pushforward convention is excluded by the explicit inverse-image formula on page 60.

The actual images confirm the missing inverse in both the introductory theorem and the proof. The later long version retains it on page 8 and in the argument on pages 112–113 [long]. No author adoption of the correction was observed in the acquired versions; the final journal version and unacquired revisions remain outside this claim.

This finding is Major for the stated core covariance guarantee: one of the advertised properties of the constructed energy-measure family is false, with distinct masses on a concrete Borel set. The short inverse correction does not change the evidence for that failure. The existence, total mass, self-similarity, and scalar symmetry of the energy remain separate results, and no downstream collapse is asserted.

# Problem 4: vertex modulus requires \(L+1\), not \(L\)

Lemma 2.4, equation (2.2), page 16, states that the existence of a path of length at most \(L\) implies \(\operatorname{Mod}_p^G(\Theta)\ge L^{1-p}\). Page 14 defines length as the number of edges, while pages 14–15 sum a density over vertices.

Take the graph with two vertices \(a,b\) joined by one edge, let \(\Theta\) contain that path, and put \(p=2\), \(L=1\). All graph and parameter hypotheses hold. Its modulus is \[\inf_{\rho(a),\rho(b)\ge0,\ \rho(a)+\rho(b)\ge1}
      \bigl(\rho(a)^2+\rho(b)^2\bigr)=\frac12,\] attained by \(\rho(a)=\rho(b)=1/2\). The printed lower bound is one.

Equation reference: vertex-repair. The corrected universal bound is \[
 \operatorname{Mod}_p^G(\Theta)\ge(L+1)^{1-p},\qquad p\ge1.\] To prove it, choose the given path, whose number \(s\) of distinct vertices is at most \(L+1\). Hölder’s inequality gives \[1\le\sum_{\theta}\rho
 \le s^{1-1/p}\left(\sum_{\theta}\rho^p\right)^{1/p}.\] Thus \(\sum_V\rho^p\ge s^{1-p}\ge(L+1)^{1-p}\). For \(p=1\) the same result follows directly, and for a simple length-\(L\) path with no other paths the uniform density attains equality.

The actual PDF confirms both conventions, so this is not an extraction ambiguity. Reading \(L\) as a vertex count would give a valid alternate statement but would conflict with the defined length. Exact rational checks for \(p=2\), \(L=1,2,3,10\) are retained. The uses on pages 19–21 require only a fixed multiplicative bound, and \((L+1)^{1-p}\ge2^{1-p}L^{1-p}\) for \(L\ge1\). No change to the scaling exponent or central conclusion has been demonstrated. The assessment is therefore Minor, unlike the directly core steps in Problems 1–3.

# Other checked reasoning and limits

The weak monotonicity argument in Theorem 6.13 has the expected structure: the coarse difference is bounded by a fine-scale ball average, Poincaré supplies the factor \(3^{\ell(\beta-d_f)}\), and separation of fiber centers bounds the overlap when the local energies are summed. The subsequent \(\Gamma\)-limit construction uses recovery sequences and weak monotonicity for two-sided comparison with the discrete supremum; the Clarkson estimates give an equivalent uniformly convex norm. The separability step uses the continuous injection into the separable \(L^p(m)\) space together with reflexivity. These arguments were read and their dependencies traced; the cited abstract compactness theorems were not independently reproved.

For the Korevaar–Schoen comparison, the partition operators \(A_r\) converge in \(L^p\), while their energies are controlled by the small-scale difference quotients. Weak compactness and convex combinations then give the density of continuous functions in \(\mathcal F_p\). In using the global converse for an initially arbitrary \(L^p\) function with finite liminf energy, one first obtains a bounded sequence of \(A_r f\) in \(\mathcal F_p\) and identifies its weak limit with \(f\) through the \(L^p\) convergence. This supplies the finite-energy membership step implicit in the concise proof. The review found no independent counterexample to this argument.

The self-similarization in (8.14) averages the iterates of the energy operator. Uniform comparison and a diagonal subsequence on a dense set give a pointwise limiting seminorm. The difference between its shifted and unshifted Cesàro averages is a bounded endpoint term divided by the number of terms, which tends to zero. This explains why the proposed repairs to the earlier estimates can preserve the self-similar construction.

The cylinder masses in Section 8.2 are consistent by the scalar self-similarity identity. Weak locality follows by considering sufficiently small cells inside an open set. The chain-rule proof was read through its local \(C^1\) truncations and inverse-function argument. Continuity of \(f\) is retained where the paper requires it; the review does not extend these statements to arbitrary representatives of \(L^p(m)\) classes.

An apparent additional concern about Proposition 8.22 was rejected after visual inspection. Equation (8.27), page 66, has \(\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle(\overline U)\), with a closure that was lost in the plain-text extraction. The neighborhood-cover argument on page 67 is consistent with that closure. The review does not claim the stronger bound with \(\Gamma_p\langle f\rangle(U)\) for every Borel \(U\).

The Loewner comparison remains conditional on Assumption 9.16. The two-weight estimates lead to comparison on balls; Lemma 9.23 and its Whitney-cover proof give comparison of measures. The norm comparison is proved on continuous functions before extending to the completions. This matters because the two reference measures need not be comparable and their almost-everywhere equivalence classes are different.
