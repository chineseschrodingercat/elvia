Abstract: This review concerns the 29-page preprint by Hesam Rajabzadeh and Pedram Safaee, arXiv:2309.05175v1 (11 September 2023), associated with DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1687. The prescribed geometric realization in Section 4.1 does not define the claimed side pairing. An explicit genus-two example, and a calculation valid for arbitrarily large primes, show that a horizontal-modulo interpretation does not remove the obstruction. This is a Major defect in the stated proof construction, rather than a counterexample to positivity itself. The positive-proportion display (5.5) is a second Major error in the main proof chain. It admits a verified conditional repair from the exact ambient dimension already stated, which does not remove the failure of the printed derivation.

# Source, hypotheses and coverage

The supplied source is the arXiv v1, not an assumed copy of the journal article published online on 15 August 2025. All 29 pages were read as extracted text, including the bibliography; fifteen selected source pages were also inspected as images. The main result retains \(g>1\), an irreducible Rauzy class, and the two specified invariant measures. The full-torus average exponent and the exponent for the ergodic homological subtorus measure are distinguished.

The review followed the chain from the twisted matrices (3.3), rational-fiber measures and Proposition 3.9, through the stopping-time construction, untwisting lemma, geometric realization, genus/singularity bounds, Proposition 5.2 and the limiting argument. The root-of-unity cancellation in (4.12)–(4.17) and the Vandermonde spanning argument were checked. For Proposition 3.9, balanced tower heights relate the induction time to \(\log N\); subexponential return blocks control the sum of tower contributions. The case of zero growth rate can be handled with the same positive-\(\epsilon\) bounds before letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).

The EKZ sum estimate, Chaika–Eskin directional regularity, affine orbit-closure theorem, and Rauzy–Veech group classification are external inputs, not independently reproved here. The discussion below does not certify every large-deviation or correlation estimate in Proposition 3.4, nor every application in Section 6.

# Confirmed findings

Problem 1 (Major: the prescribed geometric realization is not a side pairing).  The location is p. 18, Section 4.1, from the choice of \(\lambda^*\) through the top–bottom identification of the square. Equation (4.2), p. 15, gives \[F_p(\widehat\lambda,s)
 =\left(\frac{s}{p}\widehat\lambda,1-\frac{s}{p}\right).\] Equation (4.20), p. 19, correspondingly gives \(|\widetilde I|=1-s\). Nevertheless, Section 4.1 chooses \[s_*=\frac{p+1}{2p}
 \quad\hbox{and asserts}\quad |\widetilde I|=\frac12.\] The actual length is \((p-1)/(2p)\). Moreover, for \(P=[0,\sqrt2/2]^2\), the printed top–bottom map is \[(x,\sqrt2/2)\longmapsto
 \left(\sqrt2\,S_{\vec n,p}(x/\sqrt2)-\sqrt2,0\right).
 \tag{2.1}\] Since \(S_{\vec n,p}\) takes values in \([0,1)\), its first coordinate is negative. Taken literally, (2.1) does not map the top side into the bottom side.

Counterexample satisfying the geometric hypotheses. Take \[\pi=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4\\4&3&2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \widehat\lambda=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad
 \vec n=(1,1,1,1).\] This permutation is irreducible and satisfies \(\pi_b\pi_t^{-1}(4)=1\). Its intersection matrix has entries \(-1\) above the diagonal and \(1\) below it, and determinant \(1\); thus its rank is \(4\) and \(g=2\). For every prime \(p\ge3\), put \[a=\frac{p+1}{6p^2},\quad
 s=\frac{p+1}{2p},\quad
 \lambda^*=(a,a,a,1-3a).\] All coordinates are positive, sum to one, and \(\lambda^*_4>(p-1)/p\); indeed they also satisfy the stronger bound \(\lambda^*_4>p/(p+1)\) used at the start of the proof on p. 16. The condition \(p\nmid n_{\alpha_t}\) holds. Since the integer observable \(\vec n\) is constantly one, its first zero sum modulo \(p\) occurs exactly at time \(p\), so \(S_{\vec n,p}=T_{\lambda^*,\pi}^{p}\).

A modulo interpretation still fails. The left–right identification might suggest reading the bottom coordinate modulo the square width. In the normalized coordinate \(u=x/\sqrt2\), that would make the bottom map \(S(u)\bmod(1/2)\). Define \[e=\frac{1}{2p^2},\qquad
 u_1=2a-\frac e2,\qquad
 u_2=\frac12-\frac e2 .\] These are distinct interior points of \((0,1/2)\). The point \(u_1\) lies in the second small interval. The first iterate moves it into the long interval, where the remaining \(p-1\) iterates subtract \(3a\) each. Consequently \[S(u_1)=u_1+1-s.\] The point \(u_2\) lies in \(T^{-(p-1)}(I_1)\); its first \(p-1\) iterates stay in the long interval before the last step uses \(I_1\). Thus \[S(u_2)=u_2+1-s+2a,\qquad S(u_2)-S(u_1)=\frac12.\] Their bottom images modulo \(1/2\) coincide. The points are inside continuity intervals, and the two image intervals overlap in positive length. This is not a harmless identification of polygon vertices.

For \(p=3\), the data are \[\lambda^*=(2/27,2/27,2/27,7/9),\quad
 (u_1,u_2)=(13/108,17/36),\quad
 (S(u_1),S(u_2))=(49/108,103/108).\] An independently written exact-fraction computation reproduced these values and the corresponding collisions for \(p=5,7,101\); no author code was executed. The general calculation above establishes failure for arbitrarily large primes, and the positive-length overlap persists under small parameter perturbations preserving the branch itineraries.

Dependency, impact and repair boundary. The surface \(X\), its \(p\)-fold covering of \(Y\), Lemmas 4.4–4.6, and the orbit closure used at the beginning of Section 5 all depend on this realization. Thus the stated construction does not currently supply the geometric object to which the external exponent formula is applied. This is the reason for the Major assessment. No claim is made that the positivity theorem itself is false.

A plausible correction is to take \(s_*=1/2\) and replace the translation in (2.1) by \(-\sqrt2/2\). The first change gives the required half-length interval; the second has the appropriate range when the relevant image is \([1/2,1)\). A complete repair must verify the corrected edge pairing, covering/deck transformation, singularity orders, and directional return-map identity for the allowed parameters. Those geometric verifications are not replaced here by the assertion that the two textual changes look small.

Problem 2 (Major: the positive-proportion count in (5.5)).  On p. 21, writing \(D=\dim V_c\) and \(m=\#\{1\le k\le p-1:O_k(\mathbb C^d)\not\subset V_c\}\), the middle expression of (5.5) is \[\frac1{p-1}\left(1-\frac D{d-1}\right).\] It is then bounded below by \(c(g-1)/d>0\) for all sufficiently large \(p\). This is impossible: \(D\ge0\) makes the expression at most \(1/(p-1)\), which tends to zero.

Decisive repair calculation. The stated direct sum of \((d-1)\)-dimensional ranges gives \[(p-1-m)(d-1)\le D,\qquad
 \frac{m}{p-1}\ge1-\frac{D}{(p-1)(d-1)}.
 \tag{2.2}\] The coarser bound \(D\le pd-cg^*\) printed immediately before (5.5) is insufficient to recover the claimed positive constant from (2.2). However, the same paragraph explicitly places \(V_c\) in \(\mathbb C^{N}\), where \(N=p(d-1)+1\). Equation (5.4) therefore supplies the sharper bound \[D\le p(d-1)+1-cg^*.\] Using \(g^*\ge p(g-1)+1\) in (2.2) now gives \[\frac{m}{p-1}
 \ge\frac{cg^*-d}{(p-1)(d-1)}
 \ge\frac{pc(g-1)+c-d}{(p-1)(d-1)}
 \longrightarrow\frac{c(g-1)}{d-1}>
 \frac{c(g-1)}d.\] Hence the required positive proportion does follow for sufficiently large primes from the exact dimension already available, conditional on the preceding geometric and exponent inputs. The printed false inequality is nevertheless used to derive (5.8)–(5.10) and the main conclusion (5.11); it is a Major error in a core proof step. The conditional repair limits what has been disproved, but does not downgrade the error or repair the geometric construction in Problem 1.

# Further checks and limits

The lower bound in (5.2) uses \(m(m+2)/(m+1)=m+1-(m+1)^{-1}\ge m+1/2\) for \(m\ge1\). Together with \(\sum m_i=2g-2\) and at least one genuine singularity when \(g>1\), it yields the displayed \(p(4g-3)/24\) bound. The subsequent estimate on the average exponent and its positive-proportion consequence were checked arithmetically.

For (5.11), upper semicontinuity has the correct direction. Its use with the unbounded Zorich cocycle requires the integrable base domination and null discontinuity boundaries: finite-time twisted products are dominated entrywise in modulus by ordinary products, and all approximating measures have the same base projection. This explains the relevant truncation mechanism; it is not a blanket continuity claim for arbitrary cocycles or arbitrary varying base measures.

Section 6 was read with its quantified hypotheses. The discrepancy proof contains additional steps concerning the selected nonzero character and logarithmic growth that would merit a separate detailed reconstruction. They are not promoted here to further confirmed theorem failures. The spectral-dimension application retains its external spectral-cocycle theorem and almost-everywhere roof/frequency qualifications.
