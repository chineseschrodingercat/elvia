Abstract: This report concerns the acquired 26-page arXiv:2405.03460v1, not an assumed equivalent journal text. A demonstrated failure affects the displayed core proof: the conditional independence and conditional quantile reasoning used in Lemma 3.8. Four local defects concern the path-membership decomposition in (3.28), an overbroad monotonicity statement, the introductory cut-edge argument, and an omitted direct-exit event. Exact finite-network computations support the analytic counterexamples. None disproves the main resistance theorem. The initial assessment is Major within this version; the scientific decision and independent acceptance remain pending.

# Model and hypotheses used in every check

Equation reference: eq:model, eq:sum. The vertex set is \(\mathbb Z\). Every nearest-neighbour edge is present, and distinct long edges are independent, with unit conductance and \[
 \mathbb P(\{u,v\}\text{ absent})=e^{-\beta\lambda_{|u-v|}},\qquad
 \lambda_d=\log\frac{d^2}{d^2-1},\quad d\ge2,\quad \beta>0.\] The expression for \(\lambda_d\) follows by integrating (1.1), p. 1. Telescoping gives \[
 \sum_{d=2}^{\infty}\lambda_d=\log2.\] Consequently, specifying finitely many present long edges and excluding all other long edges incident to a fixed finite vertex set has positive probability: the total excluded intensity is finite. No counterexample below removes a nearest-neighbour edge, changes conductance, or substitutes a different percolation law.

Equation reference: eq:mask. Ordinary resistance minimizes unit-flow energy. The paper’s \(\widehat R(I_1,I_2)\) also deletes edges according to the terminal intervals. For \(I_1=[x_1,x_2]\), \(I_2=[x_3,x_4]\), \(x_2<x_3\), equation (3.10), p. 11, forbids \[
 \mathcal E_{(-\infty,x_2]\times[x_3,\infty)}
 \ \cup\ \mathcal E_{[x_1,x_4]^c\times(x_2,x_3)}.\] Changing terminals can therefore change the graph. In particular, \(\widehat R_n=\widehat R((-\infty,0],(2^n,\infty))\) and \(a_{\alpha,n}=\sup\{r:\mathbb P(\widehat R_n\ge r)\ge1-\alpha\}\).

Theorem 1.1, p. 2, quantifies over every \(\beta>0\) and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]\): there is an exponent depending only on \(\beta\) and a positive prefactor depending on \(\beta,\varepsilon\), uniformly in the integer size \(N\). Its second bound conditions on the absence of direct edges from \([-N,N]\) to \([-2N,2N]^c\). Failure of a particular proof step is distinguished throughout from failure of either conclusion.

# Problem 1: conditional estimates in Lemma 3.8

## Claim, location, and dependency

Page 12, immediately before (3.12), states that, given \(\mathcal E_n=\mathcal E_{(-\infty,0]\times(0,N]}\), the three events in Definition 3.4 are independent. Page 13 uses this assertion, the unconditional quantile bound (3.12), and the number of good scales to produce the exponentially small failure bound in Lemma 3.8. That lemma is explicitly used in (3.21), hence Proposition 3.6 and the quantile recursion of Proposition 3.11. This is a core probabilistic input, not a background observation.

## Exact counterexample to conditional independence

Equation reference: eq:cond1. Take \(\beta=1/20\), \(\alpha=1/4\), \(n=5\), \(N=32\), and condition on \[
 \mathcal E_n=\{\{0,1\},\{0,16\}\}.\] All other optional crossing edges are absent. This event has positive probability \[64^{-\beta}\bigl((256/255)^\beta-1\bigr)>0.\] Here \(Z=\{1,16\}\), so \(z_1=16\) is a legitimate entrance point. Use \(j=2\). The deterministic nearest-neighbour path shows \(\widehat R_2\le5\). By [eq:sum] (#eq:sum), excluding all long edges incident to \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) has probability at least \(2^{-8\beta}=0.757858\ldots>3/4\), and on that event \(\widehat R_2=5\). Thus \(a_{\alpha,2}=5\) exactly.

Write \(A_+\) and \(A_-\) for events (2) and (3) of Definition 3.4 at this point and scale. Their gap vertex sets are respectively \(\{21,22,23,24\}\) and \(\{9,10,11,12\}\). Each resistance is at most 5. Equality holds precisely when every allowed optional edge incident to its gap is absent. Indeed, any such edge joins different potentials on the length-5 nearest-neighbour chain and strictly reduces resistance. The same argument includes optional edges internal to a gap.

Conditioning in [eq:cond1] (#eq:cond1) leaves all positive-to-positive edges independent with their original laws. The two absence sets share the 16 edges \(\{u,v\}\) with \(9\le u\le12\), \(21\le v\le24\). Their total intensity is \(\log(9/8)>0\). Direct telescoping of the allowed edge intensities gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P(A_+\mid\mathcal E_n)&=(1664/17)^{-\beta},&
 \mathbb P(A_-\mid\mathcal E_n)&=64^{-\beta},\\
 \mathbb P(A_+\cap A_-\mid\mathcal E_n)&=(851968/153)^{-\beta}.\end{aligned}\] The joint probability divided by the product of marginals is \((9/8)^\beta>1\). Numerically the joint is \(0.6497008088\) and the product is \(0.6458858665\). The claimed conditional independence is false. These are analytic probabilities, with finite rational products checked independently; they are not estimates from sampling.

## Conditioning can also destroy the quantile bound

Positive association of absence events might replace independence in a single lower bound, but does not justify the conditional marginals or the product over scales used on p. 13. The following separate example makes this obstruction explicit.

Equation reference: eq:cond2. Take \(\beta=1/200\), \(\alpha=1/4\), \(n=5\), \(N=32\) and condition on \[
 \mathcal E_n=\{\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\{0,3\},\{-3,4\},
              \{-11,4\},\{-27,4\},\{-59,4\}\}.\] Again all other crossing edges are absent. Its probability is \[64^{-\beta}\prod_{d\in\{2,3,7,15,31,63\}}
       \left[\left(\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}\right)^\beta-1\right]>0.\] Here \(Z=\{1,2,3,4\}\) and \(z_3=4\). For each \(j=2,3,4,5\), put \(q=2^j\). The nearest-neighbour bound and [eq:sum] (#eq:sum) give \[\widehat R_j\le q+1,\qquad
 \mathbb P(\widehat R_j=q+1)\ge2^{-2q\beta}\ge2^{-0.32}>3/4.\] Thus \(a_{\alpha,j}=q+1\).

Event (3) would require resistance from \((4-q,4+2q]\) to \((-\infty,4-2q]\) at least \(q+1\). Under [eq:cond2] (#eq:cond2), the path \[4\longrightarrow 5-2q\longrightarrow4-2q\] is present, has energy 2, and obeys the deletion rule [eq:mask] (#eq:mask). Its first edge enters the gap; it is not a forbidden direct terminal edge. Its second edge is nearest-neighbour. Hence event (3) has conditional probability zero at every one of these four scales.

The example also fits a complete block in the displayed scale construction: set \(M=21/10\) and \(L=(19/10)/\log(21/10)>2\). Then \(b_0=5\), \(b_1=1\), \(K_n=1\), \(\bar\eta_{b_1}=2\), \(\bar\eta_{b_0}=4\), and \(i=3\) is allowed. The four good conditions (1) are compatible with [eq:cond2] (#eq:cond2). Their simultaneous conditional probability is at least \[\exp\left[-\beta\sum_{j=2}^{5}
                 \log(2+5/2^j)\right]=0.9814225\ldots>0.\] The bound follows by excluding the union of the relevant positive-side long edges; conditioning has already excluded some of them. Even given all four good conditions, all four resistance tests (3) fail. An unconditional quantile statement therefore cannot be inserted as the asserted uniform conditional success probability.

## Judgment, alternative readings, and repair boundary

This is Major: a demonstrated false independence premise and an invalid conditional-quantile inference occur in the core derivation of Lemma 3.8. The positive-probability conditioning examples do not disprove the lemma’s unconditional conclusion; averaging could in principle yield a valid estimate by another argument. The second example uses allowed scale parameters, not a claim that these equal the later sufficiently-large choice in Proposition 3.6.

The negative exponents printed in the final display on p. 13 are a separate evident sign problem; correcting them does not cure the conditional dependence. Nor does single-pair positive association supply the needed upper bound on simultaneous failure across scales. A repair must prove an appropriate conditional or averaged multi-scale estimate under the actual exposed edge set. No such replacement is verified here.

# Problem 2: path membership does not give a flow partition

## Claim and exact check

Page 16 defines \(\theta_i=\sum_{P:z_i\in P}\theta_P\), expressly explaining that \(z_i\in P\) means that \(z_i\) lies on the path. Equation (3.28) then asserts \(\sum_i|\theta_i|=1\) and \(\sum_i\theta_i=\theta\). Lemma 3.15 and the energy argument (3.33)–(3.34) use this partition.

Take \(N=4\) and the positive-probability event that \(\{0,2\}\) is present and all other long edges incident to \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) are absent. The entrance set is exactly \(Z=\{1,2\}\). After contracting source and sink, the relevant graph consists of the nearest-neighbour chain \(0,1,2,3,4,5\) and the edge \(\{0,2\}\). The minimizing unit flow has two self-avoiding paths: \[\begin{array}{c|c}
 P_0=(0,1,2,3,4,5)&1/3\\
 P_1=(0,2,3,4,5)&2/3 .
 \end{array}\] The parallel part from 0 to 2 has resistance \(2/3\); adding the three remaining unit edges gives \(\widehat R_2=11/3\). Exact grounded-Laplacian solution confirms these currents.

Both paths contain \(z_1=2\), while only \(P_0\) contains \(z_0=1\). The literal definition therefore gives \[|\theta_0|=1/3,\quad |\theta_1|=1,\quad
 \sum_i|\theta_i|=4/3.\] On edge \(\{2,3\}\) the alleged sum has current \(4/3\), whereas the original current is 1. This is already a minimizing acyclic unit flow; selecting another path-decomposition algorithm cannot avoid the overlap.

## Impact and exact local repair

This is Minor with a verified local correction: the literal objects fail the stated partition identities, but assigning paths by first entry restores those identities without changing the model or theorem. The counterexample concerns the general decomposition assertion on p. 16; it does not refute Lemma 3.13 on its auxiliary event. The prose about entering the interval, and Figure 6, strongly suggest the intended correction: partition paths by their first vertex in \((0,N]\), not by occurrence anywhere on the path. Every source-to-sink path then belongs to exactly one class. For an acyclic electrical flow, these classes inherit the same edge orientation, so both identities in (3.28) and unidirectionality hold. The example’s corrected masses are \(1/3\) and \(2/3\).

This correction of (3.28) is analytically verified. The passage arguments in Lemmas 3.14–3.15 use the first-entry property and the restored mass partition. A full independent proof of every energy charge in (3.29)–(3.34) remains outside the verified repair; this coverage limit is not evidence of another defect or a reason to increase severity. No claim that the local correction repairs Problem 1 is made.

# Problem 3: the terminal monotonicity is too broad

Lemma 3.3(2), p. 11, asserts \(\widehat R(I_1,I_3)\ge\widehat R(I_1,I_2)\) for every interval \(I_3\subset I_2\). Take \[I_1=(-\infty,0],\qquad I_2=[5,\infty),\qquad I_3=[6,\infty).\] Require \(\{0,5\}\) to be present and all other long edges incident to \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) to be absent. This has positive probability for every \(\beta>0\) by [eq:sum] (#eq:sum); other edges may be arbitrary. For \((I_1,I_2)\), edge \(\{0,5\}\) is forbidden by [eq:mask] (#eq:mask), leaving a chain of resistance 5. For \((I_1,I_3)\) that edge is allowed: \[\widehat R(I_1,I_2)=5,\qquad
       \widehat R(I_1,I_3)=(1^{-1}+5^{-1})^{-1}+1=11/6<5 .\] The rational network solve reproduces both values.

This is Minor because the displayed general lemma is false, while the uses inspected in (3.12), (4.3), (4.4), and Lemma 4.2 can be formulated with a fixed gap-facing boundary or a fixed edge mask. Shrinking only an outer endpoint, with the gap fixed, deletes admissible connections and cannot reduce resistance. Adding allowed edges with terminals and mask fixed cannot increase it. These restricted statements follow directly from inclusion of admissible flow classes. They do not establish the unrestricted terminal assertion, or the conditional probability step of Problem 1.

# Problem 4: the introductory cut-edge event is impossible

The first proof-outline paragraph on p. 3 defines a cut-edge as an edge whose removal disconnects 0 from \([-N,N]^c\). It then assigns each nearest-neighbour indicator \(\xi_i\) the probability \((2N)^{-\beta}\), for \(0<\beta<1\), and obtains an expectation of order \(N^{1-\beta}\).

The deterministic paths \[(0,1,\ldots,N+1),\qquad (0,-1,\ldots,-N-1)\] are edge-disjoint. Removing any single edge leaves at least one of them. Additional long edges cannot destroy connectivity. Thus every displayed \(\xi_i\) is identically zero. For example, \(\beta=1/2\), \(N=1\) gives actual probability 0 rather than \(2^{-1/2}\). The claimed positive expectation of their sum is also false. An independent finite graph check at \(N=8\) confirms the elementary argument for each of the listed nearest-neighbour edges.

This is Minor: the paragraph is motivational and the formal proof later uses different good-interval events. The main lower bound is not disproved. A valid replacement would need appropriately defined two-sided cutsets or a separately proved one-sided statement; changing a constant does not repair the displayed argument. No unproved replacement polynomial bound is offered.

# Problem 5: direct exits are omitted in the final flow split

On p. 24, immediately before (4.7), the proof asserts that the two portions exiting via \((0,N]\) and \([-N,0)\) have maximum at least \(1/2\). An edge directly from 0 to the exterior carries neither portion. Page 4 explicitly acknowledges this possibility, but the event \(B\) in (4.6) does not exclude it.

For a concrete positive-probability configuration, take \(N=8\), retain \(\{0,9\}\), and exclude every other long edge incident to \([-8,8]\). After contraction of the exterior, there are three parallel paths of resistances \(9,9,1\). The minimizing unit flow has \[R(0,[-8,8]^c)=9/11,\qquad
 |\theta_1|=|\theta_2|=1/11,\qquad
 |\theta_{\rm direct}|=9/11.\] The two truncated one-sided resistances in \(B\) both equal 9. The asserted maximum is false, and the corresponding deterministic quarter-of-the-minimum bound would give \(9/4>9/11\).

This is Minor with a verified local probabilistic repair, conditional on the preceding resistance lemma. Let \(G_N\) exclude all direct edges from 0 to \([-N,N]^c\). Telescoping [eq:model] (#eq:model) gives \[\mathbb P(G_N)=\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)^{2\beta},\qquad
 \mathbb P(G_N^c)\le 2\beta/N .\] Use Lemma 4.2 with failure allowance \(\varepsilon/2\) in the joint event \(B\); for \(N\ge4\beta/\varepsilon\), the union bound gives \(\mathbb P(B\cap G_N)\ge1-\varepsilon\). The flow split is valid on this intersection. For finitely many smaller \(N\), resistance is strictly positive almost surely, because the source degree is finite, so a positive finite minimum of suitable lower quantiles supplies the remaining prefactor. This repairs this step of (1.3), assuming the earlier lemmas. It does not resolve Problems 1–2.

# Other checked reasoning and limits

The firework tail statement invoked on p. 8 is present as Proposition 1 on p. 3 of [Gallo]. Its hypotheses include an i.i.d. nonnegative radius law with positive mass at zero and an exponentially approaching distribution function. The proposed dominating law \(\widetilde\alpha(s)=(1-3/(2^{s+1}+2))^{2\beta}\) has these properties. Its infinite product is positive since the logarithmic tail is summable. The source’s minimum-index/range notation must be translated consistently when using this result. Evident indexing and sign inconsistencies were not multiplied into separate findings.

The concentration step can be derived directly from Lemma 2.3: for \(Y_i=1-\xi_i\) and \(t\ge0\), \[\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+tY_i)
 =\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}t^{|S|}\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}Y_i
 \le(1+t\kappa)^n.\] Chernoff optimization yields the binary-relative-entropy bound. Thus this particular concentration use needs no independence of the \(Y_i\) beyond the stated mixed-moment hypothesis.

The \(d_k\) bounds on p. 14 yield growth of order \(k\log k\) with fixed parameters. Consequently \(\sum 1/d_k\) diverges. Conditional on the recursion, the finite-sum induction on pp. 20–21 gives a positive exponent; using a fixed median first is what removes dependence of the exponent on the requested failure probability in Theorem 3.1. These downstream implications cannot supply the missing inputs in Problems 1–2.

The reflection coupling in (4.2)–(4.4) was checked with terminals fixed, as were the conditional independence from the forbidden inner-to-outer edges in (4.10) and the distinction between the two bounds in Theorem 1.1. The final non-dyadic extension of the box-to-box statement, p. 26, and a complete re-proof of the aggregate energy charging after correcting the definitions have not been independently established. No additional error is inferred solely from these limits. This report is not a proof of whole-paper correctness outside the specified findings.
