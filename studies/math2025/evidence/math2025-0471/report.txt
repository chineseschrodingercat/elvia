Abstract: We examine the relation between the filtered Lie–Trotter scheme and the discrete variation-of-constants formula in the acquired manuscript of R. Carles. The smooth spectral cutoff is applied repeatedly by the scheme, whereas formula (5.10) retains only one cutoff in each propagation factor. A two-step calculation with admissible smooth data gives a nonzero residual. This is a substantive defect in the derivation of the main error estimates. It does not constitute a counterexample to those estimates themselves.

# Definitions and operative assumptions

The nonlinear subflow is \(N(t)v=v\exp(-itW-it\varepsilon |v|^{2\sigma})\) (p. 3). On p. 4 the cutoff satisfies \[\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R;[0,1]),\qquad

Theorem 1.4 (p. 5) gives an \(L^2\) error of order \(\tau^{1/2}\) for the specified \(\mathcal H^1\) regime; Theorem 1.5 (p. 6) gives \(L^2\) order \(\tau\) and \(\mathcal H^1\) order \(\tau^{1/2}\) for the stated \(\mathcal H^2\) regime, assuming the exact solution exists on \([0,T]\). # A propagation identity that does not follow from the scheme

Problem 1 (Repeated filtering is omitted in (5.10)).  For the smooth cutoff specified in [eq:scheme] (#eq:scheme), the discrete Duhamel formula (5.10), p. 27, is generally false.

## The exact discrepancy

Equation reference: eq:printed, eq:true. Put \(B=PS(\tau)\) and \(F(v)=N(\tau)v-v\). After changing the starting index to \(m\), the claim in (5.10) is \[
 u^{m+n}=PS(n\tau)u^m+
       \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}PS((n-k)\tau)F(u^{m+k}).\] This is a paraphrase of its formula, retaining its actual propagator definition. Iterating \(u^{j+1}=Bu^j+BF(u^j)\), however, gives \[
 u^{m+n}=B^nu^m+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}B^{n-k}F(u^{m+k}),\qquad
 B^\ell=P^\ell S(\ell\tau).\] The last equality uses the valid commutation of \(P\) and \(S(t)\). It does not remove powers of \(P\).

Equation reference: eq:residual. Already at \(n=2\), actual iteration minus the right-hand side of [eq:printed] (#eq:printed) equals \[
 D_2=(P^2-P)S(2\tau)N(\tau)u^m.\] Indeed the actual iteration is \(B^2N(\tau)u^m+BF(u^{m+1})\), while the asserted expression is \(PS(2\tau)N(\tau)u^m+BF(u^{m+1})\). Thus the discrepancy does not rely on the formula’s inclusion of \(n=0\).

## An admissible nonlinear example

Take \[d=1,\qquad V=1,\qquad W=0,\qquad \sigma=1,\qquad \varepsilon=+1,
 \qquad \lambda\ge2,\quad \tau=\lambda^{-1}.\] These potentials satisfy Assumption 1.1. The cubic exponent is both \(L^2\)-subcritical in one dimension and allowed by Theorem 1.5. With the unitary Fourier transform, \(P\) is the multiplier \[p(\xi)=\chi^2((\xi^2+1)/\lambda).\] Continuity between the cutoff plateau and its zero region gives a nonempty open frequency interval where \(1/4<p(\xi)<3/4\). Choose nonzero \(\widehat f\in C_c^\infty\) supported strictly in that interval, and put \(g=Pf\), \(u_0=af\), where \(a>0\) will be chosen below. Both \(f\) and \(g\) are Schwartz functions. Hence every initial \(\mathcal H^1\) and \(\mathcal H^2\) regularity requirement holds. The one-dimensional defocusing cubic problem supplies the requisite smooth solution on a time interval containing the two steps. This example does not set the nonlinear coefficient to zero.

The initial numerical state is \(u^0=ag\), so [eq:residual] (#eq:residual) becomes \[D_2=(P^2-P)S(2\tau)
          [ag\exp(-i\tau a^2|g|^2)].\] Define positive finite constants \[c=\left\lVert(P^2-P)g\right\rVert_2,\qquad
 M=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_\infty^2\left\lVert g\right\rVert_2.\] Plancherel’s identity gives \[c^2=\int p(\xi)^4(1-p(\xi))^2|\widehat f(\xi)|^2\,d\xi,
 \qquad c\ge\frac{3}{64}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_2>0,\] since \(p^2(1-p)\ge3/64\) on \([1/4,3/4]\). Also \(\left\lVert P^2-P\right\rVert_{2\to2}\le1/4\). Using unitarity, commutation, and \(|e^{-iz}-1|\le|z|\) for real \(z\), \[\begin{aligned}
 \left\lVert D_2\right\rVert_2
 &\ge ac-\frac a4\left\lVert g(e^{-i\tau a^2|g|^2}-1)\right\rVert_2 \notag\\
 &\ge ac-\frac{\tau a^3M}{4}.\end{aligned}\] For \(0<a\le\min\{1,\sqrt{2c/(\tau M)}\}\), it follows that \[\left\lVert D_2\right\rVert_2\ge ac/2>0.\] This disproves the asserted exact identity for admissible smooth input. It is not a counterexample to an asymptotic error rate for fixed data: the construction may select a different input when \(\tau\) changes. Nor does this check assert the auxiliary stability estimate (5.1); the failure concerns the algebraic identity used to derive the error decomposition from the scheme.

## Counterevidence and alternative readings

The cutoff does commute with the Hamiltonian. The problem is idempotence, not commutation. In particular, p. 35 correctly uses \(\Pi_{4\lambda}\Pi_\lambda=\Pi_\lambda\) to deduce localization under a wider cutoff. That identity does not give \(P^2=P\).

Interpreting \(S_\lambda(n\tau)\) as \(B^n\) would contradict its explicit definition (1.5). The spectral expansion on p. 5 also has a single cutoff factor for general \(t\). The estimates used subsequently concern that single-cutoff family. A sharp spectral projection would be idempotent, but replacing the stipulated smooth cutoff changes the hypotheses used for the preparatory estimates and is not a verified repair.

# Dependence, correction, and impact

Equation (5.11), p. 27, is expressly obtained from (5.10), and its four-term decomposition drives the proof of Theorem 5.1 on pp. 27–28. Theorem 5.1, p. 24, supplies the error conclusions of Theorems 1.4–1.5 when the relevant stability bounds hold. Section 5.4, p. 33, explicitly resumes (5.10)–(5.11) for the \(\mathcal H^1\) estimate. The defective identity therefore belongs to the core convergence argument.

The supported assessment is Major: a demonstrated invalid derivation is materially tied to the principal error estimates. The check establishes a gap in that derivation, not falsity of the convergence statements or of the independent spectral tools.

Equation [eq:true] (#eq:true) is a verified algebraic correction, by induction on the recurrence. A complete repair of the proof would additionally need suitable uniform estimates for \(P^\ell S(\ell\tau)\) and the resulting convolution, or another justified treatment of repeated filtering. Those estimates have not been proved here. No claim is made that replacing notation alone repairs the theorem.
