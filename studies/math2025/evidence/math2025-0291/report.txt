Abstract: This review examines the exact author manuscript arXiv:2309.13910v1. Two local assertions require correction: the unconditional uniform \(L^p\) statement in Theorem 2.3(iii), and global \(L^1\) convergence in the appendix. Explicit analytic examples establish both discrepancies. Their required corrections preserve the uses checked in the main energy argument; neither example refutes Theorem 2.4. The report assessment is Minor. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Necessary assumptions and checks

Write \(Kf=k*f\), where \[k(x)=\frac{x^\perp}{2\pi |x|^2},\quad
 x^\perp=(-x_2,x_1),\quad \Phi_\varepsilon=(\varepsilon I-\Delta)^{-1}.\] Theorem 2.4 (p. 11) assumes, for every finite \(T>0\), \[u_1,u_2\in L^4(0,T;L^4\cap L^{4/3}),\quad
 u_1-u_2\in L^\infty(0,T;H^{-1}),\quad
 u_1(t)-u_2(t)\in L^1\ \text{a.e.},\] together with the weak zero-initial-difference condition (2.22). These conditions must accompany the uniqueness statement. They do not follow for arbitrary measure data merely from the smoothing bound (2.4).

The regularized energy \(h_\varepsilon=\langle\Phi_\varepsilon z,z\rangle
=\|\nabla\Phi_\varepsilon z\|_2^2+\varepsilon\|\Phi_\varepsilon z\|_2^2\) has the correct nonnegative sign. The density argument at (3.16) uses both the \(H^{-1}\) bound and the stated initial trace. The interpolation at (3.19) produces a fourth power of \(\|u_2\|_{4/3}\), which is integrable under the actual hypotheses. The coefficient in the final Gronwall argument likewise has the required integrability. These checks are conditional on the stated function spaces.

# Established local findings

Problem 1 (Uniform propagation requires the corresponding initial norm).  Theorem 2.3(iii), p. 9, asserts \(u\in L^\infty(0,T;L^p)\) for every \(p\in[1,\infty]\), assuming only \(u_0\in L^q\) for some \(q\in(1,2)\), in addition to the hypotheses of Theorem 2.1. The membership assertion is false as written.

## Complete analytic check

Fix \(\nu=1\) and set \[u_0(x)=\frac{\mathbf 1_{\{0<|x|<1\}}}{2\pi |x|}.\] This is a nonnegative finite Radon density of total mass one. For every \(1\le q<2\), \[\|u_0\|_q^q=(2\pi)^{1-q}\int_0^1 r^{1-q}\,dr
 =\frac{(2\pi)^{1-q}}{2-q}<\infty.\] In particular \(q=4/3\) is admissible. Its Biot–Savart velocity is tangential with magnitude \(1/(2\pi)\) inside the unit disk and \(1/(2\pi r)\) outside. Consequently \(y_0=K(u_0)\in L^{2,\infty}\), \(\operatorname{div}y_0=0\), and \(\operatorname{curl}y_0=u_0\) distributionally. There is no point mass at the origin: circulation tends to zero with the radius. Thus the initial hypotheses of Theorem 2.1 are met.

Equation reference: eq:heat. The heat evolution \(u(t)=G_t*u_0\) remains radial. Its velocity is tangential, so \(K(u)\cdot\nabla u=0\), and it solves the full vorticity equation. It is smooth for \(t>0\), converges to \(u_0\) as a measure, and is obtained by radial smooth approximation. At the origin, \[
 u(t,0)=\frac{1}{4\pi t}\int_0^1 e^{-r^2/(4t)}\,dr
       =\frac{\operatorname{erf}(1/(2\sqrt t))}{4\sqrt{\pi t}}
       \sim\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi t}}\quad(t\downarrow0).\] Hence the essential supremum over \(t\in(0,T)\) of \(\|u(t)\|_\infty\) is infinite. The claimed membership for \(p=\infty\) fails despite all the stated initial assumptions being satisfied. This is an analytic counterexample; finite numerical values only illustrate [eq:heat] (#eq:heat).

## Counterevidence, impact and precise correction

The proof on p. 10 explicitly inserts the condition \(u_0\in L^p\) before applying Young’s inequality. Reading that condition back into the theorem gives the defensible statement \[u_0\in L^q\cap L^p,\ q\in(1,2)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \|u\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^p)}\le C_p\|u_0\|_p.\] For \(1\le p<\infty\), positivity and the two mass-one kernel identities stated on p. 10 give this propagation by Jensen’s inequality followed by integration; \(p=\infty\) follows directly. This verifies the conditional repair within the paper’s kernel representation.

An estimate with an infinite right-hand side would be vacuous and does not justify the separately asserted membership. The downstream applications checked on pp. 23 and 25 assume probability densities in \(L^4\); interpolation supplies \(L^{4/3}\), and the corrected propagation suffices there. The supported impact is Minor: an overstatement of the auxiliary theorem’s range, without demonstrated damage to these uses or a counterexample to the central uniqueness theorem.

Problem 2 (The appendix’s convergence cannot be global in \(L^1\)).  In the proof of Lemma .1, p. 26, the display after (0.19) claims \(\nabla^\perp\Phi_{\varepsilon'}\varphi\to-K\varphi\) in \(L^1\), and consequently in distributions, for \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\). The global \(L^1\) claim is false.

## Complete analytic check

Choose the radial nonnegative bump \[\varphi(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 C\exp[-1/(1-|x|^2)],&|x|<1,\\
 0,&|x|\ge1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad C^{-1}=\int_{|x|<1}\exp[-1/(1-|x|^2)]\,dx.\] It belongs to the stipulated test-function class and has integral one. For \(r>1\), radial symmetry and circulation give \(K\varphi(x)=x^\perp/(2\pi r^2)\). Therefore \[\int_{|x|>1}|K\varphi(x)|\,dx=\int_1^\infty dr=\infty.\] For each fixed \(\varepsilon'>0\), the heat-resolvent representation (0.15) gives \[\|\nabla g_{\varepsilon'}\|_1
 \le C_0\int_0^\infty e^{-\varepsilon't}t^{-1/2}\,dt<\infty.\] Thus \(\nabla^\perp\Phi_{\varepsilon'}\varphi\in L^1\), whereas its proposed limit is not in \(L^1\). In fact their difference is not in \(L^1\) for any \(\varepsilon'>0\), ruling out the printed norm convergence.

## Repair and dependence

The kernels converge in \(L^1_{\rm loc}\): on every bounded set, (0.17)–(0.18) provide the integrable dominating function \(1/(2\pi|x|)\). Convolution with a compactly supported smooth function preserves this local convergence and yields exactly the distributional convergence required next. Replace the global \(L^1\) assertion by \(L^1_{\rm loc}\), or state distributional convergence directly.

The identity (0.14) survives. Independently, its Fourier multipliers on smooth inputs reduce to \[\frac{i\xi^\perp}{\varepsilon+|\xi|^2}
 =
 \frac{i\xi^\perp}{|\xi|^2}
 -\frac{\varepsilon i\xi^\perp}{|\xi|^2(\varepsilon+|\xi|^2)}
 \qquad(\xi\ne0).\] The distributional proof handles the origin, and boundedness \(L^{4/3}\to L^4\) extends the identity by density. Also \(\|\nabla\nabla^\perp\Phi_\varepsilon z\|_2\le\|z\|_2\) follows from \(|\xi|^2/(\varepsilon+|\xi|^2)\le1\), verifying estimate (3.20) needed in the energy calculation. The false global topology is stronger than the argument requires. The impact is Minor because the necessary distributional identity and its checked central consequence remain supported, not because the correction is short.

# Overall assessment and remaining scope

The examples disprove precisely the local assertions identified above. Neither purports to be a pair-of-solutions counterexample satisfying all hypotheses of Theorem 2.4. The sign of \(zK(u_2)\) printed in (3.1) differs from direct subtraction; the subsequent estimates take its absolute value, so this sign does not invalidate the inspected energy bound. It is not treated as a separate substantive finding.

The distributional uniqueness proof was inspected in detail; external elliptic identification and probabilistic transfer theorems remain dependencies. The full scope of Corollary 3.1’s mass-conservation justification and the passage from a fixed drift to the pathwise uniqueness class in Theorem 4.5 remain outside a completed external-dependency check. No stronger defect is inferred from these limits.
