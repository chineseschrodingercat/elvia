Title: Initial review of branching random walks
on relatively hyperbolic groups

# Scope and assessment

The source reviewed is Dussaule, Wang and Yang [DWY], arXiv:2211.07213v1 (14 November 2022), comprising 50 pages. Its complete text was read. The journal publication has a different pagination, 391–452, and its full text was not obtained through the checked routes. The assessment below concerns the acquired preprint; it does not establish that the same issue persists in the journal version. All page references below are to physical pages of that preprint, which agree with its printed numbering.

One major proof issue is established. On p. 31, the proof of Proposition 5.1 passes from weak convergence of random finite measures to convergence of their quadratic energy integrals. The topology defined in Appendix A controls expectations of linear integrals, and does not imply the asserted convergence of the self-products. A counterexample below satisfies the bounded-mass, tightness, boundary support and uniform energy properties relevant to that inference. This is a defect in a derivation central to the Hausdorff dimension lower bound. It is not a counterexample to the dimension formula itself.

The theorem’s hypotheses must be retained. The group is non-elementary and relatively hyperbolic; the step distribution is symmetric, admissible and finitely supported. The offspring law is on \(\{1,2,\ldots\}\) and has mean \(r\in[1,R]\), where \(R\) is the Green function’s convergence radius. The Bowditch metric assertion uses the specified shortcut metrics with \(\lambda\) sufficiently close to one. The upper bound for the entire Floyd limit set has the additional qualifications in Remark 1.3; the general upper-bound argument concerns its conical part. Neither extinction conditioning nor a theorem for an arbitrary boundary metric should be added to these statements.

# Problem 1: the energy limit in Proposition 5.1

## The precise inference

Equation reference: eq:linear. On p. 29, equation (19) defines \[\chi_n=\frac{\mathbf1_{B_n}}{\mathbb EM_{n,L}}
              \sum_{x\in\mathcal P_{n,L}}D(x),
 \qquad
 B_n=\{\tfrac12\mathbb EM_{n,L}\leq M_{n,L}\leq C\mathbb EM_{n,L}\}.\] In particular, the random mass is bounded by \(C\). Corollary A.23, p. 47, is invoked to obtain a convergent subnet. Definitions A.7 and A.12, pp. 41–42, mean that this convergence is \[
 \mathbb E\int f(x,\omega)\,d\chi_{\alpha,\omega}(x)
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E\int f(x,\omega)\,d\chi_\omega(x)\] for each fixed bounded random continuous function \(f\). The argument on p. 30 places the limiting measure on the random boundary limit set.

Equation reference: eq:invalid. The first display after the uniform energy estimate on p. 31 asserts, for each \(\kappa\geq0\), that \[
 \mathbb E\iint K_\kappa(x,y)\,d\chi_\alpha(x)d\chi_\alpha(y)
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E\iint K_\kappa(x,y)\,d\chi(x)d\chi(y),
 \qquad K_\kappa=\kappa\wedge\widehat\delta_e^{-h}.\] Both measures in each double integral belong to the same outcome \(\omega\). Bounded continuity of \(K_\kappa\) on \(X\times X\) does not turn this expression into a fixed linear test in [eq:linear] (#eq:linear). The subsequent passage to finite limiting energy uses [eq:invalid] (#eq:invalid). Appendix A contains no additional convergence statement for these self-products.

## A bounded counterexample in a tree compactification

Let \(X\) be the Floyd compactification of the Cayley tree of the free group on generators \(a,b\), with edge length \(\lambda^j\) at depth \(j\), and take \(\lambda=3/4\). Let \(\eta_n\) be the uniform probability measure on the \(2^n\) positive words in \(a,b\) of length \(n\), and let \(\eta\) be the fair Bernoulli measure on infinite positive words. Prefix coupling gives \(\eta_n\Rightarrow\eta\): the distance from a depth-\(n\) prefix to its infinite continuation is at most \(\lambda^n/(1-\lambda)\).

Equation reference: eq:example. On a product probability space let \(Z_1,Z_2,\ldots\) be independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(1/2\), and set \[
 \chi_n=2Z_n\eta_n,\qquad \chi=\eta .\] Then \(\chi_n(X)\leq2\), \(\mathbb E\chi_n(X)=1\), and \(\pi_\Omega(\chi_n)\leq2\mathbb P\). Tightness is automatic on compact \(X\). The vertices are isolated, the supports escape each finite set of vertices, and \(\chi\) is a nonzero measure on the boundary. Thus the compactness and limiting-support properties used in the argument are present.

We check convergence in the full topology of Definition A.12, not merely against deterministic tests. For a bounded random continuous \(f\), put \(A_n(\omega)=\int f(x,\omega)d\eta_n(x)\) and \(A(\omega)=\int f(x,\omega)d\eta(x)\). Pointwise continuity and boundedness imply \(A_n\to A\) in \(L^1\). Let \(\mathcal F_m=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m)\). For \(n>m\), independence gives \[\mathbb E[(2Z_n-1)\mathbb E(A\mid\mathcal F_m)]=0,\] and hence \[\left|\mathbb E\int f\,d\chi_n-\mathbb E\int f\,d\chi\right|
 \leq2\mathbb E|A_n-A|+\mathbb E|A-\mathbb E(A\mid\mathcal F_m)|.\] First let \(n\to\infty\), then \(m\to\infty\). The martingale convergence theorem proves [eq:linear] (#eq:linear).

Equation reference: eq:contradiction. Use precisely the diagonal modification made on p. 30: \(\widehat\delta(x,x)=\lambda^{|x|}\) at a vertex, and zero on the boundary diagonal; elsewhere use the Floyd distance. All distances are at most \(2/(1-\lambda)=8\). With \(h=1\) and \(\kappa=1/8\), the bounded continuous kernel is therefore constant, \(K_\kappa\equiv1/8\), including on the boundary diagonal where the truncated infinite inverse distance is \(\kappa\). Consequently, \[
 \mathbb E\iint K_\kappa\,d\chi_n\,d\chi_n
   =\frac18\mathbb E(2Z_n)^2=\frac14,\qquad
 \mathbb E\iint K_\kappa\,d\chi\,d\chi=\frac18.\] The whole sequence converges in the prescribed topology, so choosing a subnet does not remedy this discrepancy.

The example even has uniformly bounded untruncated energy. For distinct depth-\(n\) positive words with common-prefix length \(k<n\), their distance is \(2\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\lambda^j\). The common-prefix distribution for two independent words is \(2^{-k-1}\), with equality probability \(2^{-n}\). Thus \[I_1(\eta_n)
 = (2\lambda)^{-n}
   +\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
       \frac{2^{-k-1}}{2\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\lambda^j}.\] Writing \(t=(2\lambda)^{-1}=2/3\) gives \[I_1(\eta_n)\leq t^n+\frac14\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t^k\leq\frac74,
 \qquad
 \mathbb EI_1(\chi_n)=2I_1(\eta_n)\leq\frac72.\] On the boundary the distance for common-prefix length \(k\) is \(2\lambda^k/(1-\lambda)\), and \[I_1(\eta)=\frac{1-\lambda}{4}\sum_{k=0}^\infty t^k
          =\frac3{16}.\] The same geometric domination yields \(\mathbb EI_1(\chi_n)\to3/8\), whereas \(I_1(\chi)=3/16\). Uniform integrability cannot repair the asserted equality: even the constant bounded kernel in [eq:contradiction] (#eq:contradiction) already fails.

At the level of the measure construction, one may set \(N_n=2^n Z_n\), with \(\mathbb EN_n=2^{n-1}\). Then \(\{\mathbb EN_n/2\leq N_n\leq2\mathbb EN_n\}=\{Z_n=1\}\), and normalizing the selected counting measure as in equation (19) gives [eq:example] (#eq:example). These selected sets are not claimed to be traces of a branching random walk. Their role is to refute the topological implication used in the proof, even with its stated mass and energy bounds.

## Consequence and limits

The issue is major because the disputed step supplies the finite-energy measure needed for Proposition 5.1, not an auxiliary estimate without a later use. The same measure-limit argument is used for Proposition 5.2 on p. 33. Proposition 5.1 supplies the lower bound in Theorem 1.2 and the subsequent hyperbolic specialization, Corollary 1.5; Proposition 5.2 supplies the lower bound for Theorem 1.4.

The counterexample does not refute the compactness conclusion, the support argument, the prelimit energy bound, or the Hausdorff dimension statements. Its limiting measure actually has finite energy. A suitable one-sided energy estimate, a different limiting construction, or additional structure of the branching measures could repair the argument. Any such repair must prove that it applies to the same-outcome products and preserves nonzero mass and the required support. Replacing equality by an inequality without proving that inequality would not resolve the gap. No complete repair for the paper’s measures, and no correction adopted in the journal version, is certified here.

# A local geometric correction

The last display on p. 30 places \(d(x,y)\) within a constant of \(2n-k\), where \(x,y\) have depth \(n\) and \(k\) is the depth of their approximate common branch point. In a tree the exact identity is \[d(x,y)=2n-2k .\] For example, take \(n=2k\), \(x=a^k b^k\) and \(y=a^{2k}\). The actual distance is \(2k\), whereas the printed center is \(3k\). A fixed additive constant, including the bounded error in choosing the branch point, cannot absorb this discrepancy as \(k\to\infty\). The distance-window restriction at the top of p. 31 must accordingly be corrected.

This does not by itself invalidate the exponent \(2n-k\) in equation (22). A decomposition into one common segment of length \(k\) and two segments of length \(n-k\) has total Green-growth exponent \(k\omega+2(n-k)\omega=(2n-k)\omega\). It yields the same intended geometric ratio \(\lambda^{-h}e^{-\omega}\) in the energy sum. Thus the distance formula and the Green-weight exponent must be distinguished. The tree calculation establishes a local factor-two correction; it does not establish a second substantive failure of the prelimit energy estimate in every relatively hyperbolic group.

# Checks on range, normalization and coverage

The upper trace-growth estimate on p. 23 uses \(\mathbb EM_n\leq H_r(n)\), Markov’s inequality and a summable geometric bound from the definition of the limsup growth rate. It does not assume an unproved exact exponential asymptotic. The continuity argument on pp. 13–14 uses the supremum representation and monotonicity to reach \(r=R\); the \(L^2\) Green estimate is used for \(r<R\), and need not be finite at the endpoint.

There is also a useful independent normalization check. For simple random walk on the four-regular tree, write \[u(r)=\frac{4-\sqrt{16-12r^2}}{6r},\qquad R=\frac2{\sqrt3}.\] The edge-hitting parameter satisfies \(3ru(r)^2-4u(r)+r=0\), and \(G_r(e,x)=G_r(e,e)u(r)^{|x|}\). Since the sphere has \(4\cdot3^{n-1}\) vertices, \(\omega_\Gamma(r)=\log(3u(r))\). This gives \(\omega_\Gamma(1)=0\) and \(\omega_\Gamma(R)=\frac12\log3=\frac12v\), consistently with Theorem 1.1. The quadratic identity and endpoint values were checked symbolically.

The second-moment calculations on pp. 27–28 explicitly assume finite offspring variance and initially \(r<R\). The subsequent stochastic truncation of the offspring law, followed by continuity in its mean, addresses the broader range in the main statement. The lower-bound proof on p. 30 invokes this reduction again. Thus the temporary finite-variance assumption is not, by itself, a missing hypothesis in the theorem.

The transition-point geometry, Green-function comparisons, trace growth and the Appendix A definitions were read as part of the proof chain. The checks above concentrate on the stated theorem ranges, the critical-parameter reduction and the energy construction. The full logarithmic tracking argument in Section 6 and all cited external results were not independently reconstructed. No general correctness certification is inferred from reading every page. The major issue assigned here is the specific invalid inference [eq:invalid] (#eq:invalid).
