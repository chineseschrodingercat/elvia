Title: Initial scientific review
Columnar order in random packings of \(2\times2\) squares on the square lattice

Paper. Daniel Hadas and Ron Peled, Journal of the European Mathematical Society 28 (2026), no. 11, 5023–5106, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/1680. The acquired version of record records revision on 14 November 2024 and online publication on 30 July 2025. The frozen 2025 identifier M25-0307 is retained.

# Assessment and exact scope

The inspected argument establishes a detailed route from reflection positivity to four columnar phases. One Major proof defect is identified in the conditioning argument for the quantitative offset-tile estimate, and one Minor parity defect in a sharpness construction. The first is a false intermediate assertion, not a counterexample to the main theorem; the report supplies a rigorous counterexample to that assertion and describes the necessary repair. The earlier classification of periodic Gibbs measures does not use the defective quantitative step.

All 84 pages, including references, were read as extracted text. The earlier EMFILE failure and incomplete state remain preserved. Scientific decision remains pending; this is an initial review, not independent acceptance.

The model is the hard-core model for square tiles with centers in \(\mathbb Z^2\), excluding both nearest and diagonal simultaneous centers. Parity refers to the bottom-left corner of a tile. The distinction between nearest-neighbor and diagonal connectivity is maintained throughout. The four-phase classification concerns periodic Gibbs measures at sufficiently large fugacity. It neither classifies all nonperiodic Gibbs measures nor asserts convergence for a particular boundary condition. The slow vertical correlation scale is of order \(\sqrt\lambda\) in the upper bound; matching infinite-distance lower bounds are not proved. The simulations and broader predictions in Section 10 are not treated as theorems.

# Proof-chain coverage

## Boundary comparison and chessboard estimates

Sections 2–3 were checked with the vacancy weight \(\lambda^{-v/4}\) and the exact boundary conventions intact. The boundary replacement changes only a strip, and its multiplicity and weight cost are exponential in perimeter. Reflection positivity uses conditioning on lines of vertices. The finite-torus chessboard estimate requires the stated even divisibility conditions. The infinite-volume seminorm is a limsup along factorial-sized tori, not an assumed thermodynamic limit.

Proposition 3.8 applies to periodic Gibbs measures. In its proof, a common full-rank translation sublattice exists; boundary comparison is applied to a box containing the tested blocks, so that its perimeter cost disappears relative to their number. The perturbation \(g=1+\epsilon f\) and the concavity of the geometric-mean power then recover the single-event inequality. No ergodicity assumption is inserted into this step.

## Weighted counting and mesoscopic rectangles

Section 4’s one-dimensional transfer matrix is \(T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda^{-1/2}&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The even-length trace bound and the free-boundary count with one missing tile were independently checked. For a nontrivial marked component, the inequalities \(v_H\geq4\) and \(v_H\geq2(k_H-1)\) combine with the compressed planar-graph count and at most \(M^{k_H-2}\) length assignments. The resulting series is bounded by a constant times \(M/\lambda\).

The shared-vacancy assertion in Lemma 4.11 uses planar boundary crossings. Its textual proof was followed, and the exact bound drawing in Figure 4.2 has now been visually checked for correspondence. The later multivalued retilling map has disjoint images, and its gain of order \(M^2/\lambda\) offsets at most order \(\operatorname{len}(A)/M\) marked intervals. Choosing \(M\) proportional to \(\sqrt\lambda\) then supplies the negative area exponent in Lemma 4.1. The constants can be chosen in the stated order.

## Percolation, four phases and uniqueness

Sections 5–7 keep rarity separate from independence: the finite-set bound for a rare set is the input to the Peierls argument, without an independent-site assumption. Nearest-neighbor separation and diagonal boundary connectivity give the infinite component and its finite complement components. Block changes and intersections incur the displayed roots and finite constants. Ergodicity is used when selecting one of separated orientations or parities.

The fine-width phase is obtained by decreasing the even width from the mesoscopic square scale. A change of orientation at successive widths would force crossing sticks. Opposite vertical parities in a narrow rectangle force a row of vacancies, enabling the second phase split. Translation and reflection symmetries then relate the four measures.

In Section 8, a sealed rectangle forces a disagreement component through it to stay in a single column and within a bounded vertical interval. A vacancy pair in one configuration provides a common zero with the other configuration, breaking that column’s disagreement path. Coarse nonsealed paths yield the anisotropic tail bound. Swapping finite disagreement clusters is an involution preserving the product Gibbs law, by the common external boundary and the domain Markov property. This proves uniqueness within each phase and tail triviality. Averaging over a finite quotient of translation lattices, followed by ergodic decomposition, gives the stated classification for all periodic Gibbs measures.

## Quantitative columnar order

Section 9 first seeks an exponentially weighted four-vacancy estimate to obtain an \(O(\lambda^{-1})\) offset probability. Finding P1 concerns this step. Given that estimate, the horizontal-disagreement bound follows by balancing a strip union bound against the vertical tail at \(d\asymp\sqrt\lambda\log\lambda\). The two correlation estimates are combined by partitioning the target set according to which estimate is smaller; one cannot simply interchange a minimum and a sum. Where the same-column cap is one, the trivial covariance bound by one handles the capped case. The final lower offset bound is obtained by removing one tile and sliding another, while the dominant-column deficit follows from the vacancy and occupied-face seminorm bounds.

# Major finding P1: rarity does not survive the stated conditioning

## Location and false assertion

On page 5091 (PDF page 69), the proof of Theorem 9.1 chooses an arbitrary measurable \(\widetilde\Omega\supset J\) with \[\mu(\widetilde\Omega)=q:=\lambda^{-1}\epsilon_1^{-4},
 \qquad \widetilde\mu=\mu(\,\cdot\mid\widetilde\Omega).\] In the paragraph immediately after (9.3), it claims that \(X_{K\times L}\overline{E^c}\) is \(\epsilon\)-rare under \(\eta\widetilde\mu\), where \(\epsilon=\max\{\lambda^{-1/4},\|E^c\|_R\}\). Its supporting chessboard bound has an intersection with \(J\); the asserted conditional conclusion drops that intersection. Both the arbitrary conditioning and the dropped event matter.

## Counterexample within the stated Gibbs setting

Use the paper’s measure \(\mu=\mu_{(\mathrm{ver},0)}\), whose existence was already proved. Let \(f=[0,1]^2\) and let \(V_f\) be the event that \(f\) is vacant. Set \(R=f\), \(k=K=l=L=1\), \(\eta\) equal to the identity, and \(E=V_f^c\). This is an \(R\)-local event invariant under the two central reflections, as required by Lemma 6.7(4). Corollary 4.5 and Proposition 3.8 give, for some fixed \(c>0\) and sufficiently large \(\lambda\), \[\|V_f\|_f\leq\lambda^{-1/4},
 \qquad \mu(V_f)\geq c\lambda^{-1/2}.\] Take the allowed \(J\) corresponding to a tile at \((0,0)\) and the four nearest side vacancies at horizontal positions \(-1\) and \(1\), in the notation of Section 9.1. This is a feasible local event of positive Gibbs probability; \(J\cap V_f=\varnothing\) and the paper’s four-vacancy estimate gives \(j:=\mu(J)\leq\lambda^{-1}\).

Fix \(0<\epsilon_1<\epsilon_0\). For sufficiently large \(\lambda\), \(\mu(V_f)>q\) and \(q>j\). The Gibbs measure is nonatomic. Indeed, infinitely many separated finite boxes each admit an empty interior and a buffered single-tile alternative, with a positive uniform conditional lower bound at fixed finite \(\lambda\). No specified infinite configuration can therefore be an atom. Consequently one may choose \(B\subset V_f\) with \(\mu(B)=q-j\) and set \(\widetilde\Omega=J\cup B\). This is exactly an allowed conditioning event, and \[\widetilde\mu(V_f)=1-\frac{j}{q}
 \ \geq\ 1-\epsilon_1^4
 \ >\ \lambda^{-1/4}=\epsilon\] for sufficiently large \(\lambda\). The singleton test in the definition of \(\epsilon\)-rarity already fails. No conjectural phase behavior or simulation is used in this counterexample.

## Impact and repair boundary

The false assertion is used to justify (9.3), then (9.4), (9.2) and (9.1), and hence the sharp offset estimate in Theorem 9.1 and the later quantitative parts of Theorem 1.1. It is therefore a Major quantitative proof gap. This does not disprove those conclusions, and it does not affect the earlier four-phase classification.

The relevant rare set should include the condition \(J\): outside \(J\), the auxiliary set \(\Theta\) is already all of \(\mathbb Z^2\). Moreover the conditioning parameter must remain in the bound. Writing \(s=\|E^c\|_R\), the preceding chessboard estimate and \(\mu(J)\leq\lambda^{-1}\) yield \[\widetilde\mu\bigl(J\cap\{A\subset
 X_{K\times L}\overline{E^c}\}\bigr)
 \leq \epsilon_1^4 s^{\max\{|A|-4,0\}}
 \leq \max\{\epsilon_1,s\}^{|A|}.\] They do not yield a parameter tending to zero as \(\lambda^{-1/4}\) for fixed \(\epsilon_1\). A viable repair is to choose a separate, smaller parameter \(\delta\) in \(\mu(\widetilde\Omega)=\lambda^{-1}\delta^{-4}\), retain \(J\) in every bad-set event, and use \(\max\{\delta,s\}^{1/r}\) after the block change. Choose \(\delta\) to absorb all fixed roots and intersection constants, then choose the rectangle width and \(\lambda\). The resulting prefactor \(\delta^{-4}\) is still a universal constant. The corrected quantitative chain, with this hierarchy written explicitly, is required; the original inference cannot be retained unchanged.

# Minor finding P2: odd block lengths in Remark 4.7

On page 5055 (PDF page 33), Remark 4.7 defines a global event \(\widetilde E_M\) by requiring \(\sigma(x,y)=0\) whenever \(2\mid x\) or \(M\mid y\). It then equates its fully packed and free-boundary partition functions and applies Proposition 4.4, which requires even length.

Take the even rectangle \(\Lambda=R_{2\times2}\) and \(M=1\). Then \(M\mid L\), \(M\leq\max(K,L)\), and the hypothesis \(M<\sqrt\lambda/C\) holds for any sufficiently large \(\lambda\). The event \(\widetilde E_1\) forces global emptiness. It is incompatible with the prescribed fully packed exterior, whereas with free boundary it has exactly one configuration and four vacant faces. Thus \[Z^1_\Lambda(\widetilde E_1)=0,\qquad
 Z^0_\Lambda(\widetilde E_1)=\lambda^{-1}.\] The asserted equality and lower bound greater than one fail. Restricting this particular construction to even integer \(M\) removes the parity conflict. To cover general \(M\), use a comparable even block size and handle the bounded small sizes separately. This affects the explanatory sharpness construction; it does not refute the upper bound in Lemma 4.6.

# Independent checks and limitations

An independently written program enumerated all 6,427 allowed center configurations in \(R_{6\times6}\) with a fully packed odd–odd exterior. It reconstructed 6,893 nontrivial marked components. Each satisfied \(v_H\geq4\) and \(v_H\geq2(k_H-1)\); the latter had minimum slack zero. This is finite support for the combinatorial check, not a proof of the infinite-volume statement.

Exact symbolic checks verified the periodic transfer-matrix identity and the free-boundary coefficient for even lengths 2 through 10. Rational arithmetic also checked the conditional normalization and the repaired finite-set inequality above. The arithmetic illustration is not a sampled Gibbs measure; the counterexample is the analytic construction in P1. No author program was executed.

General reflection-positivity and ergodic-decomposition theorems, the external planar-graph enumeration bound, and the external ground-state classifications in Section 10 were not independently reproved.
