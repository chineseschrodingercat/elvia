# Assessment and reviewed argument

Minor revision: four confirmed errors in auxiliary scope or indexing. No counterexample to the principal zero-cycle theorem has been established. The problems below concern statements broader than the smooth/rationally connected situations actually required, or an incorrectly indexed background formula. Their correction requires explicit mathematical amendments, but the present evidence does not establish damage to the central conclusion.

The whole 21-page source was read, including references; eighteen selected pages were inspected as images. The review traced the sequence \[\mathrm{RC}(n,2)\Longrightarrow \mathrm{S}(n,2),\qquad
 \mathrm{R}+\mathrm{F}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{D},\] followed by degeneration of Kato homology, algebraicity of special-fibre \(H_2\), the Saito–Sato cycle-class comparison and the mod-\(N\) reduction for \(\mathrm{CH}_0\). Dimensions of the generic fibre and total space were kept distinct. Characteristic zero, algebraically closed residue field, smooth projective generic fibre, simple normal crossings and the conditional higher-dimensional cycle-class assumption were not dropped.

# Confirmed problems

Problem 1 (Minor: property P6 omits smoothness in its second assertion).  On p. 6, P6 first states stable birational invariance for smooth projective varieties, then asserts invariance for a projective birational morphism between quasi-projective varieties without a smoothness condition. Its proposed proof continues on p. 7. The more general assertion is false literally.

Over \(\mathbb C\), take the irreducible nodal cubic \[C:\ y^2z=x^2(x+z),\qquad
 \nu:\mathbb P^1\longrightarrow C,\quad
 [u:v]\longmapsto[v(u^2-v^2):u(u^2-v^2):v^3].\] The finite normalization is a projective birational morphism, and the two points \([1:1]\), \([-1:1]\) map to its node. Put \(E=\{a,b\}\) and \(B=\{\text{node}\}\). Property P3 on the same page gives \[0\longrightarrow\mathrm{KH}_1(C,\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2
 \xrightarrow{(r,s)\mapsto(r+s,-r-s)}
 \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z.\] Here \(\mathrm{KH}_1(\mathbb P^1,\mathbb Z)=0\) and the point groups in degree one vanish. Consequently \(\mathrm{KH}_1(C,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), whereas \(\mathrm{KH}_1(\mathbb P^1,\mathbb Z)=0\). The normalization identity and the boundary matrix were independently checked.

Restore smoothness in the quasi-projective assertion and use the separately formulated resolution property \(\mathrm R(n,k)\) when singular spaces occur. The paper explicitly introduces that property for klt spaces and uses it on singular strata in Section 5. The nodal, nonnormal curve is not a counterexample to that restricted resolution statement or to the main theorem.

Problem 2 (Minor: Proposition 4.4 permits a coefficient-one component to remain).  Proposition 4.4, p. 9, allows any effective \(\Delta_1\le\Delta\) and claims the existence of effective \(D\equiv_{\rm num}\Delta_1+cH\) such that \((X,\Delta-\varepsilon\Delta_1+\varepsilon D)\) is klt for small positive rational \(\varepsilon\). Take \[X=\mathbb P^1,\qquad
 \Delta=[0]+[\infty],\qquad \Delta_1=[0],\qquad H=[1].\] This is a smooth projective dlt pair and \(H\) is ample. For every effective \(D\), the coefficient at infinity after perturbation is \[1+\varepsilon\,\operatorname{coeff}_{\infty}(D)\ge1.\] A smooth curve pair with that coefficient is not klt. The numerical-equivalence requirement cannot change this obstruction.

At least every coefficient-one component must be lowered; for example, require the support of \(\Delta_1\) to contain that of \(\lfloor\Delta\rfloor\), together with the appropriate perturbation conditions. Alternatively state a standard perturbation theorem with its complete hypotheses. The later argument needs the availability of a suitable perturbation, not this assertion for every \(\Delta_1\). The example’s underlying space is itself rationally connected; the failure concerns its boundary perturbation, not its zero cycles. A full arbitrary-dimensional replacement theorem is not proved here.

Problem 3 (Minor: uniruledness does not imply vanishing of the whole \(\mathrm{KH}_2\)).  The paragraph preceding Corollary 1.7, p. 4, attributes \(\mathrm{KH}_2(X,A)=0\) for a uniruled threefold to Voisin’s integral Hodge theorem. For \(A=\mathbb Z\), let \[E:\ y^2=x^3-x,\qquad X=\mathbb P^1\times E\times E.\] This is a smooth projective uniruled complex threefold. The Künneth decomposition gives \[H^{1,1}(\mathbb P^1)\otimes H^{2,0}(E\times E)
 \subset H^{3,1}(X),\qquad h^{3,1}(X)=1.\] Also \(h^{1,3}(X)=1\). Algebraic one-cycle classes lie in \(H^{2,2}(X)\), so they cannot generate all \(H^4(X,\mathbb Z)\). Using Poincaré duality and the exact sequence on p. 8, \[(\mathrm{CH}_1(X)/{\rm alg})\longrightarrow H_2(X,\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow\mathrm{KH}_2(X,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0,\] we conclude \(\mathrm{KH}_2(X,\mathbb Z)\ne0\). The Hodge polynomial calculation independently gives degree-four dimensions \(1,5,1\) in types \((3,1),(2,2),(1,3)\).

Voisin’s theorem concerns integral Hodge classes, not all degree-four classes; its original abstract confirms that scope (arXiv:math/0412279 (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412279)). Replace the sentence by the rationally connected case actually needed, or restrict the claim to the corresponding Hodge-class obstruction. The example is not rationally connected: every rational curve has constant projection to \(E\times E\). It therefore does not contradict the rationally connected threefold input to the main induction.

Problem 4 (Minor: the displayed Bloch formulas on p. 8 have wrong indices).  The two displays following Theorem 3.6 identify \(\mathrm{CH}_i(X)/n\) and \(\mathrm{CH}_i(X)/{\rm alg}\), respectively, with \(H_0(X,\mathcal H_{d-i})\). The surrounding definition makes \(\mathcal H_j\) the sheaf associated to \(U\mapsto H_j^{\rm BM}(U)\).

Set \(X=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}\) and \(d=i=1\). Every point has a noncompact connected Zariski-open neighbourhood, whose Borel–Moore \(H_0\) is zero. Hence every stalk of \(\mathcal H_0\) is zero, and the printed right-hand side is zero. But \[\mathrm{CH}_1(\mathbb P^1)/{\rm alg}=\mathbb Z,\qquad
 \mathrm{CH}_1(\mathbb P^1)/n=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\quad(n>1).\] The torsion-coefficient analogue has the same zero-stalk obstruction. Restore the appropriate Zariski cohomological degree, cohomology-sheaf index and twists in the coniveau formula. This test refutes the displayed identities; it does not refute the separate low-degree Kato exact sequence used later. That sequence needs a correctly indexed derivation.

# Checks supporting the main strategy and limits

In the final reduction, the existence of a rational point splits the degree map. If its kernel \(K\) is annihilated by \(N\), then \(K/NK=K\); injectivity modulo \(N\) therefore does imply \(K=0\). There is no missing divisibility argument at this step. The localization diagram on p. 20 requires surjectivity of the special-fibre cycle class map, which is exactly the target of Lemma 3.7 and Corollary 6.3.

For the smooth-fibration step, the affine-base reduction, fibre \(H_1=0\), Artin vanishing and the surjectivity of one-cycle pushforward supplied by sections over curves were traced. In the displayed diagram on p. 19, surjectivity on \(H_3\) and on \(\mathrm{CH}_1\), together with an isomorphism on \(H_2\), gives the advertised low-degree conclusion by an actual diagram chase. No injectivity on \(\mathrm{CH}_1\) is required.

The review also checked the role of coefficient-one strata, lower-dimensional resolution and fibration assumptions in Lemma 5.8, and the positive-discrepancy coefficients in Theorem 5.12. It did not independently reprove the full MMP existence/termination input, every connectedness and Kollár-component construction, all higher-page spectral-sequence comparison ranges, or the approximation from a formal model to a curve. Those are explicit limits of this initial review, not confirmed errors. The conjectural higher-dimensional hypotheses remain conjectural.
