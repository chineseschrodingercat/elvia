Abstract: This report audits the assigned publisher version of the strict-comparison paper of Amrutam, Gao, Kunnawalkam Elayavalli and Patchell. An exact free-group example contradicts the multiplicative-only quasi-geodesic assertion used to start the quantitative appendix. A separate proper-power example contradicts the stated scope of the centralizer-extension theorem by a strict reduced-norm obstruction. Neither example refutes the main strict-comparison conclusion. The elementary free-product route and the valid primitive-centralizer application are distinguished from the affected statements. A subsequence selection in the rapid-decay argument has a complete local repair. The report also checks ultrapower, quasitrace and Cuntz-semigroup hypotheses, including the zero convention. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# A non-quasi-geodesic path in the quantitative appendix

Problem 1 (Major: invalid input to the quantitative proof).  On p. 652 the orbit polygon \[\gamma=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z}[g^ko,g^{k+1}o]\] is asserted to be a \((\lambda,0)\)-quasi-axis for \(\lambda=d(go,o)/\tau(g)\), in the explicit sense that every subpath has length at most \(\lambda\) times its endpoint distance. This fails even for the conjugate elements used in Theorem 3.3, acting on a free-group Cayley tree.

## An exact witness satisfying the hypotheses

Let \(G=F(a,b)\), let \(X\) be its unit-edge Cayley tree, and take \(o=\operatorname{id}\). This action is free, acylindrical and \(0\)-hyperbolic. For every \(N\ge1\), put \[g_N=b^Na b^{-N}.\] Reduced words give \[|g_N|=2N+1,\qquad
 |g_N^k|=2N+k\quad(k\ge1),\qquad
 \tau(g_N)=1.\] Thus the proposed constant is \(\lambda=2N+1\), and the stable length is fixed throughout the family.

At the junction of the geodesics \([\operatorname{id},g_N]\) and \([g_N,g_N^2]\), the polygon contains the subpath \[g_Nb\ \longrightarrow\ g_N\ \longrightarrow\ g_Nb .\] Its length is \(2\), while its endpoint distance is \(0\). No finite multiplicative constant can satisfy the asserted inequality. For \(N=1\), this is the concrete path \[ba\ \longrightarrow\ bab^{-1}\ \longrightarrow\ ba .\] The original reviewer program `orbit_path_counterexample.py` carries out exact reduced-word multiplication and checks this example for several \(N\). The normal-form identity above proves it for all \(N\); the conclusion is not based on finite numerical testing.

The failure concerns the actual concatenated path, not its orbit vertices. For orbit vertices the printed estimate is valid: \[|k-\ell|\,d(g_No,o)
       \le \lambda\,d(g_N^ko,g_N^\ell o).\] It does not control subpaths that cross a junction and backtrack. As a set, the union also has hanging branches, rather than being an embedded axis, so treating the displayed union as an unparametrized set does not establish the claimed path property.

## Why this matters to the proof

The appendix uses this continuous \((\lambda,0)\) control to invoke the quantitative Morse lemma, pass to contracting families and apply Yang’s admissible-path result. On p. 654 it explicitly sets the additive quasi-geodesic constant to zero. The precise Yang definitions require control of rectifiable subpaths; an inequality only at orbit vertices is insufficient.

This is a substantive gap in the stated route to Theorem 3.3 and hence the given proof of Theorem B. The example does not contradict the nontrivial-product conclusion of Theorem A.4, nor the strict-comparison statement of Theorem B. It invalidates a claimed input actually used by their proof.

A repair could replace the orbit polygons by suitable genuine quasi-axes or by geodesic long pieces, and account for the connecting segments and additive constants. Such a replacement must prove the needed projection, contraction, overlap and length bounds uniformly in the varying conjugate element. An unquantified assertion that the paths stay near an axis does not establish the degree-six threshold. No complete replacement proof of that threshold is claimed here.

## A separate boundary in the axis setup

The appendix also says that the infimum displacement is attained. This need not hold under its stated hypotheses. Here is an explicit geodesic hyperbolic example. Let \(T\) be the unit-edge Cayley tree of \(F(a,b)\), let \(S\) be the bilateral shift of real \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), and put \[C=\{v\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z):v_k\ge0,\ 0<\|v\|<1\},\qquad
 X=T\times C\] with the Euclidean product metric. The set \(C\) is convex: a convex combination of nonzero nonnegative vectors is nonzero, and its norm is less than \(1\). It has diameter at most \(2\). Consequently \(X\) is geodesic and its triangles are \(2\)-thin: the tree coordinate of each point on one side occurs on another side, and the two fibre coordinates are at distance at most \(2\).

Let \(\chi(a)=1,\chi(b)=0\), and let \(h(t,v)=(ht,S^{\chi(h)}v)\). This is an isometric action. For each displacement bound \(r\), the number of group elements moving any given tree point by at most \(r\) is uniformly bounded by a finite word ball (replace the point by a nearest vertex). The same bound holds in \(X\), so this action is acylindrical. For \(g=a\), no nonzero square-summable vector is fixed by \(S\); hence \[d_X((t,v),a(t,v))
 =\sqrt{d_T(t,at)^2+\|Sv-v\|^2}>1.\] On the tree axis, taking \(v=\epsilon e_0\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) shows that \([a]=1\), but no point realizes this infimum. Also \(\tau(a)=1\), since the fibre displacement is bounded and the tree displacement of \(a^m\) grows as \(m\). The example is not complete or proper; neither condition is required by the source assertion. Scaling tree edges to any \(M>200\) keeps the hyperbolicity bound \(2\) and gives \([a]=\tau(a)=M\), still without attainment.

Delzant’s checked Proposition 5 assumes both a sufficiently large minimal displacement and a point realizing that displacement; it does not prove universal attainment [Delzant]. A suitable graph action or a controlled near-minimizing axis could avoid this boundary in the actual group application. This example is therefore a defect in the general setup, not a counterexample to Theorem B.

## The degree ledger and relevant counterevidence

There is a second bookkeeping issue within the same argument. The p. 653 bound is stated as \(T=O(\lambda^3)\), giving \(\nu(U)=2U+O(\lambda^3)\). With the other displayed bounds, direct substitution into p. 654 yields \[B=O(|g|^3),\quad R=O(|g|^3),\quad
 \Lambda=O(|g|^4),\quad D=O(|g|^7),\quad
 \lambda D=O(|g|^8),\] rather than the printed degrees \(2,2,3,5,6\). This calculation identifies what follows from the bounds written there; it does not show that an improved degree-six argument is impossible.

In fact, there is relevant counterevidence to treating this degree issue as a separate theorem counterexample. For the actual conjugate family, the minimal displacement \([g_N]\) is constant. More generally, the checked hyperbolic-isometry comparison \(\lvert\tau(g)-[g]\rvert\le16\delta\) bounds \([g]\) when \(\tau(g)\) is fixed. Thus the earlier factor \(Q_2(\lambda)(1+[g])\) can have degree two rather than three. This can repair that part of the degree ledger, conditional on a valid quasi-axis and the required quantitative projection transfer. It does not remove the backtracking obstruction in Problem Problem 1 (#prob:axis).

For a short connecting geodesic, the checked projection estimate is bounded by its length plus \(2\mu+\epsilon\), not just its length. With the improved \(T=O(\lambda^2)\), these terms still permit the conditional degree-six ledger. The passage from contraction to quasiconvexity also needs an excursion argument, rather than merely showing that one point is close: each component outside a prescribed neighbourhood has endpoints controlled by contraction, giving \(\sigma(U)=2\max\{U,\mu\}+\epsilon/2\), up to harmless slack. The exponent threshold must exceed \((\lambda D+c)/\tau(g)\), with an integer ceiling and a strict margin.

The primary-source checks are material here. Yang’s rectifiable-path definitions, Proposition 3.3 and Corollary 3.4 were read in arXiv:1301.5623v2 [Yang]; the corollary literally states \((\Lambda,0)\), with \(\Lambda=\lambda(6R+1)+3c\). It supplies no exception for backtracking between orbit vertices. Wan–Yang’s Definitions 2.1–2.2 and Lemmas 2.8, 2.23 were checked in v3 [WanYang]. Bestvina–Fujiwara’s contraction statements concern geodesic segments and a valid transfer to quasi-geodesics [BF]. The corrected Gouëzel–Shchur Morse theorem retains the quadratic bound \(92\lambda^2(C+\delta)\) [GS]; the historical gap in an earlier proof is not evidence that the target’s Lemma A.1 is false. None of these citations eliminates the explicit loop above.

## The group-theoretic chain and the conditional repair

The actual DGO and Osin statements were checked, rather than inferring their hypotheses from titles [DGO, Osin]. Osin’s Theorem 1.2 and DGO’s Theorem 6.14 supply a hyperbolically embedded \(F_2\times K(G)\); the finite radical here is trivial. DGO’s Theorem 4.31 and Proposition 4.35 give undistortion and transitivity. Free factors are hyperbolically embedded by the relative Cayley graph normal form. Osin’s Theorem 1.4 supplies one fixed acylindrical action in which the chosen element is loxodromic. DGO’s WPD and elementary-subgroup statements, together with almost malnormality, identify \(E(g_n)=\langle g_n\rangle\) for the primitive conjugates \(g_n=b^nab^{-n}\). Conjugacy preserves their stable length. These checks close the group-theoretic prerequisites, but do not replace the missing quantitative path argument.

There is also a complete local correction to the normal-form use in Theorem 3.3, conditional on a valid uniform polynomial big-powers threshold \(P\). Fix undistortion constants \(C,D\ge1\) so that \(|h|_{\{a,b\}}\le C|h|_G\) and \(|h|_G\le D|h|_{\{a,b\}}\) on the free subgroup. For an integer \(N\ge1\), take \[n_N=\left\lceil\max\{2CN^2,C^2D/2\}\right\rceil,\qquad
 q_N=\left\lceil P_+\bigl(D(2n_N+1)\bigr)\right\rceil+1 ,\] where \(P_+\) is an increasing nonnegative polynomial envelope for the conditional threshold. A nonzero power of \(g_{n_N}\) has free-group length \(2n_N+|k|\), hence \(G\)-length greater than \(4N^2\). Therefore no nontrivial connector of length at most \(2N\) lies in \(E(g_{n_N})\).

Map the new generator to \(g_{n_N}^{q_N}\). To prove injectivity on the radius-\(N\) ball, apply the kernel argument to words of length at most \(2N\) and cyclically reduce them. A pure \(G\)-word stays nontrivial; a pure power of the new generator maps to a nonzero loxodromic power. In a mixed cyclically reduced word all connecting \(G\)-syllables and all generator exponents are nonzero, so the conditional big-powers assertion applies. Allowing arbitrary identity connectors before cyclic reduction would be false, as the two powers \(g^k,g^{-k}\) show. This correction proves the needed normal-form step, including small \(N\) and integer thresholds. If \(P\) has degree \(d\), the image-ball radius is \(O(N^{2d+3})\): degrees \(6\) and \(8\) give \(15\) and \(19\), respectively, both sufficient for the subexponential requirement. The degree discrepancy by itself is therefore not an additional Major finding.

# Proper powers invalidate the scope of Theorem 3.10

Problem 2 (Minor: a stated theorem fails for proper powers).  Theorem 3.10 on p. 651 allows any nonidentity \(a\in F_n\) in \[G=F_n*_{\langle a\rangle}(\langle a\rangle\times\mathbb Z)\] and concludes that \(G\) is existentially \(C^*\)-residually-\(F_n\). For a proper power this is not, in general, an extension over the maximal centralizer, and the displayed conclusion is false.

## A norm obstruction, not only a noninjective group map

Take \(F_2=\langle x,y\rangle\) and \(a=x^2\). With \(t\) the new commuting generator, the displayed group is \[G=\langle x,y,t\mid[x^2,t]=1\rangle
   =K*\langle y\rangle,\qquad
 K=\langle x,t\mid[x^2,t]=1\rangle.\] Let \(w=[x,t]=xtx^{-1}t^{-1}\). The amalgamated-product normal form in \[K=\langle x\rangle*_{\langle x^2\rangle}
             (\langle x^2\rangle\times\langle t\rangle)\] shows that every positive power of \(w\) remains reduced: the \(x^{\pm1}\) and \(t^{\pm1}\) syllables lie outside the amalgam. Consequently \(w\) has infinite order. Normal forms in \(K*\langle y\rangle\) then show that \[u=w,\qquad v=ywy^{-1}\] freely generate a rank-two free subgroup.

Any homomorphism \(\phi:G\to F_2\) fixing the original \(F_2\) satisfies \[\phi(t)\in C_{F_2}(x^2)=\langle x\rangle.\] It therefore sends both \(u\) and \(v\) to the identity. Set \[z=\tfrac14(u+u^{-1}+v+v^{-1})\in\mathbb CG.\] These are four distinct basis elements, so \(\|z\|_1=1\), whereas \[\|\lambda_{F_2}(\phi(z))\|=1.\] The subgroup inclusion preserves reduced norms. On its four-regular Cayley tree, the adjacency operator has norm at most \(2\sqrt3\), giving \[\|\lambda_G(z)\|\le\frac{\sqrt3}{2}<1 .\] For completeness, this upper bound follows from the weighted Schur test with positive weight \(3^{-d(o,\cdot)/2}\). At a nonroot vertex the weighted row sum is \(2\sqrt3\); at the root it is \(4/\sqrt3\le2\sqrt3\). Thus no exact spectral-radius formula is needed.

Choose any \(0<\varepsilon<1-\sqrt3/2\) in Definition 3.4 and the fixed support of \(z\). Every homomorphism fixing \(F_2\) violates the required norm inequality. This directly disproves the conclusion as stated, even though that definition does not require the group homomorphism to be injective.

## Correct scope and unaffected applications

The actually checked published Louder–Magee Definition 3.1 requires extension over a maximal abelian subgroup; its p. 11 construction retains this hypothesis [Louder]. Proposition 1.8 and Lemma 3.4 support the valid construction. The published norm-lifting statement is Theorem 1.7, whereas the target cites Theorem 1.6 for this role. A reference-number correction alone does not fix the missing maximality assumption. Restricting \(a\) to a generator of its maximal cyclic centralizer removes the specific obstruction above. The proper-power case cannot be retained without changing the displayed group or the claimed conclusion.

The actual subsequent application takes \(a=y\), which is primitive. It does not use the false proper-power generality. Nor is its generator \(txt\), as printed, an erroneous missing inverse. To check this, in \[\langle x\rangle*(\langle y\rangle\times\langle t\rangle)\] take the index-two kernel of the parity of the exponent of \(t\). It has free-product form \[\langle x\rangle*\langle u=txt^{-1}\rangle*
          \langle y,v=t^2\mid[y,v]=1\rangle .\] The substitution \(u\mapsto uv\), fixing \(x,y,v\), is a free-product automorphism. It carries the free subgroup \(\langle x,y,u\rangle\) to \(\langle x,y,txt\rangle\), so the latter is indeed \(F_3\).

The defect is a false proper-power extension of the cited auxiliary theorem. The actual primitive application does not use this additional generality, and no core conclusion has been shown to depend on the failing proper-power case. Its demonstrated impact is therefore Minor. This classification concerns the dependency scope, not the shortness of a possible repair.

# The rapid-decay selection and the trivial-group boundary

Problem 3 (Major: liminf does not justify sampling a progression).  In Theorem 3.5, p. 649, the condition \(\liminf f(n)^{1/n}=1\) is used to choose \(m\) with \(P(f(2mR))^{1/(2m)}<1+\varepsilon/3\). A liminf over all integers does not ensure this on the progression \(2mR\).

This is not merely a concern about an arbitrary irrelevant function. For the valid free-product construction on \(F_2=\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z\), one can take the image-radius bound \(q(n)=n(4n+3)\). Keeping the same retractions, enlarge this bound to \[f(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 q(n),&n\ \text{odd},\\
 2^nq(n),&n\ \text{even}.
 \end{cases}\] It still witnesses Definition 3.1. For integer \(R\ge1\), every \(2mR\) is even. Enlarge any valid rapid-decay polynomial, if necessary, so that \(P(r)\ge r\). Then \[P(f(2mR))^{1/(2m)}\ge 2^R ,\] and the printed selection need not be possible.

There is a complete correction preserving the stated definition. Choose integers \(n_j\to\infty\) such that \(\log f(n_j)/n_j\to0\), choose an integer \(R\ge1\) containing the given finite support, and set \[m_j=\left\lfloor\frac{n_j}{2R}\right\rfloor .\] Use \(\phi_{n_j}\), not \(\phi_{2m_jR}\). For large \(j\), \(m_j\ge1\), and \[\operatorname{supp}(a_i^*a_i)^{m_j}\subset B(2m_jR)\subset B(n_j).\] Injectivity still gives exact equality of the relevant \(\ell^2\) norms. Polynomial growth and \(2m_j\sim n_j/R\) give \[\frac{\log P(f(n_j))}{2m_j}\longrightarrow0.\] One sufficiently large \(j\) works for every point of the finite net. The two remaining \(\varepsilon/3\) errors are controlled by \(\ell^1\)-contractivity of the group-ring maps and \(\|\lambda(a)\|\le\|a\|_1\). This proves the required conclusion under the original hypotheses. The invalid selection is used in Theorem 3.5, which explicitly feeds the proof of Theorem B on p. 651. Its original proof impact is therefore Major. The complete subsequence repair establishes the stated conclusion under unchanged hypotheses; this repair status is recorded separately.

Problem 4 (Minor: the trivial group in Corollary 3.8).  Corollary 3.8 does not exclude \(G=\{\operatorname{id}\}\), although its selflessness assertion needs that exclusion.

For this group, \(G*\mathbb Z=\mathbb Z\). The unique retraction is evaluation at \(1\) on \(C_r^*(\mathbb Z)=C(\mathbb T)\), which is contractive. It satisfies Definition 3.4 exactly. But \(C_r^*(G)=\mathbb C\) is excluded by Robert’s selflessness definition; equivalently, there is no injective map \(C(\mathbb T)\to\mathbb C^{\mathcal U}=\mathbb C\). The strict-comparison conclusion for \(\mathbb C\) remains true. Adding \(G\ne\{\operatorname{id}\}\) supplies the needed free-product simplicity in the argument. Every group-selfless application in this paper is infinite and already satisfies the correction.

# Norm-ultrapower and application checks

A countable exhaustion of \(\Gamma\), together with tolerances tending to zero, supplies the sequence in Proposition 3.7. The uniform finite-support inequality and \(\ell^1\)-contractivity extend it to all group-ring elements. Coordinatewise multiplication and involution then give a bounded unital star homomorphism on the reduced completion, fixing the diagonal copy of \(C_r^*(G)\). This is an argument about the norm ultrapower, not only trace moments. Injectivity is supplied separately by simplicity of the nontrivial reduced free product. For \(G\ne\{1\}\), the free product \(G*\mathbb Z\) is \(C^*\)-simple and has a unique trace by the checked de la Harpe–Préaux criterion [dHP]. The map just constructed is unital and nonzero, so simplicity makes it injective. Composing it with the ultrapower trace gives a tracial state on \(C_r^*(G*\mathbb Z)\); uniqueness identifies that state with the canonical trace. Thus the embedding is state preserving, as required by Robert’s criterion [Robert]. The argument does not assert that every star homomorphism, including the zero map, is injective.

For Proposition 3.2, the middle conjugated syllables remain nontrivial \(H\)-syllables, or start and end with such syllables, because the chosen \(h_n\) is longer than the intervening word. At the two endpoints only the inward-facing syllable is needed. The printed assertion that every parenthesized term begins and ends in \(H\) is unnecessarily strong at those endpoints; the normal-form noncancellation argument still works with the appropriate one-sided assertion.

Theorem C requires the hypotheses of Robert’s embedding result: a unital simple nonelementary algebra, stable rank one, a unique normalized quasitrace, and strict comparison. Gerasimova–Osin’s Theorem 1.1 gives stable rank one for a countable acylindrically hyperbolic group with trivial finite radical, without an RD or exactness assumption [GO]. Simplicity and infinitude give nonelementarity; BKKO’s published Theorem 1.3 gives the unique trace [BKKO]. Haagerup’s Theorem 5.11 converts quasitraces to traces under the explicit exactness hypothesis [Haagerup]. These match Robert’s Proposition 6.3.1, actually read in arXiv:1007.1964v3, including the unique normalized 2-quasitrace condition [Robert2012]. The target need not be nuclear. The conclusion is existence of a unital embedding of \(\mathcal Z\), unique up to pointwise approximate unitary equivalence; it is not actual unitary conjugacy or Jiang–Su absorption. For groups covered only through Theorem B, the application remains dependent on a correct proof of its group-theoretic input.

## An unambiguous zero convention for the Cuntz semigroup

The p. 644 discussion first identifies the strictly positive lower-semicontinuous affine functions with \((0,\infty]\), but later displays \(V(A)\sqcup[0,\infty]\) and \(\mathbb N\sqcup[0,\infty]\). If \(V(A)\) includes its usual zero and the union is literally disjoint, the latter notation would add another zero-rank element. Faithfulness rules out a nonzero positive class with zero dimension: after rescaling a nonzero positive element to a contraction, its dimension dominates its positive trace.

There is relevant counterevidence to declaring a new substantive theorem failure from this notation alone: Robert’s cited source uses the same closed-endpoint shorthand, and the placement or identification of the common zero can be a convention. The appropriate correction is to specify the model and its operations explicitly. With zero in the projection summand, the free-group model is \[\operatorname{Cu}(C_r^*(F_2))=\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\sqcup(0,\infty].\] Writing \(n\) for a compact projection class and \(r\) for a positive soft class, addition within each part is usual and \(n+r\) is the soft class of numerical value \(n+r\). The mixed order is \[n\le r \iff n<r,\qquad
      r\le n \iff r\le n\] where the right-hand comparisons are numerical. Thus the integer class \(n\) and the soft class of value \(n>0\) are distinct. The only zero is the compact class \(0\). This is a minor notation clarification, not an additional counterexample to the intended classification.

## Rank realization, comparison and projection classes

The checked Brown–Toms Theorem 2.6 assumes exactness and surjectivity of the finite-matrix rank map \(W(A)_+\to\mathrm{LAff}_b(T(A))^{++}\) [BrownToms]. It is not legitimate to drop that premise or replace it silently with a statement about \(\operatorname{Cu}(A)\). For the present exact monotracial application the following direct stabilized argument supplies the needed conclusion instead.

Antoine–Perera–Thiel–Vilalta’s Theorem 5.13 and Example 5.14 give pureness, hence almost divisibility, for the exact infinite \(C^*\)-simple groups under strict comparison [Pure]. Put \(u=[1]\). Almost divisibility gives \(kx_k\le u\le(k+1)x_k\), so \[\frac1{k+1}\le d_\tau(x_k)\le\frac1k .\] Integer multiples of these positive numbers meet any prescribed positive interval once \(k\) is sufficiently large. Given \(t>0\), choose classes \(y_j\) whose dimensions increase strictly to \(t\). Strict comparison makes \(y_j\le y_{j+1}\). Then \(y_t=\sup_jy_j\) has dimension \(t\) and is not compact: compactness would put it below some \(y_j\), contradicting \(d_\tau(y_j)<t\). The class \(\sup_jj[1]\) supplies infinity.

Brown–Toms Lemma 2.2 has no rank-surjectivity premise. It approximates each nonprojection class by increasing finite-matrix classes of strictly increasing dimensions; Proposition 2.5 and strict comparison then identify soft classes with the same dimension. For the mixed order, compactness of \(n[1]\) and this approximation give \(n[1]\le y_t\) only if \(n<t\). Conversely \(n<t\) gives strict comparison. If \(t\le n\), all the strictly smaller-rank approximants lie below \(n[1]\), hence \(y_t\le n[1]\); the converse follows from the dimension functional. A sum with a soft term cannot be compact: otherwise compactness and the same approximants would force its dimension below its own value. This proves the addition and mixed-order rules stated above.

Finally, Pimsner–Voiculescu’s published Corollary 3.2, inspected on the scanned printed p. 152, gives \(K_0(C_r^*(F_n))=\mathbb Z[1]\) [PV]. Stable rank one gives projection cancellation, so \(V(A)\) injects into \(K_0(A)\). Faithfulness of the normalized trace forces nonzero projection coordinates to be positive integers; direct sums of units realize every nonnegative integer. Thus the compact summand is precisely \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Absence of nontrivial projections in \(A\) alone would not establish this stabilized conclusion.

The broader introductory formula for arbitrary nonexact algebras and quasitrace simplexes is not independently proved here. The completed derivation concerns the exact monotracial applications, and it does not conflate stabilized rank surjectivity with the finite-matrix premise of the cited theorem.

# Disposition and verification limits

The orbit-path assertion and the proper-power generalization have explicit obstructions under their literal hypotheses. The rapid-decay subsequence selection and the trivial-group boundary have complete local corrections. None is evidence that every main theorem is false. In particular the free-product route to Theorem A is separately checked, with the repaired rapid-decay selection, and the primitive centralizer application survives the proper-power counterexample.

The bounded checks, counterevidence and citation-hypothesis mapping are complete within the stated scope. External classification, contraction and group-embedding theorems are used at their checked statements; their entire original proofs are not claimed to have been independently reproduced. Scientific decision remains pending, human reviews are empty, and no author adoption is claimed. The accompanying verification files record the independent focused checks. The lack of author code is not a defect; only original reviewer calculations were executed.
