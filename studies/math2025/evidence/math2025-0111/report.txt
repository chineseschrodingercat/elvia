Abstract: The acquired nine-page arXiv version was read in full. A regularity mismatch is demonstrated in the transfer of the sharp whole-plane upper estimate to the half-plane: odd reflection of admissible nonzero boundary data need not belong to the Sobolev class used in that estimate. This is a proof-level concern for the quantitative optimality conclusion, not a counterexample to the growth theorem. The geometric lower-growth construction was also checked, including a corrected normal-velocity comparison derived from its existing constants.

# Definitions, quantifiers, and dependencies

Write \(H=\mathbb R\times(0,\infty)\). The equation is \[\partial_t\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=0,\qquad
u=\nabla^\perp\Delta_D^{-1}\omega,\qquad
\nabla^\perp=(\partial_2,-\partial_1).\] The Dirichlet condition is imposed on the stream function; it gives impermeability of the boundary, not a zero boundary value for vorticity. Theorem 1.2 quantifies over every \(\omega_0\in W^{1,\infty}(H)\cap L^1(H)\) with \(\left\lVert\omega_0\right\rVert_\infty=1\), as well as the corresponding whole-plane class (p. 3). It asserts the upper logarithmic growth rate \(2/\pi\). Theorem 1.1 supplies an example attaining that rate.

The whole-plane argument first controls the instantaneous rate of approach of two particles. Its improvement for separations below the gradient scale uses \[|\omega(y_1,y_2)-\omega(-y_1,y_2)|
 \le 2\left\lVert\nabla\omega\right\rVert_\infty |y_1|.\] Translation and rotation then produce a gradient differential inequality with leading coefficient \(2/\pi\), on p. 5. This Lipschitz input is the relevant hypothesis in the transfer to \(H\).

# Problem 1: the reflection transfer loses its regularity hypothesis

## Source claim and precise suspect step

At the bottom of p. 5, immediately after the whole-plane differential inequality, the source invokes odd reflection in \(x_2\) to assert the same bound on \(H\), with the \(L^1\) mass doubled. Odd reflection does preserve the velocity representation and bounded-vorticity weak evolution. It does not, for the entire class in Theorem 1.2, preserve the finite Sobolev gradient used in the preceding argument.

## An admissible test of the inference

Equation reference: eq:gaussian. Consider the explicit initial datum \[
 \omega_0(x,y)=e^{-x^2-y^2},\qquad (x,y)\in H .\] It is smooth up to the boundary. Its essential supremum is one, its \(L^1(H)\) norm is \(\pi/2\), and \[|\nabla\omega_0|=2r e^{-r^2},\qquad
 \left\lVert\nabla\omega_0\right\rVert_\infty=\sqrt{2/e}<\infty .\] The maximum occurs at \(r=1/\sqrt2\), which is attained within \(H\). Thus every stated initial-data hypothesis of the half-plane part of Theorem 1.2 is satisfied. The associated Dirichlet stream-function prescription is the one in the paper; no extra vorticity boundary condition has been added.

Equation reference: eq:jump. The prescribed odd extension is \[\Omega_0(x,y)=\operatorname{sgn}(y)e^{-x^2-y^2}\quad(y\ne0).\] Its two traces at \(y=0\) differ by \(2e^{-x^2}\). Distributionally, \[
 \partial_y\Omega_0
 =-2|y|e^{-x^2-y^2}
   +2e^{-x^2}\delta_{\{y=0\}} .\] The second term is a nonzero line measure. Therefore \(\Omega_0\notin W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb R^2)\). Equivalently, at \(x=0\), \[\frac{|\Omega_0(0,h)-\Omega_0(0,-h)|}{2h}
 =\frac{e^{-h^2}}h\longrightarrow\infty.\] The whole-plane Lipschitz estimate cannot be applied to this extension with the finite half-plane gradient norm. If the full-plane gradient norm is used instead, it is not finite and the resulting estimate does not yield the stated finite bound.

These calculations are a counterexample to preservation of the proof hypothesis, not a counterexample to the long-time growth bound. No claim about the actual long-time gradient of [eq:gaussian] (#eq:gaussian) is needed or made.

## Alternative readings and counterevidence

The odd extension still belongs to \(L^\infty\cap L^1\), with mass \(\pi\), and is a valid device for constructing the half-plane velocity. Thus the basic Biot–Savart reflection in the introduction is not the problem. The coarse separation estimate, which needs only boundedness and mass, also remains available.

Reading the gradient as the classical derivative away from the reflection line does not repair the invoked \(W^{1,\infty}\) argument: the distributional jump and the displayed cross-line difference quotients remain. After the rotation used on p. 5, a pair of reflected points in the proof can straddle that line. Conversely, if zero boundary trace were separately assumed, odd extension would preserve the Lipschitz class. Such a restriction is absent from Theorem 1.2 and would not justify its stated full quantifier.

A direct half-plane strain estimate may recover the claimed rate by exploiting cancellation at the flat boundary. This report has not supplied that estimate. Consequently the conclusion is a demonstrated gap in the stated proof, with the theorem itself unresolved by this test.

## Dependency, severity, and repair status

This step supplies the sharp upper bound on the half-plane. The exact limit in Theorem 1.1 and its asserted quantitative maximality combine that upper bound with the lower bound in Section 3. The concern therefore reaches a core contribution. Under the assigned calibration it is Major at the proof level. It neither disproves the lower-growth construction nor establishes that the sharp rate is false.

A valid repair must establish the same finite gradient estimate for nonzero boundary traces, or give another upper-growth proof under the original hypotheses. Imposing a zero trace only repairs a restricted statement. No full repair of this general transfer is claimed here.

# A checked local correction in the lower-bound argument

The first display on p. 8 compares \(-2C'v_s s\alpha\) with \(-3C'v_s s_0\alpha\) for all \(s\in[s_0,e^{-1}]\). The strict comparison written there requires \(2s<3s_0\) and does not follow on the full interval. For example \(s=2s_0\) is allowed for every \(s_0\le e^{-4}\), and gives the reverse comparison. This test respects the full stated range of \(s_0\); it does not choose an \(s_0\) that might violate its earlier smallness condition.

However, the exact normal velocity gives the required stronger estimate with the constants already defined in the source. Let \[q=\sqrt{|\log s|},\quad q_0=\sqrt{|\log s_0|},\quad
 g(s)=s e^q,\quad \rho_0=\frac{s_0}{2q_0}.\] For \(s\in[s_0,e^{-1}]\), \(1\le q\le q_0\), and direct differentiation yields \[g'(s)=e^q(1-\tfrac1{2q}),\qquad
 \frac{g(s)}{g'(s)}-s=\frac{s}{2q-1}
 \ge \frac{s}{2q_0}=\frac{s\rho_0}{s_0}.\] Equation (3.9) states \(\alpha'\le-3C's_0\rho_0^{-1}\alpha<0\). The moving boundary normal speed is therefore \[V_n=\alpha'v_s\left(\frac{g}{g'}-s\right)
 \le-3C'v_s s\alpha
 <-2C'v_s s\alpha
 \le u\cdot n.\] This proves the desired barrier comparison, including the endpoints of the interval, without altering (3.8), \(\rho_0\), or the limiting rate. It is recorded as a verified local correction, not counted as a second substantive defect: the necessary information already occurs in the adjacent calculation. Author adoption has not been requested.

The remaining rate check is consistent: the angular integral is \((4/\pi)\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta=2/\pi\); \(g(s)/s\to\infty\) while \(|\log g(s)|/|\log s|\to1\). Thus the leading term in the differential inequality for \(-\log\varepsilon\) has coefficient tending to \(2/\pi\), while its square-root and constant corrections are lower order. Equation (3.11) supplies the corresponding lower rate. This check does not resolve Problem 1.
