Abstract: The full acquired author manuscript was read, with detailed checks of the operator conventions, viscosity and flatness arguments, and two-dimensional energy reduction. The shear kernel and transformed domain in Lemma 4.12 are incorrect as displayed, leaving a proof issue in the principal regularity chain. The final exponent sentence is also incorrect, but the preceding adjustable contraction and tail estimates supply the advertised range after a short argument made explicit here. An admissible half-space profile disproves the printed exterior sign, and a min–max identity lacks its symmetric cross term. These local defects are separated from their corrected consequences; no counterexample to the main regularity theorem is established. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and assumptions

Equation reference: eq:L. For \(s\in(0,1)\), the source assumes a symmetric homogeneous kernel satisfying \[\lambda |h|^{-n-2s}\le K(h)\le\Lambda |h|^{-n-2s},
 \qquad K(-h)=K(h),\qquad
 K(h)=|h|^{-n-2s}K(h/|h|),\] with \(0<\lambda\le\Lambda\). Its operator convention is \[
 Lu(x)=2\mathop{\mathrm{PV}}\int_{\mathbb R^n}(u(x)-u(y))K(x-y)\,dy\] [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~2--3, (1.2)--(1.3)]{RW}]. The main regularity statements additionally assume \(K\in C^{1-2s+\beta}(S^{n-1})\) for \(\beta>\max\{0,2s-1\}\). Theorem 1.1 concerns minimizers of the one-phase functional whose zero set near a free-boundary point lies between two sufficiently close parallel hyperplanes. Theorem 1.5 concerns viscosity solutions trapped between translated half-space profiles, with the stated small tail bound. Both claim \(C^{1,\alpha}\) free-boundary regularity for every \(\alpha\in(0,s/2)\) [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~3, 6]{RW}].

The first two findings concern steps in these proofs. They do not assert a solution violating the regularity conclusion. The third finding concerns the independently stated exterior expansion for an \(L\)-harmonic function and uses all its required geometric hypotheses.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The transformed kernel and domain in Lemma 4.12).  The shear displayed in the proof of Lemma 4.12 does not produce the displayed kernel or the asserted fixed half-ball domain.

## Claim and calculation

Equation reference: eq:shear. Lemma 4.12 assumes \(\gamma\in(0,s)\), \(f\in L^\infty(B_1)\), and \(\omega\in S^{n-1}\) with \(\omega_n\ge\delta>0\). For a viscosity solution of \[L((x_n)_+^{s-1}v)=f \quad\text{in }B_1\cap\{x_n>0\},
 \qquad
 \partial_\omega v=0 \quad\text{on }B_1\cap\{x_n=0\},\] it claims a \(C^{1,\gamma}\) boundary expansion with constants controlled by \(n,s,\lambda,\Lambda,\delta,\gamma\) [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~35--36]{RW}]. The proof defines \[a=\frac{\omega'}{\omega_n},\qquad
 S=\begin{pmatrix}I&a\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 w(x)=v(Sx).\] Here \(\det S=1\), and \(S\) preserves the last coordinate. With \(F(y)=(y_n)_+^{s-1}v(y)\) and \(W=F\circ S=(x_n)_+^{s-1}w(x)\), substitution \(y=Sz\) gives \[
 (LF)(Sx)=2\mathop{\mathrm{PV}}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
       (W(x)-W(z))K(S(x-z))\,dz.\] Consequently, the correct kernel is \[K_{\rm new}(h)=K(Sh)=K(h'+a h_n,h_n).\] The oblique derivative transforms as intended: \(\partial_nw=\omega_n^{-1}(\omega\cdot\nabla v)\circ S\).

In contrast, the actual formula on page 36 uses \(h'-a h_n\) and an additional factor \(|\omega_n|/|\omega'|\). For \(n=2\), \(s=1/2\), \(K(h)=|h|^{-3}\), and \(\omega=(1/\sqrt5,2/\sqrt5)\), all kernel and direction hypotheses hold with \(\lambda=\Lambda=1\) and \(\delta=1/2\). At \(h=(1,0)\), the correct kernel is 1 while the printed one is 2. At \(h=(1,1)\), the two values are respectively \[(13/4)^{-3/2}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 2(5/4)^{-3/2}.\] Thus neither the prefactor nor the inverse shear represents [eq:shear] (#eq:shear).

The original equation supplies the transformed equation on \(S^{-1}B_1\cap\{x_n>0\}\), rather than necessarily on the printed \(B_{3/4}\cap\{x_n>0\}\). Indeed, if \(\omega=(\sqrt3/2,1/2)\) and \(x=(0,7/10)\), then \[|x|=7/10<3/4,\qquad |Sx|=7/5>1.\] The assumed equation and \(f\in L^\infty(B_1)\) give no equation at \(Sx\). Moreover, the printed prefactor becomes unbounded as \(\omega'\to0\) while \(\omega_n\ge1/2\), so it does not supply the asserted ellipticity constants uniformly in \(\delta\).

## Impact and bounded correction

The algebraic correction is to use \(K(Sh)\) and the actual transformed domain. Since \(\|S\|,\|S^{-1}\|\le1+|a|\le1+\delta^{-1}\), the corrected kernel satisfies \[\lambda\|S\|^{-n-2s}|h|^{-n-2s}
 \le K(Sh)\le
 \Lambda\|S^{-1}\|^{n+2s}|h|^{-n-2s}.\] Local balls with radius controlled by \(\delta\), followed by a covering of the original boundary region, address the domain issue. This verifies the change-of-variable ingredients; it does not independently prove the cited nonlocal Neumann estimates or a complete replacement lemma.

The proof of Theorem 4.1 explicitly invokes Lemma 4.12 for its affine expansion on page 38. That improvement-of-flatness theorem feeds Theorem 1.5, and the proof on page 51 deduces Theorem 1.1 from Theorem 1.5. The invalid reduction is therefore a Major proof issue in the stated dependency chain. The evident local repair does not establish that the regularity theorem is false, nor does it verify every remaining step.

Problem 2 (The final argument reverses the exponent dependence).  Shrinking the iteration radius does not increase the exponent supplied by the displayed error decay.

## Claim and calculation

Equation reference: eq:gamma. At scales \(r_k=\rho_0^k/2\), Lemma 4.14 obtains error \(C2^{-ks}\). Pages 42–43 correctly relate the resulting exponent to the radius by \[
 \rho_0^\gamma=2^{-s},
 \qquad
 2=\rho_0^{-\gamma/s},
 \qquad
 \gamma=\frac{s\log2}{\log(1/\rho_0)}.\] These relations follow directly by comparing \(2^{-ks}\) with \(\rho_0^{k\gamma}\).

The last paragraph of the proof of Theorem 1.5 on page 45 instead prints \(2=\rho_0^{-s/\gamma}\), and argues that decreasing \(\rho_0\) allows \(\gamma\) to approach \(s\). It then claims every exponent \[\alpha=\frac{s\gamma}{s+\gamma}<\frac{s}{2}.\] Both the reciprocal discrepancy and the direction of the radius argument were checked on actual page images. With the relation [eq:gamma] (#eq:gamma) required by the iteration, smaller \(\rho_0\) gives smaller \(\gamma\), and hence smaller \(\alpha\). For \(s=1/2\), for example, \[\begin{array}{c|cc}
 \rho_0&\gamma&\alpha\\ \hline
 1/4&1/4&1/6\\
 1/16&1/8&1/10
\end{array}\] is an exact comparison. Approaching \(\gamma=s\) in [eq:gamma] (#eq:gamma) requires \(\rho_0\) to approach \(1/2\), rather than to decrease from the small radius supplied by the improvement theorem.

## Impact, counterevidence, and a complete parameter correction

The erroneous final argument is a Major proof issue: it does not establish the full exponent range in the principal regularity theorem. The existence of a short repair does not make this central invalid derivation merely a presentation defect. The earlier estimates supply a repair of the advertised geometric range, conditional on the other lemmas of the paper. Fix any target \(0<\alpha<s/2\), and choose \[\alpha<q<\gamma_{\rm lin}<s .\] Use \(\gamma_{\rm lin}\) in the affine estimate on pages 38–39. Equations (4.40) and the total tail estimate on pages 40–41 give, at a fixed sufficiently small radius \(\rho\), \[\text{rescaled flatness}\le (c_0/2)\varepsilon\rho^{\gamma_{\rm lin}},
 \qquad
 T_{\rm new}\le C\varepsilon
       (\rho^{\gamma_{\rm lin}}+\rho^s).\] The sum of the two tails outside the translated barriers is bounded by the tail of the absolute difference from the central profile. Set \(\theta=\rho^q\), and choose \(\rho\), once and for all, so that \[(c_0/2)\rho^{\gamma_{\rm lin}-q}\le1,\qquad
 C(\rho^{\gamma_{\rm lin}-q}+\rho^{s-q})\le\delta_0 .\] Both inequalities are possible because their powers are positive. Also require \(c_0\rho^{\gamma_{\rm lin}}<1\). Fix this radius before choosing the small flatness threshold \(\varepsilon_0\), as required by the compactness passage and Lemma 4.13. The improved flatness \(\theta\varepsilon\) then satisfies the same tail hypothesis \(T_{\rm new}\le\delta_0\theta\varepsilon\). The constants can be uniform under the rotations of the iteration, since rotation preserves the kernel bounds and its indicated regularity norm.

To retain the original flatness threshold independently of the target exponent, first apply the valid fixed-contraction iteration of Lemma 4.14 until each centered rescaling satisfies the smaller threshold required for the chosen \(q\) and \(\rho\). Its flatness parameter \(\varepsilon_k=\bar\varepsilon 2^{-k}\) and defect tail bound \(\delta_0\varepsilon_k\) tend to zero uniformly on a fixed smaller set of free-boundary centers, by page 42. Thus one common finite entry scale suffices for each target exponent; after rotation, switch to the strengthened iteration. The initial hypothesis remains the original threshold, while the strengthened estimates may depend on the target exponent.

Iterating this strengthened form with \(\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_0\theta^k\) gives \(\lvert\nu_{k+1}-\nu_k\rvert\le C\varepsilon_k\). The convergent sum yields, at every free-boundary center in a fixed smaller neighborhood, a limiting normal and a uniform strip estimate \[\bigl|(x-x_0)\cdot\nu_{x_0}\bigr|
       \le C|x-x_0|^{1+q}
 \quad\text{for nearby free-boundary points }x .\] Here the constants and the allowed radius are independent of the center; the initial translation and scaling are those on page 42. The existing argument for one positive exponent supplies a local Lipschitz graph, with its normal component bounded away from zero. The strip estimate therefore gives the uniform affine remainder \[|g(z)-g(y)-Dg(y)\cdot(z-y)|\le C|z-y|^{1+q}.\] For \(h=|x-y|\), compare this estimate at \(x\) and \(y\), evaluated at \(x\) and \(x+h e_i\), within a smaller graph domain. Subtraction gives \(h|(Dg(x)-Dg(y))\cdot e_i|\le C h^{1+q}\). Thus \(Dg\in C^q\), and in particular \(g\in C^{1,\alpha}\). The same cited boundary regularity input then has the advertised geometric regularity available.

This verifies the adjustable contraction, the tail closure, and the graph-gradient implication. It does not reverify the external Neumann or boundary regularity theorems, or remove Problem 1. The original full exponent range is not disproved; the last paragraph should be replaced by the explicit argument above.

Problem 3 (The exterior expansion in Lemma 3.3 has the wrong sign).  Under the positive operator convention [eq:L] (#eq:L), the half-space profile has a negative exterior operator, contrary to the displayed positive leading term.

## Claim and admissible example

Lemma 3.3 assumes (1.2), a \(C^{1,\alpha}\) domain \(\Omega\) with \(0\in\partial\Omega\), and \(Lu=0\) in \(B_1\cap\Omega\), \(u=0\) in \(B_1\setminus\Omega\). It asserts \[u(x)=U_0d(x)^s+O(|x|^{s+\alpha}),\qquad
 Lu(x)=B(\nu)U_0d(x)^{-s}+O(|x|^\alpha)d(x)^{-s}\] on the respective sides, with \(B(\nu)>0\). Equation (3.4) repeats this positive exterior sign for a half-space profile [Retained latex: \cite[pp.~11--12]{RW}].

Take \(n=2\), \(s=1/2\), \(K(h)=|h|^{-3}\), \(\Omega=\{x_2>0\}\), and \(u(x)=\sqrt{(x_2)_+}\). The kernel satisfies all conditions with \(\lambda=\Lambda=1\); the boundary is smooth; \(U_0=u/d^s=1\); and the weighted tail is finite, since its radial growth at infinity gives an integrable \(r^{-1-s}\) bound. Tangential integration yields \[\int_{\mathbb R}(z^2+b^2)^{-3/2}\,dz=2b^{-2}.\] For positive height, the resulting one-dimensional principal value is zero, as explicitly verified in Appendix 5 (#app:halfspace). Thus \(u\) satisfies the required interior equation.

Equation reference: eq:outside. At \(x=(0,-a)\), \(a>0\), direct integration instead gives \[
 Lu(0,-a)=-4\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt t}{(a+t)^2}\,dt
        =-2\pi a^{-1/2}<0.\] A positive \(B(e_2)a^{-1/2}\) cannot agree with [eq:outside] (#eq:outside) up to an \(O(a^\alpha)a^{-1/2}\) remainder. This is a counterexample to the exterior expansion as printed.

## Impact and correction

The missing minus persists in (3.6), page 13. The subsequent expression \(-\int uLv_\varepsilon\) is then treated as positive, which is consistent with a negative exterior operator. Replacing the exterior signs by minus repairs this immediate conflict. This is a Minor local sign defect: no damage to the positive Bernoulli normalization in Proposition 3.1 has been demonstrated. A full independent reconstruction of that variational argument and its constants has not been performed.

Problem 4 (A missing symmetric term in the min–max identity).  The pointwise identity (5.8) on page 54 omits one of the two possible orientations of a crossing pair.

Write \(a=w_1-\varphi_1\) and \(b=w_2-\varphi_2\). The correct identity is \[\begin{split}
 &[(w_1\wedge\varphi_1)-(w_2\wedge\varphi_2)]^2
 +[(w_1\vee\varphi_1)-(w_2\vee\varphi_2)]^2\\
 &\qquad=(w_1-w_2)^2+(\varphi_1-\varphi_2)^2
              -2(a_+b_-+a_-b_+).
\end{split}\] For \(w_1=0,\varphi_1=1,w_2=1,\varphi_2=0\), the left side is zero and the printed right side is two. The corrected formula follows by separating the cases \(ab\ge0\) and the two signs of \(ab<0\). After integration over the symmetric pair domain, the two cross terms have equal integrals. The displayed integrated coefficient should accordingly be four rather than two. This is a Minor local algebraic defect: the factor is absorbed by the unspecified constant in Lemma 5.8 and does not invalidate its estimate or demonstrate a counterexample to the classification theorem.

The logarithmic-cutoff argument on pages 52–55 was also checked at its scaling steps. Its annular sum is of order \(\log R\), which with \((\log R)^{-2}\) gives the stated \((\log R)^{-1}\) bound; the inverse logarithm printed for the sum on page 53 is a local typographical error. In dimension two, the three terms defining \(S_R\) are uniformly bounded by the stated growth estimates, so the subsequent \(R\to\infty\) step is consistent with that decay. These checks do not independently reprove the external energy perturbation and half-space Liouville results.

# Conclusion

The incorrect shear reduction and the invalid derivation of the full exponent range are both Major proof issues in the principal regularity chain. The geometric exponent range has a verified parameter and geometric repair using the manuscript’s preceding estimates, with a coarse-iteration entry step preserving the original flatness threshold. This does not establish exponent-uniform constants for the external boundary regularity estimate. The exterior sign and min–max algebra are local correctable defects. No counterexample to the main regularity or classification theorem, and no author adoption of these corrections, is established.

# Half-space harmonicity and exterior integral

At height one, the one-dimensional integral from \(t<0\) equals \(\int_{-\infty}^0(1-t)^{-2}\,dt=1\). For \(t>0\), the substitution \(t=q^2\) gives \[\mathop{\mathrm{PV}}\int_0^\infty\frac{1-\sqrt t}{(1-t)^2}\,dt
 =\mathop{\mathrm{PV}}\int_0^\infty\frac{2q}{(1-q)(1+q)^2}\,dq=-1,\] using \[\frac{2q}{(1-q)(1+q)^2}
 =\frac1{2(1-q)}+\frac1{2(1+q)}-\frac1{(1+q)^2}.\] An antiderivative is \(-\frac12\log|1-q|+\frac12\log(1+q)+(1+q)^{-1}\); the principal value singularities cancel. Scaling proves interior harmonicity at every positive height. For the exterior integral, \(t=az\) gives \[\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt t}{(a+t)^2}\,dt
 =a^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty\frac{\sqrt z}{(1+z)^2}\,dz
 =\frac{\pi}{2}a^{-1/2}.\] These are analytic calculations; no symbolic or numerical software execution is asserted.
