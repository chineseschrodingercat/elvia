# Identity, conventions and scope

The version metadata mentions a correction to Corollary 1.7; neither the earlier text nor the publisher PDF was compared here. No equivalence with the journal version is asserted. Page references below are PDF pages, matching the printed numbering.

The paper retains loop orientation. Its Brownian generator is \(\Delta\), so the plane kernel is \((4\pi t)^{-1}\exp(-|z-w|^2/(4t))\) and the surface quadratic variation is \(4t\). The loop measure integrates rooted bridges against \(dt/t\) and area before forgetting the root and time parametrization. Essential hyperbolic classes exclude trivial and cusp classes; \(m(\gamma)\) is the positive iteration number.

All source text was read, including the bibliography, but this does not mean that every cited theorem was independently proved or that the bibliography’s external works were read. The checks below concern the displayed arguments, their domains and selected consequences. # Central loop-mass identities

## Hyperbolic classes

In Lemmas 3.1–3.2 (pp. 10–12), write a hyperbolic element as \(h_0=\delta^m\), with \(\delta\) primitive and \(L=\ell(\gamma)>0\). Its centralizer is generated by \(\delta\). The corresponding fundamental strip has logarithmic height \(L/m\), so unfolding over the conjugacy class contributes exactly one factor \(1/m\).

Equation reference: eq:orbital, eq:hyperbolic. An independent evaluation of the orbital integral avoids relying on the paper’s Abel-transform shortcut. With \(\theta=x/y\), \[\cosh d(z,e^Lz)=\cosh L+2\sinh^2(L/2)\theta^2.\] Substitution in the nonnegative heat-kernel integral, Tonelli’s theorem and the elementary angular integral give \[
 O_\gamma(t)=
 \frac{L}{4m\sqrt{\pi t}\sinh(L/2)}
       e^{-t/4-L^2/(4t)}.\] The time integral \[\int_0^\infty t^{-3/2}e^{-t/4-L^2/(4t)}\,dt
     =\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{L}e^{-L/2}\] therefore yields \[
 \mu_X(C_X(\gamma))=\frac{1}{m(e^L-1)}.\] The antiderivative and both signs in (3.6) also passed exact symbolic checks. No additional orientation factor occurs.

Two qualifications explain compressed proof wording without changing this calculation. The fundamental-domain equality on p. 11 needs invariance under the centralizer; the actual integrand \(p_{\mathbb H}(z,h_0z;t)\) has that invariance because the heat kernel is isometry invariant and \(\delta\) commutes with \(h_0\). On p. 10 a local injectivity radius at the fixed point suffices; a globally positive injectivity radius is unnecessary on cusped surfaces. Neither qualification establishes a substantive defect in the stated loop-mass result.

## Flat tori and punctures

Equation reference: eq:torus. For a nonzero lattice displacement \(\tau\) on a flat torus of area \(A\), Lemma 3.3 (p. 13) gives directly \[
 \frac{A}{4\pi}\int_0^\infty t^{-2}e^{-|\tau|^2/(4t)}\,dt
       =\frac{A}{\pi|\tau|^2}.\] Thus the \(m\)-fold class of a primitive geodesic of length \(\ell\) has mass \(A/(\pi m^2\ell^2)\). There is no further \(1/m\): a torus lattice conjugacy class is a single displacement.

For the closed polar removal in Corollary 4.1, loops hitting the removed set have zero measure. The root set also has zero area; this holds for general polar sets, not merely countable ones. A compact loop trace avoiding a closed removed set lies in a positive-distance exhaustion, which supports the monotone-limit argument. The disjoint decomposition into free homotopy classes then gives Theorems 4.2 and 4.3.

The primitive qualification is also consistent. If \(\gamma'=(\delta')^n\) maps to \(\gamma\) upon filling punctures, then \(\gamma\) is an \(n\)th power in the original fundamental group, up to conjugacy. Its cyclic centralizer implies \(n\mid m(\gamma)\). In particular, a class mapping to primitive \(\gamma\) is primitive. Injectivity of the puncture-filling homomorphism is not required.

# Annular consequences and boundary checks

Equation reference: eq:annulus. For an annulus with parameter \(r>0\), the primitive hyperbolic length is \(2\pi^2/r\). For each fixed \(m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), Corollary 4.4 and the expansion on p. 16 give \[
 \frac{1}{|m|(e^{2\pi^2|m|/r}-1)}
   =\frac{r}{2\pi^2m^2}-\frac{1}{2|m|}+O(r^{-1}).\] The modulus difference in Corollary 4.6 tends to \(\log|\psi'(0)|\), so the leading terms yield its conformal-radius formula. In Corollary 4.8 the two-sided exhaustion retains the constant \(+1/(2|m|)\): \[\mu_{\widehat{\mathbb C}}\{\hbox{winding }m\hbox{ loops hitting }K\}
   =\frac{\theta(K)}{2\pi^2m^2}+\frac{1}{2|m|}.\] For the unit circle, \(\theta(K)=0\). Subtracting the inner and outer annular masses from the full annular mass in [eq:annulus] (#eq:annulus) indeed leaves \(1/(2|m|)\), providing a direct check on the sign and constant term.

Equation reference: eq:hit. The actual v3 Corollary 1.7 on p. 5 is \[
 \mu_T\{\eta\simeq\gamma^m,\ \eta\cap\gamma\ne\varnothing\}
 =\frac{A}{\pi\ell^2m^2}
  -\frac{1}{|m|(e^{\pi\ell^2|m|/A}-1)}.\] Cutting along a simple geodesic gives a cylinder of circumference \(\ell\) and height \(A/\ell\), hence \(r=2\pi A/\ell^2\). Equation [eq:hit] (#eq:hit) is precisely total class mass minus avoiding-annulus mass. With \(x=\pi\ell^2|m|/A>0\), it equals \(|m|^{-1}(x^{-1}-(e^x-1)^{-1})\), which is positive and tends to \(1/(2|m|)\) as \(x\downarrow0\). These checks concern the acquired formula and do not reconstruct the earlier reported typo.

# Length sums and the determinant

For Corollary 4.9 (pp. 17–18), the cited finite-type infinite-area counting asymptotic has exponent \(\delta<1\). Combining this with [eq:hyperbolic] (#eq:hyperbolic) makes the long primitive sum convergent; higher iterations decay faster. Elementary annular and punctured-disc cases are treated separately. This verifies the consequence conditional on the quoted counting and spectral inputs.

For Proposition 4.11 (pp. 19–22), put \[R(L)=N_X(L)-\widetilde{\operatorname{Li}}(e^L),\qquad w(L)=\frac{1}{e^L-1}.\] The quoted prime-geodesic remainder gives \(R(L)=O_X(e^{(1-\varepsilon)L})\) for some \(\varepsilon>0\). Consequently \(wR\to0\) and \(w'R\) is integrable at infinity. Near zero the measures vanish below the fixed surface’s systole and the cutoff \(\log2\). Integration by parts therefore justifies the renormalized Stieltjes integral. This is a cancellation statement, not the subtraction of two separately finite totals. The nonprimitive sum converges directly.

The normalization used on p. 21 reduces to \[\frac{e^{-t/4}}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}
 \int_0^\infty2\cosh(L/2)e^{-L^2/(4t)}\,dL=1.\] Completing the square proves the identity. Above \(\log2\), the difference of the comparison densities is \(e^L/L-2\sinh L/L=e^{-L}/L\); the cutoff creates no atom because \(\widetilde{\operatorname{Li}}(2)=0\). These observations verify the signs of the three constant terms on p. 22.

Retained high-precision numerical integrations of both displayed forms of \(C_2\), using stable complementary-error-function expressions, give \[C_2=0.9380116642341289954\ldots,\qquad
 C=C_2-\gamma=0.3607959993325961348\ldots.\] Calculations at two precision settings support the printed \(0.9380\) and \(0.3608\). They are numerical diagnostics, not a certified interval-error proof or a numerical evaluation of the determinant for a particular surface.
