# Problem 2: row-slot scaling does not produce the claimed invertible limit

## Locations and distinction of indices

Lemma 4.1 and the scalings (4.1), (4.33), pp. 245–246 and 252; definition (6.11), p. 264; Lemma 6.8, especially (6.26), p. 268, (6.31), p. 269, and the limit (6.36), p. 270.

A row of the preservation operator is attached to a selected caustic denominator \(q_n\). Its slot in the paired Fourier enumeration is \(n\), whose nominal frequency is \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). These numbers need not agree: the construction omits low denominators and uses several numerators for some denominators. The finite-dimensional rescaling requires the eccentricity factor \[e^{j-q_n}\] in the entry for column frequency \(j\). The left and right factors printed in (6.26) instead combine to \[e^{q_1-\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\,e^{j-q_1}
 =e^{j-\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.\] The other prefactors, which depend on the actual caustic and parity, are finite and nonzero at \(e=0\); they do not remove this discrepancy.

## A zero-row obstruction

Choose the allowed cutoff \(q_0=3\). Lemma 4.1 uses \(p_n/q_n<1/3\), so every selected denominator satisfies \(q_n>3\). For the first nonelliptic row, \(n=6\), the printed scaling gives \[e^{j-3} A_{p_6,q_6,j}(e)
 =O\!\left(e^{\,j-3+|q_6-j|}\right)
 \longrightarrow0,\qquad 3\leq j\leq q_1 .\] Opposite-parity entries vanish identically. For \(j\leq q_6\) the displayed exponent is \(q_6-3>0\); for \(j>q_6\) it is even larger. The entire finite row tends to zero. Hence it cannot tend to the corresponding row of the invertible matrix \(\widehat A\) asserted in (6.36). This obstruction remains after the coordinate correction in Problem 1. Relabelling the rows cannot assign a nonexistent selected denominator \(3\) to this slot.

## Repair and effect on the tail estimates

For each nonelliptic row replace its left eccentricity factor by \[(S^\ell)_n=C_n e^{q_1-q_n},
 \qquad
 (S^r)_i=D_i e^{j_i-q_1},\] retaining the parity-dependent nonzero constants from (4.1) or (4.33). Then the product is the required \(e^{j_i-q_n}\). Since \(q_n\leq q_1\), the corrected left exponents remain nonnegative. The finite-to-high-frequency block \(C\) still tends to zero: each selected functional tends to a harmonic of frequency at most \(q_1\), and the corrected factor is bounded. The high-to-finite block \(B\) is unaffected, because its rows have left factor one. Its bound in (6.43)–(6.44) remains a convergent geometric tail for sufficiently small \(e\). The \(D-\operatorname{Id}\) estimate follows from the square-summable \(j^{-1}\) errors.

There is a separate harmless normalization in the limiting motion block. With the usual normalized constant mode \(e_1=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\) and printed \(h_1(0)=1\), its first entry is \(\sqrt2\), not \(1\). Normalize that mode consistently or use \(\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt2,1,1,1,1)\) instead of the first identity block. Both are invertible.

Severity: Major. The stated invertible limit is the nondegeneracy input for analytic continuation and the nonlinear theorem. The literal scaling does not establish it. # Positive checks, boundary cases and proof dependencies

## Finite algebra and analytic continuation

The odd and even finite-rank mechanisms were checked independently of the erroneous operator scaling. For a new prime \(Q\), the preceding denominators give a cyclotomic field of conductor coprime to \(Q\). The real \(Q\)th-root cosine has degree \((Q-1)/2\) over that field: a smaller degree, followed by adjoining its imaginary partner, would contradict the degree of the compositum. Conjugating vanishing minors and retaining the requisite rank lower bound justifies the kernel argument. The numerator ratios form the required subgroup; a primitive root makes it the whole group.

The even-mode terminal determinant has powers \(1,\ldots,Q-1\). Removing the nonzero factor \(1+\cos(\pi p/Q)\) from each row gives an ordinary Vandermonde on distinct nodes. Five exact determinant calibrations were performed; positivity and distinctness, not those finite examples, justify the general conclusion. The noncoprime rows are higher-harmonic conditions on the same rational caustic and are legitimate.

The primitive-root input is Heath-Brown’s cited theorem for three distinct prime bases, not the full Artin conjecture [HB]. Its bibliographic identity was verified. The complete original proof was not available in this review and is not claimed to have been rechecked. No recent claimed resolution of Artin’s conjecture is used.

The analytic construction has the correct functional-analytic structure after the preceding corrections: a square-summable \(j^{-1}\) Fourier error gives a Hilbert–Schmidt perturbation, bounded holomorphic matrix elements give a holomorphic operator family, and analytic Fredholm theory yields locally finite singular points once one nonsingular eccentricity is established. ## Spectral visibility and conditional global uniqueness

The spectral proof partitions competing orbits into large winding, bounded winding with large period, and finitely many remaining types. The KAM barriers supply the needed exclusion of grazing for each fixed type. Nonprimitive periods are explicitly handled in the normal-form calculation. The \(C^{10}\) input in Lemma 8.1 supports \(C^9\) closeness of the billiard map; the intervening \(C^{11}\) claim for its generating function should be \(C^{10}\). The inverse-coordinate estimate concerns its difference from the identity, and the perimeter normalizer is \(1+O(\varepsilon)\).

Appendix B’s final no-cancellation argument is sound under its stated smooth loop parametrix and positive principal symbol. In the nondegenerate case, after the common Maslov factor is removed, the summands have positive amplitudes times \(\exp(\pm i\pi/4)\), so their real parts cannot cancel. In the degenerate case, smoothness would force the oscillatory integral to be \(O(\lambda^{-1})\), contradicting the stated weighted \(L^2\) obstruction for an exponent below \(1/2\). The complete external KAM, normal-form and microlocal theorems were not rederived from first principles; their uses and boundaries, rather than an independent certification of those works, were examined.

Proposition A.1, pp. 292–293, is expressly conditional on the stated strong global Birkhoff conjecture. Its argument compares a superpolynomial splitting of two endpoint lengths with the ellipse’s adjacent \(q^{-3}\) spacing near a perimeter multiple. After both domains are known to be ellipses, equality of the two tail sets identifies the \(q^{-2}\) coefficient: matching indices have the form \(n(q)=\lambda q+O(q^{-1})\). Consecutive integer gaps make \(\lambda\) an integer, and reverse matching makes its reciprocal an integer. Hence \(\lambda=1\). No conjectural premise has been promoted to a theorem.

The last identification of ellipses by their first and third beta coefficients is supported by Proposition 1 of Sorrentino’s explicitly retrieved arXiv version [S]. Its statement and proof on p. 28, and the rational-caustic criterion on p. 8, were actually inspected. For completeness, set \(r=E(\sqrt m)/K(\sqrt m)\). The convex quadratic is negative at both endpoints: \(P(1-m)=-m(1-m)\) and \(P(1-m/2)=-m^2/4\). This verifies strict monotonicity, hence uniqueness of eccentricity and then scale.
