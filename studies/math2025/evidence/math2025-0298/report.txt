Abstract: This report examines the exact author manuscript arXiv:2205.00935v1. The advertised toric formula omits the factor \(n!\) required by the paper’s stated measure convention. A round ellipsoid gives an exact counterexample. A universal bound under bundle automorphisms is also false, although a bound depending on the fixed automorphism repairs the asymptotic invariance argument. Additional local errors concern trace conversion, homothetic tensor formulas and a minimum on an open face. Their effects and repair boundaries are separated. The main qualitative convexity distinction is not disproved; indeed the normalization correction preserves the growth used in that construction. The supported assessment is Major for the false central quantitative formula, with scientific adjudication pending.

# Framework and scope of the conclusions

The basic data are a compact base \(Y\), a flow \(\phi\), a symplectic cocycle on a bundle \(E\) with \(c_1(E)=0\), and a homotopy class of determinant trivializations. The rotation function is obtained by lifting the determinant of the unitary polar factor; its asymptotic slope is the Ruelle density. The invariant integrates that density against the chosen invariant measure. The finite invariant measures used in the geometric applications supply the integrability needed by the ergodic argument.

For a Liouville domain the canonical Hamiltonian is defined away from the skeleton. The manuscript uses invariant annuli and a limit towards the skeleton. For a star-shaped toric domain, \(\mu_j(z)=\pi|z_j|^2\), \(\theta_j\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), and \(H=f_\Omega\circ\mu\). The flow factors as a diagonal rotation times \(I+tM\), with \(M^2=0\). The latter factor has zero asymptotic rotation, so the density \(\sum_j\partial_jf_\Omega\) is consistent with the explicit flow. The integration measure is the problematic step.

Theorem 1.12 asserts an upper bound for \(\operatorname{Ru}(X)c(X)/\operatorname{vol}(X)\) on convex domains. It uses the trace bound, a comparison of Laplacian integrals, and John’s ellipsoid theorem. The toric construction then produces unbounded Ruelle invariant with bounded volume and a systole bounded below, while strict monotonicity supplies dynamical convexity. The intended contact application is in dimensions \(2n-1\ge3\). No claim about one-dimensional contact index conventions is inferred.

# Confirmed findings

Problem 1 (Major: the toric invariant formula has a missing factorial).  Propositions 1.9 (p. 4) and 5.6 (p. 24) are false under the measure convention explicitly used elsewhere in the manuscript.

Conventions and exact calculation. Example 1.3 (p. 2) uses the invariant measure \(\omega^m\); Example 1.5 (p. 3) uses \(\alpha\wedge(d\alpha)^{n-1}\); Lemma 1.7 (p. 4) equates the domain and boundary invariants. The proof of Theorem 1.12, p. 22, explicitly writes \(\int_X\operatorname{tr}(\nabla^2H)\omega^n=n!\int_X\Delta H\,dV_g\). Thus the distinction between \(\omega^n\) and \(\omega^n/n!\) is not a matter of interpreting an unspecified notation.

On the free torus-action locus, \[\omega=\sum_{j=1}^n d\mu_j\wedge d\theta_j,\qquad
 \omega^n=n!\,d\mu_1\,d\theta_1\cdots d\mu_n\,d\theta_n.\] Each angular coordinate has period one. Coordinate hyperplanes have zero volume. Integrating the asserted, independently checked density therefore gives \[\boxed{\ \operatorname{Ru}_\omega(X_\Omega)
       =n!\int_\Omega\sum_{j=1}^n\partial_jf_\Omega\,d\mu\ }.\] Both printed propositions omit \(n!\).

Take the round ellipsoid \[X=E(a,\ldots,a)=\{\pi\textstyle\sum_j|z_j|^2\le a\},\qquad a>0,\ n\ge2.\] It is smooth, strictly convex, star-shaped and toric, with trivial tangent bundle and the standard determinant trivialization. Its canonical Hamiltonian is \(H=\pi\sum_j|z_j|^2/a\), so \(T\Phi_t=e^{2\pi it/a}I_n\). The lifted determinant has slope \(n/a\). Its moment simplex has Euclidean volume \(a^n/n!\), whereas \(\int_X\omega^n=a^n\). Consequently \[\operatorname{Ru}_\omega(X)=n a^{n-1},
 \qquad
 \text{printed toric expression}=\frac{n a^{n-1}}{n!}.\] Already \(n=2,a=1\) gives \(2\) versus \(1\). All assumptions of the toric proposition hold, and there is no degenerate-orbit exclusion in that proposition.

Dependence, impact and repairs. This is an explicit central quantitative result, advertised in the introduction and reused in Proposition 5.16, not only a typesetting error in an auxiliary proof. Major severity is based on the incorrect value of the defined invariant. It does not assert that the final existence of nonconvex dynamically convex domains is false.

Keeping the stated measures requires the factorial above. Alternatively, normalizing the domain measure by \(n!\) would require corresponding changes to the boundary measure, Lemma 1.7 and trace formulas; one cannot change only the toric formula’s interpretation. For the flat ellipsoid in Proposition 5.16, the corrected lower bound is \[n!\left(\frac{1}{n!A}-\frac{B^{n-1}}{A^n}\right)
       \left(A^n+\frac{n-1}{A}\right)
       \sim A^{n-1}\longrightarrow\infty
 \quad(n\ge2).\] Thus the normalization correction preserves the divergence used there. The replacement of an estimated volume by an equality on p. 28 should also be an inequality; the same lower bound remains valid.

The volume convention in the last inequality on p. 23 needs explicit attention: with \(V_g=\int\omega^n/n!\), the displayed chain retains a factorial; with \(V_\omega=\int\omega^n\), it cancels. This observation alone does not disprove the stated constant. In fact, the same numerical constant can be recovered even for \(V_g\) by a small, independently justified improvement of the sandwich argument, as shown in Section 4 below.

Problem 2 (Minor: the automorphism bound is not uniform over automorphisms).  Lemma 3.10 and equations (3.3)–(3.4), p. 15, replace an endpoint gauge transformation by pointwise conjugation.

For definiteness write a gauge transform as \[\Phi^\Psi(t,x)=\Psi(\phi_t x)\Phi(t,x)\Psi(x)^{-1}.\] The inverse convention gives the same issue after replacing \(\Psi\) by \(\Psi^{-1}\). In a trajectory trivialization the two endpoint factors occur at \(t\) and \(0\), respectively. Equation (3.3) instead uses \(\Psi_\Xi(t)\) on both sides of \(\Phi_\Xi(t)\), and the proof consequently claims the universal bound \(3c\), independent of \(\Psi\).

Let \(Y=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), \(\phi_t(x)=x+t\), with normalized Lebesgue measure. Take the trivial symplectic line bundle, its standard determinant trivialization, and the identity cocycle. For any \(N>0\), let \[h_N(x)=N\sin(2\pi x),\qquad \Psi_N(x)=e^{2\pi i h_N(x)}.\] This is a smooth unitary, hence symplectic, bundle automorphism homotopic to the identity through \(e^{2\pi i s h_N}\). The transformed cocycle has rotation lift \(h_N(x+t)-h_N(x)\), with zero initial lift. At \(x=1/4,t=1/2\) its value is \(-2N\), whereas the original rotation lift is zero. No universal \(C\), and in particular no fixed \(3c\), bounds all these differences. The statement concerns lifts, so an integer endpoint winding is not discarded.

For a fixed automorphism, however, a bounded lift exists over compact \(Y\) under the stated homotopy assumption. Let \(B_\Psi\) bound its rotation value in unitary trivializations, and \(D\) be a quasimorphism defect bound. Applying the quasimorphism inequality to the correct endpoint product gives, for example, \[|\widetilde u_T(\Phi^\Psi)-\widetilde u_T(\Phi)|
       \le 2B_\Psi+3D .\] Unitary changes of frame preserve the determinant polar rotation; the bound is finite and independent of \(T,x\), though it depends on \(\Psi\). Dividing by \(T\), and integrating against a finite measure when required, gives zero. This weaker bound is sufficient for the fixed change of complex structure in Proposition 3.11, pp. 15–16. The asymptotic density is zero for every cocycle in the explicit counterexample as well. Minor severity reflects this verified repair of the actual downstream use, despite the false universal statement.

Problem 3 (Minor: the complex-to-real trace conversion has a factor error).  Equation (2.2), p. 8, has coefficient \(2i\) where \(i/2\) is required.

Let \(A=I_{2n}\), \(S=I_{2n}\), and let \(J=\Omega\) be the displayed standard complex structure. All hypotheses of Lemma 2.2 hold, including positive definiteness of \(S\). The polar derivative in direction \(J\) is \(TU_I(J)=J\). The left side of (2.2) is \(\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb CJ=ni\), while its right side is \[2i\,\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb R(J^{-1}J)=4ni.\] For any skew-Hermitian \(K\), diagonalization with eigenvalues \(i\theta_j\) gives the correct identity \(\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb CK=(i/2)\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb R(J^{-1}K)\).

The subsequent real-trace estimate on p. 9 is \(8n^2\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb RS\). With the correct conversion it yields the stronger complex-trace bound \(4n^2\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb RS\), so the stated weaker \(16n^2\operatorname{tr}_\mathbb RS\) bound in Lemma 2.2 still follows. Proposition 2.9 and Lemma 3.14 therefore do not lose their stated upper constants through this error. This is a false identity with a verified harmless direction for the bound, hence Minor.

Problem 4 (Minor: two homothetic identities have incorrect factors or slopes).  The second identity in Lemma 4.4 (p. 18) and the divergence assertion in Corollary 4.6 (p. 19) are false as printed.

Use \(M=\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), \(d\ge2\), the Euclidean metric and \(V=x/2\). This is cylindrical with spherical section, \(\mathcal L_Vg=g\), and \(\nabla V=\tfrac12I\). For \(U=W=\partial_{x_1}\), the left side of the printed identity is \[\langle\nabla_UV,W\rangle+\langle U,\nabla_WV\rangle=1,\] while the stated right side is \(1/2\). The correct right side is \(\langle U,W\rangle\). The proof immediately below already obtains that correct identity; the symmetric part of \(\nabla V\) is consequently \(\tfrac12I\), as used in Lemma 4.7.

If \(\mathcal L_VU=aU\), then \(\mathcal L_V(\operatorname{div}U)=a\operatorname{div}U\). Divergence is the natural contraction of \(\nabla U\); unlike the gradient or Laplacian of a function, it introduces no inverse-metric factor. For the concrete \(U=V\), one has \(a=0\) and \(\operatorname{div}V=d/2\ne0\), which has slope zero, not \(-1\). The function statements in Corollary 4.6, \(\nabla F\) and \(\Delta F\) of slope \(a-1\), are consistent.

The variation proof on p. 20 uses the correct slope one for \(\operatorname{div}(h\nabla H)\). Correcting these local statements therefore agrees with the calculation actually needed for Propositions 4.8 and 4.10. No failure of the sandwich estimate is established by this issue.

Problem 5 (Minor: the bracket minimum need not exist on the stated open face).  Definition 5.13, p. 27, is not well-defined for every permitted \(v\), even for the round ball.

Take the moment region \(\Omega=\{(x_1,x_2)\ge0:x_1+x_2\le1\}\). Its toric domain is a smooth star-shaped ball and its complement in the orthant is convex. For \(v=(1,2)\), the support is \(S=\{1,2\}\). The definition restricts to \[\partial_+\Omega_S=\{(1-s,s):0<s<1\}.\] The objective is \(1+s\), with infimum \(1\) and no minimum. Thus the displayed minimum fails to assign a value to this admissible integer vector.

Replacing the minimum by an infimum, or taking the closure of the relative face before minimizing, repairs this defect. For smooth compact star-shaped regions the closure is compact and the linear functional is continuous; the infimum on the face equals the minimum on its closure. Radial comparison also preserves the monotonicity of these infima under inclusion. The claimed systole formula of Lemma 5.14 is cited from external capacity theory; this review does not independently reprove it. The example identifies a local domain error, not a counterexample to systole monotonicity or Proposition 5.16 after the definition is corrected.

# Verified repairs of the quantitative argument

For clarity, the following refinement handles the factorial in the convex-domain bound without claiming optimal constants. In Proposition 4.10 assume its stated convexity and self-adjointness conditions and \(G\le H\le LG\). Put \(F_t=(1-t)H+tG\). On \(X_1=\{G\le1\}\), \[-(L-1)\le G-H\le0.\] The proof of the proposition uses the weaker lower bound \(-L\). Keeping \(-(L-1)\), the variation formula and \(|\nabla F_t|^2\le2\Delta F_t\) on \(\{F_t=1\}\) give \[\frac{d}{dt}S(F_t)\ge
       -\frac{(L-1)d^2}{2}\,S(F_t),\qquad
 S(H)\le e^{(L-1)d^2/2}S(G).\] Indeed the bracket \((d+2)|\nabla F_t|^2/2-2\Delta F_t\) is at most \(d\Delta F_t\); multiplication by the nonpositive \(G-H\) yields the required lower bound, also when that bracket is negative. The homogeneity identity then converts the boundary integral of \(\Delta F_t\) to \((d/2)S(F_t)\).

Using \(L=(2n)^2\), \(d=2n\), and the source’s trace constant gives \[\operatorname{Ru}_\omega(X)c(X)
 \le 32n^3(2n)^{2n}n!\,e^{8n^4-2n^2}V_g(X).\] Since \(\log(n!)\le n\log n\le n^2\) for \(n\ge1\), this is bounded by \[C(n)V_g(X),\qquad C(n)=2^{2n+5}n^{2n+3}e^{8n^4}.\] Thus a factorial omitted in the printed chain does not by itself falsify the numerical upper constant: the repaired calculation above recovers it. This distinguishes the false exact toric formula from the much less sharp upper convexity bound.

Problem 6 (Minor: lower-index formulas exceed the declared domain of \(CZ\)).  Definition 2.11 and Lemma 2.12(b), pp. 10–11, use \(CZ\) without the nondegeneracy restriction required by its definition. The direct-sum statement for arbitrary endpoints requires \(LCZ\).

The dynamical-convexity argument was also traced through its dependencies. For a closed orbit with \(m\) nonzero coordinates, the diagonal loop contributes at least \(2m\), the unipotent block at least \(-m\), and the remaining positive rotations at least \(n-m\), giving the required Hamiltonian lower index \(n\). The conversion to the contact lower index uses the constant two-plane contribution \(-1\) in Lemma 2.16. This verification uses the lower semicontinuous index, including degenerate orbits, rather than silently imposing nondegeneracy. The printed \(CZ\) in the general direct-sum line of Lemma 2.12(b) must be read as \(LCZ\); \(CZ\) itself is not defined at an identity endpoint. Likewise the approximating sequence in Definition 2.11 must have nondegenerate endpoints. These are domain/notation corrections to the stated index convention, not a newly demonstrated failure of the \(2m-m+(n-m)\) bound.

# Assessment and limitations

The normalization counterexample changes a central explicit conclusion of the exact manuscript. Its repair is dimension-dependent and must be propagated consistently across measures, domain and boundary invariants, and toric calculations. The other confirmed issues are narrower: their corrections were checked against the specific downstream arguments.

No counterexample to the qualitative existence of dynamically convex, nonsymplectically convex domains has been established. The finite-measure ergodic framework, capacity input, contact index foundations and concave smoothing are not fully rederived here. The reviewed construction uses gauges and canonical Hamiltonians away from the origin or skeleton; no unjustified global smoothness at that singular set is used in the checks.
