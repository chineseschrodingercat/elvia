Abstract: This review identifies six concerns in the specified author manuscript: an incorrect objectwise calculation for an augmented complex, a false exterior-power formula, an invalid step in the proof of flag surjectivity, a reversed filtration convention, omitted degenerate relaxation cases, and a scalar-field mismatch in the determinant differential. Explicit examples establish the affected claims and constructions. Complete replacement arguments are supplied for flag surjectivity and rational determinant normalization; more limited repairs are distinguished from them. The maximization theorem itself is not refuted by the augmented-complex example. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

We retain the paper’s conventions. A morphism \(M\to M'\) on one ground set means that every basis of \(M'\) is a basis of \(M\); the ranks agree and \(\operatorname{rk}_{M'}(S)\leq\operatorname{rk}_M(S)\) for every \(S\) (p. 15). Write \(d(M)=\dim P(M)\), distinct from matroid rank. For a decomposition \(\mathcal Q\) of a \(d\)-dimensional polyhedron \(P\), \(C_\bullet^\Omega(\mathcal Q)\) includes both the internal-cell terms and an augmentation \(P\) in degree \(d+1\) (pp. 10, 17). The notation \(X[-i]\) places \(X\) in degree \(i\) (p. 7). Exact finite complexes of \(\mathbb Q\)-vector spaces are split exact; this does not identify exactness with contractibility in an arbitrary additive category.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The augmented complex contradicts Lemma 6.9).  Assessment: Major. On p. 29, \(C(F)_\bullet=\Delta_\psi(C_\bullet^\Omega(\mathcal Q))^F\) contains all copies of \(F\) in the full augmented complex. For \(F\) internal in \(\mathcal Q_\psi\), Lemma 6.9 (p. 30) claims \[C(F)_\bullet\simeq F[-\dim P+\dim P_\psi-\dim F].\] An allowed trivial decomposition contradicts this claim.

Take \(V=\mathbb R\), \(P=[0,1]\), \(\psi(x)=x\), and \(\mathcal Q=\{\{0\},\{1\},[0,1]\}\). This finite collection is face-closed, has intersections that are common faces, and covers \(P\); Remark 3.1 explicitly permits it. The functional is nonconstant and bounded above. Moreover \(P_\psi=F=\{1\}\) and \(\mathcal Q_\psi=\{\{1\}\}\), whose sole zero-dimensional cell is internal relative to \(P_\psi\).

Equation reference: eq:aug. Here \(\mathcal Q_1=\mathcal Q_2=\{P\}\) and \(\mathcal Q_0=\varnothing\). With agreeing orientations the source complex is \(P\) in degree \(2\) mapped by \(\mathrm{id}\) to \(P\) in degree \(1\). By the definition of \(\Delta_\psi\) (p. 26), \[
 C(F)_\bullet=(F\text{ in degree }2
       \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_F}F\text{ in degree }1)=\operatorname{Null}(F,2).\] Taking \(h_1=\mathrm{id}_F\) contracts it. After the trivial vector-space functor \(\tau\), all homology vanishes, whereas Lemma 6.9 predicts \(F[-1]\), with \(H_1=\mathbb Q\).

The same failure occurs for the nontrivial subdivision of \([0,2]\) at \(1\): the full image complex is \(\operatorname{Null}(\{2\},2)\oplus\operatorname{Null}(\{1\},1)\). The augmentation maps isomorphically to the cell ending at \(2\); the cell ending at \(1\) maps isomorphically to the internal vertex. The maximizing-face block again has zero homology.

Theorem 6.3’s proof (p. 31) uses Lemma 6.9 to determine a minimal complex \(D_\bullet\) for the full image complex. In the trivial example this minimum is zero, yet the proof identifies its shift, as a graded object, with the nonzero two-term augmented \(C_\bullet(\mathcal Q_\psi)\). This stronger identification is also false. The artificial \(P\) term cannot silently be discarded: the full and truncated complexes are separately defined, and p. 29 requires all copies of \(F\). The one-object calculation [eq:aug] (#eq:aug) is unaffected by the order repair in Problem Problem 4 (#prob:order).

Theorem 6.3 is explicitly the technical heart (p. 26). Its use in Lemma 7.4 leads to Theorem 7.3 and categorical convolution in Corollary 7.7, so this is a central invalid derivation. Theorem 6.3 itself is not refuted: its asserted homotopy equivalence holds in these examples because both augmented complexes are contractible. Replacing the blocks by those of \(\Delta_\psi(C_{\leq d}^\Omega(\mathcal Q))\) fixes these local calculations, but a general proof must then transport and verify the augmentation before taking its cone. That complete repair has not been established here.

Problem 2 (Corollary 9.8 has the wrong exterior-power degree).  Assessment: Major. Corollary 9.8 (p. 52) uses \(\bigwedge^k V_{[h-1,1]}\) in the Orlik–Solomon relaxation formula, where \(k=\operatorname{rk}M\) and \(h=|H|\). Lemma 9.7 on the same page uses \(\bigwedge^{k-1}V_{[h-1,1]}\). The corollary’s formula gives an incorrect graded dimension.

Let \(\Gamma=\{1\}\), \(E=\{1,2,3\}\), \(H=\{1,2\}\), and \[M=U_{1,\{1,2\}}\sqcup U_{1,\{3\}},\qquad k=2,\quad h=2.\] The bases are \(13,23\). The set \(H\) is a rank-one flat since adjoining \(3\) raises its rank. Its restriction and contraction are \(U_{1,2}\) and \(U_{1,1}\), respectively, so it is stressed under p. 13’s definition. Its cusp is \(\{12\}\) and relaxation gives \(\widetilde M=U_{2,3}\). The group, orbit, induction, and restriction factors are trivial.

The circuit \(12\) gives \(u_2-u_1=0\) in \(\operatorname{OS}(M)\), leaving dimensions \((1,2,1)\). For \(U_{2,3}\) the boundary of the only circuit \(123\) gives one relation among three degree-two monomials, yielding \((1,3,2)\). Equivalently, for \(1<2<3\) the no-broken-circuit sets are \(\varnothing,1,3,13\) and \(\varnothing,1,2,3,12,13\), respectively. Consequently \[\pi_{\widetilde M}(t)-\pi_M(t)=t+t^2.\] Since \(V_{[1,1]}\) is one-dimensional, \(\bigwedge^2V_{[1,1]}=0\), and the printed corollary predicts zero difference. Independent finite enumeration confirms these basis, flatness, and dimension calculations [checks].

Equation reference: eq:wedgefix. The supported local correction replaces \(k\) by \(k-1\) in the exterior power: \[
 \operatorname{OS}(\widetilde M)=\operatorname{OS}(M)+
 \operatorname{Ind}_{\Gamma_H}^{\Gamma}
 \left(\operatorname{Res}_{\Gamma_H}^{S_h}\bigwedge\nolimits^{k-1}V_{[h-1,1]}\right)
 \otimes\bigl(\mathbb Q(1-k)\oplus\mathbb Q(-k)\bigr).\] Here restriction means pullback along \(\Gamma_H\to S_h\). Where Proposition 9.6 and Lemma 9.7 apply, substitution gives [eq:wedgefix] (#eq:wedgefix), and it restores the displayed example. This does not separately settle all exceptional orbits in Problem Problem 5 (#prob:relax). The false quantitative formula is an explicitly advertised application (p. 4), and the ease of editing its exponent does not remove that impact. This example does not invalidate categorical valuativity or the corrected formula.

Problem 3 (Proposition 8.19 assumes a false intersection rank).  Assessment: Major. The induction proving Whitney-map surjectivity (p. 43) asserts that a source lift \(F_2\) with \(\operatorname{cl}_{M'}(F_2)=F'_2\) makes \(F'_1\cap F_2\) a rank-\(k_1\) flat of \(M'|F_2\). That rank does not follow from the induction hypothesis.

Take \[M=U_{3,4},\qquad
 M'=U_{2,\{1,2,3\}}\sqcup U_{1,\{4\}}.\] Every \(M'\)-basis (\(124,134,234\)) is an \(M\)-basis, so the identity map is an allowed rank-preserving weak map. The target flag \(F'_1=\{3\}\subset F'_2=\{1,2,3\}\) has ranks \(1,2\). The source flat \(F_2=\{1,2\}\) has rank two and \(\operatorname{cl}_{M'}(F_2)=F'_2\), but \(F'_1\cap F_2=\varnothing\) has rank zero. Thus an arbitrary valid inductive lift need not have the asserted compatibility with the lower flag. A different lift, such as \(\{1,3\}\), would work; the final existence claim is not refuted. The invalid step feeds directly into Corollary 8.20, a main monotonicity result highlighted on p. 5.

Verified replacement argument 1.  Every Whitney map \(\Phi_{\boldsymbol k}(M)\to\Phi_{\boldsymbol k}(M')\) associated with the paper’s weak maps is surjective.

Proof. Identify the ground sets. For a target flag \(F'_1\subset\cdots\subset F'_r\), choose nested bases \(B_1\subset\cdots\subset B_r\), with \(B_i\) a basis of \(F'_i\) in \(M'\). An \(M'\)-independent set extends to an \(M'\)-basis, hence is also \(M\)-independent. Put \(F_i=\operatorname{cl}_M(B_i)\). These are nested source flats of ranks \(k_i\), strictly nested for strictly increasing \(k_i\).

If \(x\in F_i\), then \[k_i=\operatorname{rk}_{M'}(B_i)
 \leq\operatorname{rk}_{M'}(B_i\cup\{x\})
 \leq\operatorname{rk}_M(B_i\cup\{x\})=k_i.\] Hence \(x\in\operatorname{cl}_{M'}(B_i)=F'_i\) and \(B_i\subseteq F_i\subseteq F'_i\). Taking closure in \(M'\) gives \(\operatorname{cl}_{M'}(F_i)=F'_i\), so the source flag maps to the prescribed target flag. For rank zero, take the empty basis; the same argument uses the source and target loop flats. ◻

This proves the exact proposition without a new assumption. The direct sums of shifted Whitney functors then give Corollary 8.20. For the ordinary Chow functor a looped target has zero space, and a weak map from a looped source to a loopless target cannot occur; the remaining cases use those direct sums. The augmented functor is handled directly. This is a complete analytic repair, separate from the valuativity proof issues elsewhere.

Problem 4 (The objectwise filtration uses the opposite order).  Assessment: Minor. Section 2.4 (p. 9) defines \(X\leq Y\) by an arrow \(X\to Y\), then uses lower-object collections for subcomplexes \(C^{\leq X}\) and \(C^{<X}\). Their quotient need not contain only copies of \(X\).

In the thin category with \(a<b\) and one nonidentity arrow \(f:a\to b\), take the bounded complex \(C_1=a\xrightarrow{f}C_0=b\) in its additive \(\mathbb Q\)-linear closure. A termwise-split subcomplex supported only on \(a\) is zero: otherwise \(f\) would land in the excluded \(b\) term. Thus \(C^{\leq a}=C^{<b}=0\) and \(C^{\leq b}=C\), making the paper’s \(C^b\) the full two-object complex. It is minimal in the stated sense, since the differential joins distinct objects, but its differential is nonzero. This contradicts the asserted triviality of the differential of a minimal objectwise part.

Use upper sets \(C^{\geq X},C^{>X}\) and quotient \(C^{\geq X}/C^{>X}\) instead, or reverse the order convention consistently. Upper sets are closed under arrows; the quotient contains only the object class \(X\), whose minimal complex has zero differential. This completely repairs the local filtration statement and does not refute minimal-complex uniqueness. It cannot repair the independent one-object counterexample in Problem Problem 1 (#prob:aug).

Problem 5 (Relaxation statements omit degenerate configurations).  Assessment: Minor. The exceptional branch of Theorem 3.10 (p. 14) identifies simultaneous orbit relaxation with one single-flat relaxation when \(M=\Pi\). Theorem 3.8 also misclassifies internal faces when a stressed flat has empty cusp, a case allowed by the printed definition.

For the orbit case take \(F=\{1,2\}\), \(F'=\{3,4\}\), and \[M=U_{1,F}\sqcup U_{1,F'}=\Pi_{1,2,F,E},\qquad
 \Gamma=\langle(13)(24)\rangle.\] The two blocks are rank-one flats with uniform restriction and contraction; \(\Gamma\) preserves \(M\) and swaps them. Their cusps are \(\{12\}\) and \(\{34\}\), so \[\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal B(M)&=\{13,14,23,24\},\\
 \mathcal B(\Lambda_F)&=\mathcal B(M)\cup\{12\},\\
 \mathcal B(\Lambda_{F'})&=\mathcal B(M)\cup\{34\},\\
 \mathcal B(\widetilde M)&=\binom E2.
 \end{aligned}\] Thus \(\widetilde M=U_{2,4}\) has six bases, while \(\Lambda_F\) has five. Its octahedron is divided into two maximal square pyramids by \(x_1+x_2=1\), with internal common square \(P(M)\). The point \((1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)\) witnesses internality. Theorem 3.10’s asserted trivial decomposition of \(\Lambda_F\) is false; Example 3.11 (p. 15) expressly admits this orbit.

For the empty-cusp case take \(M=U_{2,3}\), \(F=\{1\}\). It is a rank-one flat with uniform restriction \(U_{1,1}\) and contraction \(U_{1,2}\). But no two-element set contains two elements of \(F\), so the cusp is empty and \(\widetilde M=M\). Here \(\Pi=\Lambda\) has bases \(12,13\), whereas \(M\) also has basis \(23\). Although \(M\ne\Pi\), \(P(\Pi)\) is the boundary edge \(x_1=1\) of the triangle \(P(M)\), and the only internal face is \(M\). This contradicts Theorem 3.8’s second branch; p. 13 supplies no nonempty-cusp exclusion.

An orbit-singleton condition makes the first asserted trivial identification valid, while a nonempty-cusp condition excludes the second example. A complete classification of all degenerate orbits is not supplied here. The same incorrect shortcut \(\widetilde M=\Lambda_F\) opens the proof of Proposition 9.6 (p. 50), but its formula survives the square example. Writing \(\varepsilon\) for the sign character of \(C_2\), the actual determinant-twisted subdivision gives \[\Phi(U_{2,4})=\operatorname{Ind}_1^{C_2}\Phi(\Lambda_F)-\Phi(M)\otimes\varepsilon.\] The identity \(\operatorname{Ind}_1^{C_2}\operatorname{Res}_1^{C_2}\Phi(M)=\Phi(M)\otimes(1\oplus\varepsilon)\) makes this agree with Proposition 9.6’s printed formula. Thus these examples establish a false exceptional classification and proof shortcut, but not a false final representation identity.

Problem 6 (A unit normal need not be a rational morphism).  Assessment: Major. Sections 9.2–9.3 (pp. 48–49) define the rational spaces \[V(N)=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb R}\{x-y:x,y\in P(N)\}\cap\mathbb Q^E,\qquad
 L(N,N')=\bigwedge^{d(N)-d(N')}(V(N)/V(N')).\] The differential is specified as the class of an outward unit normal. That real class need not belong to the rational morphism line.

Split \(P=P(U_{2,5})=\{x\in[0,1]^5:\sum x_i=2\}\) at \(x_1+x_2=1\). Let \(H=\{1,2\}\). The upper cell \(N_+\) has all two-element bases meeting \(H\); the lower \(N_-\) has all pairs except \(12\); the common cell is \(N_0=U_{1,\{1,2\}}\sqcup U_{1,\{3,4,5\}}\). These are rank-two matroids: \(N_+\) has parallel class \(\{3,4,5\}\), \(N_-\) has parallel class \(\{1,2\}\), and \(N_0\) has the displayed two parallel classes. Basis exchange was also independently enumerated.

Their polytopes are the two closed half-hypersimplices and the common cut, as follows directly from the rank-two parallel-class inequalities. Equivalently, the cut creates no new nonintegral vertices, since \(x_1+x_2\) changes by at most one along a hypersimplex edge. Including all faces gives a matroid subdivision. The point \((1/2,1/2,1/3,1/3,1/3)\) lies in the relative interior of the cut and of \(P\), so \(N_0\) is internal. The dimensions are \(4,4,3\).

The maximal-cell tangent space is \(V=\{v\in\mathbb Q^5:\sum v_i=0\}\) and \[V(N_0)=\{v\in\mathbb Q^5:v_1+v_2=0,\ v_3+v_4+v_5=0\}.\] An orthogonal normal in \(V\) is \(n=(3,3,-2,-2,-2)\) with \(\|n\|^2=30\); the outward unit normals are \(\pm n/\sqrt{30}\). The rational functional \(\delta_H(v)=v_1+v_2\) annihilates \(V(N_0)\) and is rational-valued on the rational quotient. On the proposed unit-normal class it instead gives \(\pm6/\sqrt{30}\), an irrational number. That class is not a morphism in the specified category. This is a scalar-field defect, independent of a real-field check of \(d^2=0\).

A coherent rational repair uses \(\Lambda_N=V(N)\cap\mathbb Z^E\). For a facet \(N'\), take the outward primitive generator of the rank-one lattice \(\Lambda_N/\Lambda_{N'}\). The sublattice is saturated, so the quotient is free, and the determinant isomorphism for \[0\longrightarrow\Lambda_{N'}\longrightarrow\Lambda_N
 \longrightarrow\Lambda_N/\Lambda_{N'}\longrightarrow0\] takes primitive generators to primitive generators. Orienting these determinant lattices identifies the differentials with ordinary incidence coefficients \(\pm1\). For a codimension-two incidence the two facet paths give opposite primitive generators of the same determinant line and cancel. The augmentation uses the common tangent lattice of parent and maximal cells, so its diagonal maps remain identities. Coordinate permutations preserve both the lattices and outward rays, establishing equivariance.

This verifies the replacement differential and Proposition 9.2’s orientation comparison: primitive oriented determinant bases identify the corrected complex with the ordinary oriented decomposition complex. Merely omitting the word unit would not prescribe coherent scalars. The original undefined rational complex is central to the orientation-independent machinery of Corollary 9.3, supporting Major. The rational replacement restores that construction for a valuative functor; it does not refute the virtual-character identity or resolve Problem Problem 1 (#prob:aug).

# Surviving checks, dependence, and recorded state

Lemma 5.2 (pp. 23–24) correctly turns independence of \(S_e\cup\{e\}\) into simultaneous strict-halfspace feasibility by a convex combination of basis vertices with positive telescoping weights. For finite matroid decompositions, the relative-cell argument and finite filtered-complex step in Proposition 5.3 and Theorem 5.5 (pp. 24–25) are consistent; no substantive issue was established in that Orlik–Solomon valuativity proof. Lemma 7.1 (p. 32) follows by selecting exactly the bases with \(|B\cap E_1|=\operatorname{rk}_M(E_1)\).

For the Whitney filtration (pp. 37–38), closure under weak maps increases flat cardinalities. Unchanged total cardinality forces each flat to be preserved, explaining the associated fixed-flag summands. Loop truncation does not itself obstruct the subsequent constructions: an internal subdivision cell has the parent’s loop set, since an additional loop would place it in a proper boundary hyperplane \(x_e=0\). These checks are local; the categorical convolution route still requires a valid general proof of Theorem 6.3.

Exact counterexamples and analytic repairs require no empirical reruns. Conjectural Kazhdan–Lusztig monotonicity is not treated as a theorem.
