Abstract: This report concerns Bogdan Zavyalov’s author manuscript arXiv:2111.01830v3, not an independently matched journal version. All 101 source PDF pages were read and visually inspected. The principal finding is an incompatible normalization in the two local definitions used to glue Faltings’ trace: the generic-fiber diagram introduces an extra factor \((1-\zeta_p)^{-d}\). A smooth one-dimensional formal torus verifies the contradiction under all the stated hypotheses. This is a Major defect in the printed core construction, with a verified local correction; it does not refute mod-\(p\) Poincaré duality. A second, Minor finding concerns a false multiplication isomorphism for semistable character spaces. Scope and shift clarifications are recorded separately. External foundational results were not independently reproved in full.

# Definitions, hypotheses, and coverage

The main theorem concerns a smooth proper rigid-analytic \(K\)-space of pure dimension \(d\), for a complete rank-one valued mixed-characteristic \((0,p)\) field \(K\). Write \(C\) for its completed algebraic closure. The geometric pairing has the form \[H^i_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_C,\mathbf F_p)\otimes_{\mathbf F_p}
H^{2d-i}_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_C,\mathbf F_p(d))
 \longrightarrow H^{2d}_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_C,\mathbf F_p(d))
 \xrightarrow{t_X}\mathbf F_p.\] It is asserted to be perfect and Galois-equivariant. Connectedness is used in constructing the top-degree identification, then removed by taking the finite disjoint union of connected components. The pure-dimension hypothesis is retained throughout.

For local trace constructions, a nice formal scheme means a separated admissible formal \(\mathcal O_C\)-scheme with smooth pure-dimensional generic fiber and reduced special fiber (Definition 3.5.1). The manuscript’s admissible formal schemes are qcqs. The almost category is relative to the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m_C\); a module killed by one nonzero element of \(\mathfrak m_C\) need not be almost zero. The distinction matters in both counterexamples below.

# Problem 1: incompatible normalization of the trace (Major)

## Exact source claim and dependency

Theorem–Definition 3.4.13, p. 43, diagram (3.7), defines the generic-fiber map \((\Psi'^n_{\mathfrak X})_C\). Both left corners of the diagram are \[E_C:=\left(\widetilde{R^n\nu_*\widehat{\mathcal O}_X^+}\right)_C .\] The upward left arrow is explicitly multiplication by \((1-\zeta_p)^n\), while the bottom horizontal arrow is \((\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})_C^{-1}\). This is also explicit in `main.tex`, line 2027, within lines 2023–2035. Remark 3.4.15, p. 44, nevertheless identifies the resulting map with the inverse of Scholze’s comparison. Theorem 3.5.2, p. 44 (TeX lines 2060–2085), then requires a trace whose restriction to the smooth formal locus is the map of Definition 3.3.5, and whose generic fiber is the map of Definition 3.4.14.

The suspect step is the last sentence of the proof of Theorem 3.5.2: the two prescribed maps are said to coincide on the overlap. They do not coincide with diagram (3.7) as printed.

## Exact scalar calculation

Equation reference: eq:bad-normalization. Put \(q=1-\zeta_p\) and \[j=\operatorname{id}\otimes(\operatorname{dlog}^{\vee})^{\otimes n}.\] Theorem 3.4.12, pp. 42–43, gives, under its stated canonical identifications, \[(\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})_C=\operatorname{Sch}_X^n\circ j.\] Thus diagram (3.7) says \[(\Psi'^n_{\mathfrak X})_C\circ(q^n\operatorname{id})
       =j\circ(\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})_C^{-1}
       =(\operatorname{Sch}_X^n)^{-1}.\] Consequently its actual definition is \[
(\Psi'^n_{\mathfrak X})_C=q^{-n}(\operatorname{Sch}_X^n)^{-1}.\] All these are equalities of maps over \(C\); there is no integrality ambiguity in cancelling \(q^n\).

Equation reference: eq:good-normalization. By contrast, Theorem–Definition 3.3.4, pp. 36–37, uses the submodule \((\zeta_p-1)^nE\) at the lower left and its inclusion into \(E\). Its proof gives the explicit formula \[\Psi'^n_{\mathrm{sm}}(x)
 =\frac{j\big((\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})^{-1}((\zeta_p-1)^nx)\big)}
              {(\zeta_p-1)^n}.\] After inverting \(p\), linearity cancels the two factors, so \[
(\Psi'^n_{\mathrm{sm}})_C
   =j(\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})_C^{-1}=(\operatorname{Sch}_X^n)^{-1}.\] The sign difference between \(\zeta_p-1\) and \(1-\zeta_p\) cannot cure the discrepancy: the generic diagram adds a nonunit factor, not merely a sign.

## Counterexample with every original hypothesis

Take \(p=2\), \(C=\widehat{\overline{\mathbf Q}_2}\), and \[\mathfrak X=\operatorname{Spf}\mathcal O_C\langle T,T^{-1}\rangle,\qquad d=n=1.\] This is affine, hence separated and qcqs. It is an admissible smooth formal \(\mathcal O_C\)-scheme, flat and topologically of finite type. Its generic fiber is the smooth one-dimensional analytic torus and its special fiber is the reduced scheme \(\operatorname{Spec}k[T,T^{-1}]\). It is therefore nice. The formal smooth locus is all of \(\mathfrak X\). The prime \(2\) is allowed by the manuscript; no odd-prime hypothesis occurs in the cited constructions. Here \(\zeta_2=-1\), so \(q=2\).

Choose a generator \(\epsilon\) of the rank-one Tate module and write \[\omega=\frac{dT}{T}\otimes\epsilon^{-1}
 \in\widehat\Omega^1_{\mathfrak X}(-1).\] The smooth isomorphism in Theorem–Definition 3.3.4 gives a section \(e=(\Psi'^1_{\mathrm{sm}})^{-1}(\omega)\) of the torsion-free quotient sheaf \(E\). Equations [eq:bad-normalization] (#eq:bad-normalization) and [eq:good-normalization] (#eq:good-normalization) give, on the generic fiber, \[\Psi'^1_{\mathrm{sm}}(e)=\omega,\qquad
\Psi'^1_{\mathrm{gen}}(e)=\tfrac12\omega.\] These are unequal. The two identifications of the dualizing sheaf with differentials agree on a smooth model by Lemma 2.8.1, so passing to Definition 3.3.5 or Definition 3.4.14 does not remove the factor. The projection \(R^1\nu_*\widehat{\mathcal O}_X^+\to E\) is a sheaf surjection, so this contradiction also concerns the maps from the original cohomology sheaf required in Theorem 3.5.2.

There is an additional integrality obstruction: \(\omega/2\) does not belong to the free \(\mathcal O_C\langle T,T^{-1}\rangle\)-lattice generated by \(\omega\). Even an almost interpretation cannot identify this lattice with its \(1/2\)-rescaling. For example, an element \(a\in C\) with \(a^2=2\) lies in \(\mathfrak m_C\), but \(a/2\notin\mathcal O_C\). Thus \((\tfrac12\mathcal O_C)/\mathcal O_C\) is not annihilated by all of \(\mathfrak m_C\). No appeal to almostification repairs the printed equality.

## Counterevidence and verified correction

The surrounding text strongly indicates the intended normalization: Remark 3.4.15 explicitly says \((\operatorname{Sch}_X^n)^{-1}\), the smooth formula already gives that map, and Lemma 3.5.4 uses that same inverse. These are evidence for a local correction rather than for failure of the underlying duality theorem.

Replace the upward left arrow in diagram (3.7) by the identity, leaving the two copies of \(E_C\) and the bottom map unchanged. Then the defining relation is precisely \[(\Psi'^n_{\mathfrak X})_C=j(\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})_C^{-1}
              =(\operatorname{Sch}_X^n)^{-1}.\] On the overlap with the smooth locus this equals [eq:good-normalization] (#eq:good-normalization). The Hartogs intersection property of Theorem 2.5.5 therefore applies exactly as the proof of Theorem 3.5.2 requires: the maps to the smooth-locus sheaf and the generic-fiber sheaf agree in their common target, and hence factor through their intersection. The uniqueness argument is unaffected. Alternatively, retain the upward multiplication by \(q^n\) and replace the bottom map by \((\Phi^n_{\mathfrak X})_C^{-1}\circ(q^n\operatorname{id})\); this gives the same corrected map.

This verification restores the specific gluing step, its smooth normalization, and the claimed generic-fiber identity. It is not a substitute for independently verifying every later external theorem. Merely relabelling the upper map, or asserting that both vertical arrows are isomorphisms over \(C\), does not restore their compatibility with the already fixed integral smooth map.

## Impact

The dependency is direct: Theorem 3.5.2 constructs the trace used in Definitions 3.5.3 and 3.5.5; the local duality theorems in Section 4 use that trace; Section 5 globalizes the resulting pairing. As printed, there is no trace with the two simultaneous restrictions, already on the smooth torus above. This is an invalid derivation tied to the main construction, hence Major under standard 0.7.1 even though the scalar repair is local. No failure of the principal mod-\(p\) pairing, after the correction, has been demonstrated. No journal-version or author-adoption claim is made.

# Problem 2: multiplication of character spaces (Minor)

## Source assertion and exact example

Equation reference: eq:false-product. On p. 53 after (4.6), and in Lemma 4.2.15 on p. 54, the manuscript says that multiplication induces isomorphisms \[
V_\chi\otimes_{\mathcal O_C}V_{\chi'}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{\chi+\chi'}\] for all characters, including rational characters at each finite tower level \(m\). These assertions occur in TeX lines 2470–2472 and 2492–2500.

Use the same \(C\) and \(p=2\) as above, and choose the permitted standard polystable model \[R^+=\mathcal O_C\langle t_0,t_1\rangle/(t_0t_1-2).\] In the notation of the paper, \(l=1\), \(n_1=1\), \(d=1\), \(\varpi_1=2\), and take \(m=0\). The parameter \(\varpi_1\) is nonzero, as required; it is not required to be a unit. The normal form with basis \(1,t_0^r,t_1^s\), \(r,s\geq1\), shows \(\mathcal O_C\)-torsion-freeness and hence flatness. The generic fiber is smooth since \(t_0t_1=2\) makes the two partial derivatives \(t_1,t_0\) never simultaneously zero. The special fiber \(k[t_0,t_1]/(t_0t_1)\) is reduced. Thus this lies in the very semistable class under discussion.

The torus is \(T=\{(u,u^{-1})\}\). For the character \(\chi(u,u^{-1})=u\), the paper’s own normalized-monomial definition gives \[V_\chi=\mathcal O_Ct_0,\quad V_{-\chi}=\mathcal O_Ct_1,\quad V_0=\mathcal O_C.\] Multiplication sends \(t_0\otimes t_1\) to \(2\). Its image is \(2\mathcal O_C\), and its cokernel is \(\mathcal O_C/2\), which is nonzero. It is not even almost zero: an element of valuation strictly between \(0\) and \(v(2)\) does not kill the class of \(1\). This verifies failure of [eq:false-product] (#eq:false-product) under all original hypotheses, already at \(m=0\).

## Correct general formula

Equation reference: eq:correct-product. Write the normalized exponent vectors of the two characters as \(a=(a_{ij})\) and \(b=(b_{ij})\), with nonnegative entries and \(\min_j a_{ij}=\min_j b_{ij}=0\) in every block \(i\). Put \(c_i=\min_j(a_{ij}+b_{ij})\). The defining relations give \[
t^a t^b=\left(\prod_i\varpi_i^{c_i}\right)t^{\,a+b-c},\] where \(c_i\) is subtracted from every exponent in block \(i\). For rational exponents, use the compatible roots fixed in the tower. The rightmost monomial is the normalized generator for \(\chi+\chi'\).

Thus multiplication between these free rank-one \(\mathcal O_C\)-modules is injective and has image the principal ideal \(\prod_i\varpi_i^{c_i}\) times the target generator. It is an isomorphism precisely when that scalar is a unit. In particular, it is an isomorphism if all relevant parameters are units, or after inverting \(p\). Formula [eq:correct-product] (#eq:correct-product), rather than [eq:false-product] (#eq:false-product) in full generality, is a verified replacement. The reviewer-owned symbolic script additionally checked 49 normalized integer-exponent pairs in the two-variable model. That finite check illustrates, but is not the proof of, the general monomial identity.

## Counterevidence, downstream use, and severity

Warning 4.2.22, p. 55, correctly says that the character-class modules \(R^+_{\bar\chi}\) need not be free or even flat over \(R^+\). These are different objects from the individual \(\mathcal O_C\)-lines \(V_\chi\). For example, at \(m=0\) the class module is \(R^+\) itself; its ordinary self-duality is not contradicted by \(V_\chi V_{-\chi}=2\mathcal O_C\).

Proposition 4.3.6, pp. 61–62, is also important counterevidence. Its Case 1 uses \(V_\chi V_{-\chi}\simeq\mathcal O_C\) only when \(\varpi\) is a unit, where [eq:correct-product] (#eq:correct-product) verifies that assertion. Its Case 2 uses reduction modulo a suitably small pseudouniformizer and Theorem 4.3.2, rather than the false multiplication isomorphism for individual nonunit weight lines. The additive decompositions remain valid. The inspected proof therefore does not establish a core dependency on the false general statement. Replace the multiplication assertion by [eq:correct-product] (#eq:correct-product), retaining the unit case. The supported severity is Minor. No failure of derived duality for \(R^+_{\bar\chi}\) has been proved.

# Scope clarifications and rejected concerns

The following checks are retained for a faithful audit but are not counted as additional severity-bearing findings.

## Reduced special fiber in Theorem 4.4.24

Theorem 4.4.24, p. 74, omits reduced special fiber from its displayed hypotheses. Read literally, this is broader than the definition of Faltings’ pairing in Definition 4.1.1 and the nice-model restriction in Definitions 3.5.1–3.5.5. The opening of Section 4.4, p. 66, explicitly retains reduced special fiber, and Remark 3.5.6, p. 45, explains that the earlier more general construction was removed.

The omission is not a consequence of smooth generic fiber. For example, \[A=\mathcal O_C\langle T,S\rangle/(S^2-2T)\] is finite free of rank two over \(\mathcal O_C\langle T\rangle\), hence \(\mathcal O_C\)-flat, topologically of finite type and admissible. Its generic fiber is smooth of pure dimension one since the derivative with respect to \(T\) is the invertible scalar \(-2\) over \(C\). Its special fiber is \(k[T,S]/(S^2)\), which is not reduced. For this model the cited nice-model trace has not been defined in v3.

The precise supported reading is to retain the reduced-special-fiber hypothesis in Theorem 4.4.24 and its application of the local uniformization statement. This matches Theorem 4.1.2 and the nice formal models actually used in Section 5. No counterexample to a separately extended trace on nonnice models is asserted. Because the section explicitly supplies the missing scope and the global application stays within it, this is recorded as a scope clarification, not as another demonstrated core failure.

## Cohomological shifts in Section 5.5

The derived pairings in Theorems 5.5.2, 5.5.4, and 5.5.6, pp. 81–83, print target \(\Lambda\) without the required shift \([-2d]\), for \(\Lambda=\mathbf Z/p^n\mathbf Z,\mathbf Z_p,\mathbf Q_p\). The duality maps in the proof of Theorem 5.5.2 have the same omission (TeX lines 3771–3801, 3814–3819, and 3832–3839). The trace is actually defined on \(H^{2d}(-,\Lambda(d))\). The correctly typed derived statement is

\[\mathbf R\Gamma(X_C,\Lambda)\otimes_\Lambda^{\mathbf L}
\mathbf R\Gamma(X_C,\Lambda(d))\longrightarrow\Lambda[-2d],\] or equivalently \[\mathbf R\Gamma(X_C,\Lambda)\simeq
\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_\Lambda\big(\mathbf R\Gamma(X_C,\Lambda(d)),\Lambda[-2d]\big).\]

The literal unshifted formula is incompatible even with the paper’s Theorem 5.4.2: for nonempty geometrically connected \(X\), \(d>0\), that theorem gives \(H^{2d}(X_C,\mathbf F_p(d))\ne0\). The unshifted dual has nonzero cohomology in degree \(-2d\), whereas \(\mathbf R\Gamma(X_C,\mathbf F_p)\) has none in negative degrees. For a projective line, the degree pattern is \(0,2\) on the left and \(-2,0\) for the unshifted dual. The corrected shift restores \(0,2\). This is a degree check, not a numerical experiment.

The correct shift is explicit in the preceding almost-duality formula (5.1), and the ordinary \(\mathbf Q_p\)-pairing on p. 83 correctly pairs degrees \(i\) and \(2d-i\). Inserting the shift throughout makes the finite-level base-change square and the derived Nakayama argument compatible with Theorem 5.4.4; the integral limit and rational scalar-extension arguments preserve the shift. This is treated as a systematic notation clarification under the task’s exclusion of typographical errors alone, not as a separate claim that the intended \(p\)-adic duality theorem fails. For integral coefficients the valid assertion remains derived; one must not infer perfect pairings of torsion-containing individual \(\mathbf Z_p\)-cohomology groups without further qualifications.

## Checks that did not yield additional findings

The nonunit case of Proposition 4.3.6 can choose the auxiliary pseudouniformizer small enough in valuation to divide both \(p\) and the relevant root \(\varpi^{1/p^m}\). The available value group over \(C\) allows this. The appearance of \(n\) instead of \(m\) in that bound does not create a demonstrated existence obstruction.

In Lemma 2.5.3 and the proof of Lemma 2.5.4, the needed injectivity of restriction to a dense formal open can be justified directly using the normalization of a nonzero function supplied by Lemma B.6. If a nonzero function vanished on that open, scale it into \(A\) with nonzero reduction; its reduction would vanish on a dense open of the reduced special fiber, a contradiction. This avoids overreading connectedness as irreducibility. Similarly, the Hartogs argument only needs the canonical restriction to sections on the reduced open; it does not need an unrestricted commutation of nonaffine global sections with reduction.

For \(p=2\), the alternating exterior-power construction in Section 3 uses the torsion-free quotient. Graded commutativity gives \(2(x\cup x)=0\), and removal of \(2^\infty\)-torsion gives \(x\cup x=0\). Thus characteristic two does not invalidate the smooth comparison used in Problem 1.

For a character Koszul complex, an annihilator can be tracked at the chain level. If \(d=a\,e_1\wedge+b\,e_2\wedge\), contraction \(h\) with \(e_1\) satisfies \(dh+hd=a\operatorname{id}\). This identity survives reduction modulo \(p\). It supports the needed annihilation without the invalid general inference that an annihilator of cohomology always survives arbitrary derived base change.

The passage from completed direct sums to products in Section 4 uses the fact that for each fixed annihilator tolerance only finitely many character denominators remain exceptional. For each \(a\in\mathfrak m_C\), choose a sufficiently small valuation bound; the tail is killed by \(a\), and the finite exceptional part is already in the sum. The role of the almost category is essential.

Finite-group descent in Section 4.4 uses homotopy invariants and coinvariants, not division by \(|G|\). On a split torsor the regular module has vanishing higher group cohomology, the augmentation identifies coinvariants with the base module, and the norm vector identifies the base module with invariants. These statements remain valid when \(p\mid |G|\). They do not assert that the composite of restriction and trace on the base is invertible in that case.

For local systems in Theorem 5.4.5, the cover associated to a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup has degree prime to \(p\), and the restricted finite \(p\)-group representation over \(\mathbf F_p\) has a filtration with trivial quotients. The split trace and exact-sequence argument therefore have the required different roles. Finiteness of the coefficient groups in Lemma 5.5.1 gives the Mittag–Leffler condition; no surjectivity of every transition map is required.

# Overall impact and disposition

The central issue is the normalization needed to compare the integral smooth trace with the generic-fiber trace. Retaining the printed scalar breaks an actual existence assertion, not merely an explanation of it. Removing it is supported by the manuscript’s comparison theorem and its explicit smooth formula. The resulting overlap equality restores the specified use of the Hartogs theorem. This is the exact extent of the repair verification here.

The local multiplication correction preserves the weight decomposition and the unit-parameter calculation. The nonunit derived-duality proof takes a separate route, so the counterexample does not disprove that result. The two additional clarifications state the hypotheses and cohomological degrees needed for the later arguments.
