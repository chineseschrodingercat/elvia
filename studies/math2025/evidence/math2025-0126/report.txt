Abstract: The acquired publisher version of Alpöge, Bhargava, Ho and Shnidman is examined through its Selmer, prime-equation and rank-descent arguments. The proposed equal-squareclass simplification at dyadic ramified primes is demonstrably obstructed in a permitted field. A replacement using quadratic characters is proved for all relevant local conductors. A missing normalization in the displayed singular series is also identified; the separately cited affine-prime-pattern route remains applicable. The Selmer and Diophantine-definability dependencies receive positive bounded checks, and the infinitude argument is completed using the existing freedom to enlarge an exponent. The overall assessment is Major for the affected universal-construction step, with a verified repair; no counterexample to the main theorem is established.

Title: Local admissibility and the proof of rank stability in quadratic extensions

# Identity, exact scope and logical structure

The source is L. Alpöge, M. Bhargava, W. Ho and A. Shnidman, Rank stability in quadratic extensions and Hilbert’s tenth problem for the ring of integers of a number field, Inventiones mathematicae 243 (2025), 1129–1139, DOI 10.1007/s00222-025-01392-3 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01392-3). The acquired publisher version has 11 physical pages. PDF page numbers below are one-based; printed page numbers equal PDF page numbers plus 1128. The separately acquired arXiv:2501.18774v1 source is not presumed equivalent to the publisher text.

Theorem 1.1 [1, PDF p. 2] quantifies over every quadratic extension \(K/F\) of number fields and asserts existence of an abelian variety \(A/F\) with \[0<\operatorname{rank}A(F)=\operatorname{rank}A(K).\] It does not require a uniform variety, an elliptic curve, rank one, equality of the full point groups, or an effective procedure selecting \(A\). Corollary 1.2 concerns existential definability of \(\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathcal O_K\), and undecidability of polynomial solvability over that ring. It makes no corresponding assertion about solvability over the fraction field \(K\).

After a cyclotomic base change, the proof assumes that an odd prime \(\ell\) has \(\mu_\ell\subset F\), that \(K=F(\sqrt q)\) is quadratic and unramified above \(\ell\), and that \[C_n:y^2=x^\ell+n,\quad n\ne0,\qquad J_n=\operatorname{Jac}(C_n),
 \qquad \phi=1-\zeta_\ell.\] Yu’s input supplies \(r\in\mathcal O_F\setminus\{0\}\), a unit at primes ramifying in \(K/F\), with \(\operatorname{Sel}_\phi(J_{q^\ell r^2})=0\). The finite set \(S\) consists of the primes in \(S'\) above \(\ell\) or split in \(K\), where \(S'\) contains the primes dividing \(\ell!\) or the conductor of that Jacobian. Put \(\Sigma=S\cup S_{\mathrm{inert}}\).

The critical arithmetic step, Proposition 2.5, requires infinitely many classes \[t=a^{(\ell-1)/2}b\pmod{F^{\times\ell}},\qquad
 a,b\in\mathcal O_{F,\Sigma}^{\times},\quad a+2rb=1,
 \quad t\in F_{ p}^{\times\ell}\ ( p\in S).\] Lemma 2.4 preserves zero Selmer rank for the corresponding companion twists. Lemma 2.6 and Proposition 2.7 give positive rank on all but finitely many of their quadratic twists. The rational eigenspace decomposition then yields the required stable rank. Thus the local admissibility in Proposition 2.5 is a used step in the universal main theorem.

All principal sections of the short publisher paper were read, and all 11 source pages were actually viewed. The cited Yu, Kai and Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh inputs were checked in precisely identified preprint versions and selected passages. Their entire proofs, and every other background theorem, are not certified here.

# Problem 1: equal squareclasses do not remove the dyadic obstruction

## Location, hypotheses and a permitted counterexample to the argument

Equation reference: eq:conic. In the ramified case of the proof of Proposition 2.5 [1, PDF p. 9; printed p. 1137], the authors must prescribe inert-prime residue classes compatible with \[p_1+\beta p_2=p_3,\qquad \beta=2r\gamma^{n\ell}.\] Here \(\gamma\) has prime support exactly \(S\), and \(n\) may be made large. At primes dividing the relative conductor \(m\), both \(r\) and \(\gamma\) are units. The source reduces compatibility to \[
 t_1x^2+\beta t_2y^2=t_3z^2\pmod{ m}\] and asserts that setting \(t_1=t_3\) removes the obstruction at dyadic primes. The prime residues \(t_ix^2,t_iy^2,t_iz^2\) in this construction must be units; allowing a zero or nonunit coordinate would not give the required prime congruences.

Take the permitted cyclotomic setting \[\ell=3,\qquad F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3),\qquad K=F(i).\] It arises from the initial quadratic extension \(\mathbb Q(i)/\mathbb Q\) by the paper’s allowed base change. The extension \(K/F\) is unramified above \(3\). At its dyadic prime, \(F_{ p}\) is the unramified quadratic extension of \(\mathbb Q_2\), with residue field \(\mathbb F_4\), and the local conductor of adjoining \(i\) is \(4\). Consequently \[v_{ p}(2)=1,\qquad
 v_{ p}(r)=v_{ p}(\gamma)=0,\qquad
 v_{ p}(\beta)=1\] for every \(r\) allowed by Lemma 2.3 and every \(n\). This example does not depend on guessing a particular rank-zero twist.

Suppose \(t_1=t_3\) and all the displayed parameters and coordinates in [eq:conic] (#eq:conic) are units. Reduction modulo \(2\) forces \(x^2=z^2\), hence \(x=z\) in \(\mathbb F_4\). Therefore both \(x-z\) and \(x+z\) are divisible by \(2\), and \[x^2-z^2\in4\mathcal O_{F_{ p}}.\] But \(\beta t_2y^2/t_1\) has valuation one. Equation [eq:conic] (#eq:conic) is impossible modulo \(4\). This refutes the supplied equal-squareclass justification within the paper’s actual field assumptions; a \(\mathbb Q_2\)-only example would not have sufficed.

Reviewer-written finite-ring arithmetic independently used \(\mathbb Z[z]/(z^2+z+1,4)\). For each of the three possible nonzero residues \(2u\) with \(u\) a unit, it found no unit solution with \(t_1=t_3\), but found twelve triples when only the required inert quadratic characters were imposed. The exact inputs and counts are in `aux_primes/exact_local_checks.json`. The general obstruction is the valuation argument above.

## A replacement valid for every relevant ramified local character

The needed condition is equality of quadratic characters of the three prime residues. It is stronger than necessary to require equality of their full squareclasses. The following gives the required local freedom.

Lemma 1.  Let \(L\) be a dyadic local field containing \(\mu_\ell\) for an odd prime \(\ell\), and let \(\chi:L^\times\to\{\pm1\}\) be a ramified quadratic character. If \(\beta=2u\) with \(u\in\mathcal O_L^\times\), there is \(v\in\mathcal O_L^\times\) such that \[\chi(v)=\chi(1+\beta v)=1.\]

Proof. Write \(e=v_L(2)\), let \(c\) be the conductor exponent of \(\chi\), and put \(U^j=1+ p^j\). The last nontrivial character on \(U^{c-1}/U^c\) is an additive residue-field character. The residue field has at least four elements: reduction preserves the odd-order roots of unity, so \(\ell\mid(\#k_L-1)\).

If \(c\le e\), take \(v=1\). If \(c=e+1\), the function on residues induced by \(\chi(1+\beta v)\) is a nonzero additive character, after a unit scaling. Its kernel contains a nonzero residue because \(\#k_L\ge4\). Choose a unit lift \(v\) of such a residue. Multiplying \(v\) by a suitable element of \(U^e\) arranges \(\chi(v)=1\). This does not change \(\chi(1+\beta v)\): the induced multiplicative change belongs to \(U^{2e}\subset U^{e+1}\).

If \(c\ge e+2\), start with any unit \(v\), and replace it by \(v(1+w)\), where \(w\in p^{c-e-1}\). The ratio of the new and old expressions \(1+\beta v\) is \[1+\frac{\beta v}{1+\beta v}\,w .\] Its image runs over \(U^{c-1}/U^c\), so one can arrange \(\chi(1+\beta v)=1\). Now multiply \(v\) by an element of \(U^{c-1}\) to arrange \(\chi(v)=1\). The corresponding change of \(1+\beta v\) lies in \(U^{e+c-1}\subset U^c\), leaving its character unchanged. ◻

Choose a unit \(a\) with any prescribed character \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\) and set \[b=av,\qquad d=a(1+\beta v).\] Then \(a+\beta b=d\), and all three characters equal \(\varepsilon\). At an odd ramified prime, \(\beta\) is a unit and the residue field has size \(Q\ge7\), since it contains \(\mu_\ell\) and the prime is not above \(\ell\). The conic \(z^2-\beta y^2=1\) has \(Q-\chi_k(\beta)\) affine points; excluding \(y=0\) or \(z=0\) removes at most four. A remaining point gives \(v=y^2\) with both required characters equal to one. Hensel lifting supplies the local unit conditions.

Choose signs at the ramified places whose product under the quadratic ray character is \(-1\). The preceding construction gives three local units with these same signs. The Chinese remainder theorem combines them with the disjoint \(S\)-congruences. This supplies compatible inert-prime residue classes without the false requirement \(t_1=t_3\).

## Impact and counterevidence

The dyadic sentence is the given justification for a local condition in the ramified branch of Proposition 2.5. That proposition feeds the positive-rank construction and hence the assertion for every quadratic extension. The finding is Major for this demonstrated invalid derivation in the core chain. The existence of the preceding local repair does not make the printed derivation valid.

At the same time, the repair proves precisely the missing local compatibility in the stated setting. The example is not a counterexample to Proposition 2.5, Theorem 1.1 or the Hilbert-tenth corollary. The stronger equality of squareclasses fails; equality of the actual quadratic characters can be obtained. The independent analytic prime theorem and the rest of the construction remain explicit dependencies.

# Problem 2: the displayed singular-series count is unnormalized

Equation reference: eq:baddensity. In the sketch proving Proposition 3.1 [1, PDF p. 8; printed p. 1136], let \(N_j\) count pairs modulo \(p^j\) for which \(x,y,x+\beta y\) are units. The displayed finite local factor, with \(Q=\operatorname{Nm}( p)\), is \[
 \lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{Q}{(Q-1)^3}
 N_j.\] The source page visibly has no normalizing power of \(Q\) depending on \(j\).

For \(p\nmid\beta\), reduction modulo \(p\) leaves \((Q-1)(Q-2)\) pairs. Each pair has \(Q^{2(j-1)}\) lifts, so [eq:baddensity] (#eq:baddensity) diverges. For example, at \(Q=7\) the first three displayed factors are \[35/36,\qquad1715/36,\qquad84035/36 .\] Thus the formula is not the finite local density needed in a singular series.

The normalized factor is \[\lim_{j\to\infty}
 \frac{Q^{-2j}}{(1-Q^{-1})^3}
 N_j.\] Equivalently, insert \(Q^{-2(j-1)}\) before the count in [eq:baddensity] (#eq:baddensity). At an unexceptional prime this gives \[\frac{Q(Q-2)}{(Q-1)^2}=1-\frac1{(Q-1)^2}.\] These factors have a positive convergent product away from finitely many exceptional primes. If \(p\mid\beta\), the corresponding local factor is \(Q/(Q-1)\), with only finitely many such primes. The exact finite calculation confirms the normalization and is separate from any analytic asymptotic.

This is assessed Minor. It is a real error in a displayed calculation, but the paper also gives an independent prime-pattern proof of Proposition 3.1. Kai’s Theorem 13.1 in the consulted arXiv:2306.16983v3, PDF pp. 67–68 [3], permits affine-linear forms taking values in an ideal and supplies an asymptotic under the relevant local-admissibility condition. Its full analytic proof was not reverified.

For that alternative application, the source’s \(W\) is an affine coset, rather than a subgroup. Since \(I\) and \((C)\) are coprime, choose a residue lift \((a_1,a_2)\in I^2\) and write \[W=(a_1,a_2)+(CI)^2.\] The three maps become affine forms with linear parts \((1,0),(0,1),(1,\beta)\), pairwise independent over \(F\). At primes dividing \(C\), the prescribed unit residues give admissibility. At other primes, two nonzero residues can avoid \(x+\beta y=0\); if the residue field has two elements, \(\beta\in2\mathcal O_F\) makes that last form equal to \(x\). This verifies the needed local compatibility. The affine-coset correction is a clarification, not a failure of the theorem allowed by Kai’s statement.

# Completing infinitude of the power classes

The final paragraph of Proposition 2.5 [1, PDF p. 9] uses \[t=(p_1^k p_2 p_3^k),\qquad k=(\ell-1)/2,
 \qquad (a^k b)= t\,(\gamma^n p_3^{-1})^\ell .\] The intended conclusion is that infinitely many varying primes occur with exponents between \(1\) and \(\ell-1\). A shared residual prime in all three \((p_i)=I q_i\) would instead have exponent \(2k+1=\ell\). Infinite homogeneous triples alone also need not give infinitely many distinct ratios. These points can be resolved using the already allowed sufficiently large \(n\), rather than asserted as new defects.

Fix \(r,\gamma\) and let \(S_0\) contain the prime support of \(2r\gamma\). If \(q_1= q_2= q_3\), then \(a=p_1/p_3\) is an ordinary unit and \(b=\gamma^{n\ell}(p_2/p_3)\) is \(\gamma^{n\ell}\) times an ordinary unit. The pairs \((a,2rb)\) solve a fixed finite-\(S_0\) unit equation. By the same Siegel–Mahler finiteness invoked in the source, there are only finitely many such pairs. Since \(S\) contains primes above \(\ell\), \(\gamma\) is a nonunit. At any \(p\mid\gamma\), \[v_{ p}(b)=n\ell\,v_{ p}(\gamma),\] so only finitely many \(n\) can occur in this common-prime case. Choose \(n\) beyond these exceptions and beyond the local-power thresholds.

Outside the fixed prime support of \(2r\gamma I\), equality of two residual primes forces equality of the third by \(p_1+\beta p_2=p_3\), which has now been excluded. Every large residual prime consequently has exponent \(k\) or \(1\) in \(t\), strictly between zero and \(\ell\).

Finally, two triples with the same \((a,b)\) differ by common scaling. For distinct residual primes, comparison of their principal divisors forces the scaling to be a unit. Thus infinitely many triples modulo common units yield infinitely many pairs. Finite total prime support would contradict the \(S\)-unit theorem for those pairs. This proves the required infinitude of ideal power classes, hence of element power classes. It is a verified completion of an omitted step, compatible with the source’s choice of \(n\), and is not listed as another Problem.

# Positive checks of the remaining proof chain

## Selmer conditions, torsion and rational points

The kernel \(J_n[\phi]\) has order \(\ell\), generated by the class of \((0,\sqrt n)-\infty\), and carries the quadratic character of \(F(\sqrt n)/F\). At a nonsplit prime away from \(\ell\), its local invariants and dual invariants vanish. Local duality and the Euler characteristic give \(H^1=0\), including ramified primes of residue characteristic two. The exclusion of primes above \(\ell\) is necessary; they remain in the controlled set \(S\).

The Kummer injection gives \(J(F)/\phi J(F)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_\phi(J)\). For the torsion-free \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_\ell]\)-module of Mordell–Weil points, reduction at \(1-\zeta_\ell\) detects its rank. Thus Selmer vanishing implies rank zero without assuming finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group.

Yu’s Theorem 4 [2, PDF p. 3] applies after interchanging the affine coordinates: \(X^\ell=Y^2-q^\ell\), with a separable degree-two polynomial whose Galois group is \(S_2\). The odd prime \(\ell\) does not divide that degree. The rank-lowering proof in Proposition 4.2, pp. 15–16, uses a parameter supported at a new prime and congruent to one at the protected ray modulus. This supports the extra requirement that \(r\) be a unit at all ramified primes. The inverse twist convention converts by \(r=d^{(\ell-1)/2}\). These are checks of applicability and the relevant local control, not a re-proof of Yu’s full machinery.

In Lemma 2.4, the local cases exhaust all places. On \(S\), the twist is locally an \(\ell\)-th power. At other nonsplit primes the ambient cohomology vanishes. At the remaining split primes, both Jacobians have good reduction and the same unramified Kummer image. The common quadratic kernel character is unchanged by the square twist parameters.

For fixed \(n\), torsion points of the twists become height-zero points of \(J_n\) of degree at most \(\ell\); Northcott gives finitely many possibilities. If one non-kernel point occurred on two distinct twist classes, comparison of their twisting characters would force it to be fixed by a nontrivial power of \(\zeta_\ell\), and hence by \(\zeta_\ell\), a contradiction. This validates the finite exceptional set in Lemma 2.6.

The Fermat covering has the exact identity \[\begin{split}
 U&=a x y^{-2},\qquad
 V=a^{(\ell-1)/2}(y^{-\ell}-rb),\\
 V^2-U^\ell-r^2a^{\ell-1}b^2
 &=a^{\ell-1}y^{-2\ell}(1-a x^\ell-2rb y^\ell).
\end{split}\] At \(x=y=1\), the equation \(a+2rb=1\) gives the stated rational point with first coordinate \(a\ne0\). The Abel–Jacobi embedding is injective for this positive-genus curve and is equivariant under \(\zeta_\ell\); therefore this point is not in \(J[\phi]\). Outside the finite exceptional twists, it is not torsion. The saved reviewer-written Laurent-polynomial checks at \(\ell=3,5,7,11\) complement this arbitrary-\(\ell\) identity.

## Descent and the Diophantine conclusion

The initial choice of \(\ell\) outside the absolute discriminant of \(K\) makes \(K\) disjoint from \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\): every nontrivial subfield of the latter is ramified at \(\ell\). The compositum over \(F(\zeta_\ell)\) is therefore still quadratic. Weil restriction identifies its two relevant point groups with those over the original \(F,K\), preserving ranks while allowing a higher-dimensional variety.

For a quadratic extension, the rational Mordell–Weil space splits into the two involution eigenspaces. This proves \[\operatorname{rank}A(K)=\operatorname{rank}A(F)+\operatorname{rank}A^q(F).\] The constructed positive-rank Jacobian has zero-rank companion \(J_{q^\ell r^2a^{\ell-1}b^2}\), so this gives the required equality once Proposition 2.5 is supplied with the preceding local repair and infinitude completion.

Theorem 3.1 of Mazur–Rubin–Shlapentokh [4, PDF p. 7] requires exactly equal positive abelian ranks. Its Theorem 4.8, pp. 10–11, reduces definability over all number rings to that for quadratic extensions. The reduction uses a Galois closure and intersections of complex-conjugation fixed fields, whose intersection is totally real; it does not require a tower of quadratic extensions. Equal ranks imply only a finite torsion quotient, which is sufficient. Existential choices for each fixed field suffice; no uniform selection algorithm is needed.

Their Lemma 4.1 also reconciles finite systems with the single-equation wording. Over a number ring, choose an integral nonsquare \(u\); \(f^2-u g^2=0\) is equivalent to \(f=g=0\). Iteration combines a finite system. The deeper definability theorem and the totally-real input remain external dependencies. The separate introductory extension to all finitely generated infinite \(\mathbb Z\)-algebras was not checked.

# Overall impact and review state

The core defect established here is the invalid dyadic equal-squareclass argument, not the conclusion that suitable inert-prime congruences exist. Lemma Lemma 1 (#lem:local) and the odd-prime argument restore that compatibility in the stated range. The corrected local-density factor repairs the displayed singular series, while the separately inspected affine-prime-pattern theorem supplies another route to Proposition 3.1. The common-prime issue is resolved by a permitted large-\(n\) refinement.

With those distinctions, no counterexample to rank stability or to the ring-of-integers corollary is established. The positive checks are conditional on the explicitly retained analytic, duality and definability theorems. No author correction or adoption of the proposed repairs was independently acquired.
