Abstract: The decrease estimates for general self-concordant path following and the conditional restart comparison are useful. Two claims in the feasibility application require substantial revision. The stated square-root logarithmic feasibility bound drops a diameter factor that is essential even in one dimension; an analytic lower bound for the proposed PFS contradicts the claimed rate. The subsequent zero-gap linear-programming embedding has no strictly interior feasible point, so the positive-depth hypothesis cannot be transferred as asserted. Further, localized corrections are needed for the clipped predictor, signed path lemma, Newton hypotheses, finite indices, work counts and cubic regularization parameter. The explicit examples below are separated from the conditional conclusions that they do not refute.

# Mathematical structure and checks supporting the approach

The basic unconstrained setting is a convex \(C^3\) function with positive definite Hessian and an attained minimum, together with \[|D^3f(x)[h]^3|\leq 2M_f\langle \nabla^2f(x)h,h\rangle^{3/2}.\] The dimensionless quantities \(M_f\lambda_f(x)\) and \(\Delta=M_f^2(f(x_0)-f^\ast)\) are the appropriate scaling variables. On their stated local domains, the Hessian comparison and Taylor bounds provide the main estimates. The proof of the damped Newton bound \(\lambda_f(x_+)\leq 2M_f\lambda_f(x)^2\) was checked: integrating the Hessian sandwich gives the required negative semidefinite normalized residual matrix and its operator-norm bound. The local convergence discussion should be read with the qualifications in Problem 5.

The PFS maintains a small decrement for \(f_t(x)=f(x)-t\langle\nabla f(x_0),x\rangle\). Its proof combines a decrease proportional to \(t_k\|\nabla f(x_0)\|_{x_k}^{\ast}\) with a reciprocal-sum inequality. Correctly indexed, this gives the \(N^2/\Delta\) exponent in (37). The passage from the exact path to a small decrement at the approximate point then introduces the logarithm involving the level-set diameter \(D\) in (44). That logarithm is part of the theorem and cannot be discarded in a subsequent application. The exact path itself remains in the starting level set: differentiating its defining equation yields \[\frac{d}{dt} f(x(t))
 =t\,\langle\nabla f(x_0),[\nabla^2f(x(t))]^{-1}\nabla f(x_0)\rangle\geq0.\]

For the predictor-corrector analysis, the positive-direction form of Lemma 4 gives the intended second-order predictor error. With \(\beta=0.0015\), \(\gamma=0.158\), the corrected constants are \[\kappa=0.0350648269066\ldots,\qquad
 (\gamma\kappa)^{-1/2}=13.4349361516\ldots .\] The corresponding corrected centering estimate is \(0.001475674<0.0015\). For the barrier parameters \(\beta=0.06,\gamma=0.254\), it is \(0.059699172<0.06\). Thus the parameter choices themselves remain admissible after the identified sign and cutoff repairs.

The constrained primal certificate in Lemma 6 follows from the barrier gradient bound and the distance to the path. In the dual construction, the norm identity for the affine correction in (109) also checks out, provided the matrix inverses exist: the primal correction norm equals the dual residual norm, and the Dikin ellipsoid gives feasibility. Problems 4 and 7 specify the needed sign and data qualifications.

Finally, the Section 7 implication \[M_f=\frac{H_f}{2\sigma_f^{3/2}},\qquad
 \frac{\sigma_f^3}{2H_f^2}=\frac{1}{8M_f^2}\] is correct. Conditional on (114), the halving schedule and geometric sum give the stated restart exponents \(1/4,1/6,1/7\) for \(p=2,3,7/2\). This conditional conclusion is distinct from claiming that an arbitrary positive cubic parameter already provides (114).

# Major issues in the feasibility application

Problem 1 (Major: an essential logarithmic factor is lost in Section 4.2).  On p. 2185, item 2 claims ordinary \[O\!\left(\sqrt{\nu\log(1/\varepsilon)}\right)\] complexity for the PFS, citing (44). The following discussion uses this to claim a faster dependence on feasibility depth than the usual logarithmic rate. The source page shows an ordinary \(O\), not notation suppressing additional logarithms.

Equation (44), however, contains \[\log\!\left(
 \frac{M_f D\,\omega^{-1}(\Delta)}
 {\omega((1-\beta)(1-2\beta)/2)}
 \right).\] Substitution of \(\Delta\leq\nu\log(1/\varepsilon)\) does not bound this factor by a constant. In fact, the claimed uniform feasibility rate is false for the proposed PFS, even with fixed dimension and fixed barrier parameter.

Use the natural corrected depth convention \(0\leq\delta\leq1\) discussed in Problem 7, and let \[Q=[-1,1],\qquad F(a)=-\log(1-a)-\log(1+a),\qquad
 A=1,\quad b=1-\varepsilon,\quad 0<\varepsilon\leq\tfrac14.\] This is a standard self-concordant barrier; \(\nu=2\) is a valid fixed parameter. Its analytic center is \(0\), \(F(0)=0\), and the feasibility depth is precisely \(\varepsilon\). Consider the dual objective actually used in Section 4.2: \[f(y)=F^\ast(y)-by,\qquad y_0=0,\qquad M_f=1.\] Writing \(s=\sqrt{1+y^2}\), elementary conjugacy gives \[a(y)=(F^\ast)'(y)=\frac{y}{1+s},\qquad
 f''(y)=\frac{1}{s(s+1)},\qquad
 F''(a(y))=s(s+1).\] The minimum occurs at \(a=b\), so \[\Delta=f(0)-f^\ast=F(b)=-\log(1-b^2)
       =\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon)).\] Moreover \(y^\ast=2b/(1-b^2)\), and both \(0,y^\ast\) belong to the starting level set. Since \(f''(0)=1/2\), \[D\geq \frac{y^\ast}{\sqrt2}=\Theta(1/\varepsilon).\] The logarithm in (44) therefore grows at least on the order of \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\) in this family.

There is also a direct lower bound for the algorithm, independent of whether (44) is sharp. The PFS centering condition and update imply, with \(\eta=\beta+\gamma=0.1385\), \[|y_{k+1}-y_k|\sqrt{f''(y_k)}\leq\eta.\] Because \(\sqrt{s(s+1)}\leq s+1\leq |y|+2\), it follows that \[|y_{k+1}|+2\leq(1+\eta)(|y_k|+2),\qquad
 |y_k|+2\leq2(1+\eta)^k.\] To enter the paper’s region \(\lambda_f(y)\leq1/2\), it is necessary that \(a(y)\geq1-2\varepsilon\). Indeed, if \(a\leq b\), then \[\lambda_f(y)=(b-a)\sqrt{F''(a)}
 \geq \frac{b-a}{1-a}
 =1-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-a},\] and if \(a>b\), the same necessary lower bound on \(a\) is immediate. Consequently, \[y\geq \frac{1-2\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}
 \geq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}.\] Combining these inequalities gives \[k\geq
 \frac{\log(1+1/(8\varepsilon))}{\log(1+\beta+\gamma)}
 =\Omega(\log(1/\varepsilon)).\] This is a lower bound for the specified PFS on its specified dual problem and stopping region, not a lower bound for every possible way to solve a one-dimensional feasibility problem.

Independent 100-digit evaluations of (32) gave the following results. The analytic lower bound is the decisive check. \[\begin{array}{c|r|r}
 \varepsilon & \text{PFS steps to }\lambda_f\leq1/2
 & \text{ceiling of the displayed lower bound}\\ \hline
 10^{-2}&34&21\\
 10^{-4}&72&55\\
 10^{-8}&148&126\\
 10^{-16}&300&268\\
 10^{-32}&603&553
\end{array}\] All checked iterates maintained the prescribed centering tolerance.

Theorem 6 with its full diameter logarithm is not refuted. What fails is the stronger rate asserted in the feasibility application and the ensuing claimed improvement in the depth dependence. Retain and estimate that logarithm, then revise the corresponding contribution and comparison with long-step methods. A bound on an initial objective residual alone is insufficient for the stated conclusion.

Problem 2 (Major: the proposed LP reduction has zero feasibility depth).  On p. 2186, the paper homogenizes primal feasibility, dual feasibility and an exact zero duality gap, then intersects with a simplex and claims that the preceding positive-depth complexity applies to linear programming. Eliminating the simplex equality does not resolve the relevant obstruction.

With the paper’s block notation \(A=(I,B)\), define the homogenized dual variable \[y=\tau c_1-s_1.\] The displayed equalities imply \[Ax=\tau b,\qquad A^Ty+s=\tau c,\qquad
 c^Tx+b^Ts_1-\tau b^Tc_1=0.\] Therefore \[x^Ts=\tau(c^Tx-b^Ty)
     =\tau(c^Tx+b^Ts_1-\tau b^Tc_1)=0.\] Since \(x,s\geq0\), every feasible point satisfies complementarity. In particular, there is no feasible point with all coordinates of \(x,s,\tau\) positive. After eliminating the simplex equality, the same points remain on the boundary of the resulting full-dimensional simplex. The hypothesis \(\varepsilon>0\) required in (56) is not obtained.

For a concrete bounded-optimum example, take \[\min x\quad\text{subject to }x=1,\quad x\geq0.\] The normalized homogeneous system becomes \[x=\tau,\qquad x+s-\tau=0,\qquad x+s+\tau=1,\] whose unique solution is \((x,s,\tau)=(1/2,0,1/2)\). It lies on the boundary \(s=0\). A translation placing the simplex center at the origin does not change the lack of an interior feasible point.

There is a second extraction issue: a nonzero homogeneous solution need not have \(\tau>0\). For \(A=(1,0)\), \(b=1\), \(c=(0,0)\), the point \[x=(0,1),\qquad s=(0,0),\qquad \tau=0\] satisfies the homogeneous equalities and the unit-simplex normalization, but cannot be dehomogenized by division by \(\tau\). The original LP in this example is feasible; the example shows that an arbitrary normalized homogeneous solution is not automatically an optimal solution of it.

A valid transfer needs a suitable perturbed or strictly feasible embedding, a positive-depth bound for that construction, and extraction rules distinguishing the possible homogeneous cases. Alternatively, remove the asserted direct transfer. This objection concerns the particular construction printed here, not the established homogeneous self-dual methods cited in the bibliography.

# Localized algorithmic and proof corrections

Problem 3 (Minor: the predictor must use the clipped parameter change).  In (75), p. 2189, \(t_+\) is clipped at zero, but the predictor still uses the full amount \[\widehat\tau=\frac{\gamma}{M_f\|\nabla f(x_0)\|_x^\ast}.\] When \(t<\widehat\tau\), Lemma 4 controls the residual for \(t-\widehat\tau\), whereas the corrector uses \(t_+=0\). Thus (76) does not follow as stated.

An exact example falsifies the unconditional assertion of Theorem 8. Let \[f(x)=\tfrac12x^2+\tfrac14x^4,\quad M_f=1,\quad
 x=x_0=\frac{100}{2497},\quad t=1.\] This function is globally self-concordant: the maximum of \(3|x|/(1+3x^2)^{3/2}\) is \(2/3\), so \(M_f=1\) is admissible. Here \[\sqrt{f''(x_0)}=\frac{2503}{2497},\qquad
 \lambda_{f_t}(x)=0,\qquad \widehat\tau>1.\] With \(\gamma=79/500\), equation (75) gives \(t_+=0\) and \[y=\frac{100}{2497}-\frac{79}{500}\frac{2497}{2503},
 \qquad
 x_+=y-\frac{y+y^3}{1+3y^2}=\frac{2y^3}{1+3y^2}.\] All these points are rational. Comparing squared decrements exactly yields \[\lambda_f(x_+)=0.003121087486\ldots>0.0015=\beta.\]

Use \[\tau=\min\!\left\{t,\frac{\gamma}{M_f\|\nabla f(x_0)\|_x^\ast}\right\},
 \qquad t_+=t-\tau,\qquad
 y=x-\tau[\nabla^2f(x)]^{-1}\nabla f(x_0).\] Handle an initially zero gradient before dividing, and specify the local phase.

The impact should be calibrated carefully. Under the centering condition, clipping implies \[M_f\lambda_f(x)
 \leq M_f t\|\nabla f(x_0)\|_x^\ast+\beta
 \leq\gamma+\beta=0.1595<1/2.\] Thus the clipping problem occurs after the input has already entered the target region. It falsifies Theorem 8 as written and affects continuation of (75), but it does not refute the first-entry complexity in Theorems 9–10. This is why it is classified as Minor here.

Problem 4 (Minor: signed predictor bounds and the dual path label need correction).  Lemma 4, pp. 2187–2188, states (67) for all \(\tau\) with \(|\tau|M_fr<1\), but its bound and proof use \(\tau\) without absolute values. For negative \(\tau\), the norm identity in (69), the Hessian sandwich and integration direction are not justified in that form.

Use the same quartic, now with \[M_f=\tfrac23,\quad x=1,\quad t=0,\quad c=4,\quad
 h=1,\quad r=2,\quad \tau=-\tfrac1{10}.\] The hypothesis is satisfied and the initial decrement is \(1\). Direct substitution gives \[\lambda_{f_{t-\tau}}(x+\tau h)
 =\frac{2029}{100\sqrt{343}}>1.09,
 \qquad
 \text{printed right side of (67)}=\frac{261}{289}<0.904.\] The squared inequality was checked exactly.

Either restrict the displayed bound to \(\tau\geq0\), or replace the controlling occurrences by \(|\tau|\) and reverse the direction consistently when needed. The intended positive-step predictor estimate survives.

There is a related sign error in Lemma 7, p. 2197. Its convention is \(\Phi_t(u)=\Phi(u)-t\langle b,u\rangle\). A predictor \(v=u+\tau[\nabla^2\Phi(u)]^{-1}b\) transports the residual to parameter \(t(u)+\tau\), not \(t(u)-\tau\). Apply Lemma 4 with its plus-linear convention and initial parameter \(-t(u)\), or rewrite the lemma in the dual convention.

For the legitimate one-dimensional interval conjugate \(\Phi=F^\ast\) from Problem 1, at \(u=0\) with \(b=1,\gamma=0.254\), the printed intermediate residual is approximately \(0.523711\), while the intended bound with zero initial residual is \((\gamma/(1-\gamma))^2\approx0.1159\). The corrected-sign residual is approximately \(0.008065\). The minimized residual \(\lambda(v)\) is zero in this one-dimensional check, so the example exposes the intermediate comparison, not a failure of the corrected centering conclusion.

Problem 5 (Minor: state the local Newton hypothesis and fix the scaled distance expression).  Theorem 1, p. 2172, omits the necessary condition \(M_f\lambda_f(x)<1\) for (19). A squared denominator does not extend the local estimate past that condition.

For the globally standard self-concordant quartic \(f(x)=x^2/2+x^4/4\), take \(M_f=1\), \(x=10\). Its Newton point is \(x_+=2000/301\). Exact squared comparisons give \[\lambda_f(10)>50,\qquad \lambda_f(x_+)>25,\] whereas the displayed right side of (19) is less than \((50/49)^2<1.042\). The intended local uses of this theorem are valid once the condition is restored.

In (11), p. 2170, the first expression is correct, but its last expansion has an extra \(M_f\): \[\frac1{M_f}\omega_\ast'(M_f\lambda)
 =\frac{\lambda}{1-M_f\lambda},
 \quad\text{not}\quad
 \frac{M_f\lambda}{1-M_f\lambda}.\] For \(f(x)=x^2/2\), \(M_f=1/2\), \(x=1/5\), the distance to the minimizer is \(1/5\); the printed last expression is \(1/9\), while the correct bound is \(2/9\).

Also separate \(M_f=0\), which is admitted in Definition 1 but makes several displayed inverses undefined. A positive definite quadratic can be solved directly or assigned any positive admissible self-concordance constant. State the real domain \(0\leq\tau<1\) for \(\omega_\ast\), and terminate at zero residual instead of using a ratio \(0/0\). These qualifications do not invalidate the damped Newton proof in its intended regime.

Problem 6 (Minor: correct finite indices, constants and the unit of work).  The proofs of Theorems 5 and 9 define a sum through \(k=N\) while bounding \(t_N\). There are only \(N\) updates from \(t_0\) to \(t_N\); use \(k=0,\ldots,N-1\), or put \(t_{N+1}\) on the left. With consistent indexing, the \(N^2\) bounds (37) and (78) follow by the same argument.

Equation (43), p. 2178, is false as indexed. For \(f(x)=x^2/2\), \(M_f=1,x_0=1,N=0\), the stated pre-region and decrease conditions hold, but its right side is \(1-\gamma(\gamma-2\beta)=0.99319375<1=t_0\). Replace \(N+1\) by \(N\) when the left side remains \(t_N\). In the preceding concave optimization argument, the equal allocation is a maximum, not a minimum. The source image correctly contains a sum of logarithms; no product-of-logarithms defect is alleged.

The actual parameters in (30) give \[\left(\frac{2}{\gamma(\gamma-2\beta)}\right)^{1/2}
 =17.1419825742\ldots,\] so the literal upper bound \(17.1\) in Remark 3 needs corrected rounding or different explicitly stated parameters. In the \(\rho_k\) expression following (83), p. 2191, the argument of the last \(\omega_\ast\) must be \[\frac{\beta}{1-\gamma}+\frac{\gamma^2}{(1-\gamma)^2},\] as required by (82), rather than the printed difference. After this repair, the dimensionless lower bound for \(\rho_k\) is still \(0.0247046210\ldots>0\); this does not overturn the positive-decrease argument.

For adaptive PFS, testing trial indices \(i=0,\ldots,i_k\) costs \(i_k+1\) Newton trials, including the accepted one. Thus the total through outer iteration \(k\) is \[\sum_{j=0}^{k}(i_j+1)
 =2(k+1)+\log_2\frac{\gamma_{-1}}{\gamma_k},\] not merely \(\sum_{j=0}^{k}i_j\). For example, on a quadratic every first trial may be accepted, giving \(i_j=0\) while still requiring one trial at every iteration.

Finally, the PCPFS constants compare outer iterations. A predictor-corrector iteration evaluates Hessians at both the current point and the predictor point, whereas a PFS iteration uses one current Hessian. State this unit when describing improved constants. The algebraic outer-iteration improvement alone does not establish fewer Hessian evaluations or lower running time.

Problem 7 (Minor: feasibility depth and dual inverses need precise data assumptions).  In (56), p. 2183, the maximization must restrict \(0\leq\delta\leq1\). As written, \(\delta\geq0\) makes the depth unbounded for any nonempty affine feasible set. Indeed, because \(0\in\operatorname{int}Q\), a ball of radius \(r>0\) lies in \(Q\). For \(\delta>1\), the reflected dilation \((1-\delta)Q\) contains a ball of radius \((\delta-1)r\), hence eventually any fixed feasible point.

Section 4.2 explicitly assumes that \(0\) is an analytic center, \(\nabla F(0)=0\), and \(F(0)=0\) (p. 2183). These are operative assumptions. The barrier \(-\log(1+x)\) on \((-1,\infty)\) has no analytic center and is therefore outside that setting, not a counterexample under its assumptions. Existence must remain a restriction of the claimed scope rather than a normalization available for every closed convex \(Q\). This is a scope clarification, not an additional established defect.

The dual Hessian \[\nabla^2\Phi(u)=A[\nabla^2F(x)]^{-1}A^T\] is invertible only when \(A\) has full row rank. This is not guaranteed by the arbitrary equality matrix in Section 4.2 or by \(A=(-c^T;B)\) in Section 6.2. For \(Q=[-1,1]^2\), its separable logarithmic barrier, \(B=(1,0)\) and \(c=(1,0)\), the two rows of \(A\) are dependent and the objective is constant on \(Bx=0\). Remove redundant consistent constraints and handle such constant objectives separately before applying the displayed inverses.

Likewise, treat zero directions and initially solved problems before divisions by their local dual norms; write \(\Phi_t(u)=\Phi(u)-t\langle b,u\rangle\) in place of the vector subtraction on p. 2196; and use \(\sigma>0\) in reciprocal certificates (105)–(108). The Major counterexample in Problem 1 uses a bounded set, a genuine analytic center, full rank and a nonzero direction, so it does not depend on any of these degeneracies.

Problem 8 (Minor: specify the cubic parameter and the observable restart termination).  Equation (113), p. 2200, introduces a cubic parameter \(M>0\), followed by a convergence claim. Positivity alone is insufficient for that claim. A standard sufficient condition for the displayed cubic model to majorize \(f\) is \(M\geq H_f\); the parameter assumptions of the imported rate (114) should be stated.

Here is an exact two-cycle with a positive parameter. Let \[f(x)=\tfrac12x^2+K\log\cosh x,\qquad
 K=\frac{10}{\tanh2-4\mathop{\mathrm{sech}}^2 2}=14.6751448162\ldots .\] The denominator is positive, \(f''(x)=1+K\mathop{\mathrm{sech}}^2x\geq1\), and \[|f'''(x)|=2K\mathop{\mathrm{sech}}^2x\,|\tanh x|
 \leq H_f=\frac{4K}{3\sqrt3}=11.2969317467\ldots .\] Thus the function satisfies the strong convexity and global Lipschitz-Hessian assumptions.

Take \(M=1\). At \(x=2\), the derivative of the strongly convex cubic model with respect to its step \(h\), evaluated at \(h=-4\), is \[f'(2)-4f''(2)-8
 =K(\tanh2-4\mathop{\mathrm{sech}}^22)-10=0.\] Hence its unique minimizer sends \(2\) to \(-2\). Evenness sends \(-2\) back to \(2\). The method does not converge to the minimizer \(0\) for this allowed positive \(M\).

This example does not refute Theorem 13, whose starting assumption is the rate (114). It shows why that assumption cannot be inferred from \(M>0\) alone. With the appropriate rate available, the halving proof and the resulting exponents remain intact.

The restart description should also first handle an initial point already in \(\mathcal Q_f\), rather than applying a negative or undefined logarithmic stage bound. Remark 5 makes the stage lengths computable using a known lower bound \(\widetilde f\), but the stopping test \(f(y_k)-f^\ast\leq1/(8M_f^2)\) still involves \(f^\ast\). Supply a predetermined sufficient number of stages based on \(f(x_0)-\widetilde f\), or an observable certificate. This is a specification repair, not a counterexample to the conditional halving argument.

# Assessment

Major revision is required for the feasibility rate and the claimed direct transfer to linear programming. The interval-barrier construction is an analytic counterexample to the asserted uniform PFS rate, while the complementarity calculation identifies a failed hypothesis in the LP reduction. These objections should be propagated to the introduction and the comparison of methods.

The other findings have more limited effects. The clipped-step counterexample lies inside the target region, the signed lemma has a direct corrected version, the repaired predictor constants remain positive, and the restart comparison remains valid under its assumed rate and proper regularization parameters. No conclusion here asserts that every principal theorem of the paper is false.
