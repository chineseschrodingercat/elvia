\section*{Definitions and dependencies}
For an open set \(Z\subset\mathbb R^4\), the source defines \[V_d(Z)=\operatorname{vol}\{z\in Z:\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial Z)<d\},\qquad
 q=\liminf_{d\downarrow0}\frac{\log V_d(Z)}{\log d}.\] Equation (2.2), p. 7, calls this number the order of volume decay. It is a logarithmic exponent and does not assert \(V_d=O(d^q)\). The inner upper Minkowski dimension is \(4-q\). The subleading capacity term is \(e_k(Z)=c_k(Z)-2\sqrt{k\operatorname{vol}Z}\). A four-dimensional ball of capacity \(a\) has radius \(\sqrt{a/\pi}\) and volume \(a^2/2\). The ball and disjoint-union capacity formulas used below are stated in Section 2.2 and used in (4.4)–(4.6), pp. 16–17.\par
Theorem 3.1 is presented as the quantified form of the fractal Weyl law. Its estimate enters (5.4), p. 22, in the proof of Theorem 5.2, from which Theorems 1.2 and 1.4 follow. Proposition 6.1 constructs the bounded symplectic realization underlying Proposition 1.3. A defect in one statement does not automatically invalidate all these separate conclusions.\par
\section*{The exact exponent in Theorem 3.1}
Equation reference: eq:claimed. Problem 1 (A logarithmic correction violates the stated bound).  Theorem 3.1, p. 11, assumes that \(Z\) is relatively compact and open and has volume-decay order \(q\), and concludes that a constant \(C>0\) satisfies \[
 e_k(Z)>-Ck^{(2-q)/4}\qquad\hbox{for all }k.\] The proof on p. 13 treats the boundary tube as \(O(d^q)\). Under the definition in (2.2), [eq:claimed] (\#eq:claimed) is false.\par
\subsection*{One fixed admissible open set}
Equation reference: eq:S. Use natural logarithms and fix \[A=10^{-3},\quad m=10,\quad
 a_i=A\frac{\log(i+m)}{(i+m)^{3/5}},\quad
 r_i=\sqrt{a_i/\pi},\qquad i\ge1.\] The \(a_i\) are strictly decreasing, positive, and square summable. The derivative of \(x^{-3/5}\log x\) is negative for \(x\ge11\), and \[\begin{aligned}
 S:=\sum_i a_i^2
 &\le A^2\int_{10}^{\infty}x^{-6/5}(\log x)^2\,dx\\
 &=5A^2\,10^{-1/5}\bigl((\log10)^2+10\log10+50\bigr)
 <445A^2.\end{aligned}\] The integrand is decreasing on \([10,\infty)\), justifying the comparison; the last bound follows from \(\log10<3\).\par
Choose a dyadic length \(\ell_i\) with \(2r_i\le\ell_i<4r_i\). Then \(\sum_i\ell_i^4<256S/\pi^2<1\). Place these dyadic cubes, in nonincreasing side length, in \([0,1]^4\), each time choosing the lexicographically first unoccupied cube of the required grid. Such a cube always exists: earlier larger cubes occupy integer numbers of grid cells, and exhausting the grid would contradict the total volume bound. Put disjoint open balls of radii \(r_i\) at their centers \(c_i\) and define \[Z=\bigcup_{i\ge1}B(c_i,r_i).\] This is open in standard symplectic \(\mathbb R^4\), with compact closure in \([0,1]^4\) and volume \(S/2\). Theorem 3.1 has no connectedness hypothesis, and Example 5.3, p. 23, explicitly considers infinitely disconnected sets. Thus every domain assumption holds for this one fixed \(Z\), independent of \(k\).\par
\subsection*{The volume-decay hypothesis holds with \(q=2/3\)}
Equation reference: eq:tube, eq:N. Each constituent sphere belongs to \(\partial Z\), even when two closed balls touch. Therefore \[
 V_d(Z)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}\sum_i
 \left(r_i^4-(r_i-d)_+^4\right).\] Let \(N(d)=\max\{i:r_i\ge d\}\). Inverting the radius formula gives \[
 N(d)\asymp d^{-10/3}\bigl(\log(1/d)\bigr)^{5/3}.\] Indeed, substituting \(C d^{-10/3}(\log(1/d))^{5/3}\) in \(\log(n+m)/(n+m)^{3/5}\) yields a constant multiple of \(d^2\). Small and large fixed \(C\), together with monotonicity, give the two bounds.\par
Equation reference: eq:volume. For \(0<d\le r\), \(dr^3\le r^4-(r-d)^4\le4dr^3\). Thus integral comparison gives \[\begin{aligned}
 V_d(Z)&\asymp \sum_{i>N(d)}r_i^4+
                 d\sum_{i\le N(d)}r_i^3,\\
 \sum_{i>N}r_i^4&\asymp N^{-1/5}(\log N)^2,\qquad
 \sum_{i\le N}r_i^3\asymp N^{1/10}(\log N)^{3/2}.\end{aligned}\] Substitution of [eq:N] (\#eq:N) now yields \[
 V_d(Z)\asymp d^{2/3}\bigl(\log(1/d)\bigr)^{5/3}.\] The limit defining \(q\) therefore exists and equals \(2/3\), while \(V_d(Z)/d^{2/3}\to\infty\).\par
\subsection*{The actual capacity deficit exceeds every proposed constant}
Equation reference: eq:optimization. The capacity axioms give \[
 c_k(Z)=\max\left\{\sum_i a_i d_i:
 d_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\
 \sum_i d_i(d_i+1)/2\le k\right\}.\] Every compact Liouville subdomain meets only finitely many component balls, and finite ball unions can be exhausted from inside; hence the infinite union causes no extra hypothesis. Every feasible vector has at most \(k\) positive entries. Rearranging these onto the first \(k\) balls leaves a finite optimization problem, so a maximum exists. Its cost equals \(k\): integer slack could otherwise be spent by setting an unused positive-capacity ball’s \(d_i\) to \(1\).\par
Equation reference: eq:deficit, eq:contradiction. Take a decreasing optimizer, positive on the first \(n\) entries, and set \(D=\lceil\sqrt{2k}\rceil\), \(N=n+D\). Cauchy–Schwarz and saturation imply \[\begin{aligned}
 -e_k(Z)
 &=\sqrt{2Sk}-\sum_{i\le n}a_i d_i\\
 &\ge\sum_{i\le n}a_i(\sqrt{d_i^2+d_i}-d_i)
 \ge(\sqrt2-1)\sum_{i\le n}a_i.\end{aligned}\] For each active \(j\), reducing \(d_j\) by one frees exactly \(d_j\) units of cost. Spending them on \(n+1,\ldots,n+d_j\) gives, by optimality, \[a_j\ge\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{n+d_j}a_\ell\ge d_j a_N.\] Since \(d_j\le D\), we obtain \[k\le\sum_{j\le n}d_j^2\le S/a_N^2,\qquad
 \frac{(N+m)^{6/5}}{\log^2(N+m)}\ge \frac{A^2}{S}k.\] First using \(\log(N+m)>1\), and then substituting the resulting polynomial bound into the logarithm, proves \(N+m\gtrsim k^{5/6}(\log k)^{5/3}\). Subtracting \(D=O(k^{1/2})\) preserves this bound for \(n\). Summing over \(\lceil n/2\rceil\le i\le n\) gives \(\sum_{i\le n}a_i\gtrsim n^{2/5}\log n\). Consequently \[
 -e_k(Z)\ge c\,k^{1/3}(\log k)^{5/3}
 \quad\hbox{for every sufficiently large }k.\] For \(q=2/3\), [eq:claimed] (\#eq:claimed) requires \(-e_k(Z)<Ck^{1/3}\), contradicting [eq:contradiction] (\#eq:contradiction) for every finite \(C\). This is an analytic counterexample, not an asymptotic inference from finite numerical samples.\par
\subsection*{Alternative readings, impact, and repair boundary}
Interpreting volume-decay order as a uniform power upper bound would exclude this counterexample. That stronger premise is explicitly stated in Proposition 3.2, but is not the definition in (2.2). Alternatively, retaining the logarithmic definition gives, for each \(0<\varepsilon<q\), a bound \(V_d\le C_\varepsilon d^{q-\varepsilon}\). The uniform-bound argument gives \[e_k(Z)\ge-C_\varepsilon k^{(2-q+\varepsilon)/4}.\] Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) retains \(d_{\rm ECH}\le4-q\) in Theorem 1.9. The strict exponent separation in Theorem 5.2’s proof likewise allows a sufficiently small loss. The finding is Major for the false quantitative theorem and its asserted equivalence. It does not refute the dimension inequality or principal packing-obstruction conclusions. No claim is made about the unacquired journal text.\par
\section*{The displacement map in the bounded realization}
Problem 2 (The displayed shear is not symplectic).  In Proposition 6.1, Steps 2a–2b, pp. 25–26, the source sets \(G=v+\tfrac12\), \(H=\chi(x)G\), and \(X_G=\partial_u\). Equations (6.1)–(6.2) describe \[\Psi(x,y,u,v)=
 (x,\ y+(v+\tfrac12)\chi'(x),\ u+\chi(x),\ v).\] With \(\omega=dx\wedge dy+du\wedge dv\), this is not symplectic wherever \(\chi'\ne0\).\par
Equation reference: eq:pullback. Differentiation gives \[\begin{aligned}
 \Psi^*(dx\wedge dy)&=dx\wedge dy+\chi'(x)\,dx\wedge dv,\\
 \Psi^*(du\wedge dv)&=du\wedge dv+\chi'(x)\,dx\wedge dv,\end{aligned}\] and hence \[
 \Psi^*\omega-\omega=2\chi'(x)\,dx\wedge dv.\] The \(\chi''\) term vanishes because it multiplies \(dx\wedge dx\).\par
The defect occurs where the source prescribes \(\chi(x)=x\) for \(x\le1\). Choose the admissible smooth function \(f(r)=(1+r)^{-3/2}\): it is decreasing, \(f(0)=1\), and its integral is finite. On any interior fiber patch with \(0<x<1\), the defect is \(2dx\wedge dv\). The original pairing \(\omega(\partial_x,\partial_v)\) is \(0\); the pulled-back pairing is \(2\). The retained independent rational-matrix calculation reproduces this discrepancy exactly.\par
The convention \(X_G=\partial_u\) requires \(X_H=\chi\partial_u-G\chi'\partial_y\). Changing the global Hamiltonian convention changes both signs and cannot justify the displayed two positive shears. Negating the \(y\) displacement cancels [eq:pullback] (\#eq:pullback); this local repair is verified. The corresponding image-strip, wrapping, global injectivity, and boundary-dimension arguments have not all been rechecked, so the whole construction is not declared repaired.\par
This is Major for the explicit construction in Proposition 6.1 and its use in Proposition 1.3. The existence of a bounded symplectic realization has not been disproved. The defect concerns an essential map; a short local correction does not by itself supply the global verification.\par
\section*{The convex-domain parallel-body identity}
Problem 3 (Erosion followed by dilation need not recover a convex domain).  Lemma 2.3, p. 9, describes \(Z\) as the radius-\(d\) tube around \(Z(d)=\{z\in Z:\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial Z)\ge d\}\) and identifies the inner boundary-layer volume with \(N_d(Z(d))-N_0(Z(d))\). That equality is false.\par
Let \(Z=(0,1)^4\) and \(0<d<1/2\). Then \(Z(d)=[d,1-d]^4\). For \(x=(d/4,d/4,d/4,d/4)\), \[\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial Z)=d/4<d,\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}(x,Z(d))=3d/2>d.\] Both inequalities persist on a neighborhood, so the discrepancy has positive volume and is unaffected by open versus closed tubes. Writing \(a=1-2d\), the actual volume is \(1-a^4\), while the stated tube difference is \[8a^3d+6\pi a^2d^2+\frac{16\pi}{3}ad^3+\frac{\pi^2}{2}d^4.\] At \(d=1/4\) the values are \(15/16\) and \(1/4+13\pi/96+\pi^2/512\), respectively.\par
The lemma’s conclusion holds under its original assumptions. Translate an interior point to the origin and choose \(r\overline B\subset K=\overline Z\subset R\overline B\). For \(K_d=K\ominus d\overline B\) and \(0<d<r\), \[(1-d/r)K\subset K_d\subset(1-d/R)K.\] The first inclusion follows from convexity. For the second, in each unit direction \(u\), \(x\in K_d\) satisfies \(\langle x,u\rangle\le h_K(u)-d
\le(1-d/R)h_K(u)\), since \(h_K(u)\le R\). Taking volumes yields \[\frac{\operatorname{vol}K}{R}d\le V_d(Z)\le\frac{4\operatorname{vol}K}{r}d.\] This verifies the linear decay premise used for the convex case of Theorem 1.4, p. 22. The finding is Minor: it concerns a background identity, and the downstream geometric premise is established here without changing its assumptions or conclusion.\par
