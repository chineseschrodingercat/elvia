# Statements and hypotheses

Let \(M\) be a compact complex hyperbolic quotient, with real dimension \(2n\), and let \[(-\Delta_g-\lambda_j^2)u_j=0,\qquad
 \lambda_j\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \|u_j\|_{L^2(M)}=1.\] Set \(h_j=\lambda_j^{-1}\). A semiclassical measure \(\mu\) satisfies \[\langle\operatorname{Op}_{h_j}(a)u_j,u_j\rangle
 \longrightarrow\int_{T^*M}a\,d\mu
 \qquad(a\in C_c^\infty(T^*M)).\] It is a geodesic-flow invariant probability measure supported on \(S^*M\), and its spatial projection is the weak limit of \(|u_j|^2\,d\operatorname{vol}_g\) (pp. 1, 4–5).

Theorem 1.3 asserts the existence of a positive-dimensional compact immersed totally geodesic complex submanifold \(\Sigma\) of \(M\) such that \[S^*\Sigma\subset\operatorname{supp}\mu.\] Here the cosphere bundle is understood through its image in \(S^*M\) under the metric identification. The choice \(\Sigma=M\) is allowed. If there is no proper such submanifold, the conclusion is \(\operatorname{supp}\mu=S^*M\). Theorem 1.2 is the corresponding spatial inclusion \(\Sigma\subset\operatorname{supp}\widetilde\mu\) (pp. 3–5).

These conclusions are support inclusions. They do not assert concentration on a proper submanifold, equality of the support with a proper \(S^*\Sigma\), or positive mass assigned to that lower-dimensional set. Universal full support is separately formulated as Conjecture 1.4.

# The geometric control-set alternative

A set is \(V^\pm\)-dense if it meets every complete orbit of the corresponding fast horocyclic flow. This is an orbit-intersection condition; topological density alone does not suffice (p. 15). Theorem 3.3 identifies the closure of each joint geodesic/fast-horocyclic orbit with \(S\Sigma\) for a compact immersed totally geodesic complex submanifold; the metric identifies the tangent and cotangent statements.

The following use of that theorem in Corollary 3.4 is valid. Let \(\mathcal U\subset S^*M\) be open and invariant under the geodesic flow. If \(\mathcal U\) misses a complete \(V^+\)-orbit, its closed complement contains that orbit. Invariance makes the complement contain its geodesic saturation, and closedness makes it contain the closure of that saturation. By Theorem 3.3 this closure is \(S^*\Sigma\). The same argument applies to \(V^-\). Thus either \(\mathcal U\) meets every fast orbit of both signs, or it misses an entire \(S^*\Sigma\) (p. 17). Both openness and geodesic invariance are used; neither is dispensable in this argument.

# From the control estimate to support

Equation reference: eq:control. Theorem 4.3 states that if \(a\in S^0_{1,0}(T^*M)\) is independent of \(h\) and \(\{a\ne0\}\cap S^*M\) is \(V^+\)-dense and \(V^-\)-dense, there are constants \(C,h_0>0\), depending on \(M,a\), such that \[
 \|u\|_{L^2}
 \le C\|\operatorname{Op}_h(a)u\|_{L^2}
   +\frac{C\log(1/h)}{h}\|(-h^2\Delta_g-I)u\|_{L^2}
 \quad (u\in H^2(M),\ 0<h\le h_0).\] The following verifies its application to Theorem 1.3, conditional on this estimate and Theorem 3.3.

## A single cutoff for both signs

Suppose \(F=\operatorname{supp}\mu\) contains no \(S^*\Sigma\) of the stated type. Invariance of \(\mu\) under the geodesic flow implies invariance of its support, since the flow is a measure-preserving homeomorphism. Consequently \(\mathcal U=S^*M\setminus F\) is open and invariant. The geometric alternative just checked shows that \(\mathcal U\) is \(V^+\)-dense and \(V^-\)-dense.

Lemma 3.1(1) applies separately to the two vector fields. It gives compact sets \(K_+,K_-\subset\mathcal U\), each meeting every orbit of its respective flow. Their union \(K=K_+\cup K_-\) is compact, is contained in \(\mathcal U\), and meets every orbit of both flows. This explains the simultaneous compact-set assertion in the proof on p. 37. The lemma’s exhaustion argument uses compactness of \(S^*M\) (pp. 15–16).

Choose a fixed \(a\in C_c^\infty(T^*M)\), equal to one near \(K\), such that \[\operatorname{supp}a\cap S^*M\subset\mathcal U.\] Smooth separation of the compact set \(K\) from the closed set \(F\), followed by extension to an annular neighborhood of \(S^*M\), provides such a symbol. It satisfies the hypotheses of [eq:control] (#eq:control). In particular, \(a\) and the resulting constant \(C\) are fixed along the sequence.

## The norm limit and contradiction

The ordinary product and adjoint rules, (4.1) and (4.4), give \[\operatorname{Op}_h(a)^*\operatorname{Op}_h(a)=\operatorname{Op}_h(|a|^2)+O(h)_{L^2\to L^2}.\] The conjugation in the adjoint rule is essential; it is explicit in the source image on p. 33. Since \(\|u_j\|_{L^2}=1\), \[\begin{aligned}
 \|\operatorname{Op}_{h_j}(a)u_j\|_{L^2}^2
 &=\langle\operatorname{Op}_{h_j}(|a|^2)u_j,u_j\rangle+O(h_j)\\
 &\longrightarrow\int |a|^2\,d\mu=0.\end{aligned}\] The convergence uses the fixed compact smooth symbol \(|a|^2\). The integral vanishes because \(\mu\) is supported on \(S^*M\) and the cutoff is disjoint from \(F\) there (pp. 32–33, 37).

Smooth eigenfunctions belong to \(H^2(M)\), and \[(-h_j^2\Delta_g-I)u_j=0.\] For all sufficiently large \(j\), [eq:control] (#eq:control) therefore gives \[1\le C\|\operatorname{Op}_{h_j}(a)u_j\|_{L^2},\] a contradiction. This establishes the claimed implication without requiring an additional long-time calculus in this final step (pp. 36–37).

# Projection and boundary checks

Let \(\pi:S^*M\to M\) be the base projection. If \(q\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\), every neighborhood \(O\) of \(\pi(q)\) has an open inverse image containing \(q\), so \((\pi_*\mu)(O)>0\). Thus \(\pi(\operatorname{supp}\mu)\subset\operatorname{supp}(\pi_*\mu)\). Since \(\pi(S^*\Sigma)=\Sigma\) as images, Theorem 1.3 implies Theorem 1.2. An immersion need not be injective for this argument.

The already full-support case is consistent with \(\Sigma=M\); the contradiction argument is needed only when the stated containment fails. The absence of a proper admissible \(\Sigma\) gives the stated full-support corollary. No quantitative lower bound on \(\mu(S^*\Sigma)\) follows from the inclusion alone.

There is also a useful dimensional interpretation. For a connected complex curve, a positive-dimensional complex immersion has the same dimension as its target and is locally a diffeomorphism. Its image is open; compactness makes that image closed. Hence it is the whole curve. Accordingly, if the support conclusion is applied in complex dimension one, it has no proper-submanifold alternative. This elementary observation is not a new proof of the surface theorem or a verification of a dimension-one extension of the higher-dimensional analytic argument.

The residual term in [eq:control] (#eq:control) vanishes for the exact eigenfunctions under consideration. A quasimode application would instead require control of that residual after multiplication by \(\log(1/h)/h\); no additional quasimode theorem is certified here. Similarly, an \(h\)-dependent cutoff cannot be substituted without checking new uniformity assumptions.

# Inputs and noncounted correction

The support deduction depends on Theorem 3.3 and Theorem 4.3. The former uses orbit-closure theory. The latter uses the uniform long-word decay in Proposition 4.9 (p. 42), whose proof invokes porosity, anisotropic semiclassical calculus and Fourier-integral localization. The one-dimensional fractal uncertainty principle is stated in Proposition 5.3 with \[0\le\gamma_1<\tfrac12<\gamma_0\le1\] and attributed to the cited Bourgain–Dyatlov and Dyatlov–Jin–Nonnenmacher work (p. 49). These statements were read and their role was traced. Their complete proofs and the external references were not independently audited in this contribution.

One local notation slip is visible on p. 37: the first paragraph places \(\Sigma\) in \(S^*M\), whereas Theorem 1.3 correctly places \(\Sigma\) in \(M\) and \(S^*\Sigma\) in \(S^*M\). The argument unambiguously uses the latter meaning. This can be corrected locally and is not counted as a substantive mathematical problem.

# Conclusion
