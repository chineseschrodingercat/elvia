Abstract: The acquired 85-page preprint is examined with particular attention to hypotheses, dimension dependence and the vanishing-viscosity limit. Eight substantive corrections are established. The general-drift extension of a weighted Lipschitz estimate is false and its direct use in the gradient argument requires new justification. Separately, the literal displayed semimonotonicity assumption admits an inviscid limit with a shock; the apparent intended correction excludes that example. Six further problems concern label estimates, coupling, empirical measures, matrix arguments, representation formulae and an auxiliary compactness lemma. Their errors and repair boundaries are distinguished from the principal conclusions. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions, assumptions and dependencies

Equation reference: eq:nash, eq:weight. For quadratic Hamiltonians, the backward system is \[
-\partial_t u^i-\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\sigma I+\beta J)D^2u^i\bigr)
+\frac12|D_i u^i|^2+\sum_{j\ne i}D_j u^j\cdot D_j u^i=f^i,
\qquad u^i(T,\cdot)=g^i.\] Here \(J=J_N\otimes I_d\), \(\sigma>0,\beta\ge0\). The paper uses Euclidean vector norms and quadratic-form matrix order even for nonsymmetric matrices. Its distinguished weighted norm is \[
\|x\|_i^2=|x^i|^2+\frac1N\sum_{j\ne i}|x^j|^2.\] The mean-field conditions (MF) control own-state and off-diagonal derivatives with different powers of \(N\); they do not uniformly bound the full Frobenius norm of a diagonal matrix.

The \(\mathcal D\)-condition controls the one-sided Lipschitz constant of \(x\mapsto(D_i h^i(x))_i\). The \(\mathcal L\)-condition concerns sums of four-point rectangular differences. Neither supplies symmetry of the mixed Jacobian. Theorems 3.8 and A.1 require small semimonotonicity constants and large \(N\), with additional convexity hypotheses for general Hamiltonians. The heterogeneous limit requires bounded smooth data, symmetry in other players’ states, and label regularity (LP). Section 7 sets \(\sigma=1,\beta=0\); Section 8 instead takes \(\sigma=\sigma_N\downarrow0\) with a lower bound on \(\sigma_N\log\log N\).

The principal dependency requiring further work is the application of Lemma 4.3 in Proposition 8.1 to obtain a gradient bound independent of \(N\) and \(\sigma_N\). Problems 1 and 2 concern different aspects of that argument and its statement. The remaining problems have more local repair boundaries.

# A weighted estimate used outside its proved structure

Problem 1 (False arbitrary-drift extension; Major).  Remark 4.4 (p. 30) extends Lemma 4.3 to transport \(\sum_j b^jD_j u^i\) assuming only the Euclidean one-sided condition (4.2). Proposition 8.1 (p. 69), deriving (8.4), directly applies the weighted estimate to \(D_i u^i\), saying that only the forcing condition (4.3) remains to be checked.

## Decisive example and hypotheses

Take \(d=1,i=1,\sigma=1,\beta=0,N\ge2\), and fixed \(a>0\). Set \[b(x)=(a\sin x^2,0,\ldots,0),\qquad f=0,\qquad g(x)=\sin x^1.\] The drift and data are bounded and smooth. The eigenvalues of the nonzero block of \((Db+Db^\top)/2\) are \(\pm a\cos(x^2)/2\), so (4.2) holds with \(M=a/2\), independent of \(N\). The weighted Lipschitz constant of \(g\) is one.

Equation reference: eq:driftex. In forward time \(s=T-t\), consider the two-variable periodic solution \[
v_s-\Delta v+\frac12(v_{x^1})^2+a\sin(x^2)v_{x^1}=0,
\qquad v(0,x)=\sin x^1.\] It exists smoothly on a fixed short interval independently of \(N\): the heat-semigroup integral equation is a contraction in a bounded \(C^1\) ball for small time, because the gradient kernel has integrable \(s^{-1/2}\) norm and the quadratic term is locally Lipschitz there. Smooth data give the needed initial derivatives. Extend the solution constantly in the other coordinates.

Proof. At \(s=0\), \[v_s=-\sin x^1-\tfrac12\cos^2x^1-a\sin x^2\cos x^1,
\qquad \partial_s\partial_{x^2}v(0,0)=-a.\] Thus \(\partial_{x^2}v(s_*,0)\ne0\) for a fixed small \(s_*>0\). The proposed weighted bound, with \(c_1\) depending only on dimension-independent quantities, implies \[|\partial_{x^2}v(s_*,0)|\le c_1N^{-1/2}.\] Its left side is independent of \(N\) and nonzero, a contradiction. Omitting the quadratic term gives the same initial jet and refutes the linear-transport reading as well. ◻

If a separate \(\mathcal D[u]\) lower bound is retained in this reading, take the other components to be zero: the smooth solution has only a fixed two-dimensional nonzero Jacobian block and a uniform lower symmetric-part bound.

## Counterevidence and repair boundary

The original Nash-specific Lemma 4.3 is not refuted. At its doubling maximum, (4.6)–(4.7) give equality of the distinguished derivatives and allow the own-component cancellation in (4.9)–(4.10), pp. 28–29. Arbitrary \(b\) lacks that cancellation.

The counterexample is not asserted to be the actual Nash drift in Proposition 8.1. Its extra structure may support a different argument. Nevertheless, the false general extension does not justify the componentwise invocation, and checking only the forcing term does not establish a weighted drift hypothesis. A new game-specific gradient argument or a verified weighted one-sided estimate is needed for the \(\sigma\)-uniform compactness used on p. 70. No complete replacement proof or counterexample to the intended final theorem is claimed.

# The literal hypothesis of the inviscid-limit theorem

Problem 2 (Repeated own-state argument in (3.9); Major).  Equation (3.9), p. 23, contains \[\sum_i\bigl(D_xF(x^i,m_{x^{-i}})
-D_xF(x^i,m_{y^{-i}})\bigr)\cdot(x^i-y^i)\ge-M|x-y|^2.\] Theorem 3.17 (p. 24) expressly invokes this assumption for \(F\) and \(G\).

## A bounded smooth example

Take \(d=1,T=2,H=p^2/2,\beta=0,F=0\), and \(G(x,m)=\cos x\). All spatial derivatives are bounded and smooth and all measure derivatives vanish, so Remark 3.15’s regularity conditions hold. The printed left side of (3.9) is identically zero. For sufficiently large \(N\), choose \(\sigma_N=(\log\log N)^{-1/2}\). Then \(\sigma_N\to0\) and \(\sigma_N\log\log N\to\infty\), meeting any finite threshold; the finitely many smaller terms may be positive arbitrarily.

Equation reference: eq:cole. The uncoupled exact Cole–Hopf solution is \[
v_\sigma(s,x)=-2\sigma\log\left[
(4\pi\sigma s)^{-1/2}\int_\mathbb R
e^{-[\cos y+(x-y)^2/(2s)]/(2\sigma)}\,dy\right].\] It is bounded and smooth on finite time intervals. As \(\sigma\downarrow0\), \[v_\sigma(s,x)\longrightarrow V(s,x)=\min_y\{\cos y+(x-y)^2/(2s)\}.\] This follows by elementary Laplace upper and lower bounds near minimizers and outside a fixed compact interval; the quadratic term confines the minimizers and controls the tails. No derivative convergence is assumed.

Proof. At \(s=2,x=0\), let \(\phi(y)=\cos y+y^2/4\). Then \(\phi''(0)=-1/2\). Positive critical points satisfy \(y=2\sin y\); strict decrease of \(\sin y/y\) on \((0,\pi)\) gives one positive nonzero solution \(a\). Outside \([-\pi,\pi]\), \(\phi(y)\ge-1+\pi^2/4>1=\phi(0)\), so the global minimizers are precisely \(\pm a\). The tilt \(-xy/2\) selects \(a\) on the right and \(-a\) on the left. Hence \[\partial_x^-V(2,0)=a/2,\qquad \partial_x^+V(2,0)=-a/2.\] Independent computation gives \(a\approx1.895494267033981\) and \(\phi(a)\approx0.5792021049470533\). Thus the value limit is not differentiable, contrary to the bounded Lipschitz derivative required by Theorem 3.17 and (3.11), even along a subsequence. ◻

## Intended reading and impact

The description \(\mathcal D\)-semimonotonicity and Section 8’s invocation of Proposition 5.1 strongly suggest replacing the second \(x^i\) in (3.9) by \(y^i\). Under that correction, \(G''\ge-1\) gives a nonzero constant, and the intended smallness condition at \(T=2\) excludes the example. The literal statement is false; the intended corrected theorem is not disproved. Correcting (3.9) does not fill the separate gap in Problem Problem 1 (#prob:drift).

# Dependence on all player labels

Problem 3 (Normalization and terminal data in Lemma 7.1; Minor).  Lemma 7.1 (p. 51) bounds the value and own-gradient differences by \(C(|\lambda_1-\lambda'_1|+N^{-1}|\widehat\lambda-\widehat\lambda'|)\). The norm is Euclidean by p. 13. Equation (7.3), p. 52, also omits a terminal-gradient contribution.

## Exact Nash solutions satisfying the hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:labelg. Set \(d=1,\sigma=1,\beta=0,H=p^2/2\), and \[
f^i=0,\qquad
g^i(x)=\lambda_i\sin x^i+\frac{\varepsilon}{N-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\sin x^j,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1,\quad\lambda_i\in[0,1].\] These data are bounded and smooth uniformly in \(N\), symmetric in the other states, and satisfy (LP). They satisfy (MF), for example with \(L_g=2\), and their \(\mathcal L\)-rectangular differences vanish. Thus the \(\mathcal L\)-semimonotonicity constant is zero; arbitrarily large \(N\) and any fixed horizon are allowed.

Equation reference: eq:labelsol. Define \[v_\lambda(s,x)=-2\log\mathbb Ee^{-\lambda\sin(x+\sqrt2B_s)/2},
\qquad w_\lambda=\partial_\lambda v_\lambda.\] The identities \(v_s=v_{xx}-v_x^2/2\) and \(w_s=w_{xx}-v_xw_x\) show by direct substitution that \[
u^i(t,x)=v_{\lambda_i}(T-t,x^i)
+\frac{\varepsilon}{N-1}\sum_{j\ne i}w_{\lambda_j}(T-t,x^j)\] is the exact Nash solution with terminal data [eq:labelg] (#eq:labelg). Its own derivative is \(v'_{\lambda_i}\); the remaining terms cancel the other-player transport in [eq:nash] (#eq:nash).

Choose \(\lambda_1=\lambda'_1=0\), all other labels one versus zero, and all states zero. At any fixed \(s>0\), \(w_0(s,0)=0\), whereas \[w_1(s,0)=\frac{\mathbb E[S e^{-S/2}]}{\mathbb E[e^{-S/2}]}<0,
\qquad S=\sin(\sqrt2 B_s).\] The symmetric law gives \(\mathbb E[S e^{-S/2}]=-\mathbb E[S\sinh(S/2)]<0\). The value difference is therefore \(\varepsilon|w_1(s,0)|>0\), independent of \(N\); the asserted right side is \(C\sqrt{N-1}/N\). At \(s=1,\varepsilon=1/4\), independent quadrature gives approximately \(0.05956859624\).

## Repair and limits

The summation on p. 52 is an \(\ell^1\) sum. Its consistent scale is \[C\left(|\lambda_1-\lambda'_1|
+\frac1N\sum_{j>1}|\lambda_j-\lambda'_j|\right)
\le C\left(|\lambda_1-\lambda'_1|
+\frac{|\widehat\lambda-\widehat\lambda'|_2}{\sqrt N}\right).\] At \(\tau=T\), (7.3) would also force \(D_i(g_\lambda^i-g_{\lambda'}^i)=0\), contradicted by [eq:labelg] (#eq:labelg) when the own label changes. The terminal derivative must be retained before short-interval iteration.

The corrected counting scale is compatible with the Wasserstein continuity in Theorem 7.5, and a single-label change still has scale \(N^{-1}\). However, a valid componentwise gradient estimate with its terminal data must be supplied; a full replacement gradient proof is not certified here. The false statement is Lemma 7.1 as written, not the heterogeneous limit theorem.

# Conditional couplings and empirical weights

Problem 4 (Two coupling corrections in Lemma 7.13; Minor).  The all-label estimate (7.25), p. 60, lacks a label-distance term, and the coupling PDE there has the opposite mixed-diffusion sign from a synchronous coupling.

The lemma permits any bounded drift Lipschitz in \((\lambda,x,\mu)\). Take \(d=1\), \(B^\lambda=\lambda\), uniform label law \(\rho\) on \([0,1]\), and \(\mu_0=\widetilde\mu_0=\rho\otimes\delta_0\). All initial conditionals equal \(\delta_0\), while at time \(t\) they are \(\mathcal N(\lambda t,2t)\). Therefore \[W_2(m_t^\lambda,m_t^{\lambda'})=t|\lambda-\lambda'|,\] although the right side of (7.25) is zero. A term \(C|\lambda-\lambda'|\) is necessary.

With zero drift, the printed equation \[\partial_t\nu-\Delta_x\nu-\Delta_y\nu+2\partial_{xy}\nu=0\] is the equation of \((X,Y)=(\sqrt2W,-\sqrt2W)\), starting at \((0,0)\). It has \(\mathbb E|X_t-Y_t|^2=8t\), whereas the cost differentiation on p. 61 would give zero. Synchronous noise requires \(-2\partial_{xy}\nu\) on the left. With this sign and the label term, direct stochastic coupling and Gronwall’s inequality give the corrected estimate. The same-label case and joint-law estimate (7.24) are not refuted, nor is intended well-posedness.

Problem 5 (Missing label cost in Lemma 7.15; Minor).  After (7.32), p. 64, the squared joint label-state Wasserstein distance caused by changing population weights is bounded by \(2\max_i|X^i|^2\sum_k|a_k-\theta_k|\), where \(a_k=\#I_k^N/N\).

Take labels \(0,1\), target weights \(1/2,1/2\), zero initial states, zero costs and drift, and an alternating partition. At every odd \(N\), each weight differs from the target by \(1/(2N)\). All moment hypotheses hold, including \(q>4\), and the natural densities are correct. At time zero, \[W_2^2\left(a_1\delta_{(0,0)}+a_2\delta_{(1,0)},
\tfrac12\delta_{(0,0)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(1,0)}\right)=\frac1{2N}>0,\] but the asserted upper bound is zero. This works for arbitrarily large \(N\).

Equation reference: eq:massrepair. A local repair also avoids any rate assumption on the counts. Put \(\eta_k=\delta_{\lambda_k}\otimes\nu_k\). Retain common masses \(\min(a_k,\theta_k)\) and couple the excess and deficit: \[
W_2^2\left(\sum_k a_k\eta_k,\sum_k\theta_k\eta_k\right)
\le2\sum_k|a_k-\theta_k|\int(1+|x|^2)\,d\nu_k(x).\] Indeed the moved mass is \(\tfrac12\sum_k|a_k-\theta_k|\). Its label cost is at most one per unit mass; on a product coupling of the remaining equal masses use \(|x-y|^2\le2|x|^2+2|y|^2\). This proves [eq:massrepair] (#eq:massrepair) with room in the constant.

Bounded drift supplies uniform individual and group-average second moments, but not a uniform bound for the maximum over independent Brownian particles. The average-moment bound converges in expectation for finitely many groups whenever \(a_k\to\theta_k\), as assumed. It corrects the error entering (7.34). The periodic partitions actually chosen later have a stronger count rate. Neither the convergence conclusion nor Theorem 7.11 is refuted.

# Matrix order and dimension dependence

Problem 6 (Three auxiliary matrix implications; Minor).  Remark 3.4 (p. 15), the equivalence following (9.8) (p. 75), and the Frobenius estimate below (9.9) (p. 76) require correction under the declared conventions.

For \(N=2,d=1\), take \[h^1(x)=\sin x^1\sin x^2,\qquad h^2(x)=-\sin x^1\sin x^2.\] Their \(\mathcal L\)-rectangular expressions cancel exactly. The zero-diagonal mixed matrix is \[\begin{pmatrix}0&c\\-c&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad c=\cos x^1\cos x^2.\] It has zero quadratic form but is not generally zero. Moreover \(D_{12}h^1(0)=1\), excluding the own-state-plus-other-state separation asserted in Remark 3.4. Only the symmetric part must vanish.

Likewise \(K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) satisfies \(-0I\le K\le0I\) in that order, but \(\|K\|_{\rm op}=1\). Thus two-sided quadratic-form control is not equivalent to (9.8). The forward implication from the actual operator bound is valid and suffices for the following drift comparison.

Finally \(g^i(x)=\sin x^i\) satisfies (MF) with \(L_g=2\), independently of \(N\), but \[\sum_{i,j}\|D_{ji}g^i\|_\infty^2=N>2L_g^2\quad(N>8).\] Its gradient map still has operator norm at most one. In general, splitting diagonal and off-diagonal parts and using (MF) yields \[\|J_g\|_{\rm op}\le\|\operatorname{diag}J_g\|_{\rm op}
+\|J_g-\operatorname{diag}J_g\|_{\rm F}\le2L_g.\] Thus \(C_g^2\le4L_g^2\) suffices, without the false full-Frobenius estimate. The corrections preserve the needed \(N\) independence; no false general-Hamiltonian theorem is established.

# Representation formulae in the existence appendix

Problem 7 (Signs, Hamiltonian offsets and the heat kernel; Minor).  Theorem A.1, pp. 79–82, uses an incorrect representation (A.2) and a value bound omitting positive Hamiltonian offsets. Its kernel expansion and time integral on p. 80 also require correction.

Take \(d=1,\sigma=1,\beta=0\), \(f^i=g^i=0\), and \(H^i(x,p)=c+p^2/2\), \(c>0\). These satisfy smoothness, bounded-below, (H0), convexity, both zero-semimonotonicity alternatives and (MF), with arbitrarily large \(N\). The exact solution is \[u^i(t,x)=c(t-T),\qquad -\partial_tu^i+H^i(x,0)=0.\] Printed (A.2) gives \(c(T-t)\). The final supremum bound on p. 82 is zero because \(f=g=0\) and \((\inf H)^-=0\), although \(\sup|u^i|=cT\).

Along drift \(-D_pH\), the correct running cost is \[f^i-H^i(x^i,p)+D_pH^i(x^i,p)\cdot p,\] and the forward equation has \(-\sum_j\operatorname{div}_{x^j}(D_pH^j\,\rho)\). Convexity gives lower integrand bound \(f^i-H^i(x^i,0)\), rather than the positive sign on p. 82. The value estimate needs the positive part of \(H^i(\cdot,0)\) as well as its negative lower bound. Such dependence is compatible with fixed-Hamiltonian continuation constants; the example itself is a regular solution and does not refute existence or uniqueness.

Directly from \(J=J_N\otimes I_d\) (p. 11), \[\det(\sigma I+\beta J)=\sigma^{Nd}(1+N\beta/\sigma)^d,\qquad
(\sigma I+\beta J)^{-1}
=\sigma^{-1}\left(I-\frac{\beta J}{\sigma+N\beta}\right).\] The printed \(Nd\) substitution in a rank-one expression ignores multiplicity \(d\). For \(N=1,d=2,\sigma=\beta=1\), the determinant is four, while its printed scalar factor is three. The preceding invariant matrix kernel is correct.

The backward Duhamel integral is \(\int_t^T\Phi(s-t)*(f-w(s))\,ds\); the printed \(w(s-t)\) may lie outside its domain \([\tau,T]\). These are local formula corrections. A value bound can be restored with the indicated Hamiltonian dependence, but external Schauder and continuation inputs are not all reproved here.

# Quantifiers in the compactness lemma

Problem 8 (Arbitrary metric parameter spaces; Minor).  Lemma 7.3 (pp. 52–53) assumes an arbitrary metric space \(\Gamma\), yet asserts one subsequence converging on every compact subset of \(\Gamma\times\mathcal P_p(E)\).

Let \(\Gamma=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) carry the discrete metric, \(E=\mathbb R\), and \(h_N(\gamma,v)=\gamma_N\). These functions are symmetric in \(v\), bounded by one, and satisfy (7.4) with concave moduli \(\omega(r)=r\). For any proposed subsequence \(N_k\), choose a single \(\gamma\) with \(\gamma_{N_k}=k\bmod2\). The sets \(H=\{\gamma\}\) and \(K=\{\delta_0\}\) are compact, but \(h_{N_k}(\gamma,0)\) alternates, disproving the conclusion.

Separability of \(\Gamma\) is sufficient: \(\mathcal P_p(E)\) is separable, and a dense-set diagonal subsequence plus equicontinuity gives uniform convergence on every compact set. The actual application on p. 54 has \(\Gamma=[0,T]\times[0,1]\times\mathbb R^d\), which satisfies the added condition. This repairs the general statement without refuting the application’s compactness conclusion.
