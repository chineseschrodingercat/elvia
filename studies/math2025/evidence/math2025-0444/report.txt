Abstract: The population optimum and the common-kernel compactness argument withstand direct checks under their stated radial hypotheses. Three central statistical derivations contain invalid steps: the brackets used for the uniform law are not pointwise brackets, the finite-class complexity estimate uses an insufficient empirical radius, and the convex-body entropy bound has the wrong order. Explicit counterexamples identify these failures. The uniform law has a verified repair; the full generalization theorems are not refuted by the intermediate counterexamples. Five local qualifications concern likelihood normalization, feasible classes, radial assumptions, Gaussian scaling, and the KL interpretation. The supported overall impact is Major for the acquired preprint; journal-version equivalence and independent acceptance remain unestablished.

# Source, scope and valid main argument

The reviewed work is by Oscar Leong, Eliza O’Reilly, Yong Sheng Soh and Venkat Chandrasekaran, associated with DOI 10.1007/s10208-025-09693-y (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-025-09693-y). The acquired source is arXiv:2212.13597v4 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13597v4), revised 10 September 2024. All locators refer to that PDF, whose printed and PDF page numbers agree. These findings are not asserted to occur in an uninspected journal-final version.

The review checks the population reduction and Theorems 3–4, continuity and compactness in Section 4, the consistency and noise arguments in Section 5, the principal statistical bounds in Section 6, and Proposition 7. The parameterized applications in Sections 6.3–6.5 were read and checked for their dependence on the general bound; this is not a line-by-line certification of all imported estimates. No author code or numerical experiment was executed. The work is theoretical, and lack of code is not a defect.

Write \(g_K(x)=\|x\|_K\) for the gauge of a star body and \(\rho_K\) for its radial function. Layer-cake integration gives \[Z_K=\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{-g_K(x)}\,dx=\Gamma(d+1)\operatorname{vol}(K).\] For \(A=\mathbb Eg_K(X)\in(0,\infty)\), scaling \(K\) by \(c>0\) gives the criterion \(A/c+d\log c+\log\operatorname{vol}(K)\), minimized at \(c=A/d\). Thus unit-volume shape optimization is justified, with this scale subsequently reconstructed.

For the more general exponent \(\alpha>0\), let \[\rho_L(u)^{d+\alpha}=\int_0^\infty t^{d+\alpha-1}p(tu)\,dt .\] Under the explicit positivity and continuity assumptions of Theorems 3–4 (pp. 9–13), \(L\) is a star body and the relevant moment is finite. Polar coordinates and Hölder’s inequality give \[F_\alpha(K)=\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\rho_K^{-\alpha}\rho_L^{d+\alpha}
 \ge d\,\operatorname{vol}(L)^{(d+\alpha)/d}\operatorname{vol}(K)^{-\alpha/d}.\] Equality requires proportional radial functions; continuity promotes almost-everywhere equality to equality everywhere. Hence the unique unit-volume optimum is \(\operatorname{vol}(L)^{-1/d}L\). This verifies the principal formula, including its hypotheses and equality case.

The common-kernel assumption \(rB\subset\ker K\) makes each gauge \(1/r\)-Lipschitz. On a uniformly bounded class, the gauges on the sphere are bounded above and away from zero. Arzelà–Ascoli, followed by inversion, gives radial compactness; the segment definition of the kernel and the volume integral pass to the limit. This supports Section 4 on nonempty feasible classes. Neither a missing higher-moment assumption in Theorem 4 nor the paper’s looser cone-radius bound furnishes a counterexample to these conclusions.

# Problems in the principal statistical derivations

## P1. Theorem 11: expectation bounds do not give pointwise brackets (Major)

On p. 20 the proof takes an \(\eta\)-net in radial distance and uses \[\underline g=(g_{K_0}-CM_P\eta)_+,\qquad
\overline g=g_{K_0}+CM_P\eta,\qquad C=r^{-2},\quad M_P=\mathbb E\|X\|_2,\] as pointwise brackets for nearby gauges. Corollary 2 controls their expectations, not their pointwise difference.

An explicit admissible example is \(d=1\), \(r=1/4\), \(P=\mathrm{Exp}(1)\), \[K_0=[-1/2,1/2],\quad K=[-51/100,49/100],\quad
\eta=1/100,\quad \varepsilon=8/25.\] Both intervals have volume one and contain the required kernel ball; \(\delta(K,K_0)=\eta\), \(M_P=1\), and \(\eta=\varepsilon/(2CM_P)\). At \(x=4\), \[g_K(4)=400/49,\qquad \overline g(4)=204/25,\qquad
g_K(4)-\overline g(4)=4/1225>0.\] The inequality fails on the whole set \(x>98/25\), of positive \(P\)-probability. Thus the invocation of the bracketing strong law is not justified by the brackets written in the proof.

There is a verified repair. The radial lower bound gives, for every \(x\), \[|g_K(x)-g_L(x)|\le r^{-2}\delta(K,L)\|x\|_2.\] Use instead \((g_L-C\eta\|x\|_2)_+\) and \(g_L+C\eta\|x\|_2\). These are measurable, integrable pointwise brackets of \(L^1(P)\) width at most \(2C\eta M_P\). Finite nets and \(\eta<\varepsilon/(2CM_P)\) supply the strong law; when \(M_P=0\), every loss vanishes almost surely. A countable sequence of finite nets gives a common probability-one event, and continuity in \(K\) supplies separability and measurable suprema. Uniform convergence, compactness and continuity then restore the minimizer-subsequence conclusion of Theorem 8 on a nonempty feasible class.

The invalid step is central to the stated consistency derivation, hence its Major impact. The counterexample refutes the original brackets, not the uniform law or the repaired consistency theorem.

## P2. Theorem 14: the empirical \(L^1\) radius is insufficient (Major)

The deterministic complexity bound on p. 25 uses \[c_{P,m}=\sup_{K\in\mathcal C}\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m g_K(x_i)\] as the radius multiplying \(\sqrt{2\log N/m}\). This is not a valid finite-class bound in general.

Take \(d=m=16\), the sample \(x_i=e_i\), and \(\tau=10^{-3}\). For \(j=1,\ldots,16\) let \(K_j\) be the ellipsoid with gauge \[g_j(x)=\left(x_j^2+\tau^2\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^2\right)^{1/2}.\] All \(K_j\) contain the Euclidean unit ball; this finite class meets Theorem 14’s inner-radius and finite-cover assumptions. Its gauge map is Lipschitz in, for example, the discrete metric. The sample has norm one and can occur under a distribution supported on the basis vectors. The theorem does not impose a unit-volume restriction on this class.

At the sample points, \(g_j(e_i)=1\) if \(i=j\) and \(\tau\) otherwise. With precisely the paper’s \(1/m\) convention, \[\mathcal R(\{g_j\};X_m)
=\frac{1-\tau}{16}\mathbb E\max_j\sigma_j
=\frac{(1-\tau)(1-2^{-15})}{16}>0.0624.\] Meanwhile \(c_{P,m}=(1+15\tau)/16\) and the covering number is \(16\) for sufficiently small radius. The claimed bound, in the limit \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), is \[\frac{1+15\tau}{16}\sqrt{\frac{2\log16}{16}}<0.0374.\] This contradiction concerns an exact deterministic step, without an asymptotic or simulation assumption.

The usual finite-net argument works with the empirical \(L^2\) radius, or the uniform envelope \(1/r_{\mathcal C}\). Indeed the moment-generating-function argument bounds the maximum of the finite Rademacher sums by that \(L^2\) radius times \(\sqrt{2\log N/m}\); approximation contributes at most \(\varepsilon\). This provides a corrected route with changed constants. The example alone does not disprove the final high-probability inequality, which also has a concentration term. The proof of the advertised data-dependent bound, and applications using that precise constant, require correction.

## P3. Section 6.2: the convex-body entropy estimate has the wrong order (Major)

The proof of Corollary 5 on pp. 26–27 asserts a logarithmic-in-\(1/\varepsilon\) bound for the metric entropy of all convex bodies contained in \(R_rB\). This is already impossible in dimension two.

For \(n\ge6\), let \(u_1,\ldots,u_n\) be equally spaced on the unit circle and define \[K_S=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((1/2)B^2\cup\{u_j:j\in S\}\bigr),
 \qquad S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}.\] If \(j\in S\setminus T\), the support function of \(K_S\) in direction \(u_j\) is \(1\), whereas that of \(K_T\) is at most \(\cos(2\pi/n)\). Therefore these \(2^n\) bodies are pairwise separated in Hausdorff distance by \[h_n=1-\cos(2\pi/n)\asymp n^{-2}.\] At radius \(h_n/3\), every covering has at least \(2^n\) members. Its logarithm is at least \(n\log2\), while the paper’s displayed upper bound is only \(O(\log n)\). All bodies contain an interior ball and lie inside \(B^2\); choose, for example, \(r=1/4\), for which the outer class in the proof contains this family. Rescaling gives the same contradiction for any fixed positive outer radius.

This refutes the actual ambient entropy assertion used by the proof. The packing is not asserted to consist of unit-volume bodies, and is not presented as a direct counterexample to the final risk statement for the constrained subclass.

The same derivation also needs the correct union identity \(\rho_{\cup_iK_i}=\max_i\rho_{K_i}\), rather than the printed minimum. Reciprocals cannot be dropped in the following displayed equality. Correctly, \[\delta\!\left(\bigcup_iK_i,\bigcup_iL_i\right)
 \le\max_i\delta(K_i,L_i)
 \le (R_r/r)\max_i d_H(K_i,L_i)\] when the component bodies contain \(rB\) and lie in \(R_rB\). Intersecting arbitrary ambient cover centres with a constrained class need not retain a cover; centres can instead be chosen from occupied balls with a radius factor of two. These local corrections do not repair the false entropy order. Consequently the claimed rate does not follow from the supplied proof; a corrected quantitative rate is not certified here.

# Local claims requiring correction

## P4. Proposition 7 needs a likelihood bound or normalization (Minor)

On p. 31, the proposition assumes a real-valued potential and finite normalizers, and claims \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu_{P_1},\mu_{P_2})^2\le2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_1\Vert P_2)/Z_{P_1}^2\). Take \[P_1=\delta_0,\qquad P_2=(\delta_0+\delta_1)/2,\qquad \Phi\equiv-\log4.\] Then \(P_1\ll P_2\), \(Z_{P_1}=Z_{P_2}=4\), and each posterior equals its prior. The left side is \(1/4\) whereas the right side is \((\log2)/8<1/4\). All stated hypotheses hold.

A sufficient repair is \(0<e^{-\Phi}\le1\). More generally, an upper bound \(e^{-\Phi}\le M\) gives a factor \(2M^2/Z_{P_1}^2\): the normalized-measure estimate is \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu_1,\mu_2)\le(2M/Z_1)d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_1,P_2)\), followed by Pinsker’s inequality. This is invariant under rescaling the likelihood. The Gaussian potential displayed earlier is nonnegative, so this counterexample does not invalidate that example. The correction concerns the proposition’s general scope and its downstream interpretation.

## P5. The Gaussian example has an infeasible normalization (Minor)

The explicit formula on p. 12 uses \(K_*=\kappa_d^{1/d}\det(\Sigma)^{-1/(2d)}E_\Sigma\), where \(\operatorname{vol}(E_\Sigma)=\kappa_d\sqrt{\det\Sigma}\). Its volume is \(\kappa_d^2\), rather than one. For \(d=2\) and \(\Sigma=I\) it gives \(\sqrt\pi B^2\) of area \(\pi^2\), despite a smooth positive Gaussian satisfying Theorem 3. The correct factor is \(\kappa_d^{-1/d}\). The preceding general formula and the optimal ellipsoidal shape are sound; the error is the displayed candidate’s feasibility.

## P6. Existence requires a nonempty constraint class (Minor)

Theorem 7 (p. 17) asserts existence over \(\mathcal S^d(r,1)\) for arbitrary \(r>0\). Since every candidate contains \(rB\), \[1=\operatorname{vol}(K)\ge\kappa_dr^d .\] Thus the class is empty for \(r>\kappa_d^{-1/d}\). The necessary and sufficient feasible range is \(0<r\le\kappa_d^{-1/d}\), witnessed by the unit-volume ball. The general closed, bounded class must also be nonempty. On this corrected domain the compactness and continuity arguments establish existence; this does not refute the convergence results for feasible minimizer sequences.

## P7. The asserted sufficient radial conditions are too weak (Minor)

The paragraph before Theorem 3 (p. 9) says that a continuous density with finite second moments satisfies the theorem’s radial conditions. A smooth probability density supported in \(B(3e_1,1)\) has every finite moment but satisfies \(p(tu)=0\) for all \(t\ge0\) when \(u_1\le0\). Its radial moment vanishes in those directions. Theorem 3 itself explicitly assumes positivity and continuity of that moment and survives; the preceding sufficient-condition assertion must be restricted.

## P8. Absolute continuity alone does not justify the KL interpretation (Minor)

The equivalences on p. 4 require finiteness beyond \(P\ll p_K\). A fully explicit obstruction can satisfy the main theorem’s radial condition. Choose an even smooth probability density \(\phi\) supported in \((-1/4,1/4)\) and set \[a_n=2^{-n-1},\quad w_n=a_ne^{-4^n},\qquad
p(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_n}{w_n}
 \left[\phi\!\left(\frac{x-n}{w_n}\right)+
       \phi\!\left(\frac{x+n}{w_n}\right)\right].\] The supports are disjoint and locally finite. This is a smooth probability density with finite second moment; each one-sided first moment equals \(\sum n a_n=1\). Its radial moment is positive on \(\mathbb S^0\). However, \[\int p\log p=2\sum_{n\ge1}a_n4^n+\int\phi\log\phi=+\infty ,\] with finite negative part. For every unit-length star interval \([-a,b]\), \(a+b=1\), the gauge objective is finite and equals \(1/a+1/b\), uniquely minimized at \(a=b=1/2\). Every associated Gibbs density is positive, so absolute continuity holds, but all KL divergences are infinite. The KL criterion therefore does not select the unique cross-entropy optimizer. A finite-entropy or suitable finite-KL condition repairs this interpretation; the principal cross-entropy result is unaffected.

# Coverage, impact and limits

The conclusions distinguish three failed central proof steps from five local scope or formula corrections. P1 has a complete pointwise-bracketing repair. P2 has a verified finite-net repair with a different radius; the full published probability bound has not been disproved. P3 requires a substantive rederivation of the entropy-dependent rate. These support an overall Major assessment for the acquired version.

The noise-continuity argument was checked through the common-kernel gauge Lipschitz estimate and finite-moment coupling; no defect was established there. The finite-dimensional examples require the corrected general complexity argument. No universal optimality for downstream reconstruction tasks is claimed: the paper itself identifies such performance as further work.
