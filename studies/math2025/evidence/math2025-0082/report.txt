# Claim, assumptions and dependency structure

Theorem 1.1, page 2, assumes GRH. For every \(\eta>0\), it chooses constants \(C,K>1\) such that large averaged linear-phase correlation \[\int_X^{2X}\sup_\alpha
 \left|\sum_{x\le n\le x+H}g(n)e(\alpha n)\right|\,dx
 \ge\eta HX,\qquad H\ge(\log X)^K ,\] for a multiplicative function \(g\) with \(|g|\le1\), implies the stated bounded pretentious distance \(\mathbb D(g;CX^2/H^2,C)\le C\). The distance uses the prime cutoff, height bound and character-modulus range explicitly defined on that page. The dependence \(K=K(\eta)\) must not be replaced by one fixed universal exponent.

Lemma 2.2 reduces the problem to an \(H\)-separated configuration of phases, with primes in \([P,2P]\) and \(H^{\varepsilon^2}\le P\le H^\varepsilon\). The target inverse statement is Theorem 2.4, page 10: on a positive proportion of the configuration, \[\left\|\alpha_x-\frac{a_x}{b}-\frac{T}{x}\right\|
 \ll_\eta H^{-1},\qquad b=O_\eta(1),\quad
 |T|\ll_\eta X^2/H^2 .\] Here \(\|\cdot\|\) is distance in \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\); real absolute values cannot be substituted before a non-wrapping argument.

The proof passes through mixing in Section 2, parallel rigidity in Section 3, fixed-prime lifting in Section 4, concentration increments in Section 5, downward expansion in Section 6, a global frequency in Section 7, and sharpening in Section 8. The ratio \(Y/\widetilde H=X/H\) is preserved by simultaneous scale changes. The constants \(C_2<C[0]<C[1]<\overline C[1]<\cdots\) are chosen before the large logarithmic exponent \(K\).

The nilsequence extension and associated Liouville sign-pattern consequence in the introduction are expectations conditional on extending the method. They are not proved results of this manuscript. The passage from bounds for each fixed \(\eta\) to the stated slowly-growing \(\psi(X)\) formulation is consistent: eventually \(\psi(X)\ge K(\eta)\) for every fixed \(\eta>0\).

# Checks of the central mechanisms

## Mixing and the GRH dependency

Lemma 2.7 is explicitly conditional on GRH. Lemma 2.8, pages 11–12, uses its Dirichlet-character estimate in a Fourier integral counting paths. The congruence condition excludes primes dividing the modulus. The principal-character term carries \(1/\varphi(Q)\); the nonprincipal contribution is bounded using Cauchy–Schwarz and the large sieve. Polynomial factors in the path length are absorbed into the displayed exponential constant \(C^k\). The restrictions \(\log Q\le\log Y\le P^\epsilon\) and \(k\le\epsilon'\log(Y/\widetilde H)\) were retained.

The \(Q=k=1\) specialization in Corollary 2.9 provides the concentration upper bound; general Dirichlet moduli are used in Lemma 7.3 to force a bounded rational denominator. This distinguishes the RH-dependent archimedean step from the full GRH-dependent modular step. The underlying explicit-formula estimate and the cited large-sieve theorem were treated as external inputs, not independently re-proved.

## Rational rigidity

For Lemma 3.3, page 15, the hypotheses yield \[q_1\alpha_1-q_2\alpha_2
   =\frac{\ell_1}{p_1}+O(\epsilon)
   =\frac{\ell_2}{p_2}+O(\epsilon)\pmod1 .\] Since distinct primes \(p_1,p_2\in[P,2P]\) are coprime, a nonzero difference of these fractions modulo one is at least \(1/(p_1p_2)\ge1/(4P^2)\). The condition \(\epsilon<c/P^2\), with a sufficiently small constant accounting for the triangle inequalities, forces equality of the rational residues. Their common residue is integral, proving the claimed \(O(\epsilon)\) bound. Distinctness of the primes is essential.

The same denominator calculation was followed in Lemmas 3.5–3.7. For the two products of distinct primes in Lemma 3.6, the rational spacing is of order \(P^{-4}\), whereas the error is \(O(\epsilon/P)\); thus the stated \(\epsilon<c/P^3\) is the relevant scale. These checks use exact integer denominators rather than numerical proximity.

## Fixed-prime lifting without worsening the edge constant

At the end of the proof of Proposition 4.1, page 22, set \[d=q_1\alpha_x^\uparrow-q_2\alpha_y^\uparrow\pmod1 .\] The rigidity argument gives a representative \(|d|\ll1/\widetilde H\), and the exact lift relation gives \[\|p^\ast d\|<C_2P/\widetilde H .\] Because \(p^\ast\asymp P\) and \(\widetilde H\) is much larger than \(P\) in the operative parameter regime, \(|p^\ast d|<1/2\). Consequently the second bound is a real bound, and division yields \[|d|<\frac{C_2P}{p^\ast\widetilde H}.\] This explicitly validates why the same \(C_2\), rather than a successively larger constant, survives the lifting. The selection of the prime and the exceptional-edge counts in Section 4 were followed through the nested cluster construction; all numerical hidden constants were not extracted.

## Concentration and cumulative error

For Lemma 5.6, pages 24–26, write \(m_j=|\mathcal A_{(j)}|\) and \(C_j=\mathcal C(\mathcal A_{(j)})\). The recurrence has the form \[C_j\ge C_{j-1}\sqrt{\frac{m_{j-1}}{m_j}}-E,
 \qquad E=O((\log Y)^{-B/2}).\] Multiplying by \(\sqrt{m_j}\), summing and using \(m_j\le m_0\) gives \[C_j\ge C_0\sqrt{\frac{m_0}{m_j}}
          -jE\sqrt{\frac{m_0}{m_j}}.\] This is the mass-weighted error in the manuscript, not an unjustified loss independent of the retained mass. With \(j=O(\log Y)\) and \(B>2\) fixed sufficiently large, \(jE=o(1)\). The mixing upper bound on \(C_j\), together with \(C_0>0\), then enforces a uniform positive lower bound on \(m_j/m_0\). The proper-subset argument terminates because the configurations are finite. The bounded number of stronger expansion stages in Proposition 5.8 is kept separate from the many nearly lossless stages.

## Downward paths and the final frequency

Lemmas 6.2–6.4 were checked for product comparability and accumulated position/phase error. Writing \(p^\ast_{j-i}/q_i=(\widetilde x_{i+1}/\widetilde x_i)(1+O(H/X))\) shows why the product is bounded along the stated short paths. Different integer prime products differ by at least one; combined with the position error this gives the collision threshold in Lemma 6.4. In Lemma 6.5, removing prime divisors of \(Q\le X\) costs at most \(\log X/\log P\) primes, negligible relative to the stated degree when \(K\) is sufficiently large.

The rational-approximation and residue-class split in Lemmas 7.2–7.3 were followed, retaining the coprimality of numerators with \(b\). The error is made smaller than the rational grid spacing before congruence is concluded. In the fixed block \(i_1<j<i_2\) used there, the strong expansion part of Proposition 5.8 and Lemma 6.5 provides the needed positive-density reachable sets.

Proposition 7.4 initially retains logarithmic losses. In Section 8, the displacement \(|x-\widetilde x|\ll H\log^2X\), the bounded denominator and the smallness of \((P/H)\log^3X\) justify the non-wrapping transition from (8.1) to (8.2). The final comparison \(|T_x-T_y|\ll X/H\), followed by a common interval of \(T_x\)-values and mixing, was checked as the route to \(H^{-1}\) accuracy.

# Limits, counterchecks and conclusion

The full author TeX was read statically to line 942. This is not an execution of author code or a reconstruction of the author’s build. Original PDF pages inspected were 1–2, 10–13, 15–17 and 21–34. The original images checked the cited formulas and statement numbering against the supplied source.

No contradiction was established by the parameter or boundary checks. The review did not independently certify the stronger assertion in Lemma 7.1 that the coprime reachable-set bound holds at every lower level. Its last sentence invokes projection of whole configurations; translating that projection to paths constrained to be coprime to an arbitrary \(Q\) requires additional care when a lifting prime divides \(Q\). The bounded strong-expansion block used in Lemma 7.3 can be handled directly with Lemma 6.5. No counterexample satisfying the full arithmetic hypotheses was constructed, so this remaining scope question is not promoted to a confirmed defect.

The external reductions from multiplicative-function correlations to configurations and back to pretentiousness were identified but not re-proved from their original papers. Uniform numerical values of all hidden constants and the full range reduction for already-known large intervals were not independently reconstructed. A focused version/correction search found no identified correction to this specific v1; it was not exhaustive. No journal-final comparison was possible from the acquired materials.
