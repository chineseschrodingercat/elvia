# Definitions, hypotheses, and the bend foliation

The object on p. 2 is a smooth arc-length-preserving embedding of the flat quotient \[M_\lambda=([0,\lambda]\times[0,1])/
       \bigl((0,y)\sim(\lambda,1-y)\bigr)\] into \(\mathbb R^3\). Arc-length preservation gives a positive-definite induced metric and hence an immersion; injectivity is separately required. The distinction matters: the proof needs disjoint bends, and the strict bound is not asserted for arbitrary immersed or sharply folded objects. The triangular folded model is a limit outside the smooth embedded class. It is consequently consistent to have the strict inequality \(\lambda>\sqrt3\) and infimum \(\sqrt3\).

The central dependency is \[\text{bend foliation}\ \longrightarrow\
 \text{embedded T-pattern}\ \longrightarrow\
 \text{trapezoid bound and equality analysis}.\] The nonzero-curvature part of the foliation in Lemma 2.1, p. 6, has the following local differential-geometric check. Choose an orthonormal principal frame \(e_1,e_2\) with shape operator \(Ae_1=0,\ Ae_2=\kappa e_2,\ \kappa\ne0\). Write \(\nabla_{e_1}e_1=a e_2\) and \(\nabla_{e_2}e_1=b e_2\). Codazzi gives \(a=0\) and \(e_1\kappa=-b\kappa\). Flatness gives \(e_1b=-b^2\). Along a unit-speed \(e_1\) curve, \[b(s)=\frac{b(0)}{1+s b(0)},\qquad
 \kappa(s)=\frac{\kappa(0)}{1+s b(0)}.
 \tag{R1}\] Its ambient acceleration is zero because both its intrinsic acceleration and its normal curvature vanish. It is a straight ruling. Formula (R1) prevents its nonzero curvature from becoming zero at a finite regular interior endpoint, while a pole would contradict smoothness. This supports continuation to the boundary, the key fact used by the cited developability results.

Distinct such rulings cannot cross at a nonflat point: two independent null directions would force the shape operator to vanish. After their closure is taken, the flat complementary strips can be filled by straight segments in the developed flat domain. If their side equations are \(x=u(y)\) and \(x=v(y)\), where \(u,v\) are affine and \(u(y)<v(y)\) for \(0\le y\le1\), the interpolation \[x_t(y)=(1-t)u(y)+t v(y),\qquad 0\le t\le1,\] is affine in \(y\), stays in the strip and has disjoint leaves. This supplies the elementary interpolation needed in Proposition 2.2. The treatment of limiting flat components still uses the global developability/foliation framework cited in that proposition; no claim is made to have re-proved every version of the external regularity theorems.

There is a local editorial clarification at p. 6, `2proof.tex` lines 81–84: for nonparallel sides the interpolated supporting lines are concurrent, meeting at the common intersection of the extended sides. The word coincident would mean identical lines and should be replaced. The explicit interpolation above verifies the intended construction; no scientific obstruction follows from this wording.

# Independent check of the T-pattern argument

The bend parameter is a circle. The centerline-intersection check on p. 7 is sufficient for this parametrization: a pre-bend long enough to wind past a fundamental strip would have length at least \(\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}>\lambda\), whereas its two endpoints split the boundary loop of total length \(2\lambda\) into two arcs, each at least as long as their straight spatial chord. This is impossible. A transverse pre-bend in a single developed strip crosses the centerline once.

For ordered distinct bends, the configuration space is a cylinder. Collapsing its two ends gives a sphere. The explicit coordinates on p. 12, \[(\theta,\phi)\longmapsto
       (x_0,x_1)=(\theta-\phi,\theta+\phi),
 \quad 0<\phi<\pi,\quad \theta\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z),\] are bijective, continuous with continuous inverse, and extend across the collapsed ends. Swapping the ordered entries is precisely \((\theta,\phi)\mapsto(\theta+\pi,\pi-\phi)\), the antipodal map. This identity was also checked independently at twenty sample pairs, including pairs near both ends.

Transporting a ruling orientation once around a Moebius band reverses it. Thus the two functions in (2), p. 8, \[g=u_0\cdot u_1,\qquad
 h=(m_0-m_1)\cdot(u_0\times u_1),\] are well-defined under simultaneous reversal of both chosen directions. Reversing the order of the bends gives the transported pair \((u_1,-u_0)\), so both \(g\) and \(h\) change sign. Continuity at the two added points gives respectively \((1,0)\) and \((-1,0)\); compactness bounds the midpoint difference, while the cross product tends to zero.

The odd-map form of Borsuk–Ulam therefore supplies a common zero away from the two added points. The author’s alternative winding argument was also checked: every path between the poles would have the same half-integer winding, but the antipodal transformation followed by reversal changes its sign, an impossibility. At a zero, the directions are perpendicular and the scalar triple product vanishes. The supporting lines are coplanar and intersect. The segments remain disjoint because they belong to the fixed foliation. The argument proves exactly the embedded T-pattern required by Lemma G; it does not require that the intersection of the extended lines lie on both segments.

# The trapezoid bound, strictness, and global optimization

Figure 2 and (3), p. 9, were checked together. Cutting along one pre-bend gives the symmetric trapezoid. The two remaining boundary paths satisfy \[H+D=2\lambda,\qquad D-H=2t,\] where \(t\) is a signed horizontal displacement. Neither step requires \(t\ge0\). One of the two disjoint perpendicular bends can be chosen entirely on one open ray of its supporting line away from the line intersection. This makes the comparison triangle’s height strictly greater than the length of that bend, and hence strictly greater than or equal to \(1\).

For a triangle of base length \(B=\sqrt{1+t^2}\) and height \(h\ge1\), reflection of one base endpoint across the line through the apex gives \[\text{sum of the other two sides}\ \ge\
       \sqrt{B^2+4h^2}\ \ge\sqrt{5+t^2}.
 \tag{R2}\] The first equality requires the apex to lie above the midpoint; the second requires \(h=1\). The unrestricted horizontal coordinate of the apex is allowed in this proof. In the embedded T-pattern situation the height inequality is strict, so the strict inequality needed in (3) is justified.

Independently define \[\delta(t)=\sqrt{1+t^2}+\sqrt{5+t^2},\qquad
 \nu(t)=2\sqrt{5+t^2}-2t.\] The two path estimates yield \(2\lambda>\max(\delta(t),\nu(t))\). At \(t_0=1/\sqrt3\), both functions equal \(2\sqrt3\). Moreover \[\delta'(t)=\frac{t}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{5+t^2}}>0
       \quad(t>0),\qquad
 \nu'(t)=\frac{2t}{\sqrt{5+t^2}}-2<0
       \quad(t\in\mathbb R).\] For \(t>t_0\) the first branch exceeds \(2\sqrt3\); for \(t<t_0\) the second branch does. Thus the minimax has its unique minimum \(2\sqrt3\) at \(t_0\), and the strict geometric inequality proves \(\lambda>\sqrt3\). In particular negative displacements are covered. The independently executed 4001-point grid and ninety triangle checks are numerical corroboration; the derivative and reflection arguments, not the grid, prove the universal statement.

# The triangular limit and uniform convergence of maps

For \(\lambda_n\to\sqrt3\), the minimax is squeezed between \(2\sqrt3\) and \(2\lambda_n\). Its coercivity and unique minimizer therefore give \(t_n\to1/\sqrt3\), rather than just convergence along one unspecified subsequence. The base lengths tend to \(2/\sqrt3\). The perimeter bound and the equality case in (R2) force the comparison height to tend to \(1\) and its apex to the midpoint position, giving the equilateral limiting triangle after rigid motions.

The other bend has length \(\sqrt{1+b_n^2}\le h_n\), hence \(b_n\to0\). Its gap from the horizontal supporting line also tends to zero. This supplies the midpoint assertion used at the beginning of p. 11. The boundary length excess vanishes, so the four boundary arcs converge to the appropriate straight segments, with lengths \(1/\sqrt3,1/\sqrt3,2/\sqrt3,2/\sqrt3\). For a curve of length \(L\) with endpoint distance \(d\), every point lies within distance at most \(\sqrt{L^2-d^2}/2\) of the endpoint segment: it lies inside the prolate ellipsoid defined by the two endpoint distances. This supplies a quantitative check on the disappearance of bending when \(L-d\to0\). Arc-length parametrization also rules out uncontrolled backtracking in the limit.

The final passage from triangle boundaries to interiors can be proved without assuming that an arbitrary bent surface is planar. Let \(p=\sum_i\alpha_i v_i\) with \(\alpha_i\ge0\), \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\), in one of the three limiting triangles, and let \(A\) be the affine isometry fixed by its three vertices. If \(J\) is a 1-Lipschitz limiting map fixing these vertex images, then \[\begin{aligned}
 \sum_i\alpha_i|J(p)-A(v_i)|^2
 &=|J(p)-A(p)|^2+
       \sum_i\alpha_i|A(p)-A(v_i)|^2\notag\\
 &\le \sum_i\alpha_i|p-v_i|^2.
 \tag{R3}\end{aligned}\] The variance sums on the two sides coincide, so \(J(p)=A(p)\). The approximate version gives uniform control as the vertex errors vanish. This identity was verified with exact rational arithmetic for 66 choices of barycentric weights. Alternatively, common Lipschitz bounds give compactness and every subsequential limit is forced by (R3), establishing convergence of the full normalized sequence.

The changing domains are handled by the converging cut trapezoids and their three corresponding triangles; uniform convergence is understood using these natural piecewise affine domain identifications. Rigid motions normalize the images. The proof does not assert convergence of derivatives or curvature, and it permits the folded limiting map to cease being an embedding. The additional foliation discussion on p. 11 was checked separately and is not used to prove either main theorem.
