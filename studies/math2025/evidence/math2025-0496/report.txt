Abstract: This review concerns the 13-page arXiv version 2307.01162v2, dated 13 October 2023, by Barbara Dembin, Dor Elboim and Ron Peled. The perturbation and translation argument, quantitative influence bound, moment exponents and transversal estimate were checked. Two local errors in the written proof are identified and repaired: a claimed identity for exterior modified weights and an overlapping critical case in a geometric sum. Both are Minor; no false main theorem is demonstrated.

# Scope and assumptions

The exact acquired source is [paper]. All 13 pages were read, including the bibliography on pp. 12–13; all source pages were inspected as images. The official arXiv history still lists v2 as the latest version. The later journal article is Annals of Probability 53(2), 544–556 (2025), DOI 10.1214/24-AOP1715. Equal page counts do not establish byte or content equivalence, and the review makes no such assertion. A bounded correction and supplement search located no distinct correction; this is not a proof that none exists.

The operative setting is \(d\geq2\), IID absolutely continuous weights on \([a,b]\), \(0<a<b<\infty\), and a strictly positive uniform density lower bound. The sentence before (1.2) uses \(\beta\) where the display uses \(\alpha\); the intended positive lower-bound parameter is retained here. Write \(n=\|v\|_2\), \(p_e=\mathbb P(e\in\gamma(0,v))\). Positivity, boundedness and atomlessness have distinct roles: they give deterministic length control, bounded connecting-path costs, and almost sure uniqueness, respectively.

The paper proves a bound on the number of influential edges. It does not resolve the location of all exceptional edges or the BKS midpoint problem in \(d\geq3\). Figure 1 and the exponent discussion on pp. 3–5 are explicitly heuristic and were not treated as additional theorems.

# Problem 1: exterior weights are not unchanged as defined (Minor)

## Source and counterexample

The issue occurs in the proof of Lemma 4.3, p. 8, immediately after \(t_e^+=g_{\tau_e}^+(t_e)\). The text asserts that \(t_e^+=t_e\) outside \([-n^2,n^2]^d\), citing localization of the geodesic. However Proposition 4.1, p. 7, permits globally positive, summable smooth weights \(q\), with \(\tau_e=q_e/\|q\|_2\). The exponential envelope subsequently used on p. 10 has exactly that property.

For a concrete admissible example take \(G\) uniform on \([1,2]\) and \[q_e=\exp\{-\|c(e)-c(e_0)\|_2/\log n\},\qquad
 g_\tau^+(w)=1+\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}(w-1)+\tau\right)
 \quad(1<w<2),\] with endpoints fixed. Here \(c(e)\) is the edge center and \(\Phi\) the standard normal distribution function. The ratio condition (4.1) holds because \(e^{-2}\leq q_e/q_{e'}\leq e^2\) at distances at most \(2\log n\). The vector \(q\) belongs to \(\ell^1\cap\ell^2\), and every \(\tau_e>0\). These maps satisfy Lemma 2.1, including its upper increment bound and lower increment on Gaussian compact sets. For every exterior edge, \(g_{\tau_e}^+(w)>w\) throughout \(1<w<2\). Thus the asserted exterior identity is false almost surely in this example. Localization of a geodesic does not set the perturbation parameters to zero.

## Complete local repair and impact

Let \(W\) be a finite edge window containing every possible geodesic from \(0\) to \(v\) for environments in \([a,b]\), and define \(s_e=\tau_e\mathbf1_W(e)\), \(t_e^+=g_{s_e}^+(t_e)\). Such a window exists because \[a|\gamma(0,v)|\leq T(0,v)\leq b\|v\|_1.\] For large \(n\) one may use the source’s box. The modified weights remain in \([a,b]\), so the same localization applies to \(\gamma_0^+\). Moreover \(\sum s_e^2\leq1\), and the finite-dimensional change-of-measure estimate in Lemma 2.1 applies directly.

On \(\gamma_0^+\), \(s_e=\tau_e\), preserving the lower estimate (4.5) and the logarithmic block argument. For a shifted geodesic it is enough that \[T_h^+\leq T_h+C_0\sum_{e\in\gamma_h}s_e
          \leq T_h+C_0 f(\gamma_h).\] The triangle inequalities (4.7)–(4.8) remain valid since all weights are still at most \(b\). In particular, \(\gamma_h\) need not be contained in \(W\). This proves the required repair of Lemma 4.3 with the same type of constants.

The finite-window interpretation is plausible intended counterevidence: the authors explicitly appeal to geodesic localization, and all later bounds need only the localized perturbation just described. The correction is nevertheless a change to the defined coupling. Its severity is Minor because the complete repair uses existing hypotheses and preserves Proposition 4.1 and its applications. No final theorem is refuted.

# Problem 2: the critical geometric-sum case overlaps (Minor)

The last display on p. 11, following (5.2), bounds \[S_{j_0}(\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^{j_0}2^{j(p-\beta)},
 \qquad p=\frac{2d}{d-1}.\] Its first case includes \(1<\beta\leq p\) and bounds the sum by a constant times \(2^{j_0(p-\beta)}\); its next case separately treats \(\beta=p\) by \(Cj_0\). At \(\beta=p\), \[S_{j_0}(p)=j_0,\qquad 2^{j_0(p-p)}=1,\] so the first claimed bound is false as \(j_0\to\infty\), even allowing the constant to depend on the fixed \(\beta\). Here \(j_0=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{d+1}\log_2n-d\log_2\log n\rfloor\) does tend to infinity.

Replace \(\beta\leq p\) in the first case by \(\beta<p\). The separate critical row already gives the required logarithm, and Corollary 1.3 on p. 3 already states the correct critical result. Thus this is an endpoint error in an intermediate display, with a one-symbol repair and no changed final bound.

# Independent checks of the main argument

## Perturbation lemma

The needed version of Lemma 2.1 can be derived under (1.2) without assuming an unverified strengthening of its cited reference. Let \(F\) be the distribution function of \(G\) and \(H=F^{-1}\circ\Phi\). Then \(H\) is increasing and Lipschitz, with constant at most \(1/(\alpha\sqrt{2\pi})\). Define \(g_\tau^+(H(z))=H(z+\tau)\). At almost every \(z\), \(H'(z)>0\). Consequently \[\inf_{0<t\leq1}\frac{H(z+t)-H(z)}t>0\] there: use the positive derivative near zero and strict monotonicity and continuity on the remaining compact interval. The level sets of this infimum give the sets \(B_\delta\) whose \(G\)-mass tends to one.

For a finite Gaussian shift, let \(\mu\) be standard Gaussian measure, \(\nu\) its translate by \(\tau\), and \(L=d\nu/d\mu=\exp(\tau\cdot x-\|\tau\|_2^2/2)\). Hölder’s inequality under \(\nu\), with conjugate exponent \(p/(p-1)\), gives \[\nu(A)\geq
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{p\|\tau\|_2^2}{2(p-1)}\right\}\mu(A)^p.\] Transporting coordinatewise by \(H\) proves the stated measure comparison. The positive derivative assertion follows from the absolute continuity and positive finite density of \(F\), together with the Lipschitz inverse; no upper uniform bound on the density is required.

## Translation, rare events and dimension

Theorem 1.1’s proof on pp. 6–7 turns a positive-probability gain of order \(\sqrt{|A_\epsilon|}\) into a lower bound of order \(|A_\epsilon|\) for translated intersections within that radius. A geodesic’s segment between its first and last visits to a ball of radius \(r\) has length at most \(C(r+1)\). Summation over translations therefore yields \[|A_\epsilon|^{1+d/2}\leq C|A_\epsilon|^{3/2},\] which bounds \(|A_\epsilon|\) when \(d\geq2\). Small radii and bounded \(n\) are absorbed into constants; finiteness of \(A_\epsilon\) follows already from deterministic length localization.

For Claim 4.2, it suffices to use simple paths, as all positive-weight geodesics are simple. Directly summing the geometric union bound gives \[(2n^2+1)^d\sum_{k\geq\lceil\log n\rceil}
                 (4d)^k e^{-10dk}\leq n^{-5}\] for sufficiently large \(n\). The displayed binomial law in the source can be read as the stochastic lower comparison supplied by \(G(B_{\delta_0})\geq1-e^{-20d}\).

The level argument (4.9) is also sound: failure at every level would give \(\mu\leq\mu/3+\mu/4<\mu\). The exponent \(3/2\) in Lemma 4.3, the ball volume, and \(r=c3^k\mu\) combine into \[\mu^d\leq C(8/3^d)^k\sum_e\tau_e,\qquad k\geq-1 .\] Since \(8/3^d<1\) for \(d\geq2\), this factor is uniformly bounded. The error \(n^{-5}\) is negligible uniformly over \(k\leq\log n\).

## Quantitative and transversal conclusions

Put \(p=2d/(d-1)\) and \(A=d(d+1)/(d-1)\). The exponential envelope has \(\sum q_e\leq C(\log n)^d|A_\epsilon|\) and \(q_e\leq1\). Proposition 4.1 consequently gives \[(\epsilon|A_\epsilon|)^d
   \leq C\big((\log n)^d|A_\epsilon|\big)^{(d+1)/2},\] recovering Theorem 1.2 exactly. Combining \(N(t)\leq\min(Cn/t,C(\log n)^At^{-p})\) at \(t_0=n^{-(d-1)/(d+1)}(\log n)^d\) recovers all three cases of Corollary 1.3. The case \(\beta=1\) follows directly from expected geodesic length, without a divergent endpoint integral. The reviewer checked these exponent identities with exact symbolic algebra.

For Theorem 1.4, a shell covering of the smoothed finite cylinder gives \(\sum q_e\leq C\ell r^{d-1}\) when \(r,\ell\geq\log n\). The resulting expected tube-edge count is \(Cr^{(d^2-1)/(2d)}\ell^{(d+1)/(2d)}\). Setting \(r=c\ell^{1/(d+1)}\) gives a linear bound in \(\ell\); the assumption \(\ell\geq(\log n)^{d+2}\) ensures \(r\geq\log n\) for sufficiently large \(n\).

One can keep endpoint bookkeeping explicit: choose \(c\) so the expected tube-edge count is at most \(\ell/4\). A path crossing the slab has at least \(\ell-1\) slab vertices, and passing between vertices and incident edges costs at most one further endpoint. Thus the expected number of exterior slab vertices is at least \(3\ell/4-2\geq\ell/2\) once \(\ell\geq8\). Increasing \(n_0\) supplies this condition. This checks the precise vertex formulation rather than identifying edge and vertex counts.

# Limits and disposition

The review checked the main theorems under (1.2). The proposed extensions to \(a=0\), more general Lipschitz images and lighter-tailed distributions in Section 1.2 were not independently established here. Historical comparisons with earlier BKS and transversal-fluctuation results were not re-proved from the cited original papers. No original external-reference PDF inspection is claimed. No missing code was treated as a defect, and no author code was run. The quantitative checks and the explicit repairs support a Minor assessment; initial completion remains distinct from independent acceptance.
