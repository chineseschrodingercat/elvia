Abstract: This report checks selected determinant, spinor-norm, and mapping-torus arguments in the acquired author manuscript. A fully realized genus-two mapping torus shows that the discriminant specified in equation (C.2) can be zero under the written hypotheses. The determinant must instead be taken on the generalized \(1\)-eigenspace, as the manuscript’s earlier formula already indicates. The correction leaves the ordinary fixed-space dimension in the torsion sign unchanged. The supported assessment is Minor, with no demonstrated refutation of the main theorems. A finite-characteristic divided-derivative concern is resolved by explicit counterevidence in the source. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# A realized counterexample to the printed discriminant

Problem 1 (Ordinary invariants in equation (C.2)).  On p. 87, Lemma C.2.1 assumes \(H^0(S,\rho)=0\) and characteristic zero. Writing \(T\) for the induced action on \(H^1(S,\rho)\) and \(h=\dim\ker(T-I)\), it identifies \[(-1)^{h/2}\operatorname{RT}(M_t,\rho)=\operatorname{spinor}(T).\] The following equation (C.2) replaces the right side by \[\det{}'(1-T)\,\delta.\] Here \(\det{}'\) removes the generalized \(1\)-eigenspace, whereas the next line defines \(\delta\) on the ordinary fixed space \(H^1(S,\rho)^{T=1}\). That latter restriction can be degenerate.

## All hypotheses are realized

Let \(S\) be the closed oriented genus-two surface with presentation \[\pi_1(S)=\langle a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2\mid[a_1,b_1][a_2,b_2]=1\rangle.\] Over \(\mathbb Q\), take a rank-two local system with fiber form \[J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \rho(a_1)=-I_2,\quad \rho(b_1)=\rho(a_2)=\rho(b_2)=I_2.\] The matrices are symplectic and satisfy the surface relation. Since \(-I_2\) fixes no nonzero vector, \(H^0(S,\rho)=0\); duality gives \(H^2(S,\rho)=0\).

Choose \(t\) fixing \(a_1,b_1,a_2\) and sending \(b_2\) to \(b_2a_2\). This is the inverse of the explicitly displayed Dehn twist \(T_4\) on p. 86; it is an orientation-preserving mapping class. The identity \([a_2,b_2a_2]=[a_2,b_2]\) also verifies preservation of the relator. We have \(\rho\circ t=\rho\), so equation (C.1) holds with intertwining matrix \(M=I_2\). The representation therefore extends to the mapping torus \(M_t\).

Write the values of a cocycle on the four generators as \((x_1,y_1,u,v)\). The cocycle relation gives \(-2y_1=0\), and the coboundaries are \((-2w,0,0,0)\). Consequently \[H^1(S,\rho)\simeq\mathbb Q^2\oplus\mathbb Q^2,\qquad [\phi]\longleftrightarrow(u,v).\] This agrees with (C.5) on p. 88. The actual cocycle action on p. 89 is \(T(\phi)=M^{-1}\phi\circ t\), and hence \[T(u,v)=(u,v+u),\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}I_2&0\\ I_2&I_2\end{pmatrix}.\] The additional eigenvalue condition used later on p. 89 to obtain the computational shortcut (C.6) is not a hypothesis of (C.2); no such shortcut is used here.

## Pairing and the decisive computation

The cohomology can be represented on the second handle, where the local system is constant. More explicitly, cutting along the separating circle gives an isomorphism from the first handle’s \(H^1\) to the circle’s \(H^1\), induced by \(-2y_1\), while the constant second handle has zero boundary restriction in degree one and an isomorphism in degree zero. Mayer–Vietoris thus identifies the global \(H^1\) with the second handle’s \(H^1\). Relative representatives on that handle give \[B((u,v),(u',v'))=u^{\mathsf T}Jv'-v^{\mathsf T}Ju',
 \qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}0&J\\-J&0\end{pmatrix}.\] An overall orientation sign does not alter the following degeneracy or determinant conclusions. Direct exact arithmetic gives \[B^{\mathsf T}=B,\quad \det B=1,\quad T^{\mathsf T}BT=B,\quad \det T=1.\] With \(N=T-I\), \[N^2=0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}N=2,\qquad
 \ker N=\operatorname{im}N=\{(0,v):v\in\mathbb Q^2\}.\] The ordinary fixed space is therefore two-dimensional and its Gram matrix is zero. This is also coordinate-free: invariance implies \[B(Nx,y)=B(x,T^{-1}y)-B(x,y)=0\qquad(y\in\ker N).\] Since \(\operatorname{im}N=\ker N\), the restriction to the entire fixed space vanishes.

In contrast, the generalized \(1\)-eigenspace is all of \(\mathbb Q^4\), with determinant \(1\). Its complement is zero-dimensional, so \(\det{}'(1-T)=1\). The printed right side of (C.2) is thus \(1\cdot0=0\), which is not even an element of the asserted group \(\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2}\).

For an independent square-class check, set \(R=I+N/2\). Exact multiplication verifies \(R^{\mathsf T}BR=B\), \(\det R=1\), and \(R^2=T\). The spinor norm is a homomorphism to a group of exponent two, so \[\operatorname{spinor}(T)=\operatorname{spinor}(R)^2=1.\] Thus the expression in (C.2) fails on an input satisfying its written hypotheses. This is an exact algebraic and topological example, not a numerical approximation.

## Alternative readings, correction, and impact

The distinction cannot be removed by an unstated global notation convention. On p. 82 the manuscript explicitly distinguishes \(V^+\), the generalized \(1\)-eigenspace, from \(V^1\), the ordinary eigenspace. On p. 83 it explicitly interprets superscript \(1\) as invariants and treats unipotent blocks. No semisimplicity hypothesis is imposed in Lemma C.2.1. The later optional assumption on \(\rho(b_1)\) does not retroactively restrict that lemma.

The precise correction is \[\delta=\operatorname{disc}\!\left(H^1(S,\rho)_{\mathrm{gen},\,1}\right),
 \qquad h=\dim\ker(T-I)\ \text{unchanged}.\] This repair follows from the manuscript’s own (B.31). In its orthogonal decomposition \(V=V^+\oplus V^-\oplus W\), that formula gives \(\operatorname{spinor}(T)=\det(1-T)|_W\,\operatorname{disc}(V^+)\). Adding \(V^-\) to the determinant complement contributes \(2^{\dim V^-}\), a square because \(T\in SO(V)\) and \(\dim V^-\) is even. This yields the corrected \(\det{}'\) expression. In the example the corrected discriminant is \(1\). The ordinary dimension is \(h=2\), whereas the generalized dimension is four; replacing \(h\) as well would incorrectly change the torsion sign.

The assessment is Minor: a false local determinant reformulation with a verified limited correction. The valid generalized-space formula is already present, and semisimple-at-one or acyclic cases do not exhibit this discrepancy. The counterexample does not refute Lemma C.2.1, Theorems 2.1 or 3.1, or the existence of the nonsquare example used in Appendix C. A complete audit of every downstream use was not performed; no broader damage or author adoption is asserted.

# Further checks and rejected concerns

## The half-Euler sign

Under \(H^0(S,\rho)=H^2(S,\rho)=0\), the Wang sequence gives \(H^0(M_t,\rho)=0\) and \(H^1(M_t,\rho)=\ker(T-I)\). Thus the semicharacteristic used on p. 10 is \(\chi_{1/2}(M_t,\rho)=-h\). Its factor \((-1)^{\chi_{1/2}/2}\) equals \((-1)^{h/2}\), since \(h\) is even. The checked sign is consistent; the established problem is the space defining \(\delta\).

## Divided derivatives in finite characteristic

Equation (3.4), p. 28, uses a divided-derivative notation. Literal division by \(h!\) in the coefficient field would be undefined when its characteristic divides \(h!\). For example, over \(\mathbb F_7\), the ordinary eighth derivative of \((1-t)^8\) and \(8!\) both vanish, while the coefficient of \((t-1)^8\) is \(1\).

This calculation alone does not establish an error in the intended formula. On p. 44, the manuscript expressly identifies the notation with the leading Taylor coefficient. That coefficient, equivalently a Hasse derivative, remains defined. Moreover (3.4) itself defines the quantity by the spinor norm and correctly uses a generalized fixed space. The source therefore supplies a coherent interpretation; this concern is not counted as a second finding.

## Characteristic and dependency limits

Lemma C.2.1 is stated in characteristic zero, whereas its mapping-torus interpretation is used with finite coefficient fields in Lemma 5.2.1 (pp. 41–42). The preserved bounded helper analysis gives a conditional extension: pass to a cyclic cover of odd degree \(p^a\) to kill the unipotent part of \(T\); the spinor norm and the corrected torsion, interpreted through Theorem 2.1, are unchanged under that odd cover. The semisimple case separates nondegenerate fixed and acyclic summands and uses (B.31). This is conditional on the topological theorem and its torsion formalism, not an independent reproof of them. No failure of the finite-field application was established, and the visible characteristic restriction alone is not graded as a proved error.

# Overall assessment and evidential limits

One demonstrated local formula defect is supported in the acquired v1. The admissible example, precise source locations, coordinate-free degeneracy argument, counterevidence, and generalized-space repair fix its mathematical scope. The arithmetic theorem has stronger assumptions, and no counterexample to it has been supplied. The result is an initial technical assessment, not a certificate of all 98 pages or an independent acceptance.
