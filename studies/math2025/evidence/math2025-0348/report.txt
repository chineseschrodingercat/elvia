Abstract: The distributional dilation used in the volume-identity proof has an incorrect surface-measure coefficient. An admissible disk solution gives singular coefficients 11/4 and 121/40, respectively, for the actual and printed formulas. The corrected coefficient restores the finite-difference identity; its effect on the limiting boundary integral is bounded by a constant times the dilation parameter, so this check does not contradict the rigidity theorems. The separate boundary-limit sign error is a documented author erratum and is corrected in the retained 2026 revision. The assessment concerns an invalid derivation in a central proof, with the surviving limiting argument stated explicitly. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Definitions and hypotheses

Equation reference: eq:density, eq:source. Theorem 1.3 assumes a bounded indecomposable set \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n\) of finite perimeter with \[
 \mathcal H^{n-1}(B_r(x)\cap\partial^*\Omega)\leq A r^{n-1}
 \quad\text{for $\mathcal H^{n-1}$-a.e. $x\in\partial^*\Omega$, $0<r<1$,}\] and a function \(u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R^n)\) satisfying, distributionally, \[
 u=0\text{ a.e. outside }\Omega,\qquad
 \Delta u=c\,\mathcal H^{n-1}\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}\partial^*\Omega-\mathbf1_\Omega\,dx .\] Here \(c>0\), and \(\nu_x\) is the inward measure-theoretic normal. The conclusion is that \(\Omega\) is a ball, up to translation, with the corresponding quadratic torsion function.

The slit extension also includes a rectifiable set \(\Sigma\), an additional measure \(2c\,\mathcal H^{n-1}\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}\Sigma\), and the specified two-sided blow-up \[r^{-1}u(x+rz)\longrightarrow c\,|\nu_x\cdot z|
 \quad\text{for $\mathcal H^{n-1}$-a.e. $x\in\Sigma$.}\] This equal-slope condition is stronger than a statement about the total measure density \(2c\). It is needed for the slit contribution to the centered boundary limit to vanish; it must not be omitted when interpreting Theorem 4.1.

# A surface-measure scaling error

Problem 1 (The finite-difference Laplacian in Lemma 2.2).  The displayed formula immediately before (2.6), on page 7 of [primary], assigns coefficients \((1+\epsilon)^2c\) and \((1-\epsilon)^2c\) to the Hausdorff measures on the dilated reduced boundaries. For the stated distributional equation, the coefficients are instead \((1+\epsilon)c\) and \((1-\epsilon)c\). The same issue occurs in the slit calculation on page 14.

## Original calculation

Equation reference: eq:phi. The proof uses \[
 \phi_\epsilon(x)=
 \frac{u((1+\epsilon)x)-u((1-\epsilon)x)}{2\epsilon}-2u(x).\] Writing \(a=1+\epsilon\) and \(b=1-\epsilon\), the printed singular part of \(\Delta\phi_\epsilon\) is \[\frac{c}{2\epsilon}
 \bigl(a^2\mathcal H^{n-1}\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}a^{-1}\partial^*\Omega
       -b^2\mathcal H^{n-1}\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}b^{-1}\partial^*\Omega\bigr)
 -2c\,\mathcal H^{n-1}\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}\partial^*\Omega .\] The remaining term is explicitly absolutely continuous, of the form \(O(\epsilon^{-1})\,dx\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}N_\epsilon\). The actual page image confirms the squares; the issue is not an extraction error.

## Distributional calculation and admissible example

Equation reference: eq:correctscale. For \(v_a(x)=u(ax)\), \(a>0\), and a smooth compactly supported test function \(\eta\), \[\langle\Delta v_a,\eta\rangle
 =a^{2-n}\langle\Delta u,\eta(\,\cdot\,/a)\rangle.\] Applying this to [eq:source] (#eq:source), and changing variables in the surface integral, gives \[
 \Delta[u(a\,\cdot)]
 =ac\,\mathcal H^{n-1}\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}a^{-1}\partial^*\Omega
   -a^2\mathbf1_{a^{-1}\Omega}\,dx.\] Indeed, the factor \(a^{2-n}\) is multiplied by the surface Jacobian \(a^{n-1}\). The volume and surface terms therefore scale differently.

For a complete test under the original hypotheses, take \[n=2,\quad \Omega=B_1(0),\quad
 c=\frac12,\quad u(x)=\frac{(1-|x|^2)_+}{4}.\] The disk is bounded and indecomposable, has finite perimeter, and satisfies [eq:density] (#eq:density), for example with \(A=2\pi\). The compactly supported Lipschitz function \(u\) is in \(W^{1,2}(\mathbb R^2)\), vanishes outside \(\Omega\), and satisfies \[\Delta u=\tfrac12\mathcal H^1\!\mathbin{\rule{0.4pt}{1.1ex}\rule{0.7ex}{0.4pt}}S^1-\mathbf1_{B_1}\,dx.\] Thus all hypotheses relevant to Lemma 2.2 are met.

Equation reference: eq:numbers. Set \(\epsilon=1/10\), so \(a=11/10\). At the circle \(r=10/11\), only \(u(ar)\) in [eq:phi] (#eq:phi) has a derivative jump. Its interior radial derivative is \(-a/2\), and its exterior derivative is zero. Consequently the actual singular coefficient of \(\Delta\phi_\epsilon\) there is \[
 \frac{ca}{2\epsilon}=\frac{11}{4},
 \qquad\text{whereas the printed coefficient is}\qquad
 \frac{ca^2}{2\epsilon}=\frac{121}{40}.\] Their difference is \(11/40\). An absolutely continuous remainder cannot absorb a nonzero measure supported on this circle. An independently written exact-rational calculation, retained in the verification evidence, confirms [eq:numbers] (#eq:numbers); the distributional argument itself is exact.

## What the correction restores

Equation reference: eq:quotient. Changing variables after [eq:correctscale] (#eq:correctscale) replaces the exponent \(3-n\) in (2.6)–(2.7) by \(2-n\). This does not change the relevant limit. To see this explicitly, define \[Q_s(x)=
 \frac{a^s u(x/a)-b^s u(x/b)}{2\epsilon}.\] Then \[
 Q_{3-n}(x)-Q_{2-n}(x)
 =\frac{a^{2-n}u(x/a)+b^{2-n}u(x/b)}2.\] On \(\partial^*\Omega\), the boundary representative has \(u(x)=0\). The Lipschitz regularity obtained in the paper and boundedness of \(\Omega\) imply that the right side of [eq:quotient] (#eq:quotient) is uniformly \(O(\epsilon)\). Its integral tends to zero because the perimeter is finite. The same calculation applies to the slit term with its prescribed vanishing representative and finite surface measure.

Thus the printed finite-\(\epsilon\) distributional identity is false, while the corrected identity has the same limiting boundary contribution. The volume identity \[(n+2)\int_\Omega u\,dx=c^2n|\Omega|\] and the ensuing rigidity argument are not refuted by this example. This is an invalid derivation in the new proof of a central lemma, so its assessment is Major at the derivation level. That assessment does not assert that the main theorem is false. The correction and the vanishing of its limiting effect have been checked.

The author revision retains the squared coefficients in the slit calculation on its page 14 and the exponent \(3-n\) on its page 8. The retained erratum does not list this surface-scaling correction. Author adoption of this correction is therefore unknown; no claim of first discovery is made.

# Documented sign correction and surviving reasoning

The author erratum [erratum] separately corrects the sign of the boundary limit for \(\psi_\epsilon\), the corresponding integrated sign, the analogous slit calculation, the boundary-integral label, and a lemma reference. Its own page locators refer to a different layout. The corresponding primary-source passages are on pages 8, 14 and 16.

This sign can be checked directly. At a reduced-boundary point, put \(t=\nu_x\cdot x\). The half-space blow-up gives \[\phi_\epsilon(x)\longrightarrow \frac{ct}{2},
 \qquad
 Q_{2-n}(x)\longrightarrow-\frac{ct}{2}.\] Hence \(\psi_\epsilon=\phi_\epsilon-Q_{2-n}\) tends to \(ct\), rather than \(-ct\). With the inward normal, \[\int_{\partial^*\Omega}\nu_x\cdot x\,d\mathcal H^{n-1}=-n|\Omega|,\] and the corrected sign yields the positive volume identity above. Actual views of the separate 2026 manuscript confirm the sign and boundary-label corrections on pages 8 and 14 and the corrected Lemma 4.4 reference on page 16. These are known-issue reproductions, not new allegations against the revised text.

The final rigidity calculation also gives useful counterevidence against overstating the scaling defect. Conditional on the gradient bound and volume identity, let \(P=|\nabla u|^2+2u/n\). Since \(\Delta u=-1\) in the interior, \[\Delta P
 =2|D^2u|^2+\frac2n\Delta u
 =2|D^2u+I/n|^2.\] The intermediate sign printed before \((2/n)\Delta u\) on page 12 needs an algebraic correction, but the displayed final square has the correct value. The integral equality and \(P\leq c^2\) force \(P=c^2\); the Hessian is \(-I/n\), and indecomposability supplies the single-ball conclusion. The slit estimate likewise uses \(|a_+-a_-|\leq a_++a_-\) for the two Green-function slopes. These checks do not independently establish the regularity and harmonic-measure inputs on which that argument depends.
