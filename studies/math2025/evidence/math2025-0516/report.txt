Abstract: One Minor correction is established: two index ranges omit the wiring vertex, although the immediately surrounding formulas retain it. Taken literally, one range makes a unit flow impossible and the other makes the matrix singular. The intended grounded objects are explicit in the paper, so this is a local notation correction and does not demonstrate a failure of the transience theorems. The review independently checks the shell-flow estimate, the tree moment bound, the hierarchical comparison constants, the conductance criterion and the determinant inequality. External reduction and fluctuation results remain identified inputs; the boundary-condition extension of the latter is recorded as a verification limit, not an established false assertion.

The process at \(i\) jumps to \(j\) at rate \(W_{ij}(1+\ell_j(t))\), with symmetric weights and local occupation times. Model 1.1 requires \(d\geq1\), \(\bar W>0\), \(\alpha>1\), a positive decreasing radial function \(w\), and \[W_{ij}=w(\|i-j\|_\infty),\quad
 w(r)\geq \bar W(\log_2 r)^\alpha r^{-2d},\quad
 \sum_{j\ne0}w(\|j\|_\infty)<\infty .\] Theorem 1.2 asserts finite expected numbers of visits, uniformly over the target vertex. Model 1.5 requires \(d\geq3\), \(W_{ij}\geq\bar W\mathbf1_{\{\|i-j\|_2=1\}}\), and a uniformly finite sum of incident weights. Theorem 1.6 uses sufficiently large \(\bar W\); its stated choice is \(\bar W_1=\max\{\bar W_2,10^8\}\). Theorem 1.7 gives moments for \(1\leq m\leq\tfrac12\bar W^{1/8}\), with \(\bar W_2\geq2^8\). The strict inequality \(\bar W>\bar W_2\) in Lemma 1.8 allows an exponent \(m>1\); the endpoint is not silently enlarged.

Wiring adds a vertex \(\rho\), with \(u_\rho=0\) and \(h_i=W_{i\rho}=\sum_{j\notin\Lambda_N}W_{ij}\). These sums are finite under the stated assumptions. Up to the first exit, no outside vertex has accumulated local time, so the total exit rate is exactly the rate of a jump to this wired vertex. Uniform summability also prevents finite-time explosion: up to time \(T\), the total jump rate is bounded by \((1+T)\sup_i\sum_jW_{ij}\). The nearest-neighbour wording in Theorem 2.1 refers to adjacency in the specified graph, which includes its long Euclidean edges.

# Problem 1: retain the wiring vertex in the index ranges

Problem 1 (Minor; local notation correction).  The flow indexing immediately before (2.7), printed page 1496 (physical page 6), and the diagonal sum in (4.9), printed page 1505 (physical page 15), omit \(\rho\).

#### Flow instance.

The hypotheses are those of Theorem 2.1: a finite wired network \(G_N=(\Lambda_N\cup\{\rho\},E_N)\), positive conductances on its edges, and a source \(x\in\Lambda_N\). The paragraph before (2.7) indexes the antisymmetric flow by \(i,j\in\Lambda_N\), and its node sum also uses \(j\in\Lambda_N\), while declaring a unit flow from \(x\) to \(\rho\). Taken with this restricted range, the node equations on \(\Lambda_N\) would imply \[0=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda_N}\theta(i,j)
   =\sum_{i\in\Lambda_N}\delta_{ix}=1.\] The first equality follows from antisymmetry on a finite set. Thus no such internally indexed flow exists.

#### Matrix instance.

Section 4.1 assumes a finite vertex set \(\Lambda\), nonnegative weights on the extended complete graph, and connectedness of the positive-weight graph \(G_+\). Every component of the internal graph has some positive pinning. Equation (4.9) specifies off-diagonal entries \(-W_{ij}e^{u_i+u_j}\), but its diagonal sum is over \(k\in\Lambda:\{i,k\}\in E_+\). The resulting internal Laplacian \(D_0\) satisfies \(D_0\mathbf1=0\). It cannot have the positive determinant and inverse used in (4.8) and (4.15). For the admissible one-vertex example \(\Lambda=\{i\}\), \(h_i=2\), \(e^{u_i}=3\), the printed sum is empty and gives \(D_{ii}=0\); the grounded value is \(6\).

#### Repair, counterevidence and dependency.

Equation reference: eq:grounded. For the flow, include \(\rho\) in the vertex indices and incident node sums. For the matrix, keep the rows and columns indexed by \(\Lambda\), but sum over the neighbours in \(\Lambda\cup\{\rho\}\): \[
 D_{ii}=\sum_{\substack{k\in\Lambda\\\{i,k\}\in E_\Lambda}}
 W_{ik}e^{u_i+u_k}+h_i e^{u_i}.\] The sentence immediately after (4.9) already prints [eq:grounded] (#eq:grounded). Equation (4.13), \(D=F\mathcal W F^t\), also keeps pinning-edge columns in the reduced incidence matrix. Likewise (2.7)–(2.9) sum over all edges \(E_N\), including wiring edges. These are direct internal indications of the intended objects, not speculative repairs.

With [eq:grounded] (#eq:grounded), \[v^tDv=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_\Lambda}
 W_{ij}e^{u_i+u_j}(v_i-v_j)^2
 +\sum_i h_i e^{u_i}v_i^2>0\qquad(v\ne0),\] because each internal component has positive pinning. Thus the correct inverse and matrix-tree determinant are well-defined. The affected dependencies are Thomson’s comparison in Theorem 2.1 and the grounded determinant representation leading to Lemma 4.2. The literal index formulas are wrong; the intended derivations already use the corrected grounding. No counterexample to Theorems 1.2, 1.4, 1.6 or 1.7 follows from this finding.

# Checks of the long-range argument

## Shell flow and the role of \(\alpha>1\)

For the annuli \(B_k\) on page 1495, set \(b=2^d-1\). Their exact sizes are \(|B_0|=1\) and \(|B_k|=b\,2^{kd}\) for \(k\geq1\). The unit flow sends \(1/(|B_k||B_{k+1}|)\) along every ordered edge from \(B_k\) to \(B_{k+1}\). Each noninitial used vertex receives and sends \(1/|B_k|\); unused vertices have zero divergence. Since the distance between consecutive shells is at most \(2^{k+2}\), the lower bound for \(w\) gives energy at most \[\sum_{k\geq0}
 \frac{2^{2d(k+2)}}{\bar W(k+2)^\alpha |B_k||B_{k+1}|}
 \leq \frac{2^{3d}}{\bar W}\sum_{k\geq0}(k+2)^{-\alpha}.\] The dimension-dependent coefficient before \(\bar W^{-1}(k+2)^{-\alpha}\) is exactly \(2^{3d}/b\) at \(k=0\) and \(2^{3d}/b^2\) for \(k\geq1\). Both are at most the stated \(2^{3d}\). This checks (2.1)–(2.2), including the initial shell. At \(\alpha=1\) this particular energy estimate diverges; the paper does not claim its theorem at that endpoint.

## Tree increments and hierarchical scaling

On the comparison tree of Lemma 3.3, the edge increment \(U\) has density \[\sqrt{\frac w{2\pi}}\,
 e^{-U/2-w(\cosh U-1)}\,dU,\qquad w>0.\] Using \(\cosh U-1\geq U^2/2\) and completing the square gives \[\mathbb Ee^{\sigma mU}
 \leq\exp\!\left(\frac{(2\sigma m-1)^2}{8w}\right)
 \leq\exp\!\left(\frac{(2m+1)^2}{8w}\right).\] Independent edge increments then give (3.4). As a separate normalization check, integer moments have an exact finite form: if \(n=m-1\) for \(\sigma=1\) and \(n=m\) for \(\sigma=-1\), then \[\mathbb Ee^{\sigma mU}
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(n+k)!}{k!(n-k)!(2w)^k}.\] This follows from the half-integer Bessel integral and can also be obtained by integration by parts. In particular, \(\mathbb Ee^U=1\) and \(\mathbb Ee^{-U}=1+w^{-1}\), consistent with the asymmetry of the density.

For the antichain path, consecutive blocks have sizes \(2^{l-2}\) and \(2^{l-1}\), so their interaction is \(2^{2l-3}w^H(l)\geq\bar W^H l^\alpha/8\). The final pinning edge obeys the same bound at \(l=N+1\). The sum of reciprocal edge weights is therefore uniformly bounded by \(8(\bar W^H)^{-1}\sum_{l\geq2}l^{-\alpha}\), exactly the quantity used in (3.12). Uniform pinning is compatible with every antichain, as required for the cited reduction.

The binary interleaving map in Lemma 3.4 gives \(\|i-j\|_\infty<2^{\lceil d_H/d\rceil}\). Monotonicity of \(w\), which is an operative assumption, is needed when transferring this to an interaction lower bound. With \(\bar W^H=\bar Wd^{-\alpha}2^{-2d}\), (3.21) follows from \(\lceil l/d\rceil\geq l/d\) and \(\lceil l/d\rceil\leq1+l/d\). For (3.22), a displacement of maximum norm at least \(2^N\) from any point of the box must leave it. Reindexing by that displacement justifies the apparently unshifted shell in the pinning sum. The last estimate (3.23) uses \(d(N+1)\geq Nd+1\); its discarded factor is at least \(2^{d+2}\). Thus the quoted uniform moment constant (3.24) has the stated powers of \(d\), \(2\), \(m\) and \(\bar W\).

## From moments to expected visits

For a fixed random environment, the number of visits to \(x\) before reaching \(\rho\), conditional on first reaching \(x\), has mean \(c(x)R_N(c,x\leftrightarrow\rho)\). Using the deterministic minimizing flow in the random network gives the upper bound in (2.9), after averaging the ratios \(c_f/c_e\). Dependence between conductances does not invalidate this argument: the chosen comparison flow is deterministic.

Changing the starting point of the environment from \(\rho\) to 0 introduces the density \(e^{u_0}\). Accordingly the integrand in (2.11) has five exponential factors, not four. Hölder’s inequality with exponent five requires the \(m=5\) positive and negative moment bounds. The choice \(\bar W\geq10^8\) ensures \(\tfrac12\bar W^{1/8}\geq5\) in the high-dimensional case. Monotone convergence of the actual stopped visit counts gives the infinite-volume conclusion; a common environment coupling for different \(N\) is not needed for Theorem 2.1.

# Determinant and high-dimensional checks

With the grounded \(D\), write \(\mathcal W_e=W_e e^{u_{e+}+u_{e-}}\), \(Q_e=e^{u_{e+}+u_{e-}}/B_e\), and \(\mathcal G=\sqrt Q F^tD^{-1}F\sqrt Q\). The two square roots in (4.15) were checked on the source image. Sylvester’s identity gives \[\det(I-M\mathcal G)
 =\frac{\det(F(\mathcal W-MQ)F^t)}{\det D}.\] For \(0\leq m_e<W_e\) on positive edges and \(m_e=0\) otherwise, every modified edge weight is positive and every \(1-m_e/(W_eB_e)\) lies in \((0,1]\). The spanning-tree expansion therefore yields \[\det(I-M\mathcal G)\geq
 \prod_{e\in E_+}\left(1-\frac{m_e}{W_e}\right)>0.\] Multiplying by \(\prod B_e^{m_e}\) and using the Ward identity gives (4.18). Positivity is essential; this argument does not extend the bound to \(m_e=W_e\).

An exact rational check on a triangle with vertices \(a,b,\rho\) used \(W_{ab}=3,W_{a\rho}=2,W_{b\rho}=4\), \(e^{u_a}=2,e^{u_b}=1/3\), \(s_a=1/2,s_b=-1\), and \(m=(1,1/2,3/2)\). It gives \[D=\begin{pmatrix}6&-2\\-2&10/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det D=16,\qquad
 \det(I-M\mathcal G)=\frac{1645}{2277}>\frac5{16}.\] The vertex determinant ratio, edge determinant and tree expansion agree exactly. Further exact checks covered the 27 shell coefficients for \(d=1,2,3\), \(k=0,\ldots,8\), and 18 integer tree moments for \(w=1/8,1,10\), \(m=1,2,5\), both signs. Numerical logarithmic margins for the latter are corroboration, not interval-certified inequalities; the Gaussian proof above establishes the inequality analytically. No author code was used.

For (4.31)–(4.33), the Cauchy–Schwarz exponent is \(2m\). The restriction \(2m\leq\bar W^{1/8}\), together with \(\bar W\geq2^{8/7}\), ensures \(2m/\bar W\leq1/2\). The boundary edge has \(W_{j\rho}\geq\bar W\); hence \((1-2m/W_{j\rho})^{-1}\leq2\). Taking the square root of the product of the two bounds \(2^{2m+1}\) gives the stated \(2^{2m+1}\) bound. This checks the numerical range and local algebra, subject to the external fluctuation input discussed next.

# External inputs and the limits of this review

## Fluctuation and localization input

Theorem 1 of Disertori–Spencer–Zirnbauer [DSZ] (physical page 4 of the acquired 54-page author PDF) gives the difference-field bound for dimension three, with analogous estimates stated for higher dimensions and exponent \(m\leq\beta^{1/8}\). The remark on physical page 6 explicitly uses periodic boundaries. Section 13, physical page 41, explains that hard pinning is allowed without a lower bound on the regularization parameter. The current paper applies the fluctuation input to a wired box with general pinning in (4.32). This review has not reconstructed that entire boundary and pinning extension from the cited proof. It is a material limit of the present positive verification, not an established counterexample or a demonstrated invalid extension. Appendix C, Proposition 2 (physical page 53), states localization for smooth, integrable, invariant functions. The current paper’s Ward calculation and growth assumptions were read, but a complete independent proof of the underlying superintegration theorem is outside this review.

## Convex monotonicity input

Poudevigne-Auboiron’s Theorem 6 [PA], printed pages 794–795 (physical pages 6–7), requires finite symmetric weight matrices, zero diagonal, nonnegative off-diagonal entries, entrywise \(W^-\leq W^+\), and connectedness for \(W^-\). The comparison is for a convex function of a nonnegative Green-function ratio. Including \(\rho\) as a vertex supplies the finite connected graph used here. The comparison networks in the current paper retain connectedness, so deletion of the other positive weights is permitted. For the negative moment test \(x^{-m}\), which is not finite at zero, one may first use \((x+\varepsilon)^{-m}\) and then pass to the limit by monotone convergence. This removes a possible endpoint objection to Fact 2.2. The cited coupling theorem itself has not been independently reproved here.

## Hierarchical reduction input

The cited reduction [HIER] requires a maximal antichain compatible with the pinning, with interactions \(2^{\ell(i)+\ell(j)}w(\ell(i\wedge j))\) and pinning equal to the sum over each block. Uniform pinning satisfies compatibility. Corollary 2.3 identifies the joint law at leaves that remain in the antichain, which is the property used for the distinguished leaf in the present proof.

The official ALEA PDF was accessible to the web reader, but the single native download attempt returned a non-PDF rate-limit response. That response was retained, with no retry. An independently acquired primary preprint, `arXiv:2111.08293v1` [HIERPRE], has 24 pages. Its physical pages 7–9 contain the compatible-antichain hypotheses, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 just described. The preprint is identified separately; it is not represented as byte-identical to the 20-page journal article. The full external reduction proof was not independently reproved.
