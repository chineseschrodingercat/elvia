# F1. The algebraic boundary formula fails for an oversized center

Location. Proposition 5.10, final displayed formula after (5.12), printed pp. 66–67 (PDF 32–33). Definition 4.6 and the paragraph following it, pp. 58–59, expressly allow the center to be larger than the Zariski span of the face it meets.

Claim under test. If no smaller member of \(\mathcal B\) contains \(\sigma_W\), the proposition asserts \[L^\mathcal B\cap E_W^\mathcal B
 =(L_{\sigma_W}^{\mathcal B_W}\times P^{\mathcal B_{/W}})
 \cup(P^{\mathcal B_W}\times L_{\sigma_{/W}}^{\mathcal B_{/W}}).\] Here \(L^\mathcal B\) is the union of the Zariski closures of the facets of the blown-up polytope. This assertion concerns the ambient algebraic boundary, and is stronger than the asserted product of real polytopes.

Example satisfying the hypotheses. Take \(V=\mathbb Q^4\), let \(\sigma\) be the standard projective three-simplex, and set \[C=\mathbb R_{\geq0}^4,\qquad
 W=\langle e_1,e_2-e_3\rangle,\qquad \mathcal B=\{\mathbb P(W)\}.\] The arrangement is intersection-closed. A vector in \(C\cap W\) has the form \((a,b,-b,0)\), so \(b=0\) and \(a\geq0\). Thus \(\sigma_W=\{[e_1]\}\) is a nonempty face, and there is no smaller member of \(\mathcal B\). The quotient map \[q(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(u,v)=(x_2+x_3,x_4)\] has kernel \(W\) and maps \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R_{\geq0}^2\). Hence the normal polytope is an interval in \(\mathbb P^1\), whose boundary consists of \([1:0]\) and \([0:1]\). The arrangements on both factors are empty. The proposed right-hand side is \[\mathbb P(W)\times\{[1:0],[0:1]\},\] because the intrinsic boundary of the point \(\sigma_W\) is empty.

Actual blow-up calculation. In the affine chart \(x_1=1\), put \(z=x_2\), so \(x_3=u-z\). The center has ideal \((u,v)\). In the blow-up chart \(v=ut\), the coordinates are \((u,z,t)\), the exceptional divisor is \(E=\{u=0\}\), and the strict transforms of the three relevant original facets are \[\widetilde H_2=\{z=0\},\qquad
 \widetilde H_3=\{u-z=0\},\qquad
 \widetilde H_4=\{t=0\}.\] Each is the closure of an actual two-dimensional facet. The point \(P=(0,0,1)\) belongs to \(E\cap\widetilde H_2\cap\widetilde H_3\), and therefore to \(L^\mathcal B\cap E\). It is also in the lifted polytope: the interior points \((u,z,t)=(\varepsilon,\varepsilon/2,1)\), \(\varepsilon>0\), approach it. Under \(E\simeq\mathbb P(W)\times\mathbb P(V/W)\) its coordinates are \(([e_1],[1:1])\). This point is absent from the proposed right-hand side.

Conclusion. The final algebraic boundary formula is false with all its stated hypotheses. The real product \(\sigma^\mathcal B\cap E=\sigma_W\times\sigma_{/W}\) is not contradicted by this example; indeed it is consistent with the calculation.

# F1 continued: cause, counterevidence and repair limits

The proof treats hyperplanes cutting out the point inside the larger \(\mathbb P(W)\) as if they were intrinsic boundary hyperplanes of that point. Their intersections with \(E\) contain \(\{[e_1]\}\times\mathbb P^1\), including normal directions in the interior of the interval. This is precisely the component missed by the displayed formula.

The same center also exposes the quotient-coordinate step in Lemma 4.7 (p. 59; PDF 25). Of the three independent facet equations \(x_2,x_3,x_4\) through \([e_1]\), only \(x_4\) annihilates \(W\): their values on \(e_2-e_3\) are \(1,-1,0\). Thus one cannot choose \(p=\dim(V/W)=2\) of them as coordinates on \(V/W\), as that proof proposes.

Complete local correction of Lemma 4.7. Its convexity conclusion is nevertheless true. If \(q(a)=-q(b)\) for \(a,b\in C\), then \(a+b\in C\cap W\). Since \(C\cap W\) is a face of \(C\), both \(a\) and \(b\) belong to that face and hence to \(W\). Thus \(q(C)\) contains no nonzero line. It is a finitely generated rational cone, so its projective link is a polyhedron. This proves the needed pointedness without any descent of the original facet equations.

Impact and repair boundary. The failure of Proposition 5.10 enters the general face and boundary formalism, including the following corollary. Replacing the assertion by a formula restricted to the actual face span, or imposing appropriate span hypotheses, requires a fresh argument. A blanket assumption \(W=V_{\sigma_W}\) cannot simply be inserted throughout the paper: Remark 10.8 (p. 96) explicitly requires larger centers, and the wheel example on pp. 109–110 exhibits them. The determinant factorization and the real polytope product may survive. This review has not established a complete replacement of the algebraic boundary formalism for all later applications. Severity is Major because the false formula is a structural assertion in the main geometric construction.

# F4. The stated minimality of the core arrangement is false

Location. Footnote 3, printed p. 76 (PDF 42), read with Definition 5.13 and Proposition 5.14, pp. 69–70.

For a coordinate simplex, different coordinate subspaces meet the simplex in different faces. Each such subspace is already the unique smallest member of the maximal coordinate arrangement having that intersection. Consequently \((\mathcal B^{G,\max})^{\min}=\mathcal B^{G,\max}\), not in general \(\mathcal B^{G,\mathrm{core}}\).

For a concrete stable graph, take \(G=K_4\) and a two-edge path \(\gamma=\{12,23\}\). The coordinate center defined by \(x_{12}=x_{23}=0\) is a \(\mathbb P^3\) in \(\mathbb P^5\), belongs to the maximal arrangement, and is minimal for its face. It is absent from the core arrangement, because the path is not bridgeless. At a relative interior point of this face, no core center passes through it: no nonempty subset of the two zero edges is core. The core blow-up is locally unchanged there, whereas the maximal blow-up has an interval of positive normal directions. This is not an extraneous modification.

Counterevidence and correction. The comparison morphism from the maximal blow-up to the core blow-up still follows from inclusion of arrangements and Proposition 5.2(ii). The core construction was defined directly. Delete the false minimality assertion and use that proposition for the comparison; no failure of the graph bordification is proved. This peripheral, unnecessary justification is Minor.

# F2. The global algebraic Gram map has an unresolved base point

Location. Appendix A.5, (A.25)–(A.27), printed p. 145 (PDF 111), and the unrestricted domain in (A.31), p. 146 (PDF 112). The morphisms are used to construct the map in Theorem A.16, an input to Theorem A.2.

Exact algebraic counterexample. Take \(V=\mathbb Q^2\) and the flag \(0\subset\mathbb Qe_2\subset V\). The affine space \(\mathcal P_F\) consists of lower triangular matrices \[M=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\u&b\end{pmatrix}.\] On its projectivization the proposed map is \[[a:u:b]\longmapsto[a^2+u^2:ub:b^2]\in\mathbb P(\mathcal Q(V)).\] After the legitimate base change to \(\mathbb Q(i)\), the point \(N=[i:1:0]\) is in the stated domain and has \(M^TM=0\). It even belongs to \(\mathcal P_F^\circ\), since each of the two row components is nonzero. This is a failure of an algebraic morphism over \(\mathbb Q\); a morphism over \(\mathbb Q\) would remain defined after base change.

The base point is nonremovable. Along \([i+r:1:0]\) the target tends to \([1:0:0]\), whereas along \([i:1:r]\) it tends to \([0:1:0]\): \[M_1(r)^TM_1(r)=
 \begin{pmatrix}2ir+r^2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M_2(r)^TM_2(r)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&r\\r&r^2\end{pmatrix}.\] These are distinct projective limits as \(r\to0\). The source blow-up specified in (A.26) has center \(\mathbb P(\mathcal P_0)=\{[1:0:0]\}\). It is an isomorphism near \(N\) and therefore cannot resolve this indeterminacy. Thus the universal-property argument does not produce the stated global morphism (A.26).

There is a separate elementary domain obstruction to (A.31) as printed. The real matrix \[R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal P_F^\circ
 \quad\hbox{has}\quad R^TR=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.\] On the open monoid stratum \(\lambda_i\ne0\), its image is singular, contrary to the asserted target \(\mathbb P(\mathcal Q(V))\setminus\operatorname{Det}\). Nonzero row components do not imply invertibility.

Counterevidence. The topological space \(X(P)\) is formed from invertible real parabolic matrices. The two counterexamples are not elements of that group. Equation (A.24) already uses a determinant-complement restriction, and the explicit positive-block calculation (A.32) is consistent with the intended real map. The topological embedding is not disproved.

Impact and repair limits. The advertised unrestricted algebraic construction, and the derivation of the real map from it, are invalid as written. One should restrict to a suitable algebraic open neighborhood of the relevant real locus and prove extension over the monoid boundary using block coordinates and the pullbacks of the blow-up ideals. Merely invoking the universal property, or merely changing the order of the transpose, does not do this. The complete construction on overlapping flags has not been re-established here. This is Major for the Appendix’s central algebraic construction, with the impact confined as stated.

# F3. Closed exceptional loci retain deeper boundary strata

Location. Equation (A.28), printed p. 146 (PDF 112); the closed-locus product and \(f(e(P))=f(X^{\rm BS})\cap E_P(\mathbb R)\) in Theorem A.2, p. 131 (PDF 97); the assertion about intersections of exceptional strata in A.6.1, p. 149 (PDF 115).

The notation immediately preceding (A.28) identifies \(E_{F'}\) with a product of iterated blow-ups, namely a closed exceptional locus. It then removes only the strict transform of \(\operatorname{Det}\) and replaces those factors by unblown determinant complements. This loses deeper exceptional strata. Removing a strict transform does not remove every exceptional divisor over the original hypersurface.

Finite rank-three verification. Let \(V_1=\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\), \(V_2=\langle e_3\rangle\), and blow up \(\mathbb P(\mathcal Q(V/V_1))\) followed by the strict transform of \(\mathbb P(\mathcal Q(V/V_2))\). In the affine chart \(q_{11}=1\), first take \(q_{22}=z\) as the normal coordinate to the rank-one center. For the second blow-up take the new \(q_{33}/z\) coordinate as \(w\). A genuine chart is \[Q(z,w,b,c,e)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&zb&zwc\\
 zb&z&zwe\\
 zwc&zwe&zw
 \end{pmatrix}.\] The two exceptional divisors have equations \(z=0\) and \(w=0\). Direct expansion gives \[\det Q=z^2w
 \bigl(1-zb^2-we^2+2zwbce-zwc^2\bigr).\] The strict transform of \(\operatorname{Det}\) is the vanishing of the parenthesized factor. At \(z=w=b=c=e=0\) that factor equals \(1\). Thus the two closed exceptional divisors intersect outside the strict determinant locus. The point is approached by the positive definite matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(1,z,zw)\) with \(z,w>0\).

For the coarser flag \(F':0\subset V_1\subset V\), blow-down of the second modification sends this point to a normal quadratic form whose restriction to \(V_1\) is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\). That point is excluded by the determinant-complement factor on the right of (A.28), but is present on its stated left. The claimed canonical identification through blow-down therefore fails. The same corner belongs to the full-flag boundary stratum, not to the open stratum \(e(P_{F'})\) alone; the closed intersection in Theorem A.2 cannot isolate the latter.

Counterevidence and repair. The paper explicitly distinguishes \(S_I\) and \(S_I^\circ\) in (A.17) and (A.23). For the corresponding open exceptional stratum, one removes all additional exceptional divisors; the product of unblown determinant complements is then the appropriate local description. In the displayed chart, the coarser open stratum has \(z=0,w\ne0\), and the corner has \(z=w=0\). This confirms the intended stratified picture rather than contradicting existence of the intended embedding.

Impact. Revise the closed/open loci consistently in (A.28), Theorem A.2 and the injectivity argument. Distinct open strata are disjoint by definition; closed exceptional intersections are not forced into \(\widetilde{\operatorname{Det}}\). The incorrect algebraic identification is central to the Appendix’s boundary description, hence Major under the review standard. No counterexample to the cohomology injections or to the appropriately corrected topological correspondence is claimed.

# F5. An ambient kernel identity needs the positivity restriction

Location. Lemma 9.4, first paragraph of the proof, printed p. 89 (PDF 55). For \(f_v(Q)=Q(v,v)\), it asserts \(\ker f_v=\mathcal Q(V/\mathbb Qv)\) on the entire vector space of quadratic forms.

Take \(V=\mathbb Q^2\), \(v=e_1\) and \[Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.\] Then \(Q(v,v)=0\), but \(Qv=e_2\ne0\). The kernel of the scalar linear functional \(f_v\) has dimension \(2\), while \(\mathcal Q(V/\mathbb Qv)\) has dimension \(1\). Thus the ambient equality is false.

Complete correction and counterevidence. The lemma itself only concerns a positive polyhedron. If \(Q\) is positive semidefinite, write \(Q=B^TB\) over \(\mathbb R\); then \(Q(v,v)=\|Bv\|^2=0\) implies \(Bv=0\) and hence \(Qv=0\). Consequently the correct equality is \[\sigma\cap\mathbb P(\ker f_v)
 =\sigma\cap\mathbb P\bigl(\mathcal Q(V/\mathbb Qv)\bigr).\] Choose a basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) of \(K\). Each \(f_{v_i}\) is nonnegative on \(\sigma\), so its zero locus on \(\sigma\) is a face or empty. Their intersection equals \(\sigma\cap\mathbb P(\mathcal Q(V/K))\), using the just-proved restricted identity. A nonempty intersection of faces is a face. This proves the stated lemma. The indefinite matrix above refutes only the ambient identity, not the positive-semidefinite conclusion. The defect is Minor and the local repair is complete.

# Positive checks and boundaries of the findings

The following checks found no additional defect in the indicated step, subject to the dependencies and limits stated on the next page.

For positive semidefinite matrices and positive coefficients, \(\ker(\sum_i\lambda_iQ_i)=\bigcap_i\ker Q_i\) follows by summing their nonnegative quadratic values. This supports the essential-envelope arguments and corrects the proof of Lemma 9.4. In the blow-up block model, the initial determinant is \(z^{\dim K}\det(Q_K)\det(Q_C)\). Both rank-three splits \(\dim K=1,2\) were expanded exactly.

The wheel matrix on pp. 109–110 was reconstructed as \(H^T\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_6)H\) from the three listed cycles. Setting \(x_1=x_2=x_4=x_5=0\) gives \(\operatorname{diag}(0,x_6,x_3)\). Its two-dimensional linear span is properly contained in the three-dimensional space \(\mathcal Q(V/\mathbb Qh_1)\), confirming the paper’s own explanation of why oversized centers occur.

For odd \(g=2m+1\), \(\sum_{k=1}^m(4k+1)=g(g+1)/2-1\). Complementary exterior monomials pair in this top degree. The orientation character on symmetric matrices is \((\det h)^{g+1}\): a reflection changes the signs of precisely \(g-1\) off-diagonal coordinates. This agrees with the odd/even distinction in Lemma 15.10. The compact-support shift by one for \(\mathbb R_{>0}\) and the additional inflation shift give the stated low-rank degrees: \(H^3(\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb Z))\), and two degree-five contributions plus degree ten for \(\mathrm{SL}_6(\mathbb Z)\). These checks concern degrees and mechanisms, not a computation of the complete cohomology rings.

# Limits, adoption conditions and evidence record

Remaining scientific work. The main cohomology assertions were traced and tested at their critical interfaces, but a complete corrected proof has not been assembled. In particular, one must rebuild the algebraic boundary formalism for oversized centers, construct and glue the restricted Gram maps, and correct the open/closed exceptional strata consistently. The topology intended for the infinitely iterated blow-up and the closure assertion in Theorem A.2 also need a precise formulation. This review has not settled that assertion: rational-center blow-ups and limits toward irrational-kernel semidefinite forms require care.

The zeta-product endpoint in (15.1), p. 118, does not match the rank-five product used on p. 128 and needs normalization/rank clarification. A statement of membership up to a nonzero rational factor cannot be disproved merely by comparing numerical values of odd zeta products. No unproved irrationality assertion or additional confirmed finding is inferred from that discrepancy.

The complete external proofs of Borel stability, the invariant-form classification, the foundational results on wonderful models, and the general Tannakian minimal-subquotient construction were not independently rebuilt. The rank-two motivic identification and exact period evaluations are not newly recomputed. No numerical experiments, released software or absent optional repository are required for a purely theoretical result; their absence is not a defect.
