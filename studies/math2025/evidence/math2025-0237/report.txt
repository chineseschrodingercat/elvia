Abstract: The general Fredholm multiplicity assertion used to transfer Laplace eigenvalue counts to a refined determinant is false for an explicit transfer operator satisfying the stated Schottky hypotheses. Its use leaves a central multiplicity step unsupported by the given argument. This supports a Major assessment of the proof as written, without a counterexample to the final spectral-gap theorem. Separate local corrections concern stopping words and elementary auxiliary formulas. Scientific adjudication remains pending.

# Major: a false Fredholm multiplicity identification

On p. 17, Lemma 25 states, for every finite \(\mathcal B\subset\mathcal W_{\ge2}\) and every finite-dimensional unitary representation \(\sigma\), that the order of a zero of \(\det(I-\mathcal L_{\mathcal B,s,\sigma})\) equals \(\dim\ker(I-\mathcal L_{\mathcal B,s,\sigma})\). The zero/eigenvalue equivalence is valid. The additional equality is not a general Fredholm fact, and fails inside the family defined by (3.2).

## Admissible Schottky data

Take \(N=2\), the trivial one-dimensional representation, and \[\gamma_1=\begin{pmatrix}5&24\\1&5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \gamma_2=\begin{pmatrix}20&399\\1&20\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \gamma_3=\gamma_1^{-1},\quad\gamma_4=\gamma_2^{-1}.\] Let \(D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4\) have real centres \(5,20,-5,-20\) and radii \(2,1,1/2,1\), respectively. Their closures are pairwise disjoint. For \(a=5,20\), the corresponding map is \(\phi_a(z)=a-1/(z+a)\); the paired radii have product one. Thus these disks satisfy precisely (2.1), including the inverse pairings. The generators belong to \(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb Z)\) and give the Schottky group required by Lemma 25. No lower bound on \(\delta_\Gamma\) is an assumption of that lemma.

Choose \[\mathcal B=\{(1,1),(2,2),(1,2)\}.\] These are distinct reduced words of length two. At \(s=0\), writing \(f_i=f|_{D_i}\), equation (3.2) gives \[(\mathcal L_0f)_1=f_1\circ\gamma_1,\qquad
 (\mathcal L_0f)_2=f_1\circ\gamma_1+f_2\circ\gamma_2,\qquad
 (\mathcal L_0f)_3=(\mathcal L_0f)_4=0.\] If \(\mathcal L_0f=f\), iteration toward the attracting fixed point of \(\gamma_1\) makes \(f_1\) a constant \(c_1\). Evaluating the second equation at the attracting fixed point of \(\gamma_2\) forces \(c_1=0\). Iteration then makes \(f_2\) constant, and \(f_3=f_4=0\). Consequently \[\dim\ker(I-\mathcal L_0)=1.\] In particular, on the two constant components the operator is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).

## The full Fredholm determinant

This is also an infinite-dimensional counterexample, not just an analogy with a Jordan matrix. The operator is block triangular, with nonzero diagonal blocks \(C_{a,s}f=(\phi_a')^s f\circ\phi_a\), for \(a=5,20\). Put \[x_a=\sqrt{a^2-1},\qquad q_a=(a+x_a)^{-2},\qquad
 h_a(z)=\frac{z-x_a}{z+x_a}.\] Direct computation gives \(h_a\circ\phi_a=q_a h_a\) and \(\phi_a'(x_a)=q_a\in(0,1)\). The pole of \(h_a\) is outside the corresponding closed disk. The eigenfunctions \((h_a')^s h_a^k\) have eigenvalues \(q_a^{s+k}\). Taylor expansion at \(x_a\) identifies all nonzero eigenvalues; compactness and the strict contraction exclude further nonzero spectrum. The principal derivative branch agrees with this identity by continuation from the real interval. Hence \[\det(I-\mathcal L_s)
   =\prod_{a\in\{5,20\}}\prod_{k=0}^\infty(1-q_a^{s+k}).\] Each \(k=0\) factor has a simple zero at \(s=0\) and all other factors form a nonzero convergent product there. The zero order is therefore two, whereas the kernel dimension is one. Independent symbolic checks verify the determinants, disk inequalities, conjugacy, derivatives and Jordan calculation; the analytic argument above supplies the passage to the full Bergman operator.

## Effect on the proof and repair boundary

Proposition 27 on p. 18 invokes Lemma 25 twice. In particular, it identifies the Laplace multiplicity \(m_0\) with the kernel dimension of the standard transfer operator using Corollary 21 and Theorem 23. Those two results identify the determinant’s zero order; they do not by themselves identify its geometric kernel. Replacing the false equality by the general lower bound \[\operatorname{ord}_{s_0}\det(I-\mathcal L_s)
       \ \ge\ \dim\ker(I-\mathcal L_{s_0})\] gives the wrong direction for recovering \(m_0\) from that kernel. The subsequent inclusion of fixed spaces is not enough to fill this gap. The first use, for the squared refined operator, only needs a lower bound, but the second use needs extra information.

The counterexample has \(s_0=0\) and an arbitrary finite word set. It does not satisfy the additional \(s_0>1/2\) hypothesis of Proposition 27 and does not refute that proposition or Theorem 2. The demonstrated damage is to the stated central proof: its general multiplicity premise is false and the required special identification has not been supplied there. A satisfactory repair would prove the needed kernel/eigenspace correspondence in the real spectral range, or establish multiplicity preservation by an analytic factorization for the refinement. Merely citing basic Fredholm properties or proving existence of a fixed vector does not suffice. Dyatlov–Zworski’s cited Lemma 2.4 provides a fixed-vector implication; it is not the missing multiplicity assertion.

# Minor: stopping-word convention and range

On p. 11 a partition is defined by a unique suffix, whereas the proof of Lemma 13 uses disjoint prefix cylinders. The stopping set in (2.9), after taking its mirror, is also constructed by prefix stopping. The cited Dyatlov–Zworski construction uses prefixes and allows one-letter stopping words; these details cannot be interchanged silently.

There is a direct range counterexample to Lemma 14, independent of that terminology. Use the same Schottky data above and \(\tau=2\). Then \(L_\Gamma=4\), so \(0<\tau<L_\Gamma\). For every \(k\ge2\), both \(I_{3^k}\) and its parent lie inside \(I_3\), whose length is one. Thus \(3^k\notin\widetilde{\mathcal B(2)}\), since its parent interval cannot have length greater than \(\tau\). One-letter words are excluded by definition. Arbitrarily long words \(3^m\) therefore have no prefix or suffix in that set. It is not a partition.

For the intended asymptotic use one may restrict \(\tau<\min_a|I_a|\), use prefix partitions consistently, and invoke the correctly oriented refinement lemma. Since \(\tau_n=n^{-\alpha}\to0\), this change does not establish a failure of the final large-level conclusion. It requires adjusting the range of the intermediate assertions, including Proposition 27. No claim about the Hausdorff dimension of this example is needed for the displayed counterexample to Lemma 14.

# Minor auxiliary corrections and successful checks

The following local defects have explicit repairs and are not additional counterexamples to the spectral-gap theorem. On p. 10 the proof of Lemma 10 assumes a multiplicative norm on all \(M_2(\mathbb R)\). Such a norm cannot exist: a nonzero matrix \(E_{12}\) has square zero. The desired comparability follows instead from two-sided bounds for multiplication by fixed invertible matrices, using submultiplicativity and their inverses.

In (4.10), p. 21, the displayed subscript is \(B_2\widetilde B_2\). This concatenation is not a reduced word and represents the identity. The next line of the proof and the counting argument on p. 22 use \(B_1\widetilde B_2\), the required noncancelling word. The latter is the consistent local correction.

The threshold on p. 26 is written \(\max\{\tau_\Gamma,L_\Gamma\}^{-1/2}\). It does not ensure both smallness inequalities invoked on p. 27. Increasing the existential constant to at least \[\max\{2,\tau_\Gamma^{-1/2},L_\Gamma^{-1/2},
                (\min_a|I_a|)^{-1/2}\}\] ensures the needed restrictions for \(\alpha>2\). The geometric-sum estimate (4.21) also requires a suitable \(\varepsilon<\delta_\Gamma\); at equality its sum is \((N+1)(N+2)/2\), rather than \(O(N)\). The actual main-proof choice \(\varepsilon\le1/15<\delta_\Gamma\) lies safely inside that range. These qualifications should accompany the more broadly worded auxiliary statements.

The modular determinant calculation behind Lemma 18 was checked: writing entries as \(1+nA,nB,nC,1+nD\) forces \(d\equiv2-a\pmod{n^2}\), and the divisor count concerns a nonzero product \(BC\). The dyadic decomposition and norm-to-word estimates then lead to the stated logarithmic loss in Corollary 33 in its small-\(\tau\) range. Actual viewing confirms that the bracket in (4.23) includes both polynomial terms; no missing logarithm is alleged. For Lemma 38, exact symbolic expansion reproduces \[\varepsilon^2+(4-\delta)\varepsilon
       \le \tfrac32(\beta-t_\delta),\] and an independent 400-case parameter grid verifies all three exponent inequalities for the prescribed choices. This grid is a bounded check, not a proof for all real parameters. The prime-power lower bounds and the final old-space induction were also followed without a further confirmed defect.
