# What the paper establishes and what it imports

Theorem 1.1 aims to identify module categories with solutions of a KW cell system at the unitary root \(q=\exp(\pi i/(N+k))\), modulo gauge and graph relabelling. The chain is substantive: the oriented embedding theorem in Section 4, the simplified skein presentation in Section 3, and the coordinate system in Section 5 must all be compatible. The positivity assumption is used to deduce relations from zero norms and cannot be dropped for arbitrary Galois conjugates.

Theorem 3.4 derives a presentation from (R1), (R2), (R3), (Hecke), braid absorption, rotational invariance and normalization. Section 4 realizes the graph planar algebra in matrix-valued vector spaces, with the Frobenius–Perron vector controlling duality. Section 5 translates the diagrams into scalar equations and identifies unitary changes of edge bases as gauge transformations. The trace identities in Section 5.1 are useful consequences, not substitutes for all the defining relations.

Sections 6.1–6.6 give six exceptional constructions at levels 4, 6 and 8 and additional associator twists; the rank-eight example is asserted for \(\omega=\pm i\). The statement that there appears to be no solution at \(\omega=-1\) is not a proved nonexistence theorem. Sections 6.7–6.8 treat charge conjugation, unfolding, and an even-level orbifold family. The orbifold procedures are described partly as ways to find candidate cells; the paper then claims direct verification. The use of a heuristic to discover a candidate is therefore not itself a defect. The findings below test the claimed final formulas.

The completeness count in Theorem 1.3 is imported from [EMG23], listed as In Preparation on p. 52. Constructing the displayed families does not by itself reprove that external classification or its count. The subfactor consequences in Section 7 likewise depend on the module-category construction and the cited categorical/subfactor correspondence. Neither external proof was independently reconstructed here.

# Substantive findings

Problem 1 (The printed graph planar-algebra composition fails the identity law).  Major error in a foundational operation, pp. 8 and 20.

Definition 2.9 associates \((p,q)\) to a source path \(p\) and target path \(q\), but equation (1) gives \[(p',q')\circ(p,q)=\delta_{q',p}(p',q).\] This contracts the wrong paths for the stated source/target convention. In the graph with one vertex \(v\) and one directed loop \(e\), let \(f=(v,e):\varnothing\to+\) and \(\operatorname{id}_+=(e,e)\). The displayed formula gives \[\operatorname{id}_+\circ f=0\ne f,\] because the empty path \(v\) differs from \(e\). The graph is finite and connected, and has norm \(1\); it is also compatible with \([N]_q=1\) at level one. Thus this is not an objection obtained by leaving the class of graphs being defined.

The functor in Theorem 4.1, p. 20, sends the basis vector \(\psi_p\) to \(\psi_q\). Its composition requires \[(p',q')\circ(p,q)=\delta_{q,p'}(p,q').\] The discrepancy is therefore not resolved by silently choosing the opposite convention. As printed, the operation does not define the category used in the embedding theorem, so the subsequent formal chain cannot be applied literally. Correcting the matrix-unit convention repairs this specific failure; the intended abstract embedding theorem is not disproved.

Problem 2 (A displayed level-eight exceptional matrix is not Hermitian).  Major error in an explicit constructive witness, p. 42.

For the graph \(\Gamma_{\Lambda_1}^{4,8,\mathrm{Twist}}\), the paper displays the largest block \(U^7_{15}\) with ordered intermediate vertices \((22,23,26,29,30)\). At \(q=e^{\pi i/12}\), put \([n]=\sin(n\pi/12)/\sin(\pi/12)\). The actual PDF gives \[(U^7_{15})_{2,4}=\frac{\sqrt{[3][5]^2}}{[4][6]},
 \qquad
 (U^7_{15})_{4,2}=-\frac{\sqrt{[5]}}{\sqrt{[2]^2[3][4]^2}}.\] Every quantum integer occurring here is positive. The first entry is positive and the second is negative, so they cannot be complex conjugates. Numerically they are approximately \(0.4771487196\) and \(-0.1808094866\), respectively. These numerical values supplement an exact sign argument.

Relation (R2) explicitly requires Hermiticity; the opening paragraph of p. 42 also says the chosen cells are real and hence symmetric. The display therefore contradicts both that requirement and the nearby assertion of a computer verification of (R2). This concerns the only large block publicly printed for this construction and affects the explicit witness used for Theorem 6.15. It is not a cosmetic disagreement about notation.

A corrected complete matrix, with its basis order and version-bound verification of the coupled relations, is needed. Merely changing one sign is not proved sufficient: the magnitudes differ as well. The calculation does not show that no valid cell system exists, that every historical notebook is wrong, or that the abstract existence result and all twists in Theorem 6.16 are false. Those stronger conclusions would require evidence not supplied by this local contradiction.

Problem 3 (Even-level boundary blocks fail the Hecke relation).  Major errors in the formulas supporting the infinite family, pp. 47–51.

The relation is \(U^2=[2]U\), with ordinary matrix multiplication on the indicated path basis. Two admissible low-level instances of p. 48 fail it. Throughout, \(a=(i,j)\) and \(\bar a=(i+j,j)\), as on p. 43.

First boundary block. When \(\bar a_1=(k+2)/2\), the printed block for target \(a+\epsilon_1\pm\epsilon_2\) has denominator \([\bar a_1-\bar a_2]\) for both signs, although its numerator uses \(\bar a_1\mp\bar a_2\). Choose \(k=4\), \(a=(1,2)\), and the minus sign. Then \(\bar a=(3,2)\), and the target is \((3,1)\), which is split. Take source \(((1,2),+)\) and target \(((3,1),-,\chi_+)\). The two intermediate vertices are \[((2,2),+,\chi_+),\qquad ((2,1),+).\] They satisfy both the directed-edge sign rule on p. 45 and the split-edge character rule on p. 47. Exhaustive enumeration of the small defined graph confirmed exactly these two paths.

The printed matrix, in that order, is \[M=\frac1{[1]}
 \begin{pmatrix}[6]&\sqrt{[6][4]}\\
 \sqrt{[6][4]}&[4]\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad q=e^{\pi i/8}.\] It has rank one and \[M^2=([6]+[4])M=[2][5]M\ne[2]M,
 \qquad [5]=1+\sqrt2.\] Replacing the denominator by \([\bar a_1+\bar a_2]=[5]\) repairs this block’s Hecke equation. It does not on its own verify the rest of the cell system.

Split-to-split return block. For \(\bar a_1=(k+4)/2\) and \(\chi_\ell\chi_{\ell'}=\delta\), the last matrix on p. 48 is printed without the \(1/\lambda_a\) normalization present in the related formulas. Choose \(k=2\), \(a=(2,1)\), so \(\bar a=(3,1)\), and source/target \[((2,1),+,\chi_+),\qquad ((2,1),-,\chi_+).\] The valid intermediates are \(((1,1),-)\) and \(((1,2),-,\chi_+)\). The formal intermediate \(a+\epsilon_{-2}=(3,0)\) is not a vertex and is removed; it is not used in the counterexample.

The paper’s unsplit weights give \(\lambda_a=6\) and \(w_{a,-1}=w_{a,2}=4\sqrt3\). Here the signed-index convention is \(\bar a_{-1}=-\bar a_1=-3\), so \([\bar a_{-1}+\bar a_2+1]=[-1]=-1\); this cancels the leading minus sign in the off-diagonal entry. The mother-graph weight \(\lambda_{(3,1)}=2\sqrt3\) occurs in the printed diagonal formula, but \((3,1)\) is not being used as an orbifold intermediate path vertex. Substitution therefore reduces the displayed matrix to \[M=\begin{pmatrix}2\sqrt3&2\sqrt6\\2\sqrt6&4\sqrt3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad [2]=\sqrt3,\] and exact calculation gives \[M^2-[2]M
 =\begin{pmatrix}30&30\sqrt2\\30\sqrt2&60\end{pmatrix}\ne0.\] Dividing this local block by \(6\) makes its Hecke residual zero. The expansion in the proof of Lemma 6.22 on p. 49 already contains a \(w_{a,2}/\lambda_a\) term, consistent with a missing factor in the display.

These are failures of explicit formulas claimed to satisfy (R2), (R3) and (Hecke) and then used in Lemmas 6.22–6.23 and Theorem 6.24. They require corrections and renewed checks of all coupled boundary relations. The easy description of plausible local factors does not reduce their effect on the advertised constructive witness. Conversely, neither example proves nonexistence of the even-level module categories.

Problem 4 (The antisymmetrizer argument does not cover level one as written).  Major boundary proof gap in the presentation reduction, pp. 12–13, 19 and 26.

The recursive formula for \(p_{\Lambda_i}\) divides by \[D_i=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}q^{-2j}.\] Lemma 2.16 uses \(p_{\Lambda_{N+1}}\), its trace and positivity to deduce the additional antisymmetry relation. At the permitted level \(k=1\), \(q=e^{\pi i/(N+1)}\), one has \[D_{N+1}=0,\qquad q^2\ne1.\] For example \(N=2,k=1,q=(1+i\sqrt3)/2\) gives \(1+q^{-2}+q^{-4}=0\) exactly. Thus the stated exclusion \(q^2\ne1\) does not make the recursively normalized idempotent available.

Theorem 3.4 invokes Lemma 2.16, and the proof of Theorem 1.1 includes positive \(k\), hence this endpoint. A separate level-one argument, or a carefully formulated unnormalized antisymmetrizer argument with its positivity justification, is required. A formal \(0/0\) specialization is not a proof. This finding concerns the validity of the presented reduction at an included boundary parameter; it is not a counterexample to the expected pointed-category result.

Problem 5 (The Galois-lifting proof uses a false cyclic decomposition).  Major proof correction for the general-root reduction, pp. 12–13.

Lemma 2.14 sets \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(2(N+k),N)\) and uses a decomposition \[\mathbb Z_L\cong
 \mathbb Z_{2(N+k)}\times\mathbb Z_{L/(2(N+k))}\] to construct the required Galois automorphism. Such a decomposition requires coprime factors. With \(N=4,k=3\), they are \(14\) and \(2\), and \(L=28\). The left side has an element of order \(28\); the right side has exponent \(14\).

The needed conclusion can instead be proved by surjectivity of the reduction map \[(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow
 (\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times,\qquad m=2(N+k).\] For a unit \(\ell\bmod m\), keep that residue and choose a nonzero residue at every prime divisor of \(L\) not dividing \(m\); the Chinese remainder theorem produces a lift coprime to \(L\). At primes already dividing \(m\), any lift is automatically a unit. This supplies the automorphism used by the lemma without the false product isomorphism.

Remark 1.2 on p. 2 explicitly invokes Lemma 2.14 to extend the classification obtained from Theorem 1.1 to non-unitary roots. This is the actual core use of the failed proof step. The unit-lifting argument repairs the stated general-root reduction under the same hypotheses; its availability does not turn the printed false group decomposition into a valid proof. No counterexample to the corrected lemma is asserted. The unitary-root constructions do not acquire a counterexample from this observation. The actual image of p. 12 reads \(q^\ell=e^{2\pi i/m}\); a different exponent objection suggested by text extraction was rejected after viewing the page and is not a finding.

Problem 6 (The printed gauge formula does not preserve cell systems).  Major proof correction in the equivalence-class correspondence, pp. 2 and 25–26.

The displayed formula in Definition 5.5 multiplies the \(B\)-cell by only the matrices for its first and last edges. There is no intervening product in the actual display. The proof diagram on the same page has a \(V\)-coupon on every one of the \(N\) strands. A change of edge bases acts tensorially on all \(N\) edge factors.

For a concrete check take \(N=4,k=1,q=e^{\pi i/5},\omega=1\), the oriented four-cycle, constant positive Frobenius–Perron weights, scalar \(U=c=[2]\), and loop cells \(B_v=(-1)^v\). The graph norm is \([4]=1\), and \(c=[2]=[3]=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Every allowed boundary has a unique internal path. Thus all relations of Definition 5.2 on pp. 23–24 can be checked explicitly: (R1) is \(c=[3]\), (R2) is \(c=\overline c\), (R3) has \(c^3-c\) on both sides, and (Hecke) is \(c^2=[2]c\). Rotational invariance is \(B_{v-1}=-B_v\), braid absorption is \(cB_v=[2]B_v\), and normalization is \(|B_v|^2=1\). The starting cell system therefore satisfies the complete defining relations.

The edge phases \((i,1,1,1)\) satisfy the unitary condition in Definition 5.5. They leave \(U\) unchanged, since the two path factors cancel their conjugates. The printed two-factor formula gives \[(B'_0,B'_1,B'_2,B'_3)=(i,-i,1,-1),\] which violates \(B'_{v+1}=-B'_v\), since \(B'_2=1\ne i=-B'_1\). Including all four factors gives \(iB\), which preserves the complete relations. Thus the printed expression is not the coordinate action of general unitary changes of edge bases and does not preserve the solution set.

Theorem 1.1 on p. 2 expressly uses Definition 5.5 for its equivalence relation on cell systems. The last paragraph of its proof on p. 26 asserts that expansion of the all-strand diagram gives exactly that definition. The displayed two-factor formula contradicts this central identification. The Major assessment concerns that actual proof dependency. The diagram provides the intended repair, but the example does not prove that the existential equivalence classes have different cardinality after correcting the definition, or that any intended module category fails to exist.

# Local formula correction

## Braid eigenvalue, p. 12

Minor. Remark 2.13 gives eigenvalues \(-1,q^2\) for \(Y=U/q-\operatorname{id}\). Since \(U\) has eigenvalues \(0,[2]=q+q^{-1}\), the two values are actually \[-1,\qquad [2]/q-1=q^{-2}.\] The later calculations on pp. 15 and 18 use \(q^{-2}\), which is consistent with the corrected convention. This is a local statement correction, not a separate disproof of the later skein calculations.
